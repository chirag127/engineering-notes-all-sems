The court structure in England and Wales

The court structure in England and Wales is made up of the following courts:

The Supreme Court -The Supreme Court is the highest court in the land and is the final court of appeal in the UK.

The Court of Appeal -The Court of Appeal hears appeals from the High Court and other lower courts.

The High Court -The High Court deals with serious criminal cases and civil cases involving large amounts of money.

The Crown Court -The Crown Court deals with all criminal cases that are committed by adults.

Magistrates' courts -Magistrates' courts deal with less serious criminal cases and civil cases.

Common Law or Case law




Common law is law that is developed by judges through decisions of courts and similar tribunals rather than through legislative statutes or executive branch action. A common law system is one that gives great precedential weight to common law, on the principle that it is unfair to treat similar facts differently on different occasions. The body of common law, and precedent, began to develop under the English monarchy in the Middle Ages. It was based on custom and usage rather than any codified law. Parliament did not codify the common law until the 19th century.

Common law arises from the decisions of courts, tribunals and similar bodies. These bodies apply the law to particular cases and controversies. In doing so, they interpret the law and develop the common law. The common law is thus a body of law that is developed over time through the decisions of courts and tribunals. It is not a static body of law; it is constantly evolving and growing.

The common law is not always clear and unambiguous. There may be gaps in the law or areas where the law is uncertain. When this happens, it is up to the courts to interpret the law and fill in the gaps. The courts do this by looking at the existing body of law (precedent) and applying it to the facts of the case before them. In doing so, they may make new law or clarify existing law.

The common law is an important source of law in many jurisdictions. In some jurisdictions, such as the United States, the common law is the primary source of law. In others, such as England and Wales, the common law is a secondary source of law, behind statutory law. In many jurisdictions, the common law is used alongside statutory law to interpret and apply the law.

It is presided over by a president and has two or more members, all of whom are appointed by the state government. The district consumer forum has the power to pass orders on any matter relating to a consumer dispute, including an order for the refund of money or replacement of goods. Tribunals




A tribunal is a formal, public forum in which a dispute is heard and decided. Tribunals are typically presided over by one or more judges and may also include a jury. The parties to a dispute present their respective cases before the tribunal, which then renders a decision.

Tribunals are often used to resolve disputes that are too complex or sensitive for traditional courts. They are also used when the parties to a dispute agree to submit their case to a tribunal rather than have it decided by a judge.

Tribunals typically have the power to issue binding decisions, which the parties to a dispute are required to comply with. In some cases, however, a tribunal may issue a non-binding decision, which the parties are free to ignore.

Tribunals are often used to resolve disputes involving:

• Contracts

• Employment

• Land use

• Property

• Wills and trusts

Law at workplace.




There are many laws that apply to the workplace. Some of these laws are federal laws, which means they apply to all employers and employees in the United States. Other laws are state laws, which means they only apply to employers and employees in a particular state.

The most important federal laws that apply to the workplace are the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, and the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993. These laws prohibit discrimination in the workplace based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, or pregnancy.

The Civil Rights Act of 1964 is the most important law that prohibits discrimination in the workplace. This law makes it illegal for employers to discriminate against employees on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, or national origin. The law also makes it illegal for employers to retaliate against employees who complain about discrimination.

The Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 is a law that prohibits discrimination in the workplace against employees with disabilities. This law requires employers to provide reasonable accommodations for employees with disabilities.

The Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 is a law that requires employers to provide employees with up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for certain medical and family reasons. This law also prohibits employers from retaliating against employees who take leave.
