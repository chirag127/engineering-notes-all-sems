### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery,

Introduction to Oral Communication:

Oral communication refers to the exchange of information, ideas, and opinions through spoken words. It is a crucial aspect of human interaction and is used in a variety of contexts, including personal, academic, and professional settings.

Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery:

1. Nuances: Nuances refer to the subtle differences in tone, pitch, and inflection in spoken language. These nuances can convey different emotions, attitudes, and meanings, and are important for effective oral communication.

2. Modes of Speech Delivery: There are several modes of speech delivery, including formal and informal, impromptu and prepared, and extemporaneous and manuscript. The mode of speech delivery is determined by the context, audience, and purpose of the communication.

In conclusion, oral communication is an essential aspect of human interaction, and understanding the nuances and modes of speech delivery is important for effective communication. Effective oral communication requires an understanding of the context, audience, and purpose of the communication, as well as the ability to effectively convey information, ideas, and opinions through spoken words.
### Speaking style ; content and sequencing.

Speaking Style:

Speaking style refers to the way in which a speaker delivers a message, including their tone, pace, volume, and body language. It can have a significant impact on the effectiveness of a speech or presentation.

1. Tone: Tone refers to the speaker's emotional state and can influence the audience's perception of the message. A confident and enthusiastic tone can engage the audience and increase their interest in the topic, while a monotone or disinterested tone can disengage the audience.

2. Pace: Pace refers to the speed at which a speaker delivers a message. A slow pace can emphasize important points, while a fast pace can convey a sense of urgency.

3. Volume: Volume refers to the loudness of a speaker's voice. A loud voice can grab the audience's attention, while a quiet voice can convey a sense of intimacy.

4. Body Language: Body language refers to the nonverbal cues that a speaker uses to convey meaning, such as gestures, facial expressions, and posture. Body language can enhance or detract from the message being delivered.

Content and Sequencing:

Content and sequencing refer to the structure and organization of a speech or presentation.

1. Content: Content refers to the information that is being presented. It should be relevant, accurate, and engaging. The content should be structured in a logical and coherent manner, with clear transitions between points.

2. Sequencing: Sequencing refers to the order in which the content is presented. The content should be organized in a way that makes sense and builds towards a conclusion. The introduction should provide context and engage the audience, while the conclusion should summarize the main points and leave a lasting impression.

In conclusion, speaking style and content and sequencing are important elements of effective communication. A speaker should be aware of their tone, pace, volume, and body language, and the content should be relevant, accurate, and engaging, with a clear structure and organization.

### management.

Management:

Management is the process of planning, organizing, directing, and controlling the efforts of an organization's resources to achieve its goals and objectives. It involves making decisions, allocating resources, and ensuring that tasks are completed efficiently and effectively.

1. Functions of management: The main functions of management include planning, organizing, directing, and controlling. Planning involves setting goals and objectives, determining the best ways to achieve them, and allocating resources. Organizing involves arranging the resources and activities of the organization to achieve its goals. Directing involves motivating and guiding employees to achieve the goals and objectives of the organization. Controlling involves monitoring performance, comparing it with goals and objectives, and making adjustments as necessary.

2. Levels of management: Management can be divided into three levels: top management, middle management, and lower management. Top management is responsible for making strategic decisions and setting the overall direction of the organization. Middle management is responsible for implementing the decisions of top management and ensuring that the goals and objectives of the organization are met. Lower management is responsible for supervising the day-to-day operations of the organization.

3. Management styles: Different management styles can have a significant impact on the success of an organization. Some common management styles include autocratic, democratic, and laissez-faire. Autocratic management involves making decisions without consulting employees, while democratic management involves involving employees in decision-making. Laissez-faire management involves giving employees a great deal of freedom to make decisions and complete tasks.

4. Management theories: There are several theories of management that have been developed over the years, including scientific management, human relations, and contingency theory. Scientific management involves using scientific methods to increase efficiency and productivity. Human relations theory focuses on the importance of good relationships between employees and managers. Contingency theory recognizes that different situations require different management approaches.

In conclusion, management is a critical component of any organization. It involves making decisions, allocating resources, and ensuring that tasks are completed efficiently and effectively. The functions of management, levels of management, management styles, and management theories are all important aspects of management that can impact the success of an organization.
### Minutes of meeting,

Minutes of meeting is a written document that records the proceedings of a meeting. It includes a summary of the discussions, decisions, and action items agreed upon during the meeting. The purpose of minutes of meeting is to provide a record of what was discussed and agreed upon during the meeting, and to ensure that everyone is on the same page.

1. Preparation: Minutes of meeting should be prepared as soon as possible after the meeting, while the details are still fresh in the minds of the participants. The person responsible for preparing the minutes should have a clear understanding of the meeting's objectives, agenda, and discussions.

2. Content: Minutes of meeting should include the following information:
- Date, time, and location of the meeting
- Names of attendees and absentees
- Agenda items and discussion points
- Decisions and action items agreed upon
- Any other significant information discussed during the meeting

3. Format: Minutes of meeting can be prepared in a variety of formats, including bullet points, paragraph form, or a table. The format should be clear, concise, and easy to read.

4. Approval: Minutes of meeting should be reviewed and approved by the participants at the next meeting. This ensures that the minutes accurately reflect the proceedings of the meeting and that everyone is in agreement with the decisions and action items agreed upon.

5. Distribution: Minutes of meeting should be distributed to all participants, as well as any other relevant stakeholders. This ensures that everyone has access to the information discussed during the meeting and can take action on the agreed-upon decisions and action items.

In conclusion, minutes of meeting is a written document that records the proceedings of a meeting. It is an important tool for ensuring that everyone is on the same page and that the decisions and action items agreed upon during the meeting are carried out.
