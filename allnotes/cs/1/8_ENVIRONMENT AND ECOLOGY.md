## Unit 3 - Pollution and their Effects; Public Health Aspects of Environmental; Water Pollution, Air Pollution, Soil Pollution, Noise Pollution, Solid waste management.

Unit 3 - Pollution and their Effects; Public Health Aspects of Environmental; Water Pollution, Air Pollution, Soil Pollution, Noise Pollution, Solid Waste Management:

Unit 3 of Fundamentals of Environmental Engineering covers the topics of pollution and their effects, public health aspects of the environment, and various types of pollution, including water pollution, air pollution, soil pollution, noise pollution, and solid waste management.

1. Pollution and their Effects: Pollution refers to the presence or introduction into the environment of substances or energy that cause harmful or undesirable effects. The effects of pollution can be both physical and biological, and can have serious consequences for human health and the environment.

2. Public Health Aspects of Environmental: Public health aspects of the environment refer to the ways in which the environment affects human health. This includes the impact of pollution on human health, as well as the impact of environmental factors such as climate change, natural disasters, and exposure to hazardous substances.

3. Water Pollution: Water pollution refers to the presence in water of harmful substances or energy that cause adverse effects on human health or the environment. This can include chemicals, pathogens, and other contaminants.

4. Air Pollution: Air pollution refers to the presence in the air of harmful substances or energy that cause adverse effects on human health or the environment. This can include chemicals, particulate matter, and other contaminants.

5. Soil Pollution: Soil pollution refers to the presence in soil of harmful substances or energy that cause adverse effects on human health or the environment. This can include chemicals, pathogens, and other contaminants.

6. Noise Pollution: Noise pollution refers to excessive or unwanted noise that can cause adverse effects on human health or the environment. This can include noise from transportation, industrial activities, and other sources.

7. Solid Waste Management: Solid waste management refers to the collection, transport, treatment, and disposal of solid waste. This includes the management of household waste, industrial waste, and hazardous waste.

In conclusion, Unit 3 of Fundamentals of Environmental Engineering covers the topics of pollution and their effects, public health aspects of the environment, and various types of pollution, including water pollution, air pollution, soil pollution, noise pollution, and solid waste management. Understanding these topics is essential for addressing the challenges of environmental pollution and protecting human health and the environment.

Unit 2 - Natural Resources:

Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers the introduction and classification of natural resources, including water, mineral, energy, and forest resources.

1. Introduction: Natural resources are resources that are found in nature and are used by humans for various purposes. They include water, minerals, energy, and forests.

2. Classification: Natural resources can be classified into renewable resources, such as water and forests, and non-renewable resources, such as minerals and fossil fuels.

3. Water Resources: Water is a vital natural resource that is essential for human survival. The availability of water is determined by factors such as precipitation, evaporation, and human consumption. The quality of water is affected by factors such as pollutants, minerals, and disease-causing organisms.

4. Water Borne and Water Induced Diseases: Water-borne and water-induced diseases are caused by the consumption of contaminated water. These diseases can have a significant impact on human health, particularly in developing countries.

5. Fluoride and Arsenic Problems in Drinking Water: Fluoride and arsenic are two minerals that can occur in drinking water and can have a negative impact on human health. Fluoride can cause dental and skeletal fluorosis, while arsenic can cause skin lesions, cancers, and cardiovascular disease.

6. Mineral Resources: Minerals are non-renewable resources that are extracted from the earth for various purposes, including construction and manufacturing. The material cycles of minerals, such as carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur cycles, are important for understanding the impact of mineral extraction on the environment.

7. Energy Resources: Energy is a vital natural resource that is used to power various human activities. There are two main types of energy resources: conventional and non-conventional. Conventional energy resources, such as fossil fuels, are finite, while non-conventional energy resources, such as solar and wind energy, are renewable.

8. Forest Resources: Forests are renewable resources that provide a range of benefits, including wood products, wildlife habitat, and carbon sequestration. The depletion of forests can have a significant impact on the environment, including soil erosion, loss of biodiversity, and increased greenhouse gas emissions.

In conclusion, Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers the introduction and classification of natural resources, including water, mineral, energy, and forest resources. It is important to understand the availability, sources, and quality aspects of these resources, as well as the impact of their extraction and use on the environment.

Unit 5 - Environmental Protection:

Unit 5 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers Environmental Protection. 2. Population growth can have a significant impact on the environment, including increased demand for resources, increased pollution, and increased pressure on ecosystems.

4. Population Growth: Population growth is a major contributor to environmental degradation, as it increases demand for resources, contributes to pollution, and puts pressure on ecosystems.

5. Environmental Education: Environmental education is the process of teaching individuals about the natural environment and the impact of human activities on the environment. Environmental education helps individuals develop the knowledge, skills, and attitudes needed to protect the environment.

6. Women's Education: Women's education is an important factor in environmental protection, as it is linked to reduced population growth and improved environmental outcomes. Women who are educated are more likely to have smaller families, which reduces pressure on the environment.

In conclusion, Unit 5 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers Environmental Protection and includes topics such as the Environmental Protection Act 1986, initiatives by NGOs, human population and the environment, population growth, environmental education, and women's education. Environmental protection is important for the preservation of the natural environment and the protection of human health and well-being. ## Unit 1 - Environment: Definition, Types of Environment, Components of environment, Segments of environment, Scope and importance, Need for Public Awareness. Unit 1 - Environment:
Environment refers to the natural world and all the living and non-living things that make it up. It includes air, water, soil, living organisms, and the physical, chemical, and biological processes that occur within it.

Types of Environment:
- Terrestrial environment: Refers to the land-based environment, including forests, deserts, and grasslands.
- Aquatic environment: Refers to the water-based environment, including oceans, lakes, and rivers.
- Atmosphere: Refers to the gaseous environment surrounding the Earth, including air and clouds.

Components of Environment:
- Biotic components: Living things, such as plants, animals, and microorganisms.
- Abiotic components: Non-living things, such as air, water, soil, and minerals.

Segments of Environment:
- Physical environment: Refers to the physical features of the environment, such as mountains, rivers, and oceans.
- Chemical environment: Refers to the chemical composition of the environment, including air, water, and soil.
- Biological environment: Refers to the living things in the environment, including plants, animals, and microorganisms.

Scope and Importance:
The study of environment encompasses a wide range of topics, including ecology, geology, atmospheric science, and hydrology. It is important because it helps us understand the natural world and the relationships between living and non-living things. It also helps us understand the impact of human activities on the environment and the need for sustainable development.

Need for Public Awareness:
Public awareness of environmental issues is important because it helps to promote responsible behavior and decision-making. It also helps to ensure that environmental concerns are taken into account in policy-making and planning processes.

Ecosystem:
An ecosystem is a community of living and non-living things that interact with each other in a specific environment. It includes all the living and non-living things in a particular area, and the relationships between them.

Types of Ecosystem:
- Terrestrial ecosystems: Ecosystems that occur on land, such as forests, deserts, and grasslands.
- Aquatic ecosystems: Ecosystems that occur in water, such as oceans, lakes, and rivers.
- Marine ecosystems: Ecosystems that occur in saltwater, such as oceans and estuaries.
- Freshwater ecosystems: Ecosystems that occur in freshwater, such as lakes and rivers.

Structure of Ecosystem:
The structure of an ecosystem includes the living and non-living components and the relationships between them. It includes the biotic components, such as plants, animals, and microorganisms, and the abiotic components, such as air, water, and soil.

Food Chain:
A food chain is a series of organisms that are linked by their feeding relationships. It starts with a producer, such as a plant, and ends with a top predator, such as a lion.

Food Web:
A food web is a complex network of feeding relationships that includes multiple food chains and the interactions between them.

Ecological Pyramid:
An ecological pyramid is a graphical representation of the relationships between different levels of an ecosystem, such as producers, consumers, and decomposers.

Balance Ecosystem:
A balanced ecosystem is one in which the living and non-living components are in a state of equilibrium, with no one species dominating the others.

Effects of Human Activities:
Human activities, such as food, shelter, housing, agriculture, industry, mining, transportation, economic and social security, can have a significant impact on the environment. Activities such as deforestation, pollution, and overfishing can disrupt the balance of ecosystems and lead to declines in biodiversity.

Environmental Impact Assessment:
Environmental impact assessment (EIA) is a process that evaluates the potential environmental impacts of a proposed project or development. It is used to identify potential impacts and to develop strategies to mitigate or prevent them.

Sustainable Development:
Sustainable development is a concept that seeks to balance economic, social, and environmental considerations in the development and use of natural resources. It is based on the principle that development should meet the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
## Unit 4 - Current Environmental Issues of Importance; Global Warming, Green House Effects, Climate Change, Acid Rain, Ozone Layer Formation and Depletion, Population Growth and Automobile pollution, Burning of paddy straw.

Unit 4 - Current Environmental Issues of Importance:

Unit 4 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers the study of current environmental issues of importance. In this unit, students will learn about the following topics: 

1. Global Warming: Global warming refers to the increase in the Earth's average surface temperature due to human activities, such as the burning of fossil fuels, deforestation, and land-use changes.

2. Green House Effects: The greenhouse effect refers to the trapping of heat in the Earth's atmosphere by gases, such as carbon dioxide, that prevent heat from escaping into space. This leads to an increase in the Earth's average surface temperature.

3. Climate Change: Climate change refers to long-term changes in the Earth's climate, including changes in temperature, precipitation, and wind patterns. Climate change is largely caused by human activities, such as the burning of fossil fuels and deforestation.

4. Acid Rain: Acid rain is rain that has a pH lower than 5.6, caused by the emission of sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides into the atmosphere. Acid rain can cause damage to forests, lakes, and rivers, and can also harm human health.

5. Ozone Layer Formation and Depletion: The ozone layer is a layer of ozone in the Earth's stratosphere that protects the Earth from harmful ultraviolet radiation from the sun. The ozone layer is being depleted by human activities, such as the release of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).

6. Population Growth and Automobile Pollution: Population growth and the increasing use of automobiles have led to increased air pollution, which can harm human health and the environment.

7. Burning of Paddy Straw: The burning of paddy straw is a common practice in many parts of the world, but it releases harmful pollutants into the atmosphere and contributes to air pollution.

In conclusion, Unit 4 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers the study of current environmental issues of importance, including global warming, the greenhouse effect, climate change, acid rain, ozone layer formation and depletion, population growth and automobile pollution, and the burning of paddy straw. Understanding these issues is important for addressing the challenges facing our planet and promoting sustainable development.
