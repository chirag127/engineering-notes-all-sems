### Introduction - History and Evolution

Business Intelligence (BI) refers to the technologies, applications, and practices for the collection, integration, analysis, and presentation of business information. The purpose of BI is to support better business decision making.

The concept of BI has evolved over time, with its roots tracing back to the early days of computing. In the 1960s, businesses began using computers to store and process large amounts of data. This led to the development of decision support systems (DSS), which were designed to help managers make better decisions by providing them with relevant data and analysis.

In the 1980s, the term "executive information systems" (EIS) was introduced to describe systems that provided top-level managers with easy access to key business data. These systems were designed to be user-friendly and to present data in a graphical format.

In the 1990s, the term "business intelligence" was coined to describe a broader set of tools and techniques for analyzing business data. These tools included data warehousing, online analytical processing (OLAP), and data mining.

Since then, the field of BI has continued to evolve, with the introduction of new technologies such as big data analytics, cloud computing, and mobile BI. Today, BI is an essential tool for businesses of all sizes, helping them to make better decisions and to compete more effectively in the global marketplace.

### Functionalities & Classifications of Data Mining

Data mining is the process of discovering patterns and knowledge from large amounts of data. The data sources can include databases, data warehouses, the internet, and other sources.

The main functionalities of data mining are:

1. **Association rule learning:** This is a method for discovering interesting relationships between variables in large databases. For example, a supermarket might use association rule learning to find out which products are frequently bought together, and use this information for marketing purposes.

2. **Classification:** This is the task of assigning objects to one of several predefined categories based on their characteristics. For example, a bank might use classification to determine whether to give a loan to a customer based on their credit history.

3. **Clustering:** This is the task of grouping a set of objects in such a way that objects in the same group are more similar to each other than to those in other groups. For example, a marketing department might use clustering to segment its customer base into different groups based on their buying habits.

4. **Regression:** This is the task of predicting a continuous value based on the values of other variables. For example, a real estate agent might use regression to predict the price of a house based on its size, location, and other factors.

5. **Summarization:** This is the task of providing a compact representation of a dataset, including visualization and report generation.

Data mining can be classified into two categories: descriptive and predictive. Descriptive data mining is concerned with finding patterns that describe the data, while predictive data mining is concerned with making predictions based on the data.

### Data Modelling

### Schema – Star and Snowflake Schemas

A schema is a logical representation of the structure of a database. In the context of data warehousing, two common types of schemas are the star schema and the snowflake schema.

#### Star Schema

- The star schema is a type of schema where a central fact table is connected to multiple dimension tables.
- The fact table contains the measures or facts of the data, while the dimension tables contain descriptive information about the facts.
- The dimension tables are denormalized, meaning that they contain redundant data to improve query performance.
- The star schema is simple and easy to understand, making it a popular choice for data warehousing.

#### Snowflake Schema

- The snowflake schema is a variation of the star schema where the dimension tables are normalized.
- This means that the dimension tables are split into multiple related tables to reduce data redundancy.
- The snowflake schema is more complex than the star schema, but it can result in more efficient storage and improved query performance in some cases.

These are the basic concepts of star and snowflake schemas in the context of data warehousing. They are important to understand when designing and implementing a data warehouse for business intelligence and analytics.

### Data Transformation – Standardization and Feature Extraction

Data transformation is the process of converting data from one format or structure to another, to make it more suitable for analysis or to support specific business requirements. Two common data transformation techniques are standardization and feature extraction.

#### Standardization

Standardization is the process of transforming data to have zero mean and unit variance. This is often done when the data has different scales or units, and it is necessary to bring them to a common scale for analysis. Standardization is calculated as follows:

z = (x - mean) / standard deviation

Where z is the standardized value, x is the original value, mean is the mean of the data, and standard deviation is the standard deviation of the data.

#### Feature Extraction

Feature extraction is the process of transforming the data to create new features that better represent the underlying patterns in the data. This can be done through techniques such as principal component analysis (PCA), which identifies the most important features in the data and creates new features that are combinations of the original features. Feature extraction can help to reduce the dimensionality of the data, making it easier to analyze and visualize.

In summary, data transformation is an important step in data preparation for business intelligence and analytics. ### Data Reduction – Sampling

Data reduction is the process of reducing the amount of data that needs to be stored, processed, and analyzed while still maintaining the integrity and usefulness of the data. One of the techniques used for data reduction is sampling.

Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of the data to represent the entire population. This can be done in several ways, including:

1. **Simple random sampling:** In this method, each data point has an equal chance of being selected for the sample.

2. **Stratified sampling:** In this method, the population is divided into different strata or groups, and a sample is selected from each stratum.

3. **Cluster sampling:** In this method, the population is divided into clusters, and a sample of clusters is selected. All the data points within the selected clusters are included in the sample.

4. **Systematic sampling:** In this method, a sample is selected by choosing every kth data point from the population.

Sampling can be an effective way to reduce the amount of data that needs to be processed and analyzed, while still maintaining the accuracy and representativeness of the data. However, it is important to carefully choose the sampling method and sample size to ensure that the sample accurately represents the population.

### Root Cause Analysis

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is a systematic approach to identifying the underlying causes of problems or incidents. It is commonly used in the field of Business Intelligence and Analytics as part of the implementation process. The goal of RCA is to determine what happened, why it happened, and how to prevent it from happening again.

Here are some key points to consider when conducting a Root Cause Analysis:

1. **Define the problem:** Clearly define the problem or incident that occurred. This includes gathering data and information about the event.

2. **Gather data:** Collect data and information related to the problem. This can include interviewing those involved, reviewing documentation, and analyzing data.

3. **Identify possible causes:** Use the data and information gathered to identify possible causes of the problem. This can be done using various techniques such as brainstorming, cause-and-effect diagrams, and the 5 Whys.

4. **Determine the root cause:** Analyze the possible causes to determine the root cause of the problem. This is the underlying cause that, if addressed, will prevent the problem from recurring.

5. **Develop and implement a solution:** Develop a solution to address the root cause and implement it. This can include changes to processes, procedures, or systems.

6. **Monitor and evaluate:** Monitor the effectiveness of the solution and evaluate its impact. Make any necessary adjustments to ensure the problem does not recur.

Root Cause Analysis is an important tool in the implementation of Business Intelligence and Analytics. By identifying and addressing the underlying causes of problems, organizations can improve their processes and prevent future incidents.
