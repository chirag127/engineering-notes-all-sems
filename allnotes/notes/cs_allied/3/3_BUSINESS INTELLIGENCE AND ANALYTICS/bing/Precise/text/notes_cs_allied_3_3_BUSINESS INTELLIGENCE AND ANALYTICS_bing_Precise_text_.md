In the 1980s, executive information systems (EIS) were introduced, which provided top-level executives with easy access to internal and external information relevant to their business. These systems were designed to be user-friendly and allowed executives to view data in a graphical format.

In the 1990s, the term "business intelligence" was coined to describe the process of turning data into information and then into knowledge. This was achieved through the use of various tools and techniques, such as data warehousing, data mining, and online analytical processing (OLAP).

Since then, BI has continued to evolve, with the introduction of new technologies and techniques, such as big data analytics, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence. These advancements have allowed businesses to gain deeper insights into their data and make more informed decisions.

In summary, BI has a rich history and has evolved significantly over time. Its purpose remains the same: to help businesses make better decisions by providing them with the information they need. - **Information** is data that has been processed, organized, and presented in a meaningful way. Information is created by analyzing and interpreting data. For example, sales data can be analyzed to create a report that shows trends in sales over time.

- **Knowledge** is the understanding and insight gained from information. Knowledge is created by interpreting and analyzing information. For example, by analyzing sales trends, a business can gain knowledge about which products are selling well and which are not.

In the context of business intelligence and analytics, data is collected and processed to create information. This information is then analyzed to create knowledge, which can be used to make informed decisions. Understanding the relationship between data, information, and knowledge is essential for effective business intelligence and analytics.

### Functionalities & Classifications of Data Mining

Data mining is the process of discovering patterns and knowledge from large amounts of data. The data sources can include databases, data warehouses, the internet, and other sources.

The main functionalities of data mining are:

1. **Association rule learning:** This is a method for discovering interesting relations between variables in large databases. For example, a supermarket might gather data on customer purchasing habits. Using association rule learning, the supermarket can determine which products are frequently bought together and use this information for marketing purposes.

2. **Classification:** This is the task of assigning objects to one of several predefined categories based on their characteristics. For example, an email program might attempt to classify an email as "legitimate" or as "spam".

3. **Clustering:** This is the task of grouping a set of objects in such a way that objects in the same group are more similar to each other than to those in other groups. For example, a market researcher might use clustering to segment customers into different groups based on their demographics and purchasing behavior.

4. **Regression:** This is the task of predicting a continuous-valued attribute associated with an object. For example, a stock market analysis program might use regression to predict the future price of a stock based on its past performance.

5. **Summarization:** This is the task of providing a compact representation of a data set, including visualization and report generation.

Data mining can be classified into two categories: descriptive data mining and predictive data mining.

- **Descriptive data mining** is concerned with finding patterns that describe the data and can be interpreted by humans. Examples of descriptive data mining tasks include clustering, association rule learning, and summarization.

- **Predictive data mining** is concerned with building models that can be used to predict future behavior or unknown values of some variables. Examples of predictive data mining tasks include classification and regression.

### Data Modelling

Data modelling is the process of creating a conceptual representation of data objects and their relationships. It is an essential step in the design of databases and data warehouses, and is used to ensure that data is organized in a way that is efficient, accurate, and easy to use.

In the context of Business Intelligence and Analytics, data modelling is used to design data warehouses that support the analysis and reporting needs of an organization. This involves creating a logical model of the data, which defines the relationships between different data objects, and a physical model, which specifies how the data will be stored and accessed.

There are several approaches to data modelling, including:

1. **Entity-Relationship (ER) modelling:** This approach involves identifying the entities (or objects) in the data and defining the relationships between them. An ER model is typically represented using a diagram, with boxes representing entities and lines representing relationships.

2. **Dimensional modelling:** This approach is commonly used in data warehousing and involves organizing data into facts and dimensions. Facts represent the measures or metrics that are being analyzed, while dimensions represent the attributes or characteristics of the data.

3. **Object-oriented modelling:** This approach involves defining classes and objects, and specifying the relationships between them. It is commonly used in object-oriented programming and can be useful for modelling complex data structures.

Data modelling is an iterative process, and it is important to involve stakeholders and subject matter experts to ensure that the model accurately represents the data and supports the needs of the organization. Once the data model is complete, it can be used to design the database or data warehouse, and to develop the ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) processes that are used to populate the data.

### Schema – Star and Snowflake Schemas

A schema is a logical representation of the structure of a database. In the context of data warehousing, two common types of schemas are the star schema and the snowflake schema.

#### Star Schema

- The star schema is a type of schema where a central fact table is connected to multiple dimension tables.
- The fact table contains the measures or facts of the data, while the dimension tables contain descriptive information about the facts.
- The dimension tables are denormalized, meaning that they contain redundant data to improve query performance.
- The star schema is simple and easy to understand, making it a popular choice for data warehousing.

#### Snowflake Schema

- The snowflake schema is a variation of the star schema where the dimension tables are normalized.
- This means that the dimension tables are split into multiple related tables to reduce data redundancy.
- The snowflake schema is more complex than the star schema, but it can result in more efficient storage and faster query performance in some cases.
- However, the snowflake schema can also be more difficult to design and maintain.

In summary, the star and snowflake schemas are two common types of schemas used in data warehousing. The star schema is simple and easy to understand, while the snowflake schema is more complex but can result in more efficient storage and faster query performance in some cases. The choice between the two schemas depends on the specific needs and requirements of the data warehouse.

### Data Transformation – Standardization and Feature Extraction

Data transformation is the process of converting data from one format or structure to another, to make it more suitable for analysis or to support specific business requirements. Two common data transformation techniques are standardization and feature extraction.

1. **Standardization** is the process of rescaling the data to have a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one. This is also known as normalization or z-score normalization. Standardization is useful when the data has different scales or units, as it brings all the variables to a common scale, making it easier to compare and analyze the data.

2. **Feature extraction** is the process of creating new features or variables from the existing data, to improve the predictive power of the model or to make the data easier to analyze. Feature extraction can involve techniques such as principal component analysis, which reduces the dimensionality of the data by creating new features that capture the most important information in the data.

Both standardization and feature extraction are important steps in the data preparation process for business intelligence and analytics. They can help improve the accuracy and reliability of the analysis, and support better decision-making.



### Data Reduction – Sampling

Data reduction is the process of reducing the amount of data that needs to be stored, processed, and analyzed while still maintaining the integrity and usefulness of the data. One of the techniques used for data reduction is sampling.

Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of the data to represent the entire population. This can be done in several ways, including:

1. **Simple random sampling:** In this method, each data point has an equal chance of being selected for the sample.

2. **Stratified sampling:** In this method, the population is divided into different subgroups or strata, and then a sample is taken from each stratum.

3. **Cluster sampling:** In this method, the population is divided into clusters, and then a sample of clusters is selected. All the data points within the selected clusters are included in the sample.

4. **Systematic sampling:** In this method, a sample is selected by choosing every kth data point from the population.

Sampling can be an effective way to reduce the amount of data that needs to be processed and analyzed, while still maintaining the representativeness of the data. However, it is important to choose an appropriate sampling method to ensure that the sample accurately represents the population.

### PCA for the notes of the Unit 3 - BI – DATA PREPARTTION in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

- PCA stands for Principal Component Analysis.
- It is a statistical technique used to reduce the dimensionality of a dataset.
- It does this by identifying the most important features or variables in the dataset and transforming the data into a new coordinate system.
- The new coordinate system is defined by the principal components, which are linear combinations of the original variables.
- The first principal component accounts for the largest amount of variance in the data, the second principal component accounts for the second largest amount of variance, and so on.
- By selecting only the first few principal components, we can reduce the dimensionality of the data while retaining most of the information contained in the original dataset.
- PCA is commonly used in data analysis and visualization, as well as in machine learning for feature extraction and data compression.
- It is important to note that PCA is a linear technique and may not be suitable for datasets with non-linear relationships between variables.



### Data Discretization

Data discretization is the process of converting continuous data into discrete data by creating categories or bins for the values. This is often done in the context of data preparation for data mining or machine learning algorithms, where categorical data is preferred over continuous data.

There are several methods for data discretization, including:

1. **Equal width binning**: This method divides the range of the data into equally sized bins. The number of bins can be determined by the user or by using a rule of thumb, such as the square root of the number of data points.

2. **Equal frequency binning**: This method divides the data into bins such that each bin contains approximately the same number of data points. This can be useful when the data is skewed, as it ensures that each bin has a similar number of data points.

3. **Clustering**: This method uses a clustering algorithm to group the data into clusters, and then assigns each data point to the cluster it belongs to. This can be useful when the data has a natural grouping structure.

4. **Decision tree**: This method uses a decision tree algorithm to determine the best split points for the data. The tree is grown using the data as the training set, and the split points are determined by the tree structure.

Data discretization can be useful in many situations, including when dealing with continuous data that has a large range, when the data is skewed, or when the algorithm being used requires categorical data. However, it is important to choose the appropriate method for the data and the problem at hand, as the choice of method can have a significant impact on the results.

### Root Cause Analysis

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is a systematic process for identifying the underlying causes of problems or events. It is an important tool in the implementation of Business Intelligence (BI) as it helps to identify and address the root causes of issues that may arise during the BI process. Here are some key points to consider when conducting a Root Cause Analysis:

1. **Define the problem:** Clearly define the problem or issue that needs to be addressed. This will help to focus the analysis and ensure that the root cause is accurately identified.

2. **Gather data:** Collect data and information related to the problem. This may include interviewing stakeholders, reviewing documentation, and analyzing data.

3. **Identify possible causes:** Use the data gathered to identify possible causes of the problem. This may involve using tools such as the 5 Whys or Fishbone Diagram to help organize and analyze the information.

4. **Determine the root cause:** Use the information gathered to determine the root cause of the problem. This may involve further analysis and investigation to confirm the cause.

5. **Develop and implement a solution:** Once the root cause has been identified, develop and implement a solution to address the issue. This may involve making changes to processes, systems, or behaviors.

6. **Monitor and evaluate:** Monitor the effectiveness of the solution and evaluate its impact on the problem. This will help to ensure that the issue has been fully addressed and prevent it from recurring in the future.

Root Cause Analysis is a powerful tool for improving the implementation of BI and ensuring that issues are effectively addressed. By following a systematic process, organizations can identify and address the underlying causes of problems, leading to improved performance and outcomes.
