

# KOT 062 ENERGY HARVESTING TECHNOLOGIES AND POWERMANAGEMENT FOR IOT DEVICES

- This course covers the principles and applications of energy harvesting and power management for Internet of Things (IoT) devices in the 5G era.
- Energy harvesting is a promising solution to power IoT devices especially when they are installed in inaccessible areas and regular battery maintenance is not possible.
- Energy harvesting captures energy from ambient sources such as RF waves, light, thermal gradients, or mechanical motion to power IoT devices.
- Energy harvesting based on a renewable source enables IoT devices to generate electrical energy by absorbing energy from the environment. This technology will overcome the replacement and maintenance of the batteries resulting in more reliable and long-lasting solutions.
- Power management is the process of controlling the power consumption and supply of IoT devices to optimize their performance and efficiency. Power management techniques include voltage regulation, power conversion, energy storage, and load management.
- The course will cover the following topics:

  - Introduction to IoT and 5G networks: overview, challenges, and opportunities.
  - Energy harvesting sources and techniques: solar, piezoelectric, thermoelectric, electromagnetic, and hybrid systems.
  - Power management circuits and devices: DC-DC converters, charge pumps, energy harvesters, supercapacitors, and batteries.
  - Power management systems and architectures: power-aware design, energy harvesting networks, wireless power transfer, and energy-neutral operation.
  - Case studies and applications: smart sensors, wearable devices, biomedical implants, and environmental monitoring.

- The course objectives are:

  - To understand the fundamentals and principles of energy harvesting and power management for IoT devices.
  - To analyze and compare different energy harvesting sources and techniques for IoT applications.
  - To design and implement power management circuits and devices for IoT devices.
  - To evaluate and optimize power management systems and architectures for IoT devices.
  - To apply the knowledge and skills learned in the course to real-world problems and scenarios.



# KCS

KCS stands for Knowledge-Centered Service, which is a methodology for creating and maintaining documentation as part of the service delivery process . KCS aims to:

- Improve service quality and efficiency by capturing and reusing knowledge from service interactions
- Reduce costs and increase customer satisfaction by enabling self-service and reducing repeat incidents
- Enhance organizational learning and innovation by fostering a culture of collaboration and feedback

## KCS Principles

KCS is based on four basic principles:

- **Integrate**: Knowledge creation and maintenance should be integrated with the service delivery process, not a separate activity
- **Evolve**: Knowledge should be continuously improved and updated based on demand and usage
- **Collaborate**: Knowledge should be shared and validated by the collective experience of the service organization
- **Reward**: Knowledge workers should be recognized and incentivized for their knowledge contributions and quality

## KCS Practices

KCS consists of eight practices that support the implementation of the principles:

- **Capture**: Capture knowledge as a by-product of solving issues, using the customer's context and words
- **Structure**: Structure knowledge articles using a standard template and metadata, following the KCS article quality criteria
- **Reuse**: Reuse existing knowledge articles to solve issues, and flag any gaps or inaccuracies
- **Improve**: Improve knowledge articles based on feedback and usage, and retire obsolete or redundant articles
- **Search**: Search for existing knowledge articles before creating new ones, using a simple and consistent search method
- **Link**: Link knowledge articles to related issues, incidents, problems, and changes, to establish relationships and traceability
- **Flag**: Flag knowledge articles that need review, revision, or approval, using a workflow and governance model
- **Measure**: Measure the performance and impact of knowledge activities, using metrics and indicators that align with the KCS goals and objectives

## KCS Benefits

KCS can provide various benefits for service organizations, such as :

- Increased service quality and consistency, by providing accurate and relevant knowledge to customers and service agents
- Reduced service costs and effort, by minimizing the need for escalation, rework, and duplication of knowledge
- Increased service capacity and scalability, by enabling faster resolution, self-service, and automation of knowledge tasks
- Increased customer loyalty and satisfaction, by reducing frustration, increasing trust, and enhancing the service experience
- Increased employee engagement and retention, by empowering service agents, rewarding knowledge sharing, and fostering a learning culture
- Increased organizational agility and innovation, by leveraging knowledge insights, identifying improvement opportunities, and enabling continuous learning

## Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

- Energy harvesting systems are devices or systems that are used to convert ambient energy, which is derived from external sources existing in the atmosphere, into electrical energy.
- Energy harvesting systems are attractive alternatives to battery-operated systems, especially for long-term, low-power, and self-sustaining electronic systems.
- Energy harvesting systems can scavenge energy from various sources, such as mechanical load, vibrations, temperature gradients, light, radio frequency, etc., and convert them into electrical power in the nW-mW range .
- Energy harvesting systems typically consist of four main components: an energy transducer, an energy storage element, a power management circuit, and a load.
- Energy transducers are devices that convert one form of energy into another, such as piezoelectric, thermoelectric, photovoltaic, electromagnetic, etc.
- Energy storage elements are devices that store the harvested energy for later use, such as capacitors, batteries, supercapacitors, etc.
- Power management circuits are devices that regulate and optimize the harvested energy for the load, such as voltage converters, rectifiers, charge controllers, etc.
- Load is the device or system that consumes the harvested energy, such as sensors, microcontrollers, wireless transmitters, etc.
- Energy harvesting systems have various applications in different domains, such as biomedical, environmental, industrial, military, etc .
- Energy harvesting systems have several advantages, such as reducing the dependency on batteries, extending the lifetime of systems, enabling remote and inaccessible operations, etc .
- Energy harvesting systems also have some challenges, such as low and variable power output, high initial cost, complex design and integration, etc .
