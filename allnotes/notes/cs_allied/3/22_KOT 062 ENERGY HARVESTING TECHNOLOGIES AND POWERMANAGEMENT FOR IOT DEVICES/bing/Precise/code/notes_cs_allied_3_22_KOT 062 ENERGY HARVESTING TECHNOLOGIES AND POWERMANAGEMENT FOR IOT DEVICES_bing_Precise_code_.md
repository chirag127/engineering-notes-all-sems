### Photovoltaic Cell Technologies

Photovoltaic (PV) cells, also known as solar cells, are devices that convert sunlight into electricity. There are several types of photovoltaic cell technologies, including:

1. **Crystalline Silicon (c-Si) Cells:** These are the most common type of PV cells and are made from silicon wafers. They are further divided into two types: monocrystalline and polycrystalline. Monocrystalline cells are made from a single crystal of silicon, while polycrystalline cells are made from multiple crystals. Monocrystalline cells are more efficient but also more expensive than polycrystalline cells.

2. **Thin-Film Cells:** These cells are made by depositing a thin layer of photovoltaic material onto a substrate. The most common types of thin-film cells are made from cadmium telluride (CdTe), copper indium gallium selenide (CIGS), and amorphous silicon (a-Si). Thin-film cells are less efficient than crystalline silicon cells but are cheaper to produce.

3. **Multi-Junction Cells:** These cells are made by stacking multiple layers of photovoltaic material, each layer being sensitive to a different part of the solar spectrum. This allows the cell to capture more sunlight and convert it into electricity. Multi-junction cells are the most efficient type of PV cell but are also the most expensive.

4. **Organic Cells:** These cells are made from organic materials such as polymers or small molecules. They are less efficient than other types of PV cells but are cheaper to produce and can be made into flexible, lightweight modules.

These are some of the main types of photovoltaic cell technologies used in energy harvesting systems. Each technology has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of technology depends on factors such as cost, efficiency, and application.



### Generation of Electric Power in Semiconductor PV Cells

Photovoltaic (PV) cells, also known as solar cells, are semiconductor devices that convert sunlight into electricity. The process of converting sunlight into electricity using PV cells is called the photovoltaic effect.

1. The photovoltaic effect occurs when photons of light are absorbed by a semiconductor material, such as silicon, in a PV cell.
2. The absorbed photons transfer their energy to electrons in the semiconductor material, causing the electrons to become excited and move to a higher energy state.
3. The excited electrons are then able to flow through the semiconductor material, creating an electric current.
4. The flow of electrons is directed by an electric field within the PV cell, which is created by the arrangement of the semiconductor material and the presence of impurities, known as dopants.
5. The electric field causes the electrons to flow in a specific direction, creating a voltage difference between the two sides of the PV cell.
6. When the PV cell is connected to an external circuit, the voltage difference causes the electrons to flow through the circuit, generating electric power.

This is a brief overview of the generation of electric power in semiconductor PV cells. It is a key concept in the study of energy harvesting systems, particularly in the context of the subject KOT 062 ENERGY HARVESTING TECHNOLOGIES AND POWERMANAGEMENT FOR IOT DEVICES KCS.

- The energy generated from the vibrations is employed in cases, such as remote health monitoring of bridges, powering up of human health monitoring devices, low powered medical implants etc. The vibration energy is usually converted into electrical energy by a piezo crystal .
- Vibrations are distinguished in steady state (harmonic) and transient. In a steady-state vibration, the velocity of each particle is a continuous periodic quantity. A transient vibration occurs by an initial disturbance without any further input and fades with time due to energy dissipation .
- A simple, effective way to passively control steady-state vibrations is through "vibration isolation". As an example, consider a base-excited single-DOF system for which we would like to reduce its absolute response motion .
- When the vibration amplitude goes up or down significantly, under steady state conditions, it is usually an indication that something is changing inside the machine. The overall amplitude is the main indication of a machine malfunction, and is perfectly suited for a 4-20 mA output to a Control System (PLC, SCADA, RTU etc.) .
- The steady state response is always harmonic, and has the same frequency as that of the forcing. To see this mathematically, note that in each case the solution has the form. Recall that defines the frequency of the force, the frequency of base excitation, or the rotor angular velocity .
- In a real system, damping makes the steady-state response independent of the initial conditions. 1. **Fossil fuels:** This method involves burning fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and natural gas to produce heat, which is then used to generate steam that drives a turbine to produce electricity.

2. **Nuclear power:** This method involves using nuclear reactions to produce heat, which is then used to generate steam that drives a turbine to produce electricity.

3. **Hydroelectric power:** This method involves using the energy of falling water to turn a turbine, which generates electricity.

4. **Wind power:** This method involves using the energy of the wind to turn a turbine, which generates electricity.

5. **Solar power:** This method involves using the energy from the sun to generate electricity. This can be done through the use of photovoltaic cells or through the use of concentrated solar power.

6. **Geothermal power:** This method involves using the heat from the earth to generate steam, which drives a turbine to produce electricity.

7. **Biomass power:** This method involves burning organic matter such as wood, crops, or waste to produce heat, which is then used to generate steam that drives a turbine to produce electricity.

Each of these methods has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of method depends on factors such as the availability of resources, the cost of production, and the environmental impact. ### Power Conditioning and Losses

Power conditioning is the process of modifying the electrical power supplied to a device to improve its performance and efficiency. This is particularly important for energy harvesting wireless sensors, as the power generated by the energy harvesting source may not be suitable for direct use by the sensor.

Some common methods of power conditioning include:

1. Voltage regulation: This involves adjusting the voltage supplied to the sensor to ensure that it is within the acceptable range for the device.

2. Filtering: This involves removing unwanted noise or fluctuations from the power supply to improve the performance of the sensor.

3. DC-DC conversion: This involves converting the power supply from one DC voltage level to another, to match the requirements of the sensor.

Losses can occur during the power conditioning process, reducing the overall efficiency of the system. These losses can be minimized by using efficient power conditioning components and techniques.

In summary, power conditioning is an important aspect of energy harvesting wireless sensor systems, as it helps to ensure that the power generated by the energy harvesting source is suitable for use by the sensor. Losses can occur during the power conditioning process, but these can be minimized through careful design and selection of components.
