### Measures of Dispersion for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics in the Subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML and Data Science

In statistics, measures of dispersion are used to describe how spread out or dispersed a dataset is. Dispersion is important because it tells us how much variability there is in the data, which in turn can help us make more informed decisions or draw more accurate conclusions. In this section, we will discuss some common measures of dispersion, including:

1. Range: The range is the difference between the maximum and minimum values in a dataset. It is a simple measure of dispersion that gives a rough idea of how spread out the data is.

2. Variance: The variance is a more precise measure of dispersion that takes into account the differences between each data point and the mean of the dataset. It is calculated by squaring the differences between each data point and the mean, summing these squares, and then dividing by the number of data points.

3. Standard deviation: The standard deviation is the square root of the variance. It is a widely used measure of dispersion that is especially useful when the dataset follows a normal distribution.

4. Interquartile range (IQR): The IQR is the range of the middle 50% of a dataset. It is calculated by subtracting the value of the 25th percentile (Q1) from the value of the 75th percentile (Q3).

5. Mean absolute deviation (MAD): The MAD is a measure of dispersion that is calculated by taking the absolute value of the differences between each data point and the mean of the dataset, summing these absolute values, and then dividing by the number of data points.

6. Coefficient of variation (CV): The CV is a measure of dispersion that is calculated by dividing the standard deviation of the dataset by the mean of the dataset. It is expressed as a percentage and is useful for comparing the dispersion of datasets with different units or scales.

In conclusion, measures of dispersion are important tools in statistics that help us understand how spread out or dispersed a dataset is. By calculating these measures, we can gain valuable insights into the variability of the data and make more informed decisions or draw more accurate conclusions. It is important to choose the appropriate measure of dispersion depending on the characteristics of the dataset and the research question at hand.



### Measures of Skewness and Kurtosis

In descriptive statistics, measures of skewness and kurtosis are used to describe the shape of a distribution. Skewness measures the degree of asymmetry in a distribution, while kurtosis measures the degree of peakedness or flatness.

Here are some important points to understand about measures of skewness and kurtosis:

1. Skewness is a measure of the asymmetry of a distribution. It shows whether the distribution is skewed to the left or right. A distribution with a positive skewness has a longer tail on the right side of the distribution, while a distribution with a negative skewness has a longer tail on the left side of the distribution.

2. Skewness can be calculated using different methods, such as Pearson's moment coefficient of skewness or the Bowley skewness coefficient.

3. A skewness value of zero indicates a symmetric distribution. A positive skewness value indicates a right-skewed distribution, while a negative skewness value indicates a left-skewed distribution.

4. Kurtosis is a measure of the peakedness or flatness of a distribution. It shows how much of the distribution is in the tails versus the center. A distribution with a high kurtosis value has more of its data in the tails, while a distribution with a low kurtosis value has more of its data in the center.

5. Kurtosis can be calculated using different methods, such as Pearson's moment coefficient of kurtosis or the Fisher-Pearson standardized moment coefficient of kurtosis.

6. A kurtosis value of three indicates a normal distribution. A kurtosis value greater than three indicates a distribution with heavier tails and a sharper peak, which is called leptokurtic. A kurtosis value less than three indicates a distribution with lighter tails and a flatter peak, which is called platykurtic.

7. Skewness and kurtosis can be useful in identifying outliers and in comparing distributions.

In summary, measures of skewness and kurtosis are important tools in descriptive statistics for analyzing the shape of a distribution. Understanding these measures can help you better interpret the data and make more informed decisions.

### Inference Procedure for Correlation Coefficient

In this article, we will discuss the inference procedure for correlation coefficient.

2. The next step is to calculate the sample correlation coefficient. This is done by dividing the covariance of the two variables by the product of their standard deviations.

3. We then calculate the test statistic, which is the t-statistic. The formula for the t-statistic is:

t = r * sqrt(n - 2) / sqrt(1 - r^2)

where r is the sample correlation coefficient, n is the sample size, and sqrt represents square root.

4. We then determine the p-value associated with the test statistic. This can be done using a t-distribution table or a statistical software.

5. Finally, we compare the p-value to the level of significance, which is usually set at 0.05 or 0.01. If the p-value is less than the level of significance, we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that there is a significant correlation between the two variables. If the p-value is greater than the level of significance, we fail to reject the null hypothesis and conclude that there is no significant correlation between the two variables.

### Linear Regression and its Inference Procedure

Linear regression is a statistical method used to model the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables. It is widely used in machine learning and data science for predicting outcomes based on a set of input variables. Here are some key points to keep in mind when working with linear regression and its inference procedure:

1. Simple Linear Regression: Simple linear regression is used when there is only one independent variable. The equation for simple linear regression is Y = a + bX, where Y is the dependent variable, X is the independent variable, a is the intercept, and b is the slope.

2. Multiple Linear Regression: Multiple linear regression is used when there are two or more independent variables. The equation for multiple linear regression is Y = a + b1X1 + b2X2 + ... + bnXn, where Y is the dependent variable, X1, X2, ..., Xn are the independent variables, a is the intercept, and b1, b2, ..., bn are the slopes.

3. Inference Procedure: Inference procedure involves testing the significance of the regression coefficients and the overall fit of the model. The most commonly used test for significance of regression coefficients is the t-test, which measures the ratio of the estimated coefficient to its standard error.

4. Hypothesis Testing: Hypothesis testing is used to determine whether the regression coefficients are significantly different from zero. The null hypothesis is that the coefficient is equal to zero, and the alternative hypothesis is that it is not equal to zero.

5. Confidence Intervals: Confidence intervals are used to estimate the range of values within which the true value of the regression coefficient is likely to fall. The confidence level is usually set at 95% or 99%.

6. Goodness of Fit: Goodness of fit measures how well the model fits the data. The most commonly used measure of goodness of fit is the R-squared value, which ranges from 0 to 1. A higher R-squared value indicates a better fit.

7. Residual Analysis: Residual analysis is used to check the assumptions of linear regression. Residuals are the differences between the observed values and the predicted values. The residuals should be normally distributed and have constant variance.

Linear regression and its inference procedure are essential tools for data analysis and prediction. By understanding the key concepts and techniques involved in linear regression, you can build accurate and reliable models for a wide range of applications.



### Multiple Regression

Multiple regression is a statistical technique used to analyze the relationship between a dependent variable and multiple independent variables. In other words, it helps to determine how the independent variables affect the dependent variable.

#### Formula

The formula for multiple regression is:

Y = b0 + b1X1 + b2X2 + ... + bnxn + ε

where Y is the dependent variable, X1, X2, ..., Xn are the independent variables, b0 is the intercept, b1, b2, ..., bn are the coefficients, and ε is the error term.

#### Assumptions

Before using multiple regression, it is important to check that the following assumptions are met:

1. Linearity: The relationship between the dependent and independent variables should be linear.

2. Independence: The observations should be independent of each other.

3. Homoscedasticity: The variance of the errors should be constant across all levels of the independent variables.

4. Normality: The errors should follow a normal distribution.

#### Interpretation

The coefficients (b1, b2, ..., bn) in the multiple regression equation represent the change in the dependent variable for a unit change in the corresponding independent variable, holding all other independent variables constant. 

The intercept (b0) represents the expected value of the dependent variable when all independent variables are equal to zero.

#### Example

Suppose we want to predict the salary of an employee based on their years of experience, level of education, and age. We can use multiple regression to determine how these three independent variables affect the dependent variable (salary).

The multiple regression equation would be:

Salary = b0 + b1(Experience) + b2(Education) + b3(Age) + ε

We can interpret the coefficients as follows:

- b1: A one-year increase in experience is associated with an increase/decrease of b1 in salary, holding education and age constant.
- b2: A one-level increase in education (e.g., from a bachelor's degree to a master's degree) is associated with an increase/decrease of b2 in salary, holding experience and age constant.

4. **Basic Probability Rules**

   There are three basic probability rules:

   - **Addition Rule:** The addition rule states that the probability of either of two events occurring is the sum of their individual probabilities minus the probability of both events occurring together.
   - **Multiplication Rule:** The multiplication rule states that the probability of two events occurring together is the product of their individual probabilities.
   - **Complement Rule:** The complement rule states that the probability of an event not occurring is 1 minus the probability of the event occurring.

   Conditional probability is the probability of an event occurring given that another event has already occurred. It is calculated using the formula:

   P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B)

   Where P(A|B) is the conditional probability of A given B, P(A and B) is the probability of A and B occurring together, and P(B) is the probability of B occurring.

6. **Bayes' Theorem**

   Bayes' theorem is a formula that calculates the probability of an event occurring based on prior knowledge of related events. It is given by:

   P(A|B) = P(B|A) * P(A) / P(B)

   Where P(A|B) is the conditional probability of A given B, P(B|A) is the conditional probability of B given A, P(A) is the prior probability of A, and P(B) is the prior probability of B.

   - Calculate probabilities of events occurring.
   - Construct probability distributions.
   - Calculate measures of central tendency and dispersion.

where x̄1 and x̄2 are the means of the two samples, s_p is the pooled standard deviation, n1 and n2 are the sample sizes of the two samples.
