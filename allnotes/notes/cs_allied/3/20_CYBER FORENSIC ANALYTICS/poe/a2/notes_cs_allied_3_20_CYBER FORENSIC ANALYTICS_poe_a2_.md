# CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Digital Forensics: Digital forensics is the process of uncovering and interpreting electronic data. It is used to investigate potential cyber crimes by extracting and analyzing data from digital devices like computers, smartphones, servers, etc. Digital forensics experts use tools and techniques to preserve, collect, examine, and analyze digital evidence to reconstruct past events.

2. Types of Digital Evidence: The most common types of digital evidence include:
-   Logs: System, security, and application logs can provide a timeline of events and user activities.
-   Metadata: Metadata provides additional information about files such as creation, modification and access times which can be useful for investigations.
-   Hidden and Temporary Data: Hidden or temporary files, browsing history, etc. may contain evidence of malicious activities.
-   Network Traffic: Network traffic can be analyzed to detect potential attacks and extract key information.
-   Memory: Volatile memory contains runtime data and processes which can be analyzed to understand the system state and activities.

3. Forensic Investigation Process: The key steps in a digital forensic investigation include:
1. Securing and isolating the system: The system is secured and isolated to prevent any changes to the evidence.
2. Acquiring an image: A forensic image of the storage media is acquired to preserve evidence.
3. Analyzing the image: The image is analyzed to uncover and interpret relevant data using various forensic tools and techniques.
4. Reporting: The findings and conclusions are documented in a report and presented in court if required.

4. Challenges: Some of the key challenges in digital forensics include:
-   Large volume of data: The increasing volume of data makes it difficult to analyze everything efficiently.
-   Advanced threats: Advanced and customized malware are harder to detect and analyze.
-   Anti-forensics techniques: Attackers use various techniques to hide their activities and evade forensics.
-   New technologies: Emerging technologies like cryptocurrencies pose new challenges to forensic investigations.
-   Lack of standards: The lack of standardized procedures and tools creates additional challenges.

### ATM frauds

1. Skimming: Fraudsters attach devices to ATM machines to steal card data and PIN. They later create clone cards to withdraw money.
2. Card Trapping: Fraudsters insert devices into ATM card slots to trap cards. They later steal the trapped cards to create clone cards and withdraw money.
3. Shoulder Surfing: Fraudsters observe ATM users entering PINs and later steal cards or card data to create clone cards for fraudulent withdrawal.
4. Distraction Theft: Fraudsters distract ATM users and steal their cards to create clone cards for fraudulent withdrawal. Methods include shocking the user or claiming machine ate the card.
5. Insider Fraud: Bank employees misuse access to conduct fraudulent cash withdrawals or steal customer data/cards to facilitate ATM frauds.

To prevent ATM frauds:

- Use bank ATMs in secure locations with adequate surveillance.
- Cover PIN while entering.
- Report suspicious devices/transactions immediately.
- Ensure cards are returned quickly after transactions.
- Monitor accounts and report unauthorized withdrawals promptly.
- Use chip-based cards and ATMs with advanced security features.
- Banks should deploy strong security controls and monitor employees.

### Digital device for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Hard disk drives (HDDs)
- Made up of platters that store data magnetically
- Read/write heads access data
- Slower than solid-state drives (SSDs) but cheaper per gigabyte
- Live data acquisition for forensics must account for heads moving
- Degaussing/physical destruction needed for secure wipe

2. Solid-state drives (SSDs)
- Use integrated circuits to store data electronically instead of magnetically
- Faster access speeds but more expensive per gigabyte than HDDs
- Live data acquisition for forensics must account for flash translation layer
- Built-in encryption and TRIM commands remove difficulties of secure wipe

3. USB flash drives
- Store data on flash memory chips
- Small, portable, and relatively inexpensive
- Important source of evidence as they can be easily moved between devices
- Challenges for live data acquisition and forensics
- Difficult to fully wipe securely due to wear leveling

4. Cloud storage
- Data stored on remote servers accessed over Internet
- Prevalence reduces reliance on local digital devices
- Legal issues around jurisdiction and law enforcement access
- Volatility of data and metadata requires forensics to act quickly
- Scale of services makes comprehensive forensics impractical

### Hard disk for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Hard disk is a non-volatile storage device that stores digital data on rapidly rotating platters with magnetic surfaces.
2. It is made up of one or more aluminum or glass platters with a magnetic coating, stacked on a spindle within an airtight enclosure.

### Disk characteristics for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

1. Storage capacity - The amount of data that can be stored on a disk. Measured in bytes, kilobytes, megabytes, gigabytes, terabytes, petabytes, exabytes, zettabytes, yottabytes.
2. Access time - The time taken to retrieve data from a storage medium. Measured in milliseconds (ms) and microseconds (μs). SSDs have faster access times than HDDs.
3. Transfer rate - The rate at which data can be transferred to/from a storage medium. Measured in bits per second (bps), kilobits per second (Kbps), megabytes per second (MBps), gigabits per second (Gbps). SSDs have faster transfer rates than HDDs.
4. Seek time - The time taken for the read/write head to move to the required track to retrieve/store data. Only applicable for HDDs. Measured in milliseconds (ms).
5. Rotational latency - The time taken for the correct sector to rotate and align with the read/write head. Only applicable for HDDs. Dependent on the rotational speed of the platter.
6. Read/write reliability - The probability of data being retrieved or stored successfully without any corruption or loss.SSDs have higher read/write reliability than HDDs.

### Data Carving for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Data Carving is a process of recovering files from a disk or device where the normal methods of file system access are not working. This means the files on the media have somehow become corrupted or deleted.
2. Data Carving is done by searching for known headers and footers of files and trying to rebuild full files out of fragmented parts. Things like JPG, PNG and MP3 files all have known headers that Data Carving tools look for.
3. Data Carving is often done when a disk has been formatted or when files have been deleted. Even though the file system says the space is available, the actual file data may still be present on the media until it is overwritten. Data Carving can grab these orphaned file parts and reassemble the files.
4. Some of the tools used for Data Carving are PhotoRec, Scalpel, Foremost, bulk_extractor, etc. These tools scan through the bytes of the disk or image looking for headers and footers of known file types and then recover the full files.
5. Limitations of Data Carving:
    - It can't recover files if the headers or footers are not found.
    - It can lead to false positives where bad data is misidentified as files.
    - File names and paths are lost as Data Carving just recovers the raw files.
    - It requires a lot of processing power and time to scan large disks or images.

6. Data Carving is an important technique for Cyber Forensics analysts to retrieve and recover lost or deleted data from disks and images. It can help recover documents, images, videos, and other files that may contain relevant evidence. ### Steganography for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Steganography refers to the practice of concealing a message within another message or a physical object. In digital steganography, electronic communications may include steganographic content, such as embedding hidden data in images, audio files, or other digital media.
2. Steganography is often confused with cryptography because they are both techniques for protecting data. However, steganography hides the very existence of a message, whereas cryptography transforms a message into an unreadable form but does not hide its existence. Combining steganography and cryptography, known as "steganography cryptography", can provide stronger security.
3. Common image formats for image-based steganography include JPEG, GIF, and PNG. These formats utilize lossy and lossless data compression which introduce patterns in the bit-stream that can be exploited to hide data. Audio files can have data embedded in the least-significant bits of the audio samples or in the frequency domain. In video files, data can be embedded in the least-significant bits of luminance or chrominance components.
4. Steganalysis is the art of detecting hidden messages in steganographic carriers. Steganalysis techniques include statistical analysis, structural analysis, and distortion analysis to detect anomalies and patterns that may indicate the presence of hidden data. Statistical tools can analyze the distribution of the least-significant bits or the distribution of colors. Structural tools analyze the relationships between components of an object, such as the header and the payload of an image file. Distortion tools analyze the difference between the original and steganographed objects to detect changes.

- The relevance and strength of the evidence depends on how closely tied the circumstances are to the alleged facts. Closely related circumstances may have more probative value than loosely related ones.
