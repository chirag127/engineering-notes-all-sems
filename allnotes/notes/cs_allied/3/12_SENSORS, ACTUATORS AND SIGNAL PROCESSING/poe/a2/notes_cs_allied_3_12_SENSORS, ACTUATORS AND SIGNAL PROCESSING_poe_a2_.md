# SENSORS, ACTUATORS AND SIGNAL PROCESSING

1. Sensors: Sensors are devices that detect physical quantities and convert them into signals which can be measured or recorded. They measure physical parameters such as temperature, pressure, flow, level, displacement, humidity, vibration, etc. Some common examples are thermocouples, inductive proximity sensors, photoelectric sensors, etc.

2. Actuators: Actuators are devices that convert energy into motion or mechanical action. For example, motors, pneumatic cylinders, hydraulic cylinders, etc. They are used to control devices and systems, for example, controlling valves, moving robotic arms, etc. Actuators get signals from the controller and convert them into motion.

3. Signal Processing: The signals from sensors are usually weak, noisy, and in an unsuitable form to be used directly by the controller. Signal processing techniques are used to convert these signals into a suitable form. For example, amplification, filtering, rectification, etc. These processed signals are then sent to the controller for appropriate actions. Signal processing allows more accurate measurement and control.

## Unit 2 - Thermal Sensors: Introduction
- Thermal sensors are used to measure temperature and heat flow.
- They work on the principle that the physical properties of materials change with temperature.
- Different types of thermal sensors are gas thermometric sensors, thermal expansion sensors, resistance change sensors, thermoelectric sensors, etc.

## Gas Thermometric Sensors
- They contain gases like hydrogen, helium, nitrogen, etc. in a confined space.
- With increase in temperature, the gas expands and the corresponding pressure or volume change is measured to determine the temperature.
- They have high accuracy but are costly and complex.

## Thermal Expansion Sensors
- They contain two materials with different thermal expansion coefficients bonded together.
- With temperature change, the difference in expansion creates stress which is measured to determine temperature.
- Examples are bimetallic strip, bourdon tube, etc.
- They are inexpensive but have low accuracy.

- Magnetic sensors detect magnetic fields and convert them into electrical signals
- They are used to detect position, proximity, speed, angle, and other parameters
- The most common types are:
    - Magneto-resistive sensors
    - Hall effect sensors

### Sensors and the Principles Behind

- Magneto-resistive sensors: Use change in electrical resistance of materials in a magnetic field
- Hall effect sensors: Use Hall effect - transverse voltage developed across an electrical conductor in a magnetic field
- Inductive sensors: Use change in inductance of a coil in a magnetic field to detect motion/proximity
- SQUID sensors: Use quantum mechanical effects to detect extremely small magnetic fields with high sensitivity

### Electro Analytical Sensors:

- **Introduction:** Electroanalytical sensors detect the concentration of a target analyte by measuring the electrical signal caused by the redox reaction of the analyte. The magnitude of the electrical signal is proportional to the concentration of the analyte. These sensors are widely used for detecting various biological, environmental and industrial analytes.

- **The Electrochemical Cell:** An electrochemical cell consists of two electrodes - working electrode and reference electrode immersed in an electrolyte. The analyte undergoes redox reaction at the working electrode which produces an electrical signal. The reference electrode provides a stable reference potential to measure this electrical signal.

- **The Cell Potential:** The potential difference between the two electrodes of an electrochemical cell is called the cell potential (Ecell). It depends on the redox potentials of the electrodes and the concentration of reactants. The Nernst equation is used to calculate the cell potential at non-standard conditions.

- **Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE):** The standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) is the most common reference electrode which provides a fixed reference potential of 0 V at standard conditions. The potential of SHE depends on the pH of the electrolyte as per the Nernst equation.

- **Liquid Junction and Other Potentials:** The reference electrode contains an internal solution separated from the analyte solution by a porous junction. This liquid junction develops a potential called liquid junction potential. Other potentials like resistance potential and diffusion potential can also affect the measured cell potential. These potentials are accounted for while measuring the actual redox potential of the analyte.

- **Polarization:** The accumulation of reaction products or depletion of reactants at the electrode surface during electrolysis leads to a deviation of the electrode potential from its equilibrium value. This phenomenon is known as polarization and it affects the accuracy of electroanalytical measurements.

## Unit 4 - Actuators: Pneumatic and Hydraulic Actuation Systems

- Actuation systems: Systems that provide motion and force to perform mechanical tasks. Pneumatic and hydraulic systems are actuation systems that use pressurized fluids to generate force and motion.
- Pneumatic systems: Use compressed air to generate force and motion. Air compressors are used to supply high-pressure air to pneumatic cylinders and other pneumatic actuators.
- Hydraulic systems: Use hydraulic oil or fluid to generate force and motion. A hydraulic pump is used to apply pressure to the hydraulic fluid which is then used to power hydraulic cylinders and other hydraulic actuators.
- Directional control valves: Valves used to control the direction of fluid flow in a pneumatic or hydraulic system. They are used to extend and retract cylinders and control other actuators.
- Pressure control valves: Valves used to control the pressure of the working fluid in a pneumatic or hydraulic system. Pressure regulators and relief valves are common types of pressure control valves.
- Cylinders: The most common type of pneumatic and hydraulic actuators. They use the pressure of the working fluid to generate linear motion and force. Single-acting and double-acting cylinders are the two main types.
- Servo and proportional control valves: Valves that can control the flow rate and pressure of the working fluid in a precise and adjustable manner. They enable precise control and positioning of actuators.
- Process control valves: Valves used to control the flow rate and pressure of fluids in industrial processes. They are used to control variables such as temperature, flow, and liquid level.
- Rotary actuators: Actuators that generate rotary motion instead of linear motion. They are used to drive rotary motions such as turning, spinning, and rotating.

### Digital signal representation

1. A digital signal is a physical signal that represents data as a sequence of discrete values; at any given time it can only take on one of a finite number of values.

2. These values are represented by a series of binary numbers in computers. For example, an 8-bit digital signal can represent values from 0 to 255 using binary numbers.

3. The process of converting an analog signal to digital is called analog-to-digital conversion. The device that performs this conversion is called an analog-to-digital converter (ADC).

4. The rate at which samples are taken from a continuous analog signal to produce the digital signal is known as the sampling rate or sampling frequency. According to the sampling theorem, the sampling rate must be at least twice the highest frequency of the analog signal to capture all the necessary information. This is known as the Nyquist rate.

5. Quantization is the process of mapping the sampled analog signal to discrete values in the digital signal. The resolution refers to the number of discrete values used to represent the signal and determines the quantization error. A higher resolution (more discrete values) leads to a lower quantization error.

### Digital Signal Processors: Introduction – Architecture – Features – Addressing Formats – Functional modes – Introduction to Commercial Processors.

1. Introduction
- Digital Signal Processors (DSPs) are microprocessors designed specifically for digital signal processing applications.
- They are optimized for ultra-fast execution of repetitive and numerically intensive tasks associated with processing digitized signals.
- DSPs are widely used in audio, video, and imaging applications including speech recognition, image processing, and mobile communications.

2. Architecture
- Harvard architecture with separate program and data buses.
- Single-instruction, multiple-data (SIMD) or multiple-instruction, multiple-data (MIMD) processing capabilities.
- Specialized memory and external interfaces and special instructions to support efficient processing of digital signals.
- Programmable peripherals for I/O functions.

3. Features
- High-speed processing capabilities provided by features such as pipelining and parallelism.
- Specialized execution units including multipliers, accumulators, shifters, and address generators.
- Single-cycle and multiply–accumulate (MAC) instructions for efficient signal processing.
- Hardware support for circular addressing and bit-reversed addressing for efficient FFT implementation.
