### Network Analysis

Network analysis is a method used to study the relationships between different entities, such as people, organizations, or countries. It is commonly used in the field of social network analysis, which focuses on the study of social structures and the relationships between individuals or groups.

In network analysis, the entities being studied are represented as nodes, and the relationships between them are represented as edges. The resulting network can be visualized as a graph, with the nodes and edges forming a complex web of connections.

There are several key concepts and measures used in network analysis, including:

1. **Degree**: The degree of a node is the number of edges connected to it. In a social network, this could represent the number of friends or connections a person has.

2. **Centrality**: Centrality measures the importance of a node within the network. There are several different measures of centrality, including degree centrality, closeness centrality, and betweenness centrality.

3. **Clustering**: Clustering refers to the tendency of nodes to form groups or communities within the network. This can be measured using the clustering coefficient, which calculates the proportion of a node's neighbors that are also connected to each other.

4. **Paths**: A path is a sequence of edges that connects two nodes in the network. The shortest path between two nodes is known as the geodesic distance, and can be used to measure the closeness or distance between two nodes.

Network analysis can be used for a wide range of applications, including the study of social networks, the analysis of organizational structures, and the identification of key players or influencers within a network. It is a powerful tool for understanding the complex relationships and dynamics within a network.



### Development of Social Network Analysis

Social network analysis (SNA) is a methodology for studying social structures through the use of network and graph theories. Here are some key points in the development of SNA:

1. The origins of SNA can be traced back to the work of sociologist Georg Simmel in the early 20th century, who studied the patterns of social relationships and interactions.

2. In the 1930s, Jacob Moreno developed sociometry, a technique for measuring social relationships, which laid the foundation for SNA.

3. In the 1950s and 1960s, researchers such as John Barnes and Elizabeth Bott applied graph theory to the study of social networks, leading to the development of SNA as a distinct field.

4. In the 1970s, SNA gained popularity with the publication of several influential books, including "Social Network Analysis" by Linton Freeman, "The Social Structure of a National Securities Market" by Harrison White, and "The Strength of Weak Ties" by Mark Granovetter.

5. Since then, SNA has been applied to a wide range of fields, including sociology, anthropology, psychology, economics, and computer science, and has become an important tool for understanding social structures and relationships.

6. With the rise of the internet and social media, SNA has become increasingly relevant, as it provides a powerful tool for analyzing online social networks and the spread of information and influence.

7. Today, SNA is a rapidly growing field, with new techniques and applications being developed to address the challenges of analyzing large and complex social networks.

7. **Clustering Coefficient**: The clustering coefficient is a measure of the extent to which nodes in a network tend to cluster together. It is used to identify tightly-knit groups or communities within a network.
8. **Modularity**: Modularity is a measure of the strength of the division of a network into communities or modules. It is used to identify the optimal division of a network into communities.
9. **Assortativity**: Assortativity is a measure of the tendency of nodes in a network to connect to other nodes that are similar to them in some way, such as having the same degree or belonging to the same community.
10. **Homophily**: Homophily is the tendency of individuals to associate and bond with others who are similar to them in some way, such as having the same race, gender, or socio-economic status.




### The Global Structure of Networks

The global structure of a network refers to the overall organization and properties of the network as a whole. In the context of social network analysis, the global structure of a network can provide insights into the relationships and interactions between individuals or groups within the network.

Some key concepts and measures used to describe the global structure of networks include:

1. **Density**: This measures the proportion of possible connections in a network that are actually present. A high density indicates that the network is highly interconnected, while a low density indicates that the network is sparsely connected.

2. **Clustering coefficient**: This measures the extent to which the nodes in a network tend to cluster together. A high clustering coefficient indicates that the network has a high degree of local clustering, while a low clustering coefficient indicates that the network is more dispersed.

3. **Degree distribution**: This measures the distribution of the number of connections that each node in the network has. The degree distribution can provide insights into the heterogeneity of the network and the presence of highly connected nodes or hubs.

4. **Path length**: This measures the average shortest path length between all pairs of nodes in the network. A short average path length indicates that the network is highly interconnected and information can spread quickly, while a long average path length indicates that the network is more dispersed and information may take longer to spread.

5. **Modularity**: This measures the extent to which the network can be divided into distinct communities or modules. A high modularity indicates that the network has a strong community structure, while a low modularity indicates that the network is more homogeneous.

These are some of the key concepts and measures used to describe the global structure of networks in social network analysis. Understanding the global structure of a network can provide valuable insights into the relationships and interactions between individuals or groups within the network.

3. Social network analysis in general studies the behavior of the individual at the micro level, the pattern of relationships (network structure) at the macro level, and the interactions between the two. Social networks are both the cause of and the result of individual behavior .
4. A social network is a social structure made up of a set of social actors (such as individuals or organizations), sets of dyadic ties, and other social interactions between actors. The social network perspective provides a set of methods for analyzing the structure of whole social entities as well as a variety of theories explaining the patterns .
5. In order to reveal the deep social structure, the structure of social networks can be analyzed from three levels: microlevel, mesolevel, and macrolevel .

### Comparison to the Entity/Relationship (E/R) model and the relational model

The Entity/Relationship (E/R) model and the relational model are two common data modeling techniques used in the design of databases. Both models are used to represent the structure of data in a way that is easy to understand and manipulate.

1. The E/R model represents data as entities and relationships. Entities are objects or concepts that can be identified in the real world, such as a person or a product. Relationships represent the associations between entities, such as a person owning a product.

2. The relational model represents data as tables, with each row representing an instance of an entity and each column representing an attribute of that entity. Relationships between entities are represented by foreign keys, which are attributes that reference the primary key of another table.

3. One key difference between the two models is the level of abstraction. The E/R model is a higher-level, conceptual model that is used to represent the overall structure of the data, while the relational model is a lower-level, logical model that is used to represent the data in a way that can be easily manipulated by a database management system.

4. Another difference is the way relationships are represented. In the E/R model, relationships are represented as separate entities, while in the relational model, relationships are represented by foreign keys.

5. Both models have their strengths and weaknesses, and the choice between the two often depends on the specific needs of the application. The E/R model is often used in the early stages of database design, while the relational model is used in the implementation of the database.

6. In the context of web semantics in social network applications, both models can be used to represent the structure of data. However, the E/R model may be more suitable for representing complex relationships between entities, while the relational model may be more suitable for representing large amounts of data in a way that can be easily queried and manipulated.

### Descriptive Analysis for the Notes of Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the Subject of Social Network Analytics

Descriptive analysis is a method used to summarize and describe the main features of a dataset. In the context of social network analysis, descriptive analysis can be used to evaluate the characteristics of a social network. Here are some key points to consider when conducting a descriptive analysis of a social network:

1. **Network size**: The size of the network can be measured by the number of nodes (actors) and the number of edges (relationships) in the network.

2. **Network density**: Network density is a measure of how connected the nodes in a network are. It is calculated by dividing the number of actual edges in the network by the number of possible edges.

3. **Degree centrality**: Degree centrality is a measure of the importance of a node in a network. It is calculated by counting the number of edges connected to a node.

4. **Betweenness centrality**: Betweenness centrality is a measure of the extent to which a node acts as a bridge between other nodes in the network. It is calculated by counting the number of shortest paths between pairs of nodes that pass through the node of interest.

5. **Closeness centrality**: Closeness centrality is a measure of how close a node is to all other nodes in the network. It is calculated by taking the inverse of the average shortest path length from the node of interest to all other nodes in the network.

6. **Clustering coefficient**: The clustering coefficient is a measure of the extent to which the nodes in a network tend to cluster together. It is calculated by counting the number of closed triangles in the network and dividing by the number of possible triangles.

These are some of the key metrics that can be used in a descriptive analysis of a social network. By evaluating these metrics, one can gain a better understanding of the structure and characteristics of the network. This information can be useful for identifying patterns and trends, and for making informed decisions about how to interact with the network.
