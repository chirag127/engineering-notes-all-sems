

# INTRODUCTION TO INTERNET OF THINGS

- The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of physical devices, vehicles, buildings, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and network connectivity, which enables these objects to collect and exchange data.
- IoT allows objects to be sensed and controlled remotely across existing network infrastructure, creating opportunities for more direct integration of the physical world into computer-based systems, and resulting in improved efficiency, accuracy, and economic benefit.
- IoT is expected to offer advanced connectivity of devices, systems, and services that goes beyond machine-to-machine (M2M) communications and covers a variety of protocols, domains, and applications.
- The interconnection of these embedded devices (including smart objects), is expected to usher in automation in nearly all fields, while also enabling advanced applications like a smart grid, and expanding to areas such as smart cities.
- IoT is one of the platforms of today's Smart City, and Smart Energy Management Systems.
- IoT involves extending Internet connectivity beyond standard devices, such as desktops, laptops, smartphones, and tablets, to any range of traditionally dumb or non-internet-enabled physical devices and everyday objects.
- Embedded with technology, these devices can communicate and interact over the Internet, and they can be remotely monitored and controlled.
- IoT has evolved from the convergence of wireless technologies, micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS), microservices, and the internet.
- The convergence has helped tear down the silos between operational technology (OT) and information technology (IT), enabling unstructured machine-generated data to be analyzed for insights that drive improvements.
- IoT is a transformative technology that has the potential to change the way we live and work. It is already having a significant impact on industries such as healthcare, manufacturing, transportation, and energy, and its potential is only just beginning to be realized.

### Vision
The vision of the Internet of Things (IoT) is to create a world where everyday objects are connected to the internet, allowing them to collect and share data, and interact with each other to improve our lives.

### Definition
The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the network of physical objects, or "things," that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies to connect and exchange data with other devices and systems over the internet.

### Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components:
- **Devices**: Physical objects that are connected to the internet and can collect and share data.
- **Sensors**: Devices that detect and measure physical properties and convert them into digital data.
- **Connectivity**: The means by which data is transmitted between devices and systems.
- **Data Processing**: The analysis and manipulation of data to extract useful information.
- **User Interface**: The means by which users interact with IoT devices and systems.

The architectural view of IoT includes the following layers:
- **Perception Layer**: This layer is responsible for collecting data from the physical world through sensors and other devices.
- **Network Layer**: This layer is responsible for transmitting data between devices and systems.
- **Middleware Layer**: This layer is responsible for data processing and storage.
- **Application Layer**: This layer is responsible for providing services and applications to users.

The technology behind IoT includes sensors, connectivity, data processing, and user interfaces. Some of the key technologies used in IoT include wireless communication, cloud computing, big data analytics, and machine learning.

### Technology behind IoT

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of interconnected devices that can communicate with each other and with the internet. The technology behind IoT includes:

### IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization

IoT/M2M systems consist of several layers, each with its own set of design standards. These layers include:

1. **Device Layer:** This layer consists of the physical devices and sensors that collect data. Design standards for this layer focus on ensuring that devices are reliable, secure, and easy to use.

2. **Connectivity Layer:** This layer is responsible for transmitting data from the device layer to the network layer. Design standards for this layer focus on ensuring that data is transmitted securely and reliably.

3. **Network Layer:** This layer is responsible for routing data from the connectivity layer to the application layer. Design standards for this layer focus on ensuring that data is routed efficiently and securely.

4. **Application Layer:** This layer is responsible for processing and analyzing data from the network layer. Design standards for this layer focus on ensuring that data is processed accurately and that the resulting insights are actionable.

Design standardization is important for IoT/M2M systems because it ensures that all components of the system work together seamlessly. Standardization also makes it easier to design and develop new IoT/M2M systems, as developers can rely on established standards when building their systems. ### Data Enrichment and Consolidation

Data enrichment refers to the process of enhancing, refining, or improving raw data. This is achieved by adding information to the data set from external sources, such as public records or third-party data providers. The goal of data enrichment is to increase the quality and value of the data, making it more useful for analysis and decision-making.

Data consolidation, on the other hand, refers to the process of combining data from multiple sources into a single, comprehensive view. This is often done to improve data accuracy, eliminate redundancies, and provide a more complete picture of the information. Data consolidation can be achieved through various methods, such as data integration, data warehousing, and data virtualization.

In the context of IoT, data enrichment and consolidation are important for several reasons. First, IoT devices generate large amounts of data, which can be difficult to manage and analyze without proper enrichment and consolidation. Second, IoT data is often generated by multiple devices and systems, which can result in inconsistencies and inaccuracies. By enriching and consolidating the data, it is possible to improve its quality and reliability, making it more useful for decision-making.

Some key points to remember about data enrichment and consolidation in IoT are:

- Data enrichment and consolidation can improve the quality and value of IoT data.
- Data enrichment involves adding information to the data set from external sources.
- Data consolidation involves combining data from multiple sources into a single, comprehensive view.
- Data enrichment and consolidation are important for managing and analyzing large amounts of IoT data.
- Data enrichment and consolidation can improve the accuracy and reliability of IoT data.

### Sensors

Sensors are devices that detect and respond to changes in the environment. They are used to measure physical quantities such as temperature, pressure, humidity, and light, and convert them into electrical signals that can be processed by a computer.

### Wireless Medium Access Issues

Wireless medium access control (MAC) is a crucial component of wireless communication. It is responsible for managing access to the shared wireless medium, ensuring that multiple devices can communicate with each other without interference. Some of the issues that arise in wireless medium access include:

1. **Interference:** Interference occurs when multiple devices transmit at the same time, causing their signals to overlap and interfere with each other. This can result in lost or corrupted data, and can significantly reduce the overall performance of the network.

2. **Hidden Node Problem:** The hidden node problem occurs when two devices are within range of a common receiver, but are not within range of each other. This can result in collisions, where both devices transmit at the same time, causing interference at the receiver.

3. **Exposed Node Problem:** The exposed node problem occurs when a device is prevented from transmitting because another device, which is outside of its range, is transmitting. This can result in reduced network performance, as the device is unable to transmit even though it would not cause interference.

4. **Fairness:** Ensuring fair access to the wireless medium can be challenging, particularly in networks with a large number of devices. Some devices may be able to transmit more frequently than others, resulting in unfairness and reduced performance for some devices.

To address these issues, various MAC protocols have been developed. These protocols use different techniques to manage access to the wireless medium, and to ensure that all devices can communicate effectively. A survey of MAC protocols is provided in the next section.



### MAC Protocol Survey

The Medium Access Control (MAC) protocol is a sublayer of the Data Link Layer in the OSI model of computer networking. The MAC protocol is responsible for controlling how devices in a network gain access to a shared medium and transmit data.

In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), wireless medium access issues arise due to the large number of devices that need to communicate with each other. A survey of MAC protocols for IoT can help identify the most suitable protocol for a given application.

Some common MAC protocols used in IoT include:

1. **TDMA (Time Division Multiple Access):** This protocol divides the available time into time slots and assigns each slot to a different device. This ensures that only one device transmits at a time, avoiding collisions.

2. **FDMA (Frequency Division Multiple Access):** This protocol divides the available frequency spectrum into channels and assigns each channel to a different device. This ensures that multiple devices can transmit simultaneously without interfering with each other.

3. **CDMA (Code Division Multiple Access):** This protocol assigns a unique code to each device, allowing multiple devices to transmit simultaneously on the same frequency. The receiver can then use the code to separate the signals from different devices.

4. **CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance):** This protocol is commonly used in wireless networks. Before transmitting, a device checks if the medium is free. If it is, the device transmits. If the medium is busy, the device waits for a random period before trying again.

In addition to MAC protocols, other aspects of network and communication in IoT include survey routing protocols, sensor deployment and node discovery, and data aggregation and dissemination. These topics are important for ensuring efficient and reliable communication in IoT networks.



### Survey Routing Protocols

In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), routing protocols are responsible for finding and maintaining routes between sensor nodes and the base station. These protocols are designed to be energy-efficient, scalable, and robust to ensure reliable communication in large-scale IoT networks.

There are several types of routing protocols that can be used in IoT networks, including:

1. **Flat Routing Protocols:** In flat routing protocols, all nodes have the same role and responsibility. These protocols are typically used in small-scale networks where the number of nodes is limited. Examples of flat routing protocols include flooding and gossiping.

2. **Hierarchical Routing Protocols:** In hierarchical routing protocols, nodes are organized into clusters, with each cluster having a cluster head responsible for routing data to the base station. These protocols are more scalable and energy-efficient than flat routing protocols and are commonly used in large-scale IoT networks. Examples of hierarchical routing protocols include LEACH and PEGASIS.

3. **Location-based Routing Protocols:** In location-based routing protocols, the position of the nodes is used to make routing decisions. These protocols are typically used in mobile IoT networks where the nodes are constantly moving. Examples of location-based routing protocols include GPSR and GEAR.

4. **Data-centric Routing Protocols:** In data-centric routing protocols, the routing decisions are based on the data being transmitted. These protocols are typically used in IoT networks where the data being transmitted is more important than the identity of the nodes. Examples of data-centric routing protocols include SPIN and Directed Diffusion.

Each of these routing protocols has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of routing protocol depends on the specific requirements of the IoT network. It is important to carefully evaluate the different routing protocols to determine which one is the best fit for the network.
