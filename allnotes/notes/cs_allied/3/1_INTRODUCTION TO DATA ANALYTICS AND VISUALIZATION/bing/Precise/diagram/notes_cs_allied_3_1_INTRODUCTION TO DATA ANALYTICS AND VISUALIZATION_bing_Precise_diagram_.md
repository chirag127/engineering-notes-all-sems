### Sources and nature of data

Data is a collection of facts, such as numbers, words, measurements, observations or even just descriptions of things. Data can come from a variety of sources, including:

1. **Surveys**: Data can be collected through surveys, where individuals or organizations are asked to provide information about themselves or their opinions on a particular topic.

2. **Experiments**: Data can also be collected through experiments, where researchers manipulate one or more variables to observe the effect on an outcome.

3. **Observational studies**: Data can be collected through observational studies, where researchers observe and record the behavior or characteristics of individuals or groups without manipulating any variables.

4. **Administrative data**: Data can also be obtained from administrative sources, such as government records, medical records, or financial records.

5. **Big data**: With the rise of technology, large amounts of data are being generated and collected from sources such as social media, online transactions, and sensor networks. This type of data is often referred to as big data.

The nature of data can vary depending on the source and the context in which it is collected. Data can be quantitative or qualitative, continuous or discrete, and can be measured at different levels of measurement, such as nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio.

It is important to carefully consider the sources and nature of data when conducting data analysis, as the quality and reliability of the data can have a significant impact on the results of the analysis.



### Classification of Data

Classification of data is the process of organizing data into categories or groups based on their characteristics. This is done to make it easier to understand, analyze, and interpret the data. There are several ways to classify data, including:

1. **Quantitative and Qualitative Data:** Quantitative data is numerical and can be measured, while qualitative data is non-numerical and is based on observations and descriptions.

2. **Discrete and Continuous Data:** Discrete data can only take on certain values, while continuous data can take on any value within a range.

3. **Nominal, Ordinal, Interval, and Ratio Data:** Nominal data is data that can be placed into categories, but the categories have no order. Ordinal data is data that can be placed into categories that have a specific order. Interval data is data that has a consistent scale, but no true zero point. Ratio data is data that has a consistent scale and a true zero point.

4. **Cross-sectional and Time-series Data:** Cross-sectional data is data collected at a single point in time, while time-series data is data collected over a period of time.

These are some of the ways data can be classified in the field of data analytics and visualization. Understanding the classification of data is important for selecting the appropriate methods for analyzing and visualizing the data.



### Characteristics of Data

Data is a collection of facts, figures, and statistics related to an object. It can be processed to create information. Here are some characteristics of data:

1. **Accuracy**: Data should be accurate and free from errors. It should represent the true value of the object it is representing.

2. **Relevance**: Data should be relevant to the purpose for which it is being used. Irrelevant data can lead to incorrect conclusions.

3. **Completeness**: Data should be complete and should not be missing any important information. Incomplete data can lead to incorrect conclusions.

4. **Timeliness**: Data should be up-to-date and should reflect the current state of the object it is representing.

5. **Consistency**: Data should be consistent and should not contain any contradictions. Inconsistent data can lead to incorrect conclusions.

6. **Accessibility**: Data should be easily accessible to the people who need it. Inaccessible data is of no use.

7. **Understandability**: Data should be presented in a way that is easy to understand. Complex data can be difficult to interpret.

## Unit 2 - Data Analytics Lifecycle

The Data Analytics Lifecycle is a framework for conducting data analysis projects. It consists of several phases, each with its own set of tasks and objectives. The phases of the Data Analytics Lifecycle are:

1. **Discovery**: In this phase, the analyst works with the business stakeholders to understand the business problem and determine the objectives of the analysis. This includes identifying the data sources and the relevant data elements.

2. **Data Preparation**: In this phase, the analyst cleans, transforms, and integrates the data from various sources. This includes dealing with missing or incomplete data, removing outliers, and normalizing the data.

3. **Model Planning**: In this phase, the analyst selects the appropriate modeling techniques and prepares the data for modeling. This includes selecting the variables, splitting the data into training and validation sets, and selecting the evaluation metrics.

4. **Model Building**: In this phase, the analyst builds and tests the model using the selected techniques and the prepared data. This includes training the model, evaluating its performance, and fine-tuning the model parameters.

5. **Communicating Results**: In this phase, the analyst communicates the results of the analysis to the business stakeholders. This includes presenting the findings, explaining the model, and making recommendations.

6. **Operationalizing**: In this phase, the analyst works with the IT team to deploy the model in a production environment. This includes integrating the model into the business processes and setting up the necessary infrastructure.

The Data Analytics Lifecycle provides a structured approach to conducting data analysis projects. By following this framework, analysts can ensure that their projects are aligned with the business objectives and that the results are actionable.

### Regression Modeling

Regression modeling is a statistical technique used to analyze the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables. It is commonly used in data analysis to make predictions and to understand the relationship between variables.

There are several types of regression models, including:

1. **Linear Regression:** This model assumes a linear relationship between the dependent and independent variables. It is used to predict the value of the dependent variable based on the values of the independent variables.

2. **Logistic Regression:** This model is used when the dependent variable is binary. It predicts the probability of the dependent variable being 1 or 0 based on the values of the independent variables.

3. **Polynomial Regression:** This model is used when the relationship between the dependent and independent variables is non-linear. It fits a polynomial equation to the data.

4. **Multiple Regression:** This model is used when there are multiple independent variables. It predicts the value of the dependent variable based on the values of all the independent variables.

Regression modeling is an important tool in data analysis and can provide valuable insights into the relationships between variables. It is important to choose the appropriate type of regression model for the data being analyzed and to carefully interpret the results.



### Multivariate Analysis

Multivariate analysis is a statistical technique used to analyze data that involves more than one variable. It is used to understand the relationship between multiple variables and their impact on a particular outcome. Some common techniques used in multivariate analysis include:

1. **Multiple Regression:** This technique is used to predict the value of one variable based on the values of multiple other variables. It is commonly used in forecasting and prediction.

2. **Factor Analysis:** This technique is used to identify underlying factors or dimensions that explain the correlations among a set of variables. It is commonly used in psychology and social sciences to identify latent constructs.

3. **Discriminant Analysis:** This technique is used to classify observations into two or more groups based on a set of predictor variables. It is commonly used in medical diagnosis and credit scoring.

4. **Cluster Analysis:** This technique is used to group observations into clusters based on their similarity. It is commonly used in market segmentation and image analysis.

5. **Canonical Correlation Analysis:** This technique is used to understand the relationship between two sets of variables. It is commonly used in psychology and social sciences to study the relationship between two sets of constructs.

These are some of the common techniques used in multivariate analysis. It is important to choose the appropriate technique based on the research question and the nature of the data.



### Bayesian Modeling

Bayesian modeling is a statistical approach that is based on the Bayes' theorem. It is used to update the probability of a hypothesis as more evidence or information becomes available. Bayesian modeling is used in various fields, including data analysis, to make predictions and decisions based on uncertain or incomplete information.

Some key points to remember about Bayesian modeling are:

1. Bayesian modeling is based on the Bayes' theorem, which describes the probability of an event based on prior knowledge of conditions that might be related to the event.

3. **MinCount**: This algorithm uses a data structure called a MinHash sketch to estimate the number of distinct elements in a stream. The MinHash sketch is an array of hash values, where each hash value is the minimum hash value of all the elements in the stream that have been processed so far. The final estimate of the number of distinct elements is calculated based on the number of distinct hash values in the MinHash sketch.

These are just a few of the algorithms that can be used to solve the count-distinct problem. ### Apriori Algorithm

The Apriori algorithm is an algorithm for frequent itemset mining and association rule learning over transactional databases. It is used to find frequent itemsets in a large dataset and then generate association rules between the items. The algorithm was proposed by Rakesh Agrawal and Ramakrishnan Srikant in 1994.

The key idea behind the Apriori algorithm is the Apriori property, which states that all non-empty subsets of a frequent itemset must also be frequent. This property is used to reduce the number of candidate itemsets that need to be considered.

The Apriori algorithm works in an iterative manner. In the first iteration, it generates all the frequent itemsets of size 1. In the second iteration, it generates all the frequent itemsets of size 2, and so on. The algorithm terminates when no more frequent itemsets can be generated.

The steps of the Apriori algorithm are as follows:

1. Generate all the frequent itemsets of size 1.
2. Generate all the candidate itemsets of size k+1 from the frequent itemsets of size k.
3. Prune the candidate itemsets that do not satisfy the Apriori property.
4. Count the support of the remaining candidate itemsets and retain only the frequent itemsets.
5. Repeat steps 2-4 until no more frequent itemsets can be generated.

The Apriori algorithm is widely used in market basket analysis, where it is used to find associations between items that are frequently purchased together. It can also be used in other domains, such as bioinformatics and text mining.

# Clustering Techniques

Clustering is a technique used in data analysis to group similar data points together. Clustering is used in many fields, including marketing, biology, and social sciences.

There are several clustering techniques, including:

1. **K-means clustering:** This is a popular clustering technique that partitions the data into k clusters, where k is a user-defined parameter. The algorithm iteratively assigns each data point to the cluster with the nearest mean, and then updates the cluster means based on the new assignments.

2. **Hierarchical clustering:** This technique builds a hierarchy of clusters by either merging smaller clusters into larger ones (agglomerative clustering) or by splitting larger clusters into smaller ones (divisive clustering). These are just a few of the many clustering techniques available. Each technique has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of technique depends on the specific characteristics of the data and the goals of the analysis.



### Hierarchical Clustering

Hierarchical clustering is a method of cluster analysis which seeks to build a hierarchy of clusters. It is commonly used in the field of data analytics and visualization as a means of identifying patterns and relationships in large datasets.

There are two main approaches to hierarchical clustering: agglomerative and divisive.

1. **Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering**: This approach starts with each data point as a separate cluster and then iteratively merges the closest pairs of clusters until all clusters have been merged into a single cluster. The result is a tree-like structure called a dendrogram, which shows the order in which clusters were merged.

2. **Divisive Hierarchical Clustering**: This approach starts with all data points in a single cluster and then iteratively splits the cluster into smaller clusters until each data point is in its own cluster. The result is also a dendrogram, but the tree is built from the top down rather than from the bottom up.

In both approaches, the distance between clusters is typically measured using a linkage criterion, such as single linkage, complete linkage, or average linkage.

Hierarchical clustering is often used in exploratory data analysis to identify patterns and relationships in the data. It can also be used as a preprocessing step for other clustering algorithms, such as k-means clustering.

When clustering in non-Euclidean spaces, it is important to choose an appropriate distance measure that accurately captures the similarity between data points. This can help to ensure that the resulting clusters are meaningful and representative of the underlying data.

1. Visual objects are the graphical representations of data that are used to convey information to the viewer. These can include charts, graphs, diagrams, and other visual aids.
2. Data objects, on the other hand, are the underlying data structures that store and organize the data used to create visual objects. These can include tables, arrays, and other data structures.
3. The relationship between visual objects and data objects is important in data analytics and visualization. Visual objects are used to present data in a way that is easy for the viewer to understand, while data objects provide the structure and organization necessary to support the creation of visual objects.
4. In order to create effective visualizations, it is important to understand the relationship between visual objects and data objects, and to choose the appropriate data structures and visual representations to convey the desired information.



### Unit 7 - Human Vision: Space Perception and Data in Space

1. Space perception refers to the ability to perceive the spatial relationships between objects in the environment.
2. In the context of data visualization, space perception is important for accurately interpreting the spatial relationships between data points in a visual representation.
3. Data in space refers to the representation of data in a three-dimensional space, often using techniques such as scatter plots or surface plots.
4. The use of three-dimensional space can provide additional information and context to the data, but can also introduce challenges in terms of accurately perceiving the spatial relationships between data points.
5. Techniques such as perspective projection and shading can be used to enhance the perception of depth and spatial relationships in three-dimensional data visualizations.
6. It is important to carefully consider the use of three-dimensional space in data visualization, as it can both enhance and hinder the interpretation of the data.
