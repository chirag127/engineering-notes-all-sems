# History of Wearable Computing

- Wearable computing is the field of technology that involves devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, glasses, headsets, earbuds, rings, etc.
- Wearable computing has a long and diverse history, dating back to ancient times when people used various tools and accessories to enhance their abilities or communicate information.
- Some examples of early wearable computing devices are:

  - The abacus necklace and ring, which were used for calculation and counting by the Chinese and the Romans respectively.
  - The Nuremberg eggs, which were the first portable timepieces invented in the 16th century.
  - The covert timing devices hidden in shoes by Edward Thorp and Claude Shannon, which were used to cheat at roulette in the 1960s and 1970s.

- The modern era of wearable computing began in the late 1970s and early 1980s, when researchers and hobbyists started to experiment with wearable computers that could perform various functions, such as:

  - Capturing and displaying images, video, and audio, as done by Steve Mann, who is considered the father of wearable computing .
  - Providing navigation and location information, as done by the Trekker system developed by MIT in 1998.
  - Monitoring health and fitness, as done by the Fitbit and Nike+ devices launched in 2009 and 2006 respectively.

- The current trends and challenges in wearable computing include:

  - Developing more advanced and immersive interfaces, such as the AiRScouter and Zypad devices that use head-mounted displays and gesture recognition.
  - Improving the battery life, connectivity, and security of wearable devices, as well as addressing the privacy and ethical issues that arise from their use.
  - Integrating wearable devices with other technologies, such as the Internet of Things, artificial intelligence, and mixed reality, to create new applications and experiences.

## PIC for Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything

- PIC stands for **Problem, Idea and Contribution**. It is a framework for writing research papers, proposals and presentations that clearly communicate the main aspects of a research project.
- The PIC framework can be applied to the fields of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything, which are emerging and interdisciplinary domains that involve the integration of sensors, computing, communication, visualization and interaction technologies.

- The PIC framework can help researchers in these fields to identify and articulate the problems they want to solve, the ideas they want to propose, and the contributions they want to make to the existing knowledge and practice.
- A PIC statement consists of three sentences, each addressing one of the components of the framework. The first sentence states the problem, the second sentence states the idea, and the third sentence states the contribution. For example:

> **Problem**: Existing wearable devices are limited by their battery life and require frequent charging, which reduces their usability and convenience for the user.
> **Idea**: We propose a novel wearable device that harvests energy from the user's body heat and motion, and uses it to power the device and its functions.
> **Contribution**: We design and implement a prototype of the proposed device, and evaluate its performance, energy efficiency and user satisfaction in various scenarios.

- The PIC framework can help researchers to structure their papers, proposals and presentations in a clear and concise way, and to highlight the novelty and significance of their work. It can also help reviewers and audiences to quickly understand the main points and contributions of a research project.



### Arduino

Arduino is an open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software. Arduino boards are able to read inputs - light on a sensor, a finger on a button, or a Twitter message - and turn it into an output - activating a motor, turning on an LED, publishing something online.

Some key features of Arduino are:

- Arduino consists of both a physical programmable circuit board (often referred to as a microcontroller) and a piece of software, or IDE (Integrated Development Environment) that runs on your computer, used to write and upload computer code to the physical board .
- Arduino is based on the AVR microcontroller family, which is a type of 8-bit RISC (Reduced Instruction Set Computer) architecture. Arduino boards use either the ATmega328 or the ATmega2560 microcontrollers, which have 32 KB and 256 KB of flash memory respectively.
- Arduino boards can communicate with other devices using digital and analog input/output pins, serial communication protocols (UART, SPI, I2C), USB, Ethernet, Bluetooth, and Wi-Fi .
- Arduino boards can be powered by a USB cable, a battery, or an external power supply. The voltage regulator on the board ensures that the microcontroller receives a constant 5V or 3.3V, depending on the model .
- Arduino boards can be extended with additional hardware modules called shields, which plug into the board and provide additional functionality, such as sensors, displays, motors, etc .
- Arduino software is written in C or C++, and uses a simplified version of the Arduino API (Application Programming Interface), which provides functions for controlling the hardware and communicating with other devices .
- Arduino software can be downloaded from the official website (https://www.arduino.cc/en/software) and installed on Windows, Mac OS, or Linux operating systems. The Arduino IDE provides a text editor, a compiler, a serial monitor, and a code library manager .
- Arduino software can be uploaded to the board using a USB cable, or wirelessly using Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. The Arduino bootloader, which is a small program that runs on the board, allows the software to be transferred without the need for a separate programmer .
- Arduino is an open-source project, which means that anyone can contribute to its development, documentation, and community. Arduino is also compatible with many other open-source platforms, such as Processing, Python, Scratch, etc .

Arduino is a tool for students, hobbyists, artists, designers, and professionals to create devices that interact with their environment using sensors and actuators. Arduino can be used for a variety of projects, such as robotics, wearable computing, mixed reality, internet of everything, etc .

### Iterative coding methodology

- Iterative coding methodology is a way of developing a product or service in phases, with each phase building on the previous one.
- The basic idea behind it is that development takes place through repeated cycles (iterations) and in small parts at a time.
- The iterative methodology is commonly used in software development but is often applied in other fields.
- The key advantage of this method is that you can test your ideas early and often. This means less wasted time, money and resources later on when it comes to implementing your product or service.
- The iterative methodology also allows you to adapt to changing requirements and feedback from users or stakeholders.
- The iterative methodology consists of four main phases: planning, design, implementation and evaluation.
- Planning: This phase involves defining the scope, objectives and deliverables of the project. It also involves identifying the risks, assumptions and constraints that may affect the project.
- Design: This phase involves creating a prototype or a mock-up of the product or service based on the requirements and specifications. It also involves testing the prototype with users or stakeholders and collecting feedback.
- Implementation: This phase involves developing the actual product or service based on the prototype and the feedback. It also involves testing the product or service with users or stakeholders and collecting feedback.
- Evaluation: This phase involves assessing the quality, functionality and usability of the product or service. It also involves measuring the outcomes and impacts of the project and identifying the lessons learned and the best practices.
- The iterative methodology can be repeated as many times as needed until the product or service meets the expectations and the goals of the project.
- Iterative coding is a process where you code a small piece of your project and then test it, then code the next piece and test it, so on and so forth.
- Iterative coding helps you to avoid errors, bugs and inconsistencies in your code. It also helps you to improve your code quality and readability.
- Iterative coding also helps you to learn from your mistakes and to refine your skills and knowledge as a coder.

### Understanding Processing Code Structure

Processing is a programming language and environment for creating interactive graphics, animations, and games. It is based on Java, but has a simplified syntax and a built-in graphics library. Processing code consists of two main parts, setup and draw blocks .

- The setup block runs once when the code gets executed, and is used to initialize variables, load images, set the size of the window, and other configurations.
- The draw block runs continuously, and is used to update the display, handle user input, and perform calculations. The main idea behind Processing is, what you write within the draw block will be executed 60 times per second from top to bottom, until your program terminates.

Here is an example of a simple Processing code that draws a circle that follows the mouse:

```java
// This is a comment
void setup() {
  // Set the size of the window
  size(400, 400);
}

void draw() {
  // Set the background color to black
  background(0);
  // Set the fill color to white
  fill(255);
  // Draw a circle at the mouse position with a radius of 50
  circle(mouseX, mouseY, 50);
}
```

Some important points to note about the Processing code structure are:

- Processing code is case-sensitive, meaning that upper and lower case letters are different. For example, size and Size are not the same.
- Processing code uses semicolons (;) to end each statement. Forgetting a semicolon can cause errors or unexpected results.
- Processing code uses curly braces ({ and }) to enclose blocks of code. For example, the setup and draw blocks are enclosed by curly braces. The opening and closing braces should match and be aligned properly.
- Processing code uses parentheses (() and ()) to enclose parameters or arguments for functions. For example, size(400, 400) is a function that takes two parameters, the width and height of the window.
- Processing code uses slashes (//) to indicate comments. Comments are ignored by the computer, but are useful for humans to explain or document the code. Comments can be single-line or multi-line. Multi-line comments start with /* and end with */.

### Variables and Flow Control

Variables are named containers that store data or values that can change during the execution of a program. Variables have a name, a type, and a value. For example, a variable named `age` of type `int` can store an integer value like `25`.

Flow control is the process of determining the order of execution of statements or instructions in a program based on certain conditions or parameters. Flow control structures are blocks of code that can alter the flow of control in a program. There are three basic types of flow control structures:

- Sequence: The statements are executed in the order they appear in the program. This is the default flow of control in most programming languages. For example, the following statements are executed sequentially:

```
int x = 10; // declare and assign a variable
x = x + 5; // update the variable
print(x); // print the variable
```

- Selection: The statements are executed based on a condition or a choice. This allows the program to branch into different paths of execution depending on the outcome of the condition. For example, the following statements are executed conditionally:

```
int x = 10; // declare and assign a variable
if (x > 0) { // check a condition
  print("x is positive"); // execute if the condition is true
} else {
  print("x is negative or zero"); // execute if the condition is false
}
```

- Iteration: The statements are executed repeatedly until a condition is met or a break is encountered. This allows the program to loop over a set of instructions for a certain number of times or until a certain criterion is satisfied. For example, the following statements are executed iteratively:

```
int x = 10; // declare and assign a variable
while (x > 0) { // check a condition
  print(x); // execute while the condition is true
  x = x - 1; // update the variable
}
```

Flow control structures can be nested or combined to create more complex logic and functionality in a program. For example, the following statements use nested selection and iteration structures:

```
int x = 10; // declare and assign a variable
for (int i = 0; i < 5; i++) { // loop for 5 times
  if (x % 2 == 0) { // check if x is even
    print("x is even"); // execute if x is even
  } else {
    print("x is odd"); // execute if x is odd
  }
  x = x + 1; // update x
}
```

# Digital vs. Analog circuits

- Analog circuits operate on analog signals, which are continuous valued signals that vary smoothly over time and can have any magnitude within a certain range .
- Digital circuits operate on digital signals, which are discrete valued signals that exist at only two levels, usually called zeros and ones, and can change abruptly at fixed intervals .
- The design of an analog circuit is difficult, since every component must be positioned by hand for designing the circuits, and the performance of the circuit depends on the accuracy and tolerance of the components, the temperature, the noise, and the interference from other sources .

- Analog circuits are more suitable for applications that require high precision, high bandwidth, and low power consumption, such as audio and video processing, sensors, and transducers .
- Digital circuits are more suitable for applications that require high speed, high flexibility, and high integration, such as computers, communication, and encryption .
- Analog circuits and digital circuits can be combined to form mixed-signal circuits, which can perform both analog and digital functions, such as analog-to-digital converters, digital-to-analog converters, and modems .

### Analog to Digital Conversion

Analog to digital conversion (ADC) is an electronic process that converts an analog signal, such as a sound, light, temperature, or pressure, into a digital signal that can be processed, stored, or transmitted by a computer or a microcontroller .

Some of the applications of ADC are:

- Digital imaging systems, such as digital cameras, scanners, and printers, use ADC to digitize pixels and colors.
- Radar systems use ADC to convert signal strength to digital values for subsequent signal processing.
- Audio and video systems use ADC to encode analog sounds and images into digital formats, such as MP3, MP4, or JPEG.
- Sensor networks use ADC to measure physical quantities, such as temperature, humidity, or motion, and send them to a central controller or a cloud server.
- Wearable devices use ADC to monitor biometric data, such as heart rate, blood pressure, or oxygen saturation, and communicate them to a smartphone or a medical device.

The basic components of an ADC are:

- A sample and hold circuit, which captures and holds the analog input signal at a constant level for a fixed period of time .
- A quantizer, which assigns a discrete value to the sampled signal based on a predefined scale or resolution .
- An encoder, which converts the quantized value into a binary code that represents the digital output .

The performance of an ADC depends on several factors, such as:

- Resolution, which is the number of bits used to represent the digital output. Higher resolution means more accuracy and less quantization error  .
- Sampling rate, which is the frequency at which the analog input signal is sampled. Higher sampling rate means more information and less aliasing error  .
- Linearity, which is the degree to which the digital output is proportional to the analog input. Higher linearity means less distortion and less nonlinearity error  .
- Noise, which is the unwanted variation or interference in the analog input signal or the digital output. Lower noise means more reliability and less noise error  .

There are different types of ADC, such as:

- Flash ADC, which uses a parallel array of comparators to compare the analog input signal with a set of reference voltages and generate the digital output in one step. It is the fastest but the most expensive and power-hungry type of ADC .
- Successive approximation ADC, which uses a binary search algorithm to find the digital output in a series of steps. It is the most common and balanced type of ADC in terms of speed, cost, and power consumption .
- Delta-sigma ADC, which uses a feedback loop to oversample and filter the analog input signal and generate a high-resolution digital output. It is the slowest but the most accurate and noise-resistant type of ADC .
- Other types of ADC, such as dual-slope, ramp, integrating, or pipeline ADC, which use different methods to convert the analog input signal into a digital output .

Analog to digital conversion is an essential process for interfacing the digital world with the analog world. It enables the use of various devices and systems that can process, store, or transmit digital data from analog sources. It also allows the use of advanced techniques, such as digital signal processing, data compression, encryption, or machine learning, to enhance the quality, security, or intelligence of the digital data .

# Serial and Parallel Communication

## Introduction

- Serial and parallel communication are two methods of transferring data between computers or devices.
- Serial communication sends data one bit at a time, sequentially, over a single channel or wire.
- Parallel communication sends multiple bits at a time, simultaneously, over multiple channels or wires.
- Both methods have advantages and disadvantages depending on the speed, distance, cost, and reliability of the communication.

## Serial Communication

- Serial communication is the process of sending data one bit at a time, sequentially, over a communication channel or computer bus.
- Serial communication is used when the data rate is low, the distance is long, or the cost of wires is high.
- Serial communication can be synchronous or asynchronous, depending on whether the sender and receiver use a common clock signal or not.
- Serial communication can be simplex, half-duplex, or full-duplex, depending on whether the data can flow in one direction, both directions alternately, or both directions simultaneously.
- Serial communication can use different protocols and standards, such as RS-232, RS-485, USB, SPI, I2C, UART, etc.

## Parallel Communication

- Parallel communication is the method of conveying multiple binary digits (bits) simultaneously using multiple conductors.
- Parallel communication is used when the data rate is high, the distance is short, or the cost of wires is low.
- Parallel communication is usually synchronous, meaning that the sender and receiver use a common clock signal to synchronize the data transfer.
- Parallel communication is usually simplex or half-duplex, meaning that the data can flow in one direction or both directions alternately.
- Parallel communication can use different protocols and standards, such as PCI, SCSI, IDE, etc.

## Comparison

- Serial communication is slower than parallel communication, but it can cover longer distances and use fewer wires.
- Parallel communication is faster than serial communication, but it can cover shorter distances and use more wires.
- Serial communication is more reliable than parallel communication, as it is less prone to interference, crosstalk, and signal degradation.
- Parallel communication is more susceptible to noise, distortion, and synchronization errors than serial communication.

### I2C/IIC

- I2C or IIC stands for **Inter-Integrated Circuit**, a serial communication protocol made by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductor) in 1982 .
- It is intended for communication between chips that reside on the same Printed Circuit Board (PCB) or within a short distance .
- It is a **synchronous**, **multi-master/multi-slave**, **packet switched**, **single-ended**, **serial communication bus**.
- It uses only **two wires** to transmit and receive data: a **serial data line (SDA)** and a **serial clock line (SCL)**  .
- The **clock signal** is always controlled by the **master device**, which initiates and terminates the data transfer  .
- The **data signal** is **bidirectional**, meaning that both master and slave devices can send and receive data over the same wire  .
- The data transfer is **synchronized** to the rising and falling edges of the clock signal .
- Each device on the bus has a **unique address** that is used to identify it during the data transfer  .

### Inter-Integrated Circuit

### SPI

SPI stands for Serial Peripheral Interface. It is a synchronous serial communication protocol that provides full-duplex communication at very high speeds. SPI is a master-slave type protocol that provides a simple and low-cost interface between a microcontroller and its peripherals  .

Some of the main features of SPI are:

- SPI can support multiple slave devices on the same bus by using separate SS lines for each slave.
- SPI can transfer data without interruption, as there is no start or stop bit, parity bit, or acknowledgment bit.

### Serial UART Communication

- UART stands for **Universal Asynchronous Receiver Transmitter**  .
- It is a **serial communication device** that performs parallel-to-serial and serial-to-parallel data conversion .
- It is **universal** because the parameters like transfer speed, data speed, etc. are configurable .
- It is **asynchronous** because there is no clock signal to synchronize the output bits from the transmitting device to the receiving device.
- A UART is usually an individual or part of an integrated circuit (IC) used for serial communications over a computer or peripheral device serial port.
- One or more UART peripherals are commonly integrated in microcontroller chips.
- Specialised UARTs are used for automobiles, smart cards and SIMs.
- A UART consists of a **transmitter** and a **receiver**  .
- The transmitter and the receiver have separate data registers and control registers .
- The transmitter and the receiver operate independently but use the same baud rate and data format .
- The baud rate is the number of bits per second that can be transmitted or received  .
- The data format is the number of data bits, the parity bit and the number of stop bits in each frame  .
- The parity bit is used for error detection and the stop bit is used for signaling the end of a frame  .
- The UART uses two wires for data transmission: **RX** (receive) and **TX** (transmit).
- The RX wire of one device is connected to the TX wire of another device and vice versa.
- The UART also uses additional wires for flow control, such as **RTS** (request to send) and **CTS** (clear to send).
- The flow control wires are used to prevent data loss or buffer overflow when the sender is faster than the receiver or vice versa.
- The UART communication protocol is widely used for full-duplex serial communication between devices  .
- Full-duplex means that data can be sent and received simultaneously  .
- UART communication is simple, reliable and low-cost  .
- UART communication is suitable for short-distance and low-speed applications  .
- UART communication is not suitable for long-distance and high-speed applications because of noise, interference and signal degradation  .

MySQL is a relational database management system (RDBMS) that allows us to store, manipulate, and retrieve data in a structured way. MySQL is free, open-source, and widely used for various applications. In this section, we will learn some basic concepts and operations of MySQL.

#### What is a database?

A database is a collection of related data that is organized in a logical way. A database can have one or more tables, which are the main units of data storage. A table consists of rows and columns, where each row represents a record and each column represents an attribute or a field. For example, a table named `students` can store information about students, such as their names, IDs, majors, and grades.

#### What is SQL?

SQL stands for Structured Query Language, which is a standard language for interacting with databases. SQL allows us to perform various tasks on the data, such as creating, updating, deleting, querying, and manipulating. SQL has a set of keywords, syntax, and rules that we need to follow to write valid SQL statements. For example, the following SQL statement creates a table named `students` with four columns:

```sql
CREATE TABLE students (
  id INT PRIMARY KEY,
  name VARCHAR(50),
  major VARCHAR(50),
  grade FLOAT
);
```

#### How to connect to MySQL?

To connect to MySQL, we need a MySQL server, a MySQL client, and a MySQL user account. A MySQL server is a software that runs on a computer and manages the databases. A MySQL client is a software that allows us to communicate with the MySQL server. A MySQL user account is a set of credentials that grants us access to the databases and permissions to perform operations on them.

There are different ways to connect to MySQL, depending on the type of client we use. One of the most common ways is to use the `mysql` command-line client, which is a program that we can run in a terminal or a command prompt. - The hostname or the IP address of the MySQL server. If the server is running on the same computer as the client, we can use `localhost` or `127.0.0.1`.
- The port number of the MySQL server. The default port is `3306`.
- The username and the password of the MySQL user account. The default username is `root` and the default password is empty.
- The name of the database that we want to use. If we don't specify a database, we can use the `USE` command later to switch to a database.

For example, the following command connects to the MySQL server running on `localhost` with the username `root` and the password `1234`, and uses the database named `school`:

```bash
mysql -h localhost -P 3306 -u root -p1234 school
```

If the connection is successful, we will see a prompt like this:

```bash
mysql>
```

We can then type SQL statements and press `Enter` to execute them. To exit the `mysql` client, we can type `exit` or `quit` and press `Enter`.

# XML

XML stands for eXtensible Markup Language. It is a language that allows you to define your own tags and structure your data in a way that is meaningful to you and your applications. XML is similar to HTML, but unlike HTML, XML does not have predefined tags to use. XML is also designed to be both human- and machine-readable, which means that it can be easily stored, searched, and shared across different platforms and devices.

Some of the basic features and rules of XML are:

- XML documents must have a root element that contains all other elements.
- XML elements can have attributes and text content, but not both.
- XML elements must be properly nested and closed with a matching end tag or a self-closing tag.
- XML tags are case-sensitive and must match exactly.
- XML documents must be well-formed, which means that they must follow the XML syntax rules and not contain any errors or inconsistencies.
- XML documents can also be valid, which means that they must conform to a specific schema or a document type definition (DTD) that defines the rules and constraints for the elements and attributes in the document.

An example of a simple XML document is:

```xml
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<note>
  <to>Tove</to>
  <from>Jani</from>
  <heading>Reminder</heading>
  <body>Don't forget me this weekend!</body>
</note>
```

This document has a root element called `note`, which contains four child elements: `to`, `from`, `heading`, and `body`. Each element has a text content that represents the data. The document also has an XML declaration that specifies the version, encoding, and standalone status of the document.

XML is widely used for various purposes, such as:

- Data exchange and communication between different systems and applications.
- Data storage and retrieval in databases and files.
- Data presentation and transformation using style sheets and languages such as XSLT and XPath.
- Data validation and verification using schemas and DTDs.
- Data processing and manipulation using programming languages and tools such as Java, Python, and XML parsers.

# Arduino

Arduino is an open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software. Arduino boards are able to read inputs from sensors, buttons, or online sources, and turn them into outputs such as motors, LEDs, or online actions .

Some basic concepts of Arduino are:

- Microcontroller: This is the brain of an Arduino, and is the component that we load programs into. It can process data and control other components.
- USB port: This is used to connect your Arduino board to a computer. You can use it to upload programs, communicate with the board, or power the board.
- USB to Serial chip: This is an important component, as it helps translating data that comes from the computer to the microcontroller, and vice versa.
- Pins: These are the connectors that allow you to attach other components to the board. There are different types of pins, such as digital, analog, power, and ground.
- Sketch: This is the name of the program that you write for your Arduino. It consists of two main functions: setup() and loop(). The setup() function runs once when the board is powered on or reset, and the loop() function runs repeatedly until the board is turned off.
- IDE: This is the Integrated Development Environment, which is the software that you use to write, compile, and upload sketches to your Arduino board. It also provides a serial monitor, a code editor, a library manager, and other useful tools .
- Libraries: These are collections of code that provide extra functionality to your sketches. They can help you to use sensors, communicate with other devices, control motors, and more. You can use the libraries that come installed with the IDE, or download and install new ones from the internet.
- Circuits: These are the arrangements of components that you connect to your Arduino board. They consist of at least one active electronic component, such as a sensor, a LED, or a motor, and a conductive material, such as wires, so that current can pass through. You also need resistors, capacitors, and other passive components to control the current, voltage, and timing of your circuits .
