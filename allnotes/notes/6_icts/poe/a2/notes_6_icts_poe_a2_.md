#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

1. According to Evolutionary theorists, state is a product of gradual historical development. It arose out of the primitive tribal organizations of ancient societies.
2. As societies evolved, the institutions and practices of government also evolved in a gradual manner. There was no social contract or deliberate creation of state.
3. The main causes of evolution of state were:
- Growth in population and settlement: As population grew, simple tribal organizations were inadequate to provide security, settle disputes, and maintain law and order. This necessitated the emergence of more complex institutions of government.
- Increased economic interaction: Growth of economic activities and trade led to increased conflicts and disputes that could not be managed by informal institutions of tribe. This necessitated creation of formal institutions of state to regulate such interactions.
- Growth in social stratification: Inequality grew in societies and ruling class emerged. The elite class organized themselves and institutionalized their domination, creating state to perpetuate their power.
- Growth of warfare: As societies faced threats from external invasion and internal rebellions, informal tribal militias were replaced by organized professional armies. This increased the power of chieftains and rulers, contributing to evolution of state.

The evolutionary theory thus sees origin of state as a result of natural growth of societies and not as a deliberate creation to fulfill any social contract. The weaknesses and inefficacies of tribal organizations led to gradual development of state.

- In this way, the ancient Indian administrative system was hierarchical with the king at the top and officers and councils at lower levels to aid in effective governance of the state.

2. Sanskrit theatre originated with the Natya Shastra, an ancient Indian text on the performing arts from the 1st century BCE. It contained guidelines on acting, dance, music and other aspects of theatre. Koodiyattam and Nangiar Koothu are two surviving Sanskrit theatre art forms.
3. Folk theatre forms are varied, with distinct styles specific to each region. For example, Bhavai is a popular form of folk theatre from Gujarat, while Nautanki and Swang are from Uttar Pradesh. Villupattu is a Tamil theatre form performed in village squares.
4. Indian theatre has borrowed elements from Western styles since colonial times. This led to new genres such as Parsi theatre and Tamasha in Maharashtra which incorporated music, dance and lavish costumes. These forms remain popular as forms of musical theatre in India.
5. Post-independence, theatre in India aimed to address social issues and raise political awareness. Groups like the Indian People's Theatre Association (IPTA) employed street theatre and other non-traditional forms to take theatre to the masses. Contemporary Indian theatre continues this tradition of political and social commentary.
6. Theatre in India thus has a rich history of blending traditional and contemporary forms. It is a vibrant cultural and artistic expression that highlights the diversity of Indian culture. Recognizing and preserving the varied Indian theatre arts is vital to conserve India's cultural heritage.

### Drama as Cultural Heritage of India

1. Indian drama has a long and rich tradition spanning over 2000 years. Ancient Indian drama is based on Sanskrit plays, which were performed during religious and social ceremonies.

2. The Natyashastra by Bharata Muni (2nd century BCE) is considered as the foundation treatise of dramaturgy in Sanskrit literature. It covers various aspects of play production like stagecraft, acting, dance, music, types of drama and the audience.

3. There were different forms of drama that evolved in various regions of the country like:
    - Rupaka: ten types including Nataka (realistic social play) and Prakarana (farce)
    - Bhana (one-act monologue play)
    - Dima (folk play)
    - Jatra (folk play)

4. Famous ancient Sanskrit plays include:
    - Shakuntala by Kalidasa
    - Mricchakatika by Shudraka
    - Abhijnanashakuntala by Kalidasa

5. The tradition of Indian drama continues in the modern era with various theatre forms like Nautanki (north India), Tamasha (Maharashtra), Yakshagana (Karnataka) etc. Street plays and folk theatre are also popular in rural India.

6. Indian drama has played an important role in preserving the country's cultural heritage by promoting social reforms, awakening nationalism and educating and entertaining the masses. It represents the rich linguistic and cultural diversity of India.

### Painting as Cultural Heritage of India

1. Indian paintings date back to pre-historic times. The earliest paintings were painted on rocks or cave walls.
2. The ancient Indian art of painting reached its zenith during the Mughal rule between 16th to 18th century. Mughal paintings portrayed courtly scenes, battlefields, wildlife and portraits mainly.
3. Tanjore paintings originated in Thanjavur city of Tamil Nadu. They are characterized by vivid colors and adorned with real gold leaves and semiprecious stones. The paintings mainly depict Hindu deities and scenes from Hindu Puranas and Epics.
4. Madhubani paintings are colorful hand-painted artworks made by women of Mithila region of Bihar. These paintings are made using organic dyes and pigments and depict scenes and motifs from nature and Hindu mythologies.
5. Kangra paintings from Himachal Pradesh use cool and fresh colors and showcase Radha-Krishna themes and scenes from Krishna's life. They show influence of Mughal and Pahari styles of painting.

The above points highlight some important and popular styles of painting in India which form an integral part of India's cultural heritage. The diverse art forms reflect India's rich history and traditions.

### Martial Arts Traditions of India

1. Kalaripayattu: Originates from Kerala. Focuses on agility, coordination and physical strength. Uses weapons like swords, spears, shields and sticks. Aims to achieve a balance of mind, body and spirit.

2. Silambam: From Tamil Nadu. Uses a long stick as the primary weapon. Focusses on quick movements and deft footwork. Aims to defend and attack crucial points on the body. Develops discipline and confidence.

3. Gatka: From Punjab. Uses wooden sticks, swords and daggers. Focusses on courage, honour and chivalry. Performers display feats of bravery, strength and skill through choreographed battle reenactments.

4. Thang-Ta: From Manipur. Uses swords, spears, bows and arrows. Aims to combine attack and defence techniques seamlessly. Considered a way of life that inculcates values and disciplines aspirants mentally and physically.

The martial art forms of India originated as methods of combat and self-defence. Over time, they have evolved into ways to achieve a harmonious balance between the body and mind. Their principles of discipline, courage, honour and skill continue to be influential to this day.

### Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

1. India is a land of fairs and festivals. Fairs and festivals are an integral part of Indian culture. They reflect the entire gamut of human emotions and behavior.

2. Indian festivals and fairs are marked by colors, gaiety, enthusiasm, prayers and rituals. They are tied to important milestones of life like birth, marriage and death. The seasonal festivals celebrate the harvesting of crops and new beginnings.

3. Some of the major festivals in India are:
- Diwali - The festival of lights signifies the victory of good over evil. People light up their homes and offices with diyas and candles.
- Holi - The festival of colors is a celebration of spring and happiness. People play with colors and water.
- Onam - The harvest festival of Kerala, it is a 10 day long festival marked by boat races, dances, and other cultural activities.
- Pongal - The harvest festival of Tamil Nadu, people celebrate the harvest of rice and other crops.
- Ganesh Chaturthi - The festival dedicated to Lord Ganesha who is worshipped as the remover of obstacles. Idols of Ganesha are installed and worshipped for 1-10 days.

4. Indian fairs are marketplaces that gather people from surrounding villages. Some popular fairs are:
- Kumbh Mela - Held at 4 locations, it is the largest gathering of people for a holy dip in the Ganges.
- Pushkar Camel Fair - Held in Rajasthan, it showcases cattle trading along with various cultural events and activities.
- Dussehra Mela of Mysore - A major fair of Karnataka celebrating Dussehra with cultural programs, thrill rides and a torchlight parade.
