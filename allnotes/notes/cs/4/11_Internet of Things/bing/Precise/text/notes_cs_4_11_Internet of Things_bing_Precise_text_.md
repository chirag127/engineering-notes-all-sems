6. **Future:** The future of IoT is promising, with the number of connected devices expected to grow exponentially in the coming years. This will lead to the development of new applications and services, as well as improvements in existing ones. However, it is important to address the challenges associated with IoT to ensure its continued growth and success.

### M2M Communication

- Machine to Machine communications, often termed M2M/IoT is going to be the next generation of Internet revolution connecting more and more devices on the Internet.
- M2M communications refer to automated applications which involve machines or devices communicating through a network without human intervention.
- M2M is a general concept, a group of many different technologies, including IoT, while IoT is a specific subset of technologies that happen to rely on M2M.
- M2M communication comes in many different forms besides the communication between smart devices, and M2M communication is only one part of the Internet of Things.
- M2M is also the foundation for IoT.
- The first use of M2M communication is credited to Theodore Paraskevakos, who invented this technology related to the transmission of data over telephone lines, the basis for modern-day caller-ID.
- IoT needs M2M, but M2M does not need IoT.
- Both terms relate to the communication of connected devices, but M2M systems are often isolated, stand-alone networked equipment.
- IoT systems take M2M to the next level, bringing together disparate systems into one large, connected ecosystem.
- M2M systems use point-to-point communications between machines, sensors and hardware over cellular or wired networks, while IoT systems rely on IP-based networks to send data collected from IoT-connected devices to gateways, the cloud or middleware platforms.

### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology uses radio waves to identify people or objects .
- There is a device that reads information contained in a wireless device or “tag” from a distance without making any physical contact or requiring a line of sight .
- RFID technology has been commercially available in one form or another since the 1970s .
- RFID is used in several commercial and industrial applications, from tracking items along a supply chain to keeping track of items checked out of a library .
- An RFID system consists of a tiny radio transponder, a radio receiver and transmitter .
- When triggered by an electromagnetic interrogation pulse from a nearby RFID reader device, the tag transmits digital data .
- RFID tags are made up of an integrated circuit (IC), an antenna and a substrate .
- RFID refers to a wireless system comprised of two components: tags and readers .
- The reader is a device that has one or more antennas that emit radio waves .




### Wireless Sensor Networks for IoT

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are a subtype of broader IoT networks. They consist of wireless interconnected networks of small low-power sensor devices that sense some environmental parameters at regular intervals of time and send them over to some central storage or database. The central storage or database can also be fog or cloud across the Internet.

To connect sensors embedded in IoT devices, a communication protocol is used. A low-power wide-area network (LPWAN) is a type of wireless network designed to allow long-range communications between these IoT devices.

There are many wireless protocols that enable connectivity between sensors, including Wi-Fi. Wi-Fi (“wireless fidelity”) is a widespread and versatile Local Area Network technology that sends information over two primary frequencies, 2.4GHz and 5GHz. Wi-Fi networks can transfer large data packets at relatively fast speeds over medium ranges.

Wireless sensors are designed to measure specific parameters about their physical surroundings and produce outputs, often electrical signals, for further processing. These parameters include many different types of stimuli, including air temperature, lighting levels, movements, and liquid leakages.

The way these sensors are networked determines whether the sensors can be safely, securely, and cost-effectively deployed in the harsh environments typical of industrial settings.

### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing refers to the use of computer systems and software to perform dedicated functions within larger systems. These systems are typically designed to perform a specific task or set of tasks, and are often integrated into other devices or systems.

Here are some key points to consider when studying embedded computing basics:

1. **Embedded systems** are computer systems that are integrated into other devices or products, and are designed to perform specific functions.
2. **Microcontrollers** are small, low-cost computers that are often used in embedded systems. They typically have limited processing power and memory, but are highly efficient and can be programmed to perform a wide range of tasks.
3. **Real-time operating systems (RTOS)** are operating systems designed for use in embedded systems. They are optimized for fast, predictable response times and are often used in applications where timing is critical.
4. **Firmware** is the software that is embedded in a device and controls its operation. It is typically stored in non-volatile memory, such as flash memory, and can be updated to add new features or fix bugs.
5. **Embedded software** is the software that runs on an embedded system. It is typically written in low-level languages, such as C or assembly, and is designed to be highly efficient and to interact directly with the hardware.
6. **Hardware-software co-design** is the process of designing both the hardware and software components of an embedded system together, in order to optimize performance and reduce development time.

These are some of the basic concepts that are important to understand when studying embedded computing in the context of IoT. It is important to have a solid understanding of these concepts in order to effectively design and develop embedded systems for IoT applications.

### Wireless Medium Access Issues

Wireless medium access issues are a critical aspect of network and communication in IoT. These issues arise due to the shared nature of the wireless medium, where multiple devices compete for access to the same channel. Some of the key issues that need to be addressed in wireless medium access are:

1. **Collision Avoidance**: When multiple devices transmit data simultaneously, their signals can interfere with each other, resulting in a collision. Collision avoidance techniques are used to prevent or minimize the occurrence of collisions.

2. **Fairness**: In a shared wireless medium, it is important to ensure that all devices have fair access to the channel. Fairness can be achieved through various scheduling and resource allocation techniques.

3. **Latency**: The time it takes for a device to access the wireless medium and transmit its data can impact the performance of the network. Latency can be reduced through efficient medium access control protocols.

4. **Throughput**: The amount of data that can be transmitted over the wireless medium in a given time period is known as throughput. Maximizing throughput is important for ensuring efficient use of the wireless channel.

5. **Energy Efficiency**: Wireless devices often have limited battery life, so it is important to minimize their energy consumption. Energy-efficient medium access control protocols can help to extend the battery life of wireless devices.

These are some of the key wireless medium access issues that need to be addressed in the context of network and communication aspects in IoT. By addressing these issues, it is possible to improve the performance and reliability of wireless networks in IoT applications.

### Security Challenges

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a rapidly growing field that connects various devices and systems to the internet, allowing them to communicate and share data. However, with the increasing number of connected devices, there are also increasing security challenges that need to be addressed. Some of the most common security challenges in IoT include:

1. **Lack of standardization**: With so many different devices, protocols, and platforms, it is difficult to ensure compatibility and interoperability between them. This can lead to vulnerabilities that can be exploited by attackers.

2. **Lack of secure boot process**: A secure boot process ensures that only authorized software can run on the device. Without a secure boot process, attackers can potentially install malicious software on the device.

3. **Exposed communications ports**: Exposed communications ports can allow attackers to intercept data or gain unauthorized access to the device.

4. **Lack of encryption**: Encryption is essential for protecting the confidentiality and integrity of data transmitted between devices. Without encryption, data can be easily intercepted and read by attackers.

5. **Weak default passwords**: Many IoT devices come with weak default passwords that can be easily guessed by attackers. This can allow attackers to gain unauthorized access to the device.

6. **Viruses and malware**: IoT devices can be vulnerable to viruses and malware, which can compromise the security of the device and the data it contains.

7. **Legacy Operating Systems**: Many IoT devices run on outdated operating systems that are no longer supported and do not receive security updates. This can leave the device vulnerable to known security vulnerabilities.

8. **Insecure web interface**: An insecure web interface can allow attackers to gain unauthorized access to the device or its data.

9. **Lack of visibility into device security**: Many organizations and individuals are not aware that they are using sensitive IoT products, which can affect cybersecurity and privacy more than any conventional IT devices.

10. **Lack of regular patches and updates**: IoT products are developed with ease of use and convenience in mind, but this often means that security is not a priority. As a result, many IoT devices do not receive regular security patches and updates, leaving them vulnerable to known security vulnerabilities.

These are just some of the security challenges that need to be addressed in the IoT. Robust IoT security includes all facets of protection, including hardening components, monitoring, keeping firmware updated, access management, threat response, and remediation of vulnerabilities.
