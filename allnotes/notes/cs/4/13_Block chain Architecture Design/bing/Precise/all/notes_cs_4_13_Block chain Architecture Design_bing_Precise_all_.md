Cryptography is used to secure the data on the blockchain and to verify the identity of participants in the network. Each block in the blockchain contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, which ensures that the data is tamper-proof. Public key cryptography is also used to sign transactions and to verify the identity of participants.

### Smart Contracts
Smart contracts are self-executing contracts with the terms of the agreement between buyer and seller being directly written into lines of code. They are stored on the blockchain and can be used to automate the execution of business processes and transactions.

These are some of the key elements of blockchain architecture and design. The specific implementation of these elements can vary depending on the intended use and requirements of the blockchain system.



# Basic Crypto Primitives

In the context of blockchain technology, there are several basic cryptographic primitives that are essential to its design and functionality. These include:

1. **Hash functions:** A hash function is a mathematical function that takes an input of arbitrary length and produces a fixed-size output, often referred to as the hash value or digest. Hash functions are commonly used in blockchain technology to ensure the integrity of data and to index data in a hash table.

2. **Digital signatures:** A digital signature is a mathematical scheme for verifying the authenticity of digital messages or documents. In blockchain technology, digital signatures are used to ensure that transactions are authorized by the owner of the funds being transferred.

3. **Public-key cryptography:** Public-key cryptography, also known as asymmetric cryptography, is a cryptographic system that uses pairs of keys: public keys, which may be disseminated widely, and private keys, which are known only to the owner. In blockchain technology, public-key cryptography is used to enable secure communication and to ensure that only the owner of the private key can authorize transactions.

4. **Merkle trees:** A Merkle tree is a tree structure in which every leaf node is labeled with the hash of a data block, and every non-leaf node is labeled with the cryptographic hash of the labels of its child nodes. Merkle trees are used in blockchain technology to efficiently verify the contents of data blocks and to ensure the integrity of the data stored in the blockchain.

These basic crypto primitives form the foundation of blockchain technology and enable it to provide secure, decentralized, and transparent record-keeping and transaction processing.

# Scalability aspects of Blockchain consensus protocols

Scalability is a critical aspect of blockchain consensus protocols, as it determines the ability of the network to handle an increasing number of transactions and users. Here are some key points to consider when discussing the scalability of blockchain consensus protocols:

1. **Transaction processing capacity:** The ability of a blockchain network to process transactions is determined by the consensus protocol used. Some protocols, such as Proof of Work (PoW), can be limited in their ability to process transactions quickly due to the computational power required to solve complex mathematical problems. 3. **Sharding:** Sharding is a technique used to improve the scalability of blockchain networks. It involves dividing the network into smaller, more manageable segments, or shards, each of which can process transactions independently. This can significantly increase the transaction processing capacity of the network.

4. **Off-chain solutions:** Off-chain solutions, such as the Lightning Network, can also improve the scalability of blockchain networks. These solutions involve processing transactions off the main blockchain, reducing the load on the network and allowing for faster transaction processing times.

In summary, the scalability of blockchain consensus protocols is determined by a combination of factors, including the design of the protocol, the size of the network, and the use of techniques such as sharding and off-chain solutions. It is important to carefully consider these factors when designing and implementing blockchain networks to ensure that they can scale effectively to meet the needs of their users.

# Consensus protocols for Permissioned Blockchains

Consensus protocols are a crucial component of blockchain technology, as they ensure that all participants in the network agree on the state of the shared ledger. In permissioned blockchains, where the participants are known and trusted, consensus can be achieved through a variety of mechanisms. Here are some common consensus protocols used in permissioned blockchains:

1. **Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT):** This protocol is designed to tolerate Byzantine faults, where nodes may behave maliciously or fail arbitrarily. In PBFT, a designated leader node proposes a new block, and the other nodes in the network validate and vote on the proposal. If a supermajority of nodes agree, the block is added to the chain.

2. **Raft:** Raft is a leader-based consensus protocol that is designed to be easy to understand and implement. In Raft, a leader node is elected, and it is responsible for managing the log replication process and ensuring that the logs on all nodes are consistent.

3. **Proof of Authority (PoA):** In PoA, a set of pre-approved validator nodes are responsible for validating transactions and creating new blocks. This protocol is often used in private blockchain networks, where the validators are known and trusted entities.

4. **Federated Byzantine Agreement (FBA):** FBA is a generalization of PBFT that allows for more flexible trust models. In FBA, each node chooses a set of other nodes that it trusts, and consensus is achieved when a sufficient number of nodes in each trust set agree on the same value.

These are just a few examples of the many consensus protocols that can be used in permissioned blockchains. The choice of protocol will depend on the specific requirements of the network, such as the level of trust between participants, the desired level of decentralization, and the need for fault tolerance.

- Chaincode is the smart contract functionality of Hyperledger Fabric, allowing developers to define the business logic of their applications.
- Beyond Chaincode includes features such as private data collections, which allow for the sharing of data between specific organizations within a channel, while keeping the data private from other organizations.
- Another feature is the endorsement policy, which defines the set of organizations that must endorse a transaction before it can be committed to the ledger.
- Hyperledger Fabric also includes support for multiple channels, allowing for the creation of private subnets within the network for specific business needs.
- These features, and others, provide additional flexibility and control for developers and organizations using Hyperledger Fabric for their blockchain applications.

Hyperledger Fabric is a blockchain framework that allows for the development of enterprise-grade blockchain applications. Fabric SDKs provide a way for developers to interact with a Fabric network, allowing them to build and deploy chaincode, query the ledger, and perform other tasks.

5. The front-end communicates with the Fabric network through the SDK, sending transactions and queries to the blockchain.

In summary, Fabric SDKs provide a way for developers to interact with a Fabric network, allowing them to build front-end applications that provide a user interface for end-users. The front-end can be built using any web development framework and communicates with the Fabric network through the SDK. This allows for the development of enterprise-grade blockchain applications using Hyperledger Fabric.



# Hyperledger Composer Tool

Hyperledger Composer is a tool for building blockchain business networks. It is an open-source project under the Hyperledger umbrella, hosted by the Linux Foundation. Here are some key points to note about Hyperledger Composer:

1. Hyperledger Composer allows developers to model their business network, including assets, participants, and transactions.
2. It provides a user-friendly interface for creating smart contracts and generating REST APIs for interacting with the blockchain network.
3. Hyperledger Composer is built on top of Hyperledger Fabric, one of the blockchain frameworks under the Hyperledger umbrella.
4. It simplifies the process of creating blockchain applications by abstracting away many of the technical details and allowing developers to focus on the business logic.
5. Hyperledger Composer is not a standalone blockchain platform, but rather a tool for building blockchain applications on top of Hyperledger Fabric.

# Capital Markets

Capital markets are financial markets where long-term debt or equity-backed securities are bought and sold. These markets channel the wealth of savers to those who can put it to long-term productive use, such as companies or governments making long-term investments.

2. **Transparency:** The blockchain should provide a transparent record of all transactions, allowing all participants to see the history of asset ownership and transfers.

In summary, a well-designed blockchain architecture can provide significant benefits to the capital markets industry by improving security, transparency, efficiency, interoperability, scalability, and regulatory compliance. 7. Cryptography is used to ensure the resilience of the blockchain network, by ensuring that the network can withstand attacks and continue to function even in the face of adversity.
