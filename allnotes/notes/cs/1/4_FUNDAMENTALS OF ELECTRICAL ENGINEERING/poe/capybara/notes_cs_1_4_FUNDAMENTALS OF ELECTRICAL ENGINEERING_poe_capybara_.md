

# FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Electricity is a fundamental element of our modern world. Understanding the basic principles of electrical engineering is essential for anyone who wants to work in this field. In this guide, we will cover the fundamentals of electrical engineering, including:

## 1. Basic Concepts

- **Charge:** The fundamental unit of electricity is the charge. Charges can be positive or negative, and they can attract or repel each other.
- **Voltage:** Voltage is the measure of the amount of energy per charge that is available in a circuit. It is measured in volts (V).
- **Current:** Current is the flow of charge through a circuit. It is measured in amperes (A).
- **Resistance:** Resistance is the opposition to the flow of charge in a circuit. It is measured in ohms (Ω).

## 2. Ohm's Law

Ohm's law describes the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in a circuit. It states that:

```
V = IR
```

where V is the voltage, I is the current, and R is the resistance.

## 3. Circuit Analysis

- **Series Circuits:** In a series circuit, the components are connected end to end, with the same current flowing through each component.
- **Parallel Circuits:** In a parallel circuit, the components are connected across each other, with the same voltage across each component.
- **Kirchhoff's Laws:** Kirchhoff's laws describe the behavior of circuits. They state that the sum of the voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is zero, and the sum of the currents entering and leaving any node in a circuit is zero.

## 4. Power

- **Power:** Power is the rate at which energy is transferred in a circuit. It is measured in watts (W).
- **Energy:** Energy is the amount of work that can be done by a system. It is measured in joules (J).
- **Efficiency:** Efficiency is the ratio of useful energy output to the total energy input.

## 5. AC Circuits

- **AC Voltage:** AC voltage is a voltage that changes polarity and magnitude over time.
- **Frequency:** Frequency is the number of cycles per second in an AC voltage.
- **Phase:** Phase is the relationship in time between two AC waveforms.

## Conclusion

These are the fundamentals of electrical engineering. Understanding these concepts is essential for anyone who wants to work in this field. By mastering these fundamentals, you will be able to design and analyze electrical circuits with confidence.

### Electrical circuit elements (R, L and C)

In DC circuits, there are three basic electrical circuit elements - resistors (R), inductors (L) and capacitors (C). These elements are used to build complex circuits that can perform a variety of tasks. Here's what you need to know about them:

#### Resistors (R)

- Resistors are passive components that are used to limit the flow of current in a circuit.
- Resistors are measured in ohms (Ω) and their value can range from a few ohms to several megaohms.
- Resistors are color-coded to indicate their value. The color bands on a resistor can be used to determine the resistance value using a color code chart.
- Resistors can be connected in series or parallel to achieve the desired resistance value.
- Resistors dissipate heat when current flows through them. This heat dissipation must be taken into account when selecting a resistor for a circuit.

#### Inductors (L)

- Inductors are passive components that are used to store energy in a magnetic field.
- Inductors are measured in henries (H) and their value can range from a few microhenries to several henries.
- Inductors are made of coils of wire and the value of the inductor depends on the number of turns, the size of the coil and the material used for the core.
- Inductors can be connected in series or parallel to achieve the desired inductance value.
- When current flows through an inductor, a magnetic field is created around the coil. This magnetic field stores energy that can be released when the current is turned off.

#### Capacitors (C)

- Capacitors are passive components that are used to store energy in an electric field.
- Capacitors are measured in farads (F) and their value can range from a few picofarads to several microfarads.
- Capacitors are made of two conductive plates that are separated by a dielectric material.
- The value of the capacitor depends on the surface area of the plates, the distance between them and the type of dielectric material used.
- Capacitors can be connected in series or parallel to achieve the desired capacitance value.
- When a voltage is applied to a capacitor, an electric field is created between the plates. This electric field stores energy that can be released when the voltage is removed.

### Kirchhoff's Laws for the Notes of Unit 1 - DC Circuits in the Subject of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering

Kirchhoff's laws are fundamental laws that govern the behavior of DC circuits. These laws are named after Gustav Kirchhoff, a German physicist who developed them in the mid-19th century. Understanding these laws is essential for any electrical engineering student, and they are widely used in industry applications.

#### Kirchhoff's First Law

Kirchhoff's first law, also known as the law of conservation of charge, states that the total current entering a junction must be equal to the total current leaving the junction. In other words, the sum of all currents entering a node is equal to the sum of all currents leaving the node. This law is based on the principle of conservation of charge, which states that the total amount of charge in a closed system remains constant.

#### Kirchhoff's Second Law

Kirchhoff's second law, also known as the law of conservation of energy, states that the algebraic sum of all the voltage drops in a closed loop must be equal to zero. In other words, the sum of all voltage drops around a loop is equal to the sum of all voltage rises. This law is based on the principle of conservation of energy, which states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.

#### Applications of Kirchhoff's Laws

Kirchhoff's laws are widely used in industry applications, such as circuit design and analysis, power distribution systems, and electronic devices. These laws are also used in troubleshooting and diagnosing problems in DC circuits.

#### Conclusion

Kirchhoff's laws are essential tools for electrical engineers to understand the behavior of DC circuits. By applying these laws, engineers can design and analyze complex circuits and troubleshoot problems efficiently. Therefore, it is crucial for any electrical engineering student to have a clear understanding of Kirchhoff's laws and their applications in industry.

- The Mesh method is used to analyze a circuit that has several meshes or loops.
- A mesh is a closed loop in a circuit that does not contain any other loops within it.
- The Mesh method is based on the Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL), which states that the sum of voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is zero.
- To apply the Mesh method, we assign a current to each mesh and write the KVL equation for each mesh.
- The number of KVL equations is equal to the number of meshes in the circuit.
- The Mesh method is useful for solving circuits that have a large number of current sources.

#### Nodal Method

- The Nodal method is used to analyze a circuit that has several nodes.
- A node is a point in a circuit where two or more circuit elements are connected.
- The Nodal method is based on the Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL), which states that the sum of currents entering a node is equal to the sum of currents leaving the node.
- To apply the Nodal method, we assign a voltage to each node and write the KCL equation for each node.
- The number of KCL equations is equal to the number of nodes in the circuit.
- The Nodal method is useful for solving circuits that have a large number of voltage sources.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages

- The Mesh method is useful for solving circuits that have a large number of current sources, while the Nodal method is useful for solving circuits that have a large number of voltage sources.
- The Mesh method is generally faster and easier to use than the Nodal method.
- However, the Nodal method is more versatile and can be used to solve a wider variety of circuits.
- In some cases, it may be necessary to use both methods to solve a circuit.

In conclusion, the Mesh and Nodal methods of analysis are important tools for solving DC circuits. By understanding these methods, you can analyze and design complex circuits with ease.

In the study of electrical engineering, it is important to understand the representation of sinusoidal waveforms. Sinusoidal waveforms are commonly found in AC circuits, and their average and effective values are important concepts to grasp.

Here are some key points to remember:

- Sinusoidal waveforms are characterized by their amplitude, frequency, and phase angle.
- The average value of a sinusoidal waveform over one complete cycle is zero, since the positive and negative values cancel each other out.
- However, the average value of a sinusoidal waveform over a half-cycle is non-zero and can be calculated using the formula:

  $$
  V_{avg} = \frac{1}{T/2} \int_{0}^{T/2} V_m \sin(\omega t + \phi) \, dt = \frac{2V_m}{\pi}
  $$

  where $V_m$ is the peak amplitude, $T$ is the period, $\omega$ is the angular frequency, $\phi$ is the phase angle, and $V_{avg}$ is the average value.

- The effective value of a sinusoidal waveform is defined as the root-mean-square (RMS) value and is given by:

  $$
  V_{eff} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} V^2(t) \, dt} = \frac{V_m}{\sqrt{2}}
  $$

  where $V(t)$ is the instantaneous value of the waveform.

- The effective value is important because it represents the equivalent DC voltage that would produce the same heating effect in a resistor as the AC waveform.
- The ratio of the effective value to the peak value is known as the form factor and is equal to $\sqrt{2}$ for a perfect sinusoidal waveform.

Understanding the average and effective values of sinusoidal waveforms is crucial in the analysis and design of AC circuits. By using these concepts, engineers can accurately calculate power, voltage, and current in AC circuits and make informed design decisions.

### Apparent, Active & Reactive Power

In AC circuits, power is not simply a product of voltage and current like in DC circuits. There are two types of power in AC circuits - active power and reactive power. Additionally, there is another type of power called apparent power which is the combination of both active and reactive power. In this unit, we will study these three types of power in detail.

#### Apparent Power

Apparent power is the total power that is being supplied to the circuit. It is the product of the voltage and the current in the circuit. Apparent power is measured in volt-amperes (VA). It represents the power that the source is capable of supplying to the circuit. Apparent power is denoted by the symbol S.

#### Active Power

Active power is the power that is actually being consumed by the circuit. It is the product of the voltage, current and the power factor of the circuit. Active power is measured in watts (W). It represents the power that is being converted into useful work in the circuit. Active power is denoted by the symbol P.

#### Reactive Power

Reactive power is the power that is not being converted into useful work in the circuit. It is the product of the voltage, current and the sine of the phase angle between them. Reactive power is measured in volt-amperes reactive (VAR). It represents the power that is being used to maintain the magnetic or electric fields in the circuit. Reactive power is denoted by the symbol Q.

#### Relationship between Apparent, Active & Reactive Power

The relationship between these three types of power can be expressed using the following equation:

S² = P² + Q²

This equation is known as the power triangle. It shows that the apparent power of a circuit is equal to the square root of the sum of the squares of the active power and the reactive power.

#### Power Factor

The power factor of a circuit is the ratio of active power to apparent power. It represents the efficiency of the circuit in converting the supplied power into useful work. A circuit with a low power factor is less efficient as it uses more reactive power. The power factor of a circuit can be improved by adding power factor correction devices such as capacitors.

In conclusion, understanding the concepts of apparent power, active power and reactive power is important in the analysis of AC circuits. These concepts are essential in designing and operating efficient electrical systems.

#### Resonance in Parallel Circuits

- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is at its maximum, which means that the current through the circuit is at its minimum.
- The voltage across the resistor is also at its maximum at resonance, while the voltage across the capacitor and inductor is at its minimum.

#### Quality Factor (Q) of Resonant Circuits

- In a series resonant circuit, Q is equal to the ratio of the inductive reactance to the resistance.

### Losses in Transformers

Transformers are essential devices that are used for the transmission and distribution of electrical energy. However, they are not ideal devices, and they suffer from losses that reduce their efficiency. In this section, we will discuss the different types of losses in transformers.

#### Copper Losses

Copper losses are also known as I2R losses. These losses occur due to the resistance of the copper wire used in the transformer windings. The current flowing through the wire generates heat due to the resistance of the wire, resulting in power loss. Copper losses increase with the square of the current flowing through the transformer.

#### Iron Losses

Iron losses are also known as core losses. These losses occur due to the alternating magnetic flux in the transformer core. The magnetic flux causes eddy currents to flow in the core material, which generates heat and results in power loss. Iron losses are independent of the load current and depend on the frequency of the input voltage.

#### Stray Losses

Stray losses occur due to the leakage of magnetic flux from the transformer core. The leakage flux induces currents in the transformer windings, resulting in power loss. Stray losses depend on the load current and the design of the transformer.

#### Dielectric Losses

Dielectric losses occur due to the insulation used in the transformer windings. The insulation material has a finite dielectric constant, which results in power loss due to the charging and discharging of the insulation material. Dielectric losses depend on the voltage applied to the transformer.

#### Total Losses

The total losses in a transformer are the sum of copper losses, iron losses, stray losses, and dielectric losses. The total losses determine the efficiency of the transformer, which is defined as the ratio of output power to input power. Transformer manufacturers aim to minimize losses to increase the efficiency of the transformer.

In conclusion, losses in transformers are inevitable due to the physical properties of the transformer components. Understanding the different types of losses and their causes is crucial for designing and maintaining efficient transformers.

#### Working Principle

The working principle of a single-phase induction motor is based on the production of a rotating magnetic field. When an AC supply is given to the stator winding, a magnetic field is produced that rotates at synchronous speed. This rotating magnetic field induces an emf in the rotor winding, which causes rotor current to flow. Due to the interaction of the magnetic fields, a torque is produced that drives the rotor in the same direction as the rotating magnetic field.

#### Construction

The construction of a single-phase induction motor consists of two main parts - stator and rotor. The stator is a stationary part that consists of a cylindrical frame, stator winding, and core. The rotor is a rotating part that consists of a core, rotor winding, and bars.

#### Types

There are two types of single-phase induction motors - split-phase and capacitor-start motors. 

- Split-phase motors have a starting winding and a running winding. The starting winding is disconnected from the circuit after starting, and the motor runs on the running winding.
- Capacitor-start motors have a starting capacitor that provides a phase difference between the starting and running windings, which helps in producing the rotating magnetic field.

#### Advantages

- Simple and robust construction
- Low cost and maintenance
- High starting torque

#### Disadvantages

- Limited power range
- Low efficiency at light loads
- Cannot be used for high-speed applications

In conclusion, single-phase induction motors are widely used in household appliances due to their simple construction, low cost, and high starting torque. However, they have limited power range and low efficiency at light loads, which restricts their use in high-speed applications.

- The rotor of the synchronous motor, being excited by a DC current, is magnetically locked with the rotating magnetic field produced by the stator.

### ELCB

#### Types of ELCB

1. Voltage-Operated ELCB: It operates when there is a voltage difference between the live conductor and the earth conductor.

2. Current-Operated ELCB: It operates when there is a current difference between the live conductor and the earth conductor.

3. Residual Current Circuit Breaker (RCCB): It is a combination of both voltage-operated and current-operated ELCBs. It detects the residual current and trips the circuit if it exceeds the rated value.

#### Advantages of ELCB
