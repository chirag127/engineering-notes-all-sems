Soft skills are a combination of people skills, social skills, communication skills, character or personality traits, attitudes, career attributes, social intelligence and emotional intelligence quotients, among others, that enable people to navigate their environment, work well with others, perform well, and achieve their goals with complementing hard skills.

Here are some key soft skills that are important for personal and professional development:

1. **Communication:** The ability to effectively communicate with others, both verbally and in writing, is essential for building relationships and achieving goals.

2. **Teamwork:** The ability to work effectively with others towards a common goal, including the ability to collaborate, compromise, and resolve conflicts.

3. **Problem-solving:** The ability to identify, analyze, and find solutions to problems, including the ability to think critically and creatively.

4. **Time management:** The ability to effectively manage one's time, including the ability to prioritize tasks, set and meet deadlines, and work efficiently.

5. **Adaptability:** The ability to adapt to changing situations and environments, including the ability to learn new skills and take on new challenges.

6. **Leadership:** The ability to lead and motivate others, including the ability to set a vision, inspire others to action, and provide direction and guidance.

7. **Emotional intelligence:** The ability to understand and manage one's own emotions, as well as the emotions of others, including the ability to empathize, communicate effectively, and build relationships.

8. **Work ethic:** The ability to consistently work hard and demonstrate a strong commitment to achieving goals, including the ability to take initiative, be reliable, and be accountable for one's actions.

These are just a few examples of the many soft skills that are important for personal and professional success. Developing these skills takes time and effort, but the rewards can be significant. By improving your soft skills, you can enhance your relationships, achieve your goals, and build a successful career.

### Unit-1 Applied Grammar and Usage: 8

1. **Subject-Verb Agreement:** The verb must agree with its subject in number and person. For example, "She writes" but "They write."
2. **Tenses:** Tenses are used to indicate the time of an action or state of being. There are three main tenses: present, past, and future.
3. **Active and Passive Voice:** In the active voice, the subject performs the action. In the passive voice, the subject receives the action. For example, "The cat chased the mouse" (active) vs. "The mouse was chased by the cat" (passive).
4. **Modifiers:** Modifiers are words or phrases that provide additional information about the noun or verb they modify. For example, "The **quick** brown fox" or "She sings **beautifully**."
5. **Pronouns:** Pronouns are words that take the place of a noun. For example, "She" instead of "Mary" or "It" instead of "the book."
6. **Prepositions:** Prepositions are words that show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. For example, "The book is **on** the table" or "She arrived **after** dinner."
7. **Conjunctions:** Conjunctions are words that connect words, phrases, or clauses. For example, "She likes pizza **and** ice cream" or "I will go to the store **if** I have time."
8. **Interjections:** Interjections are words or phrases that express strong emotion or surprise. For example, "Wow!" or "Oh no!"




### Transformation of Sentences: Simple, Compound and Complex, Subject-verb agreement, Prefix and Suffix, Antonyms, Synonyms, Homophones

#### Transformation of Sentences
- A sentence can be transformed by changing its structure while keeping its meaning the same.
- There are three types of sentences: simple, compound, and complex.
- A simple sentence contains only one independent clause.
- A compound sentence contains two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction.
- A complex sentence contains one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses.

#### Subject-verb agreement
- Subject-verb agreement refers to the grammatical rule that the verb must agree with the subject in number and person.
- Singular subjects take singular verbs, while plural subjects take plural verbs.
- The verb must also agree with the subject in person, meaning that if the subject is first, second, or third person, the verb must be in the corresponding form.

#### Prefix and Suffix
- A prefix is a group of letters added to the beginning of a word to change its meaning.
- A suffix is a group of letters added to the end of a word to change its meaning.
- Prefixes and suffixes are used to form new words or to change the grammatical function of a word.

#### Antonyms, Synonyms, Homophones
- Antonyms are words that have opposite meanings.
- Synonyms are words that have the same or similar meanings.
- Homophones are words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings.



### Homonyms, New Word Formation, Select Word Power

#### Homonyms
- Homonyms are words that have the same spelling and pronunciation but have different meanings.
- Examples of homonyms include "bank" (a financial institution and the side of a river), "bark" (the sound a dog makes and the outer layer of a tree), and "bat" (a flying mammal and a piece of sports equipment).

#### New Word Formation
- New words can be formed through various processes, including compounding, blending, clipping, and back-formation.
- Compounding involves combining two or more words to create a new word, such as "toothbrush" or "sunflower".
- Blending involves combining parts of two or more words to create a new word, such as "brunch" (from "breakfast" and "lunch") or "smog" (from "smoke" and "fog").
- Clipping involves shortening a word to create a new word, such as "ad" (from "advertisement") or "gym" (from "gymnasium").
- Back-formation involves creating a new word by removing a real or supposed affix from an existing word, such as "edit" (from "editor") or "babysit" (from "babysitter").

#### Select Word Power
- Selecting the right words to use in communication can greatly enhance one's ability to convey ideas and information effectively.
- One way to improve word power is to expand one's vocabulary by learning new words and their meanings.
- Another way is to practice using words in context, such as by writing or speaking, to improve one's ability to choose the right words for a given situation.
- It can also be helpful to pay attention to the words used by others, such as in books, articles, or speeches, to learn from their effective use of language.

4. Being patient: A good listener allows the speaker to finish their thoughts without interrupting, and does not rush the conversation.

5. Being open-minded: A good listener is open to new ideas and perspectives, and is willing to consider different viewpoints.

By practicing active listening, one can become a better communicator and build stronger relationships with others. It is a valuable skill that can be applied in both personal and professional settings.

### Speaking style; content and sequencing

Speaking style refers to the way a person communicates verbally, including their tone, pace, and use of language. It is an important aspect of effective communication and can greatly influence how a message is received by the listener.

Content refers to the information or ideas being conveyed in a message. It is important to ensure that the content is relevant, accurate, and appropriate for the intended audience.

Sequencing refers to the order in which information is presented. Effective sequencing can help to make a message more clear and understandable, and can also help to keep the listener engaged.

Here are some points to consider when thinking about speaking style, content, and sequencing:

1. **Consider your audience**: Tailor your speaking style, content, and sequencing to the needs and expectations of your audience.
2. **Be clear and concise**: Use clear and concise language to convey your message. Avoid using jargon or technical terms that may be unfamiliar to your audience.
3. **Use an appropriate tone**: Use a tone that is appropriate for the situation and the message you are conveying. For example, a serious message may require a more formal tone, while a lighthearted message may be delivered with a more casual tone.

6. **Use transitions**: Use transitions to help guide your audience from one idea to the next. Transitions can include phrases like "moving on to...", "in addition to...", or "on the other hand...".

### Reading style: Skimming; Scanning; Churning & Assimilation, Effective writing

Reading is an essential skill for learning and personal growth. There are several reading styles that can be used depending on the purpose of reading and the type of material being read. These styles include skimming, scanning, churning, and assimilation.

1. **Skimming** is a reading style that involves quickly going through the text to get a general idea of its content. This style is useful when you need to quickly understand the main points of a text, such as when previewing a book or article before deciding whether to read it in detail.

2. **Scanning** is a reading style that involves quickly searching for specific information within a text. This style is useful when you need to find a particular piece of information, such as a date, name, or fact, without reading the entire text.

3. **Churning** is a reading style that involves reading a text slowly and carefully, with the aim of understanding it in depth. This style is useful when you need to fully understand a complex text, such as a legal document or a scientific paper.

4. **Assimilation** is a reading style that involves actively engaging with the text, making connections between the information it contains and your existing knowledge. This style is useful when you need to remember and apply the information you are reading, such as when studying for an exam.

Effective writing is the ability to communicate your ideas clearly and concisely in written form. To write effectively, it is important to:

- Plan and organize your writing
- Use clear and concise language
- Use correct grammar and punctuation
- Edit and revise your writing to improve its clarity and coherence.

By using these reading styles and practicing effective writing, you can improve your ability to learn and communicate through the written word.



### Tools and Methods: Inductive, Deductive, Exposition, Linear, Interrupted, Spatial

- **Inductive**: Inductive reasoning is a method of reasoning in which the premises are viewed as supplying some evidence for the truth of the conclusion. - **Deductive**: Deductive reasoning is a logical process in which a conclusion is based on the concordance of multiple premises that are generally assumed to be true. It is sometimes referred to as top-down logic.

### Minutes of Meeting

Minutes of meeting, also known as meeting minutes or MOM, are written records of the discussions and decisions made during a meeting. They serve as an official record of the meeting and are used to document the key points discussed, decisions made, and actions agreed upon.

Here are some key points to consider when writing minutes of meeting:

1. **Preparation**: Before the meeting, prepare an agenda and circulate it to all attendees. This will help to keep the meeting focused and on track.

2. **Attendance**: Record the names of all attendees, including any apologies for absence.

3. **Agenda items**: For each agenda item, record the main points of discussion, any decisions made, and any actions agreed upon.

4. **Action items**: Clearly identify any action items, including the person responsible for completing the action and the deadline for completion.

5. **Decisions**: Record any decisions made, including any votes taken and the outcome of the vote.

6. **Next steps**: Include any next steps or follow-up actions that need to be taken.

7. **Review**: After the meeting, circulate the minutes to all attendees for review and feedback.

8. **Approval**: Once the minutes have been reviewed and any necessary changes made, they should be approved by the chairperson or other designated authority.

9. **Filing**: The approved minutes should be filed in a secure location for future reference.

Remember, minutes of meeting are an important record of the discussions and decisions made during a meeting. They should be clear, concise, and accurate, and should be prepared and circulated in a timely manner.

### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas through spoken words. It is one of the most common forms of communication and is used in a variety of settings, including personal, professional, and academic.

There are several nuances to consider when delivering a speech, including tone, pace, and body language. These elements can greatly impact the effectiveness of the message being conveyed.

There are also several modes of speech delivery, including impromptu, extemporaneous, manuscript, and memorized. Each mode has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of mode will depend on the situation and the speaker's goals.

1. **Tone** refers to the attitude or emotion conveyed through the speaker's voice. It is important to match the tone to the content and purpose of the speech.
2. **Pace** refers to the speed at which the speaker delivers the speech. A good pace should be easy to follow and should not be too fast or too slow.
3. **Body language** refers to the nonverbal cues conveyed through the speaker's posture, gestures, and facial expressions. Effective body language can enhance the message being conveyed and help the speaker connect with the audience.
4. **Impromptu** speeches are delivered without preparation and are often used in informal settings or when a speaker is called upon to speak unexpectedly.
5. **Extemporaneous** speeches are delivered with some preparation, but the speaker does not read from a manuscript. This mode allows for more flexibility and spontaneity than a manuscript speech.
6. **Manuscript** speeches are written out in full and read by the speaker. This mode is often used for formal occasions or when the content of the speech is complex or technical.
7. **Memorized** speeches are written out in full and memorized by the speaker. This mode allows for the most control over the delivery of the speech, but can also be the most difficult to execute effectively.

In conclusion, oral communication is a complex process that involves many nuances and modes of delivery. Understanding these elements can help speakers deliver more effective and engaging speeches.



### Public speaking: confidence, clarity, and fluency, Individual Speaking: Elements

Public speaking is the act of delivering a speech or presentation to a live audience. ### Non verbal Communication: Kinesics, Paralinguistic features of Voice-Dynamics

Nonverbal communication refers to the ways in which we communicate without using words. It includes body language, facial expressions, gestures, and other visual cues. There are two main types of nonverbal communication: kinesics and paralinguistic features of voice-dynamics.

1. **Kinesics** refers to the study of body movements, including facial expressions, gestures, and posture. It is the way in which we communicate through our body language. For example, crossing your arms can indicate defensiveness, while leaning forward can show interest or engagement.

2. **Paralinguistic features of voice-dynamics** refer to the nonverbal elements of speech, such as tone, pitch, volume, and rate. These features can convey emotions and attitudes that are not expressed through words alone. For example, a monotone voice can indicate boredom or disinterest, while a high-pitched, fast-paced voice can convey excitement or anxiety.

Both kinesics and paralinguistic features of voice-dynamics play an important role in communication. They can convey emotions, attitudes, and intentions that are not expressed through words alone. Understanding and being able to interpret these nonverbal cues can help improve communication and relationships.

Presentation strategies involve planning and preparation for effective communication. This includes selecting the appropriate medium, organizing the content, and rehearsing the delivery.

3. **Access**: This involves seeking support from others to help manage stress. This can include talking to friends or family, seeking professional help, or joining a support group.

These 4 A's can be applied in various situations to help manage stress and anxiety. By using a combination of these techniques, individuals can develop a more effective and personalized approach to managing stress.

4. **Defer judgment**: Avoid interrupting the speaker or jumping to conclusions. Allow the speaker to finish their message before forming an opinion or providing feedback.

5. **Respond appropriately**: Respond to the speaker in a respectful and thoughtful manner. Offer your own thoughts and opinions in a way that is constructive and adds value to the conversation.

4. **Proofread**: Take the time to carefully review your message for errors in spelling, grammar, and punctuation.

5. **Be professional**: Use a professional tone and format when writing emails and proposals. Avoid using slang or overly casual language.
