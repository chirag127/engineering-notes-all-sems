## Hours

- An hour is a unit of time that is equal to 60 minutes or 3,600 seconds.
- It is one of the most commonly used units of time worldwide.
- The concept of the hour can be traced back to ancient civilizations such as the Egyptians, who divided the day into 24 equal parts.
- The hour is commonly represented using the symbol "h" or "hr".
- In many parts of the world, the 24-hour clock is used to represent time, where the hours of the day are numbered from 0 to 23.
- In other parts of the world, the 12-hour clock is used, where the hours of the day are numbered from 1 to 12, with the addition of "AM" or "PM" to distinguish between morning and afternoon/evening hours.
- The hour is also used as a measure of angle, where one hour is equal to 15 degrees.
- Time zones are regions of the Earth that have the same standard time, and they are usually separated by one hour.
- Daylight saving time is the practice of advancing clocks by one hour during the summer months to make better use of natural daylight.
- The length of an hour can vary slightly due to the irregularities in the Earth's rotation. However, this variation is usually not noticeable in everyday life.



### Unit-1 Applied Grammar and Usage: 8

1. **Subject-Verb Agreement**: The verb must agree with its subject in number and person. For example, "She writes" but "They write".
2. **Tenses**: Tenses are used to indicate the time of an action or state of being. There are three main tenses: present, past, and future.
3. **Active and Passive Voice**: In the active voice, the subject performs the action. In the passive voice, the subject receives the action. For example, "The cat chased the mouse" (active) vs. "The mouse was chased by the cat" (passive).
4. **Modifiers**: Modifiers are words or phrases that provide additional information about the noun or verb they modify. For example, "The **quick** brown fox" or "She sings **beautifully**".
5. **Pronouns**: Pronouns are words that take the place of a noun. For example, "She" instead of "Mary" or "It" instead of "the book".
6. **Prepositions**: Prepositions are words that show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. For example, "The book is **on** the table" or "She arrived **after** dinner".
7. **Conjunctions**: Conjunctions are words that join words, phrases, or clauses. For example, "She likes pizza **and** ice cream" or "I will go to the store **if** I have time".
8. **Interjections**: Interjections are words or phrases that express strong emotion. For example, "Wow!" or "Oh no!".




### Transformation of Sentences: Simple, Compound and Complex, Subject-verb agreement, Prefix and Suffix, Antonyms, Synonyms, Homophones

1. **Transformation of Sentences**: Transformation of sentences refers to the process of changing the form of a sentence without changing its meaning. There are three main types of sentences: simple, compound, and complex.
    - **Simple sentences** contain only one independent clause and no dependent clauses. For example, "The cat is sleeping."
    - **Compound sentences** contain two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction. For example, "The cat is sleeping, and the dog is playing."
    - **Complex sentences** contain one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. For example, "Although the cat is sleeping, the dog is playing."

2. **Subject-verb agreement**: Subject-verb agreement refers to the grammatical rule that the verb must agree in number with its subject. For example, "The cat is sleeping" (singular subject, singular verb) and "The cats are sleeping" (plural subject, plural verb).

3. **Prefix and Suffix**: A prefix is a group of letters added to the beginning of a word to change its meaning. For example, the prefix "un-" can be added to the word "happy" to create the word "unhappy." A suffix is a group of letters added to the end of a word to change its meaning. For example, the suffix "-ness" can be added to the word "happy" to create the word "happiness."

4. **Antonyms**: Antonyms are words that have opposite meanings. For example, "happy" and "sad" are antonyms.

5. **Synonyms**: Synonyms are words that have similar meanings. For example, "happy" and "joyful" are synonyms.

6. **Homophones**: Homophones are words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings. For example, "flower" and "flour" are homophones.



### Homonyms, New Word Formation, Select Word Power

#### Homonyms
- Homonyms are words that have the same spelling and pronunciation but different meanings.
- Examples of homonyms include "bat" (a flying mammal or a piece of sports equipment), "bank" (a financial institution or the side of a river), and "bark" (the sound a dog makes or the outer layer of a tree).

#### New Word Formation
- New words can be formed in a variety of ways, including compounding, blending, clipping, and back-formation.
- Compounding involves combining two or more words to create a new word, such as "toothbrush" or "sunflower".
- Blending involves combining parts of two or more words to create a new word, such as "brunch" (from "breakfast" and "lunch") or "smog" (from "smoke" and "fog").
- Clipping involves shortening a word to create a new word, such as "ad" (from "advertisement") or "gym" (from "gymnasium").
- Back-formation involves creating a new word by removing a real or supposed affix from an existing word, such as "edit" (from "editor") or "babysit" (from "babysitter").

#### Select Word Power
- Select word power refers to the ability to choose the most appropriate words to convey a particular meaning or message.

### Active Listening: Meaning and Art of Listening, Traits of a Good Listener

Active listening is a communication technique that involves fully concentrating, understanding, responding to, and remembering what is being said. It is an important skill that can improve relationships, reduce misunderstandings, and enhance problem-solving abilities.

The art of listening involves paying attention to both verbal and nonverbal cues, such as body language and tone of voice. It also involves providing feedback to the speaker, such as nodding or asking clarifying questions, to show that you are engaged in the conversation.

Some traits of a good listener include:
- Being non-judgmental and open-minded
- Providing undivided attention to the speaker
- Avoiding interrupting or finishing the speaker's sentences
- Asking clarifying questions to ensure understanding
- Providing appropriate feedback, such as summarizing what was said or expressing empathy
- Avoiding distractions, such as checking your phone or looking around the room

By practicing active listening, you can improve your communication skills and build stronger relationships with others. It takes effort and practice, but the benefits are well worth it.

### Reading style: Skimming; Scanning; Churning & Assimilation, Effective writing

1. **Skimming** is a reading technique that involves quickly going through the text to get a general idea of its content. This technique is useful when you need to quickly understand the main points of a text without going into detail.

2. **Scanning** is another reading technique that involves quickly searching for specific information in a text. This technique is useful when you need to find a particular piece of information, such as a date or a name, without reading the entire text.

3. **Churning** is the process of actively engaging with the text to understand it deeply. This involves reading the text carefully, taking notes, and thinking critically about its content.

4. **Assimilation** is the process of integrating new information into your existing knowledge. This involves connecting the information you have just read with what you already know, and using it to build a deeper understanding of the topic.

5. **Effective writing** is the ability to communicate your ideas clearly and concisely in writing. This involves organizing your thoughts, using appropriate language and grammar, and presenting your ideas in a logical and coherent manner.

These reading and writing techniques can help you to read and write more effectively, and to get the most out of the texts you encounter. By using a combination of these techniques, you can improve your reading and writing skills, and become a more effective communicator.



### Tools and Methods: Inductive, Deductive, Exposition, Linear, Interrupted, Spatial

- **Inductive**: Inductive reasoning is a method of reasoning in which the premises are viewed as supplying some evidence for the truth of the conclusion. It is also described as a method where one's experiences and observations, including what are learned from others, are synthesized to come up with a general truth.

- **Deductive**: Deductive reasoning is a logical process in which a conclusion is based on the concordance of multiple premises that are generally assumed to be true. It is a top-down approach to reasoning, where one starts with a general statement and deduces specific instances.

- **Exposition**: Exposition is a type of writing that explains or informs. It is one of the four traditional modes of discourse, along with description, narration, and argumentation. The purpose of exposition is to convey information and explain ideas clearly and logically.

- **Linear**: Linear writing is a method of organizing information in a clear and logical sequence. It is often used in academic writing, where the writer presents an argument or thesis and supports it with evidence and examples in a linear fashion.

- **Interrupted**: Interrupted writing is a method of organizing information in a non-linear fashion. It involves breaking up the flow of a text with interruptions, such as digressions, flashbacks, or shifts in perspective. This can be used to create a more engaging and dynamic narrative.

- **Spatial**: Spatial organization is a method of organizing information based on the physical location or arrangement of objects. It is often used in descriptive writing, where the writer describes a scene or setting by moving from one location to another, or by describing the relationships between objects in space.

### Minutes of Meeting

Minutes of meeting, also known as meeting minutes or MoM, are written records of the discussions and decisions made during a meeting. They serve as an official record of the meeting and are used to inform attendees and non-attendees about what was discussed and decided.

Here are some key points to consider when writing minutes of meeting:

1. **Preparation**: Before the meeting, prepare an agenda and a template for the minutes. This will help you to organize the information and ensure that all important topics are covered.

2. **Attendance**: Record the names of all attendees, including any guests or observers. If someone is absent, make a note of their absence.

3. **Agenda**: Follow the agenda and record the discussions and decisions for each item. If any new items are added to the agenda during the meeting, make sure to record them as well.

4. **Action Items**: Record any action items that are assigned during the meeting, including the person responsible and the deadline for completion.

5. **Decisions**: Record all decisions made during the meeting, including any votes or consensus reached.

6. **Clarity**: Write the minutes in a clear and concise manner, avoiding any jargon or technical terms that may be unfamiliar to the readers.

7. **Timeliness**: Complete the minutes as soon as possible after the meeting, while the information is still fresh in your mind.

8. **Distribution**: Distribute the minutes to all attendees and any other relevant parties, such as senior management or stakeholders.

9. **Review**: At the start of the next meeting, review the minutes from the previous meeting to ensure that all action items have been completed and that there are no outstanding issues.

By following these guidelines, you can create clear and effective minutes of meeting that serve as a valuable record of the discussions and decisions made during the meeting.

### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

Oral communication refers to the spoken exchange of information, ideas, and thoughts between two or more people. It is one of the most common and effective forms of communication. Some of the key aspects of oral communication include:

1. **Clarity:** The speaker should be clear and concise in their speech, using language that is easily understood by the audience.

2. **Tone:** The tone of voice used by the speaker can convey emotions and attitudes, and can greatly impact the effectiveness of the communication.

3. **Non-verbal cues:** Body language, facial expressions, and gestures can also convey meaning and should be used effectively in oral communication.

4. **Active listening:** Effective oral communication also involves active listening on the part of the audience, to ensure that the message is received and understood.

There are several modes of speech delivery, including:

1. **Impromptu:** This is an unprepared, spontaneous speech, often given in response to an unexpected situation or question.

2. **Extemporaneous:** This is a speech that is prepared in advance, but delivered without notes or a script.

3. **Manuscript:** This is a speech that is written out in full and read from a script.

4. **Memorized:** This is a speech that is written out in full and memorized by the speaker.

Each mode of speech delivery has its own nuances and can be effective in different situations. It is important for the speaker to choose the appropriate mode for the situation and audience.
