- Active power (P) = V x I x Cosθ

- Increased efficiency.

##### 3. Study of phenomenon of resonance in RLC series circuit and obtain resonant frequency.

1. An RLC series circuit consists of a resistor (R), inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) connected in series with an AC voltage source.
2. At certain frequencies, the reactive impedances of the inductor and the capacitor cancel each other, and the overall impedance of the circuit is purely resistive. These frequencies are known as resonant frequencies.
3. The resonant frequency (ωr) is given by: ωr = 1/√LC
4. At the resonant frequency, the phase difference between the current and voltage is 0° and the circuit absorbs maximum power from the source. This is known as resonance.
5. The current is maximum and the voltage across the resistor is also maximum at resonance. The voltage across the inductor and capacitor are minimum at resonance.
6. The bandwidth of the resonant circuit depends on the resistance. Higher the resistance, greater is the bandwidth. The resistance damps the resonant circuit and widens the bandwidth.

The above points cover the key aspects of studying the phenomenon of resonance in an RLC series circuit and obtaining the resonant frequency. - The circuit parameters like resistance (R), inductance (L) and capacitance (C) can be determined using the following methods:

1. Using voltage and current measurements:
- Measure the voltage (V) and current (I) across each element using multimeter for a known frequency (f) supply.

- Apply a voltage and note down the time period (T) of the current waveform.
- Use the relation 1/f = 1/2π√(LC) to determine the value of the product LC.

- Measure the impedance (Z) of the RLC series combination and the individual R, XLand XC.
- Use the relations: Z = R + jXLand XC = 1/2πfC to determine the parameters.

##### 1. Kirchhoff's laws:

1. Kirchhoff's current law: The total current flowing into a node is equal to the total current flowing out of the node. In other words, the algebraic sum of currents in a closed circuit must be zero.
2. Kirchhoff's voltage law: The sum of all voltage drops and rises in a closed circuit must be equal to zero. In other words, the total voltage change around a closed circuit is zero.

These laws are fundamental to the analysis of electric circuits. They help simplify circuit analysis by transforming the circuit into a set of equations that can be solved to determine unknown voltages, currents, and resistances. The laws apply to both AC and DC circuits.

##### 2. Thevenin Theorem

- Thevenin theorem states that any linear electrical network can be replaced at its terminals by an equivalent combination of a voltage source and a series resistance.
- This equivalent circuit represents the voltage-current relationship at the terminals of the network.
- To find the Thevenin equivalent:
	- Find the open-circuit voltage (VOC): Remove the load and find the voltage across the open-circuited terminals.
	- Find the Thevenin resistance (RTH): Remove the voltage sources and find the resistance between the terminals.
- The Thevenin equivalent can be used to simplify the analysis of a complex network. It can replace the complex network with a simple voltage source and resistor which is easier to analyze.
- Applications: Thevenin theorem is widely used in circuit analysis to simplify circuits. It is also useful in determining the maximum power transfer and stability of circuits.

##### 3. RLC series resonance.

- An RLC circuit is said to be in resonance when it satisfies the condition:
1/√(LC) = R
At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is minimum and the current is maximum.
- The resonant frequency (fr) is given by: fr = 1/2π√(LC)
- The bandwidth of the resonant circuit is the range of frequencies over which the amplitude of the output is greater than 70.7% of the peak output amplitude. It depends upon the resistance (R) of the circuit. Higher the resistance, greater is the bandwidth.
- Series RLC circuit is often used as bandpass filter to allow a range of frequencies to pass through and attenuate others. The center frequency is the resonant frequency of the RLC circuit.
- The Q factor of a series RLC circuit is given by: Q = XL/R at resonance. Higher the Q factor, sharper is the resonance and narrower is the bandwidth.

##### 4. Measurement of power in 3- phase circuit by two wattmeter method and determination of its power factor for star as well as delta connected load.

-- Connect one wattmeter in phase A and other in phase B.

-- Power factor = Reading of wattmeter in A / Total real power
- For delta connected load:
-- Connect one wattmeter in phase AB and other in phase BC.
-- Note the readings of both wattmeters.
-- Total real power = Reading of wattmeter in AB - Reading of wattmeter in BC
-- Power factor = Reading of wattmeter in AB / Total real power
- The power factor can be determined by this method for balanced as well as unbalanced 3-phase loads both for star and delta connections. The measured power factor will be the average power factor for the circuit.
- This method is easy and economical to implement as only two single phase wattmeters are required but it cannot be used if all the 3-phases are not available for measurement.

##### 5. Determination of parameters of ac single phase series RLC circuit.

- An ac series RLC circuit consists of a resistor (R), inductor (L) and capacitor (C) connected in series with an ac supply.
- The current (I) flowing through the circuit is same at every point. The voltage drop (V) across each component is:
V_R = IR        (1)
V_L = IωL        (2)
V_C = I/ωC        (3)
- Where, ω is the angular frequency of supply.
- Equating supply voltage (V) to the sum of voltage drops, we get:
V = IR + IωL + I/ωC
        = I(R + ωL - 1/ωC)           ....(4)
- For resonance: R + ωL - 1/ωC = 0
- At resonance, current is maximum and voltage across the elements R, L and C are in the ratio of their impedances.
- The resonant frequency (ω_r) can be determined as:
ω_r = 1/√LC        (5)
- The parameters R, L and C can be determined by measuring voltage and current waveforms at resonance and using the relations:
R = V_R(max)/I(max)
L = V_L(max)/I(max)ω_r
C = I(max)/V_C(max)ω_r

##### CO 1 Conduct experiments illustrating the application of KVL/KCL and network theorems to DC electrical circuits. K3

1. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL): The algebraic sum of all voltages around any closed loop of a circuit is zero.
2. Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL): The algebraic sum of currents into and out of any node in an electrical circuit is zero.
3. Series resistor circuit: The same current flows through all components. The total resistance is the sum of individual resistances. Voltage drops are proportional to the individual resistances.
4. Parallel resistor circuit: The applied voltage is the same across all branches. The reciprocal of the equivalent resistance is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances. The current divides into the parallel branches inversely proportional to the resistances.
5. Series-parallel resistor circuits: The circuits can be reduced to series and parallel combinations using KVL and KCL and then simplified using rules for series and parallel resistances. The equivalent resistance and current can be calculated after simplification.
6. Experimental verification: The circuit laws and theorems can be verified experimentally using DC power supply, resistors, ammeter, and voltmeter. The measured quantities can be compared with theoretical calculations. The sources of errors must be identified and minimized.

##### CO2 Demonstrate the behavior of AC circuits connected to single phase AC supply and measure power in single phase as well as three phase electrical circuits. K4

1. AC circuits connected to single phase AC supply
- An AC circuit consists of a voltage source, wires and components like resistors, inductors and capacitors
- The voltage in a single phase AC circuit reverses polarity periodically, completing one cycle in a time period known as the period of the supply frequency
- The current also reverses direction with the voltage, flowing back and forth through the circuit
- The voltage and current are out of phase, with the current lagging the voltage due to the inductive nature of the circuit
- Power in AC circuits is calculated using complex numbers to account for the out of phase voltage and current

2. Power measurement in single phase and three phase AC circuits
- In single phase AC circuits, instantaneous power is calculated as product of instantaneous values of voltage and current
- In three phase AC circuits, line currents and voltages are out of phase, requiring the use of vector algebra to calculate real and reactive power
- A wattmeter can be used to directly measure real power in AC circuits, with one coil connected in series with the circuit to measure current and other coil connected in parallel to measure voltage
- Power factor correction capacitors are added in parallel with loads in AC circuits to improve the power factor and reduce losses
