##### Transmission Grating

- A transmission grating is a thin sheet with parallel slits evenly spaced that allows light to pass through. The slits diffract the light, separating it into different wavelengths that can be observed.
- As light passes through the slits, the light waves interfere constructively and destructively, diffracting the light into a spectrum. The narrower the slits, the greater the diffraction and the higher the spectral resolution.
- The diffraction angle depends on the wavelength of light, with shorter wavelengths diffracted at larger angles. By measuring the diffraction angle, the wavelength of light can be determined, enabling transmission gratings to be used as spectrometers to study light.
- Transmission gratings are useful for observing spectra in the visible light and ultraviolet range. They can achieve high spectral resolution with an accurate, evenly-spaced slit pattern. However, the slits can become clogged over time and the material can deform at high heat or with frequent use, reducing performance.
- Key characteristics of a transmission grating are the slit width, shape, and spacing, as well as the refractive index and thickness of the material. These properties determine the grating's spectral resolution, wavelength range, and light throughput. Transmission gratings are common in spectroscopy for scientific analysis and studying light.

##### Focal Length of Combination of Lenses

- When two or more thin lenses are placed in contact, the net focal length ($f$) can be calculated using the formula:
$$1/f = 1/f_1 + 1/f_2 + ....$$
where $f_1, f_2....$ are the focal lengths of the individual lenses.
- The sign convention used is:
$f$ is considered +ve if the lens combination behaves as a converging lens and -ve if it behaves as a diverging lens.
$f_1$ and $f_2$ are +ve if the individual lenses are converging and -ve if they are diverging.
- For example, if a converging lens of focal length +20cm is combined with another converging lens of focal length +15cm:
$$1/f = 1/+20 + 1/+15 = 0.05$$
$$f = \boxed{20}$$ cm
- The net focal length is shorter than either of the individual focal lengths, showing that the power of the combination is greater than the power of either lens.
- In general, a combination of lenses can act as either a converging or diverging lens depending on whether the individual focal lengths have the same or opposite signs. The net focal length can be decreased/increased (leading to a more/less powerful lens combination) by combining lenses of appropriate focal lengths and signs.

##### Carrying coil and estimate the radius of the coil

- A carrying coil is an electrical conductor loop that is free to rotate in a magnetic field. It is used to measure the rate of change of magnetic flux through the coil which generates an emf in the coil.
- The induced emf in the carrying coil is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux through the coil. emf = -N(d(phi)/dt) where N is the number of turns in the coil and phi is the magnetic flux.
- To maximize the emf induced in the carrying coil, the number of turns (N) should be large and the rate of change of magnetic flux (d(phi)/dt) should be high.
- The radius of the carrying coil depends on the purpose it is built for and the magnetic field conditions. A larger radius coil will enclose a larger magnetic flux and hence generate a higher emf. But the coil should not be too large that it slows down the rate of change of magnetic flux through it. An optimum radius value is chosen based on the magnetic field strength and rate of change of field or rotational speed of the coil.

##### 6. To study the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit.

- An LCR circuit consists of an inductor (L), a capacitor (C) and a resistor (R) connected in series.
- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is minimum. This occurs when the capacitive reactance (Xc) is equal in magnitude to the inductive reactance (Xl).
- The resonance condition is given by:

Xc = Xl

1/ωC = Lω

ω = 1/√LC

- The resonance frequency (ωr) depends only on the values of the inductance and capacitance.
- The Q-factor indicates the sharpness of the resonance and is given by:

##### Determine its hysteresis loss:

#### CO-1 Apply the principle of interference and diffraction to find the wavelength of

1. Interference: When two or more waves superimpose, their net displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements at that point. Conditions for interference:
- The two sources must be coherent
- The two waves must have the same frequency
- The two waves must have a constant phase difference

Interference can either be constructive (increases amplitude) or destructive (decreases amplitude).

Constructive interference occurs when the two waves are in phase (phase difference = 0, 2π, 4π...). The net displacement is the sum of the individual displacements.
Destructive interference occurs when the two waves are out of phase (phase difference = π, 3π...). The net displacement is the difference between the individual displacements.

2. Diffraction: When a wave passes through a narrow opening or around a barrier, it undergoes bending which results in the spreading out of waves. The diffraction pattern depends on the width of the opening/barrier and the wavelength of the wave.

Wavelength can be calculated using the interference/diffraction pattern and the geometry of the system. The locations of maxima/minima in the interference pattern or the positions of bright fringes in the diffraction pattern can be used to calculate the wavelength.

#### Monochromatic and Polychromatic Light

- Polychromatic light refers to light containing multiple wavelengths or colors. Sunlight and light from regular bulbs are polychromatic as they contain all the colors of the visible light spectrum.
- Monochromatic light is often used in scientific applications to isolate the effect of a single wavelength. For example, monochromatic red light can be used to only excite electrons in a material that interact with red light.

- In optics, the differences between monochromatic and polychromatic light must be accounted for when analyzing light-matter interactions and designing components/systems. The full spectrum of wavelengths in polychromatic light must be considered rather than just a single wavelength as in monochromatic light.
