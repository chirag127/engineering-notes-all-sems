### 1. To enable the students to understand about the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments

Analytical instruments are devices used to measure, analyze, and determine the physical and chemical properties of substances. These instruments are essential tools in various fields such as chemistry, biology, medicine, and environmental science. Some of the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments include:

- **Accuracy and precision**: Accuracy refers to how close a measurement is to the true value, while precision refers to how close repeated measurements are to each other. Both are important in analytical measurements.

- **Sensitivity**: Sensitivity refers to the ability of an instrument to detect small changes in the quantity being measured. A highly sensitive instrument can detect very small changes in the sample.

- **Selectivity**: Selectivity refers to the ability of an instrument to distinguish between different components in a sample. A highly selective instrument can accurately measure the quantity of a specific component in the presence of other components.

- **Calibration**: Calibration is the process of adjusting an instrument to ensure that its measurements are accurate. This is typically done by comparing the instrument's measurements to those of a known standard.

- **Signal-to-noise ratio**: The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is a measure of the quality of a measurement. It is the ratio of the signal (the quantity being measured) to the noise (unwanted variations in the measurement). A high SNR indicates a high-quality measurement.

These are some of the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments that students should understand. Understanding these concepts will enable students to use analytical instruments effectively and accurately in their work.

### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

Titrimetric method is a technique used to determine the pH of a solution. This method involves the use of a titrant, which is a solution of known concentration, to react with the solution being tested. The following are the steps involved in the determination of pH by titrimetric method:

1. **Preparation of the titrant**: The titrant is prepared by dissolving a known amount of a strong acid or base in a known volume of water. The concentration of the titrant is then calculated.

2. **Selection of an appropriate indicator**: An indicator is a substance that changes color at a specific pH range. The indicator is chosen based on the pH range of the solution being tested.

3. **Titration**: The solution being tested is placed in a flask, and the indicator is added. The titrant is then added dropwise from a burette while stirring the solution. The addition of the titrant is continued until the indicator changes color, indicating that the endpoint of the titration has been reached.

4. **Calculation of pH**: The volume of the titrant used is recorded, and the number of moles of the titrant is calculated. The number of moles of the acid or base in the solution being tested is then calculated using the stoichiometry of the reaction. The pH of the solution is then calculated using the concentration of the acid or base.

This method is accurate and precise, and it is commonly used in laboratories to determine the pH of solutions. However, it requires the use of specialized equipment and chemicals, and it may not be suitable for all applications.



### 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid

Surface tension is a property of liquids that arises due to the cohesive forces between the molecules at the surface. It is defined as the force acting per unit length perpendicular to the line of separation of the liquid from another phase. There are several methods to determine the surface tension of a given liquid, including:

1. **Maximum Bubble Pressure Method**: This method involves measuring the maximum pressure required to blow a bubble of air through a liquid. The surface tension is calculated using the Laplace equation.

2. **Drop Weight Method**: This method involves measuring the weight of a droplet of liquid that detaches from the end of a thin tube. The surface tension is calculated using the weight of the droplet and the radius of the tube.

3. **Wilhelmy Plate Method**: This method involves immersing a thin flat plate into a liquid and measuring the force required to detach it from the liquid. The surface tension is calculated using the force and the perimeter of the plate.

4. **Du Nouy Ring Method**: This method involves immersing a thin ring into a liquid and measuring the force required to detach it from the liquid. The surface tension is calculated using the force and the circumference of the ring.

5. **Capillary Rise Method**: This method involves measuring the height to which a liquid rises in a thin tube due to capillary action. The surface tension is calculated using the height of the liquid, the radius of the tube, and the density of the liquid.

Each of these methods has its own advantages and limitations, and the choice of method depends on the specific requirements of the experiment. It is important to carefully calibrate the instruments and control the experimental conditions to obtain accurate results.

# Determination of pH by Titrimetric Method

Titration is a laboratory technique used to determine the concentration of an unknown solution by reacting it with a solution of known concentration. The titrimetric method can be used to determine the pH of a solution.

1. **Principle:** The principle behind the titrimetric method for determining pH is the neutralization reaction between an acid and a base. A solution of known concentration, either an acid or a base, is added to the solution being tested until the reaction is complete. The volume of the known solution required to reach the endpoint of the reaction is used to calculate the concentration of the unknown solution, and thus its pH.

2. **Procedure:** The procedure for determining pH by titration involves the following steps:
    - A solution of known concentration, either an acid or a base, is prepared.
    - A sample of the solution being tested is placed in a flask or beaker.
    - An indicator is added to the solution being tested. The indicator changes color at the endpoint of the reaction.
    - The solution of known concentration is added to the solution being tested, drop by drop, while stirring.
    - The addition of the known solution is continued until the indicator changes color, indicating that the endpoint of the reaction has been reached.
    - The volume of the known solution required to reach the endpoint is recorded.
    - The concentration of the unknown solution is calculated using the volume of the known solution and the balanced chemical equation for the reaction.

3. **Calculations:** The concentration of the unknown solution can be calculated using the formula: C1V1 = C2V2, where C1 is the concentration of the known solution, V1 is the volume of the known solution, C2 is the concentration of the unknown solution, and V2 is the volume of the unknown solution. Once the concentration of the unknown solution is known, its pH can be calculated using the formula: pH = -log[H+], where [H+] is the concentration of hydrogen ions in the solution.

4. **Advantages:** The titrimetric method for determining pH is simple, accurate, and precise. It can be used to determine the pH of a wide range of solutions, including those that are colored or turbid.

5. **Limitations:** The titrimetric method for determining pH requires the use of an indicator, which may not be suitable for all solutions. The method also requires the preparation of a solution of known concentration, which may not always be available or practical.
