### 1. To enable the students to understand about the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments.

In this section, we will cover the basic concepts and principles of analytical instruments. Understanding these fundamental concepts is crucial for interpreting and analyzing experimental data accurately. 

Here are the key points that we will cover:

- **Measurement**: Analytical instruments are designed to measure physical or chemical properties of a sample, such as mass, volume, temperature, pH, conductivity, etc.

- **Accuracy**: Accuracy is the ability of an instrument to provide measurements that are close to the true value. It is affected by factors such as calibration, sensitivity, and precision.

- **Precision**: Precision is the degree of repeatability or reproducibility of a measurement. It is affected by factors such as instrument design, sample preparation, and data analysis.

- **Sensitivity**: Sensitivity is the smallest detectable change in the measured property. It is affected by factors such as signal-to-noise ratio, resolution, and dynamic range.

- **Selectivity**: Selectivity is the ability of an instrument to measure a specific property in the presence of other interfering substances. It is affected by factors such as sample preparation, analytical method, and instrument design.

- **Signal**: The signal is the measurable response of the instrument to the sample. It can be a physical, chemical, or electrical property.

- **Noise**: Noise is the random variation in the signal that is not related to the sample. It can be caused by external factors such as temperature, humidity, or electromagnetic interference.

- **Calibration**: Calibration is the process of adjusting the instrument to ensure accurate and precise measurements. It involves comparing the instrument response to a known standard or reference material.

- **Validation**: Validation is the process of verifying that the instrument and analytical method are suitable for the intended use. It involves testing the instrument performance on a range of samples with known properties.

By understanding these fundamental concepts, students will be able to select the appropriate analytical instrument for a given application, design experiments, and interpret the experimental results accurately.



### 2. Analysis of Chloride Content, Hardness, and Alkalinity of Water

Water is an essential natural resource, and its quality is crucial for human consumption and other purposes. The analysis of water quality parameters, such as chloride content, hardness, and alkalinity, is necessary to ensure that the water is safe for use. In this section, we will discuss the methods and importance of analyzing these parameters.

#### 2.1 Chloride Content Analysis

Chloride is a common anion found in water, and its concentration is usually measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L). The chloride content in water can indicate the presence of saltwater intrusion or contamination from industrial or agricultural activities. The following methods are used to analyze chloride content in water:

- Titration method: This involves the addition of silver nitrate solution to a water sample until all the chloride ions react with the silver ions. The endpoint is reached when there is no more silver ion left to react with the chloride ion. The amount of silver nitrate used is then calculated to determine the chloride content.
- Ion-selective electrode method: This involves the use of a specialized electrode that reacts with chloride ions and generates an electrical signal. The electrical signal is then measured to determine the chloride concentration.

#### 2.2 Hardness Analysis

Water hardness refers to the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water, usually measured in parts per million (ppm). Hard water can cause scaling in pipes and appliances, as well as affect the taste of water. The following methods are used to analyze water hardness:

- Titration method: This involves the addition of a chelating agent to a water sample that reacts with calcium and magnesium ions. The endpoint is reached when all the calcium and magnesium ions have reacted with the chelating agent. The amount of chelating agent used is then calculated to determine the hardness of the water.
- Atomic absorption spectroscopy: This involves the use of a specialized instrument that measures the absorption of light by calcium and magnesium ions in a water sample. The amount of light absorbed is then used to determine the hardness of the water.

#### 2.3 Alkalinity Analysis

Alkalinity is a measure of the buffering capacity of water, which refers to its ability to neutralize acids. Alkalinity is usually measured in ppm of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). The following methods are used to analyze alkalinity:

- Titration method: This involves the addition of an acid to a water sample until the alkalinity is neutralized. The amount of acid used is then calculated to determine the alkalinity of the water.
- Colorimetric method: This involves the use of a specialized instrument that measures the color change of a water sample when a specific reagent is added. The color change is then used to determine the alkalinity of the water.

In conclusion, the analysis of chloride content, hardness, and alkalinity of water is essential to ensure that the water is safe for human consumption and other purposes. The methods discussed in this section are commonly used in water quality analysis and are important for students to understand.

Adipic acid is a dicarboxylic acid that is commonly used in the production of nylon fibers and resins. It can be synthesized by two routes: conventional and green.

##### Conventional Route

The conventional route for adipic acid synthesis involves the following steps:

1. Oxidation of cyclohexane with nitric acid to form cyclohexanone and cyclohexanol.
2. Oxidation of cyclohexanone with nitric acid to form adipic acid.

This process requires high temperatures and pressures, and generates large amounts of waste and greenhouse gases.

##### Green Route

The green route for adipic acid synthesis aims to reduce the environmental impact of the conventional process by using renewable resources and minimizing waste. It involves the following steps:

1. Catalytic oxidation of cyclohexene with hydrogen peroxide to form cyclohexene oxide.
2. Hydrolysis of cyclohexene oxide with water to form adipic acid.

This process requires lower temperatures and pressures, and generates less waste and greenhouse gases.

#### Paracetamol

Paracetamol, also known as acetaminophen, is a widely used pain reliever and fever reducer. It can be synthesized by two routes: conventional and green.

##### Conventional Route

The conventional route for paracetamol synthesis involves the following steps:

1. Nitration of phenol with nitric acid to form 4-nitrophenol.
2. Reduction of 4-nitrophenol with sodium borohydride to form 4-aminophenol.
3. Acetylation of 4-aminophenol with acetic anhydride to form paracetamol.

This process requires the use of toxic and hazardous chemicals, and generates large amounts of waste.

##### Green Route

The green route for paracetamol synthesis aims to reduce the environmental impact of the conventional process by using safer and more sustainable methods. It involves the following steps:

1. Hydrogenation of nitrobenzene with hydrogen gas over a palladium catalyst to form aniline.
2. Acetylation of aniline with acetic anhydride to form paracetamol.

This process uses less hazardous chemicals, generates less waste, and has a higher atom economy.

In conclusion, the synthesis of organic compounds such as adipic acid and paracetamol can be achieved by both conventional and green routes. ### 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method.

Hardness of water is an important parameter to determine the quality of water. It is caused by the presence of dissolved minerals such as calcium and magnesium ions. Hard water can cause scaling in pipes, decrease soap efficiency, and affect the taste of water. Hence, it is important to determine the hardness of water before it is used for any purpose. EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) method is a commonly used method to determine the hardness of water. This method involves the use of a complexometric titration to determine the amount of calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample.

The following are the steps involved in the determination of hardness of water sample by EDTA method:

1. Take a water sample of known volume in a conical flask. Add a few drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator to the flask. The indicator will form a complex with calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample.

2. Titrate the water sample with EDTA solution. The EDTA will react with the calcium and magnesium ions, forming a stable complex. The endpoint of the titration is reached when the color of the solution changes from wine red to blue. This indicates that all the calcium and magnesium ions have been complexed with EDTA.

3. Note down the volume of EDTA solution used for titration. The amount of EDTA used will be equal to the amount of calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample.

4. Calculate the hardness of water sample in terms of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) using the following formula: 

    Hardness (in ppm) = (Volume of EDTA solution used x Molarity of EDTA solution x 100) / Volume of water sample taken

    Hardness (in mg/L) = Hardness (in ppm) / 10

    Hardness (in °dH) = Hardness (in mg/L) / 0.1

    where Molarity of EDTA solution = (Molecular weight of EDTA x Normality of EDTA solution) / 1000.

5. Repeat the above steps for multiple water samples to obtain an average hardness value. 

6. Compare the obtained hardness value with the permissible limits set by the regulatory authorities. 

In conclusion, the determination of hardness of water sample by EDTA method is a simple and effective method to determine the amount of calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample. This method provides accurate results and is widely used in water quality analysis.

4. Record the volume of acid used: Record the volume of the acid solution used to reach the endpoint. This volume is used to calculate the alkalinity of the water sample.

5. Calculate the alkalinity: Calculate the alkalinity of the water sample using the formula:

Alkalinity = (Volume of acid used x Normality of acid) x 50 / Volume of water sample

where:
- Volume of acid used is the volume of the standardized acid solution used to reach the endpoint.
- Normality of acid is the concentration of the standardized acid solution in normal units.
- Volume of water sample is the volume of the water sample used for the test in milliliters.

6. Interpret the results: The alkalinity of the water sample is expressed in units of mg/L or ppm of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). The acceptable range of alkalinity for drinking water is between 20-200 ppm.

In conclusion, the determination of alkalinity is an important test in the analysis of water quality. ### 5. Determination of Surface Tension of Given Liquid

Surface tension is a phenomenon that occurs at the interface of two immiscible phases, such as liquid-liquid or liquid-gas. It is defined as the force per unit length acting perpendicular to an imaginary line drawn on the surface of the liquid, and is measured in units of N/m. Surface tension plays an important role in a number of physical and biological processes, such as capillary action, wetting, and droplet formation.

The determination of surface tension of a given liquid can be carried out using various methods. Here are some of the commonly used techniques:

1. Capillary rise method: In this method, a narrow glass tube of known radius is immersed in the liquid, and the height to which the liquid rises in the tube is measured. The surface tension of the liquid can be calculated using the formula T = (2gh)/r, where T is the surface tension, h is the height of liquid rise, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and r is the radius of the tube.

2. Drop weight method: In this method, a drop of the liquid is allowed to fall from a height onto a horizontal surface, and the diameter of the resulting circular spot is measured. The surface tension of the liquid can be calculated using the formula T = (4mgh)/(πd^2), where T is the surface tension, m is the mass of the drop, g is the acceleration due to gravity, h is the height of fall, and d is the diameter of the spot.

3. Maximum bubble pressure method: In this method, a soap film is formed by blowing air through a soap solution, and the pressure required to burst the film is measured. The surface tension of the liquid can be calculated using the formula T = (4P)/(d), where T is the surface tension, P is the pressure required to burst the film, and d is the diameter of the soap bubble.

4. Wilhelmy plate method: In this method, a thin rectangular plate of known dimensions is partially immersed in the liquid, and the force required to detach the plate from the liquid surface is measured. The surface tension of the liquid can be calculated using the formula T = (Fcosθ)/l, where T is the surface tension, F is the force required to detach the plate, θ is the contact angle between the plate and the liquid surface, and l is the length of the plate.

It is important to note that the determination of surface tension using any of these methods requires careful experimental setup and measurement, as well as consideration of factors such as temperature, atmospheric pressure, and impurities in the liquid. Additionally, the choice of method may depend on the properties of the liquid being studied and the desired accuracy of the measurement.
