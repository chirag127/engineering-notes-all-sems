### 1. Study of various types of Active & Passive Components based on their ratings.

- Active components are those that can amplify or control the flow of electric current or voltage. They usually require an external source of energy to operate. Examples of active components are transistors, diodes, integrated circuits, etc.
- Passive components are those that do not amplify or control the electric current or voltage. They only store or dissipate energy. Examples of passive components are resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.
- The ratings of components are the values of electrical parameters that specify their performance and limitations. They are usually given by the manufacturers or standards. Some common ratings of components are:
  - Power rating: the maximum amount of power that a component can handle without being damaged or overheated. It is measured in watts (W) or milliwatts (mW).
  - Voltage rating: the maximum amount of voltage that a component can withstand across its terminals without breaking down or arcing. It is measured in volts (V) or millivolts (mV).
  - Current rating: the maximum amount of current that a component can carry without exceeding its power rating or causing excessive heat. It is measured in amperes (A) or milliamperes (mA).
  - Resistance rating: the amount of opposition that a component offers to the flow of electric current. It is measured in ohms (Ω) or kiloohms (kΩ).
  - Capacitance rating: the amount of electric charge that a component can store per unit of voltage. It is measured in farads (F) or microfarads (µF).
  - Inductance rating: the amount of magnetic flux that a component can generate per unit of current. It is measured in henrys (H) or millihenrys (mH).
  - Frequency rating: the range of frequencies that a component can operate at without losing its functionality or efficiency. It is measured in hertz (Hz) or kilohertz (kHz).



### 2. Identification of various types of Printed Circuit Boards (PCB) and soldering Techniques.

- A printed circuit board (PCB) is a thin board made of fiberglass, composite epoxy, or other laminate material, on which electrical components are mounted and connected by copper traces.
- PCBs are used to create electronic circuits, such as in computers, radios, TVs, and other devices.
- There are different types of PCBs, depending on the number of layers, the mounting method, and the design complexity.
- Some common types of PCBs are:

  - Single-sided PCB: A PCB with only one layer of copper traces on one side of the board. The components are either soldered directly to the board or mounted on the other side using through-hole technology.
  - Double-sided PCB: A PCB with two layers of copper traces, one on each side of the board. The components are either soldered on both sides or connected by vias, which are holes that allow electrical signals to pass through the board.
  - Multilayer PCB: A PCB with more than two layers of copper traces, sandwiched between layers of insulating material. The components are soldered on the outer layers or connected by vias. Multilayer PCBs can have higher density, complexity, and performance than single-sided or double-sided PCBs.
  - Rigid PCB: A PCB that is stiff and cannot be bent or flexed. Rigid PCBs are usually made of fiberglass or epoxy. They are suitable for applications that require high reliability and durability.
  - Flexible PCB: A PCB that is thin and flexible, and can be bent or folded to fit into different shapes and spaces. Flexible PCBs are usually made of polyimide or polyester. They are suitable for applications that require lightweight, low-profile, or conformable designs.
  - Rigid-flex PCB: A PCB that combines rigid and flexible sections, connected by flexible interconnects. Rigid-flex PCBs can offer the advantages of both rigid and flexible PCBs, such as high reliability, low weight, and complex shapes.

- Soldering is the process of joining two or more metal parts by melting and flowing a filler metal (solder) into the joint. Soldering is used to create electrical connections between components and PCBs, as well as to repair or modify existing circuits.
- There are different types of soldering techniques, depending on the type of solder, the type of components, and the type of PCB. Some common types of soldering techniques are:

  - Through-hole soldering: A soldering technique that involves inserting the leads of a component through holes in the PCB, and then applying solder to the exposed leads on the other side of the board. Through-hole soldering is used for components that have long and sturdy leads, such as resistors, capacitors, and transistors.
  - Surface-mount soldering: A soldering technique that involves placing the components on the surface of the PCB, and then applying solder to the pads or contacts on the board. Surface-mount soldering is used for components that have short and thin leads, or no leads at all, such as integrated circuits, diodes, and LEDs.
  - Reflow soldering: A soldering technique that involves applying solder paste (a mixture of solder and flux) to the pads or contacts on the PCB, placing the components on the paste, and then heating the entire assembly to melt the solder and form the joints. Reflow soldering is used for mass production of surface-mount PCBs, as it can solder many components at once and ensure consistent quality.
  - Wave soldering: A soldering technique that involves passing the PCB over a wave of molten solder, which wets the pads or contacts and the leads of the components. Wave soldering is used for mass production of through-hole or mixed PCBs, as it can solder many joints at once and reduce the soldering time.
  - Hand soldering: A soldering technique that involves using a soldering iron (a tool that heats up a metal tip) and a solder wire (a thin metal wire that contains solder and flux) to manually solder each joint. Hand soldering is used for prototyping, repairing, or modifying PCBs, as it allows more control and precision than other soldering techniques.

A printed circuit board (PCB) is a board that electrically connects and mechanically supports electronic components using conductive tracks, pads, and other features. PCBs are widely used in various electronic devices and circuits.

The process of making a PCB involves the following steps:

- Artwork and printing: This is the design and layout of the PCB, which can be done using software tools or manually. The artwork shows the position and size of the components, the traces, the pads, the holes, and the labels. The artwork is then printed on a special paper or film, called the mask, which is used to transfer the pattern onto the copper-clad board.
- Etching: This is the removal of the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the desired traces and pads. The etching can be done using chemical solutions, such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate, or using physical methods, such as laser or plasma. The mask is placed over the board and the etching solution is applied, dissolving the exposed copper. The mask is then removed and the board is rinsed and dried.
- Drilling: This is the creation of holes in the board, which are used to mount the components and connect the traces. The drilling can be done using a drill press, a CNC machine, or a hand drill. The size and location of the holes are determined by the artwork. The board is then cleaned and inspected for any defects or errors.

### 5. Soldering shop: Soldering and disordering of Resistor in PCB. Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB. Soldering and disordering of Capacitor in PCB

Soldering is a process of joining two or more metal parts by melting and flowing a filler metal (solder) into the joint. Soldering is used to create electrical connections between components on a printed circuit board (PCB).

Disordering is a process of removing soldered components from a PCB by heating and melting the solder joint and pulling out the component. Disordering is used to replace faulty or damaged components or to modify a circuit.

The following are some general steps and tips for soldering and disordering of resistor, IC and capacitor in PCB:

- **Soldering and disordering of resistor in PCB**
  - Resistor is a passive component that limits the flow of current in a circuit. It has two leads that are soldered to the PCB pads.
  - To solder a resistor, first clean the PCB pads and the resistor leads with a flux or a soldering paste. Then, heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip. Next, place the resistor on the PCB and align the leads with the pads. Hold the soldering iron on one lead and the pad for a few seconds until the solder melts and flows. Repeat the same for the other lead. Finally, cut off the excess lead with a wire cutter and inspect the solder joint for any defects.
  - To disorder a resistor, first heat up the disordering iron or the soldering iron with a disordering pump. Then, place the tip of the iron or the pump on one lead and the pad and wait for the solder to melt. Next, quickly pull out the lead with a pair of tweezers or a disordering tool. Repeat the same for the other lead. Finally, clean the PCB pads with a flux or a soldering paste and remove any solder residue with a disordering braid or a disordering wick.

- **Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB**
  - IC (integrated circuit) is a component that contains many electronic devices on a single chip. It has multiple pins that are soldered to the PCB pads or sockets.
  - To solder an IC, first clean the PCB pads or sockets and the IC pins with a flux or a soldering paste. Then, heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip. Next, place the IC on the PCB and align the pins with the pads or sockets. Hold the soldering iron on one pin and the pad or socket for a few seconds until the solder melts and flows. Repeat the same for the other pins. Finally, inspect the solder joints for any defects or bridges.
  - To disorder an IC, first heat up the disordering iron or the soldering iron with a disordering pump. Then, place the tip of the iron or the pump on one pin and the pad or socket and wait for the solder to melt. Next, quickly pull out the pin with a pair of tweezers or a disordering tool. Repeat the same for the other pins. Finally, clean the PCB pads or sockets with a flux or a soldering paste and remove any solder residue with a disordering braid or a disordering wick.

- **Soldering and disordering of capacitor in PCB**
  - Capacitor is a passive component that stores electric charge in a circuit. It has two leads that are soldered to the PCB pads.
  - To solder a capacitor, first clean the PCB pads and the capacitor leads with a flux or a soldering paste. Then, heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip. Next, place the capacitor on the PCB and align the leads with the pads. Make sure to observe the polarity of the capacitor if it is polarized. Hold the soldering iron on one lead and the pad for a few seconds until the solder melts and flows. Repeat the same for the other lead. Finally, cut off the excess lead with a wire cutter and inspect the solder joint for any defects.

- A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It has two terminals, anode and cathode, and a junction between them.
- A rectifier is a circuit that converts alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC). Rectifiers are used in power supplies, radio receivers, and other electronic devices that require DC voltage.
- A half-wave rectifier is a rectifier that uses only one diode and passes only one half-cycle of the AC input voltage to the output. The other half-cycle is blocked by the diode. The output voltage is pulsating DC, which has a lot of fluctuations and gaps.
- A full-wave rectifier is a rectifier that uses two diodes and passes both half-cycles of the AC input voltage to the output. The diodes are connected in such a way that they reverse the polarity of the negative half-cycle and make it positive. The output voltage is also pulsating DC, but it has less fluctuations and gaps than the half-wave rectifier.
- Vrms is the root mean square value of the AC input voltage. It is the effective value of the AC voltage that produces the same heating effect as a DC voltage of the same magnitude. Vrms can be calculated by dividing the peak value of the AC voltage by the square root of 2.
- Vdc is the average value of the DC output voltage. It is the value of the DC voltage that is equivalent to the area under the curve of the pulsating DC voltage. Vdc can be calculated by multiplying the peak value of the AC voltage by a factor that depends on the type of rectifier. For half-wave rectifier, the factor is 0.318, and for full-wave rectifier, the factor is 0.636.
- Ripple factor is a measure of the amount of AC component present in the DC output voltage. It is the ratio of the root mean square value of the AC component to the average value of the DC component. Ripple factor can be calculated by dividing the root mean square value of the AC component by the average value of the DC component. ### 4. Characteristics of Zener diode: V-I characteristics of zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode that is designed to operate in the reverse direction when the voltage across its terminals reaches a certain value, called the Zener voltage  .
- A Zener diode is heavily doped, which means it has a very thin depletion region and a high concentration of impurities in the p-n junction.
- A Zener diode has two modes of operation: forward biased and reverse biased   .
  - In forward biased mode, the Zener diode behaves like a normal diode, allowing current to flow from the anode to the cathode when the applied voltage is greater than the forward voltage (about 0.7 V for silicon diodes)   .
  - In reverse biased mode, the Zener diode blocks the current flow until the applied voltage reaches the Zener voltage, at which point the diode breaks down and allows a large current to flow in the opposite direction   .

### 5. Characteristic of BJT: BJT in CE configuration.

- BJT stands for bipolar junction transistor, which is a type of semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electrical signals.
- A BJT has three terminals: the base (B), the collector (C), and the emitter (E).
- A BJT can be classified into two types: NPN and PNP, depending on the arrangement of the N-type and P-type semiconductor materials in the device.
- In the CE configuration, the base terminal is the input, the collector terminal is the output, and the emitter terminal is the common terminal for both input and output.
- The CE configuration is the most widely used configuration for BJT amplifiers, because it provides high voltage gain, high current gain, high input impedance, and low output impedance.
- The characteristic curves of a BJT in CE configuration show the relationship between the input current (IB), the output current (IC), and the output voltage (VCE).
- The characteristic curves can be divided into three regions: the cutoff region, the active region, and the saturation region.
- In the cutoff region, the base current is zero or very small, and the output current is also zero or very small. The output voltage is equal to the supply voltage (VCC).
- In the active region, the base current is positive for NPN and negative for PNP, and the output current is proportional to the base current. The output voltage is less than the supply voltage, and depends on the load resistance (RL).
- In the saturation region, the base current is large enough to make the output current reach its maximum value, which is limited by the supply voltage and the load resistance. The output voltage is very small, and close to zero.



### 6. To study Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain, direct-coupled electronic circuit that can perform various linear operations such as amplification, filtering, differentiation, integration, etc.
- An op-amp has two input terminals: the inverting input (-) and the non-inverting input (+), and one output terminal. It also has two power supply terminals: the positive supply (+V) and the negative supply (-V).
- An op-amp can be used as an adder or a subtractor by connecting resistors to its input and output terminals. The resistors determine the gain and the polarity of the output signal with respect to the input signals.
- An op-amp adder is a circuit that produces an output voltage that is proportional to the sum of the input voltages. It can be implemented by connecting the input voltages to the inverting input of the op-amp through resistors of equal value, and connecting a feedback resistor from the output to the inverting input. The output voltage is given by:

  Vout = -Rf/R1(V1 + V2 + ... + Vn)

  where Rf is the feedback resistor, R1 is the input resistor, and V1, V2, ... Vn are the input voltages.
- An op-amp subtractor is a circuit that produces an output voltage that is proportional to the difference of two input voltages. It can be implemented by connecting one input voltage to the non-inverting input of the op-amp and the other input voltage to the inverting input of the op-amp through resistors of equal value, and connecting a feedback resistor from the output to the inverting input. The output voltage is given by:

  Vout = Rf/R1(V2 - V1)

  where Rf is the feedback resistor, R1 is the input resistor, and V1 and V2 are the input voltages.
- The advantages of using op-amp as adder and subtractor are:
  - The circuit is simple and easy to design.
  - The circuit has high input impedance and low output impedance, which means it does not load the input sources or the output load.
  - The circuit has high accuracy and linearity, which means it can perform precise arithmetic operations.
  - The circuit has high common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR), which means it can reject the noise or interference that is common to both input terminals.
