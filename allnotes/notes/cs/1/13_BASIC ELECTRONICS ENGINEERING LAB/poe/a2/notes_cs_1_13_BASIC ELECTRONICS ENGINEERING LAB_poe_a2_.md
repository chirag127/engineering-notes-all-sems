### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

1. When P-N junction is reverse biased, the depletion layer widens and acts as an insulator. The reverse saturation current flows through the diode which is very small. The reverse resistance is very high.
2. When P-N junction is forward biased, the depletion layer narrows down and electrons flow from N to P and holes flow from P to N. The forward resistance is very low.
3. The voltage-current characteristics of PN junction diode can be plotted between voltage across the diode (V) and current through the diode (I).
4. The point where V=0 and I!=0, the curve shows the forward breakover voltage (V<sub>bd</sub>) and saturation current (I<sub>o</sub>).
5. The point where V=0 and I=0, gives the cut-in voltage (V<sub>t</sub>) or threshold voltage of PN junction diode.
6. For small values of forward voltage V< V<sub>t</sub>, the resistance is infinite and for V> V<sub>t</sub>, the resistance decreases rapidly. This shows the static resistance characteristics of the diode.
7. The dynamic resistance is obtained from the slope of the Voltage-Current characteristics curve. The slope gives the incremental resistance which decreases rapidly near the cut-in voltage and becomes almost constant for higher currents showing almost constant dynamic resistance.

### 4. Characteristics of Zener Diode: V-I characteristics of zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

1. Voltage-Current (V-I) characteristics:
- Zener diode exhibits almost same V-I characteristics as a normal PN junction diode in forward biased condition.
- In reverse biased condition, at a particular reverse voltage (called Zener voltage or breakdown voltage), the reverse current increases sharply. This sharp increase in current is due to avalanche effect and denotes the onset of Zener effect.
- The reverse saturation current is very small in zener diode.
- The V-I characteristics remain almost vertical in the Zener region which signifies almost constant voltage. Hence, zener diode acts as a voltage regulator for the voltages above the Zener voltage.

2. Graphical measurement of forward & reverse resistance:
- The forward resistance can be measured from the forward bias I-V curve. It is the slope of the curve near the origin.
- The reverse resistance is infinite for voltages less than Zener voltage. The sharp decrease in the slope of I-V curve gives the Zener resistance which can be measured at Zener voltage. The Zener resistance is very small.
- The Zener and forward resistances vary with temperature and hence zener diode requires temperature compensation circuits for precision voltage regulation.

### PN Junction on diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode-Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

**PN Junction:** A PN junction is formed by joining p-type and n-type semiconductors together. At the junction, holes from p-type and electrons from n-type combine and cancel each other resulting in a depletion region with no mobile charge carriers. This creates a potential barrier.

**Characteristics of PN Junction diode:**

1. Forward bias: When positive voltage is applied to p-side and negative to n-side, the potential barrier is reduced. This allows electrons and holes to recombine, causing current to flow through the diode.
2. Reverse bias: When negative voltage is applied to p-side and positive to n-side, the potential barrier is increased. This prevents electron-hole recombination and blocks current through the diode.
3. Breakdown region: At sufficiently high reverse bias, the diode breaks down and current starts flowing. This is undesirable and damages the diode.

**Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph:**
The static resistance of a diode can be measured from the forward bias region of its characteristic graph. The slope of the linear portion gives the static resistance.
The dynamic resistance is the differential of voltage over current and can be measured from the exponential portion of the forward bias curve. It decreases with increase in current.

### Verification of Truth Table of Various Logic Gates

- AND Gate: When both the inputs are high (1), the output is high (1). In all other cases, the output is low (0).
- OR Gate: When one or both the inputs are high (1), the output is high (1). The output is low (0) only when both inputs are low (0).
- NOT Gate: The output is the inverse of the input. When the input is high (1), the output is low (0) and vice versa.
- NAND Gate: The output of a NAND gate is the inverse of the AND gate. The output is high (1) when both inputs are low (0). In all other cases, the output is low (0).
- NOR Gate: The output of a NOR gate is the inverse of the OR gate. The output is high (1) only when both inputs are low (0). In all other cases, the output is low (0).
- XOR Gate: The output is high (1) when the two inputs differ (one input is high (1) and the other is low (0)). The output is low (0) when both inputs are the same (both high (1) or both low (0)).

The truth tables provide a complete picture of the outputs generated from all possible combinations of inputs. Verification of the truth table ensures that the logic gate functions as specified.

- POS form tends to use less number of gates for larger number of variables.
- SOP form leads to faster operation but more number of gates.
- POS form leads to less number of gates but slower operation.
