

# B.Sc. (Hons.) in Environmental Science

Environmental Science is an interdisciplinary field that covers the study of the natural environment, its processes, and how human beings interact with it. A Bachelor of Science (Honors) degree in Environmental Science is a four-year undergraduate program that aims to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of the environment.

Here are some of the subjects that are covered in the B.Sc. (Hons.) in Environmental Science program:

## Environmental Chemistry

Environmental Chemistry is the study of chemical processes that occur in the environment. This subject covers the chemical reactions that take place in the air, water, and soil, and the impact of pollutants on the environment. Some of the topics covered in this subject are:

- Atmospheric Chemistry
- Water Chemistry
- Soil Chemistry
- Environmental Toxicology

## Ecology

Ecology is the study of the relationships between organisms and their environment. This subject covers the interaction between living organisms and their physical surroundings. Some of the topics covered in this subject are:

- Ecosystems
- Biodiversity
- Conservation Biology
- Restoration Ecology

## Environmental Physics

Environmental Physics is the study of physical processes that occur in the environment. This subject covers the study of energy, radiation, and the behavior of matter in the environment. Some of the topics covered in this subject are:

- Climate Change
- Renewable Energy
- Optics
- Atmospheric Physics

## Environmental Policy and Management

Environmental Policy and Management is the study of how to manage and protect the environment. This subject covers the development of policies and regulations to protect the environment, and the management of natural resources. Some of the topics covered in this subject are:

- Environmental Law
- Environmental Economics
- Environmental Ethics
- Sustainable Development

## Environmental Geology

Environmental Geology is the study of the interaction between the Earth and the environment. This subject covers the study of the geological processes that shape the Earth, and the impact of human activities on the environment. Some of the topics covered in this subject are:

- Geomorphology
- Hydrogeology
- Environmental Geophysics
- Geological Hazards

In conclusion, a B.Sc. (Hons.) degree in Environmental Science covers a wide range of subjects that are aimed at providing students with a comprehensive understanding of the environment. The program prepares students for a career in environmental research, environmental policy, or environmental management.



## Unit 1 - Environment and Ecosystem

### Environment
- **Definition:** Environment refers to the surroundings, which includes both living and non-living things, that affect an organism's life and development.
- **Types of Environment:** There are two types of environment: natural environment and human-made environment.
- **Components of Environment:** The components of the environment are air, water, soil, living organisms, and physical factors such as temperature, light, and pressure.
- **Segments of Environment:** There are five segments of the environment: atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, biosphere, and anthroposphere.
- **Scope and Importance:** The environment plays a crucial role in the survival of all living organisms, including humans. It provides natural resources, regulates the climate, and influences the overall well-being of the planet.
- **Need for Public Awareness:** Public awareness is essential to promote environmental protection, conservation, and sustainable development. It involves educating the public about the importance of the environment and encouraging them to adopt environmentally friendly practices.

### Ecosystem
- **Definition:** An ecosystem is a community of living organisms (plants, animals, and microorganisms) interacting with each other and their non-living environment.
- **Types of Ecosystem:** There are two types of ecosystems: terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems.
- **Structure of Ecosystem:** The structure of the ecosystem includes producers (plants), consumers (animals), and decomposers (bacteria and fungi).
- **Food Chain:** A food chain represents the flow of energy and nutrients from one organism to another. It starts with a producer and ends with a top predator.
- **Food Web:** A food web is a network of interconnected food chains and represents the complex interactions between different organisms in an ecosystem.
- **Ecological Pyramid:** An ecological pyramid shows the relative amount of energy or biomass at each trophic level in an ecosystem.
- **Balance Ecosystem:** A balanced ecosystem maintains a stable population of different species and ensures the survival of all living organisms.
- **Effects of Human Activities:** Human activities such as food, shelter, housing, agriculture, industry, mining, transportation, economic and social security have a significant impact on the environment. They cause pollution, deforestation, loss of biodiversity, and climate change.
- **Environmental Impact Assessment:** Environmental Impact Assessment is a process that evaluates the potential environmental impacts of a proposed project or development.
- **Sustainable Development:** Sustainable development is a development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It involves promoting economic growth, social development, and environmental protection.



## Unit 2 - Natural Resources: Introduction, Classification

Natural resources refer to the resources that are derived from the environment and are considered valuable in economic, social, and cultural terms. These resources can be classified into four major categories: water resources, mineral resources, energy resources, and forest resources.

### Water Resources

- Water resources refer to the sources of water that are available for human use.
- These resources can be classified into two categories: surface water and groundwater.
- Surface water includes rivers, lakes, and reservoirs, while groundwater is found underground in rocks and soil.
- Availability of water resources is a major concern in many regions of the world due to factors such as climate change, increasing population, and pollution.
- Quality aspects of water include its physical, chemical, and biological properties that affect its suitability for human use.
- Waterborne diseases are caused by pathogens that are transmitted through contaminated water. Examples of waterborne diseases include cholera, typhoid, and dysentery.
- Water-induced diseases are caused by environmental factors such as floods and droughts that affect the availability and quality of water resources.
- Fluoride and arsenic problems in drinking water are major health concerns in many regions of the world. Excessive levels of fluoride in drinking water can cause dental and skeletal fluorosis, while arsenic can cause cancer and other health problems.

### Mineral Resources

- Mineral resources refer to the materials that are derived from the earth's crust and are used for economic purposes.
- These resources can be classified into two categories: metallic minerals and non-metallic minerals.
- Material cycles refer to the processes by which minerals are extracted from the earth, used for economic purposes, and eventually returned to the environment through natural processes.
- The carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur cycles are examples of material cycles that are important for maintaining the balance of nutrients in the environment.

### Energy Resources

- Energy resources refer to the sources of energy that are used to power human activities.
- Conventional sources of energy include fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and natural gas.
- Non-conventional sources of energy include renewable sources such as solar, wind, and hydroelectric power.
- The use of energy resources has significant environmental impacts, including air and water pollution, climate change, and habitat destruction.

### Forest Resources

- Forest resources refer to the forests and other wooded lands that provide a variety of economic, social, and environmental benefits.
- Availability of forest resources is a major concern due to deforestation caused by human activities such as logging, agriculture, and urbanization.
- Depletion of forests has significant environmental impacts, including soil erosion, loss of biodiversity, and climate change.
- The environmental impact of forest depletion on society includes impacts on water resources, air quality, and food security.



## Unit 3 - Pollution and their Effects; Public Health Aspects of Environmental; Water Pollution, Air Pollution, Soil Pollution, Noise Pollution, Solid waste management.

Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances into the environment that cause damage to living organisms and the natural world. Pollution can have negative effects on human health and the environment. In this unit, we will be discussing the different types of pollution and their effects on public health.

### Water Pollution
- Water pollution is the contamination of water bodies, such as rivers, lakes, and oceans, by human activities.
- Sources of water pollution include industrial waste, sewage, agricultural runoff, and oil spills.
- Water pollution can have harmful effects on aquatic life and can also cause water-borne diseases in humans.

### Air Pollution
- Air pollution is the presence of harmful substances in the air we breathe, such as particulate matter and gases like carbon monoxide and sulfur dioxide.
- Sources of air pollution include vehicles, industrial processes, and burning of fossil fuels.
- Air pollution can cause respiratory problems, cardiovascular disease, and other health problems in humans.

### Soil Pollution
- Soil pollution is the contamination of soil by harmful substances, such as heavy metals, pesticides, and chemicals.
- Sources of soil pollution include industrial waste, agricultural practices, and landfills.
- Soil pollution can have negative effects on plant growth and can also contaminate the food chain.

### Noise Pollution
- Noise pollution is the presence of loud or unpleasant sounds in the environment that can be harmful to human health.
- Sources of noise pollution include traffic, industrial processes, and construction sites.
- Noise pollution can cause hearing loss, stress, and other health problems in humans.

### Solid Waste Management
- Solid waste management involves the collection, transportation, and disposal of waste materials.
- Improper solid waste management can lead to environmental pollution and human health problems.
- Proper solid waste management practices include recycling, composting, and safe disposal methods.

In conclusion, pollution can have negative effects on public health and the environment. It is important to understand the different types of pollution and their sources in order to take steps towards reducing pollution and protecting public health.



## Unit 4 - Current Environmental Issues of Importance

In this unit, we will discuss some of the most significant environmental issues that affect our planet. These issues have far-reaching consequences and require immediate attention to mitigate their impact on the environment and human health. The topics we will cover in this unit include:

- Global Warming: It is the gradual increase in the Earth's surface temperature due to the emission of greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide. The burning of fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial activities are the primary contributors to global warming.

- Greenhouse Effects: It refers to the trapping of heat by gases in the atmosphere, which helps to maintain the Earth's temperature. However, the increased concentration of greenhouse gases has led to an enhanced greenhouse effect, causing global warming.

- Climate Change: It is the long-term alteration of the Earth's climate due to human activities like burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrialization. Climate change has severe consequences like rising sea levels, extreme weather events, and loss of biodiversity.

- Acid Rain: It is a form of precipitation that contains high levels of acid due to the emission of sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxide gases. Acid rain has harmful effects on aquatic life and vegetation, and it also contributes to soil erosion.

- Ozone Layer Formation and Depletion: The ozone layer is a protective layer in the Earth's atmosphere that absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation from the sun. The use of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) in refrigerators and air conditioners has led to the depletion of the ozone layer, which can cause skin cancer and other health problems.

- Population Growth: The world's population is increasing rapidly, leading to a strain on natural resources like water, food, and energy. The growing population also contributes to pollution and deforestation.

- Automobile Pollution: The exhaust emissions from vehicles contain harmful pollutants like carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxide, and particulate matter. These pollutants have adverse effects on human health and the environment.

- Burning of Paddy Straw: The burning of paddy straw after the harvest season is a significant source of air pollution in India. The smoke released contains harmful pollutants like carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, and particulate matter, which have adverse effects on human health and the environment.

In conclusion, these environmental issues have severe consequences on the planet's health and human well-being. It is essential to take immediate action to mitigate their impact on the environment and work towards sustainable development.



## Unit 5 - Environmental Protection

This unit focuses on the Environmental Protection Act 1986, initiatives by non-governmental organizations (NGOs), human population and the environment including population growth, environmental education, and women education. Here are some important points to keep in mind:

### Environmental Protection Act 1986

- The Environmental Protection Act of 1986 was enacted to provide for the protection and improvement of the environment.
- The act aims to prevent and control pollution and promote the conservation of natural resources.
- It establishes various rules and regulations for the management of hazardous substances, waste management, and the protection of wildlife.
- The act also provides for the establishment of environmental courts to deal with environmental disputes and offences.

### Initiatives by Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs)

- NGOs play a significant role in environmental protection by raising awareness and advocating for environmental conservation.
- They undertake various initiatives such as afforestation, waste management, and conservation of endangered species.
- NGOs also work towards promoting sustainable development and encouraging the use of renewable energy sources.

### Human Population and the Environment

- Population growth has a significant impact on the environment.
- The increase in population leads to an increase in demand for resources such as water, food, and energy.
- The depletion of natural resources and the increase in pollution are some of the negative effects of population growth.
- Environmental education and women education are important tools for addressing the issue of population growth and promoting sustainable development.

### Environmental Education

- Environmental education is the process of raising awareness and understanding of the environment and its issues.
- It aims to promote responsible behavior towards the environment and encourage the adoption of sustainable practices.
- Environmental education can be integrated into the curriculum of schools and can also be provided through various awareness campaigns and activities.

### Women Education

- Women education plays a crucial role in environmental protection.
- Educated women are more likely to be aware of environmental issues and are better equipped to make informed decisions regarding the environment.
- Women also play a significant role in sustainable development as they are often the primary caregivers and managers of natural resources in households and communities.

In conclusion, the Environmental Protection Act of 1986, initiatives by NGOs, human population and the environment, environmental education, and women education are all important aspects of environmental protection. It is essential to understand their significance and work towards promoting sustainable development and conserving the environment.
