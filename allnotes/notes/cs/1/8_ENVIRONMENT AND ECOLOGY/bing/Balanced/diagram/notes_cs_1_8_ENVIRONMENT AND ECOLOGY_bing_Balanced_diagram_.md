# Unit 1 - Environment and Ecosystem

## Environment: Definition, Types of Environment, Components of environment, Segments of environment, Scope and importance, Need for Public Awareness

- Environment is the sum total of all the physical, chemical, biological and social factors that surround and influence an organism or a group of organisms.
- Types of environment:
  - Natural environment: It includes the natural resources, such as air, water, land, soil, minerals, plants, animals, etc. that are essential for the survival and well-being of living beings.
  - Man-made environment: It includes the human-made structures, such as buildings, roads, bridges, dams, industries, etc. that are created to fulfill the needs and wants of human beings.
  - Social environment: It includes the cultural, political, economic, legal, ethical and religious aspects of human society that affect the behavior and interactions of individuals and groups.
- Components of environment:
  - Abiotic components: They are the non-living physical and chemical factors, such as temperature, light, moisture, pressure, wind, etc. that influence the living organisms.
  - Biotic components: They are the living organisms, such as plants, animals, microorganisms, etc. that interact with each other and with the abiotic components.
- Segments of environment:
  - Atmosphere: It is the layer of gases that surrounds the earth and protects it from the harmful radiation of the sun and the outer space.
  - Hydrosphere: It is the water component of the earth, which covers about 71% of the earth's surface and includes oceans, seas, rivers, lakes, glaciers, etc.
  - Lithosphere: It is the solid component of the earth, which forms the crust and the upper mantle and includes rocks, soils, minerals, etc.
  - Biosphere: It is the zone of life on earth, which includes all the living organisms and their interactions with the atmosphere, hydrosphere and lithosphere.
- Scope and importance of environment:
  - Environment provides the basic necessities of life, such as food, water, air, shelter, etc. to all the living beings.
  - Environment maintains the ecological balance and the biodiversity of the earth, which are essential for the stability and sustainability of the natural systems.
  - Environment offers various opportunities and challenges for the human development and welfare, such as natural resources, natural hazards, environmental problems, etc.
  - Environment influences the health, behavior, culture, economy and politics of human society, and vice versa.
- Need for public awareness of environment:
  - Public awareness of environment is the knowledge, attitude and action of the people towards the environment and its issues.
  - Public awareness of environment is necessary to:
    - Understand the interdependence and interrelationship of the human beings and the environment.
    - Appreciate the value and significance of the environment and its resources.
    - Recognize the causes and consequences of the environmental problems and their solutions.
    - Participate in the environmental conservation and management activities and policies.
    - Promote the environmental education and communication among the various stakeholders and sectors of the society.

### Introduction
- Natural resources are the materials and substances that are found in nature and can be used for economic and social purposes.
- Natural resources can be classified into renewable and non-renewable resources based on their availability and rate of regeneration.
- Renewable resources are those that can be replenished or regenerated in a short period of time, such as solar energy, wind energy, biomass, water, etc.
- Non-renewable resources are those that have a limited supply and take a long time to replenish, such as fossil fuels, minerals, metals, etc.

### Water Resources
- Water is a vital natural resource that supports life and various human activities.
- Water resources include surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), groundwater (aquifers, wells, springs, etc.), and atmospheric water (rain, snow, fog, etc.).
- Water availability depends on the hydrological cycle, which is the movement of water between the atmosphere, land, and oceans.
- Water quality refers to the physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of water that affect its suitability for different purposes, such as drinking, irrigation, industry, recreation, etc.
- Water quality is influenced by natural factors (such as geology, climate, vegetation, etc.) and human factors (such as pollution, overexploitation, land use, etc.).
- Water borne diseases are caused by the ingestion of contaminated water that contains pathogens, such as bacteria, viruses, protozoa, etc. Examples of water borne diseases are cholera, typhoid, dysentery, hepatitis, etc.
- Water induced diseases are caused by the exposure to water that contains parasites, such as worms, flukes, etc. Examples of water induced diseases are schistosomiasis, guinea worm, etc.
- Fluoride and arsenic are two common contaminants that can cause serious health problems when present in high concentrations in drinking water.
- Fluoride is a naturally occurring element that can be beneficial for dental health in low doses, but can cause dental and skeletal fluorosis in high doses. Fluorosis is a condition that affects the teeth and bones, causing discoloration, brittleness, and deformity.
- Arsenic is a toxic element that can be naturally present in groundwater or introduced by human activities, such as mining, agriculture, industry, etc. Arsenic can cause various acute and chronic effects, such as skin lesions, cancer, cardiovascular diseases, etc.

### Mineral Resources
- Minerals are natural, inorganic, solid substances that have a definite chemical composition and crystal structure.
- Minerals are classified into metallic and non-metallic minerals based on their physical and chemical properties.
- Metallic minerals are those that contain metals, such as iron, copper, gold, silver, etc. Metallic minerals can be further classified into ferrous and non-ferrous minerals based on the presence or absence of iron.
- Non-metallic minerals are those that do not contain metals, such as quartz, feldspar, gypsum, etc. Non-metallic minerals can be further classified into industrial and non-industrial minerals based on their economic value and use.
- Mineral resources are the deposits of minerals that can be extracted and used for various purposes, such as construction, manufacturing, energy, etc.
- Mineral resources are finite and non-renewable, and their exploitation can have environmental and social impacts, such as land degradation, pollution, displacement, conflict, etc.

## Unit 3 - Pollution and their Effects; Public Health Aspects of Environmental; Water Pollution, Air Pollution, Soil Pollution, Noise Pollution, Solid Waste Management

- Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or energy into the environment that cause adverse effects on living organisms and natural resources.
- Pollution can be classified into different types based on the source, the medium, and the effect of the pollutants. Some of the common types are water pollution, air pollution, soil pollution, noise pollution, and solid waste management.
- Water pollution is the contamination of water bodies such as rivers, lakes, oceans, and groundwater by human activities or natural processes. Water pollution can affect the quality and availability of water for drinking, irrigation, recreation, and ecosystem services. Some of the causes of water pollution are industrial effluents, agricultural runoff, sewage disposal, oil spills, mining, and thermal pollution.
- Air pollution is the presence of harmful substances or particles in the atmosphere that can affect human health and the environment. Air pollution can be caused by natural sources such as volcanic eruptions, dust storms, and wildfires, or by anthropogenic sources such as fossil fuel combustion, vehicle emissions, industrial activities, and biomass burning. Some of the effects of air pollution are respiratory diseases, cardiovascular diseases, asthma, cancer, and climate change.
- Soil pollution is the degradation of soil quality due to the presence of harmful substances or agents. Soil pollution can result from direct contact with polluted soil or from indirect contact with other media, such as water or food, that are affected by the soil. Some of the causes of soil pollution are over-irrigation, pesticide use, sewage dumping, garbage disposal, deforestation, and mining. Some of the effects of soil pollution are reduced soil fertility, loss of biodiversity, soil erosion, and contamination of crops and groundwater.
- Noise pollution is the excessive or unwanted sound that can cause annoyance, stress, hearing loss, and other health problems. Noise pollution can be generated by various sources, such as traffic, construction, aircraft, machinery, and entertainment. Some of the effects of noise pollution are sleep disturbance, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, cognitive impairment, and psychological disorders.
- Solid waste management is the collection, transportation, treatment, and disposal of solid wastes that are generated by human activities or natural processes. Solid waste management is essential to prevent the spread of diseases, reduce environmental pollution, conserve resources, and improve the quality of life. Some of the methods of solid waste management are recycling, composting, incineration, landfilling, and bioremediation.

## Unit 4 - Current Environmental Issues of Importance

- **Global Warming**: The increase in the average temperature of the Earth's surface and atmosphere due to the enhanced greenhouse effect caused by human activities, such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and agriculture. Global warming leads to climate change, sea level rise, melting of ice caps and glaciers, extreme weather events, and biodiversity loss  .
- **Greenhouse Effect**: The natural process by which the Earth's atmosphere traps some of the outgoing heat from the sun and keeps the planet warm enough to sustain life. The greenhouse effect is enhanced by the increase in the concentration of greenhouse gases, such as carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and fluorinated gases, which absorb and re-emit infrared radiation .
- **Climate Change**: The long-term change in the statistical distribution of weather patterns over periods ranging from decades to millions of years. Climate change can be caused by natural factors, such as volcanic eruptions, solar variations, and orbital changes, or by human factors, such as greenhouse gas emissions, land use changes, and aerosols. Climate change affects the hydrological cycle, the biosphere, the cryosphere, and the oceans .
- **Acid Rain**: The precipitation that contains high levels of acidic pollutants, such as sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which are emitted from fossil fuel combustion, industrial processes, and vehicle exhausts. Acid rain lowers the pH of soil and water, damages plants and animals, erodes buildings and monuments, and affects human health .
- **Ozone Layer Formation and Depletion**: The ozone layer is a thin layer of gas in the stratosphere that absorbs most of the harmful ultraviolet radiation from the sun and protects life on Earth. The ozone layer is formed by the photolysis of oxygen molecules and the reaction of oxygen atoms with ozone molecules. The ozone layer is depleted by the breakdown of ozone molecules by chlorine and bromine atoms, which are released from man-made substances, such as chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), halons, and methyl bromide. Ozone depletion leads to increased UV exposure, which can cause skin cancer, eye damage, immune system suppression, and crop damage .
- **Population Growth and Automobile Pollution**: The rapid increase in the number of people living on Earth, especially in developing countries, which puts pressure on the natural resources, the environment, and the social and economic systems. The population growth also leads to increased demand for transportation, which results in more automobile pollution. Automobile pollution is the emission of harmful substances, such as carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, particulate matter, and volatile organic compounds, from the exhausts of motor vehicles. Automobile pollution contributes to global warming, acid rain, smog, respiratory diseases, and cardiovascular diseases  .
- **Burning of Paddy Straw**: The practice of burning the leftover stubble of rice plants after harvesting, which is common in some parts of Asia, such as India, China, and Pakistan. Burning of paddy straw causes air pollution, greenhouse gas emissions, soil degradation, and health problems. Burning of paddy straw also reduces the availability of organic matter and nutrients for the next crop  .

### Environmental Protection Act 1986

- The Environmental Protection Act 1986 is an Act of the Parliament of India that aims to provide for the protection and improvement of the environment .
- The Act empowers the Central Government to establish authorities, issue rules and directions, and take measures to prevent, control and abate environmental pollution  .
- The Act covers various aspects of environmental protection, such as standards for emission and discharge of pollutants, hazardous substances management, environmental impact assessment, environmental audit, and penalties for non-compliance  .
