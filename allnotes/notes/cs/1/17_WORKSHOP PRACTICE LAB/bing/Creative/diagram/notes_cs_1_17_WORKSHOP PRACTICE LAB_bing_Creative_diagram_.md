#### To study layout, safety measures and different engineering materials (mild steel, medium carbon steel, high carbon steel, high speed steel and cast iron etc) used in workshop

- Workshop layout is the arrangement of the machinery and equipment in a workshop, which affects the efficiency, quality, and safety of the work process.
- There are three basic types of workshop layout: process layout, product layout, and fixed-position layout. A process layout groups machines and equipment according to their functions, such as cutting, drilling, or welding. A product layout arranges machines and equipment in a sequence that follows the production flow of a specific product or a group of similar products. A fixed-position layout keeps the product in one place and brings the machines and equipment to it.
- A good workshop layout should consider the following factors: the space required for each machine and equipment, the space required for allied equipment (such as dust collectors, heating or cooling systems, exhaust fans, etc.), the space required for employee welfare (such as coffee machine, drinking water, etc.), the space required for gangways and emergency exits, the power supply and distribution, the lighting and ventilation, the noise and vibration control, the material handling and storage, and the safety and health standards.
- Safety measures are the rules and precautions that should be followed in a workshop to prevent accidents and injuries. Some of the general safety measures are  :
  - Always wear safety gear, such as gloves, goggles, helmets, ear plugs, etc., while working in the workshop.
  - Do not wear loose, baggy, or dangling clothing, jewelry, or hair that can get caught in the machines or equipment.
  - Do not operate any machine or equipment without proper training, authorization, and supervision.
  - Do not use any machine or equipment that is defective, damaged, or missing safety guards or devices.
  - Do not overload, over speed, or force any machine or equipment beyond its capacity or limit.
  - Do not leave any machine or equipment running unattended or without proper lockout or tagout procedures.
  - Do not touch any moving parts or hot surfaces of any machine or equipment.
  - Do not use compressed air, flammable liquids, or electrical equipment near any source of ignition or fire.
  - Do not work under the influence of alcohol, drugs, or fatigue.
  - Do not horseplay, distract, or interfere with anyone who is working in the workshop.
  - Report any accident, injury, or hazard to the supervisor or the safety officer immediately.

- A lathe machine is a machine tool that rotates a workpiece on its axis and performs various operations such as cutting, drilling, turning, facing, threading, knurling and parting with the help of a tool that is fed into the workpiece.
- The main parts of a lathe machine are:
  - Headstock: It is the fixed part of the machine that holds the spindle, which rotates the workpiece. It also contains the speed and feed controls, the gear box and the chuck.
  - Tailstock: It is the movable part of the machine that supports the other end of the workpiece. It has a quill that can slide in and out to hold a drill bit, a center or a live center.
  - Bed: It is the horizontal base of the machine that supports the headstock, the tailstock and the carriage. It is rigid and aligned with the spindle axis.
  - Carriage: It is the part of the machine that holds and moves the cutting tool along the workpiece. It consists of the saddle, the cross slide, the compound slide and the tool post.
  - Feed rod and lead screw: They are the long rods that run parallel to the bed and connect the headstock and the carriage. The feed rod transmits power to the carriage for longitudinal and cross feed. The lead screw is used for thread cutting and engages with the split nut on the carriage.
- The accessories of a lathe machine are:
  - Chuck: It is a device that clamps the workpiece on the spindle. There are different types of chucks, such as three-jaw, four-jaw, collet, magnetic and faceplate chucks.
  - Centers: They are pointed devices that support the workpiece on the spindle or the tailstock. There are two types of centers, dead centers and live centers. Dead centers are fixed and do not rotate with the workpiece. Live centers are mounted on bearings and rotate with the workpiece.
  - Rests: They are devices that support the workpiece or the tool during the operation. There are two types of rests, steady rests and follower rests. Steady rests are fixed on the bed and support the workpiece near the cutting tool. Follower rests are attached to the carriage and move with the tool.
  - Tools: They are the cutting devices that remove material from the workpiece. There are different types of tools, such as turning tools, facing tools, parting tools, threading tools, boring tools, drilling tools and knurling tools.

#### 4. CNC Fundamentals and Programming, By P. M. Agrawal, V. J. Patel, Charotar Publication.

- CNC stands for Computer Numerical Control, which is a process of controlling the movement and operation of machine tools by using a computer program.
- CNC machines can perform various operations such as drilling, milling, turning, grinding, etc. with high accuracy, speed, and flexibility.
- CNC machines consist of the following components:
  - Machine tool: The physical device that performs the machining operation, such as a lathe, a mill, or a drill press.
  - Controller: The computer system that controls the machine tool, interprets the program, and sends commands to the motors, sensors, and other devices.
  - Program: The set of instructions that defines the desired shape, size, and position of the workpiece, as well as the sequence of machining operations, tool selection, feed rate, spindle speed, etc.
  - Drive system: The mechanism that converts the electrical signals from the controller into mechanical motion of the machine tool, such as stepper motors, servo motors, or hydraulic actuators.
  - Feedback system: The devices that monitor the actual position, velocity, and force of the machine tool and send the information back to the controller, such as encoders, tachometers, or load cells.
  - Tooling: The cutting tools, fixtures, and accessories that are used to hold and shape the workpiece, such as drills, end mills, chucks, collets, etc.
- CNC programming is the process of creating and editing the program that controls the CNC machine. There are two main methods of CNC programming:
  - Manual programming: The programmer writes the program using a standard code called G-code or M-code, which consists of alphanumeric characters that represent commands, parameters, and coordinates. For example, G01 X10 Y20 Z5 F100 means to move the tool in a straight line to the point (10, 20, 5) with a feed rate of 100 mm/min.
  - CAM programming: The programmer uses a software application called Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM) to create the program by drawing the geometry of the workpiece, selecting the machining operations, and defining the tool paths. The CAM software then generates the G-code or M-code automatically.
- CNC programming requires the following steps:
  - Planning: The programmer analyzes the design of the workpiece, determines the machining operations, selects the tools and fixtures, and calculates the cutting parameters.
  - Programming: The programmer creates the program using manual or CAM programming, and verifies the syntax and logic of the code.
  - Simulation: The programmer tests the program using a software simulator that mimics the behavior of the CNC machine, and checks for errors, collisions, and optimization.
  - Post-processing: The programmer converts the program into a specific format that is compatible with the CNC controller, and adds any additional commands or comments.
  - Execution: The programmer transfers the program to the CNC machine, sets up the machine tool, tooling, and workpiece, and runs the program. The programmer monitors the machining process and makes any adjustments or corrections if needed.
