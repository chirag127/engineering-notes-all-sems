### The historical development of VR

1. The history of virtual reality commences in 1950 .
2. Morton Heilig invented virtual reality technology in 1957. His multimedia device called the Sensorama is considered one of the earliest VR systems .
3. The first VR head-mounted display (HMD) system, The Sword of Damocles, was invented in 1968 by computer scientist Ivan Sutherland and his student Bob Sproull .
4. The term “virtual reality” was popularised by Jaron Lanier in the 1980s .
5. A common thread linking early VR research and technology development in the United States was the role of the federal government, particularly the Department of Defense, the National Science Foundation, and NASA .
6. VPL Research developed a range of VR equipment .
7. Furness developed a flight simulator between 1986-1989 known as the Super Cockpit. The training cockpit featured: computer-generated 3D maps, advanced infrared and radar imagery and the pilot could see and hear in real-time .

### Haptic Displays

Haptic displays are interfaces that deliver haptic feedback, typically by stimulating somatic receptors to generate a sensation of touch. The rendered feedback is generally categorized into cutaneous as well as kinesthetic or proprioceptive feedback, respectively .

Haptic display represents a promising interface for human–computer interaction tools that provide users with nuanced information in a virtual environment. Humans utilize tactile and haptic information to determine object properties using thousands of mechanoreceptors located in the hands .

Haptic displays can be categorized by the type of stimuli/output they generate, and correspondingly by the type of sensory receptors that are stimulated. Kinesthetic display is concerned with the rendering of forces. The latter are generated based on computational models, remote interaction, recordings or data-driven approaches .

Due to the amount of information required to adequately represent surface and object characteristics, haptic displays (particularly force displays) require a more complete model of the world than worlds displayed only visually and/or aurally .

Haptic display devices range from small units that sit on the desktop or are worn on the hand to large robotic devices capable of lifting a person off the ground. Because there are so many different aspects to haptic feedback (taction, proprioception, thermo-reception, electroreception, etc.) and so many different body parts that the display can be applied to, there is a wide range of haptic display devices .

Haptic technology (also kinaesthetic communication or 3D touch) is technology that can create an experience of touch by applying forces, vibrations, or motions to the user. These technologies can be used to create virtual objects in a computer simulation, to control virtual objects, and to enhance remote control of machines and devices .

5. **Compatibility:** The ability of an input device to work with different hardware and software platforms. A compatible input device can be used with a wide range of virtual and augmented reality applications and devices.

These are some of the key characteristics to consider when selecting input devices for use in 3D user interfaces and virtual and augmented reality applications. 1. In Augmented Reality, the position and orientation of the rendering device need to be in real-world coordinates for proper augmentation to happen.
2. When writing games, data visualization apps, or virtual reality apps, the typical approach is to establish one absolute world coordinate system that all other coordinates can reliably map back to.
3. In this environment, you can always find a stable transform that defines the relationship between any two objects in that world.
4. World Locking Tools (WLT) can be used to stabilize a single rigid coordinate system using an internal supply of spatial anchors spread throughout the virtual scene as the user moves around.
5. Augmented Reality depends on a dynamic, accurate 3D map: a digital twin that anchors location-based content and experiences to real-world coordinates.
6. Three values are required to fully define the position of any point in the world. These three values can be expressed by the x, y, and z-axes of the coordinate frame.

### Cullers and Occluders

Cullers and occluders are techniques used in computer graphics to improve the performance of rendering 3D scenes. These techniques are used to reduce the number of objects that need to be rendered, thus reducing the computational load on the graphics hardware.

#### Cullers
Cullers are algorithms that determine which objects in a 3D scene are visible to the camera and which are not. Objects that are not visible to the camera are culled, meaning they are not rendered. This can significantly reduce the number of objects that need to be rendered, thus improving performance.

There are several types of culling algorithms, including:
- **View frustum culling**: This algorithm checks if an object is inside the view frustum, which is the volume of space visible to the camera. Objects outside the view frustum are culled.
- **Backface culling**: This algorithm checks if the polygons of an object are facing away from the camera. If they are, the object is culled.
- **Occlusion culling**: This algorithm checks if an object is occluded by other objects in the scene. If it is, the object is culled.

#### Occluders
Occluders are objects in a 3D scene that can block the view of other objects. By identifying occluders, the occlusion culling algorithm can determine which objects are not visible to the camera and cull them.

Occluders can be static, such as walls and buildings, or dynamic, such as characters and vehicles. The effectiveness of occlusion culling depends on the complexity of the scene and the number and size of the occluders.

In summary, cullers and occluders are techniques used to improve the performance of rendering 3D scenes by reducing the number of objects that need to be rendered. These techniques are commonly used in real-time graphics applications, such as video games and virtual reality.

2. **Features:** AR incorporates three features: a combination of digital and physical worlds, interactions made in real time, and accurate 3D identification of virtual and real objects .

3. **Difference from Virtual Reality:** Unlike Virtual Reality (VR), AR does not create the whole artificial environments to replace real with a virtual one. AR appears in direct view of an existing environment and adds sounds, videos, graphics to it .

4. **Applications:** AR can be integrated into employee training and education, retail, and manufacturing. For example, employees can use AR for onboarding and training sessions, and technology can offer step-by-step instructions . It is also commonly used in electronic first-person shooter games to add environmental, health, and other information to players’ viewpoints .



### Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality
#### Difference between AR and VR

- **AR (Augmented Reality)** is a technology that overlays digital information onto the real world, enhancing the user's perception of their surroundings. This can be achieved through the use of devices such as smartphones, tablets, or AR glasses.

- **VR (Virtual Reality)**, on the other hand, is a technology that immerses the user in a completely digital environment, blocking out the real world. This is typically achieved through the use of a VR headset.

- The main difference between AR and VR is the level of immersion. While AR enhances the real world, VR creates a completely new one.

- Another key difference is the level of interaction. In AR, the user can interact with both the real world and the digital information, while in VR, the user can only interact with the digital environment.

- AR and VR can also differ in terms of hardware requirements. AR can be experienced through a range of devices, while VR typically requires a dedicated headset.

- Both AR and VR have a wide range of applications, including gaming, education, and training. However, the specific use cases for each technology can vary due to their inherent differences.

### Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality: AR systems and functionality

Augmented Reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the user's perception of the real world by overlaying digital information onto the physical environment. AR systems can be categorized into two main types: marker-based and markerless.

1. **Marker-based AR:** This type of AR system uses a physical marker, such as a QR code or image, to trigger the display of digital content. The AR device's camera scans the marker and the system uses it as a reference point to overlay digital information onto the real world.

2. **Markerless AR:** This type of AR system does not require a physical marker. Instead, it uses sensors, such as GPS, accelerometer, and gyroscope, to determine the position and orientation of the device. The system then overlays digital information onto the real world based on this data.

AR systems can also be classified based on the type of device used to display the digital content. These include:

1. **Handheld AR:** This type of AR system uses a handheld device, such as a smartphone or tablet, to display digital content. The device's camera captures the real world and the system overlays digital information onto the camera feed.

2. **Head-mounted AR:** This type of AR system uses a head-mounted display, such as a pair of smart glasses or a virtual reality headset, to display digital content. The display is positioned in front of the user's eyes, allowing them to see the digital content overlaid onto the real world.

3. **Projection-based AR:** This type of AR system uses a projector to display digital content onto a physical surface. The system tracks the position and orientation of the projector and adjusts the projection accordingly to ensure that the digital content appears correctly on the physical surface.

AR systems can provide a wide range of functionality, including:

1. **Information overlay:** AR systems can overlay digital information, such as text, images, and videos, onto the real world. This can be used to provide additional information about the physical environment, such as the name of a building or the history of a landmark.

2. **Object recognition:** AR systems can use computer vision techniques to recognize and track objects in the real world. This can be used to provide additional information about the object, such as its name or specifications.

3. **Virtual object placement:** AR systems can place virtual objects into the real world. These objects can be interacted with and manipulated by the user, providing a more immersive experience.

4. **Real-time translation:** AR systems can use natural language processing techniques to provide real-time translation of text and speech. This can be used to translate signs or spoken language in real-time, making it easier for users to navigate and communicate in foreign environments.

Overall, AR systems provide a wide range of functionality that can enhance the user's perception of the real world by overlaying digital information onto the physical environment. These systems can be used in a variety of applications, including education, entertainment, and navigation.



### Augmented Reality Methods

Augmented reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the user's perception of the real world by overlaying digital information onto it. There are several methods used to achieve this, including:

1. **Marker-based AR:** This method uses a physical marker, such as a QR code or image, to trigger the display of digital content. The device's camera is used to recognize the marker, and the digital content is then overlaid onto the real world in the correct position relative to the marker.

2. **Markerless AR:** This method does not require a physical marker, but instead uses the device's sensors, such as the accelerometer and gyroscope, to determine the position and orientation of the device. The digital content is then overlaid onto the real world based on this information.

3. **Projection-based AR:** This method involves projecting digital content onto physical surfaces, such as a wall or table. The digital content appears to be part of the real world and can be interacted with using gestures or touch.

4. **Superimposition-based AR:** This method replaces a real-world object with a digital version, or superimposes digital content onto the real-world object. This can be achieved using object recognition technology.

These are some of the common methods used in augmented reality technology. Each method has its own advantages and limitations, and the choice of method will depend on the specific application and use case.

### Evaluating AR Systems

When evaluating AR systems, there are several factors to consider. These include:

1. **Accuracy**: The accuracy of the AR system refers to how well the virtual objects are aligned with the real world. This is important for creating a seamless and immersive experience for the user.

2. **Latency**: Latency refers to the delay between the user's actions and the corresponding response from the AR system. Low latency is important for creating a responsive and interactive experience.

3. **Field of View**: The field of view refers to the extent of the real world that can be seen through the AR device. A larger field of view can enhance the user's sense of immersion in the AR experience.

4. **Resolution**: The resolution of the AR system refers to the level of detail that can be displayed. Higher resolution can improve the realism of the virtual objects.

5. **Portability**: Portability refers to how easy it is to use the AR system in different locations. A portable AR system can be used in a wider range of environments and situations.

6. **Ease of Use**: The ease of use of the AR system refers to how intuitive and user-friendly the system is. A system that is easy to use can improve the user's experience and reduce frustration.

7. **Cost**: The cost of the AR system is also an important factor to consider. The cost should be balanced against the benefits and features of the system.

These are some of the key factors to consider when evaluating AR systems for use in Augmented and Mixed Reality applications. By considering these factors, you can select an AR system that meets your needs and provides a high-quality AR experience.
