


# Knowledge Centered Support (KCS)

KCS is a methodology for providing effective support to customers. It is based on the concept of knowledge sharing, which involves capturing and sharing knowledge to improve the customer experience and reduce costs.

KCS focuses on the capture and reuse of knowledge in order to provide the best possible customer experience. It is based on the idea that knowledge should be captured, organized, and shared in order to be effective.

KCS involves the following steps:

1. Capture: Capturing knowledge in the form of articles, documents, and other content.
2. Organize: Organizing the captured knowledge into a structure that makes it easy to find and use.
3. Share: Sharing the knowledge with customers and other stakeholders.
4. Measure: Measuring the effectiveness of the knowledge sharing process.

KCS has been shown to reduce customer support costs and improve customer satisfaction. It is an effective way to provide knowledge-based customer support.




## Unit 1 - Microprocessor Evolution and Types 

- The evolution of microprocessors can be traced back to the early 1970s, when Intel released the first commercially available microprocessor, the Intel 4004. 
- Since then, microprocessors have become increasingly powerful and efficient. 
- Modern microprocessors are divided into two main categories: CISC (Complex Instruction Set Computing) and RISC (Reduced Instruction Set Computing). 
- CISC microprocessors are designed to support a wide range of instructions, while RISC microprocessors are designed to execute instructions quickly but with fewer instructions. 

## Microprocessor Architecture and Operation of its Components 
- A microprocessor is composed of several components, including an arithmetic logic unit (ALU), a control unit, a register file, and a data bus. 
- The ALU performs arithmetic and logical operations on data stored in the register file. 
- The control unit controls the flow of data between the ALU and the register file. 
- The data bus is used to transfer data between the ALU, register file, and other components of the microprocessor. 

## Addressing Modes 
- An addressing mode is a way of specifying the location of data or instructions in memory. 
- Common addressing modes include direct, immediate, register indirect, and indexed addressing. 
- Direct addressing uses the memory address of the data or instruction. 
- Immediate addressing uses the value of the data or instruction. 
- Register indirect addressing uses the contents of a register to access the data or instruction. 
- Indexed addressing uses an offset from a base address to access the data or instruction. 

## Interrupts 
- Interrupts are signals sent from external devices to the microprocessor, which cause the microprocessor to stop executing its current instruction and begin executing a special interrupt service routine. 
- The interrupt service routine is responsible for handling the interrupt and restoring the microprocessor to its original state. 

## Data Transfer Schemes 
- Data transfer schemes are used to move data between different components of a microprocessor. 
- Common data transfer schemes include memory-mapped I/O, direct memory access (DMA), and programmed I/O. 
- Memory-mapped I/O uses the same address space for both data and instructions. 
- DMA is used to transfer data directly between memory and I/O devices, without the intervention of the CPU. 
- Programmed I/O is used to transfer data to or from an I/O device using instructions. 

## Instruction and Data Flow 
- The instruction and data flow of a microprocessor is the sequence of instructions and data that the microprocessor executes and processes. 
- The instruction flow is the sequence of instructions that the microprocessor executes. 
- The data flow is the sequence of data that the microprocessor processes. 

## Timer and Timing Diagram 
- A timer is a device that generates a signal after a certain amount of time has elapsed. 
- A timing diagram is a graphical representation of the timing of the signals generated by a timer. 
- Timers are used to control the timing of operations in a microprocessor. 

## Interfacing Devices 
- Interfacing devices are hardware components that are used to connect a microprocessor to external devices, such as keyboards, mice, and printers. 
- Common interfacing devices include serial and parallel ports, USB ports, and Ethernet ports.
