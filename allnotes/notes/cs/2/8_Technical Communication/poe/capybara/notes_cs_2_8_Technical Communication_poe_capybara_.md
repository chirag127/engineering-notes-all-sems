### Distinction between General and Technical Communication

In the field of Technical Communication, it is important to understand the difference between General Communication and Technical Communication. The following points highlight the distinctions between the two:

1. **Purpose:** The purpose of General Communication is to convey information in a general sense, whereas Technical Communication has a specific purpose of conveying technical information to a specific audience.

2. **Audience:** The audience for General Communication is usually broad and diverse, while the audience for Technical Communication is specific and targeted towards those who have knowledge of the subject matter.

3. **Style:** General Communication uses a casual and conversational style, while Technical Communication is more formal and structured. Technical Communication often uses specialized terminology and jargon.

4. **Content:** General Communication covers a wide range of topics and can be about anything, while Technical Communication focuses on specific technical topics such as software manuals, technical reports, and user guides.

5. **Media:** General Communication can be conveyed through various media such as social media, letters, and emails, whereas Technical Communication is typically conveyed through more specialized media such as technical manuals, schematics, and blueprints.

6. **Accuracy:** Technical Communication requires a high level of accuracy and attention to detail, as it often deals with complex technical concepts that can have serious consequences if misunderstood.

In summary, Technical Communication is a specialized form of communication that requires a specific set of skills and knowledge. Understanding the distinctions between General Communication and Technical Communication is crucial for those entering the field of Technical Communication.

2. Cornell Notes - In this method, the page is divided into three sections: a main note-taking area, a cue column, and a summary area. The main note-taking area is used to record the most important information, while the cue column is used to write down key words or questions that help trigger recall. The summary area is used to summarize the main points of the notes.

3. Mind Maps - This type of note-taking involves creating a visual representation of the information, with the main idea in the center and supporting details branching out from it. This method is helpful for visual learners and those who prefer a more creative approach to note-taking.

4. Charting - This method involves creating a table or chart that outlines the key points of the information. This type of note-taking is useful for organizing information and making comparisons between different concepts.

5. Sentence Method - This method involves writing complete sentences that summarize the key points of the information. This type of note-taking is helpful for those who find it difficult to condense information into bullet points.

No matter which type of note-taking method you choose, it is important to review and revise your notes regularly in order to ensure that you have a clear understanding of the information. With a little practice, you can develop a note-taking system that works best for you and helps you achieve success in the field of technical communication.

### The Flow of Communication

In Technical Communication, communication is a critical aspect of delivering information effectively. The flow of communication refers to how information is transmitted from one person to another. In this unit, we will discuss the various forms of communication and how they can be used to convey technical information effectively. Some of the forms of communication we will be discussing include:

- Verbal Communication: This type of communication involves using spoken words to convey information. It can be done face-to-face, over the phone, or through video conferencing. Verbal communication is often used when discussing complex technical information since it allows for immediate feedback and clarification.

- Written Communication: Written communication involves using written words to convey information. This can be done through emails, memos, reports, or other written documents. Written communication is often used when the information being conveyed is complex or needs to be referred to at a later time.

- Visual Communication: Visual communication involves using images, graphs, charts, and other visual aids to convey information. This type of communication is often used when the information being conveyed is complex or difficult to explain using words alone.

The flow of communication in technical communication follows a specific pattern. It starts with the sender, who encodes the message and transmits it to the receiver. The receiver then decodes the message and provides feedback to the sender. This process can be illustrated using the following diagram:

```
    Sender --> Encoding --> Transmission --> Decoding --> Receiver --> Feedback
```

It is important to note that communication is a two-way process, with both the sender and receiver playing an active role in the process. Effective communication requires both parties to be engaged and actively listening to one another.

In summary, understanding the flow of communication is critical to effective technical communication. By using the appropriate forms of communication and following the communication process, technical information can be conveyed accurately and effectively.

### Impersonal Notes for Unit 3: Technical Presentation

In technical communication, it is important to maintain an impersonal tone in order to convey information effectively. Here are some guidelines for achieving an impersonal tone in technical presentations:

- Use passive voice instead of active voice. This helps emphasize the object of the sentence rather than the subject, making the tone more objective and less personal. For example, instead of saying "I developed this software," say "This software was developed."

- Avoid using personal pronouns such as "I," "we," and "you." Instead, use more neutral language such as "the author," "the team," or "the user." This helps create distance between the speaker and the audience and keeps the focus on the information being presented.

- Use technical terminology and avoid colloquial expressions. Using technical language helps create a more formal tone and can also help establish the speaker's expertise in the subject matter. Conversely, using colloquial expressions can make the tone more informal and personal.

- Use objective evidence and avoid personal opinions. When presenting technical information, it is important to rely on objective evidence such as data and research rather than personal opinions or anecdotes. This helps establish credibility and keeps the presentation focused on the facts.

- Avoid humor or sarcasm. While humor can be effective in some types of presentations, it can be risky in technical presentations because it can be misinterpreted or distract from the information being presented. Similarly, sarcasm can come across as unprofessional and can be confusing for the audience.

By following these guidelines, speakers can create an impersonal tone that helps them communicate technical information effectively and professionally.

## Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

In this unit, we will learn about the various dimensions of oral communication and voice dynamics. Here are the key points to remember:

- **Pitch:** It refers to the highness or lowness of a person's voice. A high-pitched voice is usually associated with excitement or anxiety, while a low-pitched voice is associated with calmness or seriousness.

- **Volume:** It refers to the loudness or softness of a person's voice. A loud voice is usually associated with assertiveness or aggression, while a soft voice is associated with gentleness or shyness.

- **Rate:** It refers to the speed at which a person speaks. A fast rate is usually associated with excitement or anxiety, while a slow rate is associated with calmness or thoughtfulness.

- **Articulation:** It refers to the clarity with which a person pronounces words. Good articulation makes it easier for the listener to understand what is being said.

- **Pronunciation:** It refers to the correct way of saying words. Pronouncing words incorrectly can lead to misunderstandings and miscommunications.

- **Accent:** It refers to the way a person speaks a language, which is influenced by their regional or cultural background. Accents can add richness and diversity to oral communication but can also lead to misunderstandings.

- **Intonation:** It refers to the rise and fall of a person's voice when speaking. It can convey emotions, attitudes, and meanings that are not conveyed by the words alone.

- **Pauses:** They refer to the moments of silence between words or phrases in oral communication. Pauses can be used for emphasis, to signal the end of a thought, or to allow the listener to absorb the information.

- **Stress:** It refers to the emphasis placed on certain words or syllables in a sentence. Stress can change the meaning of a sentence and can be used to convey different emotions and attitudes.

In conclusion, oral communication and voice dynamics are essential components of effective communication. Understanding and mastering these dimensions can help us convey our messages more clearly and effectively.
