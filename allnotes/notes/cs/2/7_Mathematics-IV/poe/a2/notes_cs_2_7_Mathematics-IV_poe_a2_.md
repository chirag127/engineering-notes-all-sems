    - u_x + u_y = 0 (the wave equation)

### Lagrange's Equations

1. Lagrange's equations are a set of equations that describe how a system evolves over time. They are used to solve problems in mechanics and dynamical systems.
2. The equations are named after Joseph Louis Lagrange who developed them in the 1700s. They provide an alternative way to describe Newton's laws of motion.
3. Lagrange's equations are written as:

d/dt (∂L/∂q̇i) − ∂L/∂qi = 0

Where L is the Lagrangian, qi represents the generalized coordinates of the system, and q̇i represents the time derivative of the generalized coordinates.
4. The Lagrangian is equal to the kinetic energy minus the potential energy of the system. Solving the Lagrange equations yields the trajectories and other properties of the system.
5. Lagrange's equations are useful for analyzing constrained systems and systems with symmetry. They can also be applied to fields other than mechanics, such as optics, quantum mechanics, and fluid dynamics.

### Solution of Linear Partial Differential Equation of Higher order with constant coefficients for the notes of the Module I: Partial Differential Equations in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. A linear partial differential equation of order n with constant coefficients is of the form:

$$a_0(x,y,z,...)y^{(n)} + a_1(x,y,z,...)y^{(n-1)} + ... + a_(n-1)(x,y,z,...)y' + a_n(x,y,z,...)y = f(x,y,z,...)$$

where $a_0,a_1,...,a_n$ are constants and $f(x,y,z,...)$ is a given function.

2. The method of solution is to assume a solution of the form:

$$y = e^{rx} v(y)$$

where $r$ is a constant to be determined and $v(y)$ is an arbitrary function of $y$ alone.

3. Substitute this assumed solution in the given differential equation and equate coefficients of like powers of $e^{rx}$ to zero. This will lead to a system of $n$ equations in $n$ unknowns $r$ and the derivatives of $v(y)$.

4. Solving this system, we can determine $r$ and $v(y)$. The general solution is then obtained by substituting the values of $r$ and $v(y)$ and its derivatives in the assumed solution.

5. The order of the differential equation can be reduced by one if $r$ happens to be a root of the characteristic equation $|a_0r^n + a_1r^(n-1) + ... + a_(n-1)r + a_n| = 0$. In such a case, the solution can be obtained by the method of reduction of order.

2. Assume a solution of the form:
$$u(x,y) = X(x)Y(y)$$

1. Moment generating function (MGF) is a function of a real variable `t` defined as `M_X(t) = E(e^{tX})`, where `X` is a random variable and `E` is the expectation operator.

2. The moment generating function exists only if the sequence of moments `E(X^n)` is absolutely convergent for all `n >= 0`. This means that the moment generating function exists only if `X` has a finite variance.

3. The moment generating function is used to derive the moments of a distribution. The `n`th derivative of `M_X(t)` evaluated at `t = 0` gives the `n`th moment of `X`. In other words, `M^n_X(0)` = `E(X^n)` for all `n >= 0`.

4. The moment generating function provides an alternative way of studying the distribution of a random variable. Many distributions like Normal, Exponential, Poisson, Gamma, etc. can be characterized in terms of their moment generating functions.

5. The moment generating function is useful in deriving the distribution of a function of a random variable. If `Y = g(X)` is a function of the random variable `X`, then the moment generating function of `Y` can be obtained by differentiating and evaluating the moment generating function of `X` at `t = 0`. 2. A distribution is said to be positively skewed if the tail on the right side is longer or fatter than the left side. This means that the mass of the distribution is concentrated on the left side of the figure.
3. A distribution is said to be negatively skewed if the tail on the left side is longer or fatter than the right side. This means that the mass of the distribution is concentrated on the right side of the figure.

- To determine normality of data. A normal distribution will have skewness = 0.

- Exam marks: Positively skewed (a few students may score very high)
- Income data: Positively skewed (a few earn very high incomes)

### Kurtosis for the notes of the Module III: Statistical Techniques I: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. Kurtosis is a measure of the peakedness or flatness of the probability distribution of a real-valued random variable.
2. A high kurtosis distribution has a sharper peak and longer, fatter tails, while a low kurtosis distribution has a more rounded peak and shorter, thinner tails.
3. The kurtosis of any univariate distribution is defined as the fourth moment around the mean divided by the square of the variance.
4. A distribution with a higher kurtosis than the normal distribution has more of its variance due to infrequent extreme deviations, as opposed to frequent modestly sized deviations.
5. The kurtosis of a normal distribution is 3. Distributions with kurtosis greater than 3 are said to be leptokurtic, while those with kurtosis less than 3 are platykurtic.
6. Kurtosis is a measure of outliers, not shape, and is very sensitive to outliers. Adding or removing outliers can significantly change the kurtosis.
7. Some possible distributions to model different levels of kurtosis are: normal distribution (kurtosis = 3), t-distribution, Laplace distribution, etc.

### Baye's Theorem

* Baye's theorem is a way of updating the probabilities of an event based on new information or evidence.
* It uses conditional probability to determine the probability of an event A given that event B has occurred.
* The formula for Baye's theorem is: P(A|B) = [P(B|A) x P(A)] / P(B)
* Where:
    * P(A|B) is the posterior probability of A given B
    * P(B|A) is the likelihood of B given A
    * P(A) is the prior probability of A
    * P(B) is the prior probability of B
* Baye's theorem is useful in medical testing, spam filtering, and recommendation systems.
* It allows you to update the probability of a hypothesis (such as a patient having a disease) in light of new evidence (such as a medical test result).

### Sampling Theory (Small and Large)

1. Small sample theory: When the sample size is small (less than 30), we use small sample theory. In small samples, the sample statistics like sample mean, median, etc. follow a probability distribution and are not consistent estimators of the population parameters. The distributions of sample statistics are studied and used to draw inferences about the population parameters.

2. Large sample theory: When the sample size is large (greater than 30), we use large sample theory. In large samples, the sample statistics are consistent estimators of the population parameters. The distributions of sample statistics under large sample theory can be approximated by normal distribution using central limit theorem. The inferences about the population parameters can be drawn using z-test or t-test.

The inferences based on large sample theory are more reliable compared to small sample theory as the sample statistics closely estimate the population parameters when sample size is large. ### One way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

- One way ANOVA is used to compare means of two or more independent groups to check if they are significantly different from each other.
- It assumes the data to follow normal distribution and the variables to be continuous and interval or ratio.
- The steps to perform One way ANOVA are:
1. State the null and alternative hypothesis. Null hypothesis is that the means of all the groups are equal. Alternative hypothesis is that at least two means are not equal.
2. Calculate the total sum of squares (SST). It is the sum of squared deviations of all the observations from the grand mean.
3. Calculate the sum of squares between groups (SSB). It is the sum of squared deviations of group means from the grand mean.
4. Calculate the sum of squares within groups (SSW). It is the sum of squared deviations of observations from their respective group means.
5. Calculate the F-statistic. It is the ratio of MSB and MSW.
6. Check the probability (p-value). If p-value is less than the significance level, reject the null hypothesis.
- The F-statistic follows F distribution with (k-1) and (n-k) degrees of freedom where k is the number of groups and n is the total number of observations.
- ANOVA is used to test if means of at least two groups are significantly different. It does not identify which specific groups differ. Post-hoc tests like Tukey's HSD can be used for that.

    4. If out of control, investigate and remove the assignable cause. Then re-calculate the control limits.
- Control charts help monitor both small and large changes to detect and correct problems early. They are a key tool for statistical process control and quality improvement.
