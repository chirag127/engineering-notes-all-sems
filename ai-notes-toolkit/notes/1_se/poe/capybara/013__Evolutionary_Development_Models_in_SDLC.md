### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

Evolutionary Development Models are the types of software development models that are iterative and incremental in nature. They focus on delivering a working software product in small increments, with each increment building on the previous ones. Here are some of the most popular Evolutionary Development Models used in Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC):

1. Spiral Model: 
The Spiral Model is a risk-driven model that combines the iterative nature of prototyping with the controlled and systematic aspects of the Waterfall Model. In this model, the software development process is divided into smaller cycles, each cycle consisting of four stages- planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation.

2. Agile Model:
The Agile Model is an iterative and incremental model that emphasizes collaboration, flexibility, and customer satisfaction. It is based on the Agile Manifesto, which values individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, and responding to change.

3. Scrum Model:
The Scrum Model is an Agile framework that emphasizes teamwork, accountability, and iterative progress towards a well-defined goal. It is based on the principles of transparency, inspection, and adaptation. In this model, the software development process is divided into shorter time-boxed iterations called Sprints.

4. Rapid Application Development (RAD) Model:
The Rapid Application Development (RAD) Model is a linear sequential model that emphasizes rapid prototyping and iterative development. It is based on the premise that a working software prototype can be developed quickly and then refined through successive iterations. 

5. Evolutionary Prototyping Model:
The Evolutionary Prototyping Model is an iterative and incremental model that emphasizes the use of prototypes to explore, refine, and validate software requirements. In this model, a working software prototype is developed early in the software development process, and then refined through successive iterations.

In conclusion, Evolutionary Development Models are iterative and incremental models that focus on delivering a working software product in small increments. They are flexible and adaptable, and can be customized to meet the specific needs of a software development project. Each of the above-mentioned models has its unique features and characteristics, and can be used depending on the project requirements and constraints.
