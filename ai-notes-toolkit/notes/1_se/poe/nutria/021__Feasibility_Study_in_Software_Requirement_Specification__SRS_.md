
### Feasibility Study in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

1. A feasibility study is an analysis of the viability of a proposed project or system. It is used to determine whether a project should be undertaken and if so, how it should be implemented.

2. The purpose of a feasibility study is to identify and analyze the positive and negative aspects of a proposed project in order to determine if it is a viable option. It should include research, analysis, and evaluation of the project's potential for success.

3. A feasibility study should include an assessment of the technical, economic, operational, and legal feasibility of the proposed project. It should also consider the potential risks, costs, and benefits associated with the project.

4. A software requirement specification (SRS) is a document that describes the requirements of a software system. It is used to define the functional and non-functional requirements, as well as the constraints of the system.

5. A feasibility study should be conducted before developing an SRS. It should analyze the potential for success of the proposed software system and determine if it is feasible to develop.

6. The feasibility study should consider the technical, economic, operational, and legal aspects of the proposed software system. It should also analyze the potential risks, costs, and benefits associated with the project.

7. The results of the feasibility study should be documented in the SRS. This will help to ensure that the software system meets the requirements and constraints of the project.
