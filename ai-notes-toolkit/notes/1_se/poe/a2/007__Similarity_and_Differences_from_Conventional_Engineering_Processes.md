### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes

- Project management: Agile projects also require project management to track progress, resources, budget, and schedule. However, agile project management focuses on adaptability to changes and frequent re-planning.
- Requirements: Agile methods emphasize evolutionary and incremental requirements gathering and changes. Requirements are elicited and prioritized early but details are discovered and concrete specifications emerge over time.
- Design: The design process is iterative and incremental. The overall system design emerges over time through a series of prototypes and refinements.
- Planning: Planning is done incrementally for short timeboxes. The plans are revisited regularly and adjusted as needed.
- Testing: Testing is conducted early and continuously. Testing is tightly integrated with development and a key driver for design decisions.
- Risk management: Risk management involves early identification of risks and continuous monitoring and adaptation. The shorter iterations provide more opportunities to identify and react to risks early.
- Documentation: Agile methods emphasize "barely sufficient" documentation that is directly useful to stakeholders. Much of the documentation is in the form of self-documenting code and models.
- Tools: Agile methods do not prescribe specific tools but emphasize individuals and interactions over tools. Many conventional and agile-specific tools exist to support agile development processes.

The above points highlight some of the key similarities and differences between agile and more conventional plan-driven engineering processes. The agile approach focuses on adaptability, evolutionary development, and iterative refinement over following a defined sequence of process steps. However, many of the conventional engineering activities are still required but take on a more lightweight and flexible form.
