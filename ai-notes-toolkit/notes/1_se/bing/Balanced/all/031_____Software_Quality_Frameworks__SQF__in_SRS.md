### Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) in SRS

- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) are the set of standards, guidelines, and best practices that aim to ensure the quality of software products and services.
- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) can be applied at different stages of the software development life cycle, such as planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance.
- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) can help to define the quality attributes and requirements of the software, such as functionality, reliability, usability, efficiency, maintainability, and security.
- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) can also help to measure, monitor, and improve the quality of the software, such as by using metrics, indicators, audits, reviews, and testing tools.
- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) can be based on different models, methods, and approaches, such as ISO, CMMI, Agile, Lean, Six Sigma, etc.
- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) can be tailored to the specific needs and context of the software project, such as the size, scope, complexity, domain, and stakeholders of the software.
- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) can be documented in the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document, which is a formal description of what the software system should do and how it should perform.
- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) in SRS can help to:

  - Establish the basis for agreement between the client and the supplier on what the software product will do.
  - Provide a reference for validation and verification of the final product.
  - Facilitate communication and collaboration among the project team and other stakeholders.
  - Reduce the risk of errors, defects, and failures in the software.
  - Enhance the customer satisfaction and trust in the software.

- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) in SRS should include:

  - The purpose and scope of the software.
  - The description and overview of the software.
  - The features and functionality of the software.
  - The functional and non-functional requirements of the software.
  - The third-party integrations and dependencies of the software.
  - The target devices and platforms of the software.
  - The use cases and scenarios of the software.
  - The quality attributes and criteria of the software.
  - The quality standards and models of the software.
  - The quality metrics and indicators of the software.
  - The quality assurance and control methods of the software.
  - The quality testing and evaluation tools of the software.
