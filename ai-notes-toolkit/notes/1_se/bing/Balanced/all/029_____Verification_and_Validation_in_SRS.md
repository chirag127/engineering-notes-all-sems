### Verification and Validation in SRS

- Verification and validation (V&V) are two activities that ensure the quality and correctness of the software requirements specification (SRS) document.
- Verification is the process of checking whether the SRS conforms to the standards, guidelines, and specifications that are defined for it. It answers the question: "Are we building the product right?"
- Validation is the process of checking whether the SRS meets the needs and expectations of the stakeholders. It answers the question: "Are we building the right product?"
- V&V should be done continuously throughout the development of requirements at every level and as part of baseline activities and reviewed during the System Requirements Reviews (SRR) .
- V&V can be done using different techniques, such as:

  - Completeness checks: To ensure that the SRS covers all the functional and non-functional requirements and does not omit any essential information.
  - Consistency checks: To ensure that the SRS does not contain any conflicting or contradictory requirements or statements.
  - Validity checks: To ensure that the SRS is aligned with the actual needs and expectations of the stakeholders and the intended users of the system.
  - Realism checks: To ensure that the SRS is feasible and achievable within the given constraints and resources.
  - Ambiguity checks: To ensure that the SRS is clear and unambiguous and does not leave any room for interpretation or misunderstanding.
  - Verifiability checks: To ensure that the SRS is testable and measurable and that every requirement can be verified by some method or procedure  .

- V&V can also be done using different methods, such as:

  - Peer reviews: To involve other experts or colleagues in reviewing the SRS and providing feedback and suggestions for improvement.
  - Walkthroughs: To present the SRS to a group of reviewers and explain the rationale and logic behind each requirement and answer any questions or doubts they may have.
  - Inspections: To conduct a formal and structured examination of the SRS by a team of reviewers who follow a predefined checklist and report any defects or issues they find .
  - Testing: To perform a thorough testing of the completed SRS to prove that everything works as it should and that every safety function in the SRS, as well as the SRS itself, meets every requirement .

- A mnemonic to remember the difference between verification and validation is:

  - Verification: Are we building the product right?
  - Validation: Are we building the right product?
