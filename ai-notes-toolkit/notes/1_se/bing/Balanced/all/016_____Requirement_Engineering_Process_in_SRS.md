### Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

- Software Requirements Specification (SRS) is a document that captures the complete description of how the system is expected to perform.
- SRS is the output of the requirements engineering phase of the software development process.
- SRS lays the foundation for software engineering activities and is used for validation, verification, testing, and maintenance.
- SRS should be clear, consistent, complete, correct, traceable, verifiable, modifiable, and prioritized .
- The elements that comprise an SRS can be summarized into four Ds:
  - Define your product's purpose: state the scope, objectives, and assumptions of the software project.
  - Describe what you're building: provide an overview of the system's functionality, features, interfaces, and constraints.
  - Detail the requirements: specify the functional and non-functional requirements of the system, such as user requirements, system requirements, performance requirements, quality attributes, etc.
  - Deliver it for approval: review the SRS with the stakeholders, resolve any issues, and get their feedback and approval.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the four Ds is: **D**o you know what you're **D**oing? **D**escribe it and **D**eliver it.
- A possible outline for an SRS document is:
  - Introduction: provide the purpose, scope, definitions, acronyms, abbreviations, references, and overview of the document.
  - Overall description: provide the product perspective, product functions, user characteristics, general constraints, assumptions and dependencies, and apportioning of requirements.
  - Specific requirements: provide the external interface requirements, functional requirements, performance requirements, design constraints, software system attributes, and other requirements.
  - Appendices: provide any additional information, such as data models, flowcharts, diagrams, etc.
  - Index: provide an alphabetical list of terms and topics covered in the document.
