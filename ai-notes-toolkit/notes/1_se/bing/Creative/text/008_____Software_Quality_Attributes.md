### Software Quality Attributes

Software quality attributes are the non-functional requirements that describe how well a software system performs its functional requirements. They are also known as quality factors, quality characteristics, or quality criteria. Some examples of software quality attributes are:

- **Reliability**: The ability of a software system to function correctly and consistently under specified conditions and to recover from failures.
- **Usability**: The ease of use and learnability of a software system for its intended users and stakeholders.
- **Security**: The degree of protection that a software system provides against unauthorized access, use, modification, or destruction of data or resources.
- **Performance**: The responsiveness, throughput, and resource consumption of a software system under normal and peak workloads.
- **Maintainability**: The ease of modifying, testing, and deploying a software system to cope with changing requirements, defects, or environmental factors.
- **Scalability**: The ability of a software system to handle increasing or decreasing demands without degrading its quality or performance.
- **Portability**: The ease of adapting, transferring, or deploying a software system to different platforms, environments, or configurations.
- **Availability**: The proportion of time that a software system is operational and accessible to its users and stakeholders.
- **Testability**: The degree to which a software system can be effectively and efficiently tested to verify its functional and non-functional requirements.
- **Reusability**: The extent to which a software system or its components can be reused in other software systems or contexts.
