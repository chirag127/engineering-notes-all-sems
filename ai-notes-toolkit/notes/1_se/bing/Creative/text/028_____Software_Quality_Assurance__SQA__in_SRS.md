### Software Quality Assurance (SQA) in SRS

- Software Quality Assurance (SQA) is the process of ensuring that the software products and processes meet the specified quality standards and requirements.
- SQA involves activities such as planning, monitoring, reviewing, testing, auditing, and improving the software quality throughout the software development life cycle (SDLC).
- SQA aims to prevent defects, errors, and failures in the software products and processes, and to ensure customer satisfaction and compliance with regulations and standards.
- SQA in SRS refers to the application of SQA principles and practices to the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document, which is a formal description of the software system's functional and non-functional requirements, constraints, assumptions, and dependencies.
- SQA in SRS helps to ensure that the SRS document is clear, complete, consistent, correct, feasible, testable, traceable, and verifiable, and that it meets the needs and expectations of the stakeholders and users.
- SQA in SRS involves activities such as:

  - Reviewing the SRS document for quality attributes and criteria, such as readability, accuracy, completeness, consistency, correctness, feasibility, testability, traceability, and verifiability.
  - Checking the SRS document for compliance with standards, guidelines, and best practices, such as IEEE 830, ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148, and CMMI.
  - Validating the SRS document with the stakeholders and users, such as by conducting interviews, surveys, prototyping, or walkthroughs, to ensure that the SRS document reflects their needs and expectations, and to resolve any ambiguities, conflicts, or gaps in the requirements.
  - Verifying the SRS document with the software design and implementation, such as by conducting inspections, testing, or analysis, to ensure that the SRS document is consistent and aligned with the software architecture and code, and to identify any deviations, errors, or defects in the requirements or the software.
  - Updating and maintaining the SRS document throughout the SDLC, such as by applying change control, configuration management, and traceability techniques, to ensure that the SRS document reflects the current and evolving state of the software system and its requirements, and to keep track of the changes and their impacts on the software quality.
