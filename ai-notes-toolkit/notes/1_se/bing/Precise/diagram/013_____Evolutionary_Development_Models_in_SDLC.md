### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

Evolutionary development models are iterative and incremental approaches to software development. They involve the development of a system through repeated cycles and in smaller portions at a time, allowing software developers to take advantage of what was learned during the development of earlier parts or versions of the system.

Here is an ASCII diagram that illustrates the process of evolutionary development models in SDLC:

```
+----------------+
|  Requirements  |
+-------+--------+
        |
        v
+-------+--------+
|   Design/Plan  |
+-------+--------+
        |
        v
+-------+--------+
| Implementation |
+-------+--------+
        |
        v
+-------+--------+
|   Verification |
+-------+--------+
        |
        v
+-------+--------+
|  Maintenance   |
+-------+--------+
        |
        v
+----------------+
|  Next Version  |
+----------------+
```

In this model, the development process starts with gathering and defining requirements. Then, the design and planning phase takes place, followed by the implementation of the software. After the implementation, the software is verified and tested to ensure it meets the requirements. If any issues are found, they are fixed during the maintenance phase. Once the software is stable, the process starts again for the next version of the software, incorporating any new requirements or changes that have been identified.

This model allows for flexibility and adaptability, as changes and new requirements can be incorporated into the development process as they arise. It also allows for the delivery of a working product to the customer earlier in the development process, as each iteration produces a usable version of the software. However, it can also be more difficult to manage and may require more resources than other development models.
