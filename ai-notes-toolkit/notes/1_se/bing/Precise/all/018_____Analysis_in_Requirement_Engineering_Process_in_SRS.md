### Analysis in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

Requirement analysis is the process of understanding the customer's needs and documenting them in a Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document. It is the first and most crucial phase of the requirement engineering process. The main goal of requirement analysis is to identify the needs of the stakeholders and to specify the requirements that the software must fulfill to meet those needs.

The following are the steps involved in the requirement analysis process:

1. **Elicitation**: This step involves gathering requirements from stakeholders through various techniques such as interviews, questionnaires, and observation.

2. **Analysis**: In this step, the requirements gathered in the elicitation phase are analyzed to identify any inconsistencies, conflicts, or missing information.

3. **Specification**: The requirements are then documented in a formal SRS document, which serves as a contract between the customer and the development team.

4. **Validation**: The SRS document is reviewed by the stakeholders to ensure that it accurately captures their needs and that all requirements are feasible.

The SRS document is a critical artifact of the requirement engineering process, as it serves as the foundation for the design, development, and testing of the software. A well-written SRS document can help to ensure that the software meets the needs of the stakeholders and can reduce the risk of costly rework later in the development process.

A mnemonic to remember the steps of the requirement analysis process is **EASY**: **E**licitation, **A**nalysis, **S**pecification, **Y**ield (Validation).
