### Elicitation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

Elicitation is the process of gathering information from stakeholders, customers, users, and other sources to determine the requirements for a system. It is a critical step in the requirement engineering process, as it helps to ensure that the system being developed meets the needs and expectations of its users.

1. **Interviews:** One of the most common methods of elicitation is through interviews with stakeholders, customers, and users. This can be done in person, over the phone, or through video conferencing. The goal of the interview is to gather information about the needs and expectations of the users, as well as any constraints or limitations that may impact the development of the system.

2. **Questionnaires and Surveys:** Another common method of elicitation is through the use of questionnaires and surveys. These can be distributed to a large number of people and can help to gather information about the needs and expectations of the users, as well as any constraints or limitations that may impact the development of the system.

3. **Workshops:** Workshops can be an effective way to gather information from a group of stakeholders, customers, or users. These can be structured in a variety of ways, but typically involve a facilitator who leads the group through a series of exercises and discussions to gather information about the needs and expectations of the users, as well as any constraints or limitations that may impact the development of the system.

4. **Observation:** Observation can be a useful method of elicitation, particularly when the system being developed is intended to support or automate an existing process. By observing the users as they perform their tasks, the development team can gain a better understanding of the needs and expectations of the users, as well as any constraints or limitations that may impact the development of the system.

5. **Prototyping:** Prototyping can be an effective way to gather feedback from users and stakeholders. By creating a working model of the system, the development team can demonstrate the functionality of the system and gather feedback from the users. This can help to identify any issues or areas of improvement that may need to be addressed during the development process.

Mnemonic: **IQWOP** (Interviews, Questionnaires and Surveys, Workshops, Observation, Prototyping) can help to remember the different methods of elicitation.

In summary, elicitation is a critical step in the requirement engineering process, as it helps to ensure that the system being developed meets the needs and expectations of its users. There are several methods of elicitation, including interviews, questionnaires and surveys, workshops, observation, and prototyping. Each method has its advantages and disadvantages, and the most effective approach will depend on the specific needs of the project.
