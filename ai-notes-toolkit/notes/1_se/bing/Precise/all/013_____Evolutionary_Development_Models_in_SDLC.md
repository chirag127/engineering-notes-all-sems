### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

Evolutionary Development Models in Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) are iterative and incremental approaches to software development. These models focus on the delivery of a working product in a short amount of time and then improving it through multiple iterations.

There are several types of Evolutionary Development Models, including:

1. **Prototyping Model:** This model involves building a prototype of the software to get feedback from the users and then refining the prototype until it meets the user's requirements.

2. **Spiral Model:** This model combines the iterative nature of prototyping with the controlled and systematic aspects of the Waterfall model. It involves identifying and managing risks at each iteration.

3. **Rapid Application Development (RAD) Model:** This model focuses on rapid development and delivery of a working product through the use of techniques such as reusing software components and using powerful development tools.

4. **Agile Model:** This model emphasizes flexibility and adaptability to changing requirements. It involves close collaboration between the development team and the users, and the delivery of working software in short iterations.

Advantages of Evolutionary Development Models include:

- Early delivery of a working product
- Flexibility to changing requirements
- Improved user satisfaction
- Reduced development time

Disadvantages of Evolutionary Development Models include:

- Lack of documentation
- Increased complexity
- Higher risk of scope creep

A mnemonic to remember the types of Evolutionary Development Models is **"P.S.R.A"** which stands for **Prototyping, Spiral, Rapid Application Development, and Agile**.

In conclusion, Evolutionary Development Models are iterative and incremental approaches to software development that focus on delivering a working product quickly and improving it through multiple iterations. These models have their advantages and disadvantages and are suitable for projects with changing requirements and a need for rapid delivery.
