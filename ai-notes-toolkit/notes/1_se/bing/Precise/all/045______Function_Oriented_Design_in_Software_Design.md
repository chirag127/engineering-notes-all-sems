#### Function Oriented Design in Software Design

Function Oriented Design is a software design methodology that focuses on the decomposition of the system into a set of interacting functions. The main idea behind this approach is to identify the major functions that the system must perform and design the software around these functions.

- The system is viewed as a collection of functions that transform inputs into outputs.
- The design process involves identifying the major functions and decomposing them into sub-functions.
- The functions are organized in a hierarchy, with the most important functions at the top and the least important at the bottom.
- The design is represented using a structure chart, which shows the hierarchy of functions and their interactions.
- Function Oriented Design is suitable for systems that are primarily concerned with data processing and transformation.
- It is not suitable for systems that are event-driven or have complex user interfaces.

Advantages:
- The design is easy to understand and communicate, as it is based on a clear hierarchy of functions.
- The design process is systematic and well-defined, which can help to reduce errors and improve the quality of the design.
- The design is modular, which makes it easier to maintain and modify the software.

Disadvantages:
- The design may not be suitable for systems that are event-driven or have complex user interfaces.
- The design may not be flexible enough to accommodate changes in the requirements or the environment.
- The design may not be able to take full advantage of object-oriented programming languages and techniques.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:
- Remember the acronym FOD: Function Oriented Design.
- Remember the structure chart as a visual representation of the hierarchy of functions.
- Remember the advantages and disadvantages by associating them with the characteristics of the design: easy to understand, systematic, modular, not suitable for event-driven systems, not flexible, not object-oriented.
