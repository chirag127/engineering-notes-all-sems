### Feasibility Study in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

A feasibility study is an analysis that takes all of a project's relevant factors into account—including economic, technical, legal, and scheduling considerations—to ascertain the likelihood of completing the project successfully. In the context of Software Requirement Specification (SRS), a feasibility study aims to determine whether the proposed software solution is feasible within the constraints of the project.

There are several types of feasibility studies that may be conducted in the context of SRS, including:

1. **Technical feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed solution can be implemented using the available technology and resources. It takes into account factors such as hardware and software requirements, system compatibility, and technical expertise.

2. **Economic feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed solution is financially viable. It takes into account factors such as development costs, operational costs, and expected return on investment.

3. **Operational feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed solution can be integrated into the existing business processes and whether it will be accepted by the users. It takes into account factors such as user acceptance, training requirements, and support requirements.

4. **Legal feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed solution complies with the relevant laws and regulations. It takes into account factors such as data protection, intellectual property, and contractual obligations.

5. **Scheduling feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed solution can be completed within the given timeframe. It takes into account factors such as project duration, resource availability, and project dependencies.

Conducting a feasibility study is an important step in the SRS process as it helps to identify potential problems and risks early on, allowing for better planning and decision-making. It also helps to ensure that the proposed solution is realistic and achievable, increasing the likelihood of project success.

A mnemonic to remember the types of feasibility studies is **TELOS**, which stands for **T**echnical, **E**conomic, **L**egal, **O**perational, and **S**cheduling. This can be helpful in remembering the different aspects to consider when conducting a feasibility study in the context of SRS.
