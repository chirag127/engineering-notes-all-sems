# Big Data

1. Big data refers to extremely large data sets that may be analyzed computationally to reveal patterns, trends, and associations, especially relating to human behavior and interactions.
2. The challenges include capture, storage, search, sharing, transfer, analysis, and visualization. The trend to larger data sets is due to inexpensive storage, internet connectivity advances, power (e.g. cloud computing), and sensor proliferation.
3. The field includes new software tools, data sources, architectures, and applications as social networks, Internet companies, and organizations seek competitive advantage from data analytics and new ways of data processing.
4. Data mining methods extract patterns from data, automated pattern learning, and forecasting or decision making. Machine learning uses statistical techniques, or customized algorithms, to learn and act without being explicitly programmed.
5. Visualization is a key enabler of understanding, communicating, and managing big data. Graphics and interaction make abstract data concrete, understandable, and engaging. The wave of data has led to waves of innovation in visualization.
6. Privacy concerns exist with the increased ease of aggregating and linking data. Applications and technologies simultaneously drive and are shaped by social, legal, and ethical changes surrounding privacy protection.
