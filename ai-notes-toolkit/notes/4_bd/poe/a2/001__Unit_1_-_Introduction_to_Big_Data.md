## Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data

1. What is Big Data?
- Big data refers to extremely large data sets that may be analyzed computationally to reveal patterns, trends, and associations, especially relating to human behavior and interactions.
- The data is too large or complex for standard data processing tools to capture, store, manage, and analyze.

2. The 5 V's of Big Data
- Volume: Refers to the huge amount of data.
- Velocity: Refers to the speed at which the data is generated and processed.
- Variety: Refers to the different types and formats of data.
- Veracity: Refers to the biases, noise, and abnormality in data.
- Value: Refers to the value that can be derived by analyzing the data.

3. Why is Big Data important?
- Big Data enables innovations and new discoveries. Analysis of large data sets can reveal patterns and insights that would not be apparent with smaller data sets.
- Big Data allows for more accurate predictions and decisions. The insights gained can enable improvements in processes, forecasting, and decision making.
- Big Data has applications in almost every industry like healthcare, transportation, and education. It can transform businesses and society.

4. Challenges with Big Data
- Capturing and storing the huge volume of data.
- Processing the data quickly enough (velocity).
- Handling the variety of data from different sources in different formats.
- Ensuring veracity or accuracy of data as the volume increases.
- Analyzing the data efficiently and deriving value from it.
- Privacy, security, and ethical concerns with large data sets.
