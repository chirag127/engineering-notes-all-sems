### Big Data features – security, compliance, auditing and protection

- Big data security refers to the measures and tools used to protect big data from unauthorized access, modification, or deletion. Big data security is essential to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of big data.
- Big data compliance refers to the adherence of big data to the laws, regulations, standards, and policies that govern its collection, processing, storage, and use. Big data compliance is important to ensure the legality, ethics, and accountability of big data.
- Big data auditing refers to the process of examining and verifying big data to ensure its quality, accuracy, completeness, and reliability. Big data auditing is useful to detect and correct errors, anomalies, or fraud in big data.
- Big data protection refers to the preservation and recovery of big data from accidental or intentional loss, damage, or destruction. Big data protection is necessary to ensure the durability, availability, and resilience of big data.

Some of the challenges and solutions for big data features are:

- Big data security challenges include:
  - The large volume, variety, and velocity of big data make it difficult to apply traditional security methods and tools, such as encryption, access control, or firewalls.
  - The distributed and heterogeneous nature of big data sources, platforms, and applications increase the complexity and risk of security breaches and attacks.
  - The lack of standardization and interoperability among big data technologies and vendors hamper the implementation and enforcement of security policies and best practices.
- Big data security solutions include:
  - Developing and adopting new security methods and tools that can scale and adapt to the dynamic and diverse characteristics of big data, such as homomorphic encryption, attribute-based encryption, or blockchain.
  - Implementing and integrating security mechanisms and controls at different layers and stages of the big data lifecycle, such as data collection, processing, storage, and analysis.
  - Establishing and following security standards and frameworks that can provide guidance and recommendations for big data security, such as the NIST Big Data Security and Privacy Guidelines or the CSA Big Data Security and Privacy Handbook.
- Big data compliance challenges include:
  - The global and cross-border nature of big data raises legal and ethical issues regarding the jurisdiction, sovereignty, and ownership of big data, as well as the protection of personal data and privacy rights of data subjects.
  - The dynamic and evolving nature of big data regulations and requirements make it difficult to keep track and comply with the latest and relevant laws, rules, and norms, such as the GDPR, the CCPA, or the HIPAA.
  - The lack of transparency and accountability of big data processes and outcomes make it hard to demonstrate and verify the compliance of big data, as well as to identify and address any non-compliance issues or violations.
- Big data compliance solutions include:
  - Developing and adopting new compliance methods and tools that can enable and automate the compliance of big data, such as data anonymization, data lineage, or data governance.
  - Implementing and integrating compliance mechanisms and controls at different layers and stages of the big data lifecycle, such as data collection, processing, storage, and analysis.
  - Establishing and following compliance standards and frameworks that can provide guidance and recommendations for big data compliance, such as the ISO/IEC 27000 series or the COBIT 5 framework.
- Big data auditing challenges include:
  - The high volume, variety, and velocity of big data make it difficult to audit and validate big data, as well as to ensure its consistency and correctness across different sources, platforms, and applications.
  - The complex and opaque nature of big data algorithms and models make it difficult to audit and explain big data, as well as to ensure its fairness and trustworthiness.
  - The lack of auditing skills and expertise among big data practitioners and stakeholders make it difficult to audit and improve big data, as well as to communicate and collaborate on big data auditing issues and solutions.
- Big data auditing solutions include:
  - Developing and adopting new auditing methods and tools that can enable and automate the auditing of big data, such as data quality assessment, data provenance, or data visualization.
  - Implementing and integrating auditing mechanisms and controls at different layers and stages of the big data lifecycle, such as data collection, processing, storage, and analysis.
  - Establishing and following auditing standards and frameworks that can provide guidance and recommendations for big data auditing, such as the ISACA Big Data Audit Program or the AICPA Big Data Audit Guide.
