### Zookeeper

A zookeeper is a person who works in a zoo and is responsible for the care and management of the animals. Some of the duties of a zookeeper are:

- Feeding the animals according to their dietary needs and schedules.
- Cleaning the enclosures and providing fresh water and bedding for the animals.
- Monitoring the health and behavior of the animals and reporting any signs of illness or injury to the veterinarian.
- Enriching the environment of the animals to stimulate their natural behaviors and prevent boredom and stress.
- Educating the public about the animals and their conservation status and needs.
- Assisting with the breeding and research programs of the zoo.
- Handling and restraining the animals when necessary for medical or safety reasons.
- Following the safety and ethical protocols of the zoo and the animal welfare laws and regulations.

To become a zookeeper, one usually needs to have:

- A high school diploma or equivalent.
- A bachelor's degree in zoology, biology, animal science, wildlife management, or a related field.
- Experience working with animals, preferably in a zoo or wildlife setting.
- A passion for animals and conservation.
- Physical stamina, patience, and good communication skills.

Some of the benefits of being a zookeeper are:

- Working with a variety of animals and learning about their behaviors and needs.
- Contributing to the conservation and education efforts of the zoo and the society.
- Having a rewarding and fulfilling career that makes a difference for the animals and the environment.

Some of the challenges of being a zookeeper are:

- Working in all weather conditions and sometimes in unpleasant or hazardous situations.
- Dealing with the physical and emotional demands of caring for the animals, especially when they are sick, injured, or die.
- Facing the ethical dilemmas and criticisms of keeping animals in captivity and the potential risks of zoonotic diseases and animal attacks.
- Having a low salary and limited career advancement opportunities.
