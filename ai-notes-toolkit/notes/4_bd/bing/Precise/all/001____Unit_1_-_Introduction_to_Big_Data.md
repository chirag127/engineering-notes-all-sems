## Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data

1. **Definition:** Big data refers to the large, diverse sets of information that grow at ever-increasing rates. It encompasses the volume of information, the velocity or speed at which it is created and collected, and the variety or scope of the data points being covered.

2. **Characteristics:** Big data is often characterized by the 3Vs: Volume, Velocity, and Variety.

    - **Volume:** The amount of data being generated and stored is massive and continues to grow exponentially.
    - **Velocity:** The speed at which data is being generated and processed is increasing rapidly.
    - **Variety:** Data comes in many different formats, including structured, semi-structured, and unstructured.

3. **Sources:** Big data can come from a variety of sources, including social media, online transactions, sensor data, and machine-generated data.

4. **Processing:** Processing big data requires specialized tools and techniques, such as distributed computing, parallel processing, and machine learning algorithms.

5. **Applications:** Big data has numerous applications, including business intelligence, predictive analytics, and personalized marketing.

6. **Challenges:** Some of the challenges associated with big data include data storage, data analysis, data visualization, and data privacy.

7. **Mnemonics:** A helpful mnemonic to remember the 3Vs of big data is "Big Data is a **V**ery **V**ery **V**ast subject". Another mnemonic to remember the sources of big data is "Big data **S**ources **S**eem **O**mnipresent", where the first letters of the words spell out "social media, online transactions, sensor data".

8. **Learning trick:** A helpful learning trick for understanding big data is to think of it as a large jigsaw puzzle. Each piece of data is like a puzzle piece, and the goal is to fit all the pieces together to reveal the bigger picture. Just like with a jigsaw puzzle, it can be helpful to start with the edges (i.e. the most obvious data points) and work your way inwards.
