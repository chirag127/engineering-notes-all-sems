## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is one of the oldest and richest in the world, spanning across various languages, genres, and periods. Some of the major literary traditions in India are Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali, Tamil, Persian, Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, and English. Indian literature reflects the diversity and complexity of Indian culture, religion, philosophy, and history.
- Indian culture is a synthesis of various influences, such as indigenous, Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Persian, Greek, Islamic, European, and modern. Indian culture is characterized by its diversity, pluralism, tolerance, and continuity. Some of the salient features of Indian culture are its art, architecture, music, dance, literature, languages, cuisines, festivals, customs, and values.
- Indian tradition is the collective wisdom and practices of the people of India, transmitted from generation to generation. Indian tradition encompasses various aspects of life, such as family, society, religion, ethics, law, education, health, and environment. Indian tradition is based on the principles of dharma (duty), karma (action and consequence), and moksha (liberation).
- Indian practices are the specific ways of doing things in India, influenced by its culture and tradition. Indian practices vary from region to region, community to community, and individual to individual. Some of the common Indian practices are greeting with namaste (bowing with folded hands), wearing bindi (a dot on the forehead), applying tilak (a mark on the forehead), wearing sacred thread (a symbol of initiation), performing puja (worship), celebrating festivals (such as Diwali, Holi, Eid, etc.), fasting (abstaining from food or certain foods), yoga (a system of physical and mental exercises), meditation (a technique of focusing the mind), ayurveda (a system of holistic medicine), and vegetarianism (avoiding meat and animal products).
