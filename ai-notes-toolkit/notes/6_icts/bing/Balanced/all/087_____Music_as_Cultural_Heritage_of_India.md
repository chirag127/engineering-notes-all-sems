# Music as Cultural Heritage of India

- Music is an integral part of the cultural heritage of India. It reflects the diversity, history and evolution of the Indian civilization.
- Music of India can be broadly classified into two main traditions: Indian classical music and Indian folk music. Both traditions have regional variations and subgenres, and are influenced by various religious, philosophical and social factors.
- Indian classical music is based on the concepts of raga (melodic framework) and tala (rhythmic cycle). It is divided into two main styles: Carnatic music, which is prevalent in South India, and Hindustani music, which is dominant in North India. Both styles have a rich repertoire of vocal and instrumental music, and are performed by soloists or ensembles.
- Indian folk music encompasses a wide range of genres, such as bhajan (devotional songs), bhangra (Punjabi dance music), lavani (Maharashtrian song and dance), qawwali (Sufi music), dandiya (Gujarati folk dance), and baul (Bengali mystic songs). Folk music is often associated with festivals, rituals, celebrations and social movements, and reflects the local culture and identity of the people.
- Music of India has also been influenced by various external sources, such as Persian, Arabic, Turkish, Central Asian, European and American music. These influences have resulted in the emergence of new genres, such as ghazal (poetic songs), thumri (semi-classical songs), filmi (cinema music), pop, rock, jazz, fusion and electronic music. These genres have contributed to the global recognition and popularity of Indian music.
