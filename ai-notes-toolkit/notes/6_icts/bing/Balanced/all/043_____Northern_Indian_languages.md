# Northern Indian languages

- Northern Indian languages are mainly from the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family.
- They include the Hindustani lingua franca, which has two standard forms: Hindi and Urdu. Hindi is the official language of India and Urdu is the official language of Pakistan. Both languages share a common grammar and vocabulary, but differ in their scripts and some cultural words.
- Other Northern Indian languages are Punjabi, Kashmiri, Dogri, Pahari, Sindhi, and various Bihari languages such as Bhojpuri, Magadhi, and Maithili. These languages have their own scripts, alphabets, and literary traditions.
- Northern Indian languages have evolved from the ancient Sanskrit language, which was the language of the Vedic texts and the classical literature of India. They have also been influenced by other languages such as Persian, Arabic, Turkish, English, and regional languages.
- Northern Indian languages are spoken by millions of people in India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, and other countries. They are also used as lingua francas in many regions and communities. They have rich and diverse cultures, histories, and literatures.
