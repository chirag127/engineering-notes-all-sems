# Dance as Cultural Heritage of India

- Dance is an ancient and celebrated cultural tradition in India that reflects the diversity and richness of its history, religion, and art.
- Dance in India can be classified into two main categories: classical and folk. Classical dances are based on formal rules and techniques, while folk dances are spontaneous and expressive.
- Classical dances of India have their origins in Hindu religious musical theatres, such as the Natya Shastra, a Sanskrit treatise on performing arts. The Sangeet Natak Academy recognises eight classical dance forms in India, namely, Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kuchipudi, Kathakali, Odissi, Mohiniyattam, Manipuri and Satriya.
- Bharatanatyam is one of the oldest and most popular classical dances of India, originating from Tamil Nadu. It is characterised by graceful movements, intricate footwork, expressive gestures, and elaborate costumes and jewellery.
- Kathak is a classical dance form that originated in northern India and was influenced by the Mughal culture. It is known for its fast spins, complex rhythms, and storytelling through gestures and facial expressions.
- Kuchipudi is a classical dance form from Andhra Pradesh that combines dance, drama, and music. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who enact various roles and characters from Hindu mythology.
- Kathakali is a classical dance form from Kerala that is famous for its elaborate makeup, masks, and costumes. It is a highly stylised and dramatic form of dance that depicts stories from the epics Ramayana and Mahabharata.
- Odissi is a classical dance form from Odisha that is based on the sculptures of the ancient temples. It is characterised by fluid movements, graceful poses, and intricate hand gestures.
- Mohiniyattam is a classical dance form from Kerala that is performed by female dancers. It is a graceful and lyrical form of dance that portrays the feminine aspects of the divine.
- Manipuri is a classical dance form from Manipur that is influenced by the Vaishnavite culture. It is a vibrant and colourful form of dance that involves group performances, acrobatic movements, and musical instruments.
- Satriya is a classical dance form from Assam that is associated with the Vaishnava monasteries. It is a devotional and theatrical form of dance that incorporates elements of drama, music, and literature.
- Folk dances of India are diverse and varied, reflecting the regional and cultural differences of the country. They are performed during festivals, weddings, and other occasions, and often involve themes such as harvest, love, war, and mythology.
- Some examples of folk dances of India are:

  - Dumhal and Rouff of Jammu and Kashmir, which are performed by men wearing colourful robes and tall conical caps.
  - Bhangra and Giddha of Punjab, which are energetic and lively dances that involve drumming, clapping, and singing.
  - Garba and Dandiya of Gujarat, which are circular dances that involve the use of sticks and lamps.
  - Lavani and Dhangar of Maharashtra, which are sensual and rhythmic dances that involve the use of musical instruments and props.
  - Chhau and Santhal of West Bengal, which are martial and tribal dances that involve masks, weapons, and acrobatics.
  - Bihu and Sattriya of Assam, which are festive and religious dances that involve colourful costumes, jewellery, and flowers.
  - Ghoomar and Kalbelia of Rajasthan, which are graceful and elegant dances that involve swirling movements, veils, and snake motifs.
  - Bharatnatyam and Karagattam of Tamil Nadu, which are classical and folk dances that involve balancing of pots, fire, and water.
  - Kuchipudi and Kolattam of Andhra Pradesh, which are classical and folk dances that involve sticks, ropes, and plates.
  - Kathakali and Theyyam of Kerala, which are classical and folk dances that involve elaborate makeup, masks, and costumes.
