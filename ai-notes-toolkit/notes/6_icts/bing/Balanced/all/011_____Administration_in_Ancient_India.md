# Administration in Ancient India

- Administration in ancient India refers to the structures, procedures and behaviors that the ruling dynasties or emperors used to govern their respective territories and people.
- Administration in ancient India can be divided into four main periods: the Indus Valley civilization, the Vedic period, the Mauryan period and the Gupta period.
- Each period had its own features and challenges in terms of administration, but some common elements can be identified across them.

## Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1700 BCE)

- The Indus Valley civilization was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world, covering parts of modern-day India, Pakistan and Afghanistan.
- The administration of the Indus Valley civilization was based on a well-planned and organized system of urban planning, trade, agriculture, sanitation, water management and public works.
- The Indus Valley civilization had no written records, so the exact nature and structure of its administration is not known. However, some scholars have suggested that it was a theocratic or priestly rule, while others have proposed that it was a decentralized or cooperative rule by local elites or councils.
- The Indus Valley civilization had a uniform and standardized system of weights, measures, seals, scripts and coins, which indicates a high degree of administrative coordination and control.
- The Indus Valley civilization also had a sophisticated system of drainage, sewage, wells, reservoirs and canals, which shows a remarkable level of administrative efficiency and foresight.

## Vedic Period (1500-600 BCE)

- The Vedic period was the period when the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, were composed and transmitted orally by the Indo-Aryan people who migrated to the Indian subcontinent from Central Asia.
- The administration of the Vedic period was based on a tribal or clan-based system of social and political organization, known as the varna-ashrama-dharma system.
- The varna-ashrama-dharma system divided the society into four main classes or varnas: the Brahmins (priests and teachers), the Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), the Vaishyas (merchants and farmers) and the Shudras (servants and laborers). Each varna had its own duties and responsibilities, and was expected to follow the dharma or moral code of conduct.
- The varna-ashrama-dharma system also divided the life of an individual into four stages or ashramas: the Brahmacharya (student), the Grihastha (householder), the Vanaprastha (retiree) and the Sannyasa (renunciant). Each ashrama had its own rules and goals, and was meant to guide the spiritual development of the individual.
- The administration of the Vedic period was also based on a territorial or geographical system of political units, known as the janapadas or republics. The janapadas were self-governing entities, consisting of a number of villages or towns, and headed by a king or a chief. The king or the chief was assisted by a council of elders or a assembly of citizens, who advised him on matters of governance and justice.
- The administration of the Vedic period was also influenced by the religious and ritualistic aspects of the Vedic culture, such as the performance of sacrifices, the worship of gods and goddesses, and the observance of festivals and ceremonies. The Brahmins played a key role in the administration of the Vedic period, as they were the custodians of the Vedic knowledge and the mediators between the human and the divine realms.

## Mauryan Period (321-185 BCE)

- The Mauryan period was the period when the Mauryan Empire, the first pan-Indian empire, was founded and expanded by Chandragupta Maurya and his successors, especially Ashoka the Great.
- The administration of the Mauryan period was based on a centralized and hierarchical system of bureaucracy, taxation, law and order, and military.
