# Astronomy in India and Ancient India

- Astronomy in India has a long history stretching from pre-historic to modern times. 
- Some of the earliest references to astronomy are found in the Rig Veda, which are dated 2000 BC. 
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas. 
- Astronomy in ancient India can be divided into two major classes: Vedic astronomy and Siddhantic astronomy. 
- Vedic astronomy was based on the Vedas and other ancient texts, and focused on the cosmology, mythology, and rituals related to the celestial phenomena. 
- Siddhantic astronomy was based on mathematical calculations and observations, and focused on the planetary motions, eclipses, calendars, and astrology. 
- Some of the prominent astronomers of ancient India were Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and Lalla.   
- They made significant contributions to the fields of trigonometry, algebra, geometry, arithmetic, and astronomy.   
- They also developed various astronomical instruments, such as the gnomon, the water clock, the armillary sphere, and the astrolabe.  
- They also had contact and exchange of ideas with other civilizations, such as Greece, Persia, and China.  
- Astronomy in India continued to flourish in the medieval and modern periods, with the development of new schools, texts, and techniques. 
- Some of the notable astronomers of these periods were Sripati, Madhava, Nilakantha, Jyesthadeva, Munisvara, and Raja Jai Singh. 
- They made advances in the fields of calculus, spherical astronomy, heliocentrism, and planetary theory. 
- They also built large observatories, such as the Jantar Mantar, and improved the accuracy and precision of the astronomical measurements.
