# Council of Ministers in Ancient India

- The council of ministers was a group of advisers and officials who assisted the king in governing the state.
- The council of ministers was known by different names, such as Mantri-parishad, Mantri-mandala, or Mantri-gana, in different periods and regions of ancient India .
- The council of ministers was composed of various ranks and portfolios, such as the prime minister, the chief priest, the commander-in-chief, the treasurer, the chief justice, and the provincial governors  .
- The council of ministers was selected by the king based on their qualifications, character, loyalty, and experience. They underwent rigorous training in various disciplines, such as politics, law, economics, military, and ethics  .
- The council of ministers had various functions and powers, such as advising the king, implementing his policies, administering justice, collecting revenue, maintaining security, and conducting diplomacy  .
- The council of ministers was expected to work in harmony with the king and each other, and to uphold the welfare of the people and the state. They were also accountable to the king and the law for their actions  .
