#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

- Āshrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras.
- The Āshrama system is one facet of the Dharma concept in Hinduism, which prescribes the duties and responsibilities of an individual according to their age, occupation, and social status .
- The four Āshramas are:

  - Brahmacharya (student): This stage lasts from childhood until the completion of education, usually around the age of 25. The student is expected to live a life of celibacy, devotion, and obedience to their teacher (guru), and to study the scriptures and other subjects  .
  - Gṛhastha (householder): This stage begins with marriage and lasts until the age of 50 or 60. The householder is expected to fulfill their duties towards their family, society, and the gods, by earning a livelihood, raising children, performing rituals, and supporting the other Āshramas  .
  - Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller): This stage begins when the householder has fulfilled their obligations and renounces worldly attachments. The forest dweller retires to a secluded place, usually in the wilderness, and devotes themselves to spiritual pursuits, such as meditation, penance, and service to humanity  .
  - Sannyasa (renunciate): This stage is the final and highest stage of life, in which the individual renounces all worldly ties and possessions, and seeks liberation (moksha) from the cycle of birth and death. The renunciate lives a life of detachment, austerity, and wisdom, and is respected as a spiritual authority by the society  .

- The Āshrama system was not rigid or compulsory, and some individuals could skip or combine some stages, or remain in one stage for their entire life, depending on their personal choice and circumstances .
- The Āshrama system was a way of organizing the social and spiritual life of the ancient Indian society, and reflected the values and ideals of Hinduism. It aimed to provide a balanced and harmonious development of the individual and the society, and to prepare the individual for the ultimate goal of liberation  .
