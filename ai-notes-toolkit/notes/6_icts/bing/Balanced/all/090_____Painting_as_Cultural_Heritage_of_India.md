# Painting as Cultural Heritage of India

- Painting is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of art in India, dating back to prehistoric times.
- Painting reflects the cultural, religious, and political aspects of Indian society, as well as the aesthetic and creative expressions of the artists.
- Painting is also a medium of preserving and transmitting the intangible cultural heritage of India, such as songs, dances, rituals, and stories.
- Some of the major types of Indian paintings are:

  - **Madhubani painting**: A folk art form from Bihar, characterized by intricate geometric patterns, bright colors, and symbolic motifs depicting nature, deities, and social events.
  - **Warli painting**: A tribal art form from Maharashtra, characterized by simple linear figures, monochromatic hues, and scenes of everyday life, agriculture, and ceremonies.
  - **Mural painting**: A large-scale painting on walls, ceilings, or other surfaces, often depicting religious or historical themes. Examples include the Ajanta and Ellora caves, the Brihadeshwara temple, and the Kerala murals.
  - **Miniature painting**: A small-scale painting on paper, cloth, or other materials, often illustrating manuscripts, books, or albums. Examples include the Mughal, Rajasthani, Pahari, and Deccani schools of painting.
  - **Tanjore painting**: A classical art form from Tamil Nadu, characterized by rich colors, gold foils, gems, and relief work, depicting Hindu gods and goddesses, saints, and scenes from mythology.
  - **Kalighat painting**: A folk art form from Bengal, characterized by bold outlines, flat colors, and stylized figures, depicting religious, social, and contemporary themes.
