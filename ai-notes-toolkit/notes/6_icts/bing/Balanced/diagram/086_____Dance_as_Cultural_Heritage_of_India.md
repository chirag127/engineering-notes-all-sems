### Dance as Cultural Heritage of India

- Dance is an ancient and celebrated cultural tradition in India that reflects the diversity, history and beliefs of its people.
- Dance in India can be classified into two main categories: classical and folk.
- Classical dance forms are based on the ancient texts of Natya Shastra and Abhinaya Darpana, and have a codified set of rules, techniques and gestures.
- Classical dance forms are usually performed in temples, courts and theatres, and are accompanied by classical music, costumes and makeup.
- The Sangeet Natak Academy recognises eight classical dance forms in India, namely, Bharatnatyam, Kathak, Kuchipudi, Kathakali, Odissi, Mohiniyattam, Manipuri and Satriya.
- Each classical dance form has its own regional origin, style, theme and expression, and showcases the rich heritage and cultural diversity of India.
- Folk dance forms are more spontaneous, informal and flexible, and are performed by the common people during festivals, weddings and other occasions.
- Folk dance forms are influenced by the local geography, climate, culture and traditions, and often incorporate elements of storytelling, humour and social commentary.
- Every part of India has its unique folk dance, such as Dumhal and Rouff of Jammu and Kashmir, Bhangra and Giddha of Punjab, Garba and Dandiya of Gujarat, Lavani and Koli of Maharashtra, Bihu and Bagurumba of Assam, Chhau and Santhal of West Bengal, Ghoomar and Kalbelia of Rajasthan, and many more.
- Folk dance forms are a vibrant and colourful expression of the joy, spirit and identity of the Indian people.
- Dance in India has also made its mark in the global arena, as the classical dance forms have been performed and taught by renowned artists across the world, and the folk dance forms have been adapted and fused with other genres of dance and music.
- Dance in India is not only a form of art, but also a way of life, a medium of communication, a mode of worship, and a source of inspiration.
