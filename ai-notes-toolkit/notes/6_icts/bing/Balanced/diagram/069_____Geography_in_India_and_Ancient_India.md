### Geography in India and Ancient India

- India is a large and diverse country in South Asia, with a land area of 3.28 million square kilometers and a population of 1.3 billion people.
- India is bounded by the Himalayan mountains in the north, the Indian Ocean in the south, the Arabian Sea in the west, and the Bay of Bengal in the east.
- India has a variety of landforms, such as plains, plateaus, hills, deserts, and islands. The Indo-Gangetic Plain is the most fertile and densely populated region of India, where the rivers Ganga, Yamuna, and Indus flow. The Deccan Plateau covers most of the southern and central India, and is rich in minerals and volcanic rocks. The Thar Desert is the largest desert in India, located in the northwest. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are a group of islands in the Bay of Bengal, and the Lakshadweep Islands are a group of coral islands in the Arabian Sea.
- India has a tropical monsoon climate, with four seasons: winter (December to February), summer (March to May), monsoon (June to September), and post-monsoon (October to November). The monsoon winds bring heavy rainfall to most parts of India, especially the coastal regions and the northeast. The temperature varies from region to region, with the highest temperatures in the Thar Desert and the lowest temperatures in the Himalayas.
- Ancient India was one of the earliest civilizations in the world, along with Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica. Ancient India is also known as the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished from 2500 to 1900 BCE in the northwest of India and Pakistan. The Indus Valley Civilization was known for its urban planning, sanitation, trade, writing, and art. Some of the major cities of the Indus Valley Civilization were Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro, Lothal, and Dholavira.
- Ancient India also witnessed the rise of various religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Hinduism is the oldest and the largest religion in India, based on the Vedas, the Upanishads, and the epics Ramayana and Mahabharata. Buddhism was founded by Gautama Buddha in the 6th century BCE, and spread across Asia and beyond. Jainism was founded by Mahavira in the 6th century BCE, and emphasized non-violence, asceticism, and karma. Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century CE, and combined elements of Hinduism and Islam.
- Ancient India also contributed to various fields of knowledge, such as mathematics, astronomy, medicine, literature, and architecture. Some of the famous ancient Indian scholars were Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Charaka, Sushruta, Kalidasa, and Panini. Some of the famous ancient Indian monuments are the Ajanta and Ellora caves, the Sanchi stupa, the Khajuraho temples, and the Taj Mahal.
