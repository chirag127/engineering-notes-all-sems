### Puppetry as Cultural Heritage of India

- Puppetry is one of the most imaginative and ancient ways of telling a story in the society.
- Puppetry has been in India a means of entertainment and a form of cultural and religious dissemination.
- Indian puppetry is a subject as varied as the many cultural traditions of the Indian subcontinent.
- Several regional genres make puppetry one of the richest heritages of India.
- Though there is enormous diversity, there are common traits among the different forms of puppetry in India.
- The origin of puppetry in India cannot be dated, but mythology provides at least two stories that are witness to the importance of this art.
- One story is about the sage Bharata, who created the first puppet show to illustrate the Ramayana to his disciples.
- Another story is about the sage Shukracharya, who revived the dead soldiers of the demon king Bali by using puppets.
- The main types of puppetry in India are: string puppets, shadow puppets, rod puppets, glove puppets and leather puppets.
- Each type has its own distinctive features, techniques, styles, themes and regional variations.
- Some of the famous examples of puppetry in India are: Kathputli of Rajasthan, Bommalattam of Tamil Nadu, Tholpavakoothu of Kerala, Putul Nautch of West Bengal, Gombeyatta of Karnataka, and Ravanachhaya of Odisha .
- Puppetry in India is not only a form of art, but also a medium of education, social awareness, and cultural preservation.
- Puppetry in India reflects the rich diversity, creativity, and heritage of the Indian people.
- Puppetry in India is facing many challenges due to the high-speed modernization wave that invested India in the post-colonial period.
- Several government and private initiatives have contributed to save endangered styles, but many are still at risk of extinction.
- Puppetry in India needs more support, recognition, and promotion to survive and thrive in the contemporary world.
