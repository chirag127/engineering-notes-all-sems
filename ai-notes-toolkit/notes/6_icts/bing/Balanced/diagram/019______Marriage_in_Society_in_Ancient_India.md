#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage was a sacred institution that united two halves of life, man and woman, into a single organic whole.
- Marriage was also a social contract that established kinship ties, rights and duties, and mutual support between the spouses and their families.
- Marriage was regulated by various laws and customs, such as caste, gotra, varna, and kula, that determined the eligibility and compatibility of the partners.
- Marriage was performed through different rites and ceremonies, depending on the type and status of the marriage.
- There were eight main types of marriages in ancient India, namely Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya, Gandharva, Asura, Rakshasa, and Pisaka, each with its own characteristics and implications .
- Marriage was influenced by various factors, such as religion, culture, politics, and economics, that shaped the norms and expectations of the society.
- Marriage was not a static or uniform phenomenon, but a dynamic and diverse one, that changed and evolved over time and space.
