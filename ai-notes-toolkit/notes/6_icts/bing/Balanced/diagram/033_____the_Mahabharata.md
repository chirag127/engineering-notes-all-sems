### The Mahabharata

- The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of India, along with the Ramayana .
- It is a long and complex narrative that covers the history, culture, religion, and philosophy of ancient India.
- It tells the story of the conflict between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, who fight for the throne of Hastinapura, a kingdom in northern India .
- The main events of the Mahabharata are the Kurukshetra War, a 18-day battle that ends with the victory of the Pandavas, and the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between the Pandava prince Arjuna and the god Krishna, who advises him on his duty and the meaning of life.
- The Mahabharata is divided into 18 books, or parvas, that contain about 74,000 verses, making it one of the longest poems in the world.
- The Mahabharata is considered to be both a historical account of the origins of Hinduism and a code of ethics for the followers of dharma, the moral and cosmic order .
- The Mahabharata is also a source of many stories, characters, and teachings that have influenced Indian literature, art, and culture for centuries.
