### North Indian Literature

- North Indian literature is the literature produced in the languages of northern India, such as Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Rajasthani, etc.
- North Indian literature is influenced by the ancient Indian background, which includes Sanskrit epics, Puranas, and Buddhist and Jain texts.
- North Indian literature also reflects the diversity of cultures, religions, and ethnicities in the region, such as the Mughal, Sikh, and British influences.
- Some of the prominent genres of North Indian literature are poetry, drama, novel, short story, essay, and autobiography.
- Some of the notable authors of North Indian literature are Kabir, Tulsidas, Mirza Ghalib, Premchand, Rabindranath Tagore, Amrita Pritam, Munshi Premchand, Bhisham Sahni, etc.
- North Indian literature is distinct from the literature of Northeast India, which is the literature produced in the states of Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, and Tripura.
- Northeast Indian literature is characterized by its rich cultural and ethnolinguistic diversity, as well as its representation of varied histories, socio-political issues, and environmental concerns.
- Some of the genres of Northeast Indian literature are folk literature, oral literature, poetry, fiction, non-fiction, and translation.
- Some of the notable authors of Northeast Indian literature are Birendra Kumar Bhattacharya, Mamang Dai, Temsula Ao, Easterine Kire, Mitra Phukan, etc.
