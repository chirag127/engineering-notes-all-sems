#### Understanding Gender as a Social Category in Society in Ancient India

- Gender is a social construct that defines the roles, expectations and norms associated with different sexes in a given society.
- In ancient India, gender roles were not fixed or rigid, but varied according to the time period, region, caste, class and religion.
- Generally, ancient India's society greatly favored men over women, and women were often subordinate to men in terms of rights, education, property and freedom.
- However, there were also some exceptions and variations in the status and role of women in ancient India, depending on the sources and contexts.
- Some of the main points to understand gender as a social category in society in ancient India are:

  - In the Vedic period (1500-600 BCE), women were considered with respect and dignity, and had access to education and religious rituals. There were women rishis (sages) and scholars, such as Gargi, Maitreyi and Lopamudra, who participated in philosophical debates and composed hymns. Women could also choose their husbands and remarry if widowed .
  - In the later Vedic period (600-300 BCE), the status of women declined as the society became more patriarchal and hierarchical. Women were expected to be obedient and loyal to their husbands and fathers, and were confined to the domestic sphere. They lost their rights to education, property and inheritance, and were subjected to practices such as child marriage, sati (self-immolation of widows) and dowry .
  - In the Mauryan period (321-185 BCE), the status of women varied according to their class and occupation. Women from the upper classes were secluded and oppressed, while women from the lower classes and the Buddhist and Jain communities had more freedom and opportunities. Women could also participate in trade, agriculture and crafts, and some women became rulers, such as Prabhavati Gupta and Rudramadevi .
  - In the Gupta period (320-550 CE), the status of women further deteriorated as the society became more feudal and orthodox. Women were denied education and public life, and were subjected to harsh laws and punishments. They were also exploited and discriminated by the caste system and the religious institutions. However, some women still excelled in literature, art and science, such as Kalidasa's Shakuntala, Banabhatta's Yashomati and Aryabhata's Lilavati .
  - In the medieval period (600-1700 CE), the status of women was influenced by the invasions, migrations and cultural interactions of various groups, such as the Turks, Mongols, Arabs, Persians and Europeans. Women faced violence, insecurity and oppression, and were often treated as spoils of war or objects of trade. They were also forced to follow strict codes of conduct and dress, such as purdah (veiling) and jauhar (mass suicide of women to avoid capture). However, some women also resisted and challenged the patriarchal norms, such as Razia Sultana, Rani Durgavati, Mirabai and Nur Jahan .
