### Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Jain literature refers to the literature of the Jain religion, which is one of the oldest and major religions of India.
- Jain literature is a vast and ancient literary tradition, which was initially transmitted orally by the teachers and disciples of Jainism.
- Jain literature is composed in various languages, such as Prakrit, Pali, Sanskrit, Apabhramsha, Hindi, Gujarati, Tamil, Kannada and Rajasthani.
- The most important and authoritative Jain texts are the canonical Jain Agamas, which are written in Ardhamagadhi, a Prakrit (Middle-Indo Aryan) language.
- The Jain Agamas consist of 12 Angas (limbs), 12 Upangas (subsidiary limbs), 6 Chhedasutras (disciplinary codes), 4 Mulasutras (root texts), 10 Prakirnas (miscellaneous texts) and 2 Culikas (appendices).
- The Jain Agamas contain the teachings of the 24 Tirthankaras (ford-makers), the spiritual leaders of Jainism, especially Mahavira, the last and most prominent Tirthankara.
- The Jain Agamas deal with various topics, such as ethics, cosmology, karma, rebirth, liberation, non-violence, asceticism, meditation, logic, epistemology and metaphysics.
- The Jain Agamas were compiled and codified in the 6th century CE in Valabhi, Gujarat, by the council of Jain monks and scholars.
- The Jain Agamas are divided into two main sects: the Digambaras (sky-clad) and the Svetambaras (white-clad), who differ in their interpretation and preservation of the texts.
- The Digambaras believe that the original Jain Agamas were lost and only some secondary texts, such as the Purvas, the Shatkhandagama and the Kasayaprabhrta, are available.
- The Svetambaras believe that they have preserved the original Jain Agamas and have also added some later texts, such as the Niryuktis, the Bhashyas, the Churnis and the Tikas, as commentaries and explanations.
- Apart from the canonical Jain Agamas, there are many other Jain texts written in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit, which are classified as non-canonical or extra-canonical literature.
- Some of the prominent Jain texts written in Pali are the Jaina Suttas, the Jaina Vinaya, the Jaina Abhidhamma and the Jaina Niddesa, which are similar to the Buddhist Pali Canon, but with a different perspective and emphasis.
- Some of the prominent Jain texts written in Prakrit are the Gathasaptasati, the Bhaktamara Stotra, the Samayasara, the Pravachanasara, the Niyamasara, the Pancastikayasara and the Tattvarthasutra, which are poetic, devotional, philosophical and doctrinal works.
- Some of the prominent Jain texts written in Sanskrit are the Kavyas, the Mahakavyas, the Stotras, the Subhashitas, the Natakas, the Alankaras, the Shastras and the Puranas, which are literary, artistic, scientific and historical works.
- Some of the famous Jain authors who wrote in Sanskrit are Haribhadra, Hemachandra, Siddhasena, Somadeva, Jinasena, Asadhara, Pujyapada, Akalanka, Vidyananda and Vidyadhara.
