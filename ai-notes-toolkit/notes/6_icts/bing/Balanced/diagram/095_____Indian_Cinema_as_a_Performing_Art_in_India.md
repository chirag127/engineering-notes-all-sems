### Indian Cinema as a Performing Art in India

- Indian cinema is a form of art that combines storytelling, performing arts, and science to create audio-visual narratives that reflect the culture, history, and diversity of India .
- Indian cinema has a long and rich history that dates back to the silent era in the early 20th century, when pioneers like Dadasaheb Phalke and Raja Harishchandra made the first feature films in India.
- Indian cinema has evolved over the decades, influenced by various social, political, and economic factors, as well as by the changing tastes and preferences of the audiences.
- Indian cinema has also been influenced by the classical and folk traditions of Indian performing arts, such as theatre, dance, music, and puppetry .
- Indian cinema has adopted and adapted the concepts of rasa (emotion), natya (drama), and nritta (dance) from the ancient treatise on performing arts, the Natyashastra, written by Bharata Muni.
- Indian cinema has also been influenced by the Indian People's Theatre Association (IPTA), a progressive cultural movement that emerged in the 1940s and aimed to use theatre and cinema as a medium of social change and awareness.
- Indian cinema has produced many genres, styles, and movements, such as the parallel cinema, the new wave cinema, the masala cinema, the regional cinema, and the art cinema, among others.
- Indian cinema has also produced many acclaimed and influential filmmakers, actors, and technicians, such as Satyajit Ray, Raj Kapoor, Guru Dutt, Ritwik Ghatak, Shyam Benegal, Nargis, Amitabh Bachchan, A.R. Rahman, and many more.
- Indian cinema has also gained global recognition and appreciation, winning awards and accolades at various international film festivals and platforms, such as the Cannes Film Festival, the Academy Awards, the BAFTA Awards, and the Golden Globe Awards.
- Indian cinema is a vibrant and dynamic form of art that continues to grow and innovate, exploring new themes, techniques, and formats, such as the digital cinema, the web series, the documentary cinema, and the animation cinema.
