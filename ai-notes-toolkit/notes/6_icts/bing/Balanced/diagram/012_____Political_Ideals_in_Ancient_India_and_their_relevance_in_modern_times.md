### Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

- Ancient India had a rich tradition of political thought and practice that spanned from the Vedic period to the medieval period.
- Some of the political ideals that emerged from this tradition are:

  - Democracy: The Rig Veda, the oldest of the four Vedas, extolled the concept of **Samjnana**, which means the collective consciousness of the people, the national mind to which the individual mind is to pay its homage as the source from which it derives its potency. The Rig Veda also mentioned the institution of **Sabha** and **Samiti**, which were assemblies of the people that participated in the governance of the society. The later Vedic texts also described the role of **Panchayat**, which was a council of five elders that resolved disputes and administered justice at the local level.
  - Monarchy: Monarchy was also a popular form of government in ancient India, especially in the later Vedic period and the subsequent periods of the Mauryan, Gupta, and Mughal empires. The king was considered to be the representative of God on earth and was expected to rule according to the **Dharma**, which was the moral and cosmic law that governed the universe. The king was also advised by a council of ministers, priests, and other officials, who helped him in the administration of the state. The king was also bound by the **Dandaniti** or the science of politics, which was based on the **Itihasa** or the fifth Veda, which contained the historical and legendary accounts of the past kings and their deeds.
  - Republic: Republic was another form of government that existed in ancient India, especially in the regions of the Gangetic plains and the Himalayan foothills. These were independent states that were governed by a **Gana** or a **Sangha**, which was a confederation of clans or tribes that elected their own rulers and representatives. These republics had their own constitutions, laws, and armies, and maintained diplomatic relations with other states. Some of the famous republics of ancient India were the **Shakyas**, the **Koliyas**, the **Mallas**, the **Vrijis**, and the **Lichchhavis**.

- The relevance of these political ideals in modern times are:

  - Democracy: Democracy is the most widely accepted and practiced form of government in the modern world, as it ensures the participation and representation of the people in the decision-making process. Democracy also promotes the values of freedom, equality, justice, and human rights, which are essential for the development and well-being of the society. Democracy also fosters a culture of dialogue, debate, and diversity, which enriches the intellectual and cultural life of the nation. Democracy in India is the largest and one of the oldest in the world, as it traces its roots to the ancient Vedic tradition of Samjnana, Sabha, and Samiti.
  - Monarchy: Monarchy is a form of government that is based on the hereditary succession of a single ruler, who has absolute or limited power over the state. Monarchy is not very common or popular in the modern world, as it is seen as a violation of the democratic principles and human rights. However, monarchy can also have some positive aspects, such as stability, continuity, and legitimacy, which can benefit the state in times of crisis or transition. Monarchy can also preserve the cultural and historical heritage of the nation, as the monarch can act as a symbol of national identity and unity. Monarchy in India was abolished after the independence from the British rule, but some of the former princely states still retain their titles and privileges as a part of the Indian federal system.
  - Republic: Republic is a form of government that is based on the sovereignty of the people, who elect their own representatives and rulers. Republic is similar to democracy, but it differs in the sense that it does not have a hereditary or appointed head of state, but rather a president or a prime minister who is accountable to the people. Republic also emphasizes the separation of powers, the rule of law, and the protection of the rights and liberties of the citizens. Republic is a common and desirable form of government in the modern world, as it ensures the accountability and responsiveness of the government to the people.
