### Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

Pyrotechnics are the art and science of creating and using fireworks for entertainment, military, or ceremonial purposes. Pyrotechnics have a long and rich history in India, dating back to the medieval period. Here are some points to note about pyrotechnics in India and ancient India:

- One of the earliest notes of pyrotechnical shows in India is made by Abdur Razzaq, the ambassador of the Timurid Sultan Shahrukh to the court of the Vijayanagar king Devaraya II in 1443. He wrote that he witnessed a grand display of fireworks at the king's palace, which lasted for an hour and filled the sky with various colors and shapes.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows existed as a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events and special occasions like weddings. Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of Orissa .
- Gunpowder, an essential ingredient of fire crackers, was unknown in ancient India. It was introduced to India by the Mongols and the Arabs in the 13th century. The earliest reference to gunpowder in India is found in the Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi, a historical work by Ziauddin Barani, who mentions the use of gunpowder and cannons by the Delhi Sultanate in the 14th century.
- The Mughal emperors were fond of fireworks and often organized spectacular displays for their guests and subjects. Akbar, the greatest of the Mughal rulers, is said to have employed a special department of pyrotechnists, who were skilled in making various kinds of fireworks, such as rockets, fountains, wheels, and snakes.
- The British also brought their own tradition of fireworks to India, especially during the colonial era. They celebrated events like the coronation of Queen Victoria, the Delhi Durbar, and the end of World War I with lavish fireworks shows. They also introduced new types of fireworks, such as crackers, sparklers, and roman candles, to the Indian market.
- Today, fireworks are an integral part of many Indian festivals, especially Diwali, the festival of lights. Fireworks are also used for weddings, birthdays, and other celebrations. India is one of the largest producers and consumers of fireworks in the world, with a thriving industry based in Sivakasi, Tamil Nadu. However, fireworks also pose environmental and health hazards, such as air and noise pollution, fire accidents, and injuries. Therefore, there is a need for more awareness and regulation of the use of fireworks in India.
