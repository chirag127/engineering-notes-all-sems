### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

- Shankaracharya, also known as Adi Shankara, was an Indian philosopher and theologian who expounded the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta, the non-dualistic school of Hindu philosophy .
- He was born in the 8th century CE in Kaladi, a village in Kerala, and renounced the worldly pleasures at a very young age to pursue spiritual knowledge.
- He traveled across India, debating and defeating various schools of thought, such as Buddhism, Jainism, Mimamsa, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, and Samkhya, and establishing the supremacy of Advaita Vedanta .
- He also wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, explaining the basic ideas of Advaita Vedanta and reconciling the apparent contradictions in the scriptures .
- He established four mathas (monasteries) in the four corners of India, namely Sringeri in the south, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east, and Badrinath in the north, and appointed four disciples as the heads of these mathas .
- He also composed many hymns and verses in praise of various deities, such as Shiva, Vishnu, Devi, and Ganesha, and advocated the practice of devotion (bhakti) along with knowledge (jnana) as a means of liberation (moksha) .
- He is regarded as the most authoritative philosopher of Advaita Vedanta and one of the greatest thinkers of Indian history .
- Advaita Vedanta is the school of Hindu philosophy that asserts the non-duality of reality, meaning that there is only one ultimate reality, called Brahman, which is identical with the innermost self (atman) of all beings.
- Advaita Vedanta holds that the phenomenal world of multiplicity and diversity is an illusion (maya) caused by ignorance (avidya) of the true nature of reality.
- Advaita Vedanta teaches that the goal of human life is to realize one's identity with Brahman and attain liberation from the cycle of birth and death (samsara).
- Advaita Vedanta traces its origins to the Upanishads, the ancient philosophical texts that form the core of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism.
- Advaita Vedanta was systematized and defended by Shankaracharya and his followers, who wrote commentaries and sub-commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, the three foundational texts of Vedanta .
- Advaita Vedanta has influenced various aspects of Indian culture, such as art, literature, music, ethics, and spirituality, and has also attracted the attention of Western scholars and seekers.
- Advaita Vedanta's cultural influence started only centuries after Shankaracharya, in the Vijayanagara Empire in the 14th century, when Sringeri matha started to receive patronage from the kings of the Vijayanagara Empire and became a powerful institution.
