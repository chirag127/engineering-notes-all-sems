### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

- Chemistry in ancient India was called **Rasayana** in Sanskrit, which means **extract** or **essence** of substances  .
- Chemistry in ancient India was not confined to theoretical knowledge, but found development in various practical activities, such as metallurgy, medicine, alchemy, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, agriculture, and warfare  .
- Some of the achievements of chemistry in ancient India are:
  - The discovery and extraction of metals, such as gold, silver, copper, iron, zinc, tin, and lead, from their ores, and the production of alloys, such as brass, bronze, and steel   .
  - The development of Ayurveda, the system of medicine that uses natural substances, such as herbs, minerals, and animal products, to treat various diseases and promote health and longevity   .
  - The invention of various chemical processes, such as distillation, calcination, sublimation, fermentation, and extraction, and the use of various apparatus, such as crucibles, retorts, alembics, and furnaces, to perform them   .
  - The creation of various chemical substances, such as acids, alkalis, salts, mercury, sulfur, arsenic, and borax, and their applications in medicine, alchemy, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, and warfare    .
  - The formulation of various theories and concepts, such as the atomic theory, the five elements theory, the theory of transmutation, the theory of fermentation, and the theory of taste, to explain the nature and properties of matter and its transformations    .
- Some of the prominent chemists in ancient India are:
  - Kanada, who proposed the atomic theory and classified atoms into different types based on their properties and combinations   .
  - Nagarjuna, who wrote the Rasaratnakara, a treatise on alchemy, metallurgy, and medicine, and discovered various methods of extracting and purifying metals and minerals   .
  - Charaka, who wrote the Charaka Samhita, a compendium of Ayurvedic medicine, and described various diseases, symptoms, diagnoses, treatments, and drugs   .
  - Sushruta, who wrote the Sushruta Samhita, a treatise on surgery and medicine, and performed various surgical procedures, such as cataract removal, rhinoplasty, and caesarean section, using various instruments and techniques   .
  - Patanjali, who wrote the Mahabhashya, a commentary on grammar and linguistics, and discussed various aspects of chemistry, such as the origin of metals, the properties of acids and alkalis, and the theory of taste    .
