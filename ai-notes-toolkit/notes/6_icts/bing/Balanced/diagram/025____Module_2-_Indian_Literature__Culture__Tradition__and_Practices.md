## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is the expression of the diverse and rich cultural heritage of India, which includes oral traditions, folklores, epics, myths, poetry, drama, and prose in various languages and scripts.
- Indian culture is the result of the fusion of various ethnic groups, religions, philosophies, and customs that have influenced the way of life in India for thousands of years. Some of the unique aspects of Indian culture are:
  - Greetings: Indians greet each other by folding their hands and saying "Namaste" or "Namaskar", which means "I bow to the divine in you".
  - Religious customs: India is home to many religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam, Christianity, and Zoroastrianism. Each religion has its own rituals, festivals, symbols, and sacred texts that are followed by its adherents.
  - Festivals of India: India celebrates a variety of festivals throughout the year, such as Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas, Guru Nanak Jayanti, and Pongal. These festivals reflect the diversity, unity, and joy of the Indian people.
  - Family structure and marriage: Indians value the institution of family and respect their elders and ancestors. The family is usually patriarchal, joint, and extended, with several generations living together. Marriage is considered a sacred bond and is arranged by the parents or elders of the bride and groom.
  - Symbols: India has many symbols that represent its culture, such as the national emblem, the national flag, the national anthem, the lotus, the peacock, the swastika, the om, and the bindi.
  - Cuisine and food: Indian cuisine is known for its variety, spice, and flavor. It is influenced by the regional, religious, and climatic factors of the country. Some of the common ingredients used in Indian food are rice, wheat, lentils, spices, herbs, and dairy products. Some of the popular dishes are biryani, curry, dal, roti, dosa, idli, samosa, and chaat.
  - Traditional clothing: Indians wear different types of clothing depending on the region, religion, and occasion. Some of the common types of clothing are saree, salwar kameez, kurta, dhoti, lungi, sherwani, and turban. The colors and patterns of the clothing often have symbolic meanings and reflect the culture and identity of the wearer.
  - Dances of India: India has a rich and diverse tradition of classical, folk, and tribal dances that express the emotions, stories, and values of the people. Some of the famous classical dances are Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, and Odissi. Some of the folk and tribal dances are Bhangra, Garba, Ghoomar, Bihu, and Chhau.
  - Epics and mythology: India has a vast and ancient literature of epics and mythology that narrate the deeds of gods, goddesses, heroes, and villains. Some of the most well-known epics are the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, and the Puranas. These epics contain moral lessons, philosophical insights, and cultural values that are relevant to the present day.
  - Martial arts: India has a long and varied history of martial arts that have developed over the centuries for self-defense, warfare, and spiritual development. Some of the martial arts are Kalaripayattu, Gatka, Silambam, Thang-Ta, and Malla-yuddha. These martial arts involve the use of weapons, such as swords, daggers, spears, and sticks, as well as unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, and locks.
  - Languages: India has 22 official languages and hundreds of regional and local languages and dialects that reflect the linguistic diversity of the country. Some of the major languages are Hindi, English, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Urdu, Gujarati, and Kannada. Each language has its own literature, script, and grammar that enrich the Indian culture.
