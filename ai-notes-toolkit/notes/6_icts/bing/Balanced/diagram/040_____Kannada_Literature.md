### Kannada Literature

- Kannada literature is the corpus of written forms of the Kannada language, a member of the Dravidian family spoken mainly in the Indian state of Karnataka and written in the Kannada script .
- The earliest records in Kannada are inscriptions dating from the 6th century AD onward.
- The topics of Kannada literature included grammar, philosophy, prosody, rhetoric, chronicles, biography, history, drama and cuisine, as well as dictionaries and encyclopedias.
- Kannada literature can be broadly divided into three periods: classical, medieval and modern .
- The classical period (9th to 14th century) is marked by the emergence of the champu style of composition, which combines prose and verse, and the development of various literary genres such as epics, courtly poetry, didactic works and anthologies .
- Some of the prominent writers of this period are Pampa, Ranna, Ponna, Nagavarma I, Bilhana, Harihara, Raghavanka, Keshiraja, Andayya and Kumaravyasa .
- The medieval period (14th to 18th century) is characterized by the rise of the bhakti movement, which produced a large body of devotional literature in Kannada, as well as the growth of the vachana and sharana traditions, which expressed the mystical and social aspects of the Veerashaiva faith .
- Some of the notable writers of this period are Basava, Akka Mahadevi, Allama Prabhu, Harihara, Raghavendra Swami, Purandara Dasa, Kanaka Dasa, Sarvajna, Lakshmisa, Ratnakaravarni and Honnali Ramayya .
- The modern period (19th century to present) is marked by the influence of Western education, literature and culture, as well as the emergence of various literary movements such as romanticism, realism, nationalism, social reform, progressive, Dalit, feminist and postmodern .
- Some of the influential writers of this period are B.M. Srikantaiah, Kuvempu, D.V. Gundappa, Masti Venkatesha Iyengar, Bendre, Shivaram Karanth, Gopalakrishna Adiga, U.R. Ananthamurthy, Girish Karnad, P. Lankesh, Tejaswi, Kambar, Chandrashekhara Kambara, U.R. Ananthamurthy, Girish Karnad, P. Lankesh, Tejaswi, Kambar, Chandrashekhara Kambara, and others .
