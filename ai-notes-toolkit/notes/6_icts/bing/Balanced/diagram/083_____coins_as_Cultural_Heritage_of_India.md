### Coins as Cultural Heritage of India

- Coins are one of the most important sources of information about the history, culture, religion, economy, and politics of ancient and medieval India.
- Coins reflect the artistic skills, technological innovations, political aspirations, and religious beliefs of the people who issued and used them.
- Coins also provide valuable insights into the trade, commerce, and monetary systems of different regions and periods of Indian history.
- Coins in India can be broadly classified into four categories: prehistoric and early historic coins, ancient dynastic coins, medieval coins, and modern coins.

#### Prehistoric and Early Historic Coins
- The earliest coins in India were not coins in the modern sense, but rather stamped bars of metal, called karshapanas or panas, that were used as a medium of exchange in the first millennium BCE.
- These coins were made of copper and silver, and had various symbols, such as geometric shapes, animals, plants, and human figures, punched on them.
- These symbols represented the issuing authority, the value, the weight, and the purity of the coins.
- The most common symbols were the sun, the six-armed symbol, the elephant, the bull, the lion, the tree, and the swastika.
- These coins were used by various kingdoms and republics, such as the Magadha, the Kosala, the Kuru, the Gandhara, the Kamboja, and the Shakya, that flourished in northern India during the later Vedic and post-Vedic periods.
- These coins are also mentioned in the ancient texts, such as the Rigveda, the Arthashastra, the Mahabharata, and the Buddhist and Jain scriptures.

#### Ancient Dynastic Coins
- Many dynasties ruled over India and each of them introduced their coins which depicted their culture and beliefs.
- These coins are generally placed between the 2nd century BCE and the 2nd century CE.
- The table given below describes the coins of different ancient Indian dynasties:

| Dynasty | Coins |
| --- | --- |
| Maurya | Silver punch-marked coins, copper cast coins, and lead coins. |
| Indo-Greek | Silver drachmas and tetradrachms, with bilingual legends in Greek and Kharoshthi or Brahmi scripts, and portraits of kings and deities. |
| Shunga | Silver punch-marked coins, copper cast coins, and lead coins, with symbols such as the elephant, the peacock, the hill, and the chaitya. |
| Satavahana | Copper cast coins, lead coins, and potin coins, with legends in Brahmi script, and symbols such as the ship, the lion, the bull, the horse, and the Ujjain symbol. |
| Kushana | Gold dinars and copper coins, with legends in Greek, Kharoshthi, Brahmi, and Bactrian scripts, and portraits of kings and deities, such as Shiva, Buddha, and Kartikeya. |
| Gupta | Gold dinars and silver coins, with legends in Brahmi script, and portraits of kings and deities, such as Vishnu, Lakshmi, and Garuda. |
| Harsha | Silver drachmas and copper coins, with legends in Brahmi script, and symbols such as the bull, the lion, the peacock, and the crescent. |

#### Medieval Coins
- The medieval period in India witnessed the emergence of various regional kingdoms and empires, as well as the invasions of foreign rulers, such as the Arabs, the Turks, the Mongols, and the Mughals.
- These rulers issued coins that reflected their political, religious, and cultural identities, as well as their economic and military power.
- These coins are generally placed between the 8th century CE and the 18th century CE.
