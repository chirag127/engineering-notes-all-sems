### Hindi Literature

- Hindi literature is the literature written in the various Hindi languages, which have writing systems. Hindi is one of the official languages of India and the most widely spoken language in the country.
- Hindi literature has a long and rich history, dating back to the 11th century. It has been influenced by various literary traditions, such as Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali, Persian, Arabic, Urdu, and English.
- Hindi literature can be divided into three broad styles: prose (gadya), poetry (padya), and prosimetrum (campū). Prose includes genres such as novels, short stories, essays, biographies, and travelogues. Poetry includes genres such as epics, lyrics, ballads, and bhakti (devotional) poems. Prosimetrum is a mixed form of prose and poetry, often used for satire, humor, and allegory.
- Hindi literature can also be classified into four major periods: Adikal (early period), Bhaktikal (devotional period), Ritikal (classical period), and Adhunik (modern period). Each period has its own characteristics, themes, and forms of expression.
- Some of the prominent writers and works of Hindi literature are:

  - Adikal: Chand Bardai (Prithviraj Raso), Jayasi (Padmavat), Kabir (Dohas), Tulsidas (Ramcharitmanas), Surdas (Sur Sagar), etc.
  - Bhaktikal: Ramananda, Meera, Nanak, Ravidas, etc.
  - Ritikal: Keshavdas (Rasikpriya), Bihari (Bihari Satsai), Ghananand (Ghananand Granthavali), etc.
  - Adhunik: Premchand (Godan, Nirmala, etc.), Mahadevi Varma (Yama, etc.), Jaishankar Prasad (Kamayani, etc.), Suryakant Tripathi 'Nirala' (Parimal, etc.), Harivansh Rai Bachchan (Madhushala, etc.), etc.

- Hindi literature reflects the diverse and dynamic aspects of Indian culture, society, history, and politics. It also showcases the creativity and innovation of the Hindi language and its speakers. Hindi literature is a valuable source of knowledge and inspiration for anyone interested in learning more about India and its people.
