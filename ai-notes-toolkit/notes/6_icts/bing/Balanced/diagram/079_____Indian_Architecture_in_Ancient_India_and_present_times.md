### Indian Architecture in Ancient India and present times

- Indian architecture is as old as the history of the civilization and reflects the diversity of its culture, religion, and geography.
- The earliest remains of recognizable building activity in India date back to the Indus Valley cities, which had urban planning, drainage systems, and brick houses.
- Among India's ancient architectural remains, the most characteristic are the religious structures, such as temples, chaityas, viharas, stupas, and mosques.
- The rock-cut structures present the most spectacular examples of ancient Indian art and architecture. Most of the rock-cut structures were related to various religious communities, such as Buddhism, Jainism, and Hinduism.
- The temple architecture in India developed over time and can be classified into three broad styles: Nagara, Dravida, and Vesara. Each style has its own distinctive features, such as the shape of the tower, the plan of the hall, and the decoration of the walls.
- The Indo-Islamic architecture emerged from the fusion of Indian and Islamic influences, especially during the Mughal period. Some of the prominent features of this style are the use of arches, domes, minarets, and calligraphy.
- The colonial architecture in India reflects the influence of the British, French, Portuguese, and Dutch, who built forts, churches, palaces, and public buildings in their respective styles.
- The modern architecture in India is influenced by the global trends, as well as the local traditions and needs. Some of the notable architects of modern India are Le Corbusier, Charles Correa, B.V. Doshi, Raj Rewal, and Laurie Baker.
