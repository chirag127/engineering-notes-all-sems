#### Varnāshrama System in Society in Ancient India

- The varnāshrama system was a theoretical framework of social and moral order in ancient Hindu society.
- It consisted of two main concepts: varna and ashrama.
- Varna refers to the four-fold division of society into four classes or castes: Brahman, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra.
- Ashrama refers to the four stages of life that a person should ideally follow: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest dweller) and Sannyasa (renunciate).
- The varnāshrama system was based on the principle of dharma, which means duty, law or righteousness.
- Each varna and ashrama had its own specific duties and obligations, which were prescribed by the sacred texts and the lawgivers.
- The varnāshrama system aimed to maintain the harmony and balance of society and individual, as well as the cosmic order.
- The varnāshrama system was not rigid or fixed, but flexible and adaptable to different times and circumstances.
- The varnāshrama system was not based on birth or heredity, but on qualities, actions and aptitudes of a person.
- The varnāshrama system was not a system of oppression or discrimination, but a system of mutual cooperation and interdependence among the different sections of society.
