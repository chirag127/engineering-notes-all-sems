### Evolution of script in India

- A script is a written symbolisation of speech. It is a collection of characters used to write one or more languages.
- India has a long history of writing, dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1900 BCE), which used a pictographic script that is still undeciphered.
- The earliest deciphered script of India is Brahmi, which was used to write the edicts of Emperor Ashoka (c. 3rd century BCE) and other inscriptions in various languages, such as Prakrit, Sanskrit, Pali, and Tamil.
- Brahmi is the ancestor of most modern Indian scripts, as well as the scripts of Sri Lanka, Tibet, Southeast Asia, and some parts of Central Asia.
- Brahmi evolved into different regional variants over time, depending on the cultural, linguistic, and geographical factors. Some of the major branches of Brahmi are:

  - Northern Brahmi: This includes scripts such as Kharosthi, Gandhari, Saka, Bactrian, and Kushana, which were used in the northwestern regions of India and Central Asia. These scripts were influenced by the Aramaic script of the Persian Empire and had a right-to-left direction of writing.
  - Southern Brahmi: This includes scripts such as Tamil-Brahmi, Vatteluttu, Grantha, and Pallava, which were used in the southern regions of India and Sri Lanka. These scripts were influenced by the Dravidian languages and had a rounded shape due to the use of palm leaves as writing material.
  - Eastern Brahmi: This includes scripts such as Kalinga, Siddhamatrika, and Proto-Bengali, which were used in the eastern regions of India and Southeast Asia. These scripts were influenced by the Magadhi Prakrit and had a vertical stroke at the end of each character.
  - Western Brahmi: This includes scripts such as Gupta, Nagari, Sharada, and Modi, which were used in the western and central regions of India and Nepal. These scripts were influenced by the Sanskrit language and had a horizontal line at the top of each character.

- Due to the greater emphasis on quicker and easier writing rather than monumental inscriptions, some of the Brahmi scripts developed into cursive styles that formed the basis of India's modern scripts. Some of the modern scripts of India are:

  - Devanagari: This is the most widely used script in India, as it is the official script of Hindi, the most spoken language in India. It is also used to write other languages, such as Sanskrit, Marathi, Nepali, and Konkani. It is derived from the Nagari script of Western Brahmi and has a distinctive horizontal line at the top of each character.
  - Bengali-Assamese: This is the script used to write the Bengali and Assamese languages, as well as some other languages of eastern India and Bangladesh. It is derived from the Proto-Bengali script of Eastern Brahmi and has a rounded shape with a vertical stroke at the end of each character.
  - Gujarati: This is the script used to write the Gujarati language, as well as some other languages of western India and Pakistan. It is derived from the Modi script of Western Brahmi and has a cursive style with a horizontal line at the top of each character.
  - Gurmukhi: This is the script used to write the Punjabi language, as well as some other languages of northern India and Pakistan. It is derived from the Sharada script of Western Brahmi and has a horizontal line at the top of each character and a vertical line at the left of each word.
  - Kannada: This is the script used to write the Kannada language, as well as some other languages of southern India and Sri Lanka. It is derived from the Grantha script of Southern Brahmi and has a rounded shape with a horizontal line at the top of each character.
  - Malayalam: This is the script used to write the Malayalam language, as well as some other languages of southern India and Sri Lanka. It is derived from the Vatteluttu script of Southern Brahmi and has a rounded shape with a loop at the end of each character.
  - Odia: This is the script used to write the Odia language, as well as some other languages of eastern India and Bangladesh. It is derived from the Kalinga script of Eastern Brahmi and has a rounded shape with a vertical stroke at the end of each character.
