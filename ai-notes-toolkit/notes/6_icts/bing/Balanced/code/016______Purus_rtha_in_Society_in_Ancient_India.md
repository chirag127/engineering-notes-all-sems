#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

- Purusārtha (Sanskrit: पुरुषार्थ) means "object (ive) of human pursuit" or "human goal".
- It is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life. These are Dharma, Artha, Kama and Moksha.
- Dharma means righteousness, moral values, duty, law and ethics. It is the foundation of the other three goals and guides the human conduct in accordance with the cosmic order. 
- Artha means prosperity, economic values, wealth, security and power. It is the means to achieve the other goals and to fulfill the material needs of life. 
- Kama means pleasure, love, desire, enjoyment and sensuality. It is the expression of the human emotions and passions in a balanced and harmonious way. 
- Moksha means liberation, emancipation, freedom and release. It is the ultimate goal of human life and the transcendence of the cycle of birth and death. 
- The concept of Purusārtha is a fundamental principle of social ethics and a way to seek truth and attain salvation. It is not only meant to set an ideal objective but also to convey the right means to achieve them.
- The concept of Purusārtha is also related to the concept of Karma, which means action, deed, work and causality. Karma is the law of cause and effect that governs the human actions and their consequences.
- According to the concept of Karma, the human actions are influenced by the three gunas or qualities of nature: sattva (purity, goodness, harmony), rajas (activity, passion, dynamism) and tamas (inertia, ignorance, darkness).
- The human actions also produce positive or negative results, which are called punya (merit) and papa (sin). These results affect the present and future lives of the individual and determine their destiny.
- The concept of Karma implies that the human beings are responsible for their actions and their outcomes, and that they can shape their own destiny by following the path of Dharma and pursuing the Purusārtha.
- The concept of Purusārtha also reflects the diversity and complexity of the human society and the individual needs and aspirations. The four goals are not mutually exclusive or contradictory, but complementary and interdependent. 
- The concept of Purusārtha also varies according to the four classes (varnas) and the four stages (ashramas) of the human life. The four classes are Brahmins (priests, teachers, intellectuals), Kshatriyas (warriors, rulers, administrators), Vaishyas (merchants, traders, farmers) and Shudras (servants, laborers, artisans). The four stages are Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (retired) and Sannyasa (renunciant). 
- The concept of Purusārtha also evolves and adapts to the changing times and circumstances of the human history and culture. The concept of Purusārtha is not rigid or dogmatic, but flexible and dynamic.
- The concept of Purusārtha is a unique and profound contribution of the ancient Indian civilization to the world. It is a holistic and comprehensive vision of the human life and its purpose.
