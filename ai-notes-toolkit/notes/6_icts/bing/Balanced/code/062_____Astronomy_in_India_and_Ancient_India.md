### Astronomy in India and Ancient India

- Astronomy in India has a long history stretching from pre-historic to modern times. Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilisation or earlier.
- Astronomy in ancient India developed to a sophisticated level as the sages of ancient India were keen observers of the sky generation after generation. They recorded their observations and calculations in various texts, such as the Vedas, the Puranas, the Siddhantas, the Aryabhatiya, the Surya Siddhanta, and the Brihat Samhita.
- Astronomy in ancient India can be divided into two major classes. From the third-century BCE, India started having contact with Greece and other nations outside the subcontinent. So, the basic structure of Indian astronomy had outside influence although the astronomical concepts and the major parameters were India’s own.
- The first class of Indian astronomy is called Vedic astronomy, which is based on the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism. Vedic astronomy deals with the cosmology, mythology, and ritual aspects of the celestial phenomena. It also contains some astronomical observations and measurements, such as the lunar and solar eclipses, the solstices, and the equinoxes.
- The second class of Indian astronomy is called Siddhantic astronomy, which is based on the Siddhantas, or the treatises on astronomy. Siddhantic astronomy deals with the mathematical and computational aspects of the celestial phenomena. It also contains some astronomical theories and models, such as the heliocentric, geocentric, and hybrid systems, the planetary motions, the precession of the equinoxes, and the calculation of the calendar.
- Some of the most prominent astronomers of ancient India are Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and Lalla. They made significant contributions to the fields of astronomy, mathematics, and astrology. They also influenced the development of astronomy in other regions, such as Islamic and Byzantine astronomy .
