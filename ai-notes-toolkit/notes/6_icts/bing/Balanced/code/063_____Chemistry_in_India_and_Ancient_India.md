### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

Chemistry is the science of matter and its interactions. Chemistry in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times. Here are some points to note about chemistry in India and ancient India:

- Chemistry in ancient India was called **Rasayana** in Sanskrit, the language in vogue. Rasayana derived its name from **Rasa**, which means **extract**, maybe from roots, leaves, and stems of plants .
- Chemistry in ancient India was not confined but found development in a variety of practical activities, such as metallurgy, medicine, alchemy, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, agriculture, and warfare .
- Chemistry in ancient India was closely linked to other branches of knowledge, such as astronomy, mathematics, philosophy, and religion. For example, the concept of **Panchamahabhuta** (five elements) was a common framework for understanding the nature of matter and its transformations .
- Chemistry in ancient India was influenced by and contributed to the cultures and civilizations of other regions, such as Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, China, and Persia. For example, India was a major source of **spices**, **aromatics**, **dyes**, **gems**, and **metals** for the ancient world .
- Chemistry in ancient India reached its peak during the **Gupta period** (4th-6th century CE), when many texts and treatises on chemistry were composed by eminent scholars, such as **Nagarjuna**, **Kanada**, **Varahamihira**, **Charaka**, and **Sushruta** . These texts covered topics such as **mercury**, **gold**, **iron**, **steel**, **copper**, **zinc**, **tin**, **lead**, **silver**, **alloys**, **sulfur**, **salts**, **acids**, **alkalis**, **minerals**, **ores**, **plants**, **animals**, **medicines**, **poisons**, and **elixirs**  .
- Chemistry in ancient India also developed many **instruments** and **techniques** for chemical experiments, such as **crucibles**, **furnaces**, **retorts**, **distillation**, **sublimation**, **calcination**, **dissolution**, **filtration**, **crystallization**, **precipitation**, **titration**, **colorimetry**, and **electrolysis**  .
- Chemistry in ancient India declined after the **Islamic invasion** (11th-13th century CE), when many libraries, universities, and temples were destroyed and many scholars were killed or exiled. However, some aspects of chemistry in ancient India survived and influenced the **Arabic**, **Persian**, and **European** chemistry in the later centuries  .
