### Sculptures as Cultural Heritage of India

- Sculpture is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India, dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE) and continuing to the present day.
- Sculpture in India reflects the rich cultural, religious, and historical diversity of the country, as well as the influences of various regions, dynasties, and foreign invaders.
- Sculpture in India can be classified into three broad categories: stone, metal, and terracotta. Each category has its own distinctive features, techniques, and styles.
- Stone sculpture is the most prevalent and enduring form of sculpture in India, especially in temples, caves, and monuments. Stone sculpture ranges from simple carvings to elaborate and intricate reliefs and statues. Some of the famous examples of stone sculpture in India are:
  - The Ashoka Pillar at Sarnath, which has a four-lion capital that symbolizes the spread of Buddhism and the authority of Emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE).
  - The rock-cut caves of Ajanta and Ellora, which have exquisite paintings and sculptures depicting the life of Buddha and various Hindu deities (2nd century BCE to 10th century CE).
  - The Khajuraho temples, which have erotic and sensual sculptures that celebrate human love and sexuality as a divine gift (10th to 12th century CE).
  - The Sun Temple at Konark, which has a colossal chariot-shaped structure with intricate carvings of animals, plants, and human figures (13th century CE).
- Metal sculpture is another important form of sculpture in India, especially in bronze and brass. Metal sculpture involves casting, hammering, and polishing techniques to create realistic and dynamic figures. Some of the famous examples of metal sculpture in India are:
  - The Dancing Girl of Mohenjo-daro, which is a small bronze figurine of a young woman with a confident and graceful posture (c. 2500 BCE).
  - The Nataraja or the Cosmic Dancer, which is a bronze statue of Lord Shiva as the lord of dance, who creates and destroys the universe with his movements (c. 10th century CE).
  - The Chola bronzes, which are a series of bronze statues of Hindu gods and goddesses, such as Vishnu, Shiva, Parvati, and Ganesha, that exhibit a high degree of realism and elegance (9th to 13th century CE).
  - The Meenakshi Amman Temple, which has a hall of thousand pillars, each with a brass sculpture of a different deity or mythological figure (16th to 18th century CE).
- Terracotta sculpture is another form of sculpture in India, especially in rural and folk art. Terracotta sculpture involves molding and baking clay to create various shapes and forms. Terracotta sculpture is often colorful, expressive, and playful. Some of the famous examples of terracotta sculpture in India are:
  - The Mother Goddess figurines, which are small clay idols of a female deity with prominent breasts and hips, representing fertility and prosperity (c. 2500 BCE).
  - The Bankura horses, which are large clay horses with elongated necks and ears, representing strength and vitality (c. 18th century CE).
  - The Bishnupur temples, which have terracotta panels depicting scenes from Hindu epics, such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata (17th to 18th century CE).
  - The Durga Puja idols, which are temporary clay statues of Goddess Durga and her family, that are worshipped and immersed in water during the annual festival (19th to 21st century CE).

- Sculpture in India is a valuable cultural heritage that showcases the artistic genius, spiritual vision, and social diversity of the Indian people. Sculpture in India is also a living tradition that continues to evolve and innovate with changing times and contexts.
