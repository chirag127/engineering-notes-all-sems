### Drama as Cultural Heritage of India

- Drama is a form of performing arts, where stories are enacted by the utilisation of dialogue, music, gesture, dance, and sound.
- Drama is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of cultural expression in India, dating back to the ancient Vedic period .
- Drama in India has been influenced by various religious, social, and linguistic traditions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam, and regional languages.
- Drama in India has also been shaped by various classical theatre traditions, such as Sanskrit theatre, folk theatre, and Urdu theatre  .
- Sanskrit theatre is the earliest existing form of drama and theatre in India, based on the works of Bharata, the father of Indian drama . Sanskrit theatre is known for its elaborate and stylized conventions, such as the use of masks, costumes, gestures, and music.
- Folk theatre is a diverse and popular form of drama in India, reflecting the local cultures, beliefs, and customs of different regions. Folk theatre is characterized by its spontaneity, improvisation, humor, and social commentary. Some examples of folk theatre are Nautanki, Tamasha, Jatra, and Bhavai.
- Urdu theatre is a modern form of drama in India, influenced by the Persian and Mughal cultures, as well as the British colonial rule. Urdu theatre is known for its realistic and romantic themes, as well as its use of poetry, music, and dance. Some examples of Urdu theatre are Inder Sabha, Parsi theatre, and Bombay cinema.
- Drama in India is a rich and vibrant cultural heritage, that showcases the diversity, creativity, and history of the Indian people. Drama in India is also a medium of communication, education, and social change, that reflects the contemporary issues and challenges of the Indian society.
