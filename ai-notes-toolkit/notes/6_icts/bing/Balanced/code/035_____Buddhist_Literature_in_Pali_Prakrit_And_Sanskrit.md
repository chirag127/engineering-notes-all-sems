### Buddhist Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Buddhist literature is the collection of writings that preserve the teachings and traditions of Buddhism.
- Buddhist literature can be divided into two main categories: canonical and non-canonical.
- Canonical literature refers to the scriptures that are considered authoritative and authentic by various Buddhist schools and traditions. Non-canonical literature refers to the writings that are not accepted as canonical by all Buddhists, but may have historical, doctrinal, or literary value.
- The canonical literature of Buddhism is written in various languages, depending on the region and time period of its composition. The most prominent languages are Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit.
- Pali is an ancient language that is closely related to Sanskrit, but has some differences in grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation. It is said that the Buddha delivered his sermons in Pali, or a dialect similar to it, and that his disciples preserved his teachings in the same language . Pali is the canonical language of Theravada Buddhism, the oldest and most conservative branch of Buddhism, which is mainly practiced in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. The Pali canon, also known as the Tipitaka or the Three Baskets, consists of three main collections: the Vinaya Pitaka (the Basket of Discipline), the Sutta Pitaka (the Basket of Discourses), and the Abhidhamma Pitaka (the Basket of Higher Teachings). The Pali canon also includes commentaries, sub-commentaries, and other supplementary texts that explain and interpret the scriptures.
- Prakrit is a generic term for any language that is derived from or influenced by Sanskrit, but is not Sanskrit itself. Prakrit languages were spoken by various people in ancient India, especially in the regions of Magadha, Kosala, and Avanti. Prakrit languages are the canonical languages of Mahayana Buddhism, the more liberal and diverse branch of Buddhism, which is mainly practiced in East Asia and Tibet. The Mahayana canon, also known as the Mahayana Sutras or the Perfection of Wisdom Sutras, consists of numerous texts that expound the doctrines and practices of Mahayana Buddhism, such as the concept of emptiness, the bodhisattva ideal, and the six perfections. The Mahayana canon also includes treatises, commentaries, and other works by various Mahayana masters and scholars. The most common Prakrit languages used in the Mahayana canon are Sauraseni, Maharastri, Magadhi, and Gandhari .
- Sanskrit is the classical and literary language of ancient India, which is derived from the Vedic language, the language of the oldest Hindu scriptures. Sanskrit is the canonical language of Vajrayana Buddhism, the esoteric and tantric branch of Buddhism, which is mainly practiced in Tibet, Nepal, Bhutan, and Mongolia. The Vajrayana canon, also known as the Tantras or the Secret Mantra, consists of texts that reveal the secret and advanced teachings and practices of Vajrayana Buddhism, such as the use of mantras, mudras, mandalas, and deities. The Vajrayana canon also includes works by various Vajrayana masters and adepts. Some of the Vajrayana texts are also written in Tibetan, Mongolian, and other languages .
- Buddhist literature in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit reflects the diversity and richness of the Buddhist tradition, as well as the historical and cultural contexts of its development and transmission. Buddhist literature in these languages also influences and interacts with other forms of literature, such as poetry, drama, epics, and stories, in various regions and periods of history. Buddhist literature in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit is a valuable source of knowledge and inspiration for Buddhists and non-Buddhists alike.
