### Famous Sanskrit Authors in India

Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most influential languages of India. It has a rich literary tradition that spans over two millennia. Many Sanskrit authors have contributed to various genres of literature, such as poetry, drama, philosophy, grammar, and religious texts. Here are some of the famous Sanskrit authors in India:

- **Kalidasa**: He is considered to be the greatest Sanskrit poet and dramatist of India. He lived in the 4th or 5th century CE and wrote several works of classical Sanskrit drama, such as Malavikagnimitra, Abhijnanasakuntalam, and Vikramorvasiyam. He also composed lyrical poems, such as Meghaduta, Ritusamhara, and Kumarasambhava .
- **Bhasa**: He is one of the earliest and most prolific Sanskrit dramatists of India. He is said to have lived around the 3rd century CE and wrote thirteen plays, such as Urubhanga, Madhyamavyayoga, and Svapnavasavadatta. His plays are known for their realism, humor, and innovation.
- **Bhartrihari**: He is a renowned Sanskrit poet and philosopher of the 5th or 6th century CE. He is the author of three collections of verses, namely Satakatraya, which consists of Niti Sataka, Sringara Sataka, and Vairagya Sataka. His verses deal with ethical, erotic, and spiritual themes and are noted for their wit, wisdom, and elegance.
- **Banabhatta**: He is a celebrated Sanskrit prose writer and poet of the 7th century CE. He was the court poet of King Harshavardhana and wrote two historical romances, namely Harshacharita and Kadambari. His works are admired for their style, imagination, and narration.
- **Purusottama Deva**: He was a king and a scholar of the 15th century CE. He ruled over Orissa and was a patron of Sanskrit literature and art. He wrote several works, such as Muktichintamani, Gopalarchana Vidhi, and Bhubaneswari Pujapallava. His works reflect his devotion to Lord Krishna and his interest in Tantra.
- **Satyavrat Shastri**: He is a modern Sanskrit poet and scholar of the 20th and 21st centuries. He has written many works in various genres, such as Ramakirtimahakavya, Sribodhisattvacharitam, and Brahattaram Bharatam. He has also translated several works from other languages into Sanskrit. He is the first and only Sanskrit author to win the Jnanpith Award, India's highest literary honor.
