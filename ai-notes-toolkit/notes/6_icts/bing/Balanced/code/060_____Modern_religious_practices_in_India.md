### Modern religious practices in India

- India is a diverse and secular country, with no official religion and a constitution that guarantees freedom of religion to all citizens.
- The major religions practiced in India are Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism and Jainism. Hinduism is the dominant religion, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindu .
- Religion plays a significant role in the lives of most Indians, influencing their culture, politics, economy and social relations.
- Some of the common religious practices in India are:

  - Visiting houses of worship, such as temples, mosques, churches, gurdwaras and monasteries. Most Indians visit their places of worship at least once a week, and some do so daily.
  - Giving to charitable causes, such as feeding the poor, donating to religious institutions, supporting social movements and helping the needy. Most Indians give to charity at least once a year, and some do so monthly or weekly.
  - Celebrating festivals, such as Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Vaisakhi, Buddha Purnima and Mahavir Jayanti. These festivals mark important religious events, such as the birth, enlightenment or death of a deity or a prophet, or the victory of good over evil. They are celebrated with rituals, prayers, offerings, fireworks, lights, sweets and gifts.
  - Performing rituals, such as puja, namaz, mass, kirtan and meditation. These rituals involve worshiping, praising, thanking or seeking guidance from a divine being or a sacred object. They are performed individually or collectively, at home or in public, with or without the help of a priest or a leader.
  - Following dietary rules, such as vegetarianism, halal, kosher, langar and ahimsa. These rules prescribe what foods are allowed or forbidden for religious reasons, such as purity, compassion, health or solidarity. They vary across different religions, sects and regions.
  - Wearing symbols, such as tilak, bindi, cross, khanda, om and swastika. These symbols represent the identity, beliefs, values or aspirations of a religious group or an individual. They are worn on the body, clothes, jewelry or accessories, or displayed on buildings, vehicles or objects.

- India's religious groups also share several religious practices and beliefs, such as:

  - Belief in karma, reincarnation, heaven and hell, and the existence of supernatural beings, such as gods, goddesses, angels, demons, spirits and ancestors.
  - Respect for cows, rivers, trees, mountains and other natural elements, as they are considered sacred, holy or manifestations of the divine.
  - Participation in yoga, ayurveda, astrology, numerology and other forms of alternative medicine, spirituality or divination, as they are believed to enhance physical, mental and emotional well-being.
  - Adoption of practices or customs from other religions, such as celebrating festivals, visiting shrines, wearing symbols or reciting prayers, as a sign of tolerance, curiosity or syncretism.
