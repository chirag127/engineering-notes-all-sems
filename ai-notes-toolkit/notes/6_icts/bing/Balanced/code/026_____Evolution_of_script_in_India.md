### Evolution of script in India

- A script is a written symbolisation of speech. A language is a method of communication. A script can be used to write one or more languages. 
- India has a long history of writing, dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1900 BCE), which used a pictographic script that remains undeciphered. 
- The earliest deciphered script of India is Brahmi, which was used to write the edicts of Emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE) and other inscriptions in various languages, such as Prakrit, Sanskrit, Pali, and Tamil.   
- Brahmi is the ancestor of most modern Indian scripts, as well as the scripts of Sri Lanka, Tibet, Southeast Asia, Philippines, and Indonesia. 
- Brahmi evolved into different regional variants, such as Kharosthi, Gupta, Nagari, Siddham, and Grantha, depending on the cultural and linguistic influences.   
- Brahmi also gave rise to cursive styles of writing, such as Nandinagari, Sharada, and Modi, which were faster and easier to write than the monumental inscriptions.  
- The scripts of south India, such as Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, and Tamil, became rounded due to the use of palm leaves as writing material.  
- The scripts of north India, such as Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, and Gurmukhi, retained the horizontal line on top of the letters, which was a feature of Brahmi. 
- The scripts of India reflect the diversity and richness of its culture and languages, and have been influenced by various historical, religious, and artistic factors.
