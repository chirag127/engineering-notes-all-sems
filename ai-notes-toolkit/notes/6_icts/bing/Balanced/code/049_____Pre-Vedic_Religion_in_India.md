### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people who lived in the Indus Valley or Harappan civilization, before the arrival of the Indo-Aryan speakers who brought the Vedic religion to India.
- The Pre-Vedic religion is also known as the Dravidian religion, as it is associated with the Dravidian-speaking people who are the native inhabitants of South India.
- The Pre-Vedic religion is characterized by the worship of nature, fertility, animals, and mother goddesses, as well as the use of seals, symbols, and scripts that have not been deciphered yet .
- The Pre-Vedic religion is also considered to be a form of Hinduism, as it shares some common elements with the later Vedic and post-Vedic Hindu traditions, such as the concept of karma, reincarnation, and yoga.
- The Pre-Vedic religion is also influenced by the interaction with other ancient cultures, such as the Mesopotamian, Egyptian, and Sumerian civilizations, as evidenced by the trade, art, and architecture of the Harappan culture.
- The Pre-Vedic religion is largely lost to history, as the Harappan civilization declined and disappeared around 1900 BCE, due to various factors such as environmental changes, invasions, and migrations .
- The Pre-Vedic religion is still a subject of research and debate among scholars, as there are many gaps and mysteries in the available evidence and sources  .
