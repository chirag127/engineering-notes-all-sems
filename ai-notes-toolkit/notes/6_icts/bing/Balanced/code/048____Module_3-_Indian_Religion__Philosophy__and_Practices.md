## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a land of diverse religious traditions, beliefs, and practices. Some of the major religions that originated in India are Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Other religions that have a significant presence in India are Islam, Christianity, Zoroastrianism, Judaism, and Baha'i Faith.
- Hinduism is the oldest and the largest religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a complex and diverse faith that encompasses various sects, schools, and traditions. Some of the core concepts of Hinduism are dharma (duty, law, morality), karma (action, cause and effect), samsara (cycle of rebirth), moksha (liberation from samsara), and the belief in multiple gods and goddesses, who are manifestations of the supreme reality, Brahman.
- Buddhism is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, also known as the Buddha, in the 6th century BCE. Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths, which are: the truth of suffering, the truth of the cause of suffering, the truth of the end of suffering, and the truth of the path that leads to the end of suffering. The path that leads to the end of suffering is the Noble Eightfold Path, which consists of right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. Buddhism also teaches the concept of anatman (no-self), which means that there is no permanent, independent, or inherent self or soul in any being or phenomenon. The goal of Buddhism is to attain nirvana, which is the state of cessation of suffering and ignorance.
- Jainism is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Mahavira, also known as the Jina, in the 6th century BCE. Jainism teaches the principle of ahimsa (non-violence), which means to avoid harming any living being, whether by thought, word, or deed. Jainism also teaches the concept of anekantavada (many-sidedness), which means that reality is complex and multifaceted, and that different perspectives can be valid and true. The goal of Jainism is to achieve moksha, which is the state of liberation from the cycle of birth and death, by following the three jewels of right knowledge, right faith, and right conduct.
- Sikhism is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century CE. Sikhism teaches the concept of monotheism, which means that there is only one God, who is called Waheguru, and who is the creator, sustainer, and destroyer of everything. Sikhism also teaches the concept of human equality, which means that all people are equal and should be treated with respect and dignity, regardless of their caste, gender, race, or religion. The goal of Sikhism is to attain union with God, by following the teachings of the ten Gurus, who are the spiritual guides and leaders of the Sikh community, and by living a life of service, honesty, devotion, and meditation.
- Islam is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Prophet Muhammad in the 7th century CE. Islam teaches the concept of tawhid (oneness), which means that there is only one God, who is called Allah, and who is the source of everything. Islam also teaches the concept of prophethood, which means that God has sent messengers and revelations to guide humanity, and that Muhammad is the final and seal of the prophets. The goal of Islam is to submit to the will of God, by following the five pillars of faith, which are: shahada (declaration of faith), salat (prayer), zakat (charity), sawm (fasting), and hajj (pilgrimage).
- Christianity is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Jesus Christ in the 1st century CE. Christianity teaches the concept of trinity, which means that there is one God, who exists in three persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Christianity also teaches the concept of incarnation, which means that God became human in the person of Jesus Christ, who was born of the Virgin Mary, lived a sinless life, performed miracles, taught the gospel, died on the cross, and rose from the dead. The goal of Christianity is to have a personal relationship with God, by accepting Jesus Christ as the Lord and Savior, and by following his commandments of love, faith, and service.
