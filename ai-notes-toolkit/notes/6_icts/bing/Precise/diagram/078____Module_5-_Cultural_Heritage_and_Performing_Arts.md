## Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

Cultural heritage refers to the legacy of physical artifacts and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations. Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art, and artifacts), intangible culture (such as folklore, traditions, language, and knowledge), and natural heritage (including culturally significant landscapes, and biodiversity).

Performing arts are a form of art in which artists use their voices, bodies or inanimate objects to convey artistic expression. It is different from visual arts, which is when artists use paint, canvas or various materials to create physical or static art objects. Performing arts include a range of disciplines which are performed in front of a live audience, including theatre, music, dance, opera, magic, illusion, mime, spoken word, puppetry, circus arts, performance art, recitation and public speaking.

- Cultural heritage and performing arts are closely related, as performing arts are often a means of preserving and transmitting cultural heritage.
- Cultural heritage can provide inspiration for performing arts, and performing arts can help to keep cultural heritage alive.
- The preservation of cultural heritage and the promotion of performing arts can contribute to the development of cultural tourism, which can have economic benefits for a region or country.
- Cultural heritage and performing arts can also play a role in promoting social cohesion and understanding between different cultures.
