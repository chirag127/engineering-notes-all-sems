#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

In ancient India, society was divided into four classes, or varnas, based on occupation and social status. These classes were:

1. **Brahmins**: The priestly class, responsible for performing religious rituals and providing spiritual guidance.
2. **Kshatriyas**: The warrior class, responsible for protecting the people and maintaining law and order.
3. **Vaishyas**: The merchant class, responsible for trade and commerce.
4. **Shudras**: The laboring class, responsible for performing manual labor and serving the other classes.

This system of classification was rigid and hereditary, with individuals born into a particular class and expected to follow the occupation of their parents. The system also included a fifth group, known as the "untouchables," who were considered outside the varna system and performed tasks considered impure or unclean.

The varna system was an integral part of ancient Indian society and influenced many aspects of daily life, including marriage, occupation, and social interactions. However, it has been criticized for perpetuating social inequality and discrimination. Today, the caste system is illegal in India, but its influence can still be seen in some aspects of society.
