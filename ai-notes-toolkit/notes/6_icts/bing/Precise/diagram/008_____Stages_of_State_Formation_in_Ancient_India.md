### Stages of State Formation in Ancient India

The formation of states in ancient India was a gradual process that involved the creation and appropriation of surplus. This process was closely linked with the growth and spread of agriculture, trade, industry, and urbanization. The spread of agriculture along river valleys led to the generation of agricultural surplus, which in turn led to the development of trade, commerce, and urbanization. The first states, known as Janapadas, emerged in these river valleys, and this pattern was replicated in the South and East of India.

According to ancient Indian texts, a kingdom, or rajya, was constituted of seven elements, or sapta-prakrtayah, also known as the seven limbs, or sapta-anga. These were the king (swamin), minister (amatya), city (pura), domain/territory (rastra), treasury (kosa), army (danda), and ally (suhrd/mitra).

In the course of the evolution of the state in Indian history, monarchies gradually conquered the republics in ancient India. Monarchies remained the mode of government in ancient, medieval, and modern India until the British Raj, without any revival of republicanism. The republican form of government could only be revived after India's independence in 1947.

The establishment of the Delhi Sultanate in 1206 AD changed the political landscape of northern India, with the strategically placed capital of Delhi becoming the new seat of central power. India's next imperial unification occurred under the Great Mughals, from Babur to Akbar (1526-1605 AD).
