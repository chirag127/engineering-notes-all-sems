### Sangama Literature in India

Sangama literature, also spelled as Cankam, Chankam, or Shangam, is the earliest known writings in the Tamil language. It is thought to have been produced in three literary academies, or Chankams, in Madurai, India, from the 1st to the 4th century CE .

Sangama literature is possibly unique in early Indian literature, which is almost entirely religious. The poems are concerned with two main topics: those of the first five collections are on love (akam), and those of the next two are on heroism (puram), including the praise of kings and their deeds .

Sangama literature is often known as "the poetry of the noble ones" and is South India's earliest known literature. The earliest accessible Tamil literature is known as Sangama literature .

Sangama literature is broadly classified into akam (inner) and puram (outer). The akam poetry is about emotions and feelings in the context of romantic love, sexual union, and eroticism. The puram poetry is about exploits and heroic deeds in the context of war and public life .
