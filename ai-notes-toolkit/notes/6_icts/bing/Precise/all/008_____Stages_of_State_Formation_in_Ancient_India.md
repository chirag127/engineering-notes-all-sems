### Stages of State Formation in Ancient India

1. State formation is linked with growth and spread of agriculture, consequently trade, industry and urbanisation.
2. In ancient India, this process began with the spread of agriculture along river valleys.
3. Agricultural surplus generated led to trade, commerce and urbanisation.
4. The first states (Janapadas) came about in these river valleys.
5. The pattern was replicated in the South and east.
6. In ancient India, kingdom (rajya) is constituted of seven elements (sapta-prakrtayah) or seven limbs (sapta-anga).
7. According to Manu-Smriti (1-2 century AD), these were: King (swamin), Minister (amatya), City (pura), Domain/ territory (rastra), Treasury (kosa), Army (danda), Ally (suhrd/ mitra).
8. In the course of the evolution of the state in Indian history, monarchies gradually conquered the republics in ancient India.
9. In ancient, medieval, and modern India until the British Raj, monarchies remained the mode of government without any revival of republicanism.
10. Republican form could be revived only after India's Independence in 1947.
