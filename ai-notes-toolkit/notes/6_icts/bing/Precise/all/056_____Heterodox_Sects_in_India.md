### Heterodox Sects in India

Heterodox sects refer to the religious movements that arose in ancient India as a challenge to the orthodox Brahmanical traditions. These sects were founded by ascetic wanderers and teachers who attracted groups of followers and established various sects. Their philosophies encompassed antinomian (belief that divine grace takes away the necessity of obeying moral law), materialist and fatalist elements.

Some of the major heterodox sects that arose in ancient India include:

1. **Jainism**: Founded by Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, in the 6th century BCE. Jainism is an ascetic religion that emphasizes non-violence and the attainment of liberation through the purification of the soul.

2. **Ajivika**: Founded by Makkhali Gosala in the 5th century BCE, Ajivika was a Śramaṇa movement and a major rival of Vedic religion, early Buddhism, and Jainism.

3. **Cārvāka**: Also known as Lokayata, Cārvāka was a materialist and atheist philosophical movement that rejected the authority of the Vedas and the existence of an afterlife.

These sects represented a significant challenge to the orthodox Brahmanical traditions and played an important role in the development of Indian philosophy and religion. They continue to have a significant influence on Indian culture and society to this day.
