#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage was regarded as a highly sacred institution in ancient Indian society. It involved the meeting of minds between spouses for material as well as psychological satisfaction.
- It was a meeting of two families in terms of the bond they created between them.
- Many scholars believe that the human race originally lived in a state of promiscuity, where individual marriage did not exist, where all men in a horde or tribe had indiscriminate access to all women and where children born of these unions belonged to the community at large.
- In Hinduism, marriage is considered a sacrament between individuals of the same sex and gender. Marriage in Hinduism symbolizes diverse aspects of human nature: love, selflessness, trust, affection, and compassion. Kinship relations are often established through marriages, whether between father and son, mother and daughter, or brothers.
- According to Manu Smiriti, the laws of Manu or Manava Dharma Shastra, there existed eight main forms of Hindu marriages in ancient India namely Brahma Vivah, Daiva Vivah, Arsha Vivah, Prajapatya Vivah, Gandharva Vivah, Asura Vivah, Rakshasa Vivah and Pisaka Vivah.
