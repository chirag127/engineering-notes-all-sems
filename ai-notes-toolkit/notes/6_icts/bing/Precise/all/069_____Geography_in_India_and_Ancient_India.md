# Geography in India and Ancient India

India has a diverse landscape that includes snow-capped mountains, deserts, plains, hills, and plateaus. The country is named after the River Indus . In ancient times, the geography of India was slightly different from what it is today. The Himalayan Mountains stand in the northern part of India, while the Hindu Kush stands in the northwest. The southern region of India is surrounded by three bodies of water .

Some key points about the geography of ancient India include:
- The Indian subcontinent is a diverse and fertile region with monsoons, droughts, plains, mountains, deserts, and especially rivers, along which early cities developed in the third millennium B.C .
- Along with Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica, the ancient Indian subcontinent was one of the few places in the world to develop its own civilization .
- Ancient India was bounded to the north by large mountains and to the south by the Indian Ocean. Major rivers flowed from the northern mountains .
