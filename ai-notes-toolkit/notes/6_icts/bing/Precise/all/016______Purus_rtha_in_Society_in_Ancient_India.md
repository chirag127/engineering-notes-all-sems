#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

Purusārtha is a fundamental principle of social ethics in ancient Indian society. It refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life. These four aims are Dharma (righteousness, moral values), Artha (prosperity, economic values), Kama (pleasure, love, psychological values), and Moksha (liberation, spiritual values)  .

- **Dharma**: This refers to the moral and ethical values that an individual must follow in their life. It is the foundation of the other three Purusārthas and is considered the most important .
- **Artha**: This refers to the pursuit of wealth and material prosperity. It is considered important for an individual to be able to provide for themselves and their family .
- **Kama**: This refers to the pursuit of pleasure and love. It is considered important for an individual to have a fulfilling personal life .
- **Moksha**: This refers to the pursuit of liberation and spiritual enlightenment. It is considered the ultimate goal of human life .

These four aims are not only meant to set an ideal objective but also to convey the right means to achieve them . The concept of Purusārtha is centuries old and is still relevant today .
