### Heterodox Sects in India

Heterodox sects refer to the religious movements that emerged in ancient India as a response to the ritualistic and orthodox practices of the Vedic period. These sects were founded by ascetic wanderers and teachers who attracted groups of followers and established various sects. Their philosophies encompassed antinomian, materialist, and fatalist elements .

Some of the major heterodox sects that emerged in ancient India include:

1. **Jainism**: Founded by Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, in the 6th century BCE. Mahavira was born to Siddhartha, head of the Jnatrika clan, and Trishala, in Kundagrama .
2. **Ajivika**: Founded by Makkhali Gosala in the 5th century BCE, Ajivika was a major rival of Vedic religion, early Buddhism, and Jainism. It was a Śramaṇa movement .
3. **Cārvāka**: Also known as the Materialist school of thought, Cārvāka philosophy condemned the Vedas as a "pious fraud" .

These sects challenged the orthodox practices of the time and offered alternative philosophical and religious beliefs to the people. They played a significant role in shaping the religious landscape of ancient India.
