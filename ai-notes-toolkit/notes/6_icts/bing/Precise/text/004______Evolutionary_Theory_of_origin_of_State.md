#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

The Evolutionary Theory of the origin of the state is one of the several theories that attempt to explain how states or governments came into existence. According to this theory, the state is a natural development that emerged from the gradual evolution of human society.

1. The theory suggests that the state evolved from the family, which was the earliest form of human organization. The head of the family was the authority figure, and the family members were bound by ties of kinship and obedience.

2. As the family grew in size and complexity, it evolved into a clan or tribe. The clan or tribe was a larger group of people who were related by blood or marriage and who lived together in a specific territory.

3. The clan or tribe was governed by a council of elders or a chief, who was responsible for making decisions and enforcing rules. The members of the clan or tribe were bound by common customs, traditions, and beliefs.

4. As the population grew and the territory expanded, the clan or tribe evolved into a state. The state was a political organization that had a defined territory, a government, and a system of laws.

5. The state had the power to enforce its laws and to protect its citizens from external threats. The citizens of the state were bound by a social contract, in which they agreed to obey the laws of the state in exchange for protection and other benefits.

In summary, the Evolutionary Theory of the origin of the state suggests that the state is a natural development that emerged from the gradual evolution of human society, from the family to the clan or tribe, and finally to the state. This theory emphasizes the role of social, economic, and political factors in the development of the state.
