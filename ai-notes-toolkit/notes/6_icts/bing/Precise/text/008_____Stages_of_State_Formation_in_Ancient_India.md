### Stages of State Formation in Ancient India

- State formation is linked with growth and spread of agriculture, consequently trade, industry and urbanisation.
- In ancient India, this process began with the spread of agriculture along river valleys.
- Agricultural surplus generated led to trade, commerce and urbanisation.
- The first states (Janapadas) came about in these river valleys.
- This pattern was replicated in the South and east.
- In ancient India, kingdom (rajya) is constituted of seven elements (sapta-prakrtayah) or seven limbs (sapta-anga).
- According to Manu-Smriti (1-2 century AD), these were: King (swamin), Minister (amatya), City (pura), Domain/ territory (rastra), Treasury (kosa), Army (danda), Ally (suhrd/ mitra).
- In the course of the evolution of the state in Indian history, monarchies gradually conquered the republics in ancient India.
- In ancient, medieval, and modern India until the British Raj, monarchies remained the mode of government without any revival of republicanism.
- Republican form could be revived only after India's Independence in 1947.
