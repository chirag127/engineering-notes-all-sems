### Council of Ministers in Ancient India

- The Council of Ministers, also known as **Mantri-parishad**, was a common concept in ancient Indian science of Society and Polity .
- It is defined in **Kauṭilya’s Arthaśāstra** (4th century BCE), one of the most influential treatises of political science .
- The Council of Ministers was next in importance to the king .
- The Mauryas and the Sungas had a habitual council of ministers known as **Mantri-parishad** .
- The Saka rulers ruled with the assistance of a council of **mati sachivas** and **karma-sachivas** .
- The ministers under the Maukharis exercised great power .
- The council of ministers is variously designated as **Mantra** or **Mantri-parishad**, **Mantri-maņdala** and **Mantri-gaņa** .
- The Prime Minister was known as **Mantrimukhya** or **Mahāmantrī** .
- He was the leader of the cabinet, and the chief confidant of the king .
- He presided over the deliberations of the cabinet in the absence of the king .
