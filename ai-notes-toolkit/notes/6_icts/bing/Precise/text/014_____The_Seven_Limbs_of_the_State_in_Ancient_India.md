### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

The Seven Limbs of the State, also known as the Saptanga Theory, was formulated by Kautilya in his work, the Arthashastra. According to this theory, a state has seven elements or constituents, namely:

1. **Swamin** - the King: The King is independent and his responsibility for peace, justice, and stability is stressed.
2. **Amatya** - the Minister or Bureaucrats: The Amatya is responsible for the administration of the state.
3. **Janapada** - the Land and the People or territory: The Janapada refers to the territory and its inhabitants.
4. **Durga** - the Fortress or Fort: The Durga refers to the fortifications of the state.
5. **Kosha** - the Treasury or treasure: The Kosha refers to the state's treasury.
6. **Danda** - the Army or coercive authority: The Danda refers to the state's military and its ability to enforce its authority.
7. **Mitra** - the Allies or ally: The Mitra refers to the state's allies.

This entire set-up of the kingdom was described as the Saptanga theory in ancient India. These seven limbs were considered the ideal qualities or excellencies of the state, and determined whether a state would evolve into an inferior or superior entity.
