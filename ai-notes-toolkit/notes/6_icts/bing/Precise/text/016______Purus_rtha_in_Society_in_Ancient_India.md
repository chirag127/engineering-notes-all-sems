#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

Purusārtha is a fundamental principle of social ethics in ancient Indian society. It refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life, which are Dharma (righteousness, moral values), Artha (prosperity, economic values), Kama (pleasure, love), and Moksha (liberation, spiritual values)  .

- **Dharma** refers to the moral and ethical duties of an individual, which includes both personal and social responsibilities. It is considered the foundation of the other three Purusārthas.
- **Artha** refers to the pursuit of wealth and material prosperity, which is necessary for the fulfillment of one's duties and responsibilities.
- **Kama** refers to the pursuit of pleasure and enjoyment, which includes both sensual and aesthetic pleasures.
- **Moksha** refers to the ultimate goal of liberation from the cycle of birth and death, and the attainment of spiritual enlightenment.

These four Purusārthas are not only meant to set an ideal objective but also to convey the right means to achieve them . They provide a framework for leading a balanced and fulfilling life, and for the attainment of both material and spiritual goals.
