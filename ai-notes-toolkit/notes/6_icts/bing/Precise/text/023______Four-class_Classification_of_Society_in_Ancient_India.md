#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

In ancient India, society was divided into four classes, or varnas, based on occupation and social status. These four classes were:

1. **Brahmins**: The priestly class, responsible for performing religious rituals and ceremonies, and for teaching and preserving sacred knowledge.
2. **Kshatriyas**: The warrior class, responsible for protecting the people and maintaining law and order.
3. **Vaishyas**: The merchant class, responsible for trade and commerce.
4. **Shudras**: The laborer class, responsible for performing manual labor and serving the other three classes.

This system of classification was based on the principle of division of labor and was intended to ensure the smooth functioning of society. However, over time, it became rigid and hereditary, leading to social inequality and discrimination.
