## Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

1. India is a country in South Asia with a population of over 1.3 billion people.
2. It is a federal parliamentary democratic republic, with the President of India serving as the head of state and the Prime Minister serving as the head of government.
3. The Indian Constitution, adopted in 1950, lays out the framework for the country's political system and defines the relationship between the state and its citizens.
4. India is divided into 28 states and 8 Union Territories, each with its own government.
5. The central government has jurisdiction over matters of national importance, while the state governments have jurisdiction over matters of local importance.
6. The Indian Parliament is bicameral, consisting of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People).
7. The judiciary is independent and has the power of judicial review to ensure that laws and government actions are in compliance with the Constitution.
8. India has a multi-party system, with numerous political parties representing different ideologies and interests.
9. Elections are held regularly to choose representatives at the national, state, and local levels.
10. Civil society plays an important role in the country's political system, with numerous non-governmental organizations and advocacy groups working to promote various causes and interests.
