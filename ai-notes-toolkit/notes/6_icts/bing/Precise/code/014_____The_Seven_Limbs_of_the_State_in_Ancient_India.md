### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

The Seven Limbs of the State, also known as the Saptanga Theory, was formulated by Kautilya in his work, the Arthashastra. According to this theory, a state has seven elements or constituents, namely:

1. **Swamin** - the King: The King is independent and his responsibility for peace, justice, and stability is stressed.
2. **Amatya** - the Minister or Bureaucrats: The Amatya is responsible for the administration of the state.
3. **Janapada** - the Land and the People or territory: The Janapada refers to the territory and the people of the state.
4. **Durga** - the Fortress or Fort: The Durga refers to the fortifications of the state.
5. **Kosha** - the Treasury or treasure: The Kosha refers to the treasury of the state.
6. **Danda** - the Army or coercive authority: The Danda refers to the coercive authority of the state.
7. **Mitra** - the Allies or ally: The Mitra refers to the allies of the state.

This entire set-up of the kingdom was described as the Saptanga theory in ancient India. The Saptanga theory categorically identified the ideal type qualities/excellencies of the state, determined whether a state would evolve into an inferior entity or a superior entity.
