#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

The four-class classification of society in ancient India, also known as the caste system, was a strict social hierarchy imposed by Hinduism. The Vedas, ancient Indian texts, described four main social classes:

1. **Brahmins**: This class consisted of priests and scholars. Their task was to study and advise.
2. **Kshatriyas**: This class consisted of kings, governors, and warriors. Their task was to protect.
3. **Vaishyas**: This class consisted of cattle herders, agriculturists, artisans, and merchants. Their task was to cultivate.
4. **Shudras**: This class consisted of laborers and service providers. Their task was to serve.

The Dalit, formerly known as "untouchables," were considered outside the system and were treated poorly.

This system helped in achieving greater vocational specialization and gave rise to the four classes of Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Shudras.
