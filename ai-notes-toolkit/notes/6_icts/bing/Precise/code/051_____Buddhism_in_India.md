### Buddhism in India

- Buddhism started in India over 2,600 years ago as a way of life that had the potential of transforming a person.
- It is one of the important religions of South and South-Eastern Asian countries.
- The religion is based upon the teachings and life experiences of its founder Siddhartha Gautam, born in circa 563 BCE.
- During its first century of existence, Buddhism spread from its place of origin in Magadha and Kosala throughout much of northern India, including the areas of Mathura and Ujjayani in the west.
- Buddhism spread outside of Magadha starting in the Buddha’s lifetime.
- Mahākāśyapa, a disciple of the Buddha, presided over the first Buddhist council held at Rājagṛha.
- The Second Buddhist Council is said to have taken place at Vaiśālī.
- India is organising an international conference on 'shared Buddhist heritage' to focus on the country's civilisational connect with the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) nations.
- Indian Buddhism, having become primarily a monastic movement, seems to have lost touch with its lay supporters.
- Many monasteries had become very wealthy, so much so that they were able to employ indentured slaves and paid labourers to care for the monks and to tend the lands they owned.
