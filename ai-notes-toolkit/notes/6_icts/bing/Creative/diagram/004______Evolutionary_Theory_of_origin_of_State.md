#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

- The Evolutionary Theory is regarded as the true and correct theory regarding the origin of the state .
- It explains that the state is the product of growth, a slow and steady evolution extending over a long period of time and ultimately shaping itself into the complex structure of a modern state .
- It tells us that the state was formed as a result of several social circumstances and it reached its present form through the process of evolution.
- It helps in understanding the forces and factors, which created the state and historical stage through which it passed.
- Some of the factors that contributed to the evolution of the state are:
  - The development of family, clan, tribe, and nation as the basic units of social organization.
  - The increase in population and the expansion of territory due to migration, conquest, or colonization.
  - The emergence of agriculture, trade, and industry as the sources of economic activity and wealth.
  - The rise of conflicts and wars among different groups and the need for security and order.
  - The emergence of leadership, authority, and law as the means of regulating social relations and resolving disputes.
  - The formation of political institutions, such as monarchy, aristocracy, democracy, and bureaucracy, as the organs of the state.
  - The development of culture, religion, and ideology as the sources of identity and legitimacy for the state.
- According to one version of the Evolutionary Theory, known as the Circumscription Theory, the state arose as a predictable response to certain specific cultural, demographic, and ecological conditions .
- These conditions include:
  - The presence of a high level of social and technological complexity, such as writing, metallurgy, and urbanization .
  - The presence of a large and dense population, which creates competition and pressure for resources .
  - The presence of a circumscribed environment, such as a valley, an island, or a desert, which limits the possibility of escape or expansion .
  - The presence of a centralized and coercive authority, which can impose its will and extract tribute from the subordinate groups .
- The Circumscription Theory explains why states arose where they did, such as in Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica, and why they failed to arise elsewhere, such as in Australia, New Guinea, and North America .
