### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- The Harappan Civilization, also known as the Indus Valley Civilization, was one of the earliest urban cultures of the Indian subcontinent, dating from 3300 to 1300 BCE .
- The Harappans developed various technologies and innovations in different fields, such as:
  - **Metallurgy**: They used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, ornaments, and seals. They also knew how to smelt iron, but did not use it extensively .
  - **Standardization**: They devised a system of standardized weights and measures, based on a decimal scale and a unit of length called the Indus inch. They also used a uniform script, which has not been deciphered yet .
  - **Seal carving**: They produced thousands of seals, mostly made of steatite, with various motifs and inscriptions. The seals were used for trade, administration, and ritual purposes. Some of the seals depict animals, such as the unicorn, the bull, the elephant, and the rhinoceros .
  - **Hydraulic engineering**: They built sophisticated water management systems, such as wells, reservoirs, canals, drains, and baths. They also had a sanitation system, with toilets, drains, and sewers. They practiced rainwater harvesting and irrigation for agriculture .
  - **Urban planning**: They constructed planned cities, with grid-like layouts, fortified walls, and gateways. They also built public buildings, such as granaries, warehouses, and citadels. They used baked bricks of uniform size and shape for construction .
  - **Art and craft**: They created various forms of art and craft, such as pottery, terracotta figurines, beads, jewelry, ivory, and shell objects. They also painted and decorated their pottery and walls with geometric and floral patterns .
