### Puppetry as Cultural Heritage of India

- Puppetry is an ancient and diverse art form that has been used for entertainment, education, cultural and religious dissemination in India .
- Puppetry is one of the richest heritages of India, with several regional genres that reflect the local history, culture, language, music and aesthetics of different parts of the country .
- Some of the common traits of Indian puppetry are :
  - The use of epics, myths, legends, folktales and social issues as the main themes of the puppet shows.
  - The involvement of music, songs, dialogues, narration and gestures to create a lively and engaging performance.
  - The integration of puppetry with other art forms such as dance, drama, painting, sculpture and mask-making.
  - The participation of the puppeteers as performers, storytellers, singers, musicians and manipulators of the puppets.
  - The interaction of the puppeteers and the puppets with the audience, often breaking the fourth wall and inviting feedback, comments and questions.
- Some of the major types of Indian puppetry are  :
  - String puppets: These are puppets that are controlled by strings attached to their limbs and head. They are also known as marionettes or kathputli. They are found in Rajasthan, Orissa, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Kerala.
  - Shadow puppets: These are flat puppets that are made of leather or paper and are held against a translucent screen with a light source behind them. They create silhouettes or shadows on the screen. They are also known as tholu bommalata or tolpava kuthu. They are found in Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Orissa and Tamil Nadu.
  - Rod puppets: These are puppets that are manipulated by rods attached to their limbs and head. They are also known as putul nautch or yampuri. They are found in West Bengal, Bihar, Orissa and Assam.
  - Glove puppets: These are puppets that are worn on the hands of the puppeteers and are moved by the fingers. They are also known as pavakoothu or chamadyache bahulya. They are found in Kerala, Uttar Pradesh, Orissa, West Bengal and Maharashtra.
- Indian puppetry has faced many challenges in the post-colonial period, such as the loss of patronage, the competition from modern media, the lack of training and recognition, and the threat of extinction of some styles .
- However, Indian puppetry has also witnessed many initiatives to revive, preserve and promote the art form, such as the establishment of puppetry institutions, festivals, workshops, research and documentation, and the innovation of new forms and techniques  .
- Indian puppetry is a vibrant and dynamic cultural heritage that showcases the diversity and creativity of the Indian people. It is a valuable source of knowledge, entertainment and inspiration for the present and future generations.
