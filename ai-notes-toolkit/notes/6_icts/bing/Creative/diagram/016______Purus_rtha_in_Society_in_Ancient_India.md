#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

- Purusārtha (Sanskrit: पुरुषार्थ, IAST: Puruṣārtha) literally means "object (ive) of men" or "human endeavour".
- It is a key concept in Hinduism, and refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life. The four puruṣārthas are Dharma (righteousness, moral values), Artha (prosperity, economic values), Kama (pleasure, love, psychological values) and Moksha (liberation, spiritual values).
- These are not only meant to set an ideal objective but also to convey the right means to achieve them. The four puruṣārthas are interrelated and interdependent, and are meant to harmonize the individual and social needs of a person.
- The concept of puruṣārtha is based on the belief that human life has a purpose and a direction, and that one should strive to fulfill one's potential and attain one's ultimate goal. The concept also implies a sense of duty, responsibility and ethics in one's actions.
- The concept of puruṣārtha has influenced the social structure, law, ethics, education, art and literature of ancient India. The four puruṣārthas were seen as the guiding principles of the four varnas (social classes) and the four āśramas (stages of life) in the Hindu society. The puruṣārthas also provided a framework for the development of various schools of thought and practices in Hinduism, such as Vedanta, Yoga, Sāṃkhya, Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, etc. 
- The concept of puruṣārtha is still relevant and applicable in the modern times, as it offers a balanced and holistic approach to life, that integrates the material, emotional, ethical and spiritual aspects of human existence. The concept also encourages a person to seek truth and attain salvation, by following one's dharma and pursuing one's artha, kāma and mokṣa in a harmonious and ethical manner.
