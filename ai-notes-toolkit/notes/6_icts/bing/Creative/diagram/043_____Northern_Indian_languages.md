### Northern Indian languages

- Northern Indian languages are mainly from the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family.
- They are spoken by about 75% of the population of India, mostly in the northern, central and western regions of the country.
- Some of the major Northern Indian languages are:

  - Hindi: The most widely spoken language in India and the official language of the central government and several states. It has many dialects, such as Braj, Awadhi, Bhojpuri, Maithili, Rajasthani and Haryanvi.
  - Urdu: The official language of Pakistan and some states of India, such as Jammu and Kashmir, Telangana and Delhi. It is closely related to Hindi, but uses the Perso-Arabic script and has more vocabulary from Persian and Arabic.
  - Punjabi: The official language of Punjab, Haryana and Delhi in India, and Punjab and Islamabad in Pakistan. It is written in the Gurmukhi script in India and the Shahmukhi script in Pakistan. It has many dialects, such as Majhi, Malwi, Doabi and Pothwari.
  - Kashmiri: The official language of Jammu and Kashmir in India. It is written in the Perso-Arabic script, but also has its own native script called Sharda. It has many dialects, such as Kishtwari, Poguli, Rambani and Zirak-Boli.
  - Dogri: The official language of Jammu and Kashmir in India. It is written in the Devanagari script, but also has its own native script called Takri. It has many dialects, such as Bhadrawahi, Chibhali, Kangri and Mandeali.
  - Pahari: A group of languages spoken in the Himalayan regions of India, Pakistan and Nepal. They are not mutually intelligible, but share some common features. Some of the Pahari languages are Garhwali, Kumaoni, Nepali, Himachali and Ladakhi.
