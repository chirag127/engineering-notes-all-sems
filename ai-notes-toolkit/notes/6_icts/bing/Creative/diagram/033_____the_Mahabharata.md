### The Mahabharata

- The Mahabharata is an ancient Sanskrit epic poem that tells the story of the kingdom of Kurus. It is regarded as both a historical account of Hinduism's birth and a code of ethics for the faithful .
- It tells of the struggle for supremacy between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, who are the descendants of Bharata, a legendary king .
- The Kauravas are the sons of Dhrtarastra, who is blind and cannot inherit the throne, and the Pandavas are the sons of Pandu, who becomes king but abdicates due to a curse .
- The Kauravas, led by Duryodhana, refuse to share the kingdom with the Pandavas, and plot to exile them and usurp their inheritance. The Pandavas, led by Yudhishthira, endure many hardships and challenges, but also gain allies and divine favor .
- The epic culminates in the Kurukshetra War, a massive battle that lasts for 18 days and involves many kings and warriors from different regions of India. The Pandavas emerge victorious, but at a great cost of lives and moral values .
- The Mahabharata also contains many sub-stories and philosophical discourses, such as the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between the Pandava hero Arjuna and the god Krishna, who advises him on his duty and the nature of reality .
- The Mahabharata is one of the longest and most influential works of literature in the world, with over 74,000 verses and 1.8 million words. It has been translated, adapted, and retold in various languages and media, and has inspired many artists and thinkers .
