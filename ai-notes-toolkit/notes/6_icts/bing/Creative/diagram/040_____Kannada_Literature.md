### Kannada Literature

- Kannada literature is the corpus of written forms of the Kannada language, a member of the Dravidian family spoken mainly in the Indian state of Karnataka and written in the Kannada script .
- The earliest records in Kannada are inscriptions dating from the 6th century AD onward.
- The topics of Kannada literature included grammar, philosophy, prosody, rhetoric, chronicles, biography, history, drama and cuisine, as well as dictionaries and encyclopedias.
- Kannada literature can be broadly divided into three periods: classical, medieval and modern .
- The classical period (9th to 14th century) witnessed the emergence of poets like Pampa, Ranna, Harihara, Raghavanka and Kumara Vyasa, who wrote epics, legends, religious and secular works in champu, tripadi and ragale meters .
- The medieval period (14th to 18th century) saw the development of bhakti literature, with saints like Basava, Akka Mahadevi, Allama Prabhu, Purandara Dasa and Kanaka Dasa composing devotional songs in vachana and kirtane forms .
- The modern period (19th century to present) is marked by the influence of Western education, social reform movements, nationalism and literary movements like Navodaya, Navya, Dalit and Bandaya .
- Some of the prominent writers of modern Kannada literature are B.M. Srikantaiah, Kuvempu, D.R. Bendre, Shivaram Karanth, Masti Venkatesha Iyengar, Gopalakrishna Adiga, U.R. Ananthamurthy, Girish Karnad, P. Lankesh, K.S. Narasimhaswamy, Chandrashekhara Kambara and S.L. Bhyrappa .
- Kannada literature has won eight Jnanpith awards, the highest literary honor conferred in India.
