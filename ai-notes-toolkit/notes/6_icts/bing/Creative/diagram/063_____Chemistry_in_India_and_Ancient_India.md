### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

- Chemistry in ancient India was called **Rasayana** in Sanskrit, which means **extract** or **essence** of substances  .
- Chemistry in ancient India was not a separate discipline, but was closely linked with other fields of knowledge such as medicine, metallurgy, alchemy, agriculture, cosmetics, and perfumery  .
- Chemistry in ancient India developed through practical activities such as extracting metals from ores, making alloys, purifying minerals, producing medicines, dyes, paints, cosmetics, perfumes, and other useful substances   .
- Chemistry in ancient India can be traced back to the **Indus Valley Civilization** (3rd millennium BCE), which had advanced knowledge of metallurgy, ceramics, and dyeing .
- Chemistry in ancient India reached its peak during the **Gupta period** (4th-6th century CE), when many texts on Rasayana were written by scholars such as **Nagarjuna**, **Nityanatha**, **Yashodhara**, and **Vagbhata**   .
- Chemistry in ancient India was influenced by the **Ayurvedic** system of medicine, which classified substances into five elements (**panchabhuta**), six tastes (**shadrasa**), and three humors (**tridosha**)   .
- Chemistry in ancient India was also influenced by the **Tantric** and **Alchemical** traditions, which sought to transform base metals into gold, and to attain immortality and spiritual powers through the use of **mercury** (**rasa**), **sulfur** (**gandhaka**), and other substances   .
- Chemistry in ancient India made significant contributions to the fields of metallurgy, medicine, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, and alchemy, and influenced the development of chemistry in other regions such as China, Arabia, and Europe    .
