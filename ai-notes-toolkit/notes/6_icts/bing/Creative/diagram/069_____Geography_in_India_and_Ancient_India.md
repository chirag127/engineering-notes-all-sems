### Geography in India and Ancient India

- India is a large country in South Asia, with a total area of 3.28 million square kilometers. It is the seventh-largest country in the world by area and the second-most populous country with over 1.3 billion people.
- India is situated on the Indian Plate, a major tectonic plate that was formed when it split off from the ancient continent Gondwanaland. The Indian Plate is moving northward and colliding with the Eurasian Plate, creating the Himalayan Mountains and the Tibetan Plateau.
- India has a diverse geography, with plains, plateaus, hills, mountains, deserts, coasts, and islands. India has four major physiographic regions: the Himalayan Mountains in the north, the Indo-Gangetic Plain in the north and east, the Peninsular Plateau in the south and central, and the Coastal Plains and Islands in the west and south.
- India has a monsoon climate, with four seasons: winter (December to February), summer (March to May), rainy (June to September), and post-monsoon (October to November). The monsoon winds bring heavy rainfall to most parts of India, especially the west coast and the northeast. The rainfall varies from less than 100 mm in some parts of the Thar Desert to over 11,000 mm in some parts of the Meghalaya Plateau.
- India has a rich biodiversity, with over 90,000 species of animals and 45,000 species of plants. India is one of the 17 megadiverse countries in the world, with 10 biogeographic zones and 26 biotic provinces. India is home to many endemic and endangered species, such as the Bengal tiger, the Asiatic lion, the Indian elephant, the one-horned rhinoceros, and the snow leopard.
- In ancient times, the geography of India was a little different than what it is today. The sea level was lower, exposing more land area and creating a land bridge between India and Sri Lanka. The Saraswati River, which is now a dried-up riverbed, was a major river that flowed through the northwest of India and supported the Indus Valley Civilization .
- The Indus Valley Civilization was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world, dating from 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE. It covered an area of about 1.25 million square kilometers, spanning parts of modern-day India, Pakistan, Afghanistan, and Iran. It had a sophisticated culture, with well-planned cities, advanced sanitation, standardized weights and measures, and a script that is still undeciphered.
- The Indus Valley Civilization declined around 1900 BCE, possibly due to climate change, natural disasters, or invasions by nomadic tribes. Around 1500 BCE, the Indo-Aryan people migrated from Central Asia and settled in the Gangetic Plain, bringing with them the Vedic culture, the Sanskrit language, and the Hindu religion.
- The Gangetic Plain became the cradle of ancient Indian civilization, with the emergence of kingdoms, states, and empires, such as the Magadha, the Maurya, the Gupta, and the Harsha. The Gangetic Plain also witnessed the development of Buddhism and Jainism, as well as the spread of Indian culture and influence to other parts of Asia through trade and missionary activities.
- The Peninsular Plateau was also a site of ancient Indian civilization, with the rise of the Dravidian culture, the Tamil language, and the Hindu temples. The Peninsular Plateau was home to many kingdoms and dynasties, such as the Chola, the Pallava, the Chalukya, the Rashtrakuta, and the Vijayanagara. The Peninsular Plateau also had maritime contacts with Southeast Asia, Africa, and the Middle East.
- The Coastal Plains and Islands were also important regions in ancient Indian history, as they facilitated trade and commerce with other countries and regions. The Coastal Plains and Islands had many ports and harbors, such as Lothal, Bharuch, Kaveripattinam, Muziris, and Tamralipti.
