### The Ramayana

The Ramayana is a Hindu epic that narrates the life and adventures of Rama, the prince of Kosala, and his wife Sita. The Ramayana consists of seven books and 24,000 verses. It is one of the most influential and revered works of literature in India and other parts of Asia. The Ramayana has been translated into many languages and adapted into various forms of art, such as drama, film, television, and comics.

The main plot of the Ramayana is as follows:

- Rama is the eldest son of King Dasaratha of Kosala and the incarnation of the god Vishnu. He is destined to defeat the demon king Ravana, who has acquired immense power and terrorizes the world.
- Rama wins the hand of Sita, the daughter of King Janaka of Mithila, by breaking the bow of Shiva, a feat that no one else could accomplish. They get married and return to Ayodhya, the capital of Kosala.
- Rama is about to be crowned as the heir to the throne, but his stepmother Kaikeyi, influenced by her maid Manthara, demands that Rama be exiled for 14 years and that her son Bharata be made the king instead. Dasaratha reluctantly agrees, but dies of grief soon after. Rama accepts his fate and leaves for the forest with Sita and his loyal brother Lakshmana. Bharata refuses to take the throne and goes to the forest to persuade Rama to return, but Rama insists on fulfilling his father's promise. Bharata takes Rama's sandals and places them on the throne as a symbol of Rama's authority.
- Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana wander in the forest and encounter various sages, ascetics, and animals. They also face many dangers and challenges from the demons who serve Ravana. One of them, Surpanakha, the sister of Ravana, tries to seduce Rama and attacks Sita, but Lakshmana cuts off her nose and ears. She complains to her brothers Khara and Dushana, who lead an army of 14,000 demons to kill Rama, but Rama single-handedly defeats them all. Surpanakha then goes to Ravana and tells him about Sita's beauty and virtue, and urges him to abduct her.
- Ravana devises a plan to kidnap Sita with the help of Maricha, a shape-shifting demon. Maricha disguises himself as a golden deer and lures Sita, who asks Rama to catch it for her. Rama follows the deer and shoots it with an arrow, but Maricha cries out in Rama's voice, "Help me, Lakshmana!" Sita hears this and fears that Rama is in danger. She urges Lakshmana to go and help him, but Lakshmana hesitates, knowing that it is a trick. Sita accuses him of having ulterior motives and insults him, forcing him to leave. He draws a protective circle around Sita and tells her not to cross it. As soon as he leaves, Ravana appears as a mendicant and asks Sita for alms. Sita steps out of the circle to offer him food, and Ravana reveals his true form and carries her away in his flying chariot. Sita cries out for help and drops her ornaments along the way. Rama and Lakshmana return and realize what has happened. They search for Sita and meet Jatayu, a giant eagle, who tells them that he tried to stop Ravana but was wounded and left to die. Rama and Lakshmana perform his funeral rites and continue their quest.
- Rama and Lakshmana encounter Hanuman, the monkey king Sugriva's minister, who tells them that he has seen Sita's ornaments and that Sugriva has a quarrel with his brother Vali, the king of monkeys, who has banished him and taken his wife. Rama agrees to help Sugriva in exchange for his assistance in finding Sita. Rama kills Vali with an arrow and makes Sugriva the king. Sugriva sends his monkey army in all directions to search for Sita.
