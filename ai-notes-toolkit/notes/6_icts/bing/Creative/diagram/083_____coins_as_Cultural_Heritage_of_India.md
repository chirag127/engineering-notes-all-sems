### Coins as Cultural Heritage of India

- Coins are one of the most important sources of information about the history, culture, religion, economy, and politics of ancient and medieval India.
- Coins reflect the artistic skills, technological innovations, political aspirations, and religious beliefs of the people who minted and used them.
- Coins also provide valuable insights into the trade, commerce, and cultural interactions of India with other regions and civilizations of the world.
- The origin of coinage in India can be traced back to the first millennium BCE, when punch-marked coins made of silver and copper were used as a medium of exchange.
- The earliest coins were called karshapanas or panas, and they bore various symbols and marks, such as geometric shapes, animals, plants, and human figures.
- The coins of the Mauryan Empire (4th-2nd century BCE) were mostly punch-marked coins, but some cast coins of copper and lead were also issued. The Mauryan coins depicted the royal emblem of the lion, the peacock, the bull, and the elephant, as well as the Buddhist symbols of the wheel and the lotus.
- The post-Mauryan period (2nd-1st century BCE) saw the emergence of various regional kingdoms and dynasties, such as the Shungas, the Satavahanas, the Indo-Greeks, the Indo-Scythians, the Indo-Parthians, and the Kushanas. These rulers issued coins of different metals, shapes, sizes, and designs, reflecting their diverse cultural influences and artistic styles.
- The coins of the Indo-Greek kings (2nd-1st century BCE) were the first to bear the portraits and names of the rulers, as well as the Greek and Brahmi legends. The coins also depicted various Greek and Indian deities, such as Zeus, Apollo, Athena, Heracles, Lakshmi, Shiva, and Buddha.
- The coins of the Kushana Empire (1st-3rd century CE) were remarkable for their variety and richness of iconography. The Kushana coins depicted the portraits and names of the kings, as well as the Greek, Brahmi, Kharoshthi, and Bactrian legends. The coins also depicted various Iranian, Greek, Indian, and Central Asian deities, such as Ohrmazd, Mithra, Helios, Nike, Shiva, Vishnu, Kartikeya, and Buddha.
- The coins of the Gupta Empire (4th-6th century CE) are considered to be the finest examples of Indian coinage. The Gupta coins depicted the portraits and names of the kings, as well as the Sanskrit legends. The coins also depicted various aspects of the royal life, such as hunting, archery, horse-riding, playing musical instruments, and performing religious rituals. The coins also depicted various Hindu and Buddhist deities, such as Lakshmi, Ganga, Saraswati, Shiva, Vishnu, Durga, and Buddha.
- The coins of the medieval period (7th-16th century CE) witnessed the introduction of new metals, such as gold and billon, and new techniques, such as die-striking and minting. The coins also reflected the political, religious, and cultural changes that occurred in this period, such as the rise of Islam, the emergence of regional kingdoms, and the influence of foreign invaders.
- The coins of the Delhi Sultanate (13th-16th century CE) were mostly made of silver and copper, and they bore the names and titles of the sultans, as well as the Islamic and Persian legends. The coins also depicted various religious and political symbols, such as the kalima, the crescent, the star, and the lion.
- The coins of the Vijayanagara Empire (14th-16th century CE) were mostly made of gold and copper, and they bore the names and titles of the kings, as well as the Kannada and Telugu legends. The coins also depicted various Hindu deities, such as Ganesha, Lakshmi, Shiva, Vishnu, and Hanuman, as well as the royal emblem of the boar.
- The coins of the Mughal Empire (16th-18th century CE) were mostly made of gold and silver, and they bore the names and titles of the emperors, as well as the Islamic and Persian legends. The coins also depicted various artistic and calligraphic motifs, such as floral designs, geometric patterns, and verses from the Quran.
