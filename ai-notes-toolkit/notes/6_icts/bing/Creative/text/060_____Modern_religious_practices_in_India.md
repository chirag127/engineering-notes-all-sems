### Modern religious practices in India

- India is a secular country with a diverse population that follows various religions, such as Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, Zoroastrianism, and others .
- Religion has a significant influence on the culture, politics, and economy of India, and many Indians take pride in their rich religious heritage.
- Hinduism is the dominant religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindu. Hinduism is a complex and diverse faith that encompasses many sects, schools, and traditions. Some of the common practices of Hinduism include worshiping various gods and goddesses, performing rituals and ceremonies, celebrating festivals, practicing yoga and meditation, and following ethical principles .
- Islam is the second-largest religion in India, with about 14% of the population identifying as Muslim. Islam is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of the Quran and the Prophet Muhammad. Some of the common practices of Islam include praying five times a day, fasting during Ramadan, giving alms to the poor, performing pilgrimage to Mecca, and observing dietary and dress codes .
- Christianity is the third-largest religion in India, with about 2% of the population identifying as Christian. Christianity is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of Jesus Christ and the Bible. Some of the common practices of Christianity include attending church services, celebrating festivals such as Christmas and Easter, reading and studying the Bible, and following the commandments of God .
- Sikhism is the fourth-largest religion in India, with about 2% of the population identifying as Sikh. Sikhism is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of the Guru Granth Sahib and the ten Gurus. Some of the common practices of Sikhism include wearing the five Ks (articles of faith), reciting the daily prayers, visiting the gurdwara (place of worship), and serving the community .
- Buddhism is the fifth-largest religion in India, with about 1% of the population identifying as Buddhist. Buddhism is a non-theistic religion that follows the teachings of the Buddha and the Dharma. Some of the common practices of Buddhism include following the Noble Eightfold Path, meditating, chanting, observing the precepts, and venerating the Buddha and other enlightened beings .
- Jainism is the sixth-largest religion in India, with about 0.4% of the population identifying as Jain. Jainism is a non-theistic religion that follows the teachings of the Tirthankaras (spiritual teachers) and the Jain Agamas (scriptures). Some of the common practices of Jainism include practicing non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, chastity, and non-attachment, performing rituals and ceremonies, observing fasting and festivals, and respecting all living beings .
- Zoroastrianism is a minority religion in India, with a few thousand adherents, mostly belonging to the Parsi community. Zoroastrianism is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of Zoroaster and the Avesta (sacred texts). Some of the common practices of Zoroastrianism include worshiping Ahura Mazda (the supreme god), performing rituals and ceremonies, observing ethical and moral values, and disposing of the dead through the Tower of Silence .
- Apart from these major religions, there are also some indigenous and tribal religions practiced in India, such as Sanamahism, Animism, Sarnaism, and others. These religions are often based on the worship of nature, ancestors, spirits, and local deities, and have their own customs and traditions .
- India's religious groups share several religious practices and beliefs, such as belief in reincarnation, karma, dharma, and the cycle of birth and death, respect for elders and teachers, reverence for cows and other animals, and celebration of common festivals and occasions. However, there are also differences and conflicts among the religious groups, such as over caste, conversion, cow slaughter, communal violence, and personal laws .
