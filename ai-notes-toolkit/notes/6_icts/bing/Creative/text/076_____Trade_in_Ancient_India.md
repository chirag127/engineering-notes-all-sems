### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange since the Harappan civilization (c. 2600-1900 BCE) .
- The Harappans traded gold, copper, ivory, beads, textiles, spices, and other goods with Mesopotamia, Egypt, Persia, and Central Asia .
- The decline of the Harappan civilization was followed by the rise of new urban centers such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura, which became hubs of trade and political power .
- Trade in ancient India involved both inland and maritime routes, connecting the subcontinent with regions such as Southeast Asia, China, Rome, Arabia, and Africa  .
- The trade routes were facilitated by the development of roads, ports, coins, weights, measures, and guilds .
- The trade in ancient India was influenced by the political, religious, and economic factors of different periods, such as the Mauryan empire, the Kushan empire, the Satavahana dynasty, the Gupta empire, and the Chola dynasty  .
- The trade in ancient India also contributed to the spread of art, literature, science, technology, and religion, such as Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, and Islam  .
