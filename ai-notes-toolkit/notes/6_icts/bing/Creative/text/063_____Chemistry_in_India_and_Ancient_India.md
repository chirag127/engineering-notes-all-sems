### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

- Chemistry in ancient India was called **Rasayana** in Sanskrit, which means **extract** or **essence** of substances  .
- Chemistry in ancient India was not confined to theoretical aspects, but found development in various practical activities, such as metallurgy, medicine, alchemy, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, agriculture, and warfare  .
- Some of the achievements of chemistry in ancient India are:
  - The discovery and extraction of metals, such as gold, silver, copper, iron, zinc, tin, and lead, from their ores, and the production of alloys, such as brass, bronze, and steel   .
  - The development of Ayurveda, the system of medicine that uses natural substances, such as herbs, minerals, and animal products, for diagnosis and treatment of diseases   .
  - The invention of various chemical processes, such as distillation, calcination, sublimation, fermentation, and extraction, and the use of various apparatus, such as crucibles, retorts, alembics, and furnaces, for conducting chemical experiments   .
  - The preparation of various chemical substances, such as acids, alkalis, salts, mercury, sulfur, arsenic, and borax, and their applications in medicine, alchemy, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, and warfare   .
  - The development of alchemy, the art of transforming base metals into gold or silver, and the search for the elixir of life, which was believed to confer immortality   .
- Some of the prominent chemists in ancient India are:
  - Nagarjuna (c. 8th century CE), who wrote the **Rasaratnakara**, a treatise on alchemy, metallurgy, and medicine, and who is credited with the discovery of zinc and its extraction by distillation   .
  - Kanada (c. 6th century BCE), who wrote the **Vaisheshika Sutra**, a philosophical text that postulated the atomic theory of matter and the classification of substances into six categories: earth, water, fire, air, ether, and time   .
  - Charaka (c. 2nd century CE), who wrote the **Charaka Samhita**, a compendium of Ayurvedic medicine, and who described the properties and uses of various drugs, such as opium, mercury, sulfur, and arsenic   .
  - Patanjali (c. 2nd century BCE), who wrote the **Yoga Sutra**, a text on the philosophy and practice of yoga, and who mentioned the use of mercury and sulfur for attaining physical and mental perfection   .
  - Varahamihira (c. 6th century CE), who wrote the **Brihat Samhita**, an encyclopedic work on astronomy, astrology, geography, and meteorology, and who gave the methods of preparing various chemical substances, such as acids, alkalis, salts, and dyes   .
