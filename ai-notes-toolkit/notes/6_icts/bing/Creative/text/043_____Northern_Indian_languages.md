### Northern Indian languages

- Northern Indian languages are mainly from the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family .
- They include the Hindustani lingua franca, which has two standard forms: Hindi and Urdu . Hindi is the official language of India and Urdu is the official language of Pakistan.
- They also include a variety of regional languages and dialects, such as Punjabi, Kashmiri, Pahari, Sindhi, Bihari, Rajasthani, Marwari, and Gujarati .
- Some of these languages have their own scripts, such as Devanagari for Hindi, Urdu, and Marathi; Gurmukhi for Punjabi; and Perso-Arabic for Urdu and Kashmiri. Others use the Latin alphabet or other scripts for writing.
- These languages have been influenced by various historical and cultural factors, such as Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic, Turkish, English, and local languages .
- These languages have rich literary traditions, such as the poetry of Mirza Ghalib, the novels of Premchand, the epics of Tulsidas, and the folk songs of Lata Mangeshkar.
- These languages are spoken by millions of people across India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, and other countries. They are also used as lingua francas in many regions and communities.
