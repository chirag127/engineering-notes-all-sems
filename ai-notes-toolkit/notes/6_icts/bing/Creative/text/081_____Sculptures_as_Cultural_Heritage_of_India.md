### Sculptures as Cultural Heritage of India

- Sculpture is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India, dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE) and continuing to the present day .
- Sculpture in India reflects the rich cultural, religious, and philosophical diversity of the subcontinent, as well as the influences of various regions, dynasties, and foreign invaders .
- Sculpture in India can be classified into three broad categories: stone, metal, and terracotta. Each category has its own distinctive features, techniques, and styles.
- Stone sculpture is the most prevalent and enduring form of sculpture in India, especially in temples, caves, and monuments. Stone sculpture ranges from the naturalistic and realistic to the abstract and symbolic, and often depicts deities, mythical creatures, humans, animals, and floral motifs .
- Metal sculpture is another important form of sculpture in India, especially in bronze and iron. Metal sculpture is known for its intricate craftsmanship, fluidity, and dynamism, and often depicts gods and goddesses, especially in the Chola period (c. 9th-13th century CE) . Metal sculpture also includes coins, weapons, utensils, and ornaments.
- Terracotta sculpture is the oldest and most widespread form of sculpture in India, especially in rural areas. Terracotta sculpture is made from clay and baked in fire, and often depicts animals, birds, humans, and toys. Terracotta sculpture also includes pottery, figurines, and masks .
- Some of the famous examples of sculpture in India are:

  - The Dancing Girl, Mohenjo-daro, c. 2500 BCE: A bronze figurine of a young girl in a dancing pose, considered to be one of the earliest representations of human movement and expression in art.
  - Ashoka Pillar, Sarnath, 3rd century BCE: A stone pillar with a four-lion capital, representing the emblem of the Mauryan Empire and the symbol of Buddhism.
  - Buddha statues, various locations, 3rd century BCE onwards: Stone and metal statues of the founder of Buddhism, depicting his various postures, gestures, and expressions, such as the Dharmachakra Mudra (teaching gesture), the Abhaya Mudra (fearlessness gesture), and the Mahaparinirvana (death) pose .
  - Khajuraho temples, Madhya Pradesh, 10th-12th century CE: A group of stone temples with erotic sculptures, representing the Tantric philosophy of union and liberation .
  - Nataraja, Chola period, 11th-12th century CE: A bronze statue of Shiva as the lord of dance, depicting his cosmic dance of creation and destruction, with his four arms, drum, fire, and ring of flames .
  - Konark Sun Temple, Odisha, 13th century CE: A stone temple dedicated to the sun god Surya, with elaborate carvings of animals, humans, and celestial bodies .
  - Taj Mahal, Agra, 17th century CE: A marble mausoleum built by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, with intricate inlay work, calligraphy, and floral motifs .

- Sculpture in India is a valuable cultural heritage that showcases the artistic genius, aesthetic sensibility, and spiritual vision of the Indian people. Sculpture in India also reflects the historical, social, and political changes that have shaped the subcontinent over the centuries. Sculpture in India is a source of inspiration, education, and enjoyment for the present and future generations.
