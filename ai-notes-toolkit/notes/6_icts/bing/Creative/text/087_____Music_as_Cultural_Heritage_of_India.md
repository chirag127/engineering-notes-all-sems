### Music as Cultural Heritage of India

- Music is an integral part of the cultural heritage of India, reflecting its diversity, history and evolution over the centuries.
- Music of India can be broadly classified into two main traditions: Indian classical music and Indian folk music.
- Indian classical music is based on the concepts of raga (melodic framework) and tala (rhythmic framework). It has two major branches: Carnatic music, which is prevalent in South India, and Hindustani music, which is prevalent in North India.
- Indian classical music has its origins in the ancient Hindu scriptures, the Vedas, and has been influenced by various religious and cultural movements, such as Buddhism, Islam, Sikhism, and the Bhakti movement.
- Indian classical music is performed by soloists or ensembles, using vocal or instrumental techniques. Some of the prominent instruments used in Indian classical music are the sitar, the sarod, the tabla, the harmonium, the veena, the flute, and the violin.
- Indian folk music is a diverse and vibrant genre of music that reflects the regional, linguistic, and ethnic diversity of India. It is often associated with festivals, rituals, celebrations, and social occasions.
- Indian folk music encompasses a wide range of styles, such as bhangra, lavani, dandiya, baul, qawwali, bhajan, and ghazal. Some of the popular instruments used in Indian folk music are the dhol, the dholak, the ektara, the sarangi, the bansuri, and the harmonica.
- Indian music has also been influenced by and has influenced various genres of world music, such as pop, rock, jazz, and fusion. Some of the notable Indian musicians who have contributed to the global music scene are Ravi Shankar, Zakir Hussain, A.R. Rahman, Lata Mangeshkar, Asha Bhosle, and Kishore Kumar.
