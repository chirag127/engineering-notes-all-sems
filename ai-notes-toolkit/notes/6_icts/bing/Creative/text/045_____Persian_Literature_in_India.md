### Persian Literature in India

- Persian literature in India refers to the literary works produced in the Persian language by writers, poets, historians, and scholars who lived in the Indian subcontinent or visited it.
- Persian literature in India began with the rise of the Delhi Sultanate in the 13th century, when Persian became the official language of the courts and the administration. The Delhi Sultanate rulers patronized Persian poets, scholars, and historians, who produced works on various subjects, such as history, geography, religion, ethics, philosophy, and mysticism. Some of the notable Persian writers of this period were Amir Khusrow, Minhaj al-Siraj, Ziauddin Barani, and Shams al-Din Iltutmish.
- Persian literature in India reached its peak during the Mughal Empire, which ruled most of India from the 16th to the 18th century. The Mughal emperors, especially Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan, were themselves well-versed in Persian literature and culture, and encouraged the development of Persian poetry, prose, and historiography. Some of the famous Persian writers of this era were Abul Fazl, Faizi, Nizamuddin Ahmad, Abdul Qadir Badauni, and Muhammad Qasim Firishta.
- Persian literature in India also flourished in the regional kingdoms and states that emerged after the decline of the Mughal Empire, such as the Deccan Sultanates, the Awadh Nawabs, the Bengal Nawabs, and the Hyderabad Nizams. These rulers and their nobles also supported Persian poets, historians, and scholars, who wrote works on local history, culture, politics, and society. Some of the prominent Persian writers of this period were Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, Mirza Muhammad Rafi Sauda, Mir Taqi Mir, Ghulam Ali Azad Bilgrami, and Sirajuddaula.
- Persian literature in India declined in the 19th century, as the British colonial rule gradually replaced Persian with English as the language of administration and education. However, some Persian writers continued to produce works in the fields of literature, history, and religion, such as Mirza Ghalib, Mirza Asadullah Khan Ghalib, Muhammad Iqbal, and Abul Kalam Azad.
- Persian literature in India is a rich and diverse body of literature that reflects the interaction and exchange of ideas, values, and cultures between Persia and India over centuries. It is a valuable source of information and insight into the history, society, and civilization of both regions. It is also a testament to the enduring influence and legacy of Persian language and literature in the Indian subcontinent.
