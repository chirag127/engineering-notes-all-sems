# Northern Indian languages

- Northern Indian languages are mainly **Indo-Aryan languages** that are spoken by the people of North India .
- Some of the major Northern Indian languages are **Hindi**, **Urdu**, **Punjabi**, **Kashmiri**, **Dogri**, **Bihari languages** (such as Bhojpuri, Magadhi and Maithili), and **Pahari languages** (such as Garhwali, Kumaoni and Nepali) .
- These languages have evolved from the ancient **Sanskrit language**, which was the language of the Vedic civilization and the classical literature of India .
- These languages have also been influenced by various other languages, such as **Persian**, **Arabic**, **Turkish**, **English**, and **Dravidian languages** .
- These languages have different **scripts**, **grammars**, **vocabularies**, and **accents**, but they also share some common features, such as **gender**, **number**, **case**, **tense**, **aspect**, and **mood** .
- These languages are used for various purposes, such as **communication**, **education**, **media**, **literature**, **religion**, and **culture**  .
- These languages are also recognized as **official languages** in one or more of the states and union territories of North India, such as **Uttar Pradesh**, **Jammu and Kashmir**, **Himachal Pradesh**, **Punjab**, **Haryana**, **Delhi**, **Bihar**, and **Uttarakhand** .
