#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage was a sacred institution that brought together the two halves of life, man and woman, to form a complete and organic whole.
- Marriage was also a social contract that established kinship relations, rights and duties, and mutual support among the spouses and their families .
- Marriage was regulated by various laws and customs, such as caste, gotra, varna, and kula, that determined the eligibility and compatibility of the partners .
- Marriage was performed through various rituals and ceremonies, such as the kanyadan, the saptapadi, the homa, and the mangalsutra, that symbolized the union of the couple and their commitment to each other .
- Marriage was classified into eight main types, according to the mode of acquisition of the bride and the degree of consent involved, namely: Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya, Gandharva, Asura, Rakshasa, and Paisacha .
- Marriage was expected to be monogamous, lifelong, and indissoluble, except in certain exceptional cases, such as infertility, impotence, adultery, or cruelty .
- Marriage was the basis of the joint family system, where the husband and wife lived with their parents, siblings, children, and other relatives, and shared the household responsibilities and resources .
- Marriage was influenced by various social, economic, political, and religious factors, such as the rise and fall of empires, the invasions and migrations of foreign groups, the development of trade and commerce, the emergence of new sects and movements, and the reforms and movements of modern times .
