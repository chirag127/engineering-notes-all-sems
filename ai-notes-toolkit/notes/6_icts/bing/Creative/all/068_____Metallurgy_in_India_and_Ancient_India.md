# Metallurgy in India and Ancient India

Metallurgy is the science and art of extracting and processing metals from ores and alloys. Metallurgy in India and ancient India has a long and rich history, dating back to the prehistoric times. Some of the main aspects of metallurgy in India and ancient India are:

- **Copper metallurgy**: Copper was one of the first metals to be used by humans, and copper metallurgy in India dates back to the Chalcolithic societies in the subcontinent, according to spectrometric tests on copper ore samples discovered from ancient mine pits at Khetri in Rajasthan and metal samples cut from representative Harappan artefacts unearthed from Mitathal in Haryana. Copper was used for making tools, weapons, ornaments, vessels, and coins. Copper was also alloyed with tin to produce bronze, which was harder and more durable than pure copper.
- **Iron metallurgy**: Iron was another important metal in ancient India, and iron working in India may have begun as early as 1800 BCE, and that by the early 13th century BCE, iron smelting was definitely practiced on a large scale. Iron was used for making agricultural implements, weapons, armor, nails, needles, and other items. Iron was also alloyed with carbon to produce steel, which was stronger and more resistant to corrosion than iron. Ancient Indians were masters in the production of iron and steel, and they used various methods of extraction, classification, and microstructural analysis. Some of the famous examples of ancient Indian iron and steel are the iron pillar of Delhi, the iron beams of Konark temple, and the wootz steel of south India.
- **Zinc metallurgy**: Zinc was another metal that was extracted and used in ancient India. Zinc was extracted in India as early as in the 4th to 3rd century BCE. Zinc production may have begun in India, and ancient northwestern India is the earliest known civilization that produced zinc on an industrial scale. The distillation technique was developed around 1200 CE at Zawar in Rajasthan, where zinc ore was heated in a closed vessel and the zinc vapors were condensed in a separate chamber. Zinc was used for making brass, an alloy of copper and zinc, which was used for making coins, utensils, ornaments, and musical instruments. Zinc was also used for making medicine, cosmetics, and paints.
- **Gold and silver metallurgy**: Gold and silver were also widely used in ancient India, especially for making jewelry, coins, and religious objects. Gold and silver were obtained from placer deposits, river beds, and mines. Gold and silver were also imported from other countries, such as Rome, China, and Persia. Gold and silver were refined by various methods, such as cupellation, cementation, and amalgamation. Gold and silver were also alloyed with other metals, such as copper, tin, lead, and mercury, to produce different colors and properties. Gold and silver were also used for making artistic and decorative items, such as the gold coins of Gupta dynasty, the silver punch-marked coins of Mauryan empire, and the gold and silver vessels of Mughal period.
- **Other metals and alloys**: Apart from the above-mentioned metals, ancient Indians also used and produced other metals and alloys, such as lead, tin, mercury, arsenic, antimony, and bismuth. Lead was used for making pipes, bullets, and weights. Tin was used for making bronze and solder. Mercury was used for making amalgams, medicine, and alchemy. Arsenic, antimony, and bismuth were used for making alloys, pigments, and cosmetics. Ancient Indians also developed various alloys, such as panchaloha (five metals), ashtadhatu (eight metals), and navaratna (nine gems), which were believed to have auspicious and magical properties.
