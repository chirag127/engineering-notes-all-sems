# Buddhism in India

- Buddhism is an ancient Indian religion that originated in the 6th century BCE in the region of Magadha (now Bihar) and spread throughout northern India and beyond  .
- Buddhism is based on the teachings and life experiences of Siddhartha Gautama, also known as the Buddha (the awakened one), who renounced his princely life and attained enlightenment under a bodhi tree .
- Buddhism teaches the four noble truths: the truth of suffering, the truth of the cause of suffering, the truth of the end of suffering, and the truth of the path that leads to the end of suffering .
- Buddhism also teaches the eightfold path: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration, which are the means to achieve nirvana, the ultimate goal of liberation from the cycle of rebirth .
- Buddhism has two main branches: Theravada and Mahayana. Theravada Buddhism follows the Pali canon, the oldest collection of Buddhist scriptures, and emphasizes the individual attainment of enlightenment through meditation and ethical conduct. Mahayana Buddhism follows the Sanskrit scriptures, which include additional teachings and stories, and emphasizes the universal compassion and bodhisattva ideal of helping all sentient beings to attain enlightenment  .
- Buddhism flourished in India for over a thousand years, from the 3rd century BCE to the 12th century CE, and produced many schools, sects, and traditions, such as the Madhyamaka, Yogacara, Pure Land, Zen, and Vajrayana. Buddhism also influenced Indian art, architecture, literature, philosophy, and culture   .
- Buddhism spread from India to other regions of Asia, such as Central Asia, China, Tibet, Mongolia, Korea, Japan, Southeast Asia, and Sri Lanka, through trade, pilgrimage, and missionary activities. Buddhism also adapted to the local cultures and conditions of these regions and developed diverse forms and expressions   .
- Buddhism declined in India from the 8th century CE onwards, due to various factors, such as the rise of Hinduism, the invasions of Muslim rulers, the corruption and degeneration of the Buddhist monasteries, and the loss of patronage and support from the laity. Buddhism survived in some pockets of India, such as Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, and the Himalayan regions, where it still thrives today. Buddhism also experienced a revival in the 20th century, especially among the Dalits, who converted to Buddhism under the leadership of B.R. Ambedkar, a social reformer and the chief architect of the Indian constitution   .
