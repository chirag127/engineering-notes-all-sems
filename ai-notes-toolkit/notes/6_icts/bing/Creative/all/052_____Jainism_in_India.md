# Jainism in India

- Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that teaches the path to spiritual purity and enlightenment through nonviolence and reducing harm to all living beings .
- Jainism originated in India at least 2,500 years ago and is one of the three oldest religions of India, along with Hinduism and Buddhism .
- The spiritual goal of Jainism is to attain moksha, or liberation from the cycle of rebirth, by following the three jewels of right perception, right knowledge and right conduct .
- Jains believe in the existence of eternal and indestructible souls (jivas) that can be either human, animal, plant or microbe, and that all souls are equal and deserve respect and compassion .
- Jains also believe in the existence of non-soul substances (ajivas) such as matter, space, time and motion, and that the interaction of soul and matter causes karma, which binds the soul to the cycle of rebirth .
- Jains follow the principle of ahimsa, or non-injury, in thought, word and deed, and avoid harming any living being, even insects and plants, as much as possible .
- Jains also practice other ethical principles such as truthfulness, non-stealing, non-attachment, celibacy and non-possession, and observe various vows and disciplines such as fasting, meditation, pilgrimage and charity .
- Jains are divided into two major sects: Digambara and Svetambara, which differ in their interpretation of some scriptures, doctrines and practices, such as the role of women, the use of clothing and the worship of images .
- Jains have contributed to the fields of philosophy, literature, art, architecture, science, mathematics, astronomy, medicine and ecology in India and beyond .
- Jains are a small but influential minority in India, constituting about 0.4% of the population, or about 4.5 million people, according to the 2011 census .
- Jains are mostly concentrated in the states of Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Karnataka, but also have a presence in other parts of India and abroad.
- Jains are the only religious group in India where a majority say they are members of a higher General Category caste, and they also have high levels of education, income and social status.
- Jains have been granted the minority status by the Government of India in 2014, which entitles them to certain benefits and protections under the law.
