# Music as Cultural Heritage of India

- Music is an integral part of the cultural heritage of India, reflecting its diversity, history and evolution over the centuries.
- Music of India can be broadly classified into two main traditions: Indian classical music and Indian folk music.
- Indian classical music is based on the concepts of raga (melodic framework) and tala (rhythmic framework). It has two major styles: Carnatic music, which is prevalent in South India, and Hindustani music, which is prevalent in North India.
- Indian classical music has its origins in the ancient Hindu scriptures, the Vedas, and has been influenced by various religious, philosophical and cultural movements, such as Buddhism, Islam, Sikhism, Sufism, Bhakti and Tantrism.
- Indian classical music is performed by soloists or ensembles, using vocal or instrumental techniques. Some of the prominent instruments used in Indian classical music are the sitar, the sarod, the veena, the bansuri, the shehnai, the tabla, the mridangam and the harmonium.
- Indian folk music is the music of the common people, reflecting their regional, linguistic, ethnic and religious diversity. It is often associated with festivals, rituals, celebrations, weddings, harvests, seasons and social movements.
- Indian folk music has a variety of genres, such as bhangra, lavani, dandiya, baul, qawwali, bhajan, kirtan, ghazal and thumri. It is performed by singers, dancers and musicians, using instruments such as the dhol, the dholak, the ektara, the sarangi, the daf, the harmonium and the tambura.
- Indian folk music has also influenced and been influenced by other forms of music, such as Indian popular music, Indian rock music, Indian film music and world music.
