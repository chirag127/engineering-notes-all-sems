#### Contract Theory of Origin of State

- The contract theory of origin of state is one of the theories that explains how the state or the society came into being by a voluntary agreement or contract between individuals or groups of people  .
- The contract theory assumes that there was a state of nature before the state, where people lived in natural conditions without any law or authority. In the state of nature, people enjoyed complete freedom and equality, but also faced insecurity and violence due to the lack of order and protection .
- To overcome the problems of the state of nature, people decided to enter into a social contract, where they agreed to give up some of their natural rights and liberties in exchange for the benefits of living in a civil society under a common authority  . The social contract established the state as the legitimate and sovereign power that could make and enforce laws for the common good of the people  .
- The contract theory of origin of state has been developed and elaborated by different thinkers, such as Thomas Hobbes, John Locke, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, and Immanuel Kant, who had different views on the nature and terms of the social contract, the role and limits of the state, and the rights and duties of the citizens .
- The contract theory of origin of state has been influential and controversial in moral and political philosophy, as it provides a rational and normative basis for the state's authority and the people's consent, but also raises questions about the historical validity, moral legitimacy, and practical feasibility of the social contract .
