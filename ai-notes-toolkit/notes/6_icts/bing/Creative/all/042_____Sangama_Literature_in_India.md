# Sangama Literature in India

- Sangama literature is the earliest writings in the Tamil language, thought to have been produced in three literary academies, or sangams, in Madurai, India, from the 1st to the 4th century CE.
- Sangama literature is also known as "the poetry of the noble ones" and is South India's earliest known literature.
- Sangama literature is broadly classified into two main topics: akam (inner) and puram (outer).
- Akam poetry is about emotions and feelings in the context of romantic love, sexual union and eroticism.
- Puram poetry is about exploits and heroic deeds in the context of war and public life.
- Sangama literature consists of eight collections of poems, called anthologies, and ten long poems, called idylls.
- The eight anthologies are: Ainkurunuru, Kuruntokai, Narrinai, Akananuru, Kalittokai, Patirruppattu, Paripatal and Purananuru.
- The ten idylls are: Tirumurukarruppatai, Pattinappalai, Porunararruppatai, Sirupanarruppatai, Perumparruppatai, Mullaippattu, Nedunalvadai, Maduraikkanci, Kurincippattu and Tolkappiyam.
- Sangama literature is rich in its depiction of the natural and social environment of ancient Tamil Nadu, its culture, religion, customs, arts, crafts, trade, politics and warfare.
- Sangama literature is also remarkable for its poetic excellence, its use of various meters, its imagery, its symbolism, its metaphors and its similes.
- Sangama literature is a valuable source of information and inspiration for the study of ancient Indian history, literature and civilization.
