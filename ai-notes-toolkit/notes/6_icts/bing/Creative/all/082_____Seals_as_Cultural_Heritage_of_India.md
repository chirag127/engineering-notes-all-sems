# Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

- Seals are small objects made of stone, metal, clay, or other materials that have a carved or engraved design on one side and are used to stamp or seal documents, goods, or containers.
- Seals have been used in India since ancient times, especially by the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1900 BCE), one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world.
- The Indus seals are mostly made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved, and are usually square or rectangular in shape, with a perforated knob on the back for holding or attaching a cord.
- The Indus seals have various designs on the front, such as animals, humans, mythical creatures, geometric patterns, or symbols. Some of the common animals depicted are elephants, tigers, water buffaloes, rhinoceroses, and unicorns. The human figures are often shown in a seated or yogic posture, wearing elaborate headdresses or crowns. The symbols are thought to be a form of writing or script, but their meaning and language are still unknown.
- The Indus seals are believed to have been used for identification, trade, administration, or religious purposes. They were stamped on clay tablets, tags, or sealings that were attached to goods, containers, or documents. They were also used as amulets or charms for protection or luck. Some of the seals have been found in other regions, such as Mesopotamia, Afghanistan, and Iran, indicating the extensive trade and cultural contacts of the Indus Valley Civilization.
- The Indus seals are a valuable source of information and insight into the culture, society, economy, religion, and art of the Indus Valley Civilization. They reflect the high level of craftsmanship, creativity, and sophistication of the Indus people. They also show the diversity and complexity of the Indus culture, as well as its connections and influences with other ancient civilizations.
- The Indus seals are a part of the national and cultural heritage of India, as well as of Pakistan, where most of the Indus sites are located. They are preserved and displayed in various museums and institutions, such as the National Museum of India in Delhi, the Archaeological Survey of India, and the British Museum in London. They are also studied and researched by scholars and enthusiasts from different disciplines and backgrounds.
- The Indus seals are a symbol of the ancient and rich history and culture of India, and a reminder of the achievements and contributions of the Indus Valley Civilization to the world. They are also a source of inspiration and curiosity for the modern and future generations.
