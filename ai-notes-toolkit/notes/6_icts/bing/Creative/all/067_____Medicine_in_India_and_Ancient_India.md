# Medicine in India and Ancient India

- Medicine in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE).
- The most ancient and influential system of Indian medicine is Ayurveda, which means "the science of life". Ayurveda is based on the concept of balance among the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, and ether) and the three doshas (vata, pitta, and kapha), which are the vital energies that govern the body and mind.
- Ayurveda uses various methods of diagnosis, such as pulse, urine, tongue, and eye examination, and prescribes treatments that include herbal medicines, dietary modifications, massage, yoga, meditation, and purification therapies.
- Ayurveda also has eight branches of specialization, such as internal medicine, surgery, pediatrics, toxicology, rejuvenation, aphrodisiacs, ear, nose, and throat diseases, and psychology.
- Some of the famous ancient Indian physicians and surgeons who contributed to Ayurveda are Charaka, Sushruta, Vagbhata, Nagarjuna, and Dhanvantari.
- Charaka compiled the Charaka Samhita, which is considered the oldest and most authoritative text on Ayurveda. It covers topics such as anatomy, physiology, pathology, diagnosis, pharmacology, and ethics.
- Sushruta compiled the Sushruta Samhita, which is the main source of knowledge on ancient Indian surgery. It describes various surgical procedures, instruments, techniques, and complications, as well as plastic surgery, dentistry, and ophthalmology.
- Vagbhata compiled the Ashtanga Hridaya and the Ashtanga Sangraha, which are concise and comprehensive summaries of Ayurveda. They cover topics such as diagnosis, treatment, prevention, and hygiene.
- Nagarjuna was a renowned alchemist and philosopher who wrote the Rasendra Sara Sangraha, which deals with the preparation and use of mercury and other metals in medicine. He also introduced the concept of cakras, or energy centers, in the body.
- Dhanvantari is regarded as the god of medicine and the patron saint of Ayurveda. He is said to have emerged from the ocean of milk with a pot of nectar, which he gave to the sages for the benefit of mankind.
- Apart from Ayurveda, India also has other systems of traditional medicine, such as Siddha, Unani, Tibetan, and Homeopathy. Siddha is a system of medicine that originated in Tamil Nadu and is based on the concept of 96 basic principles that constitute the human body. Unani is a system of medicine that originated in Greece and was brought to India by the Muslim invaders. It is based on the concept of four humors (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile) and four temperaments (sanguine, phlegmatic, choleric, and melancholic). Tibetan medicine is a system of medicine that originated in Tibet and is based on the concept of three humors (wind, bile, and phlegm) and five elements (earth, water, fire, air, and space). Homeopathy is a system of medicine that originated in Germany and was introduced to India by the British. It is based on the principle of "like cures like", which means that a substance that causes a disease in a healthy person can cure the same disease in a sick person if given in a diluted form.
- Medicine in India has also been influenced by modern Western medicine, especially since the colonial period. India has a large number of medical colleges, hospitals, and research institutes that offer education and training in various branches of medicine, such as allopathy, dentistry, nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy, and veterinary science. India also has a vibrant pharmaceutical industry that produces and exports various drugs and vaccines. India is also known for its medical tourism, which attracts patients from all over the world who seek affordable and quality health care services.
