# Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

- India is a land of diverse cultures, religions, languages, and traditions. It is also a land of vibrant celebrations, where various fairs and festivals are held throughout the year to mark important occasions, honor deities, commemorate saints, express gratitude, showcase arts and crafts, and promote harmony and goodwill among people.
- Fairs and festivals are an integral part of India's cultural heritage, as they reflect the rich and varied history, beliefs, customs, and values of the country. They also provide an opportunity for people to interact, exchange, and enjoy the spirit of festivity and joy.
- Some of the most famous fairs and festivals of India are:

  - **Makar Sankranti**: This is a harvest festival celebrated in January, when the sun enters the zodiac sign of Capricorn. It is also known as Pongal in South India, Maghi in Punjab, Uttarayan in Gujarat, and Bihu in Assam. People fly kites, light bonfires, prepare special dishes, and worship the sun god on this day.
  - **Holi**: This is the festival of colors, celebrated in March, to mark the arrival of spring and the victory of good over evil. People smear each other with colored powder and water, play music, dance, and enjoy sweets on this day. It is also a time to forgive and forget, and renew relationships.
  - **Diwali**: This is the festival of lights, celebrated in October or November, to mark the return of Lord Rama from exile and the triumph of light over darkness. People decorate their homes with lamps, candles, and rangoli (colorful patterns), worship Goddess Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, exchange gifts, and burst crackers on this day.
  - **Dussehra**: This is the festival of victory, celebrated in September or October, to commemorate the defeat of the demon king Ravana by Lord Rama. People enact the epic Ramayana, burn effigies of Ravana, and worship Goddess Durga, the goddess of power, on this day.
  - **Ganesh Chaturthi**: This is the festival of the elephant-headed god, celebrated in August or September, to honor his birthday and his role as the remover of obstacles. People install idols of Ganesh in their homes or public places, offer prayers, sweets, and flowers, and immerse the idols in water after a few days.
  - **Pushkar Mela**: This is the largest camel fair in the world, held in November, in the holy town of Pushkar in Rajasthan. Thousands of camels, horses, and cattle are traded, decorated, and raced at this fair. It also attracts pilgrims, tourists, and cultural enthusiasts, who enjoy the various events, performances, and competitions at the fair .
  - **Jaisalmer Desert Festival**: This is a three-day festival, held in February, in the golden city of Jaisalmer in Rajasthan. It showcases the rich and colorful culture of the desert, with folk music, dance, puppet shows, camel races, turban tying contests, and firework displays. It also features a unique event called the Mr. Desert contest, where the most attractively dressed man is crowned.
  - **Goa Carnival**: This is a four-day festival, held in February or March, in the coastal state of Goa. It is a legacy of the Portuguese rule, and celebrates the spirit of fun and frolic before the onset of Lent. It features colorful parades, floats, masks, costumes, music, dance, and street parties. It also has a tradition of choosing a King Momo, who presides over the festivities .
  - **Konark Dance Festival**: This is a five-day festival, held in December, in the backdrop of the magnificent Sun Temple of Konark in Odisha. It showcases the classical dance forms of India, such as Odissi, Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kuchipudi, and Manipuri, performed by eminent artists. It also hosts a crafts fair, where local artisans display and sell their products .
