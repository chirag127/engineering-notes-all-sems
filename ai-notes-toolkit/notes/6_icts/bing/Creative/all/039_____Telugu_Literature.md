# Telugu Literature

Telugu literature is the body of works written in the Telugu language, which is a Dravidian language spoken by about 80 million people in India and some other countries. Telugu literature has a rich and diverse history, spanning from the 10th or 11th century to the present day. Some of the main features and genres of Telugu literature are:

- **Poetry**: Telugu poetry is the most prominent and influential form of Telugu literature, with various styles, forms, and themes. Some of the famous poets of Telugu literature are Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana, Srinatha, Potana, Allasani Peddana, Krishnadevaraya, Annamacharya, Kshetrayya, Tyagaraja, Vemana, and Sri Sri. Telugu poetry is often composed in metrical verses, such as the śataka (a collection of 100 verses), the padya (a verse with a fixed number of syllables), and the kanda (a long narrative poem). Telugu poetry also includes devotional, erotic, didactic, satirical, and modernist genres.
- **Epics**: Telugu literature also has a strong tradition of epic poetry, both secular and religious. The earliest and most important epics of Telugu literature are the Telugu translations of the Sanskrit Mahabharata and Ramayana, done by Nannaya, Tikkana, and Errana in the 11th-14th centuries. These epics are considered the foundational texts of Telugu literature and culture, and have influenced many later works. Other notable epics of Telugu literature are the Harivamsa, the Bhagavata, the Basava Purana, the Panduranga Mahatmyam, and the Andhra Maha Bharatam.
- **Prose**: Telugu prose is a relatively recent development in Telugu literature, starting from the 19th century. Telugu prose includes novels, short stories, essays, biographies, memoirs, and literary criticism. Some of the pioneers of Telugu prose are Gurajada Apparao, Kandukuri Veeresalingam, Viswanatha Satyanarayana, Chalam, Ranganayakamma, and Gopichand. Telugu prose reflects the social, political, and cultural changes in Telugu society, and explores various themes such as realism, nationalism, feminism, dalitism, and modernism.
- **Drama**: Telugu drama is another important genre of Telugu literature, which originated in the 15th century with the yakshagana (a musical theatre form) and the burrakatha (a folk narrative form). Telugu drama flourished in the 19th and 20th centuries, with the influence of western theatre and cinema. Some of the prominent playwrights of Telugu drama are Chilakamarthi Lakshmi Narasimham, Dharmavaram Ramakrishnamacharyulu, D. V. Narasa Raju, C. Narayana Reddy, and Gurazada Apparao. Telugu drama deals with various topics such as history, mythology, social issues, comedy, and tragedy.
