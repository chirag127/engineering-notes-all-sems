# India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times and Ancient India

- India is one of the oldest civilizations in the world, with a history that dates back to 2500 BC, when the Indus Valley cities were just starting to grow.
- India was home to a rich cultural and religious diversity, with various empires, kingdoms, and dynasties ruling over different regions and periods.
- Some of the major empires that dominated India up to pre-colonial times were:
  - The Mauryan Empire (321-185 BC), which was the first pan-Indian empire and one of the largest in the ancient world. It was founded by Chandragupta Maurya and reached its peak under Ashoka, who promoted Buddhism and non-violence.
  - The Gupta Empire (320-550 AD), which is considered the golden age of Indian culture, science, art, and literature. It was founded by Chandragupta I and expanded by his successors, especially Samudragupta and Chandragupta II. It was known for its achievements in mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and architecture.
  - The Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526 AD), which was a series of Muslim dynasties that ruled over northern India from Delhi. It was founded by Qutb al-Din Aibak, a former slave of Muhammad of Ghor, and reached its zenith under Alauddin Khalji and Muhammad bin Tughluq. It was known for its military expansion, administrative reforms, and cultural patronage.
  - The Mughal Empire (1526-1857 AD), which was one of the most powerful and prosperous empires in the world. It was founded by Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, and reached its peak under Akbar, who was known for his religious tolerance, cultural synthesis, and administrative innovations. It was known for its artistic, architectural, and literary achievements, such as the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, and the Akbarnama .
- India also had many regional powers and kingdoms that resisted or coexisted with the imperial powers, such as the Cholas, the Vijayanagara, the Marathas, the Sikhs, the Rajputs, and the Nizams.
- India was not a unified political entity until the British colonial rule, which began in 1757 after the Battle of Plassey, where the British East India Company defeated the Nawab of Bengal and his French allies.
- The British colonial rule was marked by economic exploitation, political oppression, social discrimination, and cultural domination of the Indian people, who resisted and rebelled in various ways, such as the First War of Independence (1857-1858), the Indian National Congress (1885), the Swadeshi Movement (1905), the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920), the Civil Disobedience Movement (1930), and the Quit India Movement (1942).
- India gained its independence from the British rule in 1947, after a long and peaceful struggle led by Mahatma Gandhi and other freedom fighters. However, the independence was accompanied by the partition of India into two dominions, India and Pakistan, based on religious lines, which resulted in communal violence and mass migration.
