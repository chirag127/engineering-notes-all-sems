# Challenges faced by Women in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India had different roles and rights depending on the time period, the region, the religion, and the caste they belonged to. Generally, they faced more challenges and oppression as time went by and patriarchy became stronger.
- Some of the common challenges faced by women in ancient India were:
  - Lack of autonomy and mobility: Women were often dependent on their male relatives for their survival and protection. They had limited access to education, property, inheritance, and legal rights. They were expected to stay at home and perform domestic duties, and were restricted from going outside or interacting with strangers.
  - Child marriage and widowhood: Women were often married off at a very young age, sometimes even before puberty, to older men. They had no say in choosing their husbands or in matters of divorce or remarriage. If their husbands died, they were either forced to commit sati (self-immolation on the funeral pyre of their husbands) or to live a life of seclusion and misery as widows. They were considered inauspicious and a burden on their families .
  - Exploitation and violence: Women were often subjected to various forms of exploitation and violence, such as rape, abduction, prostitution, devadasi system (dedication of young girls to temples as servants of gods), dasi pratha (enslavement of lower caste women by upper caste men), niyog pratha (practice of allowing a widow or a barren woman to cohabit with another man for the sake of progeny), etc. They had no recourse to justice or protection from the law or society  .
  - Discrimination and oppression: Women were often discriminated and oppressed on the basis of their caste, class, religion, and gender. They were considered inferior to men and had to obey their fathers, husbands, sons, and other male authorities. They were denied the right to participate in religious rituals, social gatherings, political affairs, and cultural activities. They were subjected to various taboos and restrictions regarding their dress, food, behavior, and sexuality .
