#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India had a complex and varied status depending on the time period, the region, the religion, and the caste they belonged to.
- During the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE), women were worshiped as mother goddesses and enjoyed equal honor and freedom as men. They could participate in war, gymnastics, public activities, decision-making, and education.
- During the Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE), women also held an honored place in society and had access to the sacred scriptures, rituals, and ceremonies. They could become rishis (sages) and teachers of the Vedas. Some of the famous women rishis were Gargi, Maitreyi, Lopamudra, and Ghosha  .
- During the later Vedic period (c. 500-200 BCE), the status of women began to decline due to the rise of patriarchy, caste system, and ritualistic practices. Women were gradually excluded from the religious and educational spheres and confined to the domestic domain. They were expected to be obedient, faithful, and submissive to their husbands and elders. They had to follow the norms of pativrata (chaste wife), sati (self-immolation of widows), and child marriage  .
- During the Mauryan period (c. 321-185 BCE), women enjoyed some political and economic rights under the rule of Ashoka, who promoted the welfare of women and encouraged their participation in Buddhism. Some of the prominent women during this period were Ashoka's daughter Sanghamitra, who was a Buddhist missionary, and his chief queen Asandhimitra, who was a patron of arts and literature  .
- During the Gupta period (c. 320-550 CE), women witnessed a revival of their status and role in society due to the influence of Hinduism, which glorified the ideals of womanhood and motherhood. Women were respected as shakti (power) and depicted as goddesses in art and literature. They could pursue education, arts, sciences, and commerce. Some of the notable women during this period were Kalidasa's heroine Shakuntala, the mathematician Lilavati, and the poetess Vak  .
- During the medieval period (c. 600-1700 CE), women faced various challenges and oppressions due to the invasions of foreign rulers, the rise of feudalism, the spread of Islam, and the emergence of bhakti (devotional) movements. Women were subjected to violence, discrimination, seclusion, and exploitation. They had to follow the practices of purdah (veil), jauhar (mass suicide of women to avoid capture by enemies), and devadasi (dedication of girls to temples as dancers and prostitutes)  .
- However, women also showed resilience and courage in the face of adversity and contributed to the social, cultural, and religious spheres of life. Some of the remarkable women during this period were Razia Sultana, the first and only female ruler of Delhi Sultanate, Mirabai, the bhakti poet and saint who defied the norms of caste and gender, Nur Jahan, the influential wife of Mughal emperor Jahangir, and Rani Lakshmibai, the queen of Jhansi who fought against the British  .
