# Society in Ancient India

- Society in ancient India was based on the concept of **varna**, which means color or class, and divided the people into four main categories: **Brahmins** (priests and scholars), **Kshatriyas** (warriors and rulers), **Vaishyas** (merchants and farmers), and **Shudras** (servants and laborers). 
- Below the varna system, there were also **outcastes** or **untouchables**, who were considered impure and excluded from the society. They performed the most menial and degrading jobs, such as scavenging, leather work, and cremation. They were not allowed to touch or interact with the higher varnas. 
- The varna system was rigid and hereditary, meaning that one's varna was determined by birth and could not be changed. It also influenced one's occupation, marriage, diet, rituals, and social status. The varna system was supported by the religious texts and laws of ancient India, such as the **Vedas**, the **Upanishads**, and the **Manusmriti**. 
- Another aspect of society in ancient India was the concept of **ashrama**, which means stage of life. It divided the life of each individual into four main stages: **Brahmacharya** (student), **Grihastha** (householder), **Vanaprastha** (retiree), and **Sannyasa** (renunciant). Each stage had its own duties, responsibilities, and goals. The ashrama system was meant to guide one's spiritual and moral development throughout life. 
- Society in ancient India was also influenced by the emergence and development of different civilizations, religions, cultures, and philosophies. Some of the major civilizations that flourished in ancient India were the **Indus Valley Civilization** (2500-1900 BCE), the **Vedic Civilization** (1500-500 BCE), the **Mauryan Empire** (321-185 BCE), and the **Gupta Empire** (320-550 CE).  
- Some of the major religions that originated or spread in ancient India were **Hinduism**, **Buddhism**, **Jainism**, and **Sikhism**. Each religion had its own beliefs, practices, scriptures, and followers. They also influenced the art, architecture, literature, and culture of ancient India. 
- Some of the major philosophies that emerged in ancient India were **Samkhya**, **Yoga**, **Nyaya**, **Vaisheshika**, **Mimamsa**, and **Vedanta**. These were the six orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy that accepted the authority of the Vedas. There were also other heterodox schools of Indian philosophy, such as **Charvaka**, **Jainism**, and **Buddhism**, that rejected the Vedas and advocated different views on reality, knowledge, ethics, and liberation. 
- Society in ancient India was also characterized by its achievements and contributions in various fields of knowledge, such as mathematics, astronomy, medicine, metallurgy, literature, art, and architecture. Some of the notable examples of ancient Indian inventions and innovations are the **decimal system**, the **concept of zero**, the **Pythagorean theorem**, the **sine and cosine functions**, the **Ayurveda system of medicine**, the **iron pillar of Delhi**, the **Mahabharata and Ramayana epics**, the **Bhagavad Gita scripture**, the **Ajanta and Ellora caves**, and the **Taj Mahal monument**.
