# Agriculture in India and Ancient India

Agriculture is one of the oldest and most important activities of human civilization. India has a rich and diverse agricultural history, dating back to the Neolithic period. Here are some points to summarize the main features and developments of agriculture in India and ancient India:

- The earliest evidence of agriculture in India comes from the Mehrgarh site in present-day Pakistan, where domestication of plants and animals began around 9000 BC.
- The Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1900 BC) was one of the first urban civilizations that practiced intensive agriculture, irrigation, and trade. They cultivated wheat, barley, rice, millets, pulses, cotton, sesame, and mustard, and raised cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, and elephants.
- The Vedic period (1500-500 BC) saw the expansion of agriculture across the Indo-Gangetic plains, with the use of iron tools, ploughs, and wells. The Vedic people also followed religious customs and rituals related to agriculture, such as worshipping the rain god Indra, the earth goddess Prithvi, and the fire god Agni.
- The Mauryan Empire (321-185 BC) was the first pan-Indian empire that unified and administered a large agricultural territory. The emperor Ashoka (268-232 BC) promoted the welfare of farmers, animal husbandry, and forest conservation. He also established granaries, irrigation works, and roads for facilitating agricultural production and trade.
- The Gupta Empire (320-550 AD) is considered the golden age of ancient India, when agriculture, arts, sciences, and culture flourished. The Guptas improved the irrigation system, introduced new crops such as sugarcane and cotton, and encouraged the use of manure and fertilizers. They also developed a land revenue system based on the fertility and productivity of the soil.
- The medieval period (600-1500 AD) witnessed the rise and fall of various dynasties and kingdoms, such as the Cholas, the Rashtrakutas, the Delhi Sultanate, and the Vijayanagara Empire. Agriculture remained the mainstay of the economy, with regional variations and innovations. Some of the crops grown during this period were rice, wheat, millets, sorghum, maize, pulses, oilseeds, spices, fruits, vegetables, and flowers. The medieval period also saw the introduction of new crops from other regions, such as citrus, mango, banana, coconut, sugarcane, cotton, indigo, and tobacco.
- The Mughal Empire (1526-1857) was the last major imperial power in India, which brought about many changes and developments in agriculture. The Mughals consolidated and expanded the land revenue system, introduced new crops such as saffron, melons, grapes, and watermelons, and improved the irrigation network with canals, dams, and wells. They also promoted the cultivation of cash crops, such as cotton, indigo, and opium, for export and trade.
- The colonial period (1757-1947) marked the beginning of the British rule in India, which had a profound impact on the agricultural sector. The British imposed heavy taxes and rents on the farmers, forced them to grow cash crops for the benefit of the colonial economy, and disrupted the traditional agrarian system and society. They also introduced new crops, such as tea, coffee, rubber, and jute, and new technologies, such as railways, steam engines, and modern irrigation.
- The post-independence period (1947-present) is the current phase of Indian agriculture, which has undergone many challenges and transformations. The main goals of the post-independence period were to achieve food security, self-sufficiency, and sustainability. Some of the major initiatives and achievements of this period were the Green Revolution, the White Revolution, the Blue Revolution, the biotechnology revolution, the organic farming movement, and the agricultural diversification and export.
