# Brahmi Script

- Brahmi script is the earliest writing system developed in India after the Indus script.
- It is one of the most influential writing systems; all modern Indian scripts and several hundred scripts found in Southeast and East Asia are derived from Brahmi .
- It appeared as a fully developed script in the third century BCE and was used for writing various languages, such as Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali, Tamil, and others .
- It is mentioned in the ancient Indian texts of Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism, as well as their Chinese translations. For example, the Lalitavistara Sūtra lists 64 scripts, with the Brahmi script starting the list.
- It is written from left to right, with each character representing a consonant and an inherent vowel /a/ . The vowel can be changed by adding diacritical marks above, below, or next to the consonant .
- It has 33 basic consonants and 9 basic vowels, as well as some special symbols for numerals, punctuation, and ligatures .
- It has several regional variants, such as Ashokan Brahmi, Kharosthi, Tamil Brahmi, and Gupta Brahmi . These variants differ in the shape and number of characters, as well as the direction of writing .
- It was gradually replaced by its descendants, the Brahmic scripts, from around the 5th century CE onwards . Some of the Brahmic scripts are Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Sinhala, Thai, and Tibetan .
