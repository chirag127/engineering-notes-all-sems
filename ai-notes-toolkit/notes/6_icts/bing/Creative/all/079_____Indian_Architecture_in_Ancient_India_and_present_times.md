# Indian Architecture in Ancient India and present times

- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India. It reflects the diversity and richness of the Indian civilization and its interactions with other cultures and regions.
- Indian architecture can be broadly classified into two main styles: Hindu temple architecture and Indo-Islamic architecture. Both styles have evolved over time and have influenced each other in various ways.
- Hindu temple architecture is based on the principles of Vastu Shastra, a traditional system of architecture that prescribes the layout, design, and orientation of buildings in relation to the natural and cosmic forces. Hindu temples are usually dedicated to a specific deity and consist of a sanctum (garbhagriha), a hall (mandapa), and a tower (shikhara). Hindu temples can be further divided into two sub-styles: Nagara and Dravidian .
- Nagara style is prevalent in northern India and features a curvilinear tower with a ribbed roof, a square or rectangular plan, and a projecting porch. Examples of Nagara temples include the Kandariya Mahadeva Temple at Khajuraho, the Sun Temple at Konark, and the Lingaraja Temple at Bhubaneswar .
- Dravidian style is prevalent in southern India and features a pyramidal tower with a flat roof, a rectangular or octagonal plan, and a pillared hall. Examples of Dravidian temples include the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur, the Meenakshi Temple at Madurai, and the Ranganathaswamy Temple at Srirangam .
- Indo-Islamic architecture is a fusion of Islamic and Indian elements that emerged after the arrival of Muslim invaders and rulers in India. It is characterized by the use of arches, domes, minarets, and geometric patterns, as well as the incorporation of Hindu motifs and symbols. Indo-Islamic architecture can be further divided into several sub-styles, such as Rajput, Mughal, South Indian, and Indo-Saracenic.
- Rajput architecture is the style of the Hindu Rajput kingdoms that flourished in western and central India from the 8th to the 18th centuries. It is influenced by both Hindu and Islamic styles and features elaborate fortifications, palaces, temples, and cenotaphs. Examples of Rajput architecture include the Chittor Fort, the Hawa Mahal, and the Kumbhalgarh Fort.
- Mughal architecture is the style of the Mughal Empire that ruled most of India from the 16th to the 18th centuries. It is influenced by Persian, Turkish, and Indian styles and features monumental tombs, mosques, gardens, and forts. Examples of Mughal architecture include the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, and the Jama Masjid.
- South Indian architecture is the style of the Muslim dynasties that ruled parts of southern India from the 13th to the 18th centuries, such as the Bahmani, the Deccan, and the Qutb Shahi. It is influenced by both Hindu and Islamic styles and features rock-cut caves, mosques, tombs, and palaces. Examples of South Indian architecture include the Gol Gumbaz, the Charminar, and the Qutb Shahi tombs.
- Indo-Saracenic architecture is the style of the British colonial period that emerged in the 19th and early 20th centuries. It is a blend of Islamic, Hindu, Gothic, and Neoclassical styles and features domes, arches, spires, and columns. Examples of Indo-Saracenic architecture include the Victoria Memorial, the Gateway of India, and the Rashtrapati Bhavan.
- The evolution of architecture in India is a result of the changing socio-economic, political, and cultural factors, as well as the influence of globalisation and modernisation. The advent of the 20th century saw a growth in the economy and the population, which created a high demand for housing and urban development. The migration of people and the exposure to foreign cultures led to a shift in Indian architecture from a holistic to a functional point of view.
