# Geography in India and Ancient India

- India is a large and diverse country in South Asia, with a total area of 3,287,263 square kilometres (1,269,219 sq mi) and a population of 1,296,834,042  .
- India is bordered by the Himalayan Mountains in the north and the Indian Ocean, the Arabian Sea, and the Bay of Bengal in the south  .
- India has a variety of geographic features, such as plains, plateaus, deserts, hills, and islands  .
- India has a monsoon climate, with wet and dry seasons, and a range of temperatures from tropical to temperate  .
- India got its name from the River Indus, which was one of the major rivers that supported the ancient civilizations of the Indus Valley  .
- Ancient India was also influenced by other rivers, such as the Ganges, the Brahmaputra, and the Narmada, which provided water, fertile soil, and transportation   .
- Ancient India was divided into several regions, such as the Gangetic Plain, the Deccan Plateau, the Thar Desert, and the Coastal Plains, each with its own culture, history, and resources   .
- Ancient India was home to many kingdoms, empires, and dynasties, such as the Mauryan, the Gupta, the Kushan, the Chola, and the Mughal, which contributed to the political, economic, social, and cultural development of the region .
- Ancient India was also a center of learning, religion, art, literature, and science, with achievements in mathematics, astronomy, medicine, architecture, sculpture, and philosophy .
- Ancient India had interactions and exchanges with other regions of the world, such as China, Central Asia, Southeast Asia, and Europe, through trade, migration, and cultural diffusion .
