# Six System Indian Philosophy

The six system Indian philosophy refers to six schools of thought that originated in ancient India and are based on the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. These schools are:

- **Nyaya**: The school of logic and epistemology, founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE. It deals with the methods of acquiring valid knowledge and the criteria of truth. It also discusses topics such as perception, inference, analogy, testimony, self-awareness, and the nature of the self, God, and the world .
- **Vaisheshika**: The school of atomism and naturalism, founded by Kanada Kashyapa in the 6th century BCE. It deals with the classification and analysis of the physical and metaphysical entities, such as substance, quality, action, relation, and universality. It also proposes that the ultimate reality consists of indivisible atoms and the void, and that the atoms combine and disintegrate according to the laws of causation .
- **Samkhya**: The school of dualism and realism, founded by Kapila in the 6th century BCE. It deals with the theory of evolution and the distinction between the two fundamental principles of reality: purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter). It also discusses the concept of the three gunas (qualities) of prakriti, the 25 tattvas (categories) of existence, and the liberation of the purusha from the bondage of prakriti .
- **Yoga**: The school of discipline and practice, founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE. It deals with the methods of controlling the body, the senses, and the mind, and attaining the state of samadhi (absorption) and kaivalya (isolation) of the purusha from the prakriti. It also discusses the concept of the eight limbs of yoga, the five kleshas (afflictions), and the four stages of consciousness .
- **Purva Mimamsa**: The school of ritual and exegesis, founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE. It deals with the interpretation and application of the Vedic texts, especially the Brahmanas and the Samhitas, and the performance of the sacrificial rites and duties. It also discusses the concept of the apurva (unseen force), the dharma (moral law), and the four purusharthas (human goals) .
- **Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta**: The school of metaphysics and theology, founded by Badarayana in the 4th century BCE. It deals with the analysis and synthesis of the Upanishads, the philosophical and mystical portions of the Vedas, and the commentary on the Brahma Sutras, the aphorisms on the nature of Brahman (the supreme reality). It also discusses the concept of the atman (the individual self), the maya (the cosmic illusion), and the moksha (the liberation from the cycle of rebirth) .

These six systems of Indian philosophy are not mutually exclusive, but rather complementary and interrelated. They represent different aspects and perspectives of the same reality, and they aim to provide a comprehensive and coherent understanding of the self, the world, and the ultimate truth.
