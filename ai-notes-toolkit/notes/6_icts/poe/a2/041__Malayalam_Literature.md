### Malayalam Literature

1. Malayalam literature refers to literature written in Malayalam, a South-Dravidian language spoken mainly in the Indian state of Kerala. Malayalam literature flourished in the Middle Ages, reaching its zenith in the 12th century with Sanskrit epics and native poems.

2. The earliest extant literary works in Malayalam are inscriptions on copper and stone dating from the 9th century. Adhyatma Ramayana, written in the 14th century, is the earliest extant manuscript composed entirely in Malayalam. Malayalam literature also includes prose, poetry, plays, and folk songs.

3. Ezhuthachan (fl. 5th century) is considered the father of Malayalam literature. Cherussery's Krishnagatha (Song of Krishna), also known as Charitham, tells the life of Krishna in poetic metre. The Mahabaratham called Thaniyunnathu Bhagavathi Vilasam was written in pure Malayalam by Thunchathu Ramanujan Ezhuthachan.

4. Medieval Malayalam literature (roughly from 14th to 16th century CE) covered Sanskrit, Tamil, religious themes, folk epics and inscriptions. Notable authors include Cherusseri Namboothiri, who composed Krishnagatha in ornate manipravalam verse. Thulasidasa Kadungal (fl. c. 17th century) wrote works glorifying Rama and Shiva. The Ramacharitham, especially the Dhandakam, by Kunchan Nambiar (1705–1790), was pioneering modern Malayalam literature in subjective and social themes.
