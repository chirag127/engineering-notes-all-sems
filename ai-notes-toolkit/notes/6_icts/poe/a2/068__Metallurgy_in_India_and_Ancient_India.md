### Metallurgy in India and Ancient India

1. Metallurgy is the scientific and engineering study of metals - their properties and usage. The Indian subcontinent has a long history of metallurgy going back to the Bronze Age. Archaeological evidence suggest copper was melted and cast into objects around 4500 BCE in the area.

2. The Indus Valley Civilization shows evidence of early metalworking, with copper artefacts as early as the 5th millennium BCE. The people of the Indus valley produced tools and weapons from bronze alloys and some copper. The use of iron was introduced to India around 1800 BCE with the Harappan culture.

3. Iron working in India dates back to at least 1800 BCE in present-day Uttarakhand. Archaeological sites in the Gangetic plain show iron working around 1200 BCE. Wootz steel was produced in ancient India, around 400 BCE. Archaeological evidence suggests that wootz steel was exported from South India around 300 CE to Europe and the Middle East.

4. Ancient Tamil literature describes the working of furnaces with forced draft using large bellows and the fixing of carbon content in wootz steel by quenching and selective tempering. The Greeks and Romans valued Indian steel as the finest in the world for making swords. Wootz steel was widely exported and traded overland to the Middle East and Europe.

5. Zinc was smelted and used in ancient India as early as the 2nd century CE. Brass, a copper-zinc alloy, was produced around this time in lower Hindustan. Ancient Indians mastered zinc smelting and significantly contributed to the development of metallurgy.
