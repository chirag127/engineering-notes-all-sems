#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India.

1. In ancient Indian society, women were highly respected and had important roles to play in the family and society. However, they were also expected to be subservient to men.
2. Women in the Vedic period enjoyed a relatively high status in society. They could attend schools, participate in public debates, and even compose hymns. However, their primary role was as a wife and mother.
3. By the post-Vedic period, the status of women had started declining. The Dharmashastras and Code of Manu defined women as inferior to men and imposed many restrictions on them. They were expected to be devoted to their fathers, husbands, and sons.
4. Widow remarriage was prohibited, and they had to lead an austere life. Child marriage, and the practice of Sati where a widow immolates herself on her husband's pyre were prevalent. These practices worsened the condition of women.
5. Yet, women from royal families and upper castes had more freedom and were educated in the arts, philosophy, and science. Many women were also patrons of the arts and had considerable wealth. But they still had a secondary status in a male-dominated society.
6. Overall, while women in ancient India were given a high status in some respects, patriarchal practices resulted in the loss of independence and subservience of women to the will of men. Their lives were deeply influenced by the traditions and customs of the time.
