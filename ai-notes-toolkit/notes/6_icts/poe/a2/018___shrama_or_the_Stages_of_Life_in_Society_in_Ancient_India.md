#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

- In ancient Indian society, the life of an individual was divided into four Āshramas or stages. These were:
- Brahmacharya Āshrama: In this stage, a person was expected to learn the Vedas and acquire knowledge. This stage was for students and continued till the age of 25.
- Grihastha Āshrama: In this stage, a person was expected to marry, raise a family and earn a livelihood. This was the longest stage and continued till the age of 50.
- Vanaprastha Āshrama: In this stage, a person was expected to retire from worldly life, hand over family responsibilities to the next generation and move to a forest. This stage continued till the age of 75.
- Sannyasa Āshrama: In this final stage, a person renounced all worldly attachments and spent his time in meditation, prayers and religious reflection. This continued till the death of the person.

The four stages were meant to divide life into different pursuits, enable fulfillment of duties and prepare for ultimate liberation of the soul from the cycles of rebirth. The Āshrama system reflected the philosophical ideas and values of ancient Indian society.
