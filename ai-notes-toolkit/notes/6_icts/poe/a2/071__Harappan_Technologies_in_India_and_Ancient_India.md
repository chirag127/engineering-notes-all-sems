### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- The Harappan civilization that flourished in the Indus Valley from 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE had developed advanced technologies in various fields:
- Metallurgy: The Harappans produced copper, bronze, lead and tin. They were able to fashion tools like razors, blades and arrowheads out of these metals.
- Structural engineering: The Harappans built planned cities with grid patterns, underground sewage systems, citadels and granaries. They constructed sturdy houses of baked bricks.
- Pottery: The potters of Harappa mass produced wheel-thrown pottery with fine glossy finishes in a variety of shapes and sizes. They were also able to produce terracotta sculptures.
- Trade and transportation: The Harappans had a strong maritime trade network and also constructed boats and operated cargo ships. They had wheeled carts which were pulled by oxen.
- Agriculture: The Harappans grew wheat, barley, peas, sesame and cotton. They understood crop rotation and used plows to till the land. They were able to store grains for years in granaries.

Ancient India also had significant technological achievements from around 6000 BCE:

- Neolithic settlements with permanent houses and pottery have been excavated.
- Megalithic dolmens and stone circles from around 3000 BCE have been found.
- Iron smelting technology was developed in 1800 BCE. Iron tools and weapons became more common.
- The Mauryans built a system of roads, canals and infrastructure. Ashoka erected stone pillars and stupas.
- Astronomers from Ancient India proposed heliocentric theories and calculated astronomical constants with a fair degree of accuracy.
- Complex medical practices and operations were performed. Many medications and procedures were documented in texts like the Sushruta Samhita and Charaka Samhita.
