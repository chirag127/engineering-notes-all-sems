### Drama as Cultural Heritage of India

1. Indian drama has a long and rich tradition spanning over 2000 years. Ancient Indian drama is based on Sanskrit plays, which were performed during religious and social ceremonies.

2. The Natyashastra by Bharata Muni (2nd century BCE) is considered as the foundation treatise of dramaturgy in Sanskrit literature. It covers various aspects of play production like stagecraft, acting, dance, music, types of drama and the audience.

3. There were different forms of drama that evolved in various regions of the country like:
    - Rupaka: ten types including Nataka (realistic social play) and Prakarana (farce)
    - Bhana (one-act monologue play)
    - Dima (folk play)
    - Jatra (folk play)

4. Famous ancient Sanskrit plays include:
    - Shakuntala by Kalidasa
    - Mricchakatika by Shudraka
    - Abhijnanashakuntala by Kalidasa

5. The tradition of Indian drama continues in the modern era with various theatre forms like Nautanki (north India), Tamasha (Maharashtra), Yakshagana (Karnataka) etc. Street plays and folk theatre are also popular in rural India.

6. Indian drama has played an important role in preserving the country's cultural heritage by promoting social reforms, awakening nationalism and educating and entertaining the masses. It represents the rich linguistic and cultural diversity of India.
