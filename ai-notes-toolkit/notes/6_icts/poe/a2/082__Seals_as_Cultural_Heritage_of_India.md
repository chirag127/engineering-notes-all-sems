### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

1. Seals were an important part of the Indus Valley Civilization. They were generally made of steatite and had unique imagery and symbols engraved on them. These seals provide insights into the trade, script, art, economy, religion, and other aspects of the Indus Valley Civilization.

2. The most famous seal is the Pashupati Seal that depicts a figure sitting in a yogic posture surrounded by animals. It is believed to be a proto-Shiva figure and shows the importance of religious beliefs and practices in the Indus Valley Civilization. Other seals show unicorn-like animals, tigers, elephants, and various symbols and scripts.

3. The seals are one of the most significant discoveries from the Indus Valley Civilization and are a cultural heritage of India. They provide evidence of the technological advancement and rich culture of the civilization. The imagery and symbols on the seals have been widely studied but there is no consensus on their exact meanings due to the lack of decipherment of the Indus script.

4. Many seals have been found from Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro, and other Indus Valley Civilization sites. They are displayed in museums across India and Pakistan as a representation of the enigma and legacy of the ancient Indus Valley Civilization. As cultural artifacts, the seals give a glimpse into the art, economy, and beliefs of one of the earliest civilizations of the Indian subcontinent.
