### Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

1. Dharma and righteousness: The ancient Indian political thought was based on the concept of Dharma which meant righteousness and moral duty. The king and the state were expected to uphold Dharma. This is relevant even today as the state is expected to act in a righteous manner and not arbitrarily.

2. Welfare state: The Arthashastra states that the state's duty is the welfare of the people. It advocates policies for the welfare and prosperity of farmers, traders, and labourers. This political ideal of a welfare state is very relevant today and many of the government policies and schemes are aimed at welfare and development.

3. Limited state power: The ancient Indian philosophers were wary of concentration of power in the state. The king's power was thought of as limited by Dharma. This idea is relevant in modern democracies where the power of the state is limited by the constitution.

4. Decentralisation: Ancient Indian polity was highly decentralised with village communities and guilds enjoying a great deal of autonomy. This led to diversity and balance of power. In today's context, decentralisation of power to local governments is an important ideal to ensure greater participation of people.

The political ideals of ancient India such as dharma, welfare of people, limits on power of the state, and decentralisation contain insights that are relevant in modern democracies. While the modern context is very different, these ancient ideals can enrich political philosophy and policymaking today.
