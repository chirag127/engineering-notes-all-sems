### Mathematics in India and Ancient India

1. The Indus Valley civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE) had a advanced system of weights and measures. They used a decimal place value system and the length units were based on a scale of 10.

2. The Sulba Sutras (800 BCE - 500 BCE) contained geometry techniques to construct altars and temples. They contained approximations of pi and the Pythagorean triple. The Baudhayana Sulba Sutra in particular contained a correct statement of the Pythagorean theorem.

3. The Jain astronomical text Surya Prajnapti (dated to around 400 CE) classified all numbers into three categories: enumerable, innumerable, and infinite. They also estimated the diameters of the sun and moon and their distances from the earth using formulas.

4. The famous work Siddhanta Shiromani (11th century) by Bhaskara II contains mathematical chapters that cover topics from arithmetic, algebra, geometry and trigonometry including solutions of quadratic equations and indeterminate equations. It also covers work on infinite series, fractions and second-order equations.

5. Kerala mathematicians independently discovered results of calculus, infinite series and trigonometry between the 14th and 16th centuries. Madhava of Sangamagrama and Nilakantha Somayaji found approximations of pi and calculations of the area and perimeter of ellipses and infinite series expansions of trigonometric functions.

The mathematical works in ancient India were composed in Sanskrit and were written in poetic verse form with meters making them difficult to understand today without assistance. However, they provide evidence of an advanced mathematical tradition in ancient India that was influential in the regions development of mathematics.
