### Kingship in states in Ancient India

1. In Ancient India, most states were monarchies ruled by kings. The king was the supreme ruler and wielded considerable power and influence.

2. The king was not elected but inherited his position based on birth. The eldest son of the previous king usually succeeded to the throne. Primogeniture was the most common form of succession.

3. The king was expected to be a just and ethical ruler. He had to uphold dharma (righteousness) and ensure the welfare and prosperity of his subjects. The king's dharma was called rajadharma - the duties of a king.

4. The daily affairs of the state were managed by ministers and officials appointed by the king. The king depended on their advice and assistance in administering the kingdom and implementing policies.

5. The power of the king was not absolute. He had to rule according to the codes of righteousness and could not act arbitrarily. If he did so, his authority could be challenged. Also, Brahmins and ministers could restrain a king acting against dharma.

6. The coronation of a king was a elaborate ritual which symbolized his divine authority and sanction to rule. By performing the ritual, the king acquired religious and mystical power to rule justly and wisely.

7. The stability and efficiency of a kingdom depended greatly on the personal qualities and capabilities of the king. A strong, virtuous and competent king could lead his kingdom to prosperity while a weak or tyrannical king could lead to chaos and decline.
