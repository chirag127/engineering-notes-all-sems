## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- Hinduism is the dominant religion of India. It includes Shaivism, Vaishnavism and Shaktism as major traditions. It includes belief in reincarnation, karma, and the worship of Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Ganesha and other deities.
- Buddhism originated in ancient India. It was founded by Siddhartha Gautama (Buddha) around the 6th century BCE. Buddhists follow the teachings of the Buddha and believe in concepts like karma and rebirth. The major branches are Theravada and Mahayana.
- Jainism is an ancient Indian religion. Followers of Jainism are called Jains, and they follow the teachings of the 24 Tirthankaras. Jains believe in karma and rebirth, and aim to liberate the soul from the cycle of rebirth by following strict ethical guidelines. The main principles are non-violence, non-attachment and non-absolutism.
- Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century CE. The Holy Book of Sikhism is the Guru Granth Sahib. Sikhs follow the teachings of the Gurus and the core beliefs include faith and meditation on the name of the creator, devotion and remembrance of the creator, engaging in selfless service, striving for social justice for the benefit and prosperity of all, honest conduct and livelihood while living a householder's life.
- Other Indian philosophies include Charvaka, an atheist materialist school; Ajivika, a ascetic movement; and Lokayata, which emphasized direct perception and rejected inference. Various ideas from Indian philosophies have influenced other traditions.
