#### Varnāshrama System in Society in Ancient India

- The Varnāshrama system was prevalent in ancient Indian society which stratified people into hierarchical categories based on their occupations and stages of life.
- The system divided people into four varnas - Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors), Vaishyas (traders and merchants), and Shudras (labourers).
- Each varna had certain duties and responsibilities as per their designated occupations which was meant to ensure a smooth functioning of society with all parts working in harmony with each other.
- The Āshram system divided a person's life into four stages - Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate) which laid down the stages and duties in each phase of life from youth to old age.
- The Varnāshrama system aimed to achieve social stability and order through this hierarchical structure and division of duties based on occupations and life stages. However, it was criticized as being rigid and inflexible and leading to social stratification and inequality.
- This system was an important part of the ancient Indian social structure but started declining around the 6th century CE with the rise of devotional movements that rejected the rigid hierarchical system. It has little relevance in modern Indian society.
