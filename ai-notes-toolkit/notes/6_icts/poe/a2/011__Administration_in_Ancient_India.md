### Administration in Ancient India

- The king was the supreme ruler and head of the administration. He had ministers and officers to assist him in governing the kingdom.
- The king's chief advisor was the Pradhan Mantri or the Prime Minister. He aided and advised the king on important matters of the state.
- There were other ministers in charge of different departments like taxation, army, agriculture, etc. They implemented the king's orders in their respective departments.
- The kingdom was divided into provinces ruled by governors. They were responsible for law and order and collecting taxes in their provinces.
- There were village councils or panchayats at the local level. They looked after the affairs of the village including irrigation, sanitation, resolving local disputes, etc.
- The king's officers like the revenue collectors, accountants and tax assessors maintained accounts and records of taxes that came from provinces and villages.
- The spy system was used to know about the people's sentiments and check if the officers were honest in their work. Spies directly reported to the king.
- The army played an important role in maintaining law and order and expanding the kingdom's boundaries. The commander-in-chief led the army on the king's orders.
- In this way, the ancient Indian administrative system was hierarchical with the king at the top and officers and councils at lower levels to aid in effective governance of the state.
