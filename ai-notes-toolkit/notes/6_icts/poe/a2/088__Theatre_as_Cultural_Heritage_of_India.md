### Theatre as Cultural Heritage of India

1. Indian theatre has a long and rich tradition spanning over 2000 years. It started with religious rituals and slowly evolved into an independent art form.
2. Sanskrit theatre originated with the Natya Shastra, an ancient Indian text on the performing arts from the 1st century BCE. It contained guidelines on acting, dance, music and other aspects of theatre. Koodiyattam and Nangiar Koothu are two surviving Sanskrit theatre art forms.
3. Folk theatre forms are varied, with distinct styles specific to each region. For example, Bhavai is a popular form of folk theatre from Gujarat, while Nautanki and Swang are from Uttar Pradesh. Villupattu is a Tamil theatre form performed in village squares.
4. Indian theatre has borrowed elements from Western styles since colonial times. This led to new genres such as Parsi theatre and Tamasha in Maharashtra which incorporated music, dance and lavish costumes. These forms remain popular as forms of musical theatre in India.
5. Post-independence, theatre in India aimed to address social issues and raise political awareness. Groups like the Indian People's Theatre Association (IPTA) employed street theatre and other non-traditional forms to take theatre to the masses. Contemporary Indian theatre continues this tradition of political and social commentary.
6. Theatre in India thus has a rich history of blending traditional and contemporary forms. It is a vibrant cultural and artistic expression that highlights the diversity of Indian culture. Recognizing and preserving the varied Indian theatre arts is vital to conserve India's cultural heritage.
