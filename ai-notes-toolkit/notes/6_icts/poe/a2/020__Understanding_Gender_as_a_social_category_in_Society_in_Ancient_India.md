#### Understanding Gender as a social category in Society in Ancient India.

- In ancient Indian society, gender was an important social category that determined a person's rights, privileges, and social identity. A person's gender at birth fixed their position and sphere of activity in society.
- Men were considered superior to women. Women faced discrimination and subordination in social, economic, and political spheres of life.
- A woman's primary roles were as a wife and mother. She was expected to be devoted to her husband and bear sons. Women had limited freedom and were expected to be subservient to men - fathers, husbands, and sons.
- Gender roles were strict and segregation was prevalent. There were gender-specific occupational roles. Separate spheres of activity were prescribed for men and women through religious ordinances.
- The binary of "pure/impure" was additionally applied to women, restricting their participation in religious/social activities during menstruation or pregnancy. This symbolized their lower social status.
- However, women were not entirely powerless. Women of upper castes/classes, especially royal women, enjoyed more freedom and wielded considerable influence in familial and courtly matters. Some women also received education and participated in intellectual pursuits.
- In conclusion, while gender was an important marker of social identity in ancient India and patriarchal norms were prevalent, the experience of gender differed based on other factors like caste, class, and region. Gender roles were more fluid and complex than a simple binary of domination/subordination.
