### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India.

1. Introduction
- 19th century India witnessed many social and religious reform movements across the country as a response to the rigid social practices and outdated customs and rituals.
- These movements critiqued harmful practices like sati, child marriage, polygamy, etc. and aimed to reform the Indian society.

2. Brahmo Samaj
- Founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828.
- Derived ideas from Christianity and Western philosophy.
- Rejected idols and emphasis on one formless God.
- Sought to reform Hinduism and uplift the status of women.

3. Arya Samaj
- Founded by Swami Dayananda Saraswati in 1875.
- Emphasized on the Vedas as the sole authority and rejected idolatry.
- Promoted reformation of Hinduism to its Vedic roots.
- Encouraged widow remarriage, equality for women, and education.

4. Other movements
- Theosophical Society by Helena Blavatsky and Henry Olcott to revive spiritual ideas.
- Ramakrishna Mission by Swami Vivekananda combined Vedanta, yoga, and social service.
- Several Sikh and Muslim reform movements also emerged to reinterpret religious teachings.

5. Impact and significance
- These movements attacked evil practices, promoted modern and scientific thought, and empowered women.
- They prepared the ground for the growth of nationalism and independence struggle in India.
- However, they were limited to upper castes and ethnocentric in their worldview. Dalit and lower caste voices were marginalized.
