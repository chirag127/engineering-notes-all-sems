### Jainism in India

1. Jainism originated in ancient India around the 6th century BCE. It was founded by Mahavira, a contemporary of the Buddha.
2. Jains believe that liberation from karma and the cycle of rebirth can be attained by following a path of non-violence and non-attachment. Jains do not believe in a creator deity.
3. Jain practices include meditation, asceticism, and following strict ethical principles such as non-harming, truthfulness, non-stealing, chastity, and non-possessiveness. Jains are vegetarians and many observe fasting during holy periods.
4. There are around 4 million Jains worldwide, with most living in India. The largest Jain communities are in the states of Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Karnataka.
5. The Jain flag and symbol represents the Three Jewels of Jainism: right perception or faith, right knowledge, and right conduct. The swastika and hand with wheel symbols are also commonly seen in Jain art and architecture.
6. The primary Jain scriptures are known as Agamas. Jains are divided into two major sects: Digambara which means "sky-clad" ( they practice nudity), and Svetambara which means "white-clad".
7. The tirthankaras are great Jain teachers who have attained liberation and are worthy of worship. The 24th and most recent tirthankara was Mahavira. Jain temples often contain statues and images of the tirthankaras.
