### Bhakti Movement in India

1. The Bhakti movement was a reform movement that originated in South India in the 7th-8th centuries and spread to North India in the 14th-15th centuries.
2. The Bhakti poets and saints rejected the ritualism of the Brahmanical system and criticized the caste system. They promoted devotion and direct individual connection with God through songs and poetry in vernacular languages.
3. The Alvars and Nayanars were the Tamil saint-poets who pioneered the Bhakti movement. In North India, saints like Kabir, Guru Nanak, Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai propagated the path of Bhakti through their verses and examples.
4. The Bhakti Saints worshipped different deities - Vishnu, Shiva and Devi. They rejected rituals and ceremonies and believed in self-surrender and pure devotion to God as the path to Moksha. Their teachings had a significant impact on Indian society.
5. The Bhakti movement led to the growth of regional languages as the saints composed poetry in local languages to reach out to the masses. It also strengthened the status of women as some renowned Bhakti saints like Mirabai were women. The principles of equality, brotherhood and tolerance inherent in the Bhakti movement had an influence on Sikhism and certain aspects of Islam as well.
