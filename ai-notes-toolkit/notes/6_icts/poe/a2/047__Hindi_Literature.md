### Hindi Literature

1. Hindi literature refers to literature in the Hindi language, including the literature of Khariboli dialect that evolved into modern Standard Hindi. Hindi literature encompasses some of the oldest surviving works of literature in India.

2. The earliest works in Hindi literature were composed in other varieties of Hindi, especially Avadhi and Braj Bhasha. The most notable figures from the 11th century are the poet Keshava Das and the polymath Kalidasa. Later poets include Bihari, in whose honor the Bihari dialect is named, and the epic poet Tulsidas, who composed the Ramcharitmanas.

3. Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, a bhakti saint, composed devotional poems in a Braj dialect considered a forerunner of modern Hindi. Late medieval Hindi literature covered topics from religion to political treatises on statecraft. Mughal court poets in the Hindi language include Kaviraj, Bihari and Sundardas.

4. In the 19th century, Hindi literature was modernized by authors such as Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi, Munshi Premchand, Jaishankar Prasad, Sumitranandan Pant, Suryakant Tripathi 'Nirala', Mahadevi Varma and Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar'.

5. In the 20th century, Hindi literature moved into the modern age with romantic, social and nationalist themes gaining popularity. In contemporary Hindi literature, subtly explores themes ranging from existentialism to issues such as corruption. Literary modernism in Hindi literature reached its peak with the "little magazine movement" which started in the mid-1960s.
