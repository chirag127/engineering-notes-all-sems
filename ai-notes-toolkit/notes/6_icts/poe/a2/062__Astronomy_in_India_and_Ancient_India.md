### Astronomy in India and Ancient India

1. Ancient Indian astronomers made significant contributions to astronomy. Their discoveries and calculations include:
- Estimation of sidereal rotation period of 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4.1 seconds.
- Calculation of annular solar eclipses and lunar eclipses.
- Division of zodiac into 27 constellations.
- Discovery of planets, lunar phases and theories on planetary motion.

2. Aryabhata was a notable mathematician and astronomer who formulated the heliocentric theory of gravity in the 5th century. He explained planetary motion and eclipses scientifically contrary to religious belief at the time. He calculated the sidereal rotation as 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4.1 seconds.

3. Brahmagupta was another prominent mathematician and astronomer who made contributions to algebra and arithmetic in the 7th century. He theorized that the earth was spherical and attracted objects, and calculated the ellipticity of the earth around 1,000 years before Newton. He also proposed laws of motion and made advances in trigonometry.

4. Jain astronomers including Mahavira made contributions to astronomy and proposed ideas such as moonlight being reflected sunlight and calculations on planetary diameters, orbits and eclipses. They contributed to geometry and algebra as well.

5. From ancient to medieval India, Indian astronomy was influenced by Hellenistic astronomy and Islamic astronomy which led to translations of foreign works and new observations and calculations by Indian astronomers. Notable astronomers during this time include astronomer and mathematician Bhaskara II. Overall, Indian astronomy has a long history and made significant contributions to the field.
