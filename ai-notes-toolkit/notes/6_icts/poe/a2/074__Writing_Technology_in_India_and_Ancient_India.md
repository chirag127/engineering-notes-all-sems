### Writing Technology in India and Ancient India

1. The Indus Valley civilization which flourished during 3300-1300 BCE in northwestern India had a writing system which is evidenced by archaeological finds of seals and tablets with symbols and signs engraved over them. However, the script has not yet been deciphered.

2. The Brahmi script was the first writing system developed in India around the 6th century BCE. It was adapted to write various Indian languages like Prakrit and Sanskrit. The Gupta script evolved from Brahmi and was used to write Sanskrit.

3. The Tamil Brahmi script was developed around 3rd century BCE to write the Tamil language. It evolved into the Grantha and Pallava scripts. The Kadamba script was developed in the 4th century CE to write Kannada.

4. The Nagari script originated from the Gupta script around the 6th century CE. It evolved into the Devanagari script which is used to write Hindi, Marathi, Nepali and other languages. The Bengali script also evolved from the Nagari script around the 10th century CE.

5. Palm leaves and birch bark were initially used as writing materials where letters were scratched using a stylus. Paper and ink were introduced later. The Namputhiri Brahmins in Kerala developed a system of "Telegu Lipi" or inscription of letters on palm leaves around 12th century CE.

6. Ancient Indian mathematics made significant advances and discoveries in the topics of arithmetic, algebra, geometry and trigonometry that were transmitted to the Middle East and Europe. The Bakhshali manuscript which is a birch bark mathematical text was composed around 3rd or 4th century CE.
