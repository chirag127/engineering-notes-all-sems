### Six System Indian Philosophy

1. Samkhya: Dualism. Assumes two eternal realities - Prakriti (matter) and Purusha (soul). Prakriti is the first source of the world, and Purusha is the conscious principle. Liberation is attained by the separation of Purusha from Prakriti.

2. Yoga: Based on Samkhya philosophy but gives methods to attain liberation. Yoga prescribes an eightfold path to achieve Kaivalya (loneliness) or liberation. The eight steps are: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi.

3. Mimamsa: Based on the ritualistic portion of the Vedas. Its aim is to define the nature of Dharma and to show how it can be attained. Emphasizes on the performance of various rituals and duties.

4. Vedanta: Considered the culmination of Indian philosophy. Based on the Upanishads. Provides methods to attain liberation through knowledge of Brahman. The three main schools are Advaita, Visishtadvaita and Dvaita.

5. Nyaya: Focuses on logic and epistemology. It explores sources of knowledge and criteria for verifying truth. Tries to prove the existence of God based on inference.

6. Vaisheshika: Focuses on outlining a systematic philosophy of physics and metaphysics. Proposes an atomic theory of matter where atoms combine in different ways to form the world. Explains qualities, actions, universals, etc. through atoms and their combinations.
