### Six System Indian Philosophy

The Six System Indian Philosophy refers to the six major schools of thought that have influenced the Indian philosophy since ancient times. Each of these schools has its own unique approach and perspective towards understanding the nature of reality, consciousness, existence, and the purpose of human life. Here are the six systems:

1. Samkhya: This school of thought emphasizes the dualism between Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter). According to Samkhya, the ultimate goal of human life is to attain liberation (Moksha) by realizing the difference between the two.

2. Yoga: This system focuses on the union of the individual self (Atman) with the universal self (Brahman) through various practices such as meditation, ethical disciplines, and physical postures.

3. Nyaya: This school of thought is concerned with the logical analysis of knowledge and perception. It emphasizes the use of reason and logic to arrive at the truth.

4. Vaisheshika: This system is based on the idea of atoms and their combinations. It emphasizes the study of the nature of reality and the structure of the universe.

5. Mimamsa: This school of thought is concerned with the interpretation of the Vedic scriptures and the performance of rituals. It emphasizes the importance of proper interpretation of the scriptures and proper performance of rituals in order to achieve the ultimate goal of human life.

6. Vedanta: This system is based on the interpretation of the Upanishads and the Brahma Sutras. It emphasizes the oneness of the individual self (Atman) with the universal self (Brahman) and the ultimate goal of human life to realize this oneness.

Understanding the Six System Indian Philosophy is essential to understanding the Indian culture and its spiritual heritage. Each of these systems provides a unique perspective on the nature of reality and the purpose of human life. By studying these systems, one can gain a deeper insight into the Indian worldview and its contribution to the world's philosophical heritage.
