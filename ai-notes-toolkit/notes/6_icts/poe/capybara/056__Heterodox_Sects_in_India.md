### Heterodox Sects in India

India is known for its diverse religious landscape, and within Hinduism, there are several heterodox sects that have emerged over time. Here are some of the most notable ones:

- **Arya Samaj**: Founded in 1875 by Swami Dayanand Saraswati, Arya Samaj emphasizes the Vedas as the only authoritative texts and rejects idol worship, caste system, and other practices that are not mentioned in the Vedas. They also advocate for social reforms such as women's rights, education, and widow remarriage.

- **Brahma Kumaris**: Founded in 1936 by Lekhraj Kripalani, Brahma Kumaris is a spiritual movement that believes in the practice of Rajyoga meditation to connect with God. They also promote vegetarianism, celibacy, and simplicity in lifestyle.

- **Jainism**: Although not strictly a sect within Hinduism, Jainism originated in India and shares some similarities with Hinduism. Jains believe in the principles of non-violence, truthfulness, and non-attachment to worldly possessions. They also follow a strict vegetarian diet and practice asceticism.

- **Kabir Panth**: Named after the 15th-century saint Kabir, this sect emphasizes the unity of all religions and rejects caste system, idol worship, and other rituals. They also promote the use of Hindi language in religious practices and literature.

- **Radhasoami**: Founded in the 19th century, Radhasoami is a spiritual movement that emphasizes the practice of Surat Shabd Yoga to connect with the divine. They also believe in the existence of a spiritual master who can guide individuals on their spiritual journey.

- **Sikhism**: Although not strictly a sect within Hinduism, Sikhism originated in India and shares some similarities with Hinduism. Sikhs believe in one God, equality of all human beings, and the importance of selfless service to others. They also follow the teachings of the ten Sikh gurus and the holy book, Guru Granth Sahib.

These are just a few examples of the heterodox sects within Hinduism in India. Each of these sects has its own unique beliefs, practices, and traditions, but they all contribute to the rich religious and cultural diversity of India.
