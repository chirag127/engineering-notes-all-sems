#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

In ancient India, society was divided into four stages of life, known as Āshrama. These stages were based on a person's age and responsibilities, and each stage had its own duties and obligations.

The four stages of life in ancient India were:

1. Brahmacharya - This was the stage of life dedicated to learning and education. It began at the age of five and continued until the age of 25. During this stage, young boys would live with a teacher and learn about religion, philosophy, and other subjects. They were expected to remain celibate and focus on their studies.

2. Grihastha - This was the stage of life dedicated to family and household duties. It began at the age of 25 and continued until the age of 50. During this stage, men were expected to marry and start a family. They were also expected to work and provide for their family. Women were expected to support their husbands and raise children.

3. Vanaprastha - This was the stage of life dedicated to spiritual pursuits. It began at the age of 50 and continued until the age of 75. During this stage, men would retire from their work and hand over their responsibilities to their sons. They would then go into seclusion and focus on spiritual pursuits. Women could also enter this stage of life, but they would typically stay with their husbands and support them.

4. Sannyasa - This was the stage of life dedicated to renunciation and detachment from the material world. It began at the age of 75 and continued until death. During this stage, men would give up all their possessions and become ascetics. They would focus solely on spiritual pursuits and detach themselves from the material world.

These four stages of life were an important part of ancient Indian society. They provided a framework for individuals to live a fulfilling life and contribute to their community. By following these stages, individuals could find meaning and purpose in their lives and achieve spiritual enlightenment.
