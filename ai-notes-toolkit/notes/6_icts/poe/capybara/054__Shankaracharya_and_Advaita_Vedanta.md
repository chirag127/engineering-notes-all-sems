### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

Shankaracharya was an Indian philosopher and theologian who lived in the 8th century AD. He is known for his contribution to the Advaita Vedanta school of Hindu philosophy.

Advaita Vedanta is a school of Hindu philosophy that is based on the idea that there is only one ultimate reality, or Brahman, and that all other entities in the universe are merely manifestations of this reality. Here are some key points to understand Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta:

- Shankaracharya was born in Kerala, India, and is believed to have lived from 788 to 820 AD.
- He is credited with writing commentaries on the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Brahma Sutras, which are considered to be the foundational texts of Advaita Vedanta.
- Shankaracharya's philosophy is based on the idea that the self, or the individual soul, is not separate from Brahman, but is rather a part of it. This is known as non-dualism or monism.
- In Advaita Vedanta, the ultimate goal of human existence is to realize this oneness with Brahman through spiritual practices such as meditation and self-inquiry.
- Shankaracharya also emphasized the importance of karma yoga, or the path of action, as a means of preparing oneself for the realization of Brahman.
- He believed that the Vedas, the ancient Hindu scriptures, were the ultimate source of knowledge and authority, and that they contained the teachings necessary for attaining liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
- Shankaracharya's teachings had a profound influence on Hindu philosophy and spirituality, and his ideas continue to be studied and debated by scholars and practitioners to this day.
