
#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

1. In ancient India, society was divided into four classes: Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Shudra. 
2. The Brahmin class was comprised of priests, scholars, and teachers. They were considered to be the highest class in society and had the most prestige. 
3. The Kshatriya class consisted of warriors, rulers, and administrators. They were second in the class hierarchy and had a great deal of power and influence. 
4. The Vaishya class was made up of merchants, traders, and farmers. They were third in the class hierarchy and had a significant amount of wealth and influence. 
5. The Shudra class was comprised of manual laborers and servants. They were the lowest class in the hierarchy and had very little power or influence. 
6. This four-class system was known as the Varna system and was the basis of the social structure in ancient India. It was very rigid and hierarchical, and there was little social mobility.
