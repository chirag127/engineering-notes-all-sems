
### Council of Ministers in Ancient India

- In ancient India, the Council of Ministers was responsible for the administration of the state and the implementation of royal edicts.
- The Council of Ministers was headed by a Prime Minister, also known as the Mahamantri or Mahapradhana.
- The Prime Minister was appointed by the king and was responsible for the day-to-day running of the state.
- The other members of the Council of Ministers were appointed by the Prime Minister, and their roles included military, finance, justice, and foreign affairs.
- The Council of Ministers was also responsible for the implementation of royal edicts, such as the appointment of officials, the construction of public works, and the enforcement of laws.
- The Council of Ministers was an important part of the royal court, and its members were held in high esteem by the king.
