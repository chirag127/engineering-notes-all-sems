
### Administration in Ancient India

1. In ancient India, the administration was mainly centered around the monarch. The monarch was the highest authority and had the power to make laws, pass judgments and appoint officers to carry out his orders. 
2. The monarch was assisted by a council of ministers, who were responsible for advising the monarch and executing his orders. 
3. The kingdom was divided into provinces and districts, with each province being ruled by a governor appointed by the monarch. 
4. The provinces were further divided into smaller administrative units called the taluks, which were managed by the local rulers. 
5. The local rulers were responsible for collecting taxes, maintaining law and order and administering justice. 
6. The village councils were responsible for the day-to-day administration of the villages. 
7. The administration was based on the principles of justice, fairness and equity. 
8. The administration was also based on the principles of dharma, which was the moral code of conduct for the people. 
9. The administration was also based on the caste system, which divided people into different social classes. 
10. The administration was also based on the principle of decentralization, which allowed the local rulers to have a certain degree of autonomy in their respective provinces.
