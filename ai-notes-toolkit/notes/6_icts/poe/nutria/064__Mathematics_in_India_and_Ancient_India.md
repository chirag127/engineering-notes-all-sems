
### Mathematics in India and Ancient India

* Ancient Indian mathematics dates back to the Vedic period (1700–1200 BCE). It was mainly focused on the use of geometry and algebraic equations to solve practical problems.
* The Sulbasutras, which were written during the period of 800–500 BCE, are the earliest known works on geometry in India. They contain the rules for constructing altars and other ritualistic structures.
* Jain mathematicians developed the concept of the infinite in their work on the theory of numbers and made significant contributions to the development of algebra.
* The most famous mathematician of ancient India was Aryabhata (476–550 CE). He is credited with the invention of the digit zero and the decimal system. He also developed the concept of trigonometry and made important contributions to astronomy and astrology.
* During the Gupta period (320–550 CE), Indian mathematicians made important contributions to the development of algebra, geometry, and trigonometry.
* The most famous mathematician of this period was Brahmagupta (598–668 CE). He developed the concepts of negative numbers, zero, and infinity, and made significant contributions to the development of algebra, trigonometry, and astronomy.
* During the Middle Ages (1000–1500 CE), Indian mathematicians made important contributions to the development of algebra, geometry, and trigonometry.
* The most famous mathematicians of this period were Bhaskara (1114–1185 CE) and Madhava (1340–1425 CE). They developed the concepts of calculus and made significant contributions to the development of trigonometry, algebra, and astronomy.
* In the modern era (1800–present), Indian mathematicians have made significant contributions to the development of mathematics.
* The most famous mathematicians of this period are Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920), Harish Chandra (1923–1983), and S. R. Srinivasa Varadhan (1940–present). They have made important contributions to the fields of analysis, algebra, and number theory.
