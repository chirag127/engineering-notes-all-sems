#### Transmission media in Computer Networks

Transmission media refers to the physical pathways through which data is transmitted from one device to another in a computer network. There are two main types of transmission media: guided and unguided.

1. Guided media: Guided media, also known as wired or bounded transmission media, refers to a physical link that guides the transmission of data signals along a specific path. Examples of guided media include twisted pair cable, coaxial cable, and fiber optic cable.

2. Unguided media: Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded transmission media, refers to the transmission of data signals through the air or space without the use of a physical conductor. Examples of unguided media include radio waves, microwaves, and infrared waves.

Each type of transmission media has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of media depends on factors such as the distance between devices, the required data transfer rate, and the environment in which the network is operating. For example, fiber optic cable is capable of transmitting data at very high speeds over long distances, but it is more expensive and difficult to install than other types of guided media. On the other hand, wireless transmission media is more flexible and easier to install, but it is generally slower and less secure than wired media.
