### IPv6 in CN

- IPv6 stands for Internet Protocol version 6.
- It is the most recent version of the Internet Protocol (IP), the communications protocol that provides an identification and location system for computers on networks and routes traffic across the Internet.
- IPv6 was developed by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) to deal with the long-anticipated problem of IPv4 address exhaustion.
- IPv6 is intended to replace IPv4, which still carries the vast majority of Internet traffic as of 2021.
- One of the key features of IPv6 is its vastly larger address space, which allows for many more devices to be connected to the Internet.
- IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses, compared to the 32-bit addresses used by IPv4. This means that IPv6 can support 2^128 (approximately 340 undecillion) addresses, compared to the 4.3 billion addresses supported by IPv4.
- In addition to the larger address space, IPv6 also includes improvements to routing and network autoconfiguration, as well as improved support for mobile devices and better security features.
- The adoption of IPv6 has been slow, but it is gradually increasing as more devices and networks support the protocol.
- In CN (Computer Networks), IPv6 is an important topic as it is the future of Internet communication and networking. Understanding the features and capabilities of IPv6 is essential for anyone working in the field of computer networking.
