#### Network performance and transmission impairments in Computer Networks

Network performance refers to the level of quality of service of a telecommunications product as seen by the customer. It should not be seen merely as an attempt to get "more through" the network. Performance can also be modeled in terms of the availability of network resources.

Transmission impairments are factors that degrade the quality of a transmitted signal. These impairments can be caused by a variety of factors, including attenuation, delay distortion, and noise.

- **Attenuation**: Attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it travels through a medium. This can be caused by the absorption of the signal by the medium, as well as by scattering and reflection. Attenuation is measured in decibels (dB) and is typically greater at higher frequencies.

- **Delay distortion**: Delay distortion occurs when different frequency components of a signal arrive at the receiver at different times. This can cause the signal to become distorted, as the different frequency components are no longer in phase with one another. Delay distortion is typically caused by the dispersion of the signal as it travels through the medium.

- **Noise**: Noise is any unwanted signal that interferes with the transmission of the desired signal. There are many sources of noise, including thermal noise, intermodulation noise, and crosstalk. Noise can cause errors in the received signal, which can result in a degradation of the quality of the transmitted information.
