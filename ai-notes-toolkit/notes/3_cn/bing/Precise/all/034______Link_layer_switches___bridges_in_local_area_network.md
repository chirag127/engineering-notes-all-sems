#### Link layer switches & bridges in local area network

- Link layer switches and bridges are networking devices that operate at the data link layer (layer 2) of the OSI model.
- They are used to connect multiple devices on a local area network (LAN) and to filter and forward data packets between them.
- Switches and bridges work by learning the MAC addresses of the devices connected to them and forwarding data packets to the appropriate destination based on the destination MAC address.
- Switches are more advanced than bridges and can have multiple ports, allowing them to connect multiple devices simultaneously.
- Bridges, on the other hand, typically have only two ports and are used to connect two separate LANs or to divide a larger LAN into smaller segments.
- Both switches and bridges can improve network performance by reducing the number of unnecessary data transmissions and collisions.
- A mnemonic to remember the difference between switches and bridges is: **S**witches have multiple **S**ockets, while **B**ridges **B**ridge two LANs.
- An example of a switch in a LAN is a home router that connects multiple devices such as computers, smartphones, and smart home devices to the internet.
- An example of a bridge in a LAN is a wireless bridge that connects two separate LANs, such as two buildings on a campus, using a wireless link.
