#### Categories of networks in computer networks

Computer networks can be categorized based on their size, geographical area, and the number of devices connected. Here are the main categories of computer networks:

1. **Local Area Network (LAN):** A LAN is a network that connects computers and devices within a limited geographical area, such as a home, school, or office building. LANs are typically owned, controlled, and managed by a single person or organization.

2. **Wide Area Network (WAN):** A WAN is a network that covers a large geographical area, such as a city, country, or even the entire world. WANs are typically owned and operated by large corporations or telecommunications companies.

3. **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN):** A MAN is a network that connects multiple LANs within a metropolitan area, such as a city or a large campus. MANs are typically owned and operated by a single entity, such as a government or a large corporation.

4. **Personal Area Network (PAN):** A PAN is a network that connects devices within the close proximity of a single person, such as a smartphone, smartwatch, and wireless headphones. PANs can be either wired or wireless.

5. **Storage Area Network (SAN):** A SAN is a network that provides access to consolidated, block-level data storage. SANs are primarily used to enhance storage devices' accessibility to servers so that the devices appear as locally attached devices to the operating system.

6. **Enterprise Private Network (EPN):** An EPN is a network that is built, owned, and operated by an enterprise, and is used to securely connect various company locations, such as branch offices and data centers.

7. **Virtual Private Network (VPN):** A VPN is a network that uses a public network, such as the internet, to provide remote users with secure access to their organization's network.

A helpful mnemonic to remember these categories is **L**ucy **W**ants **M**ore **P**ancakes **S**o **E**veryone **V**olunteers.

Each of these categories of networks has its own advantages, disadvantages, and applications. For example, LANs are typically faster and more secure than WANs, but WANs can cover a much larger geographical area. SANs are used to improve storage performance and availability, while VPNs are used to provide secure remote access to a network. It is important to understand the differences between these categories when designing and implementing a computer network.
