#### Transmission media in Computer Networks

Transmission media refers to the physical pathways that carry data signals between devices in a computer network. There are two main types of transmission media: guided and unguided.

1. Guided media: Guided media, also known as wired or bounded transmission media, use a physical conductor to transmit data signals. Examples of guided media include:
    - Twisted pair cable: Consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce interference.
    - Coaxial cable: Consists of a central copper conductor surrounded by an insulating layer, a braided shield, and an outer insulating layer.
    - Fiber optic cable: Uses light to transmit data signals through a glass or plastic core surrounded by a cladding layer.

2. Unguided media: Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded transmission media, use air or space as the transmission medium. Examples of unguided media include:
    - Radio waves: Electromagnetic waves that can travel through the air and are commonly used for wireless communication.
    - Microwaves: Electromagnetic waves with a higher frequency than radio waves, commonly used for long-distance communication.
    - Infrared: Electromagnetic waves with a higher frequency than microwaves, commonly used for short-range communication.

Each type of transmission media has its own advantages and disadvantages. For example, guided media generally offer higher data rates and are more secure, but are limited by the physical constraints of the conductor. Unguided media offer more flexibility and mobility, but are more susceptible to interference and have lower data rates.

Mnemonics: 
- Guided media can be remembered as "Twisted Coaxial Fiber" (TCF).
- Unguided media can be remembered as "Radio Microwave Infrared" (RMI).
