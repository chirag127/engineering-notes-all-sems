#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks

Signal transmission is the process of transmitting data from one point to another in a computer network. Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted over a communication channel.

1. **Analog and Digital Signals**: Signals can be either analog or digital. Analog signals are continuous and can take on any value within a range, while digital signals are discrete and can only take on a finite number of values.

2. **Modulation**: Modulation is the process of varying one or more properties of a carrier signal, such as its amplitude, frequency, or phase, in order to encode information onto it. This is commonly used in analog transmission.

3. **Encoding Schemes**: There are several encoding schemes used in digital transmission, including Non-Return-to-Zero (NRZ), Manchester, and 4B/5B. These schemes define how the digital data is represented as a sequence of voltage levels or light pulses.

4. **Error Detection and Correction**: Transmission errors can occur due to noise or interference on the communication channel. Error detection and correction techniques, such as parity checking and cyclic redundancy check (CRC), can be used to detect and correct these errors.

5. **Multiplexing**: Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple signals into a single signal for transmission over a shared communication channel. This allows multiple users to share the same channel, increasing the efficiency of the network.

A mnemonic to remember the OSI model layers is **"All People Seem To Need Data Processing"** which stands for Application, Presentation, Session, Transport, Network, Data Link, and Physical.
