#### LAN standards in local area network

A local area network (LAN) is a computer network that interconnects computers within a limited area such as a residence, school, laboratory, university campus or office building. LAN standards define the protocols and technologies used to create and maintain a LAN.

Some of the most common LAN standards include:

1. **Ethernet (IEEE 802.3):** Ethernet is the most widely used LAN technology. It uses a bus or star topology and supports data transfer rates of 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, and 10 Gbps.

2. **Token Ring (IEEE 802.5):** Token Ring is a LAN technology that uses a ring topology. It supports data transfer rates of 4 Mbps and 16 Mbps.

3. **Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI):** FDDI is a LAN technology that uses a dual-ring topology and supports data transfer rates of 100 Mbps.

4. **Wireless LAN (IEEE 802.11):** Wireless LAN, also known as Wi-Fi, is a LAN technology that uses radio waves to transmit data. It supports data transfer rates of up to 1.3 Gbps.

Each of these standards has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of which standard to use depends on the specific needs and requirements of the network.

A mnemonic to remember the LAN standards is **"E.T. Found Wi-Fi"** which stands for **Ethernet, Token Ring, FDDI, and Wireless LAN**.
