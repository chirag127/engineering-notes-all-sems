### Connection management in transport layer

- Connection management is the process of establishing, maintaining, and terminating a logical connection between two or more hosts in a network.
- Connection management is performed by the transport layer protocols, such as TCP and UDP, to provide reliable or unreliable end-to-end communication services to the application layer.
- Connection management involves three phases: connection establishment, data transfer, and connection termination.
- Connection establishment is the phase where the hosts agree on the parameters and rules for the data transfer, such as the port numbers, sequence numbers, window sizes, etc. Connection establishment can be active or passive, depending on whether the host initiates or waits for a connection request.
- Data transfer is the phase where the hosts exchange data packets according to the agreed parameters and rules. Data transfer can be reliable or unreliable, depending on whether the transport layer protocol guarantees the delivery, order, and integrity of the data packets. Reliable data transfer protocols, such as TCP, use mechanisms such as acknowledgments, retransmissions, flow control, and congestion control to ensure the quality of service. Unreliable data transfer protocols, such as UDP, do not use such mechanisms and leave the error handling and recovery to the application layer.
- Connection termination is the phase where the hosts gracefully or abruptly end the logical connection and release the resources allocated for the data transfer. Connection termination can be initiated by either host or by a network failure. Connection termination can be symmetric or asymmetric, depending on whether the hosts exchange messages to confirm the end of the connection or not.

- A mnemonic to remember the three phases of connection management is: **E**stablish, **T**ransfer, **T**erminate (**ETT**).
- A learning trick to understand the difference between reliable and unreliable data transfer protocols is to compare them with postal services. Reliable data transfer protocols are like registered mail, where the sender gets a receipt when the mail is delivered, and can track the status of the mail along the way. Unreliable data transfer protocols are like ordinary mail, where the sender does not get any confirmation or feedback about the delivery of the mail, and has no way of knowing if the mail was lost, delayed, or damaged.
