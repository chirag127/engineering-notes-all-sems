#### Categories of networks in computer networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications. Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the size, the topology, the transmission medium, and the organizational intent. Here are some of the common categories of networks in computer networks:

- **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: A PAN is the smallest and simplest type of network. It connects devices within the range of an individual, such as a smartphone, a laptop, a printer, or a smartwatch. A PAN is usually no larger than 10 meters and can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Bluetooth, USB, or infrared. A PAN can enable personal communication, data synchronization, and device control. A mnemonic to remember PAN is **P**ersonal **A**ccess to **N**earby devices.

- **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited physical area, such as a home, an office, a school, or a laboratory. A LAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or fiber optics. A LAN can enable fast and reliable data transfer, resource sharing, and network communication. A mnemonic to remember LAN is **L**imited **A**rea **N**etworking.

- **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices across a large geographical area, such as a city, a country, or the world. A WAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as telephone lines, satellite links, or cellular networks. A WAN can enable long-distance communication, data exchange, and internet access. A mnemonic to remember WAN is **W**orldwide **A**rea **N**etworking.

- **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A MAN is a network that connects devices within a metropolitan area, such as a city or a town. A MAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as cable, DSL, or WiMAX. A MAN can enable high-speed data transfer, internet access, and public services. A mnemonic to remember MAN is **M**etropolitan **A**rea **N**etworking.

- **Campus Area Network (CAN)**: A CAN is a network that connects devices within a campus, such as a university, a hospital, or a military base. A CAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or fiber optics. A CAN can enable data sharing, network communication, and security. A mnemonic to remember CAN is **C**ampus **A**rea **N**etworking.

- **Storage Area Network (SAN)**: A SAN is a network that connects storage devices, such as hard disks, tape drives, or optical disks. A SAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as SCSI, Fibre Channel, or iSCSI. A SAN can enable high-performance data storage, backup, and recovery. A mnemonic to remember SAN is **S**torage **A**rea **N**etworking.

- **Virtual Private Network (VPN)**: A VPN is a network that creates a secure and encrypted connection between devices over a public network, such as the internet. A VPN can use various protocols, such as IPsec, SSL, or PPTP. A VPN can enable remote access, data protection, and anonymity. A mnemonic to remember VPN is **V**irtual **P**rivate **N**etworking.
