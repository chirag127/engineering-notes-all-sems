#### Link layer switches & bridges in local area network

- Link layer switches and bridges are network devices that operate at the data link layer (layer 2) of the OSI model and connect multiple LANs (local area networks) together to form a larger LAN.
- They use MAC addresses to forward Ethernet frames from one device to another device in an Ethernet standard based LAN.
- They store (store-and-forward mode) and then regenerate the frame, which helps to reduce errors and collisions in the network.
- They can also interconnect data link layer domains that have different technologies, such as Ethernet to FDDI, by converting the frame format and adjusting the maximum frame size.
- Link layer switches are more advanced than bridges, as they can have multiple ports and perform filtering, learning, and forwarding functions based on MAC addresses.
- Link layer switches can also support VLANs (virtual LANs), which are logical groups of devices that share the same broadcast domain, regardless of their physical location or connection.
- Link layer switches and bridges can improve the performance, scalability, and security of a LAN.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for link layer switches and bridges are:

- Switches and bridges work at layer 2, which rhymes with "glue". They glue multiple LANs together to form a bigger LAN.
- Switches and bridges use MAC addresses, which are also called physical addresses. They deal with the physical devices in the network, not the logical ones.
- Switches and bridges store and forward frames, which sounds like "store and reward". They reward the network by reducing errors and collisions.
- Switches and bridges can interconnect different technologies, such as Ethernet and FDDI. They are like translators that can speak different languages and convert them to a common one.
