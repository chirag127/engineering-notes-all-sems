#### Network architecture with reference to Computer Networks

- Network architecture is the design of a computer network    .
- It is a framework for the specification of a network's physical components and their functional organization and configuration, its operational principles and procedures, as well as communication protocols used   .
- Network architecture components include hardware, software, transmission media (wired or wireless), network topology, and communications protocols  .
- Based on the network's size and purpose, numerous ways to design network architecture exist .
- Here are the most common and widely used computer network types  :
  - LAN (local area network): A LAN connects computers over a relatively short distance, allowing them to share data, files, printers, and other resources. A LAN typically covers a single building or a group of buildings in a campus. A LAN can be wired or wireless. An example of a LAN is a home network or a school network.
  - WLAN (wireless local area network): A WLAN is just like a LAN but connections between devices on the network are wireless, using radio waves or infrared signals. A WLAN can provide mobility and flexibility to the users. A WLAN typically uses a wireless access point (AP) to connect to a wired network and provide wireless coverage to a certain area. An example of a WLAN is a Wi-Fi network or a hotspot.
  - MAN (metropolitan area network): A MAN connects computers over a larger geographic area than a LAN, such as a city or a town. A MAN can span several kilometers and can interconnect several LANs. A MAN can use various technologies, such as fiber-optic cables, microwave links, or satellite links. An example of a MAN is a cable TV network or a municipal network.
  - WAN (wide area network): A WAN connects computers over a very large geographic area, such as a country or a continent. A WAN can span thousands of kilometers and can interconnect several LANs and MANs. A WAN can use various technologies, such as leased lines, public switched telephone network (PSTN), cellular networks, or the Internet. An example of a WAN is the Internet or a corporate network.
  - PAN (personal area network): A PAN connects devices over a very short distance, such as a few meters. A PAN can provide personal connectivity to the user and can support various devices, such as laptops, smartphones, tablets, printers, scanners, or wearable devices. A PAN can be wired or wireless. An example of a PAN is a Bluetooth network or a USB network.
  - SAN (storage area network): A SAN connects storage devices, such as hard disks, tape drives, or optical drives, to servers or other computers. A SAN can provide high-speed data transfer, high availability, and scalability to the storage devices. A SAN can use various technologies, such as fiber channel, iSCSI, or SCSI. An example of a SAN is a data center network or a backup network.
  - CAN (campus area network): A CAN connects computers and devices within a limited geographic area, such as a university campus or a military base. A CAN can be considered as a larger LAN or a smaller MAN. A CAN can use various technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or ATM. An example of a CAN is a university network or a military network.
  - VPN (virtual private network): A VPN connects computers and devices over a public network, such as the Internet, in a secure and private way. A VPN can provide encryption, authentication, and tunneling to the data traffic. A VPN can use various technologies, such as IPsec, SSL, or PPTP. An example of a VPN is a remote access network or a site-to-site network.

- A mnemonic to remember the network types is **LWMWPSCV** (pronounced as "Lum-Wum-Pus-Cee-Vee"). The first letter of each network type forms the mnemonic. For example, L stands for LAN, W stands for WLAN, M stands for MAN, and so on.
