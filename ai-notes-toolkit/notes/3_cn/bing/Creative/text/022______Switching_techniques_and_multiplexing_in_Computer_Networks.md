#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks

- Switching techniques are methods of connecting multiple devices in a network and enabling them to communicate with each other efficiently.
- There are three main types of switching techniques: circuit switching, message switching, and packet switching.
- Circuit switching is a technique where a dedicated communication path is established between two nodes before data transmission. The path remains active until the communication is terminated. Circuit switching is suitable for real-time applications that require constant bandwidth and low delay, such as voice and video calls .
- Message switching is a technique where the whole message is treated as a data unit and stored and forwarded by intermediate nodes until it reaches the destination. Message switching does not require a dedicated path, but it introduces variable delay and overhead. Message switching is suitable for applications that can tolerate delay and do not require synchronization, such as email and file transfer .
- Packet switching is a technique where the message is broken down into smaller chunks called packets, which are transmitted independently and reassembled at the destination. Packet switching also does not require a dedicated path, but it allows efficient sharing of network resources and dynamic routing. Packet switching is suitable for applications that require high throughput and reliability, such as web browsing and streaming .
- Multiplexing is a technique of combining multiple signals into one signal over a shared medium. Multiplexing allows the utilization of the available bandwidth and reduces the cost of transmission. There are different types of multiplexing, such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), and statistical multiplexing .
- Frequency division multiplexing (FDM) is a technique where the available bandwidth is divided into multiple frequency bands, and each signal is modulated by a different carrier frequency. FDM allows simultaneous transmission of multiple signals without interference, but it requires filters and guard bands to separate the signals. FDM is suitable for analog signals, such as radio and television .
- Time division multiplexing (TDM) is a technique where the available time is divided into multiple time slots, and each signal is assigned a different time slot. TDM allows sequential transmission of multiple signals without interference, but it requires synchronization and buffering to align the signals. TDM is suitable for digital signals, such as telephone and data .
- Statistical multiplexing is a technique where the available bandwidth is dynamically allocated to the signals based on their demand and priority. Statistical multiplexing does not require fixed division of bandwidth or time, but it introduces variable delay and congestion. Statistical multiplexing is suitable for packet switching networks, where the traffic is bursty and unpredictable .
- Switching and multiplexing are complementary techniques that enable efficient and reliable communication in computer networks. Switching connects multiple devices and routes data, while multiplexing shares network resources and reduces cost. Together, they form the basis of modern network architectures and protocols.
