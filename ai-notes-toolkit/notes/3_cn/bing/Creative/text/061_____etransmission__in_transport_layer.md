### etransmission in transport layer

- The transport layer is the fourth layer of the OSI model and the third layer of the TCP/IP model. It provides end-to-end communication services for applications, such as reliable data transfer, flow control, congestion control, and error detection and correction.
- The transport layer uses protocols such as TCP and UDP to segment the data from the application layer into smaller units called segments or datagrams, and to add headers that contain information such as source and destination port numbers, sequence numbers, and checksums.
- The transport layer is responsible for ensuring that the data is transmitted reliably and efficiently across the network. It uses various mechanisms to achieve this, such as:
  - Acknowledgements: The receiver sends back a message to the sender to confirm the receipt of a segment or datagram. This helps the sender to keep track of the progress of the data transfer and to detect any lost or corrupted packets.
  - Retransmission: The sender re-sends a segment or datagram that has not been acknowledged by the receiver within a certain time interval. This helps to recover from packet loss and to ensure that the data is delivered completely and correctly.
  - Flow control: The sender adjusts the rate of data transmission according to the feedback from the receiver and the capacity of the network. This helps to avoid overwhelming the receiver or the network with too much data and to maintain a smooth and efficient data flow.
  - Congestion control: The sender monitors the level of congestion in the network and adapts the data transmission accordingly. This helps to prevent or reduce the congestion and to improve the network performance and quality of service.
  - Error detection and correction: The sender and the receiver use checksums or other methods to verify the integrity of the data and to detect any errors or corruption. If an error is detected, the sender or the receiver can request a retransmission or correct the error using techniques such as parity bits or error-correcting codes.
- The transport layer also provides other functions, such as:
  - Multiplexing and demultiplexing: The transport layer uses port numbers to identify different applications or processes on the same host and to deliver the data to the correct destination. This allows multiple applications or processes to share the same network connection and to communicate simultaneously.
  - Connection-oriented and connectionless services: The transport layer can provide different types of services depending on the protocol used and the requirements of the application. TCP provides a connection-oriented service, which means that it establishes a logical connection between the sender and the receiver before transmitting the data and maintains the connection until the data transfer is complete. UDP provides a connectionless service, which means that it does not establish or maintain any connection and simply sends the data without any guarantee of delivery or reliability.
  - Quality of service: The transport layer can support different levels of quality of service (QoS) for different applications or processes, such as delay, bandwidth, jitter, and reliability. The transport layer can use various methods to achieve QoS, such as priority, reservation, or differentiation.
