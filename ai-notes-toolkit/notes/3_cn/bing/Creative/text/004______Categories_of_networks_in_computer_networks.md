#### Categories of networks in computer networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications. Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the transmission medium, the network size, the topology, and organizational intent. Based on a geographical scale, the different types of networks are:

- **Personal area network (PAN)**: This is the smallest and simplest type of network. PANs connect devices within the range of an individual and are no larger than about 10 meters. Examples of PANs are wireless headphones, Bluetooth speakers, smartwatches, etc. 
- **Local area network (LAN)**: This is the most frequently used network. A LAN is a computer network that connects devices in a limited physical area, such as a home, office, school, or laboratory. LANs use wired or wireless links to transmit data rapidly and reliably. Examples of LANs are Ethernet, Wi-Fi, etc.  
- **Wide area network (WAN)**: This is a computer network that spans a large geographical area, such as a country, continent, or the whole world. WANs use public or private communication channels, such as telephone lines, satellites, or optical fibers, to connect devices that are far apart. Examples of WANs are the Internet, cellular networks, etc. 
- **Metropolitan area network (MAN)**: This is a computer network that covers a large city or a metropolitan area. MANs are usually composed of several LANs that are interconnected by high-speed links, such as fiber optics or microwave. Examples of MANs are cable TV networks, municipal Wi-Fi networks, etc. 
- **Campus area network (CAN)**: This is a computer network that connects devices within a campus, such as a university, college, or corporate campus. CANs are similar to LANs, but they cover a larger area and may have multiple buildings or sites. Examples of CANs are academic networks, research networks, etc. 
- **Storage area network (SAN)**: This is a computer network that connects storage devices, such as hard disks, tape drives, or optical disks, to servers or other devices. SANs provide fast and secure access to large amounts of data, and they are often used for backup, recovery, or data replication. Examples of SANs are Fibre Channel, iSCSI, etc. 
- **Nanoscale network (NAN)**: This is a computer network that enables communication between minuscule sensors and actuators, such as nanomachines, nanodevices, or nanorobots. NANs are used for biomedical, environmental, or industrial applications, and they may use molecular, electromagnetic, or acoustic signals. Examples of NANs are nanonetworks, nanosensors, etc.
