#### Network devices in Computer Networks

- Network devices are hardware components that connect computers and other devices to a network. They enable data transmission and communication among different network nodes.
- Some common network devices are:

  - **Router**: A device that forwards packets between different networks based on their destination IP addresses. Routers can also perform network address translation (NAT), firewall, and routing protocols functions.
  - **Switch**: A device that connects multiple devices on the same network segment and forwards packets based on their destination MAC addresses. Switches can also perform VLAN, QoS, and spanning tree functions.
  - **Hub**: A device that connects multiple devices on the same network segment and broadcasts packets to all connected devices. Hubs are less efficient and secure than switches, as they create more collisions and do not filter traffic.
  - **Bridge**: A device that connects two network segments and forwards packets based on their destination MAC addresses. Bridges can also perform filtering, learning, and flooding functions.
  - **Repeater**: A device that amplifies and regenerates signals on a network segment. Repeaters can extend the range of a network, but they do not filter or forward traffic.
  - **Modem**: A device that modulates and demodulates analog signals into digital signals and vice versa. Modems can enable data transmission over telephone lines, cable lines, or wireless networks.
  - **NIC**: A device that provides a physical interface between a computer and a network. NICs can also perform encoding, decoding, framing, and error detection functions.
  - **Firewall**: A device that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predefined rules. Firewalls can also perform packet filtering, stateful inspection, proxy, and NAT functions.
  - **Access point**: A device that allows wireless devices to connect to a wired network. Access points can also perform encryption, authentication, and roaming functions.
