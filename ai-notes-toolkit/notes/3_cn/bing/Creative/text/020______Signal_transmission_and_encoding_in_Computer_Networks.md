#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks

- Signal transmission is the process of sending and receiving data over a physical medium, such as a wire, a fiber optic cable, or a wireless channel.
- Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted as a signal, such as a voltage level, a light pulse, or a radio wave.
- The purpose of encoding is to ensure reliable and efficient transmission of data, by minimizing errors, interference, and bandwidth requirements.
- There are different types of encoding, depending on the nature of the data and the signal. Some common types are:

  - Digital-to-digital encoding: This is the conversion of binary data (0s and 1s) into a digital signal, such as a series of voltage pulses. This is also known as line coding. Examples of digital-to-digital encoding schemes are NRZ, Manchester, and 4B/5B.
  - Analog-to-digital encoding: This is the conversion of analog data, such as voice or video, into a digital signal, such as a sequence of bits. This involves sampling, quantization, and encoding. Examples of analog-to-digital encoding schemes are PCM, DM, and DPCM.
  - Digital-to-analog encoding: This is the conversion of digital data into an analog signal, such as a sine wave. This involves modulation, which is the process of varying one or more properties of the analog signal, such as amplitude, frequency, or phase, according to the digital data. Examples of digital-to-analog encoding schemes are ASK, FSK, and PSK.
  - Analog-to-analog encoding: This is the conversion of analog data into another analog signal, such as a different frequency band. This also involves modulation, which is the process of varying one or more properties of the analog signal, such as amplitude, frequency, or phase, according to the analog data. Examples of analog-to-analog encoding schemes are AM, FM, and PM.
