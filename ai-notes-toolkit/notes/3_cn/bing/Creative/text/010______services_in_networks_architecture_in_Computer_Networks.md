#### Services in Networks Architecture in Computer Networks

- Services in networks architecture are the **functions** or **operations** that each layer of the network model provides to the layer above it or below it.
- Services are **defined** by a set of **primitives** or **commands** that specify the **parameters** and **results** of the service.
- Services are **implemented** by using **protocols** or **rules** that govern the **interaction** and **communication** between the entities at each layer.
- Services can be **classified** into two types: **connection-oriented** and **connectionless**.
  - Connection-oriented services require the establishment of a **logical connection** between the sender and the receiver before any data can be transferred. The connection is **terminated** after the data transfer is complete. An example of a connection-oriented service is **TCP**.
  - Connectionless services do not require any prior **arrangement** or **coordination** between the sender and the receiver. The sender can send data to the receiver at any time, without any **acknowledgment** or **confirmation**. An example of a connectionless service is **UDP**.
- Services can also be **categorized** into two levels: **peer-to-peer** and **end-to-end**.
  - Peer-to-peer services are the services that are **exchanged** between the **same layer** of different network devices. For example, the **data link layer** of one device can exchange **frames** with the data link layer of another device using a peer-to-peer service.
  - End-to-end services are the services that are **delivered** from the **source** to the **destination** across multiple network devices and layers. For example, the **application layer** of one device can send **messages** to the application layer of another device using an end-to-end service.
- Some examples of services in networks architecture are:
  - **DHCP** (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol): A service that **assigns** IP addresses and other network configuration parameters to devices on a network.
  - **DNS** (Domain Name System): A service that **translates** domain names into IP addresses and vice versa.
  - **FTP** (File Transfer Protocol): A service that **transfers** files between devices on a network.
  - **HTTP** (Hypertext Transfer Protocol): A service that **requests** and **delivers** web pages and other resources on the internet.
  - **SMTP** (Simple Mail Transfer Protocol): A service that **sends** and **receives** email messages on a network.
