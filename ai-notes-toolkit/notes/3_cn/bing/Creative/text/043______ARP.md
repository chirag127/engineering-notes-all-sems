#### ARP
- ARP stands for Address Resolution Protocol   .
- It is a communication protocol used for discovering the link layer address, such as a MAC address, associated with a given internet layer address, typically an IPv4 address   .
- It is a critical function in the Internet protocol suite, as it enables devices to communicate within a local area network (LAN)   .
- ARP was defined in 1982 by RFC 826, which is Internet Standard STD 37.
- ARP works by sending broadcast messages to all devices in the LAN, asking for the MAC address of the device that has a specific IP address.
- The device that has the requested IP address replies with its MAC address, and the sender updates its ARP cache with the mapping of the IP address and the MAC address.
- ARP cache is a table that stores the mappings of IP addresses and MAC addresses for a certain period of time.
- ARP can be divided into two types: static ARP and dynamic ARP.
- Static ARP is when the mappings of IP addresses and MAC addresses are manually entered and do not change.
- Dynamic ARP is when the mappings of IP addresses and MAC addresses are automatically learned and updated by ARP messages.
- ARP can also be classified into four types based on the types of addresses involved: ARP, RARP, InARP and Proxy ARP.
- ARP is when the sender knows the IP address of the destination and wants to find its MAC address.
- RARP is when the sender knows the MAC address of the destination and wants to find its IP address.
- InARP is when the sender knows the MAC address of a device on another network and wants to find its IP address.
- Proxy ARP is when a device acts as an intermediary and responds to ARP requests on behalf of another device.
