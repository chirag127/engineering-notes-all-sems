#### Categories of networks in computer networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications. Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the size, the topology, the transmission medium, and the organizational intent. Based on the size, the most common types of computer networks are:

- **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: A PAN is the smallest and simplest type of network. It connects devices within the range of an individual, such as a smartphone, a laptop, a printer, or a smartwatch. A PAN is usually no larger than 10 meters. A PAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Bluetooth, USB, or infrared. A PAN can enable personal data transfer, device synchronization, or internet access.   

- **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited physical area, such as a home, an office, a school, or a laboratory. A LAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or fiber optics. A LAN can enable data sharing, resource sharing, or internet access. A LAN can have multiple subnetworks, such as VLANs or WLANs. A LAN can have different topologies, such as bus, ring, star, or mesh.    

- **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A MAN is a network that connects devices within a large geographical area, such as a city, a town, or a campus. A MAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as cable, DSL, or WiMAX. A MAN can enable data sharing, resource sharing, or internet access. A MAN can have multiple LANs or WANs connected to it. A MAN can have different topologies, such as ring, star, or tree.  

- **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices across a large geographical area, such as a country, a continent, or the world. A WAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as telephone lines, satellite, or cellular. A WAN can enable data sharing, resource sharing, or internet access. A WAN can have multiple LANs, MANs, or other WANs connected to it. A WAN can have different topologies, such as point-to-point, point-to-multipoint, or mesh.   

- **Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)**: A WLAN is a network that connects devices using wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or infrared. A WLAN can be a type of LAN, MAN, or WAN, depending on the size and the coverage. A WLAN can enable data sharing, resource sharing, or internet access. A WLAN can have different topologies, such as infrastructure, ad hoc, or mesh.  

- **Storage Area Network (SAN)**: A SAN is a network that connects storage devices, such as hard disks, tape drives, or optical disks. A SAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as fiber channel, iSCSI, or FCoE. A SAN can enable data storage, data backup, data recovery, or data replication. A SAN can have different topologies, such as point-to-point, arbitrated loop, or switched fabric.  

- **Virtual Private Network (VPN)**: A VPN is a network that connects devices using encryption and tunneling techniques. A VPN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as IPsec, SSL, or PPTP. A VPN can enable secure data transfer, remote access, or network extension. A VPN can have different topologies, such as site-to-site, client-to-server, or peer-to-peer.
