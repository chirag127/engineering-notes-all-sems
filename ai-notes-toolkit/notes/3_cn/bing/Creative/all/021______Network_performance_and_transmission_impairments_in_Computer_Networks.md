#### Network performance and transmission impairments in Computer Networks

- Network performance is the measure of how well a network can deliver data and services to its users. It can be evaluated by various metrics, such as throughput, delay, jitter, packet loss, availability, reliability, etc.
- Transmission impairments are the factors that degrade the quality of a signal as it travels through a transmission medium. They can cause errors, distortion, or loss of data in the communication process. Some common types of transmission impairments are:
  - Attenuation: The gradual weakening of a signal as it travels over a distance. It can be caused by the resistance of the medium, the absorption of the signal by the medium, or the leakage of the signal from the medium. Attenuation can be measured in decibels (dB), which indicate the ratio of the input power to the output power of a signal. A negative dB value means that the signal has lost power, while a positive dB value means that the signal has gained power (e.g., by amplification).
  - Distortion: The alteration of the shape or form of a signal as it travels through a medium. It can be caused by the non-linear characteristics of the medium, the interference of other signals, or the variation of the propagation speed of the signal in the medium. Distortion can affect the amplitude, phase, or frequency of a signal, and can result in errors or misinterpretation of the data. Distortion can be classified into two types: linear distortion and nonlinear distortion. Linear distortion occurs when the amplitude and phase of the signal are affected uniformly across the frequency spectrum, while nonlinear distortion occurs when the amplitude and phase of the signal are affected differently at different frequencies.
  - Noise: The unwanted or random signals that are added to the original signal as it travels through a medium. It can be caused by the thermal agitation of electrons in the medium, the electromagnetic interference from other sources, or the impulse or spike signals from external events. Noise can reduce the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), which is the ratio of the power of the signal to the power of the noise. A low SNR means that the signal is hard to distinguish from the noise, while a high SNR means that the signal is clear and easy to detect. Noise can be classified into three types: thermal noise, crosstalk, and impulse noise. Thermal noise is the random noise generated by the thermal motion of electrons in the medium, and it is proportional to the temperature and the bandwidth of the signal. Crosstalk is the noise caused by the coupling of signals from adjacent wires or channels, and it can result in interference or leakage of data. Impulse noise is the noise caused by sudden and short-duration changes in the voltage or current of the signal, and it can result from lightning, switching, or other external events.

- A mnemonic to remember the types of transmission impairments is: **A**ttenuation, **D**istortion, and **N**oise (ADN).
- A learning trick to understand the concept of attenuation is to think of a flashlight. As the flashlight moves away from a wall, the light beam becomes weaker and dimmer. This is similar to how a signal loses power as it travels over a distance.
- A learning trick to understand the concept of distortion is to think of a music player. As the music player plays a song, the sound quality may vary depending on the volume, the equalizer settings, or the interference from other devices. This is similar to how a signal changes shape or form as it travels through a medium.
- A learning trick to understand the concept of noise is to think of a radio. As the radio tunes to a station, the sound may be clear or fuzzy depending on the signal strength, the frequency, or the presence of other stations. This is similar to how a signal is affected by unwanted or random signals as it travels through a medium.
