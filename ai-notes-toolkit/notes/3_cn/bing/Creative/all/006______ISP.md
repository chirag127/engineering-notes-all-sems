#### ISP

- ISP stands for **Internet Service Provider** .
- An ISP is a company that provides **access to the internet** to both personal and business customers .
- ISPs make it possible for their customers to **surf the web, shop online, conduct business, and connect with family and friends**—all for a fee.
- ISPs may also provide other services such as **email services, domain registration, web hosting, and browser services** .
- ISPs can host websites for businesses and can also build the websites themselves.
- ISPs are classified into different types based on the **technology** they use to deliver internet access, such as **dial-up, DSL, cable, fiber, satellite, wireless, mobile, etc**.
- ISPs are also classified into different tiers based on the **scope** of their network and the **number** of customers they serve, such as **Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3, etc**.
- Tier 1 ISPs are the **largest** and have **global** coverage. They have **direct access** to the internet backbone and do not pay any fees to other ISPs. Examples of Tier 1 ISPs are **AT&T, Verizon, CenturyLink, etc**.
- Tier 2 ISPs are **smaller** and have **regional** coverage. They have **indirect access** to the internet backbone and pay fees to Tier 1 ISPs. Examples of Tier 2 ISPs are **Comcast, Cox, Charter, etc**.
- Tier 3 ISPs are the **smallest** and have **local** coverage. They have **no access** to the internet backbone and pay fees to Tier 1 and Tier 2 ISPs. Examples of Tier 3 ISPs are **local cable companies, wireless providers, etc**.
- A mnemonic to remember the types of ISPs is **Dial-up, DSL, Cable, Fiber, Satellite, Wireless, Mobile**. A possible sentence is **Did David Call Fiona's Sister Without Mentioning**.
- A mnemonic to remember the tiers of ISPs is **Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3**. A possible sentence is **The Internet's Top Three**.
