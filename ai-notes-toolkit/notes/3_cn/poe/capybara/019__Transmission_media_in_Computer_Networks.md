#### Transmission Media in Computer Networks

Transmission media refers to the physical medium used to transmit data between devices in a computer network. There are several types of transmission media used in computer networks, including:

- **Twisted Pair Cable**: This type of cable contains two or more twisted pairs of copper wires, which are used to transmit data. Twisted pair cable is commonly used in Ethernet networks, and it is relatively inexpensive and easy to install.

- **Coaxial Cable**: This type of cable consists of a central conductor, surrounded by insulation, and then a braided or foil shield. Coaxial cable is commonly used in cable TV networks, and it can also be used for high-speed data transmission.

- **Fiber Optic Cable**: This type of cable uses glass or plastic fibers to transmit data using light waves. Fiber optic cable is commonly used for long-distance data transmission, as it can transmit data over longer distances than other types of cable.

- **Wireless Transmission**: This type of transmission uses radio waves or microwaves to transmit data between devices without the need for physical cables. Wireless transmission is commonly used in Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth devices, and cellular networks.

When selecting a transmission medium for a computer network, it is important to consider factors such as the distance between devices, the amount of data being transmitted, and the cost of the transmission medium. Each type of transmission medium has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of medium will depend on the specific needs of the network.
