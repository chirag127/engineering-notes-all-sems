#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks.

1. Signal transmission refers to the process of sending electrical or electromagnetic signals over a physical medium in a computer network. The signals are converted into a form suitable for transmission over the medium using a signal encoding technique.
2. Some common signal transmission media in computer networks are:
- Twisted pair cables - Pairs of copper wires twisted together to reduce interference. Used for telephone and Ethernet networks.
- Coaxial cables - A single copper wire surrounded by insulation and a grounded shield. Used for cable internet and older Ethernet networks.
- Fiber optic cables - Thin strands of glass that transmit light signals. Used for high-speed internet and telecommunication networks.
- Wireless signals - Radio frequency waves transmitted through the air. Used for Wi-Fi and cellular data networks.
3. Common signal encoding techniques include:
- Amplitude modulation - Variation in amplitude of a carrier signal based on the information signal. Used in radio transmission.
- Frequency modulation - Variation in frequency of a carrier signal based on the information signal. Also used in radio transmission.
- Pulse-code modulation - Information signal is converted into a coded pulse train. Used in telephone networks.
- Ethernet framing - Data is packaged into Ethernet frames for transmission over twisted pair or coaxial cables.
