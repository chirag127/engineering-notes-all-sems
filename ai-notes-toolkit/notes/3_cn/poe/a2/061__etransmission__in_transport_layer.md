### Etransmission in Transport Layer

1. Etransmission refers to the process of resending the packets that get corrupted or lost during transmission. It is done by the transport layer to provide reliable data transmission between two hosts.
2. The two transport layer protocols TCP and UDP handle etransmission differently:
- TCP provides reliable data transmission with etransmission and ordering of packets. It uses sequence numbers and acknowledgments to identify and resend lost or corrupted packets.
- UDP provides fast but unreliable data transmission without etransmission of packets. It is used for applications that require speed and can tolerate some loss of packets like video streaming.
3. The etransmission process adds some overhead in the form of bandwidth usage and delay. However, for applications where reliability is important, the benefits of etransmission outweigh these costs. The etransmission process continues until all packets are received correctly at the destination.
4. In summary, etransmission is an important mechanism used by the transport layer to provide reliable data transmission between two hosts. It helps recover from packet loss or corruption for uninterrupted data delivery.
