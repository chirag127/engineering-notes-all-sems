#### Channel allocation in medium access control

1. Fixed channel allocation: In this, channels are allocated to wireless devices permanently. For example, one channel can be allocated to Bluetooth devices and another channel to Wi-Fi devices. There is no sharing of channels between devices. Though there is no interference, this method is not spectrum efficient.

2. Dynamic channel allocation: In this, channels are allocated to devices on temporary and short-term basis. The channels are reused by different wireless devices based on traffic and availability. This improves spectrum efficiency but wireless devices have to continuously sense the channel and compete for channel access which can lead to collisions and interference. Various medium access control protocols are designed to handle this.

3. Cognitive radio based channel allocation: Cognitive radios can sense spectrum holes and reuse them without interfering with licensed users. The secondary users can utilize the spectrum opportunistically while avoiding interference to primary users. This improves spectrum efficiency but requires cognitive radios with additional sensing capabilities and database access to identify occupied channels. Various medium access control mechanisms are required to facilitate such opportunistic spectrum access.

The above points cover the key types of channel allocation approaches in medium access control for wireless networks.
