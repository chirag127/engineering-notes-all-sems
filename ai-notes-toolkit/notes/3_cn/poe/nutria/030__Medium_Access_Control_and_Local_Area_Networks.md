
#### Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

* Medium Access Control (MAC) is a protocol used in Local Area Networks (LANs) to ensure that all devices connected to the network have equal access to the network's resources.
* MAC protocols are responsible for controlling who has access to the network, when they have access, and what type of data they can access.
* The most common MAC protocols are Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA) and Demand Priority (DP).
* CSMA is a contention-based protocol, which means that all devices connected to the network contend for access to the network's resources.
* DP is a token-based protocol, which means that a token is passed from one device to another in order to grant access to the network's resources.
* MAC protocols are also responsible for ensuring that data is transmitted reliably and securely.
* MAC protocols are implemented at the Data Link Layer of the OSI model.
