
#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks

1. Switching techniques are used in computer networks to enable data communication between two or more nodes. These techniques can be broadly classified into circuit switching and packet switching. 

2. Circuit switching is a technique in which a dedicated physical connection is established between two nodes for the duration of the communication session. It is used in telephone networks where a dedicated circuit is established between two nodes for the duration of the call. 

3. Packet switching is a technique in which data is broken into small packets and sent to the destination node. Each packet contains the source and destination address, and the data is reassembled at the destination node. This technique is used in most modern computer networks and the Internet. 

4. Multiplexing is a technique in which multiple signals are combined into one signal for transmission. It is used to increase the efficiency of data transmission by combining multiple signals into one. It is used in both circuit switching and packet switching networks. 

5. Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) is a technique in which multiple signals are combined into one signal by dividing the time into discrete intervals. Each signal is allocated a time slot in the multiplexed signal. 

6. Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM) is a technique in which multiple signals are combined into one signal by dividing the frequency spectrum into multiple channels. Each signal is allocated a frequency channel in the multiplexed signal. 

7. Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) is a technique in which multiple signals are combined into one signal by dividing the wavelength spectrum into multiple channels. Each signal is allocated a wavelength channel in the multiplexed signal.
