

#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks

1. Signal transmission is the process of sending data from one point to another, usually via a communication medium such as a wire or radio waves.
2. Encoding is the process of transforming data into a format that can be sent and received over the network.
3. Digital signals are the most commonly used type of signal in computer networks. These signals are usually binary, meaning they consist of two states, 1 and 0.
4. Analog signals are also used in computer networks, but they require more complex hardware and are less reliable than digital signals.
5. Modulation is the process of changing the properties of a signal in order to make it more suitable for transmission over a network.
6. Error detection and correction is the process of detecting and correcting errors in transmitted data. This is done by adding extra bits of data to the signal, which can then be used to detect and correct any errors that may occur during transmission.
7. Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple signals into one signal for transmission over a network. This allows multiple users to share a single communication channel.
8. Encryption is the process of transforming data into a form that is unreadable to anyone except the intended recipient. This is done to protect the data from unauthorized access.
9. Compression is the process of reducing the size of data for more efficient transmission over a network. This is done by removing redundant data or by using algorithms to reduce the size of the data.
