### The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model

The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model, also known as the Job Characteristics Model, was developed by organizational psychologists J. Richard Hackman and Greg Oldham. It is a normative approach to job enrichment, which specifies five core job dimensions that will lead to critical psychological states in the individual employee.

The five core job dimensions identified are:
1. Autonomy
2. Feedback
3. Skill variety
4. Task identity
5. Task significance

The model suggests that these five core job dimensions affect certain personal and work-related outcomes, including job satisfaction. The model is based on the idea that the key to maintaining motivation is in the job itself. Mundane tasks reduce motivation and productivity, while varied tasks improve them.

The model considers only job-related factors that can be changed to increase positive work behaviors and outcomes, such as higher performance and less absenteeism. It does not look at how relationships and other interpersonal issues affect job satisfaction and performance.
