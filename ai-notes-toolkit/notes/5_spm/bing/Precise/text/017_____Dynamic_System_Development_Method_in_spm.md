### Dynamic System Development Method (DSDM) in Software Project Management (SPM)

Dynamic System Development Method (DSDM) is an agile project delivery framework, initially used as a software development method. It is an iterative and incremental approach that emphasizes continuous user involvement. Here are some key points about DSDM:

1. DSDM is based on eight principles that focus on delivering projects on time and within budget while meeting the changing needs of the business.
2. The principles include active user involvement, empowered teams, frequent delivery of products, integrated testing, and stakeholder collaboration.
3. DSDM uses a five-phase project lifecycle: Feasibility, Business Study, Functional Model Iteration, Design and Build Iteration, and Implementation.
4. Each phase is time-boxed to ensure that the project is delivered on time.
5. DSDM encourages active user involvement throughout the project, with users working closely with the development team to define and prioritize requirements.
6. The framework also emphasizes the importance of empowered teams, where team members are given the authority to make decisions and take responsibility for delivering the project.
7. DSDM promotes frequent delivery of products, with the aim of delivering usable software to the business as early and as often as possible.
8. Integrated testing is also a key aspect of DSDM, with testing being carried out throughout the project lifecycle to ensure that the final product meets the business needs.

Overall, DSDM is a flexible and adaptable approach to project delivery that can be used in a variety of industries and project types. It is particularly well-suited to projects that require a high level of user involvement and where requirements are likely to change during the project lifecycle.
