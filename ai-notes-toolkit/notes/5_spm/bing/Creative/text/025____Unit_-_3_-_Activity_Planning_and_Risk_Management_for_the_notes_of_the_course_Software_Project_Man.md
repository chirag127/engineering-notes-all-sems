## Unit - 3 - Activity Planning and Risk Management

- Activity planning is the process of defining and sequencing the tasks, milestones, and deliverables of a software project.
- Risk management is the process of identifying, analyzing, and mitigating the potential threats and uncertainties that may affect the project's objectives, quality, schedule, or budget.
- The main goals of activity planning and risk management are to ensure that the project is completed on time, within budget, and meets the stakeholder's expectations and requirements.
- The main steps of activity planning and risk management are:

  - Plan risk management: Define how to perform risk management activities for the project, such as the roles and responsibilities, the tools and techniques, the risk categories and sources, and the risk tolerance and thresholds.
  - Identify risks: Identify and document the possible events or conditions that could have a positive or negative impact on the project's objectives, scope, quality, schedule, or budget.
  - Analyze risks: Assess the probability and impact of each identified risk, and prioritize them according to their severity and urgency.
  - Evaluate risks: Determine the best response strategy for each risk, such as avoiding, accepting, reducing, or transferring the risk, and allocate the necessary resources and contingency plans.
  - Treat risks: Implement the chosen risk response strategy, and monitor and control the risk throughout the project lifecycle, by updating the risk register, reporting the risk status, and taking corrective actions if needed.
  - Plan activities: Define and document the work breakdown structure (WBS), which is a hierarchical decomposition of the project scope into manageable tasks, deliverables, and milestones.
  - Sequence activities: Determine the logical order and dependencies among the activities, and create a network diagram that shows the critical path and the float of each activity.
  - Estimate resources: Estimate the type, quantity, and availability of the resources (such as human, material, equipment, and financial) needed for each activity.
  - Estimate duration: Estimate the time required to complete each activity, based on the resource estimates, the activity complexity, and the historical data.
  - Develop schedule: Develop a project schedule that shows the start and finish dates of each activity, the milestones, and the project deadline, and adjust the schedule according to the project constraints and risks.
  - Develop budget: Develop a project budget that shows the cost of each activity, the total project cost, and the contingency reserves, and control the budget according to the project changes and risks.

- Some of the benefits of activity planning and risk management are:

  - They help to align the project scope, schedule, and cost with the stakeholder's expectations and requirements.
  - They help to identify and mitigate the potential issues and problems that could affect the project's performance and quality.
  - They help to optimize the use of the project resources and reduce the project waste and rework.
  - They help to increase the project team's confidence and commitment, and improve the project communication and collaboration.
  - They help to enhance the project's value and success.
