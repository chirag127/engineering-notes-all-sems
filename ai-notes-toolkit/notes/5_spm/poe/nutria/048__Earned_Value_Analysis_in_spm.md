
### Earned Value Analysis in spm

1. Earned Value Analysis (EVA) is a project management technique used to measure and compare the progress of a project against its original plan. 
2. It is used to measure the performance of a project in terms of cost, schedule, and scope. 
3. EVA is based on the principle that the value of a project should be measured in terms of the work that has been completed, not just the amount of money that has been spent. 
4. EVA combines three elements of a project: the planned value (PV), the actual cost (AC), and the earned value (EV). 
5. The PV is the budgeted cost of the work that has been planned to be completed by a certain time. 
6. The AC is the actual cost of the work that has been completed. 
7. The EV is the budgeted cost of the work that has been completed by a certain time. 
8. The difference between the PV and the EV is called the cost variance (CV). 
9. The difference between the PV and the AC is called the schedule variance (SV). 
10. The ratio of the EV to the PV is called the cost performance index (CPI). 
11. The ratio of the EV to the AC is called the schedule performance index (SPI). 
12. EVA can be used to identify problems in a project, such as cost overruns or schedule delays, and to analyze the causes of those problems. 
13. EVA can also be used to track the progress of a project and to make decisions about how to adjust the project plan in order to achieve the desired results.
