
### The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model in SPM

The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model is a model developed by John Oldham and J. Richard Hackman to measure job characteristics. This model is used to determine the impact of job characteristics on job satisfaction and performance.

The model consists of five core job characteristics: skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, and feedback.

1. **Skill variety** refers to the degree to which a job requires the employee to use a variety of different skills and knowledge.

2. **Task identity** is the degree to which a job requires the employee to complete a whole and identifiable piece of work.

3. **Task significance** is the degree to which a job has a substantial impact on the lives of other people.

4. **Autonomy** is the degree to which a job provides the employee with freedom, independence, and discretion in carrying out the job.

5. **Feedback** is the degree to which a job provides the employee with information about the effectiveness of their performance.

The model suggests that these five job characteristics can lead to three psychological states which, in turn, can lead to job satisfaction and improved performance. The three psychological states are: experienced meaningfulness of the work, experienced responsibility for the work outcomes, and knowledge of the actual results of the work activities.
