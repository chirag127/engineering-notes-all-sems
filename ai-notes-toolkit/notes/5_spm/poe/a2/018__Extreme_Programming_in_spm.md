### Extreme Programming in spm

1. Extreme Programming (XP) is an agile software development methodology which is intended to improve software quality and responsiveness to changing customer requirements.
2. The key practices of XP include:
 - Short iterations: Releases are made early and often, in 1-4 week cycles.
 - Small releases: The application is broken down into small pieces that can be released individually.
 - Simple design: The system is kept as simple as possible to meet customer needs. Extra features are not added until they are needed.
 - Test-driven development: Unit tests are written before the code they are testing. The tests help drive the design.
 - Refactoring: As the system evolves, the code is restructured to remove duplication and keep the design simple.
 - Pair programming: Programmers work in pairs, reviewing each other's work.
 - Collective ownership: The whole team is responsible for the whole system, not just specific modules or code.
 - Continuous integration: The system is integrated and built frequently, at least daily.
 - On-site customer: A customer representative is part of the development team and available on-site.
 - Sustainable pace: Developers work at a pace that can be sustained long term to reduce burnout.

The above points outline the key practices of Extreme Programming which aims to produce high quality software through iterative development and close customer involvement. The methodology focuses on maintaining a simple design, constant testing and refactoring of code as well as teamwork and sustainable pace for developers.
