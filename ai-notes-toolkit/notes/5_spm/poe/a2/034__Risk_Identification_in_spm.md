### Risk Identification in spm

1. Determine objectives and constraints
- Clearly define the project objectives and constraints. Unclear objectives and constraints can lead to missed risks or inappropriate responses.

2. Identify sources of risk
- Consider sources such as external events, dependencies on third parties, ground conditions, novel technical issues, estimation errors, etc. Brainstorming can be useful here.

3. Assess impact and likelihood
- Assess the potential impact and likelihood of each risk materializing. Consider both positive and negative impacts. The product of impact and likelihood can be used to prioritize risks.

4. Prioritize responses
- Prioritize identified risks for response planning based on a combination of likelihood and impact. Higher priority should be given to risks with higher potential impact and likelihood.

5. Develop risk responses
- For major risks, determine appropriate responses such as:
- Avoid: Eliminate the threat or protect the project objective from its impact.
- Mitigate: Reduce the likelihood or impact.
- Accept: No action is taken on a risk, but its impact is accepted if it occurs.
- Exploit: Ensure a positive risk occurs or increase its benefits.

6. Monitor and control
- Continuously monitor for risk events and triggers. Track identified risks and identify any new risks. Adjust responses as needed. Regularly inform stakeholders of high-priority risks and status.
