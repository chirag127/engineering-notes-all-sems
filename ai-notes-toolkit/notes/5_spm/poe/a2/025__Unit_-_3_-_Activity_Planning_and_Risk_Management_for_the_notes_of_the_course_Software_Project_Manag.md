## Unit - 3 - Activity Planning and Risk Management

- Activity planning involves breaking down the project into manageable components called activities. Each activity is estimated, scheduled, and assigned resources.
- The work breakdown structure (WBS) is a hierarchy that breaks down the project into small, manageable parts. It helps to organize and define the total scope of the project.
- Activity sequencing involves determining the order and logic relationships between project activities. There are three types of logic relationships: finish-to-start, start-to-start, and finish-to-finish.
- Estimating activity resources involves estimating the types and quantities of resources required to execute each activity. Resources include people, equipment, materials, and funds.
- Estimating activity durations involves estimating the number of work periods needed to complete each activity. Tools and techniques for estimating durations include expert judgment, analogous estimating, parametric estimating, and three-point estimating.
- Risk management is concerned with identifying potential problems that could negatively impact the project and developing plans to mitigate their effects. It involves risk identification, analysis, response planning, and control.
- A risk register is used to document and track risks. It contains information such as the risk, possible causes, impacts, probability of occurrence, status, and responses.
- Risk responses include avoiding, mitigating, transferring, or accepting the risk. Avoiding or mitigating risk is preferable as it reduces negative impacts, but accepting risk may be necessary due to constraints. Transference shares risk with a third party.
