### Motivation in SPM

1. Achievement motivation: The drive to pursue and attain goals that are challenging but still attainable. People with a high achievement motivation seek to master tasks or goals, improve their skills, and meet high standards of excellence.

2. Affiliation motivation: The drive to establish and maintain interpersonal relationships. People with a high affiliation motivation have a strong desire for friendly social interactions, acceptance, and belonging.

3. Power motivation: The drive to influence, teach, help, or control others. People with a high power motivation want to make an impact, leave their mark, mentor others, and guide activities.

4. Security motivation: The drive to avoid threat, harm, or loss. People with a high security motivation are focused on safety, stability, and protection rather than risk, change, or uncertainty. Security motivation underlies risk avoidance.

The motivations are interconnected and individuals can be driven by multiple motivations simultaneously. Understanding one's dominant motivations can help in setting goals, making choices, and handling conflicts. Motivations evolve over time and are shaped by experiences, life events, and the environment. Recognizing and appreciating the motivations of others leads to more effective communication and collaboration.
