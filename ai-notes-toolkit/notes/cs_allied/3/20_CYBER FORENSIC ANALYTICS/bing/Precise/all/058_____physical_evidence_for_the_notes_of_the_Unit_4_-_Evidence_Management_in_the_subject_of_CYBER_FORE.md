### Physical Evidence

Physical evidence refers to any material object that can establish that a crime has been committed or can link a crime and its victim or perpetrator. Physical evidence can be found in many forms, including but not limited to:

1. **Fingerprints**: Fingerprints are unique to each individual and can be used to identify a suspect or victim.
2. **DNA**: DNA evidence can be collected from blood, saliva, hair, or other bodily fluids and can be used to identify a suspect or victim.
3. **Weapons**: Weapons such as guns or knives can be used to link a suspect to a crime.
4. **Documents**: Documents such as letters, receipts, or contracts can provide evidence of a crime or link a suspect to a crime.
5. **Digital Evidence**: Digital evidence can include data from computers, mobile phones, or other electronic devices and can provide evidence of a crime or link a suspect to a crime.

Physical evidence is collected and analyzed by forensic scientists to help solve crimes. It is important to properly collect, preserve, and analyze physical evidence to ensure its integrity and usefulness in a criminal investigation. Proper evidence management is crucial in ensuring that physical evidence is admissible in court.
