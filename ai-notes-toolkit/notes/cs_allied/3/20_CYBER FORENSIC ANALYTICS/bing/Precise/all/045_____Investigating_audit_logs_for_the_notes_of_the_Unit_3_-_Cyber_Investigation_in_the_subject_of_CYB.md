### Investigating Audit Logs for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

Audit logs are records of events that occur on a computer system or network. They are used to track user activity, system changes, and other events that may be relevant to an investigation. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, audit logs can provide valuable information for investigators.

Here are some key points to consider when investigating audit logs:

1. **Understand the system**: It is important to have a good understanding of the system that generated the audit logs. This includes knowing what types of events are logged, where the logs are stored, and how they are formatted.

2. **Identify relevant events**: Not all events recorded in an audit log will be relevant to an investigation. Investigators must be able to identify which events are important and filter out the rest.

3. **Correlate events**: Audit logs often contain information about multiple events that are related to each other. Investigators must be able to correlate these events to build a timeline of activity and understand the sequence of events.

4. **Analyze data**: Audit logs can contain a large amount of data. Investigators must be able to analyze this data to identify patterns, trends, and anomalies that may be relevant to the investigation.

5. **Preserve evidence**: Audit logs can be valuable evidence in a cyber investigation. Investigators must take steps to preserve the integrity of the logs and ensure that they are admissible in court.

In summary, investigating audit logs is an important part of cyber forensic analytics. Investigators must have a good understanding of the system, be able to identify relevant events, correlate events, analyze data, and preserve evidence. By following these steps, investigators can extract valuable information from audit logs to support their investigation.
