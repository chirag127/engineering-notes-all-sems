### Internet Gambling

Internet gambling is a form of cybercrime that involves betting on sports or gambling contests using a "wire communication facility," which includes the Internet. Federal law prohibits individuals from engaging in this activity .

The online gambling industry has become extremely lucrative in the last few years and a major target for cybercriminals. This is because these websites and mobile applications are a gateway to the customers’ credit cards, bank accounts, and other sensitive information .

One common cybercriminal tactic is to cash out balances on compromised online gambling accounts. Online gambling accounts can be compromised through brute-forcing activity, botnets, or credential-stealing malware sold on underground forums .

Online gambling could provide criminal actors with the potential to be anonymous to an even greater extent than in physical casinos. Using online tools, like TOR networks and Virtual Private Networks (VPNs), individuals can hide their identity and location while engaging in illegal online gambling activities .

Like any form of gambling, the risks of online gambling include addiction and the potential rapid loss of any funds invested in the game. Additionally, there are risks from viruses, Trojan horses, computer worms, and spyware .
