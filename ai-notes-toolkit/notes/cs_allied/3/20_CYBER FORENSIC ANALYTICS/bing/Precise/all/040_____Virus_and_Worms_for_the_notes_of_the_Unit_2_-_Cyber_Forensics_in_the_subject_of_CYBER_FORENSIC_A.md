# Virus and Worms

Virus and worms are two types of malicious software (malware) that can harm computer systems. They are both self-replicating programs that spread from one computer to another, often without the user's knowledge or consent.

## Virus
- A virus is a type of malware that attaches itself to a host program or file and spreads by infecting other programs or files on the computer.
- Viruses can cause a wide range of damage, from deleting or corrupting files to slowing down the computer's performance.
- Viruses can be spread through email attachments, downloads from the internet, or by sharing infected files on removable media such as USB drives.

## Worms
- A worm is a type of malware that spreads by exploiting vulnerabilities in network security.
- Unlike viruses, worms do not need to attach themselves to a host program or file. Instead, they can spread independently by exploiting vulnerabilities in network protocols or by tricking users into downloading and running them.
- Worms can cause damage by consuming network bandwidth, slowing down the network, and potentially allowing attackers to gain control of infected computers.

In conclusion, both viruses and worms are types of malware that can cause harm to computer systems. It is important to practice safe computing habits, such as keeping software up to date and being cautious when downloading files or opening email attachments, to protect against these threats. Additionally, using anti-virus software can help detect and remove malware from your computer.
