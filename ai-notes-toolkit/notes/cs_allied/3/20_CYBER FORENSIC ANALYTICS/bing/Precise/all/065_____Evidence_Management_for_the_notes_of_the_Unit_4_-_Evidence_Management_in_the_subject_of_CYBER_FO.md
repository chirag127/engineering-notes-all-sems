### Evidence Management

Evidence management is a crucial aspect of cyber forensic analytics. It involves the collection, preservation, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence in a manner that is admissible in a court of law. Here are some key points to consider when managing digital evidence:

1. **Collection**: Digital evidence must be collected in a forensically sound manner, following established procedures and guidelines. This includes the use of specialized tools and techniques to ensure that the evidence is not altered or damaged during the collection process.

2. **Preservation**: Once collected, digital evidence must be preserved to prevent tampering, loss, or degradation. This involves creating a chain of custody to document the handling of the evidence, as well as storing the evidence in a secure location.

3. **Analysis**: Digital evidence must be analyzed to extract relevant information and establish its significance to the case. This involves the use of specialized software and techniques to recover and interpret data from digital devices.

4. **Presentation**: The results of the analysis must be presented in a clear and concise manner, using visual aids and other tools to help convey the information to a non-technical audience. The presentation must be objective and based on the facts of the case.

In summary, evidence management is a critical component of cyber forensic analytics, and it is essential to follow established procedures and guidelines to ensure the integrity and admissibility of digital evidence.
