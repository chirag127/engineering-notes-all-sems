### Internet Gambling

Internet gambling is a form of cyber crime that has become increasingly popular in recent years. Federal law prohibits individuals from betting on sports or gambling contests using a "wire communication facility," which includes the Internet. Yet the Internet allows immediate and anonymous communication that makes it difficult to trace gambling activity.

The online gambling industry has become extremely lucrative and a major target for cyber criminals. This is because these websites and mobile applications are a gateway to the customers’ credit cards, bank accounts, and other sensitive information.

One common cybercriminal tactic is to cash out balances on compromised online gambling accounts. Online gambling accounts can be compromised through brute forcing activity, botnets, or credential-stealing malware sold on underground forums.

Online gambling could provide criminal actors with the potential to be anonymous to an even greater extent than in physical casinos. Using online tools, like TOR networks and Virtual Private Networks (VPNs), individuals can hide their identity and location while engaging in illegal online gambling activities.

In addition to the risks associated with illegal online gambling, there are also risks from viruses, Trojan horses, computer worms, and spyware. Like any form of gambling, the risks include addiction and the potential rapid loss of any funds invested in the game.
