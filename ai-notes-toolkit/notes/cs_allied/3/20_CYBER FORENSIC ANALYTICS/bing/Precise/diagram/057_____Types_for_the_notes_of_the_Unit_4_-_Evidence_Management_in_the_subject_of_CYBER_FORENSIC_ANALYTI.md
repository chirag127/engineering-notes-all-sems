### Unit 4 - Evidence Management in Cyber Forensic Analytics

#### Types of Evidence

1. **Volatile Evidence**: This type of evidence is temporary and can be lost when the system is powered off. Examples include data stored in RAM, cache memory, and registers.

2. **Non-Volatile Evidence**: This type of evidence is permanent and is not lost when the system is powered off. Examples include data stored on hard drives, solid-state drives, and USB drives.

3. **Network Evidence**: This type of evidence is related to network traffic and can be collected by monitoring and capturing network packets.

4. **Physical Evidence**: This type of evidence includes physical items such as computer hardware, peripherals, and storage media.

5. **Documentary Evidence**: This type of evidence includes documents, emails, and other written or printed materials.

6. **Testimonial Evidence**: This type of evidence is obtained through interviews and statements from witnesses or suspects.

7. **Digital Evidence**: This type of evidence is any information stored or transmitted in digital form that may be relevant to an investigation. It can include data from computers, mobile devices, and other digital storage media.

Each type of evidence has its own unique characteristics and requires specific methods for collection, preservation, and analysis. It is important for forensic analysts to understand the different types of evidence and how to handle them properly in order to ensure the integrity of the investigation.
