# Unit 1 - Cyber Crime: Cyber Stalking

### Definition
- Cyberstalking is the repeated use of electronic communications to harass or frighten someone, for example by sending threatening emails.
- It is a technologically-based "attack" on one person who has been targeted specifically for that attack for reasons of anger, revenge or control.
- Cyberstalking can take many forms, including harassment, embarrassment, and humiliation of the victim.
- It may include false accusations, defamation, slander, and libel. It may also include monitoring, identity theft, threats, vandalism, solicitation for sex, doxing, or blackmail.

### Examples
- Sending threatening or harassing emails or messages.
- Posting false or defamatory information about the victim online.
- Monitoring the victim's online activity.
- Stealing the victim's identity or personal information.
- Making threats or engaging in other forms of intimidation.
- Soliciting for sex or engaging in other forms of sexual harassment.
- Doxing (publishing private or identifying information about the victim online).
- Blackmailing the victim.

### Legal Consequences
- In the United States, the Violence Against Women Act of 2000 placed cyberstalking under the prevue of federal law.
- Cyberstalking is potentially a crime in the United States.

### Prevention and Protection
- Be cautious about the personal information you share online.
- Use strong and unique passwords for all your online accounts.
- Be mindful of the privacy settings on your social media accounts.
- Block or report any users who engage in harassing or threatening behavior.
- Keep records of any harassing or threatening messages or posts.
- If you feel threatened or unsafe, contact law enforcement.
