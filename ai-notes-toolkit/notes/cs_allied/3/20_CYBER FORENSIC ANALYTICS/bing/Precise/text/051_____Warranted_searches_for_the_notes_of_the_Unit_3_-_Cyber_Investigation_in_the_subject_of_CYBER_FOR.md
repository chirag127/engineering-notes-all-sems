### Warranted Searches

Warranted searches are searches that are conducted with a warrant, which is a legal document that is issued by a judge or magistrate. The warrant gives law enforcement officers the authority to conduct a search of a specific location for specific evidence related to a crime.

1. **Obtaining a Warrant**: In order to obtain a warrant, law enforcement officers must present evidence to a judge or magistrate that shows probable cause that a crime has been committed and that evidence related to the crime is likely to be found at the location specified in the warrant.
2. **Executing a Warrant**: Once a warrant has been obtained, law enforcement officers are authorized to conduct a search of the specified location. The search must be conducted in a reasonable manner and must be limited to the scope of the warrant.
3. **Warrant Exceptions**: There are certain situations in which a warrant is not required for a search to be considered legal. These exceptions include consent searches, searches incident to arrest, and exigent circumstances.
4. **Challenging a Warrant**: If evidence is obtained through a search that was conducted with a warrant, the defendant may challenge the validity of the warrant. If the warrant is found to be invalid, the evidence obtained through the search may be excluded from the trial.
