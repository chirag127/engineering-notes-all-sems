### Physical Evidence

Physical evidence refers to any material object that can establish that a crime has been committed or can link a crime and its victim or perpetrator. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, physical evidence can include:

1. **Computer hardware**: This can include the computer itself, as well as any peripheral devices such as external hard drives, USB drives, and other storage media.

2. **Mobile devices**: Smartphones and tablets can contain a wealth of information that can be useful in a cyber forensic investigation.

3. **Network devices**: Routers, switches, and other network devices can provide valuable information about the flow of data in a network.

4. **Printed documents**: Printed documents can provide evidence of communications, transactions, and other activities.

5. **Digital storage media**: CDs, DVDs, and other digital storage media can contain evidence in the form of files and data.

It is important to properly collect, preserve, and analyze physical evidence in a cyber forensic investigation to ensure its integrity and admissibility in legal proceedings. This process is known as evidence management and is a critical component of cyber forensic analytics.
