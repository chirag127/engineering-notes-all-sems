### Commercial Piracy

- Commercial piracy is the act of illegally reproducing or disseminating copyrighted material, such as computer programs, books, music, and films.
- Pirated software takes the form of counterfeited commercial products and illegal downloads and reproductions, as well as violations of licensing agreements that limit the number of users who can access a program.
- Software piracy has become a worldwide issue with China, the United States, and India being the top three offenders.
- The commercial value of pirated software is $19 billion in North America and Western Europe and has reached $27.3 billion in the rest of the world.
- Computer forensics are used to convict people who have performed physical and digital crimes, including copyright infringement.
- Commercial organizations have used digital forensics in cases of Intellectual Property theft and Industrial espionage.
- Anti-piracy measures include online piracy detection, which combines expert piracy oversight with powerful technology to discover, verify and remove pirated content.
- Forensic watermarking is another anti-piracy measure that can be used to track and identify the source of pirated content.
