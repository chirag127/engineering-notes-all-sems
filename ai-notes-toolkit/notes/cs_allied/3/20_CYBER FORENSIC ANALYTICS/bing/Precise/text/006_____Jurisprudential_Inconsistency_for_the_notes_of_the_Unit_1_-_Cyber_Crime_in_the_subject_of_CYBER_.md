### Jurisprudential Inconsistency in Cyber Crime

1. Jurisprudential inconsistencies may be due mainly to the lack of experiences of the judges on computer related crimes, cyber-crimes, or crimes committed with high-tech devices and/or against them.
2. Digital Forensics investigations jurisprudence is the theory and philosophy of the study of law and the principles upon which a law is based.
3. For digital evidence to appear at court and be legally admissible, the evidence must be authentic, accurate, complete, and convincing to the jury.
4. Cyber forensics is a process of extracting data as proof for a crime (that involves electronic devices) while following proper investigation rules to nab the culprit by presenting the evidence to the court.
5. Digital forensics sits at the intersection of cybersecurity and criminal justice.
6. Computer forensic investigators help retrieve information from computers and other digital storage devices. The retrieved data can then be used in criminal investigations or as evidence in cases of cyber crimes.
7. Cyber forensics is the discipline of studying digital sources to find reliable evidence of serious data security incidents.
8. A cyber forensics investigation involves looking for clues from sources such as physical devices, network logs, databases, and cloud services.
9. With the increase in the number of computer crimes, law enforcement agencies in the field of digital forensics are expected to investigate more cases than before.
10. Due to the complexity of digital forensic tasks, various investigation models have been developed to provide a structured approach to the investigation process.
