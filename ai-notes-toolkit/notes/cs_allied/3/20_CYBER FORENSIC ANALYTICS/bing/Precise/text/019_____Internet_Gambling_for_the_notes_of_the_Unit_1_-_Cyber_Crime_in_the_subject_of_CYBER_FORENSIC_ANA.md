### Internet Gambling

- Internet gambling is a form of cyber crime that involves betting on sports or gambling contests using a "wire communication facility," which includes the Internet.
- Federal law prohibits individuals from engaging in this activity, yet the Internet allows immediate and anonymous communication that makes it difficult to trace gambling activity.
- The online gambling industry has become extremely lucrative in the last few years and a major target for cyber criminals.
- These websites and mobile applications are a gateway to the customers’ credit cards, bank accounts, and other sensitive information.
- One common cybercriminal tactic is to cash out balances on compromised online gambling accounts.
- Online gambling accounts can be compromised through brute forcing activity, botnets, or credential-stealing malware sold on underground forums.
- Online gambling could provide criminal actors with the potential to be anonymous to an even greater extent than in physical casinos.
- Using online tools, like TOR networks and Virtual Private Networks (VPNs), individuals can hide their identity and location while engaging in illegal online gambling activities.
- Like any form of gambling, the risks include addiction and the potential rapid loss of any funds invested in the game.
