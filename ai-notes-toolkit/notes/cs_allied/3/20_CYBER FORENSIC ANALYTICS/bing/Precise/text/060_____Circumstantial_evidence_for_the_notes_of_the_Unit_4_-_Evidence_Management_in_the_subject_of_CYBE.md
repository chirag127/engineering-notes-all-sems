### Circumstantial evidence

Circumstantial evidence, also known as indirect evidence, is evidence that relies on an inference to connect it to a conclusion of fact. It is used in court to establish the likelihood of a fact by proving other events or circumstances which logically lead to the fact in question. Here are some key points to consider when discussing circumstantial evidence in the context of evidence management in cyber forensic analytics:

1. Circumstantial evidence is often used in conjunction with direct evidence to build a stronger case.
2. The strength of circumstantial evidence depends on the strength of the inference that can be drawn from it.
3. Circumstantial evidence can be used to corroborate or contradict direct evidence.
4. The use of circumstantial evidence requires careful analysis and evaluation to ensure that the inference drawn is reasonable and logical.
5. In some cases, circumstantial evidence may be the only evidence available to establish a fact.
6. The admissibility of circumstantial evidence is determined by the rules of evidence in the jurisdiction in which the case is being heard.
