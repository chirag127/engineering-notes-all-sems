### Real evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Real evidence refers to physical or tangible evidence that can be seen, touched, or examined.
2. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, real evidence may include physical devices such as computers, hard drives, and mobile phones, as well as digital evidence such as log files, emails, and digital photographs.
3. The process of evidence management involves the identification, collection, preservation, analysis, and presentation of evidence in a manner that is admissible in a court of law.
4. Proper evidence management is crucial in ensuring the integrity and reliability of the evidence, as well as its admissibility in legal proceedings.
5. Some key considerations in the management of real evidence in cyber forensic analytics include:
    - Proper documentation and chain of custody to ensure the integrity of the evidence.
    - Use of forensically sound methods and tools to collect and analyze the evidence.
    - Adherence to relevant laws, regulations, and standards governing the handling of evidence.
    - Proper storage and preservation of the evidence to prevent tampering or degradation.
    - Clear and concise presentation of the evidence and its relevance to the case at hand.
