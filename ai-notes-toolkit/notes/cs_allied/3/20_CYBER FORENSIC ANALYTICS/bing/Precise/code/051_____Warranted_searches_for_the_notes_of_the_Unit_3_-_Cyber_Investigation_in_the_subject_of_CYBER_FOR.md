### Warranted Searches

Warranted searches are searches that are conducted with a warrant, which is a legal document issued by a judge or magistrate that authorizes law enforcement officers to conduct a search of a person, location, or vehicle for evidence of a crime.

1. A warrant is required for most searches under the Fourth Amendment of the United States Constitution, which protects against unreasonable searches and seizures.
2. To obtain a warrant, law enforcement officers must demonstrate probable cause, which means that there is a reasonable belief that evidence of a crime will be found in the place to be searched.
3. Warrants must be specific, describing the place to be searched and the items to be seized.
4. Warranted searches are an important tool for law enforcement in investigating and solving crimes, as they allow officers to gather evidence in a lawful manner.
5. However, there are exceptions to the warrant requirement, such as exigent circumstances, consent, and searches incident to a lawful arrest.
6. It is important for law enforcement officers to follow proper procedures when conducting warranted searches to ensure that the evidence gathered is admissible in court.
