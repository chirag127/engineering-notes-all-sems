### Circumstantial evidence

Circumstantial evidence is evidence that relies on an inference to connect it to a conclusion of fact. It is also known as indirect evidence. This type of evidence is used in court to establish the likelihood of a fact being true. Here are some key points to remember about circumstantial evidence:

1. Circumstantial evidence is not direct evidence, meaning it does not directly prove the fact in question.
2. Circumstantial evidence can be used to support or refute a fact, but it must be considered in the context of all the evidence presented.
3. The strength of circumstantial evidence depends on the strength of the inference that can be drawn from it.
4. Circumstantial evidence can be very powerful, especially when there is a lot of it that all points to the same conclusion.
5. Circumstantial evidence is often used in criminal cases, where direct evidence may be difficult to obtain.
6. Examples of circumstantial evidence include fingerprints, DNA, and surveillance footage.
