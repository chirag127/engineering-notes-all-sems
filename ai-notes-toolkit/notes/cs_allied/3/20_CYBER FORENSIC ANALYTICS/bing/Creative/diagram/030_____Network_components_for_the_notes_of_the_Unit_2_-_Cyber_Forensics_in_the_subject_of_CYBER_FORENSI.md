### Network components for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Network components are the devices and software that enable the communication and data exchange between different nodes on a network.
- Network components can be classified into three categories: end devices, intermediary devices and network media.
- End devices are the sources and destinations of data on a network, such as computers, servers, printers, phones, cameras, etc.
- Intermediary devices are the devices that connect end devices and facilitate the flow of data, such as routers, switches, firewalls, access points, etc.
- Network media are the physical or wireless means of transmitting data, such as cables, fibers, radio waves, etc.
- Network components play a vital role in cyber forensics, as they can provide valuable evidence and information about the source, nature and impact of cyberattacks.
- Network forensics is the capture, recording and analysis of network packets in order to determine the source of network security attacks.
- Network forensics can help identify the attackers, their methods, their motives, their targets and their effects on the network and the end devices .
- Network forensics can also help prevent future attacks, by providing insights into the vulnerabilities and weaknesses of the network and the end devices, and by recommending countermeasures and best practices.
- Network forensics can be performed in two modes: live and post-mortem.
- Live network forensics involves monitoring and analyzing the network traffic in real time, using tools such as packet sniffers, network analyzers, intrusion detection systems, etc.
- Post-mortem network forensics involves examining the network logs, records and artifacts that are stored on the network components or the end devices, using tools such as forensic software, data recovery tools, etc.
- Network forensics faces several challenges, such as the large volume and complexity of network data, the encryption and obfuscation of network packets, the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of network components, the legal and ethical issues of data collection and analysis, etc.
- Network forensics requires a combination of technical skills, analytical skills, investigative skills and legal knowledge, as well as the use of appropriate tools and methods.
- Network forensics is one of the key components in a defense team’s arsenal, as it can help draw several conclusions during an investigation and provide digital evidence that can be relied on in court   .
