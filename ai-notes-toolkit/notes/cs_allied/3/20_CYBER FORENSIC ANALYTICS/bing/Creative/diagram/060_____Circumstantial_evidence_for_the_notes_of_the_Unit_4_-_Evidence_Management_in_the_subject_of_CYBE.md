### Circumstantial evidence

- Circumstantial evidence is evidence that **indirectly** proves a fact or a connection between facts by **inference** or **reasoning**.
- Circumstantial evidence is **not** direct evidence that **explicitly** shows the existence or non-existence of a fact or a connection between facts.
- Circumstantial evidence is often used in **cyber forensics** to establish the **identity**, **intent**, **motive**, **opportunity**, **means**, or **modus operandi** of a **suspect** or a **victim** in a **cybercrime** .
- Circumstantial evidence can be derived from various sources of **digital evidence**, such as **computer hard drives**, **mobile phones**, **network logs**, **social media posts**, **emails**, **metadata**, **encryption keys**, **digital signatures**, **biometric data**, etc.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **corroborated** or **contradicted** by other evidence, such as **eyewitness testimony**, **physical evidence**, **confessions**, **alibis**, etc .
- Circumstantial evidence can be **strong** or **weak** depending on the **quality**, **quantity**, **relevance**, **reliability**, and **consistency** of the evidence and the **plausibility** of the inference or reasoning .
- Circumstantial evidence can be **admissible** or **inadmissible** in court depending on the **rules of evidence**, the **jurisdiction**, the **type of case**, and the **discretion of the judge** .
- Circumstantial evidence can be **challenged** or **disputed** by the **defense** or the **prosecution** based on the **credibility**, **authenticity**, **integrity**, **chain of custody**, **preservation**, **analysis**, and **presentation** of the evidence .
