## Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities

Cyber laws are the rules and regulations that govern the use and security of information technology, especially the internet. Cyber laws cover various aspects of cyberspace, such as data protection, e-commerce, intellectual property, cybercrime, and cyberwarfare. Cyber laws aim to protect the rights and interests of individuals, organizations, and governments in the digital domain.

Cyber authorities are the entities that enforce, regulate, or oversee cyber laws. Cyber authorities can be governmental, such as agencies, departments, or courts, or non-governmental, such as certifying bodies, industry associations, or international organizations. Cyber authorities have different roles and responsibilities, such as issuing licenses, setting standards, investigating breaches, imposing sanctions, or resolving disputes.

Some examples of cyber laws and authorities are:

- The Information Technology Act, 2000, is the primary legislation in India that deals with cyber law issues. It provides legal recognition to electronic documents and digital signatures, defines cyber offences and penalties, and establishes a regulatory framework for certifying authorities and intermediaries. The act also empowers the central government to issue rules and regulations on various matters related to cyber law.
- The Executive Order on Improving the Nation’s Cybersecurity, issued by the U.S. President in May 2021, is a directive that aims to enhance the security and resilience of the federal government and the critical infrastructure against cyber threats. The order mandates the adoption of best practices, such as zero-trust architecture, multifactor authentication, and encryption, and the development of standards and guidelines for the private sector. The order also establishes a Cybersecurity Safety Review Board, a National Cyber Director, and a Cybersecurity Incident Review Process.
- The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), adopted by the European Union in 2016, is a comprehensive law that protects the privacy and personal data of individuals in the EU and the European Economic Area. The GDPR grants data subjects various rights, such as the right to access, rectify, erase, or port their data, and the right to object or withdraw consent. The GDPR also imposes obligations on data controllers and processors, such as the duty to inform, secure, and report data breaches, and the requirement to appoint a data protection officer. The GDPR is enforced by the national data protection authorities of the member states, and the European Data Protection Board.
