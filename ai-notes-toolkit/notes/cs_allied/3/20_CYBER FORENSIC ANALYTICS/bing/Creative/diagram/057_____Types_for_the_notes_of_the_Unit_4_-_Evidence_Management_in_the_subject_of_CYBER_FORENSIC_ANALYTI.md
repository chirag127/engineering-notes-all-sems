### Types of Evidence in Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence from various sources, such as computers, mobile devices, networks, databases, emails, etc. Cyber forensics can be used to investigate crimes, disputes, security incidents, or any other situation that involves digital data.

There are different types of evidence in cyber forensics, depending on the nature, source, format, and location of the data. Some of the common types are:

- **Logs**: Logs are records of events or activities that occur on a system, network, or application. Logs can provide information such as timestamps, IP addresses, user names, actions, errors, etc. Logs can be found in various places, such as operating systems, web servers, firewalls, routers, databases, etc. Logs can be used to reconstruct the sequence of events, identify the source and destination of data, and detect any anomalies or malicious activities.  
- **Video footage and images**: Video footage and images are visual data that can capture the appearance, behavior, location, or identity of a person, object, or scene. Video footage and images can be obtained from various sources, such as cameras, smartphones, drones, CCTV, etc. Video footage and images can be used to verify the presence, absence, or movement of a person, object, or scene, and to compare or match the features or characteristics of a person, object, or scene. 
- **Archives**: Archives are collections of files or data that are compressed, encrypted, or stored in a specific format. Archives can be created by various tools, such as zip, rar, tar, etc. Archives can be used to reduce the size, protect the confidentiality, or preserve the integrity of the data. Archives can be used to store or transfer large amounts of data, such as backups, documents, emails, etc. Archives can be used to recover the original files or data, and to analyze the metadata, content, or history of the files or data. 
- **Active data**: Active data is the data that is readily accessible and visible on a system or device. Active data can include files, folders, documents, emails, messages, contacts, calendars, bookmarks, history, etc. Active data can be used to provide information about the user, system, device, or activity. Active data can be used to identify the owner, user, or account of a system or device, and to analyze the usage, preferences, or behavior of a user, system, or device.  
- **Metadata**: Metadata is the data that describes or provides information about other data. Metadata can include attributes, properties, tags, headers, etc. Metadata can be found in various types of data, such as files, images, videos, audio, documents, emails, etc. Metadata can be used to provide information such as the name, size, type, format, creation, modification, or access date and time, location, author, camera, device, etc. of the data. Metadata can be used to verify the authenticity, origin, or provenance of the data, and to detect any modifications, alterations, or tampering of the data.  
- **Residual data**: Residual data is the data that is not readily accessible or visible on a system or device, but can be recovered by using special tools or techniques. Residual data can include deleted files, fragments, cache, cookies, temporary files, swap files, slack space, etc. Residual data can be used to recover the data that was intentionally or unintentionally erased, overwritten, or corrupted. Residual data can be used to reveal the previous state, content, or history of the data, and to detect any attempts to hide, destroy, or manipulate the data.  
- **Volatile data**: Volatile data is the data that is stored in the memory or RAM of a system or device, and is lost when the system or device is powered off or restarted. Volatile data can include processes, services, drivers, network connections, open files, registry keys, etc. Volatile data can be used to provide information about the current state, configuration, or activity of the system or device. Volatile data can be used to identify the running programs, applications, or malware, and to analyze the network traffic, communication, or interaction of the system or device.  [^
