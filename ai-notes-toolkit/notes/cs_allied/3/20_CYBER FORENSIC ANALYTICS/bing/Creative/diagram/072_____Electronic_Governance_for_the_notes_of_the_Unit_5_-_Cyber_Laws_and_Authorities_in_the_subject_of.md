### Electronic Governance

- Electronic governance or e-governance is the application of information technology for delivering government services, exchange of information, communication transactions, integration of various stand-alone systems between government to citizen (G2C), government-to-business (G2B), government-to-government (G2G), government-to-employees (G2E) as well as back-office processes and interactions within the entire governance framework .
- E-governance aims to enhance the efficiency, transparency, accountability, and participation of the public sector by using ICTs.
- E-governance can be classified into four categories based on the direction of interaction: G2C, G2B, G2G, and G2E.
  - G2C: Government to citizen e-governance involves the delivery of public services and information to citizens through various channels, such as websites, portals, kiosks, mobile apps, call centers, etc. Examples of G2C services are online tax filing, voter registration, passport application, etc.
  - G2B: Government to business e-governance involves the facilitation of business activities and transactions with the government through online platforms, such as e-procurement, e-tendering, e-licensing, e-customs, etc. Examples of G2B services are online registration of businesses, online payment of taxes, online submission of bids, etc.
  - G2G: Government to government e-governance involves the integration and coordination of various government agencies and departments through ICTs, such as shared databases, networks, interoperability standards, etc. Examples of G2G services are online data sharing, online monitoring and evaluation, online decision support systems, etc.
  - G2E: Government to employee e-governance involves the improvement of internal administration and human resource management of the government through ICTs, such as intranets, e-learning, e-payroll, e-performance appraisal, etc. Examples of G2E services are online training, online leave application, online grievance redressal, etc.
- E-governance can also be classified into three stages based on the level of sophistication and maturity: informational, transactional, and transformational.
  - Informational: The informational stage of e-governance involves the dissemination of basic information and data to the public through static websites, brochures, newsletters, etc. The main objective of this stage is to increase the awareness and accessibility of government services and policies. The interaction is one-way and passive.
  - Transactional: The transactional stage of e-governance involves the provision of interactive and dynamic services and transactions to the public through online platforms, such as portals, applications, forms, etc. The main objective of this stage is to increase the convenience and efficiency of government services and transactions. The interaction is two-way and active.
  - Transformational: The transformational stage of e-governance involves the re-engineering and integration of various government processes and systems through ICTs, such as databases, networks, standards, etc. The main objective of this stage is to increase the transparency, accountability, and participation of government services and processes. The interaction is multi-way and collaborative.
