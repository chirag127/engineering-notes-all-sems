# DoS for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- DoS stands for Denial-of-Service, which is a type of cyber-attack that occurs when an attacker seeks to render a computer or other network inaccessible to its authorized users by momentarily or permanently interrupting the normal operations of a host linked to the Internet.
- DoS attacks can be launched from a single or a group of computers, and can target various services, such as web servers, email servers, online gaming platforms, etc.
- DoS attacks can have various motives, such as extortion, sabotage, vandalism, protest, or competition.
- DoS attacks can have various impacts, such as loss of revenue, reputation, productivity, customer trust, or legal liability.
- DoS attacks can be classified into different types, such as:
  - Volume-based attacks: These attacks aim to consume the bandwidth of the target network or service by sending a large amount of traffic, such as UDP floods, ICMP floods, or spoofed-packet floods.
  - Protocol-based attacks: These attacks exploit the weaknesses of the network protocols, such as TCP, IP, or HTTP, by sending malformed or incomplete packets, such as SYN floods, ping of death, or HTTP floods.
  - Application-based attacks: These attacks target the specific applications or services running on the target network or service, by sending requests that consume a lot of resources, such as slowloris, HTTP POST, or DNS amplification.
- DoS attack forensics is the process of investigating and analyzing the DoS attack, in order to identify the attacker, the attack method, the attack source, the attack impact, and the attack mitigation.
- DoS attack forensics has several steps, such as:
  - Data collection: This step involves collecting the relevant data from the target network or service, such as logs, packets, traffic statistics, etc.
  - Data analysis: This step involves analyzing the collected data, using various tools and techniques, such as packet sniffers, network analyzers, traffic filters, etc., to identify the attack characteristics, such as attack type, attack duration, attack frequency, attack volume, etc.
  - Data interpretation: This step involves interpreting the analyzed data, using various methods and frameworks, such as attack models, attack signatures, attack patterns, etc., to identify the attack origin, such as attack source IP, attack source location, attack source network, etc.
  - Data reporting: This step involves reporting the findings and conclusions of the DoS attack forensics, using various formats and media, such as reports, graphs, charts, etc., to communicate the attack details, such as attack motive, attack impact, attack mitigation, etc.
- DoS attack forensics is important for various reasons, such as:
  - To prevent or reduce the damage caused by the DoS attack, by blocking or filtering the attack traffic, or by restoring the normal operations of the target network or service.
  - To trace or prosecute the attacker, by collecting and preserving the evidence of the DoS attack, or by cooperating with the law enforcement agencies or the Internet service providers.
  - To learn or improve the security of the target network or service, by understanding the attack method, the attack vulnerability, or the attack countermeasure, or by implementing the best practices or the security policies.
