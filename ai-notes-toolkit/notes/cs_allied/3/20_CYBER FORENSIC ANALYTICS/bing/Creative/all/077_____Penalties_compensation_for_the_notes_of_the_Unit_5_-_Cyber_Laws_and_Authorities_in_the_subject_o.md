# Penalties and Compensation for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

- Cyber laws are the legal frameworks that regulate the use, protection, and governance of information and communication technologies (ICTs) in various domains, such as e-commerce, privacy, data security, cybercrime, intellectual property, and cyberwarfare.
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, depending on the jurisdiction, the level of development, and the socio-cultural values of the society. However, some common principles and standards have emerged from international conventions, treaties, and organizations, such as the United Nations, the Council of Europe, the European Union, the Organization of American States, and the African Union.
- Cyber laws also entail the enforcement mechanisms and authorities that are responsible for ensuring compliance, investigating violations, imposing sanctions, and resolving disputes. These may include courts, tribunals, regulators, law enforcement agencies, and alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms, such as arbitration and mediation.
- Penalties and compensation are two types of remedies that cyber laws may provide for the victims of cyberattacks, data breaches, or other forms of cyber harm. Penalties are the punitive measures that aim to deter and punish the offenders, while compensation is the restorative measure that aims to restore the losses or damages suffered by the victims.
- Penalties and compensation may vary depending on the nature, severity, and impact of the cyber incident, the applicable cyber law, and the jurisdiction. Some examples of penalties and compensation are:

  - Under the Information Technology Act, 2000 of India, Section 43 provides for penalty and compensation for damage to computer, computer system, or computer network, ranging from INR 1 lakh to INR 1 crore, depending on the extent of damage .
  - Under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) of 1996 of the United States, the fine for violating the data privacy and security rules is calculated based on the number of medical records exposed, with fines ranging from USD 50 to USD 50,000 per record. Fines are capped at USD 1.5 million per year, but organizations may receive the maximum fine for multiple years .
  - Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) of 2016 of the European Union, the fine for violating the data protection and privacy rules is up to EUR 20 million or 4% of the annual global turnover of the offender, whichever is higher.
  - Under the Cybersecurity Act of 2015 of Singapore, the fine for failing to comply with the cybersecurity obligations or directions is up to SGD 100,000 or 10% of the annual turnover of the offender, whichever is higher.
  - Under the Consumer Privacy Act (CPA) of 2018 of California, the fine for violating the consumer data privacy rights is up to USD 20,000 per violation and injunctive relief.
