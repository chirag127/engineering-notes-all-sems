# Physical Evidence

Physical evidence is any tangible object or material that can be used to prove or disprove a fact or a hypothesis in a cyber forensic investigation. Physical evidence can be collected from various sources, such as computers, mobile devices, removable media, cables, publications, trash, etc. Physical evidence can contain digital evidence, which is information stored or transmitted in binary form, such as files, emails, logs, etc. Physical evidence can also provide clues about the identity, location, motive, and behavior of the suspect or the victim.

Some of the points to remember about physical evidence are:

- Physical evidence requires seizing the equipment used by the suspect. Proper authorization should be requested to legally access the equipment.
- Physical evidence should be handled carefully to avoid contamination, damage, or loss. Proper chain of custody should be maintained to document the history of the evidence, such as who collected it, when, where, how, and why.
- Physical evidence should be analyzed using appropriate tools and techniques to extract and interpret the digital evidence. The analysis should follow the principles of accuracy, completeness, reliability, and repeatability.
- Physical evidence should be presented in a clear and concise manner to support the findings and conclusions of the investigation. The presentation should include the source, type, and relevance of the evidence, as well as the methods and tools used to obtain and analyze it.
- Physical evidence should be stored securely and properly to preserve its integrity and admissibility. The storage should follow the legal and ethical guidelines and standards of the jurisdiction and the organization.
