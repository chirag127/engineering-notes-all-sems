# Cyber Crime

Cyber crime is the use of a computer or a network as an instrument to commit illegal acts, such as fraud, identity theft, data theft, cyberextortion, ransomware, phishing, spoofing, etc. Cyber crime can harm individuals, organizations, or even nations by compromising their security, privacy, or financial well-being.

Some of the types and examples of cyber crime are:

- **Email and internet fraud**: This involves sending deceptive or fraudulent messages or links to trick the recipients into revealing their personal or financial information, or to perform some action that benefits the fraudster. For example, phishing emails that impersonate legitimate entities and ask for login credentials, bank details, or payment information.
- **Identity fraud**: This involves stealing and using someone else's personal information, such as name, date of birth, social security number, credit card number, etc., to commit fraud, access accounts, or obtain benefits. For example, identity theft that leads to unauthorized purchases, loans, or tax refunds.
- **Theft of financial or card payment data**: This involves stealing and using the financial or card payment data of individuals or organizations, such as bank account numbers, credit card numbers, PINs, CVVs, etc., to make unauthorized transactions, withdrawals, or transfers. For example, skimming devices that capture card data at ATMs or POS terminals, or malware that infects devices and captures keystrokes or screenshots.
- **Theft and sale of corporate data**: This involves stealing and selling the confidential or proprietary data of organizations, such as trade secrets, customer data, financial data, intellectual property, etc., to competitors, hackers, or other parties. For example, data breaches that expose sensitive information to unauthorized parties, or insider threats that leak data to external sources.
- **Cyberextortion**: This involves demanding money or other benefits from individuals or organizations by threatening to harm them or their data, systems, or reputation. For example, ransomware attacks that encrypt the data or devices of the victims and demand a ransom for decryption, or denial-of-service attacks that disrupt the availability or functionality of the services or systems of the victims and demand a payment for stopping the attack.
- **Cryptojacking**: This involves using the computing resources of individuals or organizations without their consent or knowledge to mine cryptocurrency, which is a digital currency that is generated by solving complex mathematical problems. For example, malicious scripts or programs that run in the background of the devices or browsers of the victims and consume their CPU power, memory, or bandwidth to mine cryptocurrency.
