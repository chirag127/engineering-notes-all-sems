## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

- Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a forensically sound manner.
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and recognizing potential sources of digital evidence, such as devices, files, logs, etc.
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, chain of custody, etc.
  - Preservation: protecting digital evidence from alteration, loss, or damage, such as using write blockers, encryption, backups, etc.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting digital evidence using various methods and tools, such as carving, decoding, recovery, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and presenting digital evidence in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, such as using charts, graphs, timelines, etc.
- Evidence management requires following the principles of digital forensics, such as:
  - Admissibility: ensuring that digital evidence is legally acceptable and relevant to the case, such as following the rules of evidence, standards, and guidelines, etc.
  - Authenticity: ensuring that digital evidence is genuine and trustworthy, such as verifying the integrity, origin, and ownership of digital evidence, etc.
  - Completeness: ensuring that digital evidence is comprehensive and sufficient to support the investigation, such as collecting all relevant and available digital evidence, etc.
  - Reliability: ensuring that digital evidence is consistent and accurate, such as using validated and tested tools and techniques, etc.
  - Reproducibility: ensuring that digital evidence can be reproduced and verified by other experts, such as documenting and sharing the procedures and results of digital evidence analysis, etc.
