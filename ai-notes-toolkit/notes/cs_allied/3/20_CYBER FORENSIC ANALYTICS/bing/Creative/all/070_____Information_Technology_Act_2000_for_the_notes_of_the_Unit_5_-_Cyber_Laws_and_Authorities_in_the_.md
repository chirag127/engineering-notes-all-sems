# Information Technology Act 2000

- The Information Technology Act 2000 (also known as ITA-2000, or the IT Act) is an Act of the Indian Parliament (No 21 of 2000) notified on 17 October 2000 .
- It is the primary law in India dealing with cybercrime and electronic commerce .
- It came into force on October 17, 2000 and applies to the whole of India and to any offense committed inside or outside India's geographic boundaries and irrespective of nationality.
- It has 13 chapters and 94 sections, and covers various aspects of information technology, such as digital signatures, electronic records, cyber offenses, network service providers, intermediaries, data protection, cyber security, cyber appellate tribunal, etc .
- It also amended several existing laws, such as the Indian Penal Code, the Indian Evidence Act, the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, and the Reserve Bank of India Act, to make them compatible with the use of information technology .
- It was amended in 2008 by the Information Technology (Amendment) Act, 2008, which introduced new provisions on cyber terrorism, child pornography, phishing, identity theft, etc., and increased the penalties for cyber offenses .
- It is administered by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), which is responsible for formulating policies and implementing programs related to information technology in India .
- It also empowers the Central Government to appoint a Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) to regulate the issuance of digital signature certificates, and to establish a Cyber Appellate Tribunal (CAT) to hear appeals against the orders of the CCA or the adjudicating officers appointed under the Act .
- It also provides for the appointment of a Cyber Regulations Advisory Committee (CRAC) to advise the Central Government and the CCA on matters relating to the administration of the Act .
- It also confers powers on the police officers and other authorities to investigate, search, seize, and arrest for cyber offenses, and to request and obtain information from intermediaries and service providers .
