# DDoS Attacks

- A **distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack** is a malicious attempt to disrupt the normal traffic of a targeted server, service or network by overwhelming the target or its surrounding infrastructure with a flood of Internet traffic.
- A DDoS attack results in either degraded network performance or an outright service outage of critical infrastructure.
- A DDoS attack attempts to exhaust an application’s resources, such as bandwidth, memory, CPU, or disk space.
- A DDoS attack can be achieved by thwarting access to virtually anything: servers, devices, services, networks, applications, and even specific transactions within applications.
- A DDoS attack is different from a simple denial-of-service (DoS) attack, which is launched from a single source and can be easily blocked by filtering the source IP address.
- A DDoS attack is launched from multiple compromised computer systems, often called bots or zombies, that are controlled by a malicious actor, often called a botmaster or a DDoS attacker.
- A DDoS attack can use various techniques to generate and amplify traffic, such as TCP SYN flooding, UDP flooding, ICMP flooding, DNS amplification, NTP amplification, SSDP amplification, HTTP flooding, and more.
- A DDoS attack can have various motives and impacts, such as extorting money from the victim, disrupting a competitor's business, protesting against a political or social cause, or simply causing chaos and damage.
- A DDoS attack can be mitigated by various methods, such as deploying firewalls, routers, switches, or load balancers that can filter or drop malicious traffic, using cloud-based services that can absorb and redirect the traffic, or implementing DDoS defense solutions that can detect and block DDoS attacks in real time.
