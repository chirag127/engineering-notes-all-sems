# On Scene Activities for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

- On scene activities are the actions and procedures that a computer forensic investigator performs at the location where digital evidence is found, such as a crime scene, a suspect's home, or a workplace.
- The main objectives of on scene activities are to identify, secure, collect, document, and preserve digital evidence in a way that maintains its integrity, reliability, and admissibility in court.
- Some of the steps involved in on scene activities are:

  - **Preparation**: The investigator should prepare for the on scene activities by gathering the necessary tools, equipment, authorization, and information about the case and the scene. The investigator should also be aware of the potential risks and challenges that may arise at the scene, such as encryption, booby traps, or hostile suspects.
  - **Arrival and securing the scene**: The investigator should arrive at the scene as soon as possible and secure the perimeter to prevent unauthorized access, tampering, or contamination of the digital evidence. The investigator should also coordinate with other first responders, such as law enforcement, fire, or medical personnel, and establish a chain of custody for the evidence.
  - **Initial survey and documentation**: The investigator should conduct an initial survey of the scene to identify and locate the digital devices and media that may contain relevant evidence. The investigator should document the scene using notes, sketches, photographs, or video recordings, and record the date, time, location, and condition of each item of evidence. The investigator should also document any actions taken or observations made at the scene, such as turning off or unplugging devices, or seizing passwords or encryption keys.
  - **Collection and preservation**: The investigator should collect and preserve the digital evidence using appropriate methods and techniques, such as making forensic copies, using write blockers, or applying anti-static bags. The investigator should label and package each item of evidence securely and carefully, and transport it to a secure location for further analysis. The investigator should also update the chain of custody and inventory records for the evidence.
  - **Final survey and release of the scene**: The investigator should conduct a final survey of the scene to ensure that no evidence is left behind, and that the scene is restored to its original condition as much as possible. The investigator should also release the scene to the appropriate authority or owner, and obtain a receipt or acknowledgment for the evidence collected.
