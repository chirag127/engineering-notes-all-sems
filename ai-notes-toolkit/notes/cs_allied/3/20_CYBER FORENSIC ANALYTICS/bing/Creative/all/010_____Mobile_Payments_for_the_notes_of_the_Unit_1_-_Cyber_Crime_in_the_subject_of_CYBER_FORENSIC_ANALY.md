# Mobile Payments

Mobile payments are transactions that are made using a mobile device, such as a smartphone, tablet, or wearable device. Mobile payments can be done through various methods, such as mobile wallets, QR codes, NFC, SMS, or biometrics.

Mobile payments offer convenience, speed, and flexibility for consumers and merchants, as they can be done anytime, anywhere, and with minimal contact. However, mobile payments also pose significant challenges and risks for cyber security and cyber crime.

Some of the cyber threats and risks associated with mobile payments are:

- Data theft: Cyber criminals can steal sensitive data, such as credit card numbers, bank account details, personal identification numbers, or biometric information, from mobile devices, mobile apps, or mobile networks. They can use this data to conduct fraudulent transactions, identity theft, or blackmail. Data theft can occur through various means, such as malware, phishing, hacking, or skimming  .
- Data breach: Cyber criminals can access or compromise the databases or systems of mobile payment service providers, merchants, or financial institutions, and expose or leak the data of millions of customers or users. Data breach can result in financial losses, reputational damage, legal liabilities, or regulatory penalties for the affected parties.
- Data manipulation: Cyber criminals can alter or tamper with the data or transactions of mobile payments, such as changing the amount, recipient, or currency of the payment, or creating fake or duplicate transactions. Data manipulation can cause errors, disputes, or frauds in the mobile payment process.
- Denial of service: Cyber criminals can disrupt or disable the mobile payment services or networks, by overwhelming them with excessive traffic, requests, or commands, or by exploiting their vulnerabilities or flaws. Denial of service can prevent or delay the mobile payments from being completed, and affect the availability, reliability, or performance of the mobile payment systems.

To protect themselves from these cyber threats and risks, mobile payment users and providers should adopt some of the following measures:

- Use strong passwords, encryption, and authentication methods for mobile devices and apps, and avoid using public or unsecured Wi-Fi networks or devices for mobile payments .
- Use tokenization, which replaces the sensitive data with a random and unique code, or token, that is useless to cyber criminals, and protects the data while in transit or at rest .
- Update the mobile devices and apps regularly, and install anti-virus and anti-malware software, to prevent or detect any malicious software or activity on the mobile devices or apps .
- Monitor and review the mobile payment transactions and statements, and report any suspicious or unauthorized activity or transaction to the mobile payment service provider, merchant, or financial institution, as soon as possible .
- Educate and train the mobile payment users and providers on the best practices and policies of mobile payment security, and raise awareness of the potential cyber threats and risks of mobile payments .
