# Cyber warfare

- Cyber warfare means the use of computer technology to disrupt the activities of a state or organization, especially the deliberate attacking of information systems for strategic or military purposes.
- Cyber warfare is usually waged against government and military networks in order to disrupt, destroy, or deny their use.
- Cyber warfare can have intended outcomes such as espionage, sabotage, propaganda, manipulation or economic warfare.
- Cyber warfare can involve different types of cyber attacks, such as computer viruses, denial-of-service attacks, phishing, ransomware, botnets, logic bombs, etc.
- Cyber warfare can be conducted by state actors or their proxies, such as hackers, terrorists, or cybercriminals.
- Cyber warfare can pose serious threats to national security, critical infrastructure, public services, economic stability, and social cohesion.
- Cyber warfare can also have legal, ethical, and humanitarian implications, such as the violation of sovereignty, human rights, and international law.
- Cyber warfare requires effective cyber defense strategies, such as deterrence, prevention, detection, response, and recovery.
- Cyber warfare is a dynamic and evolving phenomenon that challenges the traditional concepts and norms of warfare.
