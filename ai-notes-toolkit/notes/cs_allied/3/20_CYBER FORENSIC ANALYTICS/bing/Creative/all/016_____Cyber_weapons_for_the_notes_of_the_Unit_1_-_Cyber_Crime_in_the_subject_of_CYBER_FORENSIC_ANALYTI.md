# Cyber weapons

- Cyber weapons are a piece of computer software or hardware used to commit cyberwarfare.
- Cyberwarfare is the use of cyberattacks to disrupt, damage, or destroy the information systems or networks of another nation or organization.
- Cyberattacks are malicious actions that exploit the vulnerabilities of computers, networks, or data, such as hacking, phishing, denial-of-service, ransomware, or spyware.
- Cyber weapons can be classified into two types: **kinetic** and **non-kinetic**.
  - Kinetic cyber weapons are those that cause physical damage or destruction to the target, such as sabotaging a nuclear reactor, disabling a power grid, or crashing a plane.
  - Non-kinetic cyber weapons are those that cause functional or mental harm to the target, such as stealing or manipulating data, spreading propaganda, or influencing public opinion.
- Cyber weapons can be used for various purposes, such as espionage, sabotage, coercion, deterrence, or warfare.
- Cyber weapons pose several challenges and risks, such as attribution, escalation, proliferation, legality, and ethics.
  - Attribution is the difficulty of identifying the source and responsibility of a cyberattack, due to the anonymity and complexity of the cyberspace.
  - Escalation is the possibility of a cyberattack triggering a conventional or nuclear response, due to the lack of norms and rules of engagement in the cyberspace.
  - Proliferation is the spread and availability of cyber weapons to various actors, such as states, terrorists, criminals, or hackers, due to the low cost and high accessibility of the cyberspace.
  - Legality is the uncertainty and ambiguity of the applicability and enforcement of international law and human rights in the cyberspace, due to the novelty and diversity of the cyberattacks.
  - Ethics is the moral and ethical implications and dilemmas of using cyber weapons, such as the proportionality, necessity, and collateral damage of the cyberattacks.
