# Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities

- Cyber laws, more commonly known as internet laws, are laws that are related to legal informatics, regulating the digital distribution of information, e-commerce, software, and information security.
- Cyber laws usually cover many related areas, such as usage and access to the Internet, freedom of speech, and privacy.
- Cyber laws are important to prevent and punish cybercrimes, such as hacking, identity theft, phishing, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, and cyberterrorism .
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, and sometimes from state to state, depending on the jurisdiction and the nature of the cybercrime.
- Cyber laws also involve international cooperation and coordination, as cybercrimes often cross borders and affect multiple parties.
- Cyber authorities are the entities that enforce, regulate, and oversee cyber laws and cybersecurity in a given domain.
- Cyber authorities can be governmental, such as the Department of Homeland Security, the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the National Security Agency, and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency in the United States .
- Cyber authorities can also be non-governmental, such as the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers, the Internet Engineering Task Force, the World Wide Web Consortium, and the Electronic Frontier Foundation.
- Cyber authorities may have different roles and responsibilities, such as issuing certificates, setting standards, conducting audits, investigating incidents, imposing sanctions, and providing guidance and education  .
- Cyber authorities may also collaborate and coordinate with each other, as well as with other stakeholders, such as private sector, academia, civil society, and international organizations, to enhance cybersecurity and cyber resilience  .
