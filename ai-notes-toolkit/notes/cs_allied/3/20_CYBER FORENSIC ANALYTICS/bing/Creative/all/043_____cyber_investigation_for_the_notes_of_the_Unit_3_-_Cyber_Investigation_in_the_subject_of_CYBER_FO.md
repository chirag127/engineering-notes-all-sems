# Cyber Investigation

Cyber investigation is the process of investigating, analyzing, and recovering forensic data for digital evidence of a crime. Cyber investigation can be used to track criminals via the computer, such as in cases of computer crimes, identity theft, cyberstalking, or cyberterrorism . Cyber investigation can also be used to track records of criminals using computer forensics, such as in cases of fraud, money laundering, or homicide .

Some of the objectives of cyber investigation are:

- To identify the source and nature of the cyber attack or crime
- To collect and preserve the digital evidence in a forensically sound manner
- To analyze the digital evidence and reconstruct the events and actions of the cybercriminals
- To attribute the cyber attack or crime to the responsible parties
- To provide the digital evidence and findings to the relevant authorities or stakeholders
- To impose risk and consequences on the cyber adversaries and deter future cyber crimes

Some of the challenges of cyber investigation are:

- The complexity and diversity of the cyber environment and the cyber threats
- The volatility and fragility of the digital evidence
- The jurisdictional and legal issues involved in cross-border cyber crimes
- The ethical and privacy issues involved in accessing and handling the digital evidence
- The shortage of skilled and trained cyber investigators and analysts
- The need for collaboration and coordination among various agencies and sectors involved in cyber investigation

Some of the tools and techniques used in cyber investigation are:

- Digital forensics tools, such as EnCase, FTK, Autopsy, or Cellebrite, that can extract, analyze, and report on the digital evidence from various devices and media
- Network forensics tools, such as Wireshark, Nmap, or Snort, that can capture, monitor, and analyze the network traffic and packets
- Malware analysis tools, such as IDA Pro, OllyDbg, or VirusTotal, that can reverse engineer, debug, and identify the malicious code and its behavior
- Cryptography tools, such as Hashcat, John the Ripper, or OpenSSL, that can encrypt, decrypt, or crack the passwords and keys used in the cyber crimes
- OSINT tools, such as Google, Shodan, or Maltego, that can gather and analyze the open source information and intelligence from the internet and social media
- Cyber threat intelligence tools, such as MISP, ThreatConnect, or Recorded Future, that can collect, process, and share the information and indicators of the cyber threats and actors
