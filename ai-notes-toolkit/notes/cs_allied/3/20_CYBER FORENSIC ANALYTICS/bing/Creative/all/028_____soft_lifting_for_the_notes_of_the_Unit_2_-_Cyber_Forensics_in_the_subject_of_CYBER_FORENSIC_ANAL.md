# Soft Lifting for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

Soft lifting is a term used to describe the unauthorized copying or use of software, data, or digital content without proper permission or license. Soft lifting can be considered a form of digital piracy or theft, and it can have legal and ethical implications for both the perpetrators and the victims. Some examples of soft lifting are:

- Installing a software program on multiple computers or devices without paying for additional licenses.
- Downloading or sharing copyrighted music, movies, books, or games from illegal sources or without paying for them.
- Using someone else's login credentials or subscription to access online services or content.
- Modifying or cracking a software program to bypass its security or functionality features.
- Copying or plagiarizing someone else's work or ideas without giving proper credit or citation.

Cyber forensics is a branch of digital forensics that deals with the investigation and analysis of evidence related to cybercrimes, such as hacking, phishing, malware, identity theft, fraud, or cyberterrorism. Cyber forensics involves the use of various tools and techniques to collect, preserve, examine, and present digital evidence in a manner that is admissible in a court of law. Some of the skills and knowledge required for a career in cyber forensics are:

- Computer and network security: Understanding the principles and practices of securing computer systems and networks from unauthorized access, modification, or damage.
- Operating systems and file systems: Understanding the structure and functionality of different operating systems and file systems, such as Windows, Linux, Mac OS, NTFS, FAT, or EXT.
- Data recovery and analysis: Understanding the methods and tools for recovering and analyzing data from various sources, such as hard drives, memory cards, flash drives, or cloud storage.
- Cryptography and steganography: Understanding the concepts and applications of encryption and decryption, as well as hiding or embedding data within other data, such as images, audio, or video.
- Malware analysis and reverse engineering: Understanding the types and characteristics of malicious software, such as viruses, worms, trojans, or ransomware, and the techniques for dissecting and examining their code and behavior.
- Legal and ethical issues: Understanding the laws and regulations that govern the collection and use of digital evidence, as well as the ethical and professional standards that apply to cyber forensics practitioners.
