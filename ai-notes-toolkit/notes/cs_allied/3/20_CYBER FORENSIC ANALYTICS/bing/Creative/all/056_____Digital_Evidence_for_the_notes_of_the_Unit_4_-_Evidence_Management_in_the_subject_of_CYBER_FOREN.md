# Digital Evidence

- Digital evidence is any information stored or transmitted in binary form that may be relied on in court .
- Digital evidence can be found on various devices, such as computers, hard drives, mobile phones, cameras, etc.
- Digital evidence is often associated with electronic crimes, such as child pornography, credit card fraud, cyberattacks, etc.
- Digital evidence can be classified into four types:
  - **Stored digital evidence**: data that is stored on a device or a medium, such as files, folders, emails, etc.
  - **Transient digital evidence**: data that is temporarily stored in a device's memory, such as cache, RAM, etc.
  - **In transit digital evidence**: data that is being transmitted over a network, such as packets, messages, etc.
  - **Correlated digital evidence**: data that is derived from combining or analyzing other types of digital evidence, such as logs, timelines, etc.
- Digital evidence has some unique characteristics that distinguish it from physical evidence:
  - **Volatile**: digital evidence can be easily altered, deleted, or corrupted by various factors, such as user actions, system operations, environmental conditions, etc.
  - **Replicable**: digital evidence can be easily copied, duplicated, or reproduced without affecting its original state or quality.
  - **Cross-jurisdictional**: digital evidence can be stored or transmitted across different geographical locations, legal systems, or jurisdictions, which may pose challenges for its admissibility or validity in court.
  - **Hidden**: digital evidence can be concealed, encrypted, or obfuscated by various techniques, such as steganography, cryptography, or malware, which may require special tools or skills to recover or analyze.
  - **Massive**: digital evidence can be generated or collected in large quantities, which may require significant storage space, processing power, or time to handle or examine.
