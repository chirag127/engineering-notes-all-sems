### Mobile Payments

Mobile payments are transactions that use mobile devices such as smartphones, tablets, or smartwatches to initiate, authorize, or confirm the payment of goods or services. Mobile payments can be made through various methods, such as mobile wallets, QR codes, NFC, SMS, or biometrics.

Mobile payments offer convenience, speed, and flexibility for consumers and merchants, as well as new opportunities for innovation and inclusion in the digital economy. However, mobile payments also pose significant challenges and risks for cyber security, privacy, and consumer protection.

Some of the cyber crimes that can affect mobile payments are:

- Data breaches: Cyber criminals can hack into mobile payment systems or devices and steal sensitive data such as card numbers, personal information, or transaction records. Data breaches can result in identity theft, fraud, or blackmail. For example, in 2014, hackers stole the data of 56 million customers from Home Depot's payment terminals that used NFC technology.
- Malware: Cyber criminals can infect mobile devices or payment applications with malicious software that can monitor, manipulate, or redirect mobile payments. Malware can also compromise the security features of mobile devices, such as encryption, authentication, or biometrics. For example, in 2019, a malware called EventBot targeted Android devices and stole banking and cryptocurrency information from over 200 mobile applications.
- Phishing: Cyber criminals can use fake emails, texts, or calls to trick mobile users into revealing their payment credentials or clicking on malicious links. Phishing can also exploit the trust and familiarity of mobile users with their contacts or service providers. For example, in 2020, a phishing campaign targeted users of Cash App, a popular mobile payment service, and asked them to verify their accounts by sending money to a fake account.
- SIM swapping: Cyber criminals can impersonate mobile users and request their mobile service providers to transfer their phone numbers to a new SIM card. SIM swapping can allow cyber criminals to bypass the two-factor authentication or verification codes that are sent to mobile devices for mobile payments. For example, in 2018, a hacker used SIM swapping to steal $24 million worth of cryptocurrency from a mobile wallet.

To protect themselves from cyber crimes, mobile users and merchants should:

- Use strong passwords, PINs, or biometrics to lock their mobile devices and payment applications.
- Update their mobile devices and payment applications regularly to fix any security vulnerabilities.
- Avoid using public or unsecured Wi-Fi networks or devices to make mobile payments.
- Verify the identity and legitimacy of the payment recipients, service providers, or contacts before making mobile payments.
- Review their payment transactions and statements regularly and report any suspicious or unauthorized activities.
- Use tokenization, encryption, or other security features that are offered by mobile payment systems or applications. Tokenization is a process that replaces sensitive data with random codes that are useless to cyber criminals. Encryption is a process that scrambles data into unreadable formats that can only be decoded with a key.
