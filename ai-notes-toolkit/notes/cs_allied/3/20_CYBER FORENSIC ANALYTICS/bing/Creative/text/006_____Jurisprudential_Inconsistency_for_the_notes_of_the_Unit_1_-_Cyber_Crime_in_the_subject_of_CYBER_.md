### Jurisprudential Inconsistency for the notes of the Unit 1 - Cyber Crime in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Jurisprudential inconsistency refers to the lack of uniformity or coherence in the legal system or the legal theory that deals with cyber crime and digital forensics.
- Cyber crime challenges the traditional notions of crime and jurisdiction because it is non-local in nature, which throws law enforcement into confusion regarding who should investigate and prosecute the criminal.
- Digital forensics investigations jurisprudence is the theory and philosophy of the study of law and the principles upon which a law is based for digital evidence to appear at court and be legally admissible.
- Digital forensics investigations face various issues of intangibility, complications, and inconsistencies that may affect the validity and reliability of the digital evidence.
- Some of the sources of jurisprudential inconsistency in cyber crime and digital forensics are:
  - The lack of universal or harmonized legal frameworks or standards for cyber crime and digital forensics across different jurisdictions and countries .
  - The rapid evolution and complexity of cyber technologies and digital objects that may outpace the existing laws and regulations .
  - The difficulty of establishing the authenticity, accuracy, completeness, and convincingness of the digital evidence in the court of law .
  - The anomalies, omissions, exaggerations, or other loopholes found in forensic investigations that may contradict the evidence or render it inadmissible .
  - The ethical and social implications of cyber crime and digital forensics that may raise questions of privacy, human rights, and social justice .
