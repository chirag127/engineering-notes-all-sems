### Botnets

- A botnet is a network of compromised computers controlled by attackers, usually for malicious purposes such as spamming, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, stealing sensitive information, or spreading malware .
- Botnet forensics is the science that determines the scope of the breach and applies the methodology to find out the type of the infection. It is an investigation of the botnet attacks that includes a collection of activities like collection, identification, detection, acquisition, and attribution.
- Botnet forensics uses postmortem techniques to collect, identify, detect, examine, analyze, and postmortem document for bot shreds of evidence from digital sources. It uses network security tools to uncover facts related to the cybercrimes specifically on the botnet.
- Botnet forensics can be divided into three phases: malware, botnet forensic investigator, and botnet forensic analyzer.
  - Malware: The first phase is the malware phase. It involves propagation, infection, communication, and attack that are performed by the botnet. The malware phase can be further divided into four subphases: infection vector, command and control (C&C) channel, botnet topology, and botnet payload.
  - Botnet Forensic Investigator: This is the second phase of the botnet forensics framework. This phase focuses on the identification and acquisition of the botnet evidence from the infected machines and the network traffic. The botnet forensic investigator can use various tools and techniques to collect the botnet evidence, such as malware analysis, network traffic analysis, memory forensics, disk forensics, etc.
  - Botnet Forensic Analyzer: This is the third and final phase of the botnet forensics framework. This phase involves the examination and analysis of the botnet evidence to determine the characteristics, behavior, and impact of the botnet. The botnet forensic analyzer can use various methods and tools to analyze the botnet evidence, such as static analysis, dynamic analysis, reverse engineering, data mining, machine learning, etc.
- Botnet forensics can also employ deep learning mechanisms to detect and classify botnet activities in large-scale and complex IoT systems. Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks to learn from data and perform tasks. Deep learning can help botnet forensics to overcome the challenges of scalability, heterogeneity, and dynamism of IoT systems.
- Botnet attacks can have serious consequences for the security and privacy of the victims, as well as the availability and performance of the network and the internet. Some of the biggest botnet attacks to date are:
  - Mirai: Mirai is a botnet that targets IoT devices such as routers, cameras, and DVRs. It exploits the default or weak credentials of these devices and infects them with malware. Mirai was responsible for launching a massive DDoS attack against Dyn, a DNS service provider, in October 2016, which disrupted the access to many popular websites such as Twitter, Netflix, and Reddit.
  - Necurs: Necurs is a botnet that is mainly used for spamming and distributing malware. It is one of the largest and longest-running botnets in history, with millions of infected computers worldwide. Necurs can send billions of spam emails per day, and can also deliver ransomware, banking trojans, and other malicious payloads to the victims.
  - Emotet: Emotet is a botnet that started as a banking trojan, but later evolved into a modular and versatile malware platform. It can steal financial and personal information, download and execute other malware, and spread through network shares and email attachments. Emotet is considered one of the most dangerous and destructive botnets in the world, and has caused millions of dollars of damage to various organizations and individuals.
