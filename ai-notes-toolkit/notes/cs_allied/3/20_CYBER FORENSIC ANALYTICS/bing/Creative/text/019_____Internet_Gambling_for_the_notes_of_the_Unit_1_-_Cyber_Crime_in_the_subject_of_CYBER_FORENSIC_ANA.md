### Internet Gambling

- Internet gambling is the activity of placing bets on sports, games, or contests using a "wire communication facility", which includes the Internet.
- Internet gambling is illegal in many countries, including the United States, where federal law prohibits it under the Wire Act of 1961 .
- Internet gambling is a lucrative industry, with an estimated global revenue of $66.7 billion in 2020.
- Internet gambling is also a potential threat to online security, as cybercriminals can target online gambling platforms and users for various malicious purposes  .
- Some of the cybercrime risks associated with internet gambling are:
  - Compromising online gambling accounts and cashing out balances using brute force, botnets, or credential-stealing malware.
  - Laundering money through online gambling sites using stolen or fake identities, cryptocurrencies, or offshore accounts.
  - Exploiting vulnerabilities in online gambling software or networks to manipulate the outcomes of bets or games.
  - Infecting online gambling users with viruses, Trojan horses, worms, or spyware that can steal personal or financial information, or damage their devices.
  - Extorting online gambling users or operators with ransomware, denial-of-service attacks, or blackmail.
- To prevent or mitigate the cybercrime risks of internet gambling, online gambling users and operators should:
  - Use strong and unique passwords, and change them regularly .
  - Use reputable and licensed online gambling sites, and avoid clicking on suspicious links or pop-ups .
  - Use antivirus software, firewalls, and encryption to protect their devices and data .
  - Avoid sharing personal or financial information with strangers or third parties online .
  - Report any suspicious or fraudulent activity to the authorities or the online gambling site .
