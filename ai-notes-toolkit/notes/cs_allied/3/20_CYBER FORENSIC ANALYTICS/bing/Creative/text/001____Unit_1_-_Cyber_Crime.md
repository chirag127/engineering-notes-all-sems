## Unit 1 - Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime is any criminal activity that involves a computer, a network, or a device connected to the internet.
- Cyber crime can be classified into two categories: cyber-dependent and cyber-enabled.
- Cyber-dependent crimes are those that can only be committed using computers, networks, or other forms of information technology, such as hacking, denial-of-service attacks, malware, ransomware, etc.
- Cyber-enabled crimes are those that are traditional crimes that are facilitated or amplified by the use of computers, networks, or other forms of information technology, such as fraud, identity theft, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, online child exploitation, etc.
- Cyber crime can have various impacts on individuals, organizations, and society, such as financial losses, reputational damage, emotional distress, physical harm, national security threats, etc.
- Cyber crime can be prevented or mitigated by adopting various measures, such as using strong passwords, updating software, avoiding phishing emails, encrypting data, backing up data, reporting incidents, etc.
- Cyber crime can be investigated and prosecuted by various agencies, such as law enforcement, cyber security experts, forensic analysts, prosecutors, etc.
- Cyber crime can be governed by various laws and regulations, such as the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, the Electronic Communications Privacy Act, the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act, the General Data Protection Regulation, etc.
