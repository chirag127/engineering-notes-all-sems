### Network components for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Network components are the devices and software that enable the communication and data exchange between different nodes on a network.
- Some common network components are routers, switches, firewalls, network interface cards (NICs), cables, wireless access points, servers, clients, etc.
- Network components play a vital role in cyber forensics, as they can provide valuable evidence and information about the source, nature, and impact of cyberattacks.
- Network forensics is the capture, recording, and analysis of network packets in order to determine the source of network security attacks.
- Network forensics can help identify the attackers, their methods, their motives, their targets, and their impact on the network and its users.
- Network forensics can also help prevent future attacks by detecting anomalies, vulnerabilities, and malicious activities on the network.
- Network forensics can be performed in two modes: live and post-mortem.
  - Live network forensics involves monitoring and analyzing the network traffic in real-time, using tools such as packet sniffers, network analyzers, intrusion detection systems (IDS), etc.
  - Post-mortem network forensics involves examining the network logs, records, and artifacts that are stored on the network devices or servers, using tools such as log analyzers, forensic software, etc.
- Network forensics faces several challenges, such as the large volume and complexity of network data, the encryption and obfuscation of network traffic, the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of network environments, the legal and ethical issues of network monitoring, etc .
- Network forensics requires a systematic and rigorous approach, following the steps of identification, preservation, collection, examination, analysis, and presentation of network evidence.
- Network forensics is one of the key components in a defense team’s arsenal, as it can help draw several conclusions during an investigation, and support the prosecution or defense of cybercrime cases  .
