### Secure electronic records for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Secure electronic records are digital documents or data that are protected from unauthorized access, modification, disclosure, or destruction by applying appropriate security measures and controls.
- Cyber laws are the legal frameworks and regulations that govern the use, security, and privacy of information and communication technologies (ICTs) in cyberspace.
- Cyber laws cover various aspects of ICTs, such as intellectual property, contract, jurisdiction, data protection, privacy, and freedom of expression.
- Cyber laws also provide legal recognition and validity to electronic records and digital signatures, as well as prescribe the duties and liabilities of various entities involved in the creation, transmission, storage, and processing of electronic records.
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, depending on the level of development, culture, and political system of each nation. However, there are also some international conventions and agreements that aim to harmonize and coordinate the cyber laws of different countries, such as the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).
- Cyber authorities are the governmental or non-governmental organizations or agencies that are responsible for implementing, enforcing, and overseeing the cyber laws and policies of a country or a region. Cyber authorities may also provide guidance, education, and awareness on cyber issues to the public and the private sector.
- Cyber authorities may have different names, functions, and powers, depending on the legal system and the organizational structure of each country or region. Some examples of cyber authorities are the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) in the United States, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) in India, and the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) in the European Union.
- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of scientific methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and present digital evidence from electronic records and devices in order to support or refute a hypothesis or a claim in a legal or investigative context.
- Cyber forensic analytics involves various steps, such as identification, preservation, extraction, examination, analysis, and reporting of digital evidence. Cyber forensic analytics also requires the use of specialized tools, software, and hardware to perform the tasks efficiently and accurately.
- Cyber forensic analytics can be used for various purposes, such as criminal investigation, civil litigation, regulatory compliance, internal audit, fraud detection, incident response, and threat intelligence. Cyber forensic analytics can also help in identifying and mitigating the risks and vulnerabilities of ICT systems and networks.
