### Circumstantial evidence

- Circumstantial evidence is evidence that **indirectly** proves a fact or a connection between facts by **inference** or **reasoning**.
- Circumstantial evidence is **not** direct evidence that **explicitly** shows the existence or non-existence of a fact or a connection between facts.
- Circumstantial evidence is often used in **cyber forensics** to establish the **identity**, **intent**, **motive**, **opportunity**, **means**, or **alibi** of a suspect or a perpetrator of a cybercrime.
- Circumstantial evidence can be derived from various sources of **digital evidence**, such as **computer hard drives**, **mobile phones**, **network logs**, **social media posts**, **emails**, **cookies**, **metadata**, **encryption keys**, **digital signatures**, etc.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **corroborated** or **contradicted** by other evidence, such as **eyewitness testimony**, **physical evidence**, **expert opinion**, **confession**, etc.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **classified** into different types, such as **physical circumstantial evidence**, **biological circumstantial evidence**, **behavioral circumstantial evidence**, **trace circumstantial evidence**, **locational circumstantial evidence**, etc.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **evaluated** based on its **relevance**, **reliability**, **sufficiency**, **weight**, and **admissibility** in court.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **challenged** by the defense based on its **source**, **collection**, **preservation**, **analysis**, **interpretation**, or **presentation** in court.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **strengthened** by using **scientific methods**, **forensic tools**, **standard procedures**, **chain of custody**, **documentation**, **peer review**, and **expert testimony** in court.
