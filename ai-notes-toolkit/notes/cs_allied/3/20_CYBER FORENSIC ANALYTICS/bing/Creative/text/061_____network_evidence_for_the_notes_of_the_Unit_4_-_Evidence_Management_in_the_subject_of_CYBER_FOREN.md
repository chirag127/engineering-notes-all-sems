### Network Evidence

Network evidence is the information that is collected and analyzed from a networked environment to investigate a cybercrime or a cyberattack. Network evidence can be used to establish the source, nature, and impact of the malicious activity, as well as to identify the perpetrators and their motives. Network evidence can also be used to support legal actions or to prevent future attacks.

Some of the sources of network evidence are:

- **Network traffic data**: This is the data that is transmitted or received over a network, such as packets, frames, segments, or datagrams. Network traffic data can be captured and recorded by various tools, such as network sniffers, packet analyzers, or network monitors. Network traffic data can reveal the details of the communication between the attacker and the victim, such as the IP addresses, ports, protocols, payloads, timestamps, and flags. Network traffic data can also show the patterns, anomalies, or signatures of the attack, such as the frequency, volume, or type of the traffic. Network traffic data can be analyzed using various techniques, such as filtering, decoding, parsing, or reconstructing  .

- **Network devices**: These are the hardware components that are used to connect, route, or manage the network, such as routers, switches, firewalls, or intrusion detection systems (IDS). Network devices can store or generate various logs, records, or alerts that can provide valuable information about the network activity, such as the configuration, status, performance, or events of the devices. Network devices can also be compromised or tampered by the attacker, such as by changing the settings, installing malware, or deleting evidence. Network devices can be examined using various methods, such as physical inspection, memory analysis, or firmware analysis .

- **Network applications**: These are the software programs that are used to provide or access network services, such as web browsers, email clients, or chat applications. Network applications can contain or produce various data, files, or messages that can be relevant to the network investigation, such as the content, metadata, or history of the network communication. Network applications can also be exploited or manipulated by the attacker, such as by injecting code, stealing credentials, or altering data. Network applications can be analyzed using various tools, such as file viewers, hex editors, or hash calculators .

- **Network users**: These are the human actors that are involved or affected by the network activity, such as the attacker, the victim, or the witness. Network users can provide or possess various information, documents, or testimonies that can be useful for the network investigation, such as the identity, location, or motive of the network actors. Network users can also be deceived or coerced by the attacker, such as by phishing, social engineering, or blackmailing. Network users can be interviewed, interrogated, or surveilled using various techniques, such as questioning, profiling, or monitoring .

Network evidence is a type of digital evidence, which is information stored or transmitted in binary form that may be relied on in court. Therefore, network evidence must be collected, preserved, and analyzed following the principles and standards of digital forensics, such as the chain of custody, the integrity, the authenticity, and the admissibility of the evidence. Network evidence must also be presented and reported in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, using appropriate formats, tools, and methods .
