### DDoS Attacks

- A DDoS attack is a distributed denial-of-service attack, which is a malicious attempt to disrupt the normal traffic of a targeted server, service or network by overwhelming the target or its surrounding infrastructure with a flood of Internet traffic.
- A DDoS attack is different from a simple denial-of-service (DoS) attack, which is launched from a single source and can be easily blocked or mitigated. A DDoS attack involves multiple compromised computer systems, called bots or zombies, that act as sources of attack traffic. These systems are often infected with malware that allows the attacker to control them remotely.
- A DDoS attack can have various goals, such as exhausting the target's bandwidth, CPU, memory, disk space, or network resources, disrupting the target's application logic, or exploiting vulnerabilities in the target's software or hardware. A DDoS attack can also be used as a diversionary tactic to hide other malicious activities, such as data theft, ransomware, or sabotage.
- A DDoS attack can be classified into three main categories, based on the layer of the OSI model that they target: volumetric, protocol, and application layer attacks.
  - Volumetric attacks aim to consume the target's bandwidth by sending a large amount of traffic, such as UDP packets, ICMP requests, or spoofed IP packets. These attacks can be amplified by using reflection or amplification techniques, which involve sending requests to third-party servers that generate larger responses to the target.
  - Protocol attacks aim to exploit weaknesses in the network protocols, such as TCP, UDP, or IP, by sending malformed or incomplete packets, or by opening and closing connections repeatedly. These attacks can exhaust the target's connection tables, firewalls, load balancers, or other network devices.
  - Application layer attacks aim to disrupt the target's specific application or service, such as HTTP, DNS, or SMTP, by sending requests that consume the target's processing resources, or by exploiting vulnerabilities in the application's code or logic. These attacks can be more difficult to detect and mitigate, as they mimic legitimate traffic and require less bandwidth.
- A DDoS attack can be prevented or mitigated by using various techniques, such as filtering, rate limiting, blackholing, scrubbing, or deploying DDoS protection services. These techniques can help to identify and block malicious traffic, reduce the impact of the attack, or redirect the traffic to a different location for analysis and cleaning.
