### Disk characteristics for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Disk forensics is a branch of cyber forensics that deals with extracting data from storage media by searching active, modified, or deleted files  .
- Disk forensics can be applied to various types of storage media, such as hard disk drives, solid state drives, optical disks, flash drives, memory cards, etc.
- Disk forensics can help recover evidence of cybercrimes, such as malware infection, data theft, unauthorized access, fraud, etc.
- Disk forensics can also help recover lost or damaged data due to accidental deletion, formatting, corruption, or physical damage.
- Disk forensics involves the following steps:
  - Acquiring the disk image: This is the process of creating an exact copy of the original disk or its contents, without altering or modifying it. The disk image can be acquired by using hardware or software tools, such as write blockers, forensic duplicators, or imaging software.
  - Analyzing the disk image: This is the process of examining the disk image to identify and extract relevant data, such as files, folders, partitions, file systems, metadata, etc. The analysis can be done by using various tools, such as hex editors, file viewers, carving tools, etc.
  - Reporting the findings: This is the process of documenting and presenting the results of the analysis, such as evidence, artifacts, timelines, etc. The report should be clear, concise, and accurate, and should follow the legal and ethical standards of the forensic field.
- Disk forensics faces some challenges, such as encryption, compression, steganography, anti-forensics, etc., that can hinder the acquisition or analysis of the disk image . Therefore, disk forensic experts need to have adequate knowledge and skills to overcome these challenges and perform a thorough and reliable investigation.
