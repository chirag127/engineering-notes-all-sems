### Real evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Real evidence is any physical object that can be directly observed or examined by the court .
- Real evidence can be used to prove or disprove a fact or a connection between a suspect and a crime .
- Real evidence can also be used to demonstrate how a crime was committed or what the impact of the crime was .
- Examples of real evidence in cyber forensics are:
  - The actual computer, hard drive, mobile phone, or other device that contains digital evidence  .
  - The fingerprints, DNA, or other biometric data of the suspect or the victim that are found on the device or its peripherals .
  - The physical documents, notes, or other materials that are related to the digital evidence or the crime .
  - The tools, weapons, or other items that are used to commit the crime or to access the device .
- Real evidence must be properly collected, preserved, and analyzed by cyber forensic experts to ensure its integrity, reliability, and admissibility in court  .
- Real evidence must be documented with a chain of custody that records its origin, location, transfer, and handling by authorized personnel  .
- Real evidence must be stored in a secure and controlled environment that prevents any unauthorized access, alteration, or damage  .
- Real evidence must be presented in a clear and understandable manner that explains its relevance, significance, and limitations to the judge and the jury  .
