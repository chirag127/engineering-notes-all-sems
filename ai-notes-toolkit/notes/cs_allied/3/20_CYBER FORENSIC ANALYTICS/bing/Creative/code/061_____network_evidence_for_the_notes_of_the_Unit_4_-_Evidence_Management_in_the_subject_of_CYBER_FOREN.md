### Network Evidence

Network evidence is the information that is collected and analyzed from a networked environment to investigate a cybercrime or a cyberattack. Network evidence can be used to establish the source, nature, and impact of the malicious activity, as well as to identify the perpetrators and their motives. Network evidence can also be used to support legal actions or to prevent future attacks.

Some of the sources of network evidence are:

- **Network traffic data**: This is the data that is transmitted and received over a network, such as packets, frames, segments, and messages. Network traffic data can be captured and recorded by various tools and devices, such as network analyzers, firewalls, intrusion detection systems, routers, switches, and servers. Network traffic data can reveal the details of the communication between the attacker and the victim, such as the IP addresses, ports, protocols, payloads, timestamps, and flags. Network traffic data can also show the patterns and anomalies of the network activity, such as the frequency, volume, duration, and direction of the traffic. Network traffic data can be analyzed using various techniques, such as packet filtering, packet decoding, packet reconstruction, packet carving, and packet correlation  .

- **Network logs**: These are the records of the events that occur on a network, such as the connections, sessions, transactions, requests, responses, errors, and alerts. Network logs can be generated and stored by various tools and devices, such as network operating systems, applications, services, firewalls, intrusion detection systems, routers, switches, and servers. Network logs can provide the metadata of the network activity, such as the date, time, user, device, action, status, and outcome. Network logs can also show the evidence of the attacker's actions, such as the commands, queries, inputs, outputs, and modifications. Network logs can be analyzed using various techniques, such as log parsing, log filtering, log searching, log aggregation, log correlation, and log visualization .

- **Network devices**: These are the physical or virtual components that are used to connect, transmit, receive, process, and store network data, such as computers, mobile phones, tablets, routers, switches, servers, firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and storage devices. Network devices can contain the evidence of the attacker's presence, access, and activity on the network, such as the configuration, status, memory, cache, registry, files, folders, processes, services, and applications. Network devices can also contain the evidence of the victim's data, such as the documents, images, videos, audio, emails, messages, and passwords. Network devices can be analyzed using various techniques, such as device imaging, device cloning, device hashing, device acquisition, device examination, and device recovery .
