### Regulation of certifying authorities

- A certifying authority (CA) is an entity that issues digital certificates to verify the identity and authenticity of the users of electronic transactions.
- The Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act) has established a regulatory framework for the CAs in India.
- The IT Act empowers the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) to license and supervise the CAs and to lay down the standards and conditions for their functioning.
- The CCA also maintains the Root Certifying Authority of India (RCAI), which is the apex trust anchor in the Indian public key infrastructure (PKI).
- The CAs are required to follow the provisions of the IT Act and the rules and regulations made thereunder, such as:
  - The Certifying Authorities Rules, 2000, which prescribe the eligibility criteria, application procedure, license fee, security guidelines, and obligations of the CAs.
  - The Information Technology (Certifying Authority) Regulations, 2001, which specify the operational and technical requirements for the CAs, such as the format and contents of the digital certificates, the certification practice statement, the audit and inspection procedures, and the revocation and suspension of the digital certificates.
  - The Information Technology (Security Procedure) Rules, 2004, which lay down the security standards and procedures for the CAs, such as the encryption and decryption methods, the key management, the backup and recovery, and the disaster management.
- The CAs are also subject to the oversight and enforcement actions of the CCA, such as the issuance of directions, the imposition of penalties, the cancellation of license, and the prosecution for offences under the IT Act.
