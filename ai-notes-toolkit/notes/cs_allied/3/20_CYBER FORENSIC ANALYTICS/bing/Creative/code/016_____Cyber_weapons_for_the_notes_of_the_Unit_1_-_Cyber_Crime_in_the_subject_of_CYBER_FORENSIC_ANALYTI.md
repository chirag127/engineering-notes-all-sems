# Cyber weapons

- Cyber weapons are a piece of computer software or hardware used to commit cyberwarfare.
- Cyberwarfare is the use of cyberattacks to damage, disrupt, or destroy the information systems or networks of another nation or organization.
- Cyberattacks are malicious actions that exploit the vulnerabilities of computers, networks, or data, such as hacking, phishing, denial-of-service, ransomware, or spyware.
- Cyber weapons can be classified into two types: **kinetic** and **non-kinetic**.
  - Kinetic cyber weapons are those that cause physical damage or destruction to the target, such as sabotaging a nuclear reactor, a power grid, or a military vehicle.
  - Non-kinetic cyber weapons are those that cause functional or mental harm to the target, such as stealing or manipulating data, disrupting communications, or influencing public opinion.
- Cyber weapons can be used for various purposes, such as espionage, sabotage, coercion, deterrence, or warfare.
- Cyber weapons pose several challenges and risks, such as attribution, escalation, proliferation, legality, and ethics.
  - Attribution is the process of identifying the source and responsibility of a cyberattack, which can be difficult due to the anonymity and complexity of the cyber domain.
  - Escalation is the possibility of a cyberattack triggering a more severe or violent response from the target or a third party, which can lead to a larger conflict or war.
  - Proliferation is the spread of cyber weapons to other actors, such as rogue states, terrorists, or criminals, who may use them for malicious or destructive purposes.
  - Legality is the question of whether cyber weapons and cyberattacks are governed by existing international laws and norms, such as the laws of war, human rights, or sovereignty.
  - Ethics is the question of whether cyber weapons and cyberattacks are morally acceptable or justified, considering the potential harm they can cause to innocent civilians, critical infrastructure, or the environment.
