# ATM Frauds

ATM frauds are a type of cyber crime that involves stealing or compromising the debit card or personal identification number (PIN) of the users of automated teller machines (ATMs). ATM frauds can result in financial losses, identity theft, and other criminal activities. Some of the common types of ATM frauds are:

- **Skimming**: This is the process of installing a counterfeit device on the card reader or the keypad of the ATM that can capture the data on the card's magnetic strip and the user's PIN. The device can be a small camera, a fake overlay, or a wireless transmitter. The criminals can then use the stolen data to clone the card or make online purchases .
- **Shimming**: This is similar to skimming, but the device is inserted inside the card slot of the ATM, where it can intercept the data from the chip of the card. The device can be a thin metal or plastic shim with a microchip and flash storage. The criminals can then retrieve the device and use the data to create a magnetic strip version of the card.
- **Card trapping**: This is the process of inserting a device or a mechanism into the card slot of the ATM that can prevent the card from being ejected after the transaction. The device can be a metal loop, a spring, or a plastic sleeve. The criminals can then wait for the user to leave the ATM and retrieve the card, or they can distract the user and take the card while they are still at the ATM.
- **Cash trapping**: This is the process of attaching a device or a mechanism to the cash dispenser of the ATM that can prevent the cash from being dispensed or retrieved by the user. The device can be a metal plate, a lever, or a claw. The criminals can then wait for the user to leave the ATM and collect the cash, or they can distract the user and take the cash while they are still at the ATM.
- **PIN cracking**: This is the process of using software or hardware tools to guess or bypass the PIN of the user. The tools can be cracking programs, brute force attacks, or keyloggers. The criminals can then use the PIN to access the user's account or make unauthorized transactions.
- **Phishing**: This is the process of sending fraudulent emails, text messages, or phone calls to the user that claim to be from the bank or the ATM provider and ask for the user's card or account details, PIN, or security codes. The criminals can then use the information to access the user's account or make unauthorized transactions.
- **Bitcoin ATMs**: This is the process of using bitcoin ATMs, which are machines that allow users to buy or sell bitcoins using cash or debit cards, for criminal purposes. The criminals can use the bitcoin ATMs to launder money, evade taxes, or conduct illegal transactions, such as drug trafficking or fraud. The criminals can also target the users of the bitcoin ATMs by installing skimming devices, trapping devices, or hacking the machines.
