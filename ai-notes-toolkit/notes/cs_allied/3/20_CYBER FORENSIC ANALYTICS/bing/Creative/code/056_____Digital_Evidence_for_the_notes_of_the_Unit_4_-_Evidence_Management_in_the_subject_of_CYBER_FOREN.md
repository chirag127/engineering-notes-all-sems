### Digital Evidence

- Digital evidence is any information stored or transmitted in binary form that may be relied on in court .
- Digital evidence can be found on various devices, such as computers, hard drives, mobile phones, cameras, etc.
- Digital evidence can be associated with different types of crimes, such as cybercrime, fraud, terrorism, child abuse, etc.
- Digital evidence can be classified into four categories:
  - Volatile: data that is stored in memory and can be lost when the device is powered off, such as RAM, cache, etc.
  - Non-volatile: data that is stored on a permanent or semi-permanent storage medium and can be preserved when the device is powered off, such as hard disk, flash drive, etc.
  - Transient: data that is transmitted over a network and can be intercepted, captured or modified, such as packets, messages, etc.
  - Static: data that is stored on a read-only medium and cannot be altered, such as CD-ROM, DVD, etc.
- Digital evidence can be collected, preserved, analyzed and presented using various tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, carving, encryption, etc.
- Digital evidence can be challenged in court based on its authenticity, integrity, reliability and admissibility.
- Digital evidence can be affected by various factors, such as human error, malicious tampering, environmental damage, technical failure, etc.
- Digital evidence can be managed using a systematic process that involves identification, collection, preservation, examination, analysis, reporting and presentation.
- Digital evidence can be subject to various standards, guidelines, policies and laws, such as ISO/IEC 27037, NIST 800-86, SWGDE, etc.
