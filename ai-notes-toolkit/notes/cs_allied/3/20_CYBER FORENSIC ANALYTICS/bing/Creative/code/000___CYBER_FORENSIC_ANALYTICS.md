# CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of data analysis techniques and tools to investigate cyber incidents and collect digital evidence for legal purposes .
- Cyber forensic analytics involves the following steps:
  - Identification: Identifying the sources and scope of the cyber incident and the potential evidence to be collected.
  - Preservation: Isolating and securing the evidence from tampering, alteration, or destruction.
  - Extraction: Extracting the relevant data from the evidence using forensic tools and techniques.
  - Analysis: Analyzing the data to identify patterns, anomalies, indicators, and attribution of the cyber incident.
  - Presentation: Presenting the findings and conclusions in a clear and concise manner that can be understood by the intended audience and can withstand legal scrutiny.
- Cyber forensic analytics requires the use of various tools and technologies, such as:
  - Forensic software: Software that can perform tasks such as disk imaging, file recovery, data carving, malware analysis, memory analysis, network analysis, etc.
  - Forensic hardware: Hardware that can facilitate the acquisition and examination of evidence, such as write blockers, forensic duplicators, forensic workstations, etc.
  - Forensic standards: Standards that can ensure the reliability, validity, and admissibility of the evidence, such as ISO/IEC 27037, ISO/IEC 27041, ISO/IEC 27042, etc.
- Cyber forensic analytics can be applied to various domains and scenarios, such as  :
  - Cybercrime: Investigating crimes that involve the use of computers, networks, or digital devices, such as hacking, fraud, identity theft, cyberstalking, etc.
  - Cybersecurity: Detecting and preventing cyberattacks that target the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information systems, such as malware, denial-of-service, phishing, ransomware, etc.
  - Cyber intelligence: Gathering and analyzing information about the capabilities, intentions, and activities of cyber adversaries, such as nation-states, terrorists, hackers, etc.
  - Cyber law: Applying and enforcing the legal and ethical principles and regulations that govern the use of cyberspace, such as privacy, intellectual property, cyberwarfare, etc.
