### Evidence Management

- Evidence management is the process of protecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a cyber forensic investigation .
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and selecting potential sources of digital evidence, such as computers, mobile devices, networks, cloud services, etc. 
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting the digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, logging, etc. 
  - Preservation: ensuring the integrity, authenticity and continuity of the digital evidence by preventing any alteration, loss or contamination, such as using write blockers, encryption, seals, etc. 
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting the digital evidence to extract relevant information and insights, such as using software, hardware, algorithms, etc. 
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the findings and opinions on the digital evidence to the relevant stakeholders, such as using charts, graphs, tables, etc. 
- Evidence management requires following the best practices and standards of the digital forensics field, such as the Scientific Working Group on Digital Evidence (SWGDE), the International Organization on Computer Evidence (IOCE), the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), etc. 
- Evidence management is important for the security of the digital infrastructure, as it can help to identify, prevent, respond and prosecute cybercrimes, such as hacking, fraud, theft, espionage, etc.
