### Profiling for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Profiling is an investigative technique that aims to identify the major personality and behavioral characteristics of the offender based on the analysis of the crime.
- Profiling can be useful in cybercrime investigations, where the identity and location of the offender may be unknown or difficult to trace.
- Profiling can help to narrow down the pool of suspects, generate leads, provide investigative strategies, and assist in interrogation and prosecution.
- Profiling can also help to understand the motives, methods, and targets of the cybercriminals, as well as their level of skill, sophistication, and experience.
- Profiling can be based on different sources of information, such as digital evidence, online behavior, communication patterns, and geographic locations  .
- Profiling can be classified into two types: idiographic and nomothetic.
  - Idiographic profiling is an in-depth analysis of a specific subject's digital footprints, such as browsing history, social media activity, and online transactions, to reveal information that can help identify or better understand the subject.
  - Nomothetic profiling is a general analysis of a group of subjects with similar characteristics, such as age, gender, education, and occupation, to create a profile that can be applied to a population of potential offenders.
- Profiling can be performed using different methods, such as case-based reasoning, machine learning, statistical analysis, and geographic profiling .
  - Case-based reasoning is a method that compares the current case with previous similar cases to find the most likely solution or outcome.
  - Machine learning is a method that uses algorithms to learn from data and make predictions or classifications.
  - Statistical analysis is a method that uses mathematical techniques to summarize, describe, and infer patterns or relationships from data.
  - Geographic profiling is a method that uses the locations of the crime scenes to estimate the most probable area of the offender's residence or activity.
