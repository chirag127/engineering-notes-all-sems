### Types of Evidence in Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence from electronic devices and networks. There are different types of evidence that can be found in digital media, depending on the nature and source of the data. Some of the common types of evidence in cyber forensics are:

- **Logs**: Logs are records of events or activities that occur on a system or a network. They can include operating system logs, application logs, web server logs, firewall logs, router logs, etc. Logs can provide information about the actions, identities, locations and timestamps of the users or devices involved in an incident .
- **Video footage and images**: Video footage and images are visual data that can capture the scenes or activities of interest. They can include surveillance cameras, dash cams, body cams, webcams, mobile phones, etc. Video footage and images can provide evidence of the presence, appearance, behavior and movements of the suspects or victims.
- **Archives**: Archives are collections of files or data that are compressed, encrypted or organized in a specific format. They can include zip files, rar files, tar files, iso files, etc. Archives can contain evidence of the contents, sources, destinations and modifications of the data.
- **Active data**: Active data is the data that is readily accessible and visible to the user or the system. It can include files, folders, documents, emails, messages, etc. Active data can provide evidence of the creation, deletion, modification and usage of the data .
- **Metadata**: Metadata is the data that describes or provides information about other data. It can include file names, file sizes, file types, file dates, file permissions, etc. Metadata can provide evidence of the attributes, properties and history of the data .
- **Residual data**: Residual data is the data that is not easily accessible or visible to the user or the system. It can include deleted files, temporary files, swap files, cache files, etc. Residual data can provide evidence of the previous states, traces and remnants of the data .
- **Volatile data**: Volatile data is the data that is stored in the memory or the cache of the system or the device. It can include processes, services, network connections, open files, etc. Volatile data can provide evidence of the current state, activity and configuration of the system or the device .
- **Replicant data**: Replicant data is the data that is copied, duplicated or replicated from the original source. It can include backups, clones, images, snapshots, etc. Replicant data can provide evidence of the integrity, consistency and authenticity of the data .

These are some of the types of evidence that can be found in cyber forensics. Each type of evidence has its own characteristics, challenges and methods of collection, preservation, analysis and presentation. A cyber forensics examiner should be familiar with the different types of evidence and how to handle them properly and effectively.
