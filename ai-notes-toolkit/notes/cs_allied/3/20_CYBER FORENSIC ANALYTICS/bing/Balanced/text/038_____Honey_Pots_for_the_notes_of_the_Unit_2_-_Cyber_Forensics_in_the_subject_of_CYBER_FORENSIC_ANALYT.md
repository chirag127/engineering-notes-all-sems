### Honey Pots

- A **honeypot** is a **cybersecurity mechanism** that uses a **manufactured attack target** to lure cybercriminals away from legitimate targets, and also gathers intelligence about the identity, methods and motivations of adversaries .
- A honeypot can be modeled after any digital asset, including software applications, servers or the network itself .
- Honeypots are typically configured to look like attractive targets to attackers—such as servers running vulnerable versions of operating systems or databases with easy passwords.
- When attackers exploit the honeypot, their activities are monitored and recorded.
- Honeypots can be used to **attract, detect, and deflect** cybercriminals from hacking into legitimate targets.
- Honeypots can also serve **forensic purposes**, quietly gathering data about the hackers who stole it.
- Honeypots can be classified into different types based on their level of interaction, purpose, and deployment.
  - **Low-interaction honeypots** provide minimal services and functionality to the attackers, and are easier to deploy and maintain, but offer less information and realism.
  - **High-interaction honeypots** provide full services and functionality to the attackers, and are harder to deploy and maintain, but offer more information and realism.
  - **Research honeypots** are used to study the behavior and techniques of attackers, and are usually deployed by academic or security organizations.
  - **Production honeypots** are used to protect the network and assets of an organization, and are usually deployed by businesses or government agencies.
  - **Pure honeypots** are real systems that are exposed to the attackers, and are not isolated or monitored by any other system.
  - **Honeynets** are networks of honeypots that simulate a real network environment, and are isolated and monitored by a separate system.
