### Network components for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Network forensics is a science that centers on the discovery and retrieval of information surrounding a cybercrime within a networked environment.
- Network forensics can help draw several conclusions during an investigation, such as the source, target, motive, and impact of cyberattacks.
- Network forensics involves the capture, recording and analysis of network packets in order to establish the evidence of cyberattacks.
- Network forensics can be performed in two modes: live and post-mortem.
  - Live network forensics involves monitoring and analyzing network traffic in real-time, using tools such as network sniffers, intrusion detection systems, and firewalls.
  - Post-mortem network forensics involves examining network logs, backups, and other data sources after an incident has occurred, using tools such as network analyzers, packet reconstructors, and forensic software.
- Network forensics requires a systematic approach that follows the following steps:
  - Identification: The first step is to identify the scope, nature, and source of the network incident, and to preserve the integrity of the evidence.
  - Collection: The second step is to collect the relevant network data, such as packets, logs, and files, using appropriate tools and methods.
  - Examination: The third step is to examine the collected data, using various techniques such as filtering, sorting, searching, and decoding, to extract useful information.
  - Analysis: The fourth step is to analyze the extracted information, using various methods such as correlation, comparison, and reconstruction, to establish the facts and hypotheses of the incident.
  - Reporting: The fifth step is to report the findings and conclusions of the analysis, using clear, concise, and accurate language, and to present the evidence in a logical and structured manner.
- Network forensics faces several challenges, such as the large volume and complexity of network data, the dynamic and volatile nature of network traffic, the encryption and obfuscation of network packets, and the legal and ethical issues of network monitoring and data privacy .
- Network forensics is one of the key components of digital forensics, which is the broader discipline of collecting, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in court.
- Digital forensics is commonly associated with electronic crime, such as child pornography or credit card fraud, but it can also be used to investigate other types of crimes, such as terrorism, espionage, or murder, that involve digital devices or data.
- Digital forensics can be divided into several branches, such as computer forensics, mobile forensics, cloud forensics, and network forensics, depending on the type and location of the digital evidence.
- Digital forensics is closely related to incident response, which is the process of identifying, containing, eradicating, and recovering from cyberattacks, and preventing or minimizing their recurrence.
