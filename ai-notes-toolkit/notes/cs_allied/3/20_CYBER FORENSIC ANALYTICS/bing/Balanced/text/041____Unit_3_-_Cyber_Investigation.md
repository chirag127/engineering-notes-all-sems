## Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation

- Cyber investigation is the process of collecting, analyzing, and preserving digital evidence from various sources, such as computers, networks, mobile devices, cloud services, and social media platforms.
- Cyber investigation can be conducted for different purposes, such as criminal investigation, civil litigation, intelligence gathering, incident response, or cyber security auditing.
- Cyber investigation involves various steps, such as:
  - Planning and preparation: defining the scope, objectives, and legal authority of the investigation, identifying the sources and types of digital evidence, and obtaining the necessary tools and resources.
  - Evidence acquisition: locating, accessing, and collecting digital evidence from various sources, using appropriate methods and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, logging, and documentation.
  - Evidence analysis: examining, interpreting, and correlating digital evidence, using various tools and techniques, such as file system analysis, network analysis, malware analysis, and data recovery.
  - Evidence presentation: reporting and presenting the findings and conclusions of the investigation, using various formats and methods, such as written reports, oral testimony, charts, graphs, and timelines.
- Cyber investigation faces various challenges, such as:
  - Volume and complexity: digital evidence can be large, diverse, and dynamic, requiring specialized skills and tools to handle and analyze.
  - Volatility and fragility: digital evidence can be easily altered, deleted, or corrupted, requiring careful handling and preservation.
  - Privacy and legality: digital evidence can contain sensitive and personal information, requiring compliance with ethical and legal standards and regulations.
  - Adversaries and countermeasures: digital evidence can be manipulated, hidden, or encrypted by malicious actors, requiring advanced techniques and methods to detect and overcome.
