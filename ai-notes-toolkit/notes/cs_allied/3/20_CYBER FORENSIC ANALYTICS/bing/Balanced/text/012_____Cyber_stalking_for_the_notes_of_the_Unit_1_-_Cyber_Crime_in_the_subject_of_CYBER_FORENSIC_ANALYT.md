### Cyber stalking

- Cyber stalking is the repeated use of electronic communications to harass or frighten someone, for example by sending threatening emails.
- Cyber stalking can take many forms, such as harassment, embarrassment, humiliation, defamation, slander, libel, monitoring, identity theft, threats, vandalism, solicitation for sex, doxing, or blackmail.
- Cyber stalking is a criminal offense under various state anti-stalking, slander and harassment laws. A conviction can result in a restraining order, probation, or criminal penalties against the assailant, including jail.
- Cyber stalking is different from cyberbullying, which is the use of electronic communication to bully someone, typically by sending messages of an intimidating or threatening nature. Cyberbullying is more common among children and teenagers, while cyberstalking is more common among adults.
- Cyber stalking can have serious psychological and emotional effects on the victim, such as anxiety, depression, fear, paranoia, isolation, low self-esteem, and post-traumatic stress disorder.
- Cyber stalking can be prevented or stopped by taking some measures, such as blocking or reporting the stalker, changing passwords and privacy settings, saving evidence of the harassment, informing friends and family, contacting the authorities, and seeking professional help .
