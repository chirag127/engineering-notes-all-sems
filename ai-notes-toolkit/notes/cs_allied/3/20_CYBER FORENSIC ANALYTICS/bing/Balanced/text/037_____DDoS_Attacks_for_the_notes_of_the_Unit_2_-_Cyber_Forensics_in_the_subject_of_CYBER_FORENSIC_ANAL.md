### DDoS Attacks

- A DDoS attack is a **distributed denial-of-service** attack that targets websites and servers by disrupting network services.
- A DDoS attack attempts to **exhaust** an application's resources by sending multiple requests to it, so large and massive that the service becomes **unavailable**.
- A DDoS attack works by using multiple **exploited** machines as a source to attack network traffic. These machines are called **botnets** and they are controlled by the attacker .
- DDoS attacks are one of the powerful cyber weapons threat actors use today. They can cause significant **damage** and **loss** to the victims, such as financial losses, reputation damage, customer dissatisfaction, and legal consequences.
- Cyber forensics can help **investigate** and **analyze** DDoS attacks by collecting and examining network traffic data, identifying the source and nature of the attack, determining the intent and extent of the breach, and providing evidence for legal actions .
- The forensic procedure in investigating DDoS attacks may include the following steps:
  - **Preparation**: Define the scope and objectives of the investigation, obtain the necessary tools and authorization, and document the process and findings.
  - **Preservation**: Isolate and secure the affected network and devices, and collect and store the network traffic data in a forensically sound manner.
  - **Analysis**: Examine the network traffic data using various tools and techniques, such as packet capture, flow analysis, signature analysis, anomaly detection, and traceback methods, to identify the characteristics and patterns of the attack, such as the type, duration, frequency, intensity, and origin of the attack.
  - **Reporting**: Summarize and present the findings and conclusions of the analysis, and provide recommendations and suggestions for mitigation and prevention of future attacks.
