### CERNTin for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- CERNTin stands for **Cyber Emergency Response Team International**. It is a global network of national and sectoral computer security incident response teams (CSIRTs) that cooperate and coordinate to respond to cyber threats and incidents.
- CERNTin was established in 2003 by the European Network and Information Security Agency (ENISA) and the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to facilitate information sharing and collaboration among CSIRTs across the world.
- CERNTin aims to improve the global cyber resilience by enhancing the capabilities and cooperation of CSIRTs, promoting best practices and standards, and providing training and awareness programs.
- CERNTin operates under the following principles:
  - Voluntary participation and mutual trust
  - Respect for the sovereignty and jurisdiction of each country
  - Protection of the confidentiality and integrity of the shared information
  - Compliance with the applicable laws and regulations
- CERNTin has four main functions:
  - Information exchange: CERNTin facilitates the exchange of technical and operational information among CSIRTs, such as alerts, advisories, indicators of compromise, and mitigation strategies.
  - Coordination: CERNTin coordinates the response to cross-border or large-scale cyber incidents that affect multiple countries or sectors, such as denial-of-service attacks, ransomware, or cyber espionage.
  - Support: CERNTin provides support to CSIRTs in need of assistance, such as technical expertise, tools, or resources, to handle cyber incidents or improve their capabilities.
  - Outreach: CERNTin promotes the awareness and understanding of cyber threats and incidents among the public and private sectors, as well as the general public, through publications, events, and campaigns.
- CERNTin has a steering committee that oversees its strategic direction and governance, and a secretariat that manages its daily operations and activities. CERNTin also has working groups that focus on specific topics or issues, such as malware analysis, cybercrime, or critical infrastructure protection.
- CERNTin is not a legal entity or a formal organization, but rather a network of trusted partners that collaborate on a voluntary basis. CERNTin does not have any enforcement or regulatory powers, nor does it interfere with the national sovereignty or jurisdiction of its members.
- CERNTin is one of the key actors in the global cyber security landscape, along with other international organizations, such as the United Nations, the International Telecommunication Union, the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe, and the North Atlantic Treaty Organization. CERNTin works closely with these organizations to harmonize and complement their efforts and initiatives.
