# ATM Frauds

ATM frauds are a type of cyber crime that involves stealing money or personal information from ATM users. ATM frauds can have serious consequences for the victims, such as financial losses, identity theft, or compromised accounts. Some of the common types of ATM frauds are:

- **Skimming**: This is when criminals attach a device to the ATM card reader that can capture the data on the magnetic strip of the card. They may also install a hidden camera or a fake keypad to record the PIN of the user. The criminals can then use the stolen data to clone the card or make online transactions.
- **Shimming**: This is similar to skimming, but the device is inserted inside the card slot of the ATM, where it can intercept the data from the chip of the card. The device is usually very thin and hard to detect. The criminals can then use the stolen data to create a fake card or make online transactions.
- **Card trapping**: This is when criminals insert a device or a substance into the card slot of the ATM that can prevent the card from being ejected. The user may think that the card is stuck or swallowed by the machine, and may leave the ATM or call for help. The criminals can then retrieve the card and use it to withdraw money or make online transactions.
- **Cash trapping**: This is when criminals attach a device or a substance to the cash dispenser of the ATM that can prevent the cash from being dispensed. The user may think that the ATM is out of order or has no cash, and may leave the ATM or call for help. The criminals can then collect the cash from the device.
- **Phishing**: This is when criminals send fake emails, text messages, or phone calls to the ATM users, pretending to be from the bank or the ATM provider. They may ask the users to provide their card details, PIN, or other personal information, or to click on a link that can install malware on their devices. The criminals can then use the stolen information or malware to access the users' accounts or make online transactions.
- **Bitcoin ATMs**: This is when criminals target the users of bitcoin ATMs, which are machines that allow the exchange of cash for cryptocurrency or vice versa. They may use various methods, such as posing as law enforcement officials, tax authorities, or utility companies, and demanding the users to pay them in bitcoin through the ATM. They may also use fake QR codes, malware, or counterfeit devices to steal the users' cryptocurrency or cash.

Some of the ways to prevent or avoid ATM frauds are:

- **Inspect the ATM**: Before using an ATM, check for any signs of tampering, such as loose or damaged parts, unfamiliar devices, or stickers. If you notice anything suspicious, do not use the ATM and report it to the bank or the ATM provider.
- **Cover the keypad**: When entering your PIN, use your hand or a shield to cover the keypad, so that any hidden camera or fake keypad cannot record it. Do not share your PIN with anyone, and change it regularly.
- **Be alert**: When using an ATM, be aware of your surroundings, and avoid any distractions, such as strangers offering help, phone calls, or messages. If you feel unsafe or uncomfortable, cancel the transaction and leave the ATM. Do not leave your card or cash unattended, and take your receipt with you.
- **Use secure ATMs**: Prefer to use ATMs that are located in well-lit, busy, and monitored areas, such as inside banks, malls, or stores. Avoid using ATMs that are isolated, unfamiliar, or in poor condition.
- **Monitor your accounts**: Check your account statements and online banking regularly, and report any unauthorized or suspicious transactions to your bank or card issuer. If you lose your card or suspect that it has been compromised, notify your bank or card issuer and request a replacement or a block.
- **Be cautious with bitcoin ATMs**: If you use bitcoin ATMs, make sure you understand how they work, and verify the legitimacy of the ATM provider and the transaction. Do not send bitcoin to anyone you do not know or trust, and do not fall for any scams that ask you to pay in bitcoin for taxes, fines, bills, or other reasons. Do not scan any QR codes that are not displayed on the ATM screen, and do not download any software or apps that are not authorized by the ATM provider.
