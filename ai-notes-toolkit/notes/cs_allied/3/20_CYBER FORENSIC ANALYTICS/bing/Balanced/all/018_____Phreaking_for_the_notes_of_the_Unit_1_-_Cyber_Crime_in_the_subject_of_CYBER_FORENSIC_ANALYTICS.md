# Phreaking

- Phreaking is a slang term coined to describe the activity of a culture of people who study, experiment with, or explore telecommunication systems, such as equipment and systems connected to public telephone networks.
- Phreaking involves fraudulent manipulation of telephone signaling in order to make free phone calls or to access restricted services.
- Phreaking originated in the 1960s and 1970s, when phreaks used devices such as whistles or electronic boxes to generate specific tones that could trigger the automatic switches of the phone network .
- Phreaking was also motivated by curiosity, exploration, and the challenge of understanding and manipulating complex systems.
- Phreaking techniques include:
  - Blue boxing: using a device that produces a 2600 Hz tone to seize control of a trunk line and route calls anywhere in the world .
  - Red boxing: using a device that simulates the sound of coins being deposited in a payphone to make free calls .
  - Black boxing: using a device that prevents the phone line from disconnecting after the called party hangs up, allowing the caller to make another call without paying .
  - Beige boxing: using a device that connects to a phone line outside a building and allows the user to make or receive calls from that line .
  - Green boxing: using a device that generates the tones used by operators to control phone lines and services .
- Phreaking is considered a form of cybercrime, as it violates the laws and regulations of the telecommunication providers and authorities .
- Phreaking can also pose security risks, as phreaks can intercept, monitor, or tamper with phone calls, voice mail, or other services.
- Phreaking has become less common and less effective in the modern era, as the phone network has switched from analog to digital technology, and the signaling system has become more secure and encrypted .
- Phreaking is still possible in some areas where analog technology is still in use, or where digital systems have vulnerabilities or backdoors .
- Phreaking is also related to other forms of hacking, such as social engineering, spoofing, or wardialing .
