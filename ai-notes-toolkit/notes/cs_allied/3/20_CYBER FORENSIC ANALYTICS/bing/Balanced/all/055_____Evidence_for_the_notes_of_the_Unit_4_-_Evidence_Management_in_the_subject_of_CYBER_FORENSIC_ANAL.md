# Evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence management is the process of protecting, preserving, and presenting digital evidence in a cyber forensic investigation.
- Evidence management involves the following steps :
  - Identification: finding and selecting potential sources of digital evidence related to the cybercrime, such as computers, mobile phones, networks, etc.
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting the digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, logging, etc.
  - Preservation: ensuring the integrity and authenticity of the digital evidence by preventing any alteration, loss, or contamination, such as using write blockers, encryption, seals, etc.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting the digital evidence to extract relevant information and insights, such as using software, hardware, or manual methods, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the findings and conclusions of the digital evidence analysis to the relevant stakeholders, such as using charts, graphs, tables, etc.
  - Disposition: storing, transferring, or destroying the digital evidence according to the legal and ethical requirements, such as using secure storage, chain of custody, or disposal methods, etc.
- Evidence management is important for the security of the cyber forensic analytics because  :
  - It helps to recover, analyze, and preserve digital evidence that can be used to identify, prosecute, and prevent cybercrimes, such as identity theft, phishing, hacking, etc.
  - It helps to establish the credibility and reliability of the digital evidence in a court of law by following the standards and procedures of the forensic science.
  - It helps to protect the rights and privacy of the victims, suspects, and witnesses involved in the cybercrime by ensuring the confidentiality and legality of the digital evidence.
