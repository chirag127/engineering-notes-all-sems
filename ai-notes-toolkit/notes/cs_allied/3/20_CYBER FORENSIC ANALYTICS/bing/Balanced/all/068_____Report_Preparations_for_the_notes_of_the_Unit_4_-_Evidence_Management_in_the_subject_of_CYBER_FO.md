# Report Preparations for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a forensically sound manner.
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: The process of finding and recognizing potential sources of digital evidence, such as devices, files, logs, etc.
  - Collection: The process of acquiring digital evidence from the identified sources, using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, copying, etc.
  - Preservation: The process of protecting digital evidence from alteration, loss, or damage, using methods such as encryption, authentication, backup, etc.
  - Analysis: The process of examining digital evidence to extract relevant information, such as timelines, artifacts, patterns, etc.
  - Presentation: The process of reporting and communicating the results of the analysis, using formats such as charts, graphs, tables, etc.
- Evidence management requires following certain standards and guidelines, such as:
  - Chain of custody: The documentation of the history and handling of digital evidence, including who, what, when, where, why, and how.
  - Legal admissibility: The compliance of digital evidence with the rules and requirements of the court or jurisdiction, such as relevance, reliability, authenticity, etc.
  - Best practices: The application of the principles and methods that are widely accepted and recommended by the forensic community, such as NIST, ISO, etc.
- Evidence management faces various challenges and issues, such as:
  - Volume: The large and increasing amount of digital data that needs to be collected and analyzed, which can overwhelm the available resources and tools.
  - Variety: The diverse and complex types and formats of digital data that need to be interpreted and understood, which can require specialized skills and knowledge.
  - Volatility: The dynamic and transient nature of digital data that can change or disappear over time, which can affect the availability and integrity of evidence.
  - Validation: The verification and evaluation of the quality and accuracy of digital evidence, which can involve testing, comparison, and peer review.
