## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence from electronic devices, such as computers, smartphones, or networks, in a manner that is legally admissible and scientifically sound.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- The principles and objectives of cyber forensics
- The types and sources of digital evidence
- The steps and tools involved in cyber forensic investigations
- The legal and ethical issues related to cyber forensics
- The challenges and future trends of cyber forensics

### Principles and objectives of cyber forensics

The main principles of cyber forensics are:

- To preserve the integrity and authenticity of digital evidence
- To follow a systematic and documented methodology
- To adhere to the relevant laws and standards
- To maintain the confidentiality and privacy of the data and the parties involved

The main objectives of cyber forensics are:

- To identify the sources and perpetrators of cybercrimes or incidents
- To recover and reconstruct the data and events related to the cybercrimes or incidents
- To analyze and interpret the data and events to establish facts and motives
- To present and report the findings and conclusions in a clear and convincing manner

### Types and sources of digital evidence

Digital evidence is any data that is stored or transmitted by electronic devices, such as computers, smartphones, or networks, that can be used to support or refute a hypothesis or a claim in a legal or investigative context.

Some of the types of digital evidence are:

- Volatile data: Data that is stored in the memory or cache of a device, such as RAM, CPU registers, or network packets, that can be easily lost or altered when the device is powered off or rebooted
- Non-volatile data: Data that is stored in the permanent or semi-permanent storage of a device, such as hard disks, flash drives, or optical disks, that can be retained or recovered even when the device is powered off or rebooted
- Transient data: Data that is temporarily stored or transmitted by a device, such as cookies, browser history, or email messages, that can be deleted or overwritten by the user or the system
- Residual data: Data that is left behind or hidden by a device, such as deleted files, slack space, or encryption keys, that can be recovered or extracted by using special techniques or tools

Some of the sources of digital evidence are:

- Computers and peripherals: Devices that are used to process, store, or communicate data, such as desktops, laptops, keyboards, or printers
- Mobile devices and accessories: Devices that are used to perform various functions, such as calling, texting, browsing, or gaming, such as smartphones, tablets, smartwatches, or headphones
- Networks and servers: Devices that are used to connect, transmit, or host data, such as routers, switches, firewalls, or web servers
- Cloud and online services: Platforms that are used to store, access, or share data over the internet, such as email, social media, or cloud storage
- Digital media and content: Files that are used to store or display data, such as images, videos, audio, or documents
- Digital traces and metadata: Data that is used to record or describe the characteristics, activities, or locations of a device, such as logs, timestamps, or geotags

### Steps and tools involved in cyber forensic investigations

The steps involved in cyber forensic investigations are:

- Preparation: The phase where the investigator defines the scope, objectives, and plan of the investigation, and acquires the necessary resources, tools, and permissions
- Collection: The phase where the investigator identifies, locates, and acquires the digital evidence from the devices or sources, and documents the process and the chain of custody
- Examination: The phase where the investigator extracts, verifies, and organizes the digital evidence from the devices or sources, and applies various techniques or tools to recover or reveal the data
- Analysis: The phase where the investigator interprets, correlates, and evaluates the digital evidence, and applies various methods or tools to establish facts and motives
- Reporting: The phase where the investigator summarizes, presents, and explains the findings and conclusions of the investigation, and prepares the necessary reports and documentation

Some of the tools involved in cyber forensic investigations are:

- Imaging tools: Tools that are used to create an exact copy or a snapshot of the data from a device or source, such as dd, FTK Imager, or EnCase
