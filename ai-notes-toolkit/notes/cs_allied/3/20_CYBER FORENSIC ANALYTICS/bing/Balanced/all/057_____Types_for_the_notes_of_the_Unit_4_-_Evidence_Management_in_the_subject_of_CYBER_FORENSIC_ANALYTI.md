# Types of Evidence

Evidence is any information that can be used to support a claim or a hypothesis. Evidence can be classified into different types based on its source, nature, and relevance. Some of the common types of evidence are:

- **Physical evidence**: This is any tangible object or material that can be collected, examined, or analyzed. Physical evidence can include fingerprints, DNA, weapons, tools, documents, etc. Physical evidence can provide direct or circumstantial proof of a crime or an incident.

- **Digital evidence**: This is any data or information that is stored or transmitted in digital form. Digital evidence can include files, emails, texts, images, videos, logs, etc. Digital evidence can be extracted from various devices, such as computers, smartphones, cameras, etc. Digital evidence can provide direct or circumstantial proof of a crime or an incident.

- **Testimonial evidence**: This is any statement or declaration made by a person who has knowledge or experience of a crime or an incident. Testimonial evidence can include eyewitness accounts, confessions, alibis, expert opinions, etc. Testimonial evidence can provide direct or circumstantial proof of a crime or an incident.

- **Documentary evidence**: This is any written or printed material that can be used to support a claim or a hypothesis. Documentary evidence can include reports, records, contracts, certificates, etc. Documentary evidence can provide direct or circumstantial proof of a crime or an incident.

- **Demonstrative evidence**: This is any visual or auditory representation that can be used to illustrate or explain a claim or a hypothesis. Demonstrative evidence can include diagrams, charts, graphs, maps, models, animations, etc. Demonstrative evidence can provide direct or circumstantial proof of a crime or an incident.

- **Trace evidence**: This is any small or microscopic material that can be transferred from one person or object to another during a crime or an incident. Trace evidence can include hair, fibers, soil, dust, pollen, etc. Trace evidence can provide direct or circumstantial proof of a crime or an incident.
