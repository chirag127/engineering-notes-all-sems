# Cyber Forensic Analytics

- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of data analysis techniques and tools to investigate cyber incidents and collect digital evidence for legal purposes.
- Cyber forensic analytics involves the following steps:
  - Identification: The process of identifying the sources and types of digital evidence relevant to a cyber incident, such as devices, networks, logs, files, etc.
  - Preservation: The process of securing and maintaining the integrity of digital evidence, such as by creating copies, hashes, or encryption.
  - Extraction: The process of accessing and retrieving digital evidence from various sources, such as by using forensic software, hardware, or cloud services.
  - Analysis: The process of examining and interpreting digital evidence, such as by using data visualization, anomaly detection, pattern recognition, or machine learning.
  - Reporting: The process of presenting and documenting the findings and conclusions of the analysis, such as by using charts, graphs, tables, or narratives.
- Cyber forensic analytics can help address various challenges and objectives, such as:
  - Detecting and responding to cyberattacks, such as malware, ransomware, phishing, denial-of-service, etc.
  - Identifying and attributing cybercriminals, such as hackers, fraudsters, terrorists, etc.
  - Recovering and reconstructing lost or damaged data, such as files, emails, passwords, etc.
  - Protecting and enhancing cybersecurity, such as by improving network defense, security awareness, or risk management.
  - Supporting and complying with legal and regulatory requirements, such as by preserving chain of custody, admissibility, or privacy.
