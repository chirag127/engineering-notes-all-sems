# Evidence collection for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence collection is the process of identifying, preserving, and acquiring digital data that can be used in cyber forensic investigations or legal proceedings .
- Evidence collection involves following the principles of digital forensics, such as:
  - Adhering to the legal and ethical standards of the jurisdiction and the organization.
  - Maintaining the integrity and authenticity of the evidence by using proper tools and techniques, and documenting the chain of custody.
  - Minimizing the alteration or damage to the original evidence by using write blockers, imaging devices, and anti-virus software.
  - Analyzing the evidence in a forensically sound manner by using appropriate software and hardware, and following the standard operating procedures.
  - Reporting the findings in a clear, concise, and accurate manner by using proper terminology, evidence labels, and references  .
- Evidence collection can be performed on various types of digital devices and media, such as:
  - Computers and laptops, which may contain hard drives, solid state drives, optical disks, flash drives, memory cards, and other storage devices.
  - Mobile phones and tablets, which may contain SIM cards, internal memory, external memory, and cloud data.
  - Network devices and servers, which may contain routers, switches, firewalls, logs, and configuration files.
  - Internet and web-based applications, which may contain emails, social media, instant messages, browsing history, and cookies  .
- Evidence collection can be performed in different scenarios and environments, such as:
  - Live forensics, which involves collecting evidence from a running system or device, without shutting it down or disconnecting it from the network. This may be necessary to capture volatile data, such as RAM, cache, and network connections, or to avoid encryption or deletion of data. However, this may also pose risks of altering or losing data, or triggering anti-forensic mechanisms.
  - Dead forensics, which involves collecting evidence from a powered-off or disconnected system or device, after creating a forensic image or a copy of the original data. This may be preferable to preserve the integrity and authenticity of the evidence, or to comply with the legal or organizational requirements. However, this may also result in missing some volatile or encrypted data, or facing technical challenges in accessing or analyzing the data.
  - Remote forensics, which involves collecting evidence from a system or device that is located in a different physical location, by using network or cloud-based tools and techniques. This may be useful to overcome geographical or logistical constraints, or to perform covert or proactive investigations. However, this may also raise legal or ethical issues, such as jurisdiction, consent, and privacy  .
