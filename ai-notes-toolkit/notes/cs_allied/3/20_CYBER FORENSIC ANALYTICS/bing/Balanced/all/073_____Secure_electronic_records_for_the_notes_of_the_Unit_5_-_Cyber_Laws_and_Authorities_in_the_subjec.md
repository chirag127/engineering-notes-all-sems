# Secure electronic records for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Secure electronic records are digital documents or data that are protected from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction by using encryption, authentication, or other security measures.
- Cyber laws are the legal rules and regulations that govern the use, creation, and distribution of electronic records and information in cyberspace.
- Cyber laws aim to prevent and punish cybercrimes, such as hacking, phishing, identity theft, cyberstalking, cyberterrorism, and cyberwarfare, as well as to protect the rights and interests of the users, providers, and regulators of electronic records and information.
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, depending on the jurisdiction, culture, and level of development of each nation. However, some common principles and frameworks of cyber laws include:
  - The recognition and validity of electronic contracts, signatures, and records, as well as the evidentiary value and admissibility of electronic records in courts of law.
  - The protection of intellectual property rights, such as copyrights, patents, trademarks, and trade secrets, in cyberspace.
  - The regulation of e-commerce, online transactions, and consumer protection, such as the disclosure of terms and conditions, privacy policies, and security measures, as well as the prevention of fraud, spam, and unfair trade practices.
  - The safeguarding of data privacy, confidentiality, and security, such as the collection, processing, storage, and transfer of personal and sensitive data, as well as the notification and consent of the data subjects, and the rights and obligations of the data controllers and processors.
  - The enforcement of cyber ethics, netiquette, and online responsibility, such as the respect for human dignity, freedom of expression, and cultural diversity, as well as the prohibition of cyberbullying, cyberhate, and cyberharassment.
  - The establishment of cyber authorities, agencies, and institutions, such as the national and international bodies, committees, and organizations that are responsible for the formulation, implementation, and oversight of cyber laws, policies, and standards, as well as the coordination, cooperation, and collaboration among different stakeholders, sectors, and jurisdictions in cyberspace.
- Some examples of cyber laws and authorities in different countries and regions are:
  - The Information Technology Act 2000 and the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) in India.
  - The Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 2002 and the Cybersecurity Hub in South Africa.
  - The Computer Misuse Act 1990 and the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) in the United Kingdom.
  - The Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act 2000 and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) in the United States.
  - The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) in the European Union.
  - The Convention on Cybercrime and the Cybercrime Convention Committee (T-CY) in the Council of Europe.
  - The Budapest Convention on Cybercrime and the Cybercrime Convention Committee (T-CY) in the Council of Europe.
