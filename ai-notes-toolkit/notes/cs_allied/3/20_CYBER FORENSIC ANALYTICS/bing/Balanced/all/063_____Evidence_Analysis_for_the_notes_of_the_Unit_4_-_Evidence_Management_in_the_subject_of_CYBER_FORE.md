# Evidence Analysis

Evidence analysis is the process of examining and interpreting the digital evidence collected from a cybercrime scene. Evidence analysis aims to answer questions such as:

- What happened?
- When did it happen?
- Who was involved?
- How did it happen?
- Why did it happen?

Evidence analysis involves applying various techniques and tools to extract, recover, correlate, and present the relevant information from the digital evidence. Evidence analysis can be performed at different levels, such as:

- Physical level: This involves analyzing the physical characteristics of the evidence, such as the type, model, serial number, and condition of the device or media.
- Logical level: This involves analyzing the logical structure of the evidence, such as the file system, partitions, directories, files, and metadata.
- Application level: This involves analyzing the application-specific data and artifacts, such as the documents, emails, internet history, and registry entries.
- Network level: This involves analyzing the network-related data and artifacts, such as the IP addresses, MAC addresses, protocols, packets, and logs.

Evidence analysis should follow the principles of cyber forensics, such as:

- Preservation: The evidence should be preserved in its original state and protected from any alteration, damage, or loss.
- Authentication: The evidence should be verified for its authenticity and integrity, and any changes or modifications should be documented and justified.
- Chain of custody: The evidence should be tracked and accounted for throughout the investigation, and any transfers or handlings should be recorded and authorized.
- Admissibility: The evidence should be collected and analyzed in a manner that complies with the legal and ethical standards and can be presented in court.

Evidence analysis should also follow a systematic and structured approach, such as:

- Planning: The evidence analysis should be planned based on the objectives, scope, and resources of the investigation, and the available tools and techniques.
- Preparation: The evidence analysis should be prepared by acquiring the necessary equipment, software, and documentation, and creating a suitable environment and backup for the analysis.
- Examination: The evidence analysis should be performed by applying the appropriate techniques and tools to the evidence, and documenting the findings and observations.
- Analysis: The evidence analysis should be analyzed by interpreting the findings and observations, and drawing conclusions and inferences.
- Reporting: The evidence analysis should be reported by presenting the results and recommendations, and supporting them with the evidence and documentation.
