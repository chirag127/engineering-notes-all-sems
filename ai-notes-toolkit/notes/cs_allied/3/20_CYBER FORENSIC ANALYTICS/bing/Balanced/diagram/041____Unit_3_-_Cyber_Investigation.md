## Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation

Cyber investigation is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence related to cybercrime or cyber incidents. Cyber investigation can be conducted by law enforcement agencies, private sector organizations, or individuals.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- **Cybercrime and cyber incidents**: Cybercrime is any illegal activity that involves a computer, a network, or a device connected to the internet. Cyber incidents are any events that compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information systems or data. Examples of cybercrime and cyber incidents are hacking, phishing, malware, denial-of-service attacks, data breaches, identity theft, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, etc.
- **Cyber investigation process**: Cyber investigation process consists of four main phases: preparation, collection, examination, and reporting. Preparation involves planning, scoping, and obtaining legal authority for the investigation. Collection involves identifying, acquiring, and preserving digital evidence from various sources, such as devices, networks, cloud services, social media, etc. Examination involves analyzing and interpreting the digital evidence using various tools and techniques, such as forensic software, data carving, hashing, encryption, etc. Reporting involves documenting and presenting the findings and conclusions of the investigation in a clear, concise, and accurate manner.
- **Cyber investigation challenges**: Cyber investigation faces many challenges, such as the volatility, diversity, and complexity of digital evidence, the large volume and variety of data, the encryption and obfuscation of data, the jurisdictional and legal issues, the ethical and privacy issues, the skills and resources gap, the collaboration and coordination issues, etc.
- **Cyber investigation best practices**: Cyber investigation best practices are the guidelines and standards that help ensure the quality, reliability, and admissibility of digital evidence and the investigation process. Some of the best practices are following the chain of custody, using write blockers, verifying the integrity of evidence, documenting the steps and actions, using validated and tested tools, following the legal and ethical principles, etc.
