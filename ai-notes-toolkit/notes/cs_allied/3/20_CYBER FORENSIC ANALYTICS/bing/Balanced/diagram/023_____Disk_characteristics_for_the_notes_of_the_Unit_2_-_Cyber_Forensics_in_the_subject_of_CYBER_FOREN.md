### Disk Characteristics

- A disk is a physical device that stores data on one or more platters, which are circular disks coated with a magnetic material.
- A disk has two main characteristics: capacity and performance.
- Capacity is the amount of data that can be stored on a disk, measured in bytes (B), kilobytes (KB), megabytes (MB), gigabytes (GB), terabytes (TB), etc.
- Performance is the speed at which data can be read from or written to a disk, measured in revolutions per minute (RPM), milliseconds (ms), megabytes per second (MB/s), etc.
- Some factors that affect the capacity and performance of a disk are:
  - The number and size of platters: more platters and larger platters can store more data, but may also increase the weight and power consumption of the disk.
  - The number and density of tracks: tracks are concentric circles on a platter where data is stored. More tracks and higher density tracks can store more data, but may also increase the seek time and latency of the disk.
  - The number and size of sectors: sectors are the smallest units of data that can be read or written on a disk. More sectors and smaller sectors can store more data, but may also increase the overhead and fragmentation of the disk.
  - The interface and cache: the interface is the connection between the disk and the computer, such as SATA, SCSI, USB, etc. The cache is a small amount of memory on the disk that stores frequently accessed data. A faster interface and a larger cache can improve the performance of the disk, but may also increase the cost and complexity of the disk.

- A disk can be classified into different types based on its technology, such as:
  - Hard disk drive (HDD): a disk that uses a spinning platter and a moving read/write head to store and access data. HDDs are the most common and affordable type of disk, but they are also prone to mechanical failure, noise, and heat.
  - Solid state drive (SSD): a disk that uses flash memory chips to store and access data. SSDs are faster, quieter, and more reliable than HDDs, but they are also more expensive and have a limited number of write cycles.
  - Optical disk: a disk that uses a laser beam to read and write data on a reflective surface, such as a CD, DVD, or Blu-ray. Optical disks are cheap and portable, but they have lower capacity and performance than HDDs and SSDs, and they can be easily scratched or damaged.
  - Hybrid disk: a disk that combines the features of HDDs and SSDs, such as a HDD with a SSD cache or a SSD with a HDD backup. Hybrid disks can offer a balance between cost, capacity, and performance, but they may also have compatibility and reliability issues.
