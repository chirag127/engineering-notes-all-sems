### Real evidence

- Real evidence is any physical object that can be directly observed or examined by the court or the jury .
- Real evidence can be used to prove or disprove a fact or a connection between a suspect and a crime .
- Real evidence can also be used to corroborate or contradict other types of evidence, such as testimonial or documentary evidence .
- Examples of real evidence in cyber forensics are:
  - The actual computer, device, or storage media that contains digital evidence .
  - The fingerprints, DNA, or other biometric data of the suspect or the victim found on the computer or device .
  - The physical damage or alteration of the computer or device caused by the suspect or the crime .
- Real evidence must be properly collected, preserved, and analyzed by cyber forensics experts following the principles of identification, acquisition, examination, and presentation  .
- Real evidence must also be authenticated and admissible in court, meaning that it must be relevant, reliable, and not prejudicial .
