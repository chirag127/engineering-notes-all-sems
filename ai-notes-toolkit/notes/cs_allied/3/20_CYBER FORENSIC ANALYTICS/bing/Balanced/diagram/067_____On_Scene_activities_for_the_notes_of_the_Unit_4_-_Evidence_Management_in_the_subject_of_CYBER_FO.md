### On Scene Activities for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

- On scene activities are the actions and procedures that a cyber forensic analyst performs at the location where a cyber incident or a cybercrime has occurred or is suspected to have occurred.
- The main objectives of on scene activities are to identify, preserve, collect, document, and analyze digital evidence that can help in the investigation and prosecution of the cyber incident or crime.
- Some of the common on scene activities are:

  - Securing the scene and preventing unauthorized access or tampering with the digital devices and networks involved in the incident or crime.
  - Identifying the potential sources and types of digital evidence, such as computers, mobile phones, cloud services, network devices, logs, etc.
  - Preserving the digital evidence by making copies or images of the data and devices, using write blockers or other tools to prevent data alteration, and maintaining the chain of custody and integrity of the evidence.
  - Collecting the digital evidence by following the standard operating procedures and best practices, using appropriate tools and techniques, and labeling and packaging the evidence properly.
  - Documenting the digital evidence by recording the details of the scene, the devices, the evidence, the actions taken, the tools used, and the personnel involved, using forms, reports, photographs, sketches, etc.
  - Analyzing the digital evidence by using specialized software and methods to extract, interpret, correlate, and present the relevant information and findings from the evidence.

- On scene activities require a high level of technical skills, analytical skills, and investigative skills, as well as a solid understanding of the legal and ethical aspects of cyber forensics. A cyber forensic analyst must follow the principles of accuracy, completeness, reliability, and admissibility when conducting on scene activities.
