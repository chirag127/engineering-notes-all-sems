### Trojans and Backdoors

- Trojans and backdoors are types of malware used to infect and compromise computer systems.
- A Trojan is a malicious program disguised as something benign. In many cases, the Trojan appears to perform a desirable function for the user but actually allows a hacker access to the user’s computer system.
- A backdoor is a hidden or undocumented way of accessing a computer system, bypassing the normal authentication mechanisms. A backdoor can be created by hackers using trojans that already present on a device. A backdoor trojan, once triggered, allows hackers to take control of the infected device remotely. They may steal, receive, and delete files, reset the device, and install malware, among other hazardous behaviours.
- Trojans and backdoors are different from viruses and worms in that they do not self-replicate or spread to other systems. They rely on social engineering or other methods to trick users into running them.
- Trojans and backdoors pose a serious threat to the security and privacy of computer users and organizations. They can be used to conduct cyberattacks, espionage, sabotage, or identity theft. They can also compromise the integrity and availability of data and systems.
- Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence related to cybercrimes. Cyber forensics can help identify, investigate, and prosecute the perpetrators of trojans and backdoors, as well as recover the lost or damaged data and systems.
- Cyber forensics involves various techniques and tools to detect, extract, and analyze trojans and backdoors from infected devices. Some of the common techniques are:
  - Static analysis: examining the code or binary of the suspected program to determine its functionality, origin, and behavior.
  - Dynamic analysis: executing the suspected program in a controlled environment (such as a sandbox or a virtual machine) to observe its runtime behavior, network activity, and system changes.
  - Memory analysis: dumping and examining the contents of the memory of the infected device to find traces of the trojan or backdoor, such as processes, files, registry keys, or network connections.
  - Network analysis: capturing and analyzing the network traffic of the infected device to identify the communication channels, protocols, and commands used by the trojan or backdoor.
- Cyber forensics also involves various challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Anti-forensics: techniques used by hackers to evade, obstruct, or destroy the forensic analysis, such as encryption, obfuscation, deletion, or overwriting of data.
  - Trojan defense: a legal defense strategy used by suspects to claim that they were not responsible for the cybercrime, but rather a victim of a trojan or backdoor that was planted on their device by someone else.
  - Legal and ethical issues: the need to comply with the laws and regulations of different jurisdictions, as well as the ethical principles of privacy, consent, and proportionality, when conducting the forensic analysis and using the evidence.
