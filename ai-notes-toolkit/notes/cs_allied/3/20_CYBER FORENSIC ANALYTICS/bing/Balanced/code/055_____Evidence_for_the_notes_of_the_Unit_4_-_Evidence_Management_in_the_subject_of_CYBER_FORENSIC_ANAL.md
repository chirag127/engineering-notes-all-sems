### Evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence management is the process of protecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a cyber forensic investigation.
- Digital evidence is information stored or transmitted in binary form that may be relied on in court. It can be found on a computer hard drive, a mobile phone, among other places.
- The objectives of evidence management are to:
  - Recover, analyze and preserve digital evidence in a manner that maintains its integrity and admissibility in court.
  - Track the chain of custody of digital evidence from the scene of the crime to the courtroom.
  - Postulate the motive, identity and modus operandi of the cybercriminals.
  - Support the prosecution or defense of a cybercrime case.
- The steps of evidence management are:
  - Identification: finding and recognizing potential sources of digital evidence in a cybercrime scene.
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting digital evidence using proper tools and techniques.
  - Preservation: securing and storing digital evidence in a way that prevents unauthorized access, modification or deletion.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting digital evidence using specialized software and methods.
  - Presentation: reporting and presenting digital evidence in a clear and concise manner that is understandable to the court.
- The challenges of evidence management are:
  - The volatility and fragility of digital evidence, which can be easily altered or destroyed by malicious or accidental actions.
  - The diversity and complexity of digital devices, formats and protocols, which require different tools and skills to handle.
  - The large and growing volume of digital data, which can overwhelm the storage and processing capabilities of forensic investigators.
  - The legal and ethical issues of digital evidence, such as privacy, consent, jurisdiction and admissibility.
