### Secure electronic records for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Secure electronic records are digital documents or data that are protected from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction by using encryption, authentication, or other security measures.
- Cyber laws are the legal rules and regulations that govern the use of information and communication technologies (ICTs) in cyberspace, such as the internet, mobile devices, cloud computing, and social media.
- Cyber laws cover various aspects of ICTs, such as intellectual property, contract, jurisdiction, data protection, privacy, and freedom of expression.
- Cyber laws also provide legal recognition to e-documents, e-signatures, e-contracts, and e-commerce transactions, as long as they comply with certain security standards.
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, depending on the national legislation, policies, and practices. Some examples of cyber laws in different countries are:
  - The Information Technology Act 2000 (IT Act) in India, which is the primary law that deals with cybercrime, cybersecurity, and e-governance in the country. The IT Act also defines the roles and responsibilities of various authorities, such as the Central Government, the State Governments, the Certifying Authorities, the Controller of Certifying Authorities, the Adjudicating Officers, and the Cyber Appellate Tribunal.
  - The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act 1996 (HIPAA) in the United States, which is the main law that regulates the privacy and security of electronic protected health information (e-PHI) in the health care sector. The HIPAA Security Rule establishes a national set of security standards for protecting e-PHI that is held or transferred in electronic form by covered entities and business associates.
  - The General Data Protection Regulation 2016 (GDPR) in the European Union, which is the comprehensive law that harmonizes the data protection and privacy rules across the EU member states. The GDPR applies to any organization that processes personal data of individuals in the EU, regardless of where the organization is located. The GDPR also grants individuals various rights over their personal data, such as the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict, port, and object.
- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of scientific methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and present digital evidence in cybercrime investigations, litigation, or audits. Cyber forensic analytics involves the use of various tools and methods, such as data extraction, data mining, data visualization, data modeling, machine learning, artificial intelligence, and blockchain.
- Cyber forensic analytics can help in identifying, preserving, recovering, and presenting relevant and reliable digital evidence from various sources, such as computers, mobile devices, networks, cloud services, social media platforms, and internet of things (IoT) devices. Cyber forensic analytics can also help in detecting, preventing, and responding to cyberattacks, fraud, and other malicious activities.
