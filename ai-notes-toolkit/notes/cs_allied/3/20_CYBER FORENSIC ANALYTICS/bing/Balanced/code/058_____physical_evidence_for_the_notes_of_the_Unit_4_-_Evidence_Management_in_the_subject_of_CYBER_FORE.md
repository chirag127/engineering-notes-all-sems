### Physical Evidence

Physical evidence is any tangible object or material that can be used to prove or disprove a fact or a hypothesis in a cyber forensic investigation. Physical evidence can be collected from various sources, such as:

- **Removable media**: This includes any storage device that can be detached from a computer system, such as USB flash drives, external hard drives, CDs, DVDs, memory cards, etc. Removable media can contain digital evidence, such as files, folders, logs, metadata, etc. that can reveal information about the user's activities, preferences, contacts, etc. Removable media can also be used to transfer or hide malicious software, such as viruses, worms, trojans, etc. that can compromise the security or functionality of a computer system.

- **Cables**: This includes any wires or cords that connect different devices or components of a computer system, such as power cables, network cables, monitor cables, keyboard cables, etc. Cables can provide physical evidence of the configuration, connection, and communication of a computer system. Cables can also be used to tamper with or bypass the security or authentication mechanisms of a computer system, such as by using a keylogger, a network sniffer, a jumper, etc.

- **Publications**: This includes any printed or electronic materials that can provide information or guidance about a computer system, such as manuals, guides, books, magazines, newsletters, etc. Publications can provide physical evidence of the user's knowledge, skills, interests, or intentions regarding a computer system. Publications can also be used to learn or teach about the vulnerabilities, exploits, or techniques of a computer system, such as by using a hacking tutorial, a security bulletin, a cheat sheet, etc.

- **All computer equipment, including peripherals**: This includes any hardware or software components that make up or interact with a computer system, such as the CPU, the motherboard, the RAM, the hard drive, the keyboard, the mouse, the monitor, the printer, the scanner, the webcam, the microphone, the speakers, etc. All computer equipment can provide physical evidence of the specifications, performance, functionality, or usage of a computer system. All computer equipment can also be used to store, process, display, or transmit digital evidence, such as by using a hidden partition, a steganography tool, a encryption software, a network protocol, etc.

- **Items taken from the trash**: This includes any discarded or unwanted materials that can be found in or near a computer system, such as paper, plastic, metal, glass, etc. Items taken from the trash can provide physical evidence of the user's behavior, habits, or preferences regarding a computer system. Items taken from the trash can also be used to recover or destroy digital evidence, such as by using a shredder, a magnet, a hammer, a fire, etc.

- **Miscellaneous items**: This includes any other objects or materials that can be related to or associated with a computer system, such as a calendar, a notebook, a pen, a phone, a camera, a wallet, a key, a card, etc. Miscellaneous items can provide physical evidence of the user's identity, location, schedule, or contacts regarding a computer system. Miscellaneous items can also be used to access or control a computer system, such as by using a password, a PIN, a biometric, a remote, etc.

Physical evidence can be collected, preserved, analyzed, and presented by following the standard procedures and guidelines of cyber forensic investigations, such as:

- **Identification**: This is the process of finding and recognizing the potential sources and types of physical evidence that can be relevant to a cyber forensic investigation. Identification can be done by using various methods and tools, such as observation, inspection, documentation, photography, etc.

- **Collection**: This is the process of acquiring and securing the physical evidence that has been identified in a cyber forensic investigation. Collection can be done by using various methods and tools, such as seizing, labeling, packaging, transporting, etc.

- **Preservation**: This is the process of protecting and maintaining the integrity and authenticity of the physical evidence that has been collected in a cyber forensic investigation. Preservation can be done by using various methods and tools, such as storing, locking, sealing, hashing, etc.

- **Analysis**: This is the process of examining and interpreting the physical evidence that has been preserved in a cyber forensic investigation. Analysis can be done by using various methods and tools, such as testing, comparing, extracting, decoding, etc.
