### DDoS Attacks

- A DDoS attack is a **distributed denial-of-service** attack that aims to **disrupt the normal traffic** of a targeted server, service or network by **overwhelming** the target or its surrounding infrastructure with a **flood of Internet traffic**   .
- A DDoS attack is carried out by **multiple compromised computer systems** that act as sources of attack traffic, also known as **bots** or **botnets**  .
- A DDoS attack can have various **motives**, such as **cybercrime**, **hacktivism**, **cyberwarfare**, **extortion**, **revenge**, **competition**, or **fun**  .
- A DDoS attack can have various **impacts**, such as **slowing down** or **crashing** the target website or service, **disrupting** the normal operations of the target organization, **damaging** the reputation and trust of the target, **stealing** sensitive data or credentials, **costing** money and resources to mitigate and recover from the attack, and **affecting** other users or customers who rely on the target  .
- A DDoS attack can use various **techniques**, such as **volumetric attacks**, **protocol attacks**, **application layer attacks**, **amplification attacks**, **reflection attacks**, **TCP SYN flood attacks**, **UDP flood attacks**, **HTTP flood attacks**, **DNS flood attacks**, **NTP amplification attacks**, **SSDP amplification attacks**, **Slowloris attacks**, **Ping of Death attacks**, **Smurf attacks**, **Teardrop attacks**, **Fraggle attacks**, **ICMP flood attacks**, and **RUDY attacks**   .
- A DDoS attack can be **prevented** or **mitigated** by using various **measures**, such as **monitoring** the network traffic and performance, **filtering** or **blocking** the malicious traffic, **deploying** anti-DDoS hardware or software solutions, **scaling** up the network bandwidth and resources, **distributing** the network load across multiple servers or locations, **updating** the security patches and configurations, **encrypting** the data and communications, **reporting** the attack to the Internet service provider or law enforcement, and **cooperating** with other organizations or experts to share information and best practices   .
