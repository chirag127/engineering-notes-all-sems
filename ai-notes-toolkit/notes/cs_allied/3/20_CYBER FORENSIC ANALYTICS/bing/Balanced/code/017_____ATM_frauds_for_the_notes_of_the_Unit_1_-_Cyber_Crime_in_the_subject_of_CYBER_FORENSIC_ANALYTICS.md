### ATM frauds

ATM frauds are a type of cyber crime that involves stealing money or personal information from ATM users. ATM frauds can have serious consequences for the victims, such as financial losses, identity theft, or compromised accounts. Some of the common types of ATM frauds are:

- **Skimming**: This is when criminals attach a device to the card reader of an ATM that can capture the data on the magnetic strip of the card. They may also install a hidden camera or a fake keypad to record the PIN of the user. The criminals can then use the stolen data to clone the card or make online purchases.
- **Shimming**: This is similar to skimming, but the device is inserted inside the card slot of the ATM, where it can intercept the data from the chip of the card. The device is usually very thin and hard to detect. The criminals can then use the stolen data to create a fake card or make online transactions.
- **Card trapping**: This is when criminals insert a device into the card slot of the ATM that can prevent the card from being ejected after the transaction. The device may look like a normal part of the ATM, such as a green flashing light. The criminals can then retrieve the card when the user leaves the ATM, or trick the user into revealing their PIN by pretending to help them.
- **Cash trapping**: This is when criminals attach a device to the cash dispenser of the ATM that can prevent the cash from being dispensed to the user. The device may look like a metal plate or a plastic sleeve. The user may think that the ATM is out of order or malfunctioning, and leave the ATM without the cash. The criminals can then remove the device and collect the cash.
- **PIN compromise**: This is when criminals use various methods to obtain the PIN of the user, such as shoulder surfing, phishing, or social engineering. Shoulder surfing is when criminals watch over the user's shoulder as they enter their PIN at the ATM. Phishing is when criminals send fake emails or text messages to the user, asking them to verify their PIN or account details. Social engineering is when criminals impersonate bank staff or other authorities, and persuade the user to reveal their PIN or other personal information.
- **Bitcoin ATMs**: This is a relatively new type of ATM fraud that involves targeting cryptocurrency transactions. Bitcoin ATMs are machines that allow users to buy or sell bitcoins using cash or debit cards. Criminals can use bitcoin ATMs for drug trafficking, money laundering, or various frauds, such as impersonating the IRS or the FBI, and demanding payments in bitcoins from the victims. Bitcoin ATMs are largely anonymous and unregulated, making them attractive for criminals and risky for users.

Some of the preventive measures that ATM users can take to avoid ATM frauds are:

- Check the ATM for any signs of tampering, such as loose or damaged parts, unfamiliar devices, or stickers.
- Cover the keypad with your hand when entering your PIN, and do not share your PIN with anyone.
- Be alert and aware of your surroundings, and avoid using ATMs in isolated or dark areas.
- Do not accept help from strangers or respond to unsolicited emails or calls regarding your ATM card or account.
- Report any suspicious or unusual activity or transactions to your bank or the police immediately.
- Review your bank statements regularly and monitor your account activity online.
