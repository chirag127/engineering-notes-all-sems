### Techniques for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a legally acceptable manner.
- The main techniques for cyber forensics are:
  - Acquisition: The process of obtaining a copy or an image of the digital media or device that contains potential evidence, without altering or damaging the original data.
  - Preservation: The process of ensuring the integrity and authenticity of the acquired data, by using methods such as hashing, encryption, write-protection and documentation.
  - Analysis: The process of examining the preserved data to identify, recover, interpret and reconstruct relevant information and events related to the investigation.
  - Presentation: The process of reporting and communicating the findings and conclusions of the analysis, using clear, concise and accurate language and formats, such as charts, graphs, tables and timelines.
- Some of the tools and methods used for cyber forensics are:
  - Forensic software: Applications that assist in the acquisition, preservation, analysis and presentation of digital evidence, such as FTK, EnCase, Autopsy, X-Ways and Cellebrite.
  - Forensic hardware: Devices that facilitate the connection, extraction, duplication and protection of digital media or devices, such as write blockers, adapters, duplicators and forensic workstations.
  - Forensic standards: Guidelines and best practices that ensure the quality, reliability and admissibility of digital evidence, such as ISO/IEC 27037, NIST 800-86, SWGDE and ACPO.
  - Forensic procedures: Steps and protocols that define the scope, objectives, methods and documentation of a cyber forensic investigation, such as planning, preparation, collection, examination, analysis and reporting.
