### Information Technology Act 2000

- The Information Technology Act 2000 (also known as ITA-2000, or the IT Act) is an Act of the Indian Parliament (No 21 of 2000) notified on 17 October 2000 .
- It is the primary law in India dealing with cybercrime and electronic commerce .
- It came into force on October 17, 2000 and applies to the whole of India and to any offense committed inside or outside India's geographic boundaries and irrespective of nationality.
- It has 13 chapters, 94 sections and 4 schedules .
- Some of the main objectives of the IT Act are :
  - To provide legal recognition for transactions carried out by means of electronic data interchange and other means of electronic communication, commonly referred to as "electronic commerce".
  - To facilitate electronic filing of documents with the Government agencies and further to amend the Indian Penal Code, the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891 and the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.
  - To provide for the constitution of the Cyber Regulations Advisory Committee and the Cyber Appellate Tribunal.
  - To define various terms and concepts related to information technology, such as digital signature, electronic record, computer, computer network, computer resource, computer system, data, information, communication device, secure system, etc.
  - To prescribe the duties of intermediaries, such as network service providers, to ensure due diligence and compliance with the provisions of the Act and the rules and regulations made thereunder.
  - To provide for the authentication of electronic records by means of digital signatures and to prescribe the manner and procedure of affixing such signatures.
  - To provide for the legal recognition of electronic records and digital signatures and to establish the presumption of validity and authenticity of such records and signatures.
  - To provide for the appointment of Controller of Certifying Authorities and to regulate the activities of such authorities and the Certifying Authorities who issue digital signature certificates.
  - To provide for the security of electronic records and digital signatures and to prescribe the conditions and limitations for their use and disclosure.
  - To provide for the protection of personal data and sensitive personal data and to prescribe the penalties for breach of confidentiality and privacy of such data.
  - To provide for the prevention and punishment of various cybercrimes, such as hacking, tampering, publishing or transmitting obscene material, cyber terrorism, identity theft, phishing, etc.
  - To provide for the adjudication of disputes and the establishment of the Cyber Appellate Tribunal to hear appeals from the orders of the adjudicating officers or the Controller.
  - To provide for the powers of the Central Government and the State Governments to issue directions, make rules and regulations, and take necessary measures for the implementation of the Act.
