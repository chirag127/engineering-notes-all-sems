## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a forensically sound manner. Evidence management involves the following steps:

- **Identification**: The first step is to identify the potential sources and types of digital evidence that are relevant to the case. This may include computers, mobile devices, storage media, network devices, cloud services, etc. The identification process should also consider the legal and ethical implications of accessing and acquiring the evidence, such as obtaining proper authorization, consent, or warrants.
- **Collection**: The next step is to collect the digital evidence from the identified sources using appropriate tools and techniques. The collection process should follow the principles of forensic acquisition, such as maintaining the integrity, authenticity, and chain of custody of the evidence, minimizing the alteration or contamination of the original data, and documenting the actions and procedures performed.
- **Preservation**: The preservation step involves storing and protecting the collected evidence from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction. The preservation process should ensure the availability, reliability, and security of the evidence, as well as comply with the legal and organizational policies and standards. The preservation process may include creating backups, copies, or hashes of the evidence, encrypting or locking the evidence, and storing the evidence in a safe and controlled environment.
- **Analysis**: The analysis step involves examining and interpreting the evidence to extract relevant information and insights that can support the investigation objectives and hypotheses. The analysis process should use valid and reliable methods and tools, and follow the principles of forensic examination, such as verifying the evidence, recovering deleted or hidden data, correlating and cross-referencing data from different sources, and identifying anomalies or patterns.
- **Presentation**: The final step is to present the evidence and the analysis results to the intended audience, such as the investigators, the management, the court, or the public. The presentation process should communicate the evidence and the analysis findings in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, using appropriate formats, languages, and media. The presentation process should also address the limitations, uncertainties, and challenges of the evidence and the analysis, and provide recommendations or conclusions based on the evidence.
