### Digital signature for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- A digital signature is a mathematical scheme to verify the authenticity of digital documents or messages.
- A digital signature allows the recipient to trust that a known sender sent the message and it was not altered in transit.
- A digital signature also provides non-repudiation, which means that the sender cannot deny sending the message after signing it.
- A digital signature uses a digital certificate from a trust service provider (TSP), such as a certificate authority (CA), to authenticate a signer's identity.
- A digital signature is based on an asymmetric cryptography system, which uses a pair of keys: a public key and a private key.
- The public key is shared with anyone who needs to verify the signature, while the private key is kept secret by the signer.
- The signer uses the private key to generate a signature for a message, which is a hash of the message encrypted with the private key.
- The recipient uses the public key to decrypt the signature and compare it with the hash of the message.
- If the signature and the hash match, it means that the message is authentic and has not been tampered with.
- There are numerous laws and regulations surrounding digital signatures and their legality in different countries and regions.
- Some of the laws and regulations that govern digital signatures are:
  - The Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN) of 2000, which establishes the legal validity of electronic signatures and records in the United States.
  - The Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA) of 1999, which provides a legal framework for electronic transactions and signatures in the United States.
  - The Electronic Identification and Trust Services (eIDAS) regulation of 2014, which harmonizes the rules for electronic identification and trust services in the European Union.
  - The Information Technology Act (ITA) of 2000, which defines the legal status of digital signatures and electronic records in India.
  - The Cybersecurity Act of 2022, which strengthens the federal cyber workforce and fosters collaboration across all levels of government in the United States.
