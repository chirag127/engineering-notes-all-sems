### DDoS Attacks

DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) attacks are a type of cyber attack that aims to disrupt the normal functioning of a website, server or network. The goal of a DDoS attack is to overload the targeted system with a flood of requests or traffic, rendering it unavailable to legitimate users.

Here are some important points related to DDoS attacks:

- DDoS attacks are carried out by a group of attackers who use multiple devices to flood the targeted system with requests or traffic.

- The devices used in a DDoS attack are often infected with malware, which allows the attackers to control them remotely and use them as part of the attack.

- DDoS attacks can be launched using a variety of methods, including UDP floods, ICMP floods, SYN floods, HTTP floods, and DNS amplification attacks.

- Different types of DDoS attacks have different characteristics and can be countered using different mitigation techniques.

- DDoS attacks can cause a wide range of problems for the targeted system, including slow performance, crashing, or even total shutdown.

- Cyber forensic analysts can use a variety of tools and techniques to investigate DDoS attacks and trace them back to their source.

- Preventive measures such as firewalls, intrusion detection and prevention systems, and content delivery networks can help mitigate the effects of DDoS attacks.

Overall, DDoS attacks are a serious threat to the stability and security of online systems. Understanding how they work and how to prevent them is an important part of cyber forensic analytics.
