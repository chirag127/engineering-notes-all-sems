### Disk Characteristics

The following are important characteristics of a disk that are relevant to cyber forensics:

- **Capacity**: The capacity of a disk refers to the amount of data that can be stored on it. This is an important characteristic for investigators to consider when analyzing digital evidence, as it will impact the amount of data that can be recovered from a disk.

- **File system**: The file system is the method used by the operating system to store and organize files on the disk. Understanding the file system is critical for investigators to properly recover data from a disk.

- **Partitioning**: Partitions are logical divisions within a disk that allow different file systems to be used on the same physical disk. Investigators must understand how a disk is partitioned in order to properly recover data.

- **Formatting**: Formatting refers to the process of preparing a disk for use by an operating system. Different formatting methods can impact the ability to recover data from a disk.

- **Bad sectors**: Bad sectors are areas on a disk that are damaged and cannot be used for data storage. Understanding the location and extent of bad sectors is important for investigators to properly recover data from a disk.

- **Fragmentation**: Fragmentation occurs when files are split into smaller pieces and scattered across a disk. This can impact the ability to recover data and must be taken into consideration by investigators.

- **Access times**: Access times refer to the amount of time it takes for the disk to retrieve data. Investigators can use access times to reconstruct a timeline of activity on the disk.

By understanding these important characteristics of a disk, investigators can properly analyze digital evidence and recover data in a forensically sound manner.
