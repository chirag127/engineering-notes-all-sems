### Cyber Crime

1. Cyber Crimes refers to criminal activities done using computers and the internet. Some examples are:
- Hacking: unauthorized access to computer systems or networks to get data or control the system.
- Phishing: attempting to trick users into disclosing personal information like passwords, account numbers, etc. by impersonating as a trustworthy entity.
- Malware attacks: using malicious software like viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, etc. to steal data, gain access or damage systems.
- Cyber Stalking: harassing or threatening behavior directed at a specific person over the internet.
- Online Frauds: carrying out fraudulent activities like scams, identity theft, credit card fraud, etc. over the internet.
- Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) Attacks: flooding the target machine or network with excessive internet traffic to disrupt services.

2. The reasons for the rise in cyber crimes are:
- Wide reach of the internet allowing criminals to target more people and systems.
- Anonymity of the internet making it difficult to trace criminals.
- Monetary potential of cyber attacks with crypto currencies and ransom payments.
- Availability of easy-to-use hacking tools and malware kits even for amateur criminals.

3. To prevent and tackle cyber crimes, the following measures can be taken:
- Use strong and unique passwords and two-factor authentication whenever possible.
- Install a reputed antivirus or anti-malware software and keep it up-to-date.
- Apply security patches promptly to software and systems.
- Be cautious of unsolicited communications and never share personal information or click on suspicious links.
- Laws and enforcement mechanisms to deter cyber criminals and hold them accountable.
- International co-operation to effectively investigate and prosecute transnational cyber crimes.
