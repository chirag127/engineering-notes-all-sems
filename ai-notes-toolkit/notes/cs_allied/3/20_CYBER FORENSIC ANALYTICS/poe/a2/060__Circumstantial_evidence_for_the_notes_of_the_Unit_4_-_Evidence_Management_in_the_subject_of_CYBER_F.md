### Circumstantial evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS:

1. Circumstantial evidence is indirect evidence that implies the existence or non-existence of a fact. It is evidence of surrounding circumstances that can be used to infer the existence of a fact at issue.
2. In cyber forensics, circumstantial evidence could include:
- Log files showing access to a system or resource at a particular time.
- Metadata associated with a file that implies when and from where it was created or modified.
- The presence of a malware executable file on a system and the time it was created.
- The presence of a user's account on a system and login/logoff times.
3. Circumstantial evidence on its own is not enough to prove a fact, but it can be useful to support other evidence. Multiple pieces of circumstantial evidence can be more compelling.
4. Some limitations and considerations when evaluating circumstantial evidence:
- It can be open to interpretation. Different people may draw different inferences from the same circumstances.
- It does not directly prove a fact; it can only imply a fact or make a fact more or less probable.
- The circumstances may have alternative explanations or the implications drawn may be incorrect. Additional evidence may be needed to verify inferences.
- The relevance and strength of the evidence depends on how closely tied the circumstances are to the alleged facts. Closely related circumstances may have more probative value than loosely related ones.
