### Steganography for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Steganography refers to the practice of concealing a message within another message or a physical object. In digital steganography, electronic communications may include steganographic content, such as embedding hidden data in images, audio files, or other digital media.
2. Steganography is often confused with cryptography because they are both techniques for protecting data. However, steganography hides the very existence of a message, whereas cryptography transforms a message into an unreadable form but does not hide its existence. Combining steganography and cryptography, known as "steganography cryptography", can provide stronger security.
3. Common image formats for image-based steganography include JPEG, GIF, and PNG. These formats utilize lossy and lossless data compression which introduce patterns in the bit-stream that can be exploited to hide data. Audio files can have data embedded in the least-significant bits of the audio samples or in the frequency domain. In video files, data can be embedded in the least-significant bits of luminance or chrominance components.
4. Steganalysis is the art of detecting hidden messages in steganographic carriers. Steganalysis techniques include statistical analysis, structural analysis, and distortion analysis to detect anomalies and patterns that may indicate the presence of hidden data. Statistical tools can analyze the distribution of the least-significant bits or the distribution of colors. Structural tools analyze the relationships between components of an object, such as the header and the payload of an image file. Distortion tools analyze the difference between the original and steganographed objects to detect changes.
