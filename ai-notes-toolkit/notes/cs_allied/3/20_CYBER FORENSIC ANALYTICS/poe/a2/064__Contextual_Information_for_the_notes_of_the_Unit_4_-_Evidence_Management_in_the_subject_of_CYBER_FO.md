### Contextual Information for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Evidence: Anything that can be used to determine whether a crime has been committed or can provide information about the crime, criminals or victims. This could be physical objects, documents, surveillance footage, digital data, etc.
2. Digital evidence: Evidence that is stored or transmitted in binary form. This includes files, documents, emails, photos, databases, logs, etc.
3. Importance of evidence management: Proper management of evidence is critical to ensure the integrity and admissibility of evidence. If evidence is mishandled or contaminated, it can be challenged in court and possibly thrown out. Care must be taken to establish and maintain chain of custody.
4. Chain of custody: Chronological documentation showing the seizure, custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of evidence. This establishes that the evidence was handled properly and not tampered with or modified.
5. Preservation: The act of protecting digital evidence from modification or damage. This is done by making a bit-level copy of the data and ensuring the original medium is not modified.
6. Collection: The process of identifying, labeling, recording, and acquiring evidence in a forensically sound manner. Proper techniques and tools must be used to maintain integrity.
7. Examination: The in-depth analysis of collected evidence using various forensic techniques and tools to extract and interpret data of forensic interest.
8. Reporting: The process of documenting the forensic examination, including findings and conclusions, in an official report. The report should be comprehensive, accurate, objective, and understandable to non-technical parties.
