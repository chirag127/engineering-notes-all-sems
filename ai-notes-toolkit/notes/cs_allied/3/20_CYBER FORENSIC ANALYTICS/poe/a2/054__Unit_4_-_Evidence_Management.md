## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

1. Evidence collection - Proper collection of evidence is critical to ensuring its admissibility in court. Evidence should be collected carefully by trained professionals using approved techniques and equipment to avoid contamination or damage. Documentation of the collection process, including chain of custody, is necessary.

2. Evidence preservation - Evidence must be properly preserved to maintain its integrity. This could include refrigeration, vacuum sealing, or other techniques depending on the type of evidence. An evidence log or ledger should track the location and status of all evidence at all times.

3. Evidence analysis - Evidence is analyzed using various scientific techniques to extract information and interpret its meaning. Analysis is performed by trained forensic scientists, ideally in an accredited lab using validated methods. Analysis results are documented and preserved for potential use in legal proceedings.

4. Evidence presentation - During legal proceedings, evidence must be presented properly in accordance with applicable laws and rules of evidence. This ensures evidence is admissible and considered by the judge or jury. Evidence is typically presented through witness testimony and documented reports. Proper presentation includes establishing evidence credibility and validity.

5. Evidence storage - Evidence must be securely stored, often for long periods of time. Storage conditions are dependent on evidence type and must be properly maintained and documented. Secure facilities, restricted access, and backup/redundancy of evidence are important. Periodic reviews of evidence may be needed if storage time is lengthy.
