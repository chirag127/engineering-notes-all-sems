## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

1. Introduction to Cyber Forensics
- Cyber forensics involves collecting, analyzing and reporting on digital data to determine potential legal evidence.
- The goal is to conduct an structured investigation while maintaining a documented chain of evidence to find out what happened, who was involved and what can be done.
- Common steps include collection, examination, analysis, reporting, and presentation.

2. Data Collection and Acquisition
- Data can come from devices, servers, applications, networks, etc.
- Live data needs to be handled carefully to avoid changing the original data.
- Secure collection involves following proper legal procedures and using forensically sound tools and methods.
- Hashing is used to verify data integrity and authenticity.

3. Examination and Analysis
- The collected data is examined to filter out unimportant information and find relevant data.
- Various tools and techniques are used to uncover hidden, encrypted or deleted data.
- The analysis aims to reveal insights into what happened, sequence of events, parties involved, motive, etc.
- Logs, metadata, and other digital footprints are analyzed to reconstruct the crimes or events.

4. Reporting and Presentation
- The findings and conclusions are documented in a formal report.
- The reports should be objective, detailed, and well-presented to convey the key information to the intended parties.
- Depending on the purpose, reports can be technical or non-technical. They may be presented in courts as evidence.
