### Electronic signature certificates for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS.

- An electronic signature is a legal concept that refers to data in electronic form which is attached to or logically associated with other electronic data and which is used by the signatory to sign the data.
- Electronic signatures are recognized as a valid way to sign contracts, documents and transactions in many countries that have adopted electronic signature laws.
- For electronic signatures to be legally valid, they must meet certain requirements such as:
- The signer must have control over the signature
- The signature must be capable of verification to authenticate the signature and verify the integrity of the signed document
- The method used to affix the signature must be dependent on and under the sole control of the signer
- Electronic signature certificates use digital certificates to verify the identity of the person signing the electronic document and ensure that the electronic signature was affixed by that person. The certificate binds the identity of the person to the electronic signature.
- To be legally valid, electronic signatures and the certificates used must comply with national electronic signature laws and regulations. The specific requirements vary between countries but generally involve the use of trusted certificate authorities and secure signature creation devices or cryptographic methods.
- In summary, for electronic signatures and certificates to have legal validity and enforceability, they must follow the electronic signature laws and regulations of the relevant country or jurisdiction. The specific requirements aim to ensure that electronic signatures have the same legal validity as traditional handwritten signatures.
