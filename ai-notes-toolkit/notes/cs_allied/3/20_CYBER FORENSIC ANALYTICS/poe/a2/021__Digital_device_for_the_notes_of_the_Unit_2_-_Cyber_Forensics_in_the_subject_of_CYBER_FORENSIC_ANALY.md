### Digital device for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Hard disk drives (HDDs)
- Made up of platters that store data magnetically
- Read/write heads access data
- Slower than solid-state drives (SSDs) but cheaper per gigabyte
- Live data acquisition for forensics must account for heads moving
- Degaussing/physical destruction needed for secure wipe

2. Solid-state drives (SSDs)
- Use integrated circuits to store data electronically instead of magnetically
- Faster access speeds but more expensive per gigabyte than HDDs
- Live data acquisition for forensics must account for flash translation layer
- Built-in encryption and TRIM commands remove difficulties of secure wipe

3. USB flash drives
- Store data on flash memory chips
- Small, portable, and relatively inexpensive
- Important source of evidence as they can be easily moved between devices
- Challenges for live data acquisition and forensics
- Difficult to fully wipe securely due to wear leveling

4. Cloud storage
- Data stored on remote servers accessed over Internet
- Prevalence reduces reliance on local digital devices
- Legal issues around jurisdiction and law enforcement access
- Volatility of data and metadata requires forensics to act quickly
- Scale of services makes comprehensive forensics impractical
