
### Cyber Space for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Cyber Crime in the Subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Cyber crime is defined as any criminal activity that involves the use of computers or other digital devices. It can include activities such as hacking, identity theft, fraud, cyberbullying, and more. 

2. Cyber criminals use a variety of techniques to gain access to computers, networks, and data. These techniques include phishing, malware, and social engineering. 

3. Cyber forensics is the process of using digital evidence to identify, collect, analyze, and present evidence of a crime. It involves the use of specialized software and hardware tools to examine digital evidence. 

4. Cyber forensics analysts must be knowledgeable in computer science, digital forensics, and criminal law. They must also be familiar with the various types of cyber attacks and the methods used to investigate them. 

5. Cyber forensics analysts must also be able to interpret and analyze digital evidence. They must be able to identify patterns in the data and draw conclusions from it. 

6. Cyber forensics analysts must also be familiar with the legal aspects of cyber crime. They must be able to identify potential legal issues and advise on how to address them. 

7. Cyber forensics analysts must also be able to communicate effectively with law enforcement and other stakeholders. They must be able to provide clear and concise reports on their findings.
