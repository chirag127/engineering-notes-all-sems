
### Evidence Management 

1. Evidence management is a critical component of cyber forensics analytics, as it involves the collection, preservation, and analysis of digital evidence. 
2. The primary goal of evidence management is to ensure that digital evidence is collected, stored, and analyzed in a manner that is consistent with the principles of the law. 
3. The first step in evidence management is to collect the evidence in a manner that preserves its integrity. This involves using techniques such as hashing, encryption, and digital signatures to ensure that the evidence is not tampered with during the collection process. 
4. The evidence must then be preserved in a manner that ensures its authenticity. This involves storing the evidence in a secure environment and ensuring that it is not modified or destroyed during the storage process. 
5. Once the evidence has been collected and preserved, it must be analyzed in order to determine its relevance to the case. This involves using various forensic tools and techniques to examine the evidence and draw conclusions about its meaning and implications. 
6. Finally, the evidence must be presented in a manner that is consistent with the principles of the law. This involves providing the evidence in a manner that is admissible in court and that is understandable to the jury.
