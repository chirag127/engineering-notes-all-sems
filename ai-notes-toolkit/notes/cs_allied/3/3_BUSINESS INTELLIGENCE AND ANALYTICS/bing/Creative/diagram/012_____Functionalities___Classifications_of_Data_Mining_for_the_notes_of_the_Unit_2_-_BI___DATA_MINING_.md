### Functionalities & Classifications of Data Mining

Data mining is the process of discovering useful patterns and insights from large and complex data sets. Data mining can help organizations to improve their decision making, enhance their productivity, and gain competitive advantages.

Data mining functionalities are used to represent the type of patterns that have to be discovered in data mining tasks. In general, data mining tasks can be classified into two types including descriptive and predictive.

- Descriptive mining tasks define the common features of the data in the database and summarize the characteristics of the data. Examples of descriptive mining tasks are:

  - Association analysis: finding frequent patterns, associations, or correlations among a set of items or attributes in a transaction database, such as market basket analysis.
  - Clustering analysis: grouping a set of data objects into clusters such that objects within a cluster are similar to each other and dissimilar to objects in other clusters.
  - Outlier analysis: identifying data objects that deviate significantly from the normal behavior or the expected pattern, such as fraud detection.
  - Summarization: providing a concise and general description of the data, such as data visualization, report generation, or statistical analysis.

- Predictive mining tasks perform inference on the current data in order to make predictions about future or unknown data. Examples of predictive mining tasks are:

  - Classification: determining a model that describes and differentiates data classes with the intention of using it to predict the class of unknown or anonymous objects, such as spam filtering, credit risk analysis, or medical diagnosis .
  - Regression: estimating or predicting a numeric value based on the input variables, such as sales forecasting, stock price prediction, or house value estimation.
  - Time series analysis: modeling and forecasting the trends and behaviors of data that change over time, such as stock market analysis, weather forecasting, or traffic analysis.

Data mining can also be classified based on the kind of data to be mined, such as relational data, text data, multimedia data, web data, spatial data, temporal data, or stream data. Different types of data may require different techniques and algorithms to discover the patterns and insights.

Data mining usually consists of four main steps: setting objectives, data gathering and preparation, applying data mining algorithms, and evaluating results.

- Setting objectives: This can be the hardest part of the data mining process, and many organizations spend too little time on this important step. The objectives should be clear, measurable, and aligned with the business goals. The objectives should also specify the type of data mining task, the expected output, and the evaluation criteria.
- Data gathering and preparation: This is the most time-consuming and labor-intensive part of the data mining process, and it can affect the quality and accuracy of the results. The data should be collected from various sources, such as databases, files, web pages, or sensors. The data should also be cleaned, integrated, transformed, reduced, and normalized to make it suitable for data mining.
- Applying data mining algorithms: This is the core part of the data mining process, where the data mining techniques and tools are applied to the prepared data. The choice of the algorithms depends on the type of data, the type of task, and the objectives. The algorithms can be supervised, unsupervised, or semi-supervised, and they can use different approaches, such as statistics, machine learning, artificial intelligence, or neural networks.
- Evaluating results: This is the final part of the data mining process, where the results of the data mining algorithms are assessed and validated. The results should be compared with the objectives and the evaluation criteria, and they should be interpreted and explained in a meaningful way. The results should also be tested for their reliability, validity, and generalizability.

Data mining is a key component of business intelligence. Data mining tools are built into executive dashboards, harvesting insight from Big Data, including data from social media, Internet of Things (IoT) sensor feeds, location-aware devices, unstructured text, video, and more. Data mining can help organizations to:

- Understand customer behavior and preferences, and improve customer satisfaction and loyalty.
- Identify new opportunities and markets, and develop new products and services.
- Optimize business processes and operations, and reduce costs and risks.
- Enhance innovation and creativity, and gain competitive advantages.
