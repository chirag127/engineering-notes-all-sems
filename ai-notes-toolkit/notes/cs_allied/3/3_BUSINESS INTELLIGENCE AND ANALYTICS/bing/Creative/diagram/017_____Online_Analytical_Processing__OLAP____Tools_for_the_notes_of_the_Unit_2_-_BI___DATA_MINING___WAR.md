### Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) – Tools

- OLAP is a technology that is used to organize large business databases and support business intelligence.
- OLAP tools enable to analyze multidimensional data interactively from multiple perspectives.
- OLAP involves relational database, report writing and data mining and consists of three basic analytical operations: consolidation, drill-down, and slicing and dicing.
- Consolidation is the aggregation of data along one or more dimensions, such as summing up sales by region or by product category.
- Drill-down is the opposite of consolidation, where data is broken down into finer levels of detail, such as viewing sales by individual products or by specific customers.
- Slicing and dicing is the process of selecting and viewing a subset of data based on certain criteria, such as filtering sales by a certain time period or by a certain product type.
- OLAP tools can be classified into three types: MOLAP, ROLAP, and HOLAP .
- MOLAP (Multidimensional OLAP) uses a multidimensional cube that accesses stored data through various combinations of dimensions and measures .
- ROLAP (Relational OLAP) stores data in relational tables using columns and rows and queries data using SQL statements .
- HOLAP (Hybrid OLAP) combines the features of both MOLAP and ROLAP and allows data to be stored in both multidimensional cubes and relational tables .
- OLAP tools are the basis for many business applications for Business Performance Management, Planning, Analysis, Budgeting, Simulation Models, and Data Warehouse Reporting.
- OLAP tools allow for complex analytical and specific queries with a fast execution time using a multidimensional data model.
- OLAP tools also provide features such as data visualization, dashboards, scorecards, and drill-through.
- Some examples of OLAP tools are Microsoft Excel, IBM Cognos, Oracle Essbase, SAP BusinessObjects, and MicroStrategy.
