### Root Cause Analysis

- Root cause analysis (RCA) is a method of problem solving used for identifying the root causes of faults or problems.
- A root cause is the core issue that sets in motion the entire cause-and-effect reaction that ultimately leads to the problem.
- RCA is a team approach methodology that works by understanding that systems and actions are related and that one action can trigger an event and that event can trigger another event and so on until you have a problem or multiple problems.
- There are four main steps in an RCA:
  - Identify the problem
  - Analyze the problem
  - Identify the root causes
  - Implement solutions
- RCA is often used in proactive management to identify the root cause of a problem, that is, the factor that was the leading cause.
- RCA is also useful for data analysis, as it can help discover the root causes of problems in data quality, data accuracy, data completeness, data timeliness, and data relevance.
- Some of the benefits of RCA are:
  - It can prevent recurrence of problems and reduce costs
  - It can improve customer satisfaction and loyalty
  - It can enhance quality and performance
  - It can foster a culture of continuous improvement and learning
- Some of the tools and techniques used for RCA are:
  - The 5 Whys: A simple technique that involves asking "why" repeatedly until the root cause is revealed
  - Fishbone Diagram: A visual tool that shows the relationship between a problem and its possible causes, organized into categories
  - Pareto Chart: A graphical tool that displays the frequency or impact of different causes, based on the 80/20 rule
  - Fault Tree Analysis: A logical tool that uses a tree-like structure to trace the events and conditions that lead to a failure
  - Cause and Effect Matrix: A matrix tool that ranks the causes of a problem based on their severity and frequency
