## Unit 5 - IMPLEMENTATION OF BI

Business intelligence (BI) is the process of transforming data into insights that help businesses make better decisions, improve performance, and gain a competitive edge. BI involves collecting, integrating, analyzing, and presenting data from various sources, such as internal databases, external sources, or cloud services. BI can also include advanced techniques, such as data mining, machine learning, or artificial intelligence, to discover hidden patterns, trends, or anomalies in the data.

To implement BI successfully in an organization, there are several steps that need to be followed. These steps can vary depending on the size, scope, and goals of the BI project, but generally they include:

- Creating a BI strategy: This involves defining the vision, objectives, scope, and benefits of the BI initiative, as well as identifying the key stakeholders, users, and sponsors. A BI strategy should align with the overall business strategy and goals, and should address the current and future data needs and challenges of the organization.
- Setting the key performance indicators (KPIs): These are the metrics that will measure the progress and success of the BI project, and help evaluate the impact and value of the BI solutions. KPIs should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound, and should reflect the strategic goals and priorities of the organization.
- Appointing stakeholders and educating the staff: This involves assigning roles and responsibilities to the different parties involved in the BI project, such as the project manager, the BI team, the data owners, the data analysts, the end users, and the vendors. It also involves providing training and education to the staff on the benefits, features, and usage of the BI tools and solutions, as well as fostering a data-driven culture and mindset in the organization.
- Building a strong BI team or outsourcing: This involves deciding whether to build an in-house BI team or outsource the BI project to a third-party vendor. The decision depends on factors such as the budget, the timeline, the complexity, and the availability of skills and resources. A strong BI team should have a mix of technical, analytical, and business skills, and should be able to collaborate effectively with the stakeholders and users.
- Finding the best software for your needs: This involves selecting the BI tools and platforms that best suit the data sources, the data types, the data volume, the data quality, the data analysis, and the data visualization needs of the organization. There are many BI software options available in the market, such as Tableau, Power BI, Qlik, or SAP, that offer different features, functionalities, and pricing models. The selection criteria should include the ease of use, the scalability, the security, the integration, the customization, and the support of the BI software.
- Choosing your data storage, environment, and platform: This involves deciding where and how to store, access, and process the data for the BI project. The data storage options include on-premise, cloud, or hybrid solutions, that offer different advantages and disadvantages in terms of cost, performance, reliability, and security. The data environment options include data warehouses, data lakes, or data marts, that offer different levels of data integration, standardization, and granularity. The data platform options include relational, non-relational, or multi-model databases, that offer different capabilities and limitations in terms of data structure, query, and analysis.
- Finetuning your data preparation process: This involves cleaning, transforming, and enriching the data to make it ready for analysis and visualization. Data preparation is a crucial and time-consuming step in the BI project, as it ensures the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and validity of the data. Data preparation can involve tasks such as data extraction, data integration, data validation, data cleansing, data transformation, data enrichment, and data profiling.
- Consider more advanced solutions: This involves exploring the possibilities of using more sophisticated and innovative techniques and technologies to enhance the BI project. These include data mining, machine learning, artificial intelligence, natural language processing, sentiment analysis, predictive analytics, prescriptive analytics, and cognitive analytics, that can help discover deeper insights, uncover hidden patterns, identify anomalies, forecast outcomes, recommend actions, and automate decisions.
- Implementing and testing the BI solutions: This involves deploying and launching the BI tools and solutions in the organization, and ensuring that they work as expected and meet the requirements and expectations of the stakeholders and users. This also involves conducting tests, audits, and evaluations to check the functionality, performance, usability, and reliability of the BI solutions, and to identify and resolve any issues or errors that may arise.
