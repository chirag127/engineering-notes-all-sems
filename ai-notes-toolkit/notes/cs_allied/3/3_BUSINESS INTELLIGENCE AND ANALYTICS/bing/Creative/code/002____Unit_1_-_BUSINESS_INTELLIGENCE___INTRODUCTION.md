## Unit 1 - BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE – INTRODUCTION

- Business intelligence (BI) is the process of collecting, analyzing, and transforming data into meaningful information that can help businesses make better decisions and improve their performance.
- BI can be used for various purposes, such as:
  - Reporting: presenting data in a structured and easy-to-understand format, such as tables, charts, dashboards, etc.
  - Analytics: applying statistical and mathematical techniques to data to discover patterns, trends, correlations, outliers, etc.
  - Data mining: applying advanced algorithms to data to extract hidden and valuable knowledge, such as associations, classifications, clusters, etc.
  - Data warehousing: creating and maintaining a centralized repository of data from various sources, such as operational systems, external databases, web pages, etc.
  - Data quality: ensuring that the data is accurate, complete, consistent, and relevant for the intended use.
  - Data governance: establishing and enforcing policies, standards, and procedures for managing data throughout its lifecycle, such as data security, privacy, ownership, access, etc.
- BI can provide various benefits to businesses, such as:
  - Improving decision making: by providing timely, accurate, and relevant information to support strategic, tactical, and operational decisions.
  - Enhancing customer satisfaction: by understanding customer needs, preferences, and behavior, and providing personalized products, services, and offers.
  - Increasing efficiency and productivity: by optimizing business processes, reducing costs, and increasing revenue.
  - Gaining competitive advantage: by identifying opportunities, threats, and best practices, and creating innovative solutions and strategies.
  - Fostering a data-driven culture: by encouraging data literacy, collaboration, and learning among employees, managers, and stakeholders.
