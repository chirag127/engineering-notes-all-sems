### Selection

- Selection is the process of choosing the relevant data for a specific analysis or modeling task from a larger dataset or multiple data sources.
- Selection can help reduce the complexity, noise, redundancy, and bias in the data, and improve the efficiency, accuracy, and interpretability of the analysis or modeling results.
- Selection can be applied at different levels of granularity, such as selecting rows, columns, cells, or values from the data.
- Selection can be based on different criteria, such as data quality, data relevance, data diversity, data balance, data distribution, or data compatibility.
- Selection can be performed manually or automatically, depending on the availability of domain knowledge, metadata, labels, or algorithms.
- Selection can be done before, during, or after the data preparation process, depending on the objectives, methods, and tools used for the analysis or modeling task.

Some examples of selection methods are:

- **Filtering**: Removing rows or columns from the data that do not meet certain conditions, such as missing values, outliers, duplicates, or irrelevant attributes.
- **Sampling**: Selecting a subset of rows or columns from the data that represent the characteristics or patterns of the whole data, such as random sampling, stratified sampling, or cluster sampling.
- **Feature selection**: Choosing a subset of attributes or variables from the data that have the most predictive power or explanatory value for the analysis or modeling task, such as correlation analysis, mutual information, or wrapper methods.
- **Dimensionality reduction**: Transforming the data into a lower-dimensional space that preserves the essential information or structure of the data, such as principal component analysis, singular value decomposition, or autoencoders.
- **Data integration**: Combining data from different sources or formats that share common attributes or entities, such as join, union, or merge operations.
