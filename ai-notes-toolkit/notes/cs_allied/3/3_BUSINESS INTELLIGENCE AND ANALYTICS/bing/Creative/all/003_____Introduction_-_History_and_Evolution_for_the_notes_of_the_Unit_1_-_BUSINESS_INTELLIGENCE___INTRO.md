# Introduction - History and Evolution

- Business intelligence (BI) is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting data to support decision making in various domains of business.
- The term BI was coined in 1989 by Howard Dresner, a former Gartner analyst, who defined it as "concepts and methods to improve business decision making by using fact-based support systems" .
- However, the origins of BI can be traced back to the 19th century, when Richard Millar Devens described how a banker used information about market trends to gain a competitive advantage in his book "Cyclopaedia of Commercial and Business Anecdotes" .
- The evolution of BI can be divided into four stages: pre-digital, digital, modern, and future.

## Pre-digital stage

- In the pre-digital stage, BI was mainly based on manual methods of data collection and analysis, such as census, surveys, and accounting records.
- The data was often limited, inaccurate, and outdated, and the analysis was mostly descriptive and intuitive.
- The main users of BI were government agencies, military organizations, and large corporations.

## Digital stage

- The digital stage began in the 1950s, with the emergence of computers and databases, which enabled faster and more efficient data processing and storage.
- The first computerized BI system was the Generalized Information Processing System (GIPS), developed by IBM in 1958, which allowed users to query and manipulate data using natural language .
- The 1960s and 1970s saw the development of decision support systems (DSS), which integrated data from multiple sources and provided analytical tools and models for decision making .
- The 1980s and 1990s witnessed the growth of BI vendors and tools, such as spreadsheets, data warehouses, online analytical processing (OLAP), data mining, and reporting .
- The data became more structured, standardized, and accessible, and the analysis became more multidimensional, statistical, and predictive.
- The main users of BI were business managers, analysts, and consultants.

## Modern stage

- The modern stage started in the 2000s, with the advent of the internet, cloud computing, big data, and artificial intelligence, which enabled more scalable, flexible, and diverse data sources and platforms.
- The emergence of self-service BI, data visualization, and dashboards allowed users to explore and interact with data more easily and intuitively .
- The rise of social media, mobile devices, and the internet of things (IoT) generated more unstructured, real-time, and geospatial data, which required more advanced and complex analysis techniques, such as natural language processing (NLP), machine learning, and deep learning .
- The data became more dynamic, heterogeneous, and granular, and the analysis became more contextual, prescriptive, and cognitive.
- The main users of BI expanded to include various stakeholders, such as customers, employees, partners, and regulators.

## Future stage

- The future stage is still unfolding, with the continuous innovation and integration of new technologies, such as blockchain, quantum computing, augmented reality, and edge computing, which will enable more secure, fast, and immersive data experiences .
- The emergence of automated BI, conversational BI, and embedded BI will allow users to access and leverage data more seamlessly and naturally .
- The rise of collective intelligence, ethical intelligence, and emotional intelligence will require more collaborative, responsible, and empathetic data practices .
- The data will become more ubiquitous, intelligent, and personalized, and the analysis will become more holistic, adaptive, and human-centric.
- The main users of BI will encompass all aspects of society, such as individuals, communities, and ecosystems.
