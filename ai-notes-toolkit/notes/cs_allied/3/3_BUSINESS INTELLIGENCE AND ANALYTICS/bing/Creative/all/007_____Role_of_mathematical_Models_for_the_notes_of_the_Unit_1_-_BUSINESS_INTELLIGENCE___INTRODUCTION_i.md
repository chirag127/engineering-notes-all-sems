# Role of Mathematical Models in Business Intelligence

- A mathematical model is an abstract representation of a real-world system or problem that uses mathematical language and symbols.
- A mathematical model can help to understand, analyze, predict, optimize, and control the behavior and performance of a system or problem.
- A business intelligence system provides decision makers with information and knowledge extracted from data, through the application of mathematical models and algorithms .
- The role of mathematical models in business intelligence can be summarized as follows:
  - They help to transform data into meaningful information and insights that can support decision making.
  - They help to identify patterns, trends, relationships, and anomalies in data that may not be obvious or intuitive.
  - They help to quantify the impact of various factors and scenarios on the outcomes and objectives of interest.
  - They help to optimize the allocation of resources, the design of strategies, and the evaluation of alternatives.
  - They help to learn from data and improve the accuracy and reliability of predictions and recommendations.
- Some examples of mathematical models used in business intelligence are:
  - Descriptive models: These models describe the current or past state of a system or problem, such as summary statistics, charts, graphs, dashboards, etc.
  - Predictive models: These models forecast the future state of a system or problem, such as regression, classification, time series, etc.
  - Prescriptive models: These models prescribe the best course of action for a system or problem, such as optimization, simulation, decision analysis, etc.
  - Learning models: These models learn from data and update themselves over time, such as artificial neural networks, deep learning, reinforcement learning, etc.
