### Role of mathematical models in business intelligence

- A mathematical model is an abstract representation of a real-world system or problem that uses mathematical language and symbols.
- A mathematical model can help to understand, analyze, predict, optimize, and control the behavior and performance of a system or problem.
- A business intelligence system provides decision makers with information and knowledge extracted from data, through the application of mathematical models and algorithms .
- The role of mathematical models in business intelligence can be summarized as follows:
  - To transform data into meaningful information and insights that can support decision making.
  - To identify patterns, trends, relationships, and anomalies in data that can reveal opportunities, risks, and challenges.
  - To test hypotheses, scenarios, and strategies that can help to evaluate alternatives and outcomes.
  - To optimize resources, processes, and objectives that can improve efficiency, effectiveness, and profitability.
  - To learn from data and feedback that can enhance knowledge and intelligence over time.
- Some examples of mathematical models used in business intelligence are:
  - Descriptive models: These models describe the current or past state of a system or problem, such as statistics, charts, graphs, dashboards, etc.
  - Predictive models: These models forecast the future state or behavior of a system or problem, such as regression, classification, clustering, time series, etc.
  - Prescriptive models: These models recommend the best course of action or decision for a system or problem, such as optimization, simulation, decision analysis, etc.
  - Learning models: These models adapt and improve their performance based on data and feedback, such as artificial neural networks, deep learning, reinforcement learning, etc.
