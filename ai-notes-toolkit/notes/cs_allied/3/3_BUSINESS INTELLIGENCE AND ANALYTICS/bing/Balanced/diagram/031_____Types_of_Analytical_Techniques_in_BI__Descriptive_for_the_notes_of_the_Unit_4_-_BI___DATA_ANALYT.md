### Types of Analytical Techniques in BI –Descriptive

- Descriptive analytics is the process of summarizing and visualizing historical data to provide insights into what has happened in the past.
- Descriptive analytics can help answer questions such as: How many customers visited our website last month? What are the most popular products or services? How satisfied are our customers with our service?
- Descriptive analytics can also help identify patterns, trends, outliers, and correlations in the data, which can be useful for further analysis or decision making.
- The historical data collection for descriptive analytics is done using two main techniques – data aggregation and data mining.
  - Data aggregation is the process of collecting and organizing data into manageable data sets, such as tables, charts, graphs, or dashboards.
  - Data mining is the process of applying various tools and methods to analyze the data and extract useful information, such as summary statistics, frequency distributions, or clustering algorithms.
- Some examples of descriptive analytics techniques are:
  - Descriptive statistics: These are numerical measures that describe the characteristics of a data set, such as mean, median, mode, standard deviation, variance, range, or quartiles.
  - Data visualization: This is the process of presenting data in graphical or pictorial forms, such as bar charts, pie charts, line graphs, scatter plots, or heat maps.
  - Data exploration: This is the process of examining the data to discover its properties, structure, quality, and relationships, such as using histograms, box plots, or correlation matrices.
  - Data profiling: This is the process of assessing the data quality and completeness, such as using data validation, data cleansing, or data auditing techniques.
