### Role of mathematical models in business intelligence

- A mathematical model is an abstract representation of a real-world system or problem that uses mathematical language and symbols.
- A mathematical model can help to understand, analyze, predict, optimize, and control the behavior and performance of a system or problem.
- A business intelligence system provides decision makers with information and knowledge extracted from data, through the application of mathematical models and algorithms .
- The role of mathematical models in business intelligence can be summarized as follows:
  - To transform data into meaningful information and insights that can support decision making.
  - To identify patterns, trends, anomalies, and relationships in data that may not be obvious or intuitive.
  - To test hypotheses, scenarios, and assumptions about the system or problem and evaluate their outcomes and impacts.
  - To optimize the objectives, constraints, and trade-offs of the system or problem and find the best or most feasible solutions.
  - To learn from data and improve the accuracy, efficiency, and effectiveness of the system or problem over time.
- Some examples of mathematical models used in business intelligence are:
  - Descriptive models: These models describe the current or past state of the system or problem, such as statistics, charts, graphs, dashboards, etc.
  - Predictive models: These models forecast the future state or behavior of the system or problem, such as regression, classification, clustering, time series, etc.
  - Prescriptive models: These models recommend the optimal or best actions or decisions for the system or problem, such as optimization, simulation, decision analysis, etc.
  - Learning models: These models adapt and improve their performance based on the feedback or data from the system or problem, such as machine learning, artificial intelligence, neural networks, etc.
