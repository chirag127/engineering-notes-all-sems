### Types of Analytical Techniques in BI –Descriptive

- Descriptive analytics is the process of summarizing and visualizing historical data to provide insights into what has happened in the past.
- Descriptive analytics is the most basic and common type of analytics, and it is often the first step in any data analysis project.
- Descriptive analytics can help answer questions such as: How many customers visited our website last month? What are the most popular products or services we offer? How satisfied are our customers with our service?
- Descriptive analytics can also help identify patterns, trends, outliers, and anomalies in the data, which can inform further analysis or decision making.
- The historical data collection for descriptive analytics is done using two main techniques – data aggregation and data mining.
  - Data aggregation is the process of collecting and organizing data into manageable data sets, such as tables, charts, graphs, or dashboards.
  - Data mining is the process of applying various tools and methods to analyze the data and extract useful information, such as summary statistics, correlations, or associations.
- Some of the common tools and methods used for descriptive analytics are:
  - Frequency tables and histograms: These show the distribution and frequency of values in a data set, such as the number of customers in each age group or the number of sales in each month.
  - Measures of central tendency and dispersion: These show the average, median, mode, range, standard deviation, and variance of a data set, which indicate the center, spread, and variability of the data.
  - Cross-tabulation and contingency tables: These show the relationship between two or more categorical variables, such as the gender and income level of customers or the product category and region of sales.
  - Pivot tables and charts: These allow the user to summarize and rearrange data by different dimensions and measures, such as the total sales by product and month or the average customer satisfaction by service and location.
  - Line charts and time series plots: These show the change and trend of a variable over time, such as the monthly revenue or the daily web traffic.
  - Bar charts and pie charts: These show the comparison and proportion of different categories or groups, such as the market share of different competitors or the percentage of customers in different segments.
  - Scatter plots and correlation coefficients: These show the relationship and strength of association between two numerical variables, such as the price and demand of a product or the age and income of customers.
  - Box plots and outliers: These show the distribution and range of a variable, as well as the minimum, maximum, median, and quartiles of the data, and identify any extreme or unusual values that deviate from the norm.
