# Data Warehousing - Introduction to Data Warehousing

- A data warehouse is a database designed to enable and support business intelligence (BI) activities, especially analytics .
- A data warehouse is a type of data management system that aggregates data from different sources into a single, central, consistent data store.
- A data warehouse is solely intended to perform queries and analysis and often contains large amounts of historical data.
- A data warehouse helps users understand and enhance their organization's performance by providing them with relevant, accurate, and timely information.
- A data warehouse can also support data mining, artificial intelligence (AI), and machine learning applications by providing them with high-quality and structured data.
- A data warehouse typically follows a dimensional model, which consists of fact tables and dimension tables. Fact tables store quantitative measures of business events, while dimension tables store descriptive attributes that provide context to the facts.
- A data warehouse can be implemented using various architectures, such as the star schema, the snowflake schema, the data vault, or the hub-and-spoke.
- A data warehouse can be built using various approaches, such as the top-down approach, the bottom-up approach, or the hybrid approach.
- A data warehouse can be integrated with other data management systems, such as data lakes, data marts, operational data stores, or data virtualization .
- A data warehouse can provide various benefits to an organization, such as improved decision making, enhanced customer service, increased competitive advantage, reduced costs, and increased revenue .
