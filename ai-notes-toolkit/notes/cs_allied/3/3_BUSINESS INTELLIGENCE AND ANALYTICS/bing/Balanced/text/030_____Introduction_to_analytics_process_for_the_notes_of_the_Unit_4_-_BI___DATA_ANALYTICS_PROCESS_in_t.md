### Introduction to analytics process

- Analytics is the process of discovering, interpreting, and communicating significant patterns in data.
- Analytics helps us see insights and meaningful data that we might not otherwise detect.
- Analytics can be applied to different domains, such as website analytics, business analytics, or social media analytics.
- Analytics can be classified into four types: descriptive, predictive, prescriptive, and diagnostic.
  - Descriptive analytics summarizes what has happened in the past using data visualization and reporting tools.
  - Predictive analytics forecasts what might happen in the future using statistical models and machine learning techniques.
  - Prescriptive analytics suggests what actions should be taken to optimize a desired outcome using optimization and simulation methods.
  - Diagnostic analytics explains why something happened in the past using root cause analysis and anomaly detection tools.
- Analytics can be used to support various business goals, such as improving customer satisfaction, increasing revenue, reducing costs, or enhancing decision making.
- Analytics requires a systematic and iterative process that involves the following steps:
  - Define the problem or opportunity: Identify the business question or issue that needs to be addressed using analytics.
  - Collect and prepare the data: Gather the relevant data from various sources and clean, transform, and integrate it for analysis.
  - Analyze the data: Apply the appropriate analytical techniques and tools to explore, model, and test the data.
  - Interpret and communicate the results: Extract the key insights and findings from the analysis and present them in a clear and concise manner to the stakeholders.
  - Implement and monitor the solution: Execute the recommended actions based on the analysis and measure the impact and performance of the solution.
