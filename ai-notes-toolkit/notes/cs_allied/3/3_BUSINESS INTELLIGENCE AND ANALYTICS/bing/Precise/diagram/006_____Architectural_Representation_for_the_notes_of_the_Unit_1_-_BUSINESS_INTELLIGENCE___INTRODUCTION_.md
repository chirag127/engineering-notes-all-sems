### Architectural Representation

Architectural representation is the graphical representation of a building or structure. It is used to communicate design ideas and concepts to clients, contractors, and other stakeholders. Architectural representation can take many forms, including drawings, models, and computer-generated images.

1. **Drawings**: Drawings are the most common form of architectural representation. They can be created by hand or using computer-aided design (CAD) software. Drawings can include floor plans, elevations, sections, and details.

2. **Models**: Physical models are another form of architectural representation. They are typically created using materials such as cardboard, foam board, or wood. Models can be used to show the overall form of a building or to demonstrate how different design elements will work together.

3. **Computer-Generated Images**: Computer-generated images (CGIs) are becoming increasingly popular in architectural representation. They can be used to create photorealistic images of a building or to create animations that show how a building will look from different angles.

Architectural representation is an important part of the design process. It allows architects to communicate their ideas and concepts to others and to test different design options. It is also an essential tool for presenting design proposals to clients and for obtaining planning permission.
