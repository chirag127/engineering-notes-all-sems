### Data, Information, and Knowledge

Data, information, and knowledge are three key concepts in the field of business intelligence and analytics. These concepts are closely related, but they are not the same.

- **Data** refers to raw, unprocessed facts and figures. Data can be collected from various sources, such as sensors, surveys, and transaction records. Data by itself has no meaning and must be processed and organized to be useful.

- **Information** is data that has been processed, organized, and presented in a meaningful way. Information is created by analyzing and interpreting data. For example, sales data can be analyzed to create a report that shows trends in sales over time.

- **Knowledge** is the understanding and insight gained from information. Knowledge is created by interpreting and analyzing information. For example, by analyzing sales trends, a business can gain knowledge about which products are selling well and which are not.

In the context of business intelligence and analytics, data is collected and processed to create information. This information is then analyzed to create knowledge, which can be used to make informed decisions. Understanding the relationship between data, information, and knowledge is essential for effective business intelligence and analytics.
