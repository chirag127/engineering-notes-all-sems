1. Metadata refers to "data about data". It is the information that describes and gives context to the data.
2. Metadata is critical for easily understanding and using data. It provides an organization with a common vocabulary to share and find data, applications, and other resources.
3. Some examples of metadata are:
- Data names, descriptions, and definitions
- Locations of databases and data files
- Technical details about databases and data models
- Owners and stewards of data
- Access permissions and limitations
- Data refresh cycles and latency
4. Benefits of metadata:
- It enables discovery and understanding of available data.
- It enables consistent interpretation and use of data.
- It increases the value and usage of data.
- It supports data governance and security.
- It enables automation and integrations.
