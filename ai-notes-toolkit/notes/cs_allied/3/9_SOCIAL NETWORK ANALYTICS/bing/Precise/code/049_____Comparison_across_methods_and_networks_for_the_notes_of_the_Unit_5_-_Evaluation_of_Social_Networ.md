- Social Network Analysis (SNA) is a method used to study the structure and patterns of relationships between individuals or groups within a network.

- When comparing across methods, it is important to consider the research question and the type of data being analyzed.
- Different methods may be more appropriate for different types of networks or research questions.
- For example, centrality measures such as degree centrality or betweenness centrality may be more appropriate for analyzing the importance of individuals within a network, while community detection methods may be more useful for identifying subgroups within a network.
- When comparing across networks, it is important to consider the characteristics of the networks being compared, such as the size, density, and type of relationships between nodes.
- Different networks may require different methods or techniques for analysis.
