## Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data

1. **Introduction to Social Network Data:** Social network data refers to the information that is generated through the interactions and relationships between individuals or entities within a social network. This data can be used to model and analyze the structure and behavior of the network.

2. **Graph Theory:** Graph theory is a branch of mathematics that is used to model and analyze the relationships between entities in a network. In the context of social network data, a graph is used to represent the network, where nodes represent individuals or entities and edges represent the relationships or interactions between them.

3. **Centrality Measures:** Centrality measures are used to identify the most important or influential nodes in a network. There are several different centrality measures, including degree centrality, closeness centrality, betweenness centrality, and eigenvector centrality.

4. **Community Detection:** Community detection refers to the process of identifying groups of nodes within a network that are more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the network. There are several different algorithms and methods that can be used for community detection, including modularity-based methods, hierarchical clustering, and spectral clustering.

5. **Aggregation of Social Network Data:** Aggregation of social network data refers to the process of combining and summarizing the data in order to extract meaningful insights and patterns. This can be done at different levels of granularity, such as at the individual, group, or network level.

6. **Applications of Social Network Data:** Social network data has a wide range of applications, including in fields such as marketing, public health, and political science. It can be used to understand and predict consumer behavior, to identify and track the spread of diseases, and to analyze the structure and dynamics of political networks.
