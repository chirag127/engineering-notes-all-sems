# Development of Social Network Analysis

Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying social structures through the use of network and graph theories. It is a way to map and measure relationships and flows between people, groups, organizations, computers, or other information/knowledge processing entities.

Here are some key points in the development of social network analysis:

1. The origins of SNA can be traced back to the work of sociologist Georg Simmel in the early 20th century, who studied the patterns of social relationships and interactions.

2. In the 1930s, Jacob Moreno introduced the sociogram, a visual representation of social relationships, which became a key tool in SNA.

3. In the 1950s and 1960s, researchers such as John Barnes and Elizabeth Bott developed the concept of social networks and began to apply mathematical and statistical methods to analyze them.

4. In the 1970s, the field of SNA grew rapidly, with the development of new techniques and software for analyzing social networks.

5. In the 1980s and 1990s, SNA became increasingly popular in a variety of disciplines, including sociology, anthropology, psychology, and organizational studies.

6. In recent years, the rise of online social networks and the availability of large-scale data sets have led to new developments and applications of SNA, including the study of online communities, social media, and the spread of information and influence.

Overall, the development of social network analysis has been characterized by the integration of theoretical, methodological, and empirical advances, and the field continues to evolve and expand.
