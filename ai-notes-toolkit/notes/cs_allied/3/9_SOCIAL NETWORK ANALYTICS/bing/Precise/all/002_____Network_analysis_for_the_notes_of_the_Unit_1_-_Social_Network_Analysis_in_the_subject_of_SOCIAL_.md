# Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

### Network Analysis

Network analysis is a method used to study the relationships between entities in a network. In the context of social network analysis, these entities are often individuals, groups, or organizations, and the relationships between them can represent a variety of interactions, such as friendship, communication, or collaboration.

Some key concepts in network analysis include:

- **Nodes**: These represent the entities in the network, such as individuals or organizations.
- **Edges**: These represent the relationships or connections between the nodes.
- **Degree**: This is a measure of the number of edges connected to a node. In a social network, this could represent the number of friends or contacts a person has.
- **Centrality**: This is a measure of the importance of a node in the network. There are several different ways to calculate centrality, including degree centrality, closeness centrality, and betweenness centrality.
- **Clustering**: This refers to the tendency of nodes to form groups or clusters within the network. In a social network, this could represent the formation of communities or subgroups.

Network analysis can be used to study a wide range of social phenomena, including the spread of information or disease, the formation of groups and communities, and the dynamics of social influence. It can also be used to identify key individuals or organizations within a network, and to understand the patterns of relationships between different entities.
