### Ontological representation of social individuals

Ontological representation of social individuals refers to the way in which individuals are represented in the context of social ontology. Social ontology is a subfield of general ontology that focuses on the basic entities, properties, and kinds studied by the social sciences. There are two kinds of social entities: social individuals and social complexes or collectives.

A closely related view to social ontology is ontological individualism, which is the thesis that social or historical groups, processes, and events are nothing more than complexes of individuals and individual actions . This view precludes explanations that appeal to social factors that cannot in turn be individualistically explained.

One example of an ontological representation of social individuals is the Friend-of-a-Friend (FOAF) ontology, which is an OWL-based format for representing personal information and an individual’s social network. FOAF started as an experimentation with Semantic Web technology.

In summary, ontological representation of social individuals is concerned with how individuals are represented within the framework of social ontology. This can include the use of specific ontologies, such as the FOAF ontology, to represent personal information and social networks.
