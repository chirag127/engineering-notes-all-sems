### Distributed Systems

A distributed system is a collection of independent computers that appears to its users as a single coherent system. The computers in a distributed system communicate with each other through a network, and work together to achieve a common goal.

Here are some key points to remember when studying distributed systems:

1. **Scalability**: Distributed systems can easily scale horizontally by adding more machines to the system. This allows the system to handle more load and data.

2. **Fault tolerance**: Distributed systems are designed to be fault-tolerant, meaning that they can continue to operate even if one or more machines fail.

3. **Consistency**: Ensuring consistency in a distributed system can be challenging, as data may be replicated across multiple machines. Various consistency models and algorithms have been developed to address this issue.

4. **Concurrency**: Distributed systems must be able to handle concurrent access to data and resources by multiple users or processes.

5. **Transparency**: Distributed systems aim to provide transparency to their users, meaning that the system appears as a single, unified system, rather than a collection of independent machines.
