### On the notion of equality

- Equality is a social and political ideal that aims to ensure that every individual within a particular group or society enjoys the same rights, liberties, and possibly status.
- Equality can be understood in different dimensions, such as legal, political, economic, social, and cultural. Each dimension may have different criteria and indicators to measure the degree of equality or inequality among individuals or groups.
- Equality can also be distinguished from equity, which is the principle of fairness and justice in the distribution of resources and opportunities. Equity may require different treatment for different people or groups based on their needs, abilities, or circumstances.
- Social network analysis (SNA) is a research method that investigates social structures through the use of networks and graph theory. It characterizes networked structures in terms of nodes (individual actors, people, or things within the network) and the ties, edges, or links (relationships or interactions) that connect them.
- SNA can be used to study various aspects of equality or inequality in social networks, such as the distribution of power, influence, resources, information, or opportunities among nodes or groups of nodes. SNA can also examine how network structures affect or are affected by social processes, such as cooperation, competition, diffusion, or mobilization.
- SNA can reveal how social networks amplify or mitigate inequalities by fundamental mechanisms of social tie formation, such as homophily (the tendency of similar nodes to connect) and triadic closure (the tendency of two nodes that share a common neighbor to connect). These forces shape social segregation, which is reflected in fragmented or clustered network structures.
- SNA can also show how social networks are influenced by geographical, physical, or administrative boundaries that reinforce or challenge social segregation. For example, SNA can measure how the Web connects or disconnects people and organizations across different regions or countries, and how this affects the level of inequality or diversity in the online space .
- SNA can provide insights and tools for promoting or achieving equality in social networks, such as identifying key actors or groups that can influence or mobilize others, designing interventions or policies that can enhance or reduce network connectivity or diversity, or evaluating the outcomes or impacts of network interventions or policies on equality or inequality indicators.
