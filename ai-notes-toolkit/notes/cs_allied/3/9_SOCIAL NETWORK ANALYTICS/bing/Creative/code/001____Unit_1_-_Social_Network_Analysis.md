## Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

Social network analysis (SNA) is the study of the structure and dynamics of social networks, which are collections of entities (nodes) and their relationships (edges).

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- The basic concepts and terminology of SNA, such as nodes, edges, degree, centrality, density, clustering, path, distance, shortest path, diameter, connected components, network types, network models, etc.
- The methods and tools for collecting, representing, visualizing, and analyzing social network data, such as surveys, interviews, observation, web scraping, network graphs, matrices, adjacency lists, network packages in R and Python, etc.
- The applications and implications of SNA in various domains, such as sociology, psychology, communication, politics, economics, business, health, education, etc.
- The ethical and practical challenges and limitations of SNA, such as privacy, consent, validity, reliability, sampling, generalization, causality, etc.

The learning objectives of this unit are:

- To understand the basic concepts and terminology of SNA and how they relate to social phenomena and processes.
- To acquire the skills and knowledge to collect, represent, visualize, and analyze social network data using appropriate methods and tools.
- To apply SNA to various domains and contexts and evaluate its strengths and weaknesses.
- To critically reflect on the ethical and practical issues and challenges of SNA and how to address them.
