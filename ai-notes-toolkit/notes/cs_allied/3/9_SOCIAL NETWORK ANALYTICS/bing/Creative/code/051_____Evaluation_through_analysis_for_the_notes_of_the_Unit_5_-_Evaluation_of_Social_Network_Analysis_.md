### Evaluation through analysis for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is an approach for examining the relationships (i.e., connections) among individuals, groups, organizations or other entities within a specified network through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA helps the understanding of phenomena that emerge from the interaction of individuals or institutions and is useful to measure the impact, effectiveness and sustainability of complex interventions.
- SNA can be used in different phases of complex healthcare intervention research, such as design, implementation and evaluation.
- SNA can help to answer questions such as:
  - Who are the key actors or stakeholders in a network and what are their roles and positions?
  - How are the actors connected and what are the characteristics of their relationships (e.g., frequency, intensity, reciprocity, trust, etc.)?
  - What are the patterns and structures of the network (e.g., density, centrality, clustering, etc.) and how do they affect the network performance and outcomes?
  - How does the network change over time and what are the factors that influence the network dynamics?
  - How does the network compare to other networks or to a desired state or benchmark?
- SNA can provide both quantitative and qualitative data and can be combined with other methods and tools, such as surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, etc..
- SNA can be conducted at different levels of analysis, such as individual, dyadic, triadic, subgroup or whole network.
- SNA can be applied to different types of networks, such as personal, professional, organizational, social, etc..
- SNA can be used for different purposes, such as:
  - Mapping and describing the network structure and characteristics.
  - Identifying and assessing the network actors and their roles, positions, attributes and behaviors.
  - Analyzing and explaining the network processes and mechanisms that affect the network outcomes.
  - Evaluating and improving the network performance and impact.
  - Designing and implementing network interventions and strategies.
  - Monitoring and learning from the network changes and dynamics.
- SNA can have some challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Defining the network boundaries and scope.
  - Collecting reliable and valid network data.
  - Analyzing and interpreting complex and large network data.
  - Dealing with ethical and privacy issues.
  - Communicating and disseminating network findings and recommendations.
  - Engaging and involving network actors and stakeholders.
- SNA can benefit from some good practices and tips, such as:
  - Clarifying the network research questions and objectives.
  - Choosing the appropriate network data collection methods and tools.
  - Using the suitable network analysis software and techniques.
  - Visualizing and reporting the network results and insights.
  - Providing actionable and feasible network recommendations and solutions.
  - Collaborating and consulting with network experts and practitioners.
