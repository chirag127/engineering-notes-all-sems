### Structural and cognitive effects on scientific performance

- Scientific performance is the ability to produce and disseminate scientific knowledge and innovations.
- Scientific performance can be influenced by various factors, such as individual characteristics, social networks, institutional contexts, and environmental conditions.
- Structural effects refer to the impact of the size, shape, composition, and dynamics of social networks on scientific performance.
- Cognitive effects refer to the impact of the mental processes, such as attention, memory, reasoning, creativity, and problem-solving, on scientific performance.
- Some examples of structural and cognitive effects on scientific performance are:

  - Structural effects:
    - The degree of centrality, or the number and strength of connections, in a social network can affect the access to information, resources, and opportunities for scientific collaboration and recognition.
    - The degree of diversity, or the variety of backgrounds, perspectives, and disciplines, in a social network can affect the generation of novel and interdisciplinary ideas and solutions.
    - The degree of cohesion, or the density and frequency of interactions, in a social network can affect the trust, cooperation, and social support among scientific actors.
    - The degree of evolution, or the change and adaptation, of a social network can affect the responsiveness and resilience to external challenges and opportunities.
  - Cognitive effects:
    - The level of cognitive function, or the efficiency and accuracy of mental processes, can affect the performance on various cognitive tasks, such as learning, memory, attention, reasoning, and problem-solving, that are essential for scientific work  .
    - The level of cognitive reserve, or the ability to cope with brain damage or aging, can affect the maintenance and decline of cognitive function over time .
    - The level of cognitive flexibility, or the ability to switch between different modes of thinking and perspectives, can affect the creativity and innovation in scientific work .
    - The level of cognitive load, or the amount of mental effort and resources required for a task, can affect the performance and efficiency of cognitive function.
