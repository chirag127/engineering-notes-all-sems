### Evaluating smushing for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Smushing is a technique for aggregating social network data by combining nodes that share some common attributes or characteristics.
- Smushing can be used to reduce the complexity and noise of large social networks, and to reveal patterns and insights that are otherwise hidden.
- Smushing can also be used to anonymize social network data by removing or masking the identities of individual nodes, while preserving the overall structure and properties of the network.
- Smushing can be done in different ways, depending on the criteria and goals of the analysis. Some common methods are:
  - Attribute-based smushing: Nodes are grouped by their values on one or more attributes, such as gender, age, location, etc. For example, smushing a social network of students by their major would create a network of majors, where each node represents a group of students who share the same major.
  - Structural-based smushing: Nodes are grouped by their positions or roles in the network, such as hubs, bridges, cliques, communities, etc. For example, smushing a social network of researchers by their co-authorship patterns would create a network of research areas, where each node represents a group of researchers who frequently collaborate with each other.
  - Hybrid smushing: Nodes are grouped by a combination of attribute and structural criteria, such as homophily, similarity, or influence. For example, smushing a social network of Twitter users by their hashtags and retweets would create a network of topics, where each node represents a group of users who tweet about the same or related topics.
- Smushing can be evaluated by comparing the original and aggregated networks on various metrics, such as size, density, diameter, degree distribution, clustering coefficient, modularity, centrality, etc. The evaluation should consider the trade-off between the loss of information and the gain of simplicity and clarity.
- Smushing can also be evaluated by comparing the results of different smushing methods or criteria, and by testing the robustness and validity of the aggregated networks. The evaluation should consider the purpose and context of the analysis, and the assumptions and limitations of the smushing technique.
