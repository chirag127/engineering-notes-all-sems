## Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of network and graph theories.
- SNA can be used to study various phenomena, such as communication patterns, information diffusion, social influence, group cohesion, community detection, and network evolution.
- SNA can be applied to different domains, such as sociology, psychology, computer science, economics, biology, and epidemiology.
- SNA can be performed using different methods, such as surveys, interviews, observations, experiments, and online data collection.
- SNA can be evaluated using different criteria, such as validity, reliability, generalizability, and ethicality.

### Validity
- Validity refers to the extent to which a measurement or a method accurately reflects the concept or phenomenon it is intended to measure or study.
- Validity can be assessed in different ways, such as face validity, content validity, criterion validity, and construct validity.
- Face validity is the degree to which a measurement or a method appears to measure or study what it claims to measure or study, based on common sense or expert judgment.
- Content validity is the degree to which a measurement or a method covers all the relevant aspects of the concept or phenomenon it is intended to measure or study, based on a theoretical framework or a literature review.
- Criterion validity is the degree to which a measurement or a method correlates with or predicts an external criterion that is known to measure or study the same concept or phenomenon, such as a test score or a behavioral outcome.
- Construct validity is the degree to which a measurement or a method measures or studies the underlying construct or theory that it is based on, and not other confounding factors, such as measurement errors or biases.

### Reliability
- Reliability refers to the extent to which a measurement or a method produces consistent and stable results over time and across different conditions or settings.
- Reliability can be assessed in different ways, such as test-retest reliability, inter-rater reliability, internal consistency reliability, and parallel forms reliability.
- Test-retest reliability is the degree to which a measurement or a method produces the same results when repeated over time, under the same or similar conditions or settings.
- Inter-rater reliability is the degree to which a measurement or a method produces the same results when performed by different raters or observers, who are trained or instructed to follow the same criteria or protocol.
- Internal consistency reliability is the degree to which a measurement or a method produces consistent results within itself, such as across different items, indicators, or dimensions that are supposed to measure or study the same concept or phenomenon.
- Parallel forms reliability is the degree to which a measurement or a method produces the same results as another measurement or method that is designed to measure or study the same concept or phenomenon, but with different items, indicators, or dimensions.

### Generalizability
- Generalizability refers to the extent to which a measurement or a method can be applied or extended to different populations, contexts, or situations, beyond the specific sample, setting, or scenario that it is based on or derived from.
- Generalizability can be assessed in different ways, such as external validity, ecological validity, and cross-cultural validity.
- External validity is the degree to which a measurement or a method can be generalized to other populations or groups that are different from the original sample or target population, such as in terms of age, gender, ethnicity, education, or occupation.
- Ecological validity is the degree to which a measurement or a method can be generalized to other contexts or settings that are different from the original setting or environment, such as in terms of location, time, culture, or technology.
- Cross-cultural validity is the degree to which a measurement or a method can be generalized to other cultures or societies that are different from the original culture or society, such as in terms of values, norms, beliefs, or practices.

- Ethicality refers to the extent to which a measurement or a method respects the rights, dignity, and well-being of the participants, stakeholders, and society at large, and does not cause any harm, distress, or injustice to them.
