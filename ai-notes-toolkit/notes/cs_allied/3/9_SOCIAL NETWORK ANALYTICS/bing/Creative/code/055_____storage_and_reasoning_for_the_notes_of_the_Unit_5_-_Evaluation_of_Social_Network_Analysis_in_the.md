### Storage and Reasoning for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the study of the structure, dynamics, and functions of social networks, which are sets of actors and their relations.
- SNA can be used to understand various social phenomena, such as communication, collaboration, influence, trust, diffusion, and community detection.
- SNA requires data collection, preparation, modeling, analysis, and interpretation, which are the main stages of the SNA process.
- Evaluation of SNA is the process of assessing the quality, validity, and usefulness of the SNA results, as well as the ethical and practical implications of the SNA process.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done at different levels, such as the data level, the network level, the actor level, and the application level.
- Evaluation of SNA can involve various methods and criteria, such as descriptive statistics, inferential statistics, network metrics, visualization, comparison, validation, verification, sensitivity analysis, robustness analysis, and impact analysis.
- Evaluation of SNA can help to improve the SNA process, to identify the strengths and limitations of the SNA results, to communicate the SNA findings, and to support the decision making based on the SNA results.
- Evaluation of SNA can also pose some challenges and issues, such as data quality, data privacy, data availability, data representation, data analysis, data interpretation, data generalization, data contextualization, and data ethics.
