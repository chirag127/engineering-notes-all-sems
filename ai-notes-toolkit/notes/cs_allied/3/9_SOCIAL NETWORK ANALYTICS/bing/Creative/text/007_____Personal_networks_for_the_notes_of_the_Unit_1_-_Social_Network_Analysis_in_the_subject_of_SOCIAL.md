### Personal networks

- Personal networks are subsets of social networks that focus on the relationships and interactions of a specific individual or ego.
- Personal networks can be used to study and understand social support, which is the provision of emotional, instrumental, informational, or appraisal resources by others.
- Personal network analysis involves collecting data on the structure, composition, and functional content of personal networks, and applying network measures and techniques to analyze them .
- Personal network structure refers to the patterns of connections among the alters (the people who are connected to the ego) in a personal network, such as density, centrality, clustering, and brokerage.
- Personal network composition refers to the attributes of the alters and the ego, such as demographics, roles, attitudes, and behaviors.
- Personal network functional content refers to the types and quality of resources and support that are exchanged between the ego and the alters, such as emotional, instrumental, informational, or appraisal support.
- Personal network analysis can reveal how personal networks vary across individuals and groups, how they affect the well-being and outcomes of the ego, and how they change over time and contexts.
- Personal network analysis can also be used to design and evaluate interventions that aim to enhance social support and improve health and social outcomes.
- Personal network analysis is a way of conceptualizing, describing, and modeling society as sets of people or groups linked to one another by specific relationships .
- Personal network analysis is a part of social network analysis, which is a broader field of data analytics that uses networks and graph theory to understand social structures .
