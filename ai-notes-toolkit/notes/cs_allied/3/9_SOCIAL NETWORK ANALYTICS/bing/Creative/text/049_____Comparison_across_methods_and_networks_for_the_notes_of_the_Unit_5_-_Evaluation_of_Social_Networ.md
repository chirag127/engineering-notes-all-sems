### Comparison across methods and networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a set of techniques for studying the structure and dynamics of social relationships among actors, such as individuals, groups, organizations, or countries.
- SNA can be applied to different types of networks, such as personal networks, communication networks, affiliation networks, citation networks, or collaboration networks.
- SNA can use different methods to collect, represent, analyze, and visualize network data, such as surveys, interviews, observation, archival records, matrices, graphs, indices, models, or software tools  .
- SNA can be used for different purposes, such as describing network properties, identifying network patterns, testing network hypotheses, explaining network phenomena, or evaluating network interventions  .
- SNA can be compared across methods and networks based on various criteria, such as validity, reliability, generalizability, scalability, complexity, or applicability  .
- Some examples of comparison across methods and networks are:

  - Comparing the validity and reliability of different methods for collecting network data, such as surveys versus observation, or self-reports versus third-party reports .
  - Comparing the generalizability and scalability of different methods for analyzing network data, such as descriptive indices versus inferential models, or local measures versus global measures .
  - Comparing the complexity and applicability of different methods for visualizing network data, such as graphs versus matrices, or static images versus dynamic animations .
  - Comparing the structure and dynamics of different types of networks, such as personal networks versus communication networks, or affiliation networks versus citation networks .
  - Comparing the patterns and phenomena of different types of networks, such as centrality versus density, or homophily versus heterophily .
  - Comparing the hypotheses and explanations of different types of networks, such as social capital versus social influence, or diffusion versus contagion .
  - Comparing the interventions and evaluations of different types of networks, such as network formation versus network change, or network effects versus network outcomes  .
