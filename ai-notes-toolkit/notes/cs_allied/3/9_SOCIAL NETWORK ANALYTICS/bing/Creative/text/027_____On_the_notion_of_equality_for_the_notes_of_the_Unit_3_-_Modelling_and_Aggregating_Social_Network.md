### On the notion of equality for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Equality is a social and ethical concept that implies that all individuals or groups have the same rights, opportunities, and status in a society.
- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of investigating social structures and relationships through the use of networks and graph theory .
- SNA can be used to study various aspects of equality, such as:
  - How social networks affect the distribution of resources, information, and influence among individuals or groups.
  - How social networks reflect or reinforce social inequalities based on attributes such as gender, race, class, or education.
  - How social networks enable or constrain social movements and collective action for social change.
- Some key concepts and measures in SNA that are relevant for studying equality are:
  - Homophily: the tendency of individuals to associate with others who are similar to them in some attributes .
  - Segregation: the degree to which individuals or groups are separated or isolated from each other in a network.
  - Centrality: the importance or influence of a node or a group of nodes in a network, based on their position or connections .
  - Cohesion: the degree to which nodes are connected or reachable within a group or a network .
  - Brokerage: the role of a node or a group of nodes in connecting or facilitating the flow of resources or information between otherwise disconnected nodes or groups .
  - Community: a subset of nodes that are more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the network .
