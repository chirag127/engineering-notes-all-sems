### Optimizing goodness of fit for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a statistical model fits the observed data. It can be used to compare different models and select the best one for a given dataset.
- In social network analysis, goodness of fit can be applied to various types of models, such as exponential random graph models (ERGMs), relational event models (REMs), or two-mode networks.
- ERGMs are models that specify the probability of observing a network based on a set of structural features, such as density, reciprocity, transitivity, or homophily. ERGMs can capture complex dependencies among network ties and account for network heterogeneity.
- REMs are models that describe the occurrence and timing of events in a network, such as communication, collaboration, or conflict. REMs can capture the dynamics and evolution of network structures over time and account for temporal dependencies among events.
- Two-mode networks are networks that consist of two sets of nodes, such as actors and events, and links between nodes of different sets, such as participation or affiliation. Two-mode networks can reveal the patterns of interaction and association among actors and events, and the duality of social structure and agency.
- To assess the goodness of fit of social network models, various methods and criteria can be used, such as:
  - Likelihood ratio tests: These tests compare the log-likelihood of a model with a null model or a nested model, and determine whether the model provides a significantly better fit to the data than the null or nested model.
  - Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC): These criteria balance the fit and complexity of a model, and penalize models that have too many parameters. The model with the lowest AIC or BIC is preferred.
  - Graphical diagnostics: These methods compare the observed network statistics with the simulated network statistics from the fitted model, and check whether the model can reproduce the features of the observed network. Examples of graphical diagnostics are histograms, boxplots, or scatterplots of network statistics, such as degree distribution, geodesic distance, triad census, or edgewise shared partners.
  - Predictive accuracy: These methods evaluate how well a model can predict future or unseen network data, such as events or ties. Examples of predictive accuracy measures are mean squared error, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, or precision and recall.
- To optimize the goodness of fit of social network models, various strategies can be used, such as:
  - Model selection: This involves choosing the most appropriate type of model for the research question and the data, and selecting the relevant parameters or features to include in the model. Model selection can be guided by theory, data exploration, or statistical criteria.
  - Model specification: This involves defining the functional form and the distributional assumptions of the model, and ensuring that the model is identifiable and estimable. Model specification can be informed by previous studies, simulation experiments, or sensitivity analysis.
  - Model estimation: This involves finding the best-fitting values of the model parameters that maximize the likelihood or minimize the error of the model. Model estimation can be performed by various algorithms, such as Markov chain Monte Carlo, maximum pseudolikelihood, or stochastic approximation.
  - Model validation: This involves checking the robustness and reliability of the model results, and assessing the uncertainty and variability of the model parameters. Model validation can be done by various techniques, such as bootstrap, cross-validation, or posterior predictive checks.
