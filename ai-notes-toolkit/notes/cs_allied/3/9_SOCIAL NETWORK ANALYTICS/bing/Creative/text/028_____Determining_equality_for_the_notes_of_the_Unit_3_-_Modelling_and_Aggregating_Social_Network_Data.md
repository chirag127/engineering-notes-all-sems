### Determining equality for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Equality is a concept that refers to the degree of similarity or difference between two or more entities, such as individuals, groups, or networks.
- Equality can be measured in various ways, depending on the context and the purpose of the analysis.
- Some common dimensions of equality are:
  - **Structural equality**: the extent to which the entities have the same or similar positions, roles, or connections in a network. For example, two nodes are structurally equal if they have the same degree, centrality, or clustering coefficient.
  - **Attribute equality**: the extent to which the entities have the same or similar values or characteristics on one or more attributes. For example, two nodes are attribute equal if they have the same gender, age, or occupation.
  - **Relational equality**: the extent to which the entities have the same or similar patterns or types of relationships with other entities. For example, two nodes are relationally equal if they have the same number and strength of ties, or the same balance or reciprocity of ties.
- Determining equality can help to understand the structure, dynamics, and outcomes of social networks, such as:
  - Identifying clusters, communities, or roles based on structural, attribute, or relational similarity or difference.
  - Explaining the formation, maintenance, or dissolution of ties based on structural, attribute, or relational homophily or heterophily.
  - Predicting the behavior, performance, or influence of actors based on structural, attribute, or relational advantages or disadvantages.
