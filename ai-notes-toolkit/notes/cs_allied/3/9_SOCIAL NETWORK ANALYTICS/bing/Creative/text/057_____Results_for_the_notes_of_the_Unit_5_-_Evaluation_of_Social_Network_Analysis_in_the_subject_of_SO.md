### Results for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Evaluation of social network analysis (SNA) is the process of assessing the quality, validity, and usefulness of SNA methods, models, and applications.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done at different levels, such as:
  - Data level: evaluating the quality and reliability of the network data, such as completeness, accuracy, timeliness, and representativeness.
  - Method level: evaluating the appropriateness and effectiveness of the SNA methods, such as metrics, algorithms, and visualizations, for the network data and the research questions.
  - Model level: evaluating the fit and predictive power of the SNA models, such as network models, generative models, and statistical models, for the network data and the research hypotheses.
  - Application level: evaluating the impact and value of the SNA applications, such as network interventions, network recommendations, and network analytics, for the network stakeholders and the social outcomes.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done using different criteria, such as:
  - Internal validity: the extent to which the SNA results are consistent and robust within the network data and the SNA methods.
  - External validity: the extent to which the SNA results are generalizable and transferable to other network data and contexts.
  - Reliability: the extent to which the SNA results are reproducible and replicable by other researchers using the same or similar network data and SNA methods.
  - Utility: the extent to which the SNA results are relevant and useful for the network stakeholders and the social goals.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done using different methods, such as:
  - Descriptive statistics: summarizing and describing the network data and the SNA results using numerical and graphical measures, such as mean, standard deviation, histogram, and boxplot.
  - Inferential statistics: testing and comparing the network data and the SNA results using statistical tests and models, such as t-test, ANOVA, regression, and correlation.
  - Sensitivity analysis: examining and measuring the effect of changing the network data or the SNA methods on the SNA results, such as adding or removing nodes or edges, changing the network boundary or the network sampling, and varying the network metrics or the network algorithms.
  - Benchmarking: evaluating and ranking the SNA results using standard or reference values, such as ground truth, best practices, or state-of-the-art methods.
  - Case studies: illustrating and explaining the SNA results using specific and detailed examples, such as real-world networks, success stories, or failure cases.
