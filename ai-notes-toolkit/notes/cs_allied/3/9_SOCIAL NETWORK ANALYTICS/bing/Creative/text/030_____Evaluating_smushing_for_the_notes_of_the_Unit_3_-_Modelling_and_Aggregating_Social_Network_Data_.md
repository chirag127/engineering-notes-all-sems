### Evaluating smushing for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Smushing is a technique for aggregating social network data by combining nodes that share some common attributes or characteristics.
- Smushing can help reduce the complexity and noise of large social networks, and reveal patterns and insights that are otherwise hidden.
- Smushing can also help anonymize social network data by removing identifying information from individual nodes, such as names, locations, or contact details.
- However, smushing also has some drawbacks and challenges, such as:
  - Smushing can result in information loss and distortion, as some nodes and edges may be eliminated or merged.
  - Smushing can introduce bias and error, as the choice of attributes and criteria for smushing may affect the outcome and interpretation of the aggregated network.
  - Smushing can be computationally expensive and time-consuming, as it requires finding and matching nodes based on multiple attributes and criteria.
  - Smushing can be difficult to validate and verify, as it may not be clear how the original network data was transformed and aggregated.
- Therefore, smushing should be used with caution and care, and only when it is appropriate and necessary for the research question and the network data.
- Some examples of smushing applications are:
  - Smushing social media users based on their hashtags, keywords, or topics of interest, to identify communities and influencers.
  - Smushing web pages based on their domain names, keywords, or links, to analyze the structure and content of the web.
  - Smushing locations based on their geographic coordinates, names, or regions, to visualize and compare the spatial distribution of social network activity.
