## Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of network and graph theories.
- SNA can be used to study various phenomena, such as communication patterns, information diffusion, social influence, group cohesion, community detection, and network evolution.
- SNA can be applied to different domains, such as sociology, psychology, computer science, economics, biology, and epidemiology.
- SNA can be performed on different types of data, such as texts, images, videos, audio, and online interactions.
- SNA can be conducted at different levels of analysis, such as individual, dyadic, triadic, group, or network.

- Evaluation of SNA is the process of assessing the quality, validity, reliability, and usefulness of SNA methods, models, and results.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done for different purposes, such as testing hypotheses, comparing alternative approaches, improving existing techniques, or informing decision making.
- Evaluation of SNA can be based on different criteria, such as accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, sensitivity, F1-score, AUC, ROC, R2, MSE, MAE, RMSE, NRMSE, MAPE, SMAPE, MASE, correlation, causation, significance, robustness, scalability, efficiency, interpretability, generalizability, and applicability.
- Evaluation of SNA can be performed using different methods, such as experiments, simulations, surveys, interviews, case studies, meta-analyses, and reviews.
- Evaluation of SNA can be influenced by different factors, such as data quality, data availability, data preprocessing, data representation, data analysis, data visualization, data interpretation, data communication, and data ethics.
