- Evaluation of SNA can be done at different levels, such as data, model, method, measure, and application level.
- Data level evaluation focuses on the quality and suitability of the data used for SNA, such as its completeness, accuracy, reliability, representativeness, and timeliness.
- Model level evaluation examines the assumptions and limitations of the models used to represent and analyze social networks, such as graph models, matrix models, and statistical models.
- Method level evaluation compares and contrasts the strengths and weaknesses of different SNA methods and techniques, such as centrality, clustering, community detection, diffusion, and influence analysis.
- Measure level evaluation evaluates the properties and performance of different SNA measures and metrics, such as degree, closeness, betweenness, eigenvector, and PageRank centrality, modularity, density, diameter, and average path length.
- Application level evaluation assesses the impact and value of SNA results for specific domains and contexts, such as social media, health, education, business, and security.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done using different criteria, such as validity, reliability, robustness, scalability, interpretability, and usability.
- Evaluation of SNA can also involve different stakeholders, such as researchers, practitioners, users, and beneficiaries, who may have different perspectives and expectations from SNA results.
- Evaluation of SNA can be challenging due to the complexity, diversity, and dynamics of social network data, models, methods, measures, and applications, as well as the lack of standard benchmarks, guidelines, and best practices for SNA evaluation.
