## Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

Social network analysis (SNA) is the study of the structure, dynamics, and properties of social networks, which are collections of entities (nodes) and their relationships (edges).

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- The basic concepts and terminology of SNA, such as nodes, edges, degree, centrality, density, clustering, path, distance, shortest path, diameter, connected components, network types, and network models.
- The methods and tools for collecting, representing, visualizing, and analyzing social network data, such as adjacency matrix, adjacency list, graph, networkx, Gephi, and igraph.
- The applications and implications of SNA in various domains, such as sociology, psychology, communication, politics, economics, health, education, and online platforms.
- The challenges and limitations of SNA, such as ethical issues, data quality, sampling bias, network boundaries, and causality inference.

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Define and identify the basic elements and properties of social networks.
- Use appropriate methods and tools to collect, represent, visualize, and analyze social network data.
- Apply SNA to understand and explain real-world phenomena and problems in different domains.
- Evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of SNA and its assumptions.
