### The macro-structure of social networks

- A social network is a social structure made up of a set of social actors (such as individuals or organizations), sets of dyadic ties, and other social interactions between actors.
- The macro-structure of social networks refers to the overall pattern of relationships (network structure) at the aggregate level, as well as the effects of social networks for various macrohistorical outcomes, such as civil uprising, state formation, policy diffusion, and economic development .
- The macro-structure of social networks can be analyzed from three levels: microlevel, mesolevel, and macrolevel.
  - The microlevel focuses on the behavior of the individual actors and their attributes, such as their motivations, preferences, strategies, and outcomes.
  - The mesolevel focuses on the social networks that are organized by the social institutions and institutionalized social relationships, such as families, groups, organizations, and communities.
  - The macrolevel focuses on the social networks that span across different social domains and regions, such as global and national networks, inter-organizational networks, and social movements.
- The macro-structure of social networks can be measured and visualized by using various methods and techniques, such as graph theory, matrix algebra, network models, network metrics, and network diagrams .
- The macro-structure of social networks can reveal the properties and dynamics of the whole social system, such as its density, centrality, clustering, cohesion, homophily, heterogeneity, diffusion, and influence .
- The macro-structure of social networks can also explain the patterns and mechanisms of social change and social action, such as how social networks enable or constrain collective action, how social networks facilitate or hinder information and innovation diffusion, how social networks shape or reflect power and inequality, and how social networks generate or resolve social conflicts .
