### Comparison across methods and networks for social network analysis

Social network analysis (SNA) is a research method that focuses on patterns of relations among people and groups, such as organizations and states. SNA can be applied to various types of networks, such as web hyperlinks, communication flows, friendship ties, collaboration networks, etc. SNA can also use different methods to analyze network data, such as descriptive statistics, graphical models, matrix algebra, network algorithms, etc.

To compare across methods and networks for SNA, one needs to consider the following aspects:

- The research question and the purpose of the analysis. Different methods and networks may be more suitable for different types of questions and goals, such as describing network structure, identifying influential actors, detecting communities, testing hypotheses, etc.
- The data availability and quality. Different methods and networks may require different levels of data completeness, accuracy, and validity. For example, some methods may need longitudinal data, while others may need attribute data. Some networks may be easier to collect and measure than others, such as web hyperlinks versus friendship ties.
- The assumptions and limitations of the methods and networks. Different methods and networks may have different underlying assumptions and limitations that affect their validity and reliability. For example, some methods may assume that the network is static, while others may account for network dynamics. Some networks may be more prone to measurement errors, biases, or ethical issues than others, such as communication flows versus collaboration networks.
- The results and interpretations of the methods and networks. Different methods and networks may produce different results and interpretations that need to be compared and evaluated. For example, some methods may provide more descriptive statistics, while others may provide more inferential statistics. Some networks may reveal more structural properties, while others may reveal more relational properties.

To illustrate, let us consider an example of a research question and two possible methods and networks for SNA:

- Research question: How do scientists collaborate in different disciplines?
- Method 1: Co-authorship network analysis. This method uses co-authorship data from publications to construct a network of scientists, where nodes are scientists and links are co-authorship relations. This method can measure network properties such as degree centrality, clustering coefficient, modularity, etc. to describe the structure and dynamics of scientific collaboration.
- Method 2: Citation network analysis. This method uses citation data from publications to construct a network of scientists, where nodes are scientists and links are citation relations. This method can measure network properties such as prestige, authority, hub, etc. to describe the influence and impact of scientific collaboration.
- Network 1: Physics network. This network consists of scientists who publish in physics journals. This network may have a high density, low diversity, and high modularity, indicating a high level of collaboration, homophily, and specialization in physics.
- Network 2: Interdisciplinary network. This network consists of scientists who publish in interdisciplinary journals. This network may have a low density, high diversity, and low modularity, indicating a low level of collaboration, heterophily, and integration across disciplines.

To compare across methods and networks for this research question, one may consider the following points:

- The purpose of the analysis is to describe and compare the patterns of scientific collaboration in different disciplines. Both methods and networks are suitable for this purpose, as they can provide different perspectives and insights on the research question.
- The data availability and quality may vary depending on the source and scope of the publications. Co-authorship data may be more complete and accurate than citation data, as co-authorship relations are more direct and observable than citation relations. However, citation data may be more valid and reliable than co-authorship data, as citation relations are more indicative and reflective of scientific collaboration than co-authorship relations.
- The assumptions and limitations of the methods and networks may affect the results and interpretations. Co-authorship network analysis may assume that co-authorship relations are symmetric and reciprocal, while citation network analysis may assume that citation relations are asymmetric and directional. Physics network may be more representative and generalizable than interdisciplinary network, as physics is a more established and coherent discipline than interdisciplinary studies.
- The results and interpretations of the methods and networks may differ and complement each other. Co-authorship network analysis may reveal more about the structure and dynamics of scientific collaboration, such as who collaborates with whom, how often, and how long. Citation network analysis may reveal more about the influence and impact of scientific collaboration, such as who cites whom, how much, and why. Physics network may show more similarities and differences within the discipline, while interdisciplinary network may show more similarities and differences across disciplines.
