### Evaluation of web-based social network extraction

- Web-based social network extraction is the process of identifying and describing the actors and their relations from online sources, such as web pages, social media platforms, blogs, etc.  
- Web-based social network extraction can benefit many applications, such as web mining, social network analysis, recommender systems, sentiment analysis, etc. 
- Web-based social network extraction can be performed using different techniques, such as superficial methods, supervised methods, unsupervised methods, and hybrid methods.   
- Superficial methods rely on the textual similarity between web pages to determine the actors and their relations. They are simple and fast, but they may not capture the semantic meaning of the web content.  
- Supervised methods use labeled data and machine learning algorithms to extract the actors and their relations from web pages. They can achieve high accuracy and precision, but they require a lot of training data and domain knowledge. 
- Unsupervised methods use clustering, topic modeling, or graph analysis to discover the actors and their relations from web pages without any prior knowledge. They can handle large and diverse data, but they may suffer from noise and ambiguity. 
- Hybrid methods combine different techniques to leverage their strengths and overcome their weaknesses. They can achieve better performance and robustness, but they may be complex and computationally expensive. 
- Evaluation of web-based social network extraction can be done using different criteria, such as completeness, correctness, consistency, coverage, scalability, efficiency, etc.  
- Evaluation of web-based social network extraction can also be done using different metrics, such as precision, recall, F1-score, accuracy, NMI, modularity, etc.  
- Evaluation of web-based social network extraction can help to compare different techniques, identify their strengths and weaknesses, and improve their performance and quality.
