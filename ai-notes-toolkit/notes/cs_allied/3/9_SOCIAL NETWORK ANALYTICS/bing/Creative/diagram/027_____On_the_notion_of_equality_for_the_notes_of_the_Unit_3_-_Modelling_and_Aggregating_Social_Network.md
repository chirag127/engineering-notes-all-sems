### On the notion of equality

- Equality is a complex and contested concept that can be understood in different ways depending on the context and perspective.
- In social network analysis, equality can refer to various aspects of the structure and dynamics of social networks, such as:
  - The distribution of resources, opportunities, and outcomes among network actors, such as income, power, influence, status, health, etc. This is also known as distributive equality or equity.
  - The quality of relationships and interactions among network actors, such as respect, recognition, inclusion, participation, etc. This is also known as relational equality or social equality.
  - The similarity or difference of network actors in terms of their attributes, preferences, behaviors, etc. This is also known as homophily or heterophily.
  - The equivalence or diversity of network actors in terms of their positions, roles, functions, etc. in the network. This is also known as positional equality or structural equivalence.
- Equality can have different implications and consequences for social network analysis, such as:
  - Equality can be a normative goal or value that guides the design, evaluation, and intervention of social networks, such as promoting fairness, justice, solidarity, etc. This is also known as ethical or political equality.
  - Equality can be a descriptive or explanatory variable that characterizes the features, patterns, and processes of social networks, such as measuring inequality, diversity, similarity, etc. This is also known as analytical or empirical equality.
  - Equality can be a causal or predictive factor that influences the outcomes and impacts of social networks, such as affecting performance, innovation, cooperation, etc. This is also known as functional or instrumental equality.
- Equality is not a fixed or static notion, but a dynamic and contextual one that can change over time and across situations. Therefore, social network analysis needs to adopt a flexible and nuanced approach to understand and address the multiple dimensions and implications of equality in social networks.
