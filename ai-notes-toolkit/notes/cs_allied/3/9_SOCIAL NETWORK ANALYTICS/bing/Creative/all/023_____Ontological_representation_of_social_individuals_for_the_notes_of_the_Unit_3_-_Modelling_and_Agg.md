# Ontological Representation of Social Individuals

- Ontology is the study of the nature and categories of being and existence.
- Social ontology is the subfield of ontology that deals with the entities, properties and kinds that arise from social interaction, such as social groups, norms, roles, institutions, etc.
- Ontological individualism is the thesis that social or historical phenomena are nothing more than complexes of individuals and individual actions, and that social explanations must be reducible to individualistic ones.
- Ontological representation is the process of modeling the concepts and relations of a domain using a formal language, such as logic, RDF, OWL, etc.
- Ontological representation of social individuals is the task of describing the attributes, preferences, behaviors and relations of human agents in a social context using an ontological language.
- Ontological representation of social individuals can enable knowledge sharing, processing, reuse, capturing and communication among different agents and systems, as well as support social network analysis, personalization, recommendation, etc.
- One example of ontological representation of social individuals is the Friend-of-a-Friend (FOAF) ontology, which is an OWL-based format for representing personal information and social networks of individuals.
- The FOAF ontology defines classes and properties for describing basic information about a person, such as name, gender, age, email, homepage, etc., as well as their social relations, such as friends, family, colleagues, etc.
- The FOAF ontology also allows for linking to other ontologies and vocabularies, such as Dublin Core, GeoNames, SKOS, etc., to enrich the representation of social individuals with additional information, such as interests, hobbies, locations, topics, etc.
- The FOAF ontology can be used to create FOAF profiles, which are RDF documents that describe a person and their social network using the FOAF vocabulary. FOAF profiles can be published on the web and linked to each other, forming a distributed and decentralized social graph.
