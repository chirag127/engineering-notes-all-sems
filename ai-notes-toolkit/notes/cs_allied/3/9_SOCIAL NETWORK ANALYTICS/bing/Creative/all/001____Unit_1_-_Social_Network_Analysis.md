## Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the study of the structure, patterns, and dynamics of social relationships among individuals, groups, or organizations.
- SNA uses mathematical and computational methods to represent and analyze social networks as graphs, where nodes are actors (such as people, organizations, or events) and edges are ties (such as friendship, collaboration, or communication).
- SNA can be used to answer various questions about social phenomena, such as how information, influence, or diseases spread in a network, how groups or communities form and evolve, how network structure affects individual or collective behavior, and how network interventions can improve social outcomes.
- SNA can be applied to various domains, such as sociology, psychology, economics, political science, public health, computer science, and communication.
- SNA involves several steps, such as defining the research question, collecting and preprocessing network data, choosing appropriate network measures and models, performing network analysis and visualization, and interpreting and reporting the results.
- SNA requires some basic concepts and terminology, such as:

  - Degree: the number of ties a node has with other nodes in the network.
  - Path: a sequence of nodes and edges that connects two nodes in the network.
  - Distance: the length of the shortest path between two nodes in the network.
  - Diameter: the maximum distance among any pair of nodes in the network.
  - Centrality: a measure of the importance or influence of a node in the network, based on its position, degree, or connections.
  - Density: the ratio of the number of actual edges to the number of possible edges in the network.
  - Clustering: the tendency of nodes to form groups or communities within the network, based on their shared ties or attributes.
  - Homophily: the tendency of nodes to associate with similar nodes in the network, based on their attributes or preferences.
  - Heterophily: the tendency of nodes to associate with dissimilar nodes in the network, based on their attributes or preferences.
  - Modularity: a measure of the quality of the network partition into communities, based on the density of edges within and between communities.
  - Network type: a classification of the network based on its characteristics, such as:

    - Directed or undirected: whether the edges have a direction or not, indicating the direction of the relationship or interaction.
    - Weighted or unweighted: whether the edges have a weight or not, indicating the strength or intensity of the relationship or interaction.
    - Binary or valued: whether the edges have a binary value (0 or 1) or a continuous value, indicating the presence or absence of the relationship or interaction.
    - Simple or complex: whether the network has only one type of node and edge or multiple types of nodes and edges, indicating the diversity or multiplexity of the relationship or interaction.
    - Static or dynamic: whether the network is fixed or changes over time, indicating the stability or evolution of the relationship or interaction.
