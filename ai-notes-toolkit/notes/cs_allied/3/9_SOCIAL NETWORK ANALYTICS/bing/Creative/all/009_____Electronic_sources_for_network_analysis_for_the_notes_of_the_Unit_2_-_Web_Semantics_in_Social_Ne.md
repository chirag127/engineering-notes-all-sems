# Electronic sources for network analysis

- Network analysis is the process of determining the electrical parameters of a circuit or a network of interconnected components, such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, voltage sources, current sources, etc. 
- Electronic sources for network analysis are devices that provide electrical energy to the circuit or network, such as batteries, generators, solar cells, etc. 
- Electronic sources can be classified into two types: independent sources and dependent sources. 
- Independent sources are sources that have a fixed value of voltage or current, regardless of the rest of the circuit. For example, a battery is an independent voltage source that provides a constant voltage across its terminals. 
- Dependent sources are sources that have a value of voltage or current that depends on another voltage or current in the circuit. For example, a current-controlled voltage source is a dependent voltage source that provides a voltage proportional to a current elsewhere in the circuit. 
- Electronic sources can also be classified into two types: ideal sources and practical sources. 
- Ideal sources are sources that have no internal resistance or impedance, meaning that they can provide any amount of power to the circuit without any loss. For example, an ideal voltage source can maintain a constant voltage across its terminals, regardless of the current drawn from it. 
- Practical sources are sources that have some internal resistance or impedance, meaning that they cannot provide unlimited power to the circuit and have some voltage drop or power loss. For example, a practical voltage source has a finite internal resistance, which causes the voltage across its terminals to decrease as the current drawn from it increases. 
- Electronic sources for network analysis can be used to model and simulate various electrical phenomena, such as power generation, distribution, transmission, consumption, etc. 
- Electronic sources for network analysis can also be used to study and analyze various social phenomena, such as communication, collaboration, influence, diffusion, etc. 
- Electronic sources for network analysis can be accessed and manipulated using various tools and methods, such as search engines, databases, software, algorithms, etc.
