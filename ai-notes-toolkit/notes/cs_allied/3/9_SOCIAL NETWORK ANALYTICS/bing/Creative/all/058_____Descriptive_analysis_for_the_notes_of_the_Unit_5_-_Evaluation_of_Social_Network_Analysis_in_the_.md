# Descriptive analysis for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Descriptive analysis is the process of summarizing and visualizing the characteristics and patterns of social networks, such as the size, density, centrality, clustering, and community structure of the network.
- Descriptive analysis can help to understand the nature and dynamics of social interactions, identify the key actors and groups, and compare different networks or subnetworks.
- Descriptive analysis can be performed using various methods and tools, such as graphs, matrices, tables, charts, and network measures.
- Some of the common network measures used in descriptive analysis are:
  - Degree: the number of ties that a node has with other nodes in the network. It indicates the popularity or activity of a node.
  - Betweenness: the extent to which a node lies on the shortest paths between other nodes in the network. It indicates the influence or brokerage of a node.
  - Closeness: the average distance from a node to all other nodes in the network. It indicates the accessibility or efficiency of a node.
  - Eigenvector: the weighted sum of the degrees of a node's neighbors in the network. It indicates the prestige or status of a node.
  - Density: the ratio of the number of actual ties to the number of possible ties in the network. It indicates the cohesion or connectedness of the network.
  - Diameter: the longest shortest path between any pair of nodes in the network. It indicates the reachability or dispersion of the network.
  - Clustering coefficient: the proportion of a node's neighbors that are also neighbors of each other. It indicates the tendency of the network to form cliques or subgroups.
  - Modularity: the extent to which the network can be divided into communities or clusters of nodes that are more densely connected within than between them. It indicates the heterogeneity or diversity of the network.
- Descriptive analysis can be applied to various types of social networks, such as personal networks, organizational networks, online networks, and global networks.
- Descriptive analysis can provide valuable insights for social network analysis, but it cannot explain the causes or consequences of the network structure or behavior. For that, inferential analysis or modeling is needed.
