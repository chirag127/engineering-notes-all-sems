# Representing identity for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Representing identity is the process of capturing and describing the characteristics and relationships of the actors (individuals or entities) in a social network.
- Representing identity can help to understand the social structure, behavior, and dynamics of the network, as well as to identify the influential or representative actors, the communities or groups, and the patterns or trends in the network.
- Representing identity can be done using different methods and techniques, such as:
  - Social network analysis (SNA): a methodology for researching and evaluating an interconnected set of actors and their relationships using graphs and metrics .
  - Network science: a field of data analytics that uses networks and graph theory to understand complex systems, such as social structures.
  - Deep representation learning: a branch of machine learning that uses neural networks to learn high-level features and embeddings from network data, such as node attributes, link types, and network structure.
- Representing identity can involve different types of data and analysis, such as:
  - Socio-graph representations: a way of modeling social networks using nodes and edges to represent actors and their relationships, respectively.
  - Node attributes: the properties or features of the actors in the network, such as age, gender, location, interests, etc.
  - Link types: the categories or labels of the relationships between actors in the network, such as friendship, collaboration, communication, etc.
  - Network structure: the shape or pattern of the network, such as density, diameter, clustering, etc.
  - Network metrics: the numerical measures of the network or its components, such as degree, centrality, modularity, etc.
  - Network visualization: the graphical representation of the network or its components, such as nodes, edges, colors, shapes, etc.
- Representing identity can have different ethical implications and challenges, such as:
  - Privacy and consent: the protection of the personal information and preferences of the actors in the network, and the permission to collect and use their data.
  - Bias and fairness: the avoidance of discrimination or favoritism of certain actors or groups in the network, and the promotion of diversity and inclusion.
  - Validity and reliability: the accuracy and consistency of the data and analysis of the network, and the verification and replication of the results.
