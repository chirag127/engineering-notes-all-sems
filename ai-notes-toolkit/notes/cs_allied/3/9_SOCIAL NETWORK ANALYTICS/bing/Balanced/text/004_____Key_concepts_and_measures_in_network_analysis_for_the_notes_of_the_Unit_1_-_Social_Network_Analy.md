### Key concepts and measures in network analysis

- Network analysis is a method of studying the structure and behavior of complex systems composed of many interacting elements, such as social networks, computer networks, biological networks, etc.
- Network analysis involves collecting data on the elements (nodes) and their relationships (edges) in a network, and applying mathematical and computational techniques to analyze and visualize the network properties and patterns.
- Some of the key concepts and measures in network analysis are:

  - **Nodes and edges**: Nodes are the basic units of a network, such as individuals, organizations, devices, genes, etc. Edges are the connections or interactions between nodes, such as friendships, collaborations, communications, influences, etc. Nodes and edges can have attributes, such as name, age, gender, weight, type, etc.
  - **Degree**: Degree is the number of edges connected to a node. It measures the local popularity or activity of a node. Nodes with high degree are called hubs or central nodes. Degree can be divided into in-degree (number of incoming edges) and out-degree (number of outgoing edges) for directed networks, where edges have a direction or orientation.
  - **Path and distance**: A path is a sequence of edges that connects two nodes in a network. The distance between two nodes is the length of the shortest path between them. It measures how close or far two nodes are in a network. The average distance of all pairs of nodes in a network is called the characteristic path length. It measures the global efficiency or navigability of a network.
  - **Clustering coefficient**: Clustering coefficient is the fraction of possible triangles that exist in a network. A triangle is a set of three nodes that are all connected to each other. Clustering coefficient measures the local density or cohesion of a network. It indicates how likely two nodes are to be connected if they share a common neighbor. The average clustering coefficient of all nodes in a network is called the global clustering coefficient. It measures the overall tendency of a network to form clusters or communities.
  - **Centrality**: Centrality is a measure of the importance or influence of a node or an edge in a network. There are different types of centrality, such as degree centrality, closeness centrality, betweenness centrality, eigenvector centrality, etc. Each type of centrality captures a different aspect of the node's or edge's role or position in a network. For example, degree centrality measures the local connectivity, closeness centrality measures the global reachability, betweenness centrality measures the control over information flow, and eigenvector centrality measures the influence of neighbors.
  - **Modularity**: Modularity is a measure of the quality of a partition of a network into subgroups or communities. A community is a group of nodes that are more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the network. Modularity compares the actual number of edges within communities to the expected number of edges if the network were random. Modularity ranges from -1 to 1, where higher values indicate better partitions. Modularity can be used to detect and evaluate communities in a network.
