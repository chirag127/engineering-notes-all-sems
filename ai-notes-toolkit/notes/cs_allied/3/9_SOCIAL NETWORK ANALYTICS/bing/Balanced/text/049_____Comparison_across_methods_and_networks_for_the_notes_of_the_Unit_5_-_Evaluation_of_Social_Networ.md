### Comparison across methods and networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a set of techniques for studying the structure and dynamics of social relationships among actors, such as individuals, groups, organizations, or countries .
- SNA can be applied to different types of networks, such as personal networks, communication networks, affiliation networks, citation networks, collaboration networks, or trade networks .
- SNA can use different methods for collecting, representing, analyzing, and visualizing network data, such as surveys, interviews, observation, archival records, matrices, graphs, centrality measures, density measures, clustering methods, or network models .
- SNA can be used for different purposes, such as describing network patterns, identifying influential actors, detecting communities, testing network hypotheses, or evaluating network interventions .
- SNA can be compared across methods and networks based on several criteria, such as validity, reliability, generalizability, scalability, complexity, or applicability .
- Some examples of comparison across methods and networks are:

  - Comparing the validity and reliability of different methods for collecting personal network data, such as name generators, position generators, or egocentric surveys.
  - Comparing the generalizability and scalability of different methods for analyzing large-scale communication networks, such as sampling methods, network models, or machine learning methods.
  - Comparing the complexity and applicability of different methods for visualizing collaboration networks, such as graph layouts, node-link diagrams, or matrix representations.
  - Comparing the structure and dynamics of different types of networks, such as affiliation networks and citation networks, based on their density, centrality, clustering, or homophily.
  - Comparing the influence and performance of different actors in different networks, such as individuals in personal networks and organizations in trade networks, based on their degree, closeness, betweenness, or eigenvector centrality.
  - Comparing the outcomes and impacts of different network interventions, such as creating new ties, strengthening existing ties, or removing ties, in different contexts and domains.
