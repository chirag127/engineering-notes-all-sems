## Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying the structure, patterns, and dynamics of social relationships and interactions among individuals, groups, organizations, or communities.
- SNA can be used for various purposes, such as understanding social phenomena, identifying influential actors, detecting communities, predicting behaviors, and evaluating interventions.
- Evaluation of SNA involves assessing the quality, validity, reliability, and usefulness of the data, methods, models, and results of SNA.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done at different stages of the research process, such as before, during, and after data collection, analysis, and interpretation.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done by using different criteria, such as accuracy, completeness, consistency, relevance, timeliness, and ethics of the data; appropriateness, rigor, and transparency of the methods; fit, robustness, and generalizability of the models; and clarity, coherence, and applicability of the results.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done by using different techniques, such as data quality checks, sensitivity analysis, cross-validation, replication, triangulation, peer review, and stakeholder feedback.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done by using different sources, such as literature review, expert opinion, empirical evidence, and user experience.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done by using different perspectives, such as internal, external, formative, summative, process, outcome, and impact evaluation.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done by using different standards, such as scientific, practical, ethical, and social standards.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done by using different frameworks, such as logic models, theory of change, evaluation questions, indicators, and metrics.
