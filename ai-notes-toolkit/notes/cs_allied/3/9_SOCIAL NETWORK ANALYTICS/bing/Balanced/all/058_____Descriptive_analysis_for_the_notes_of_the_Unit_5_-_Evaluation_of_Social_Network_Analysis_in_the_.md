# Descriptive Analysis for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA studies the behavior of the individual at the micro level, the pattern of relationships (network structure) at the macro level, and the interactions between the two.
- SNA is based on theoretical constructs of sociology and mathematical foundations of graph theory.
- SNA can be used to study various phenomena, such as communication, collaboration, influence, diffusion, power, trust, and social capital .
- SNA can be applied to different domains, such as health, education, business, politics, media, and the Web  .
- SNA uses various methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and visualize network data, such as surveys, interviews, observation, content analysis, web scraping, network metrics, centrality measures, clustering, community detection, and network graphs  .
- SNA can be performed on different types of networks, such as whole (socio-centric) networks, egocentric networks, two-mode networks, and dynamic networks .
- SNA can provide insights into the structure, function, and evolution of social networks, as well as the roles, positions, and behaviors of network actors  .
- SNA can also help to identify and evaluate the effects of network interventions, such as network formation, network change, network disruption, and network enhancement .
- SNA faces some challenges and limitations, such as ethical issues, data quality, data availability, data complexity, data analysis, data interpretation, and data generalization  .
