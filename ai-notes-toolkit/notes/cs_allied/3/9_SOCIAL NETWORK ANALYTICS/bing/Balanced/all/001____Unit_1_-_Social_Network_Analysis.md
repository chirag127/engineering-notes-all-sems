## Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA measures and maps the flow of relationships and relationship changes between knowledge-possessing entities.
- SNA is based on theoretical constructs of sociology and mathematical foundations of graph theory.
- SNA is useful for examining the social structure and interdependencies (or work patterns) of individuals or organizations.
- SNA characterizes networked structures in terms of nodes (individual actors, people, or things within the network) and the ties, edges, or links (relationships or interactions) that connect them.
- SNA can be used to study various types of social networks, such as friendship, collaboration, communication, information, influence, trust, disease transmission, etc.
- SNA can be applied to various domains, such as public health, education, business, politics, sociology, psychology, etc.
- SNA can be performed using various methods, such as surveys, interviews, observations, experiments, archival data, online data, etc.
- SNA can be visualized using various tools, such as graphs, matrices, maps, etc.
- SNA can be analyzed using various metrics, such as degree, centrality, density, cohesion, clustering, etc.
