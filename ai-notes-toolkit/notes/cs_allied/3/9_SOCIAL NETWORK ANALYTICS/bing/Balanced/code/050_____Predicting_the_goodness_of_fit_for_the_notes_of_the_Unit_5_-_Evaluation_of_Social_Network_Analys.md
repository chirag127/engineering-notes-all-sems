# Predicting the goodness of fit for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying the patterns and structures of relationships between individuals or groups in a network.
- SNA can be used to understand the dynamics, influences, and outcomes of social interactions in various domains, such as public health, sociology, psychology, and education.
- Evaluation of SNA is the process of assessing the quality, validity, and usefulness of SNA methods and models for a given research question or application.
- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a SNA model fits the observed network data, that is, how well it captures the essential features and characteristics of the network.
- Goodness of fit can be evaluated using various criteria, such as:
  - Statistical tests, such as chi-square tests, likelihood ratio tests, or Bayesian information criterion, that compare the observed network statistics with the expected statistics under the model  .
  - Graphical methods, such as Q-Q plots, density plots, or network visualizations, that compare the distribution of network statistics or the structure of network graphs between the observed and simulated data .
  - Sensitivity analysis, such as bootstrap or jackknife methods, that assess the stability and robustness of the model parameters and predictions to different samples or subsets of the data .
  - Model comparison, such as cross-validation or out-of-sample prediction, that compare the performance and accuracy of different models or model specifications on the same or new data .
- Goodness of fit can help to:
  - Select the most appropriate and parsimonious model for the data and the research question.
  - Identify the strengths and limitations of the model and the data.
  - Diagnose and correct potential problems or errors in the model or the data.
  - Provide evidence and support for the validity and reliability of the model and the data.
  - Communicate and interpret the results and implications of the model and the data.
