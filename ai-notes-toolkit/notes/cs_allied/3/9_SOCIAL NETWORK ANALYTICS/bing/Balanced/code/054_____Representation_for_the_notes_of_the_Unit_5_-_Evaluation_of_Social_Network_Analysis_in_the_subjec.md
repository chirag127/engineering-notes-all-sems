# Representation for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Social network analysis (SNA) is an evaluation approach that uses mathematics and visualization to represent the structure of relationships between people, organizations, goals, interests, and other entities within a larger system.
- SNA helps to understand the phenomena that emerge from the interaction of individuals or institutions and is useful to assess the impact, effectiveness, and sustainability of interventions that aim to change or improve social networks.
- SNA can be used to find the type, importance, and quantity of relationships in a social network, allowing for a graphical output in conjunction with mathematical analysis.
- SNA can be applied in various domains, such as health, education, development, business, and policy, to answer different evaluation questions, such as:
  - Who are the key actors or influencers in a network and what are their roles and positions?
  - How are the resources, information, or knowledge distributed or exchanged in a network and what are the patterns or flows of these exchanges?
  - How are the network members connected or disconnected and what are the factors that affect their connectivity or isolation?
  - How does the network structure affect the performance, behavior, or outcomes of the network members or the network as a whole?
  - How does the network change over time and what are the causes or consequences of these changes?
- SNA involves the following steps:
  - Define the evaluation purpose and scope, including the network boundaries, the types of relationships, and the level of analysis (individual, group, or network).
  - Collect the network data, using methods such as surveys, interviews, observation, or secondary sources, and ensure the data quality and validity.
  - Analyze the network data, using software tools such as UCINET, NetDraw, Gephi, or NodeXL, and calculate network metrics such as density, centrality, cohesion, or subgroups.
  - Visualize the network data, using graphs, maps, or matrices, and apply colors, shapes, or sizes to highlight the network features or attributes.
  - Interpret the network data, using the evaluation questions, the network theory, and the contextual knowledge, and compare the network results with other sources of evidence or benchmarks.
  - Report and disseminate the network findings, using clear and concise language, graphics, and tables, and provide recommendations or implications for the network stakeholders or decision-makers.
