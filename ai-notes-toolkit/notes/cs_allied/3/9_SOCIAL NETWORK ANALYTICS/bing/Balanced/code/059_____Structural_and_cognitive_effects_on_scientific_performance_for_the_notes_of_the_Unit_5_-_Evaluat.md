### Structural and cognitive effects on scientific performance

- Scientific performance is the ability to conduct, communicate and apply scientific research in various domains and contexts.
- Scientific performance can be influenced by both structural and cognitive factors, such as the brain structure and function, the cognitive abilities and skills, the motivation and attitude, the social and environmental factors, and the feedback and evaluation mechanisms.
- Structural factors refer to the physical and biological aspects of the brain that enable and constrain cognitive processes, such as memory, attention, reasoning, problem-solving, creativity, etc.
- Cognitive factors refer to the mental and psychological aspects of the cognitive processes that affect and are affected by scientific performance, such as learning, knowledge, intelligence, metacognition, self-regulation, etc.
- Structural and cognitive factors can interact and influence each other in complex and dynamic ways, depending on the individual, the task, the context and the time.
- Some examples of structural and cognitive effects on scientific performance are:

  - Chronic cocaine use can change the brain structure and cognitive function in rhesus monkeys, leading to impaired memory, attention and executive function.
  - Aging can affect the memory and brain structure in humans, resulting in reduced hippocampal volume, altered neurotransmitter levels and increased inflammation.
  - Creatine supplementation can enhance the cognitive performance and brain function in healthy individuals, especially in tasks that require high mental effort and working memory.
  - Transcranial magnetic stimulation can modulate the brain activity and cognitive performance in humans, depending on the location, intensity and frequency of the stimulation.
  - Physical exercise can improve the cognitive functioning and well-being in children and adolescents, by increasing the blood flow, oxygen and nutrients to the brain, and by stimulating the neurogenesis, synaptic plasticity and neurotrophic factors.
  - Music can boost the cognitive performance and mood in humans, by activating the reward, emotion and memory systems in the brain, and by enhancing the attention, creativity and problem-solving skills.

- Structural and cognitive effects on scientific performance can have implications for the evaluation of social network analysis, as they can affect the quality, validity and reliability of the data, methods and results of the analysis, as well as the interpretation, communication and application of the findings.
