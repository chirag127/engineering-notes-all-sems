### Storage and Reasoning for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the study of the structure, dynamics, and functions of social networks, which are sets of actors and their relations.
- SNA can be used to understand various phenomena, such as social influence, diffusion, collaboration, community detection, and network evolution.
- To perform SNA, one needs to collect, store, and analyze network data, which can be challenging due to the complexity, heterogeneity, and volume of such data.
- Some of the steps involved in SNA are:
  - Problem definition: Identify the research question, the unit of analysis, the level of analysis, and the scope of the network.
  - Data gathering and preparation: Collect network data from various sources, such as surveys, interviews, observations, archives, or online platforms. Clean, validate, and format the data for analysis.
  - Social network modeling: Represent the network data as graphs, where nodes are actors and edges are relations. Define the attributes of nodes and edges, and the types of relations (e.g., binary, valued, directed, undirected, etc.).
  - Social network analysis: Apply various methods and techniques to measure and visualize the network structure and properties, such as centrality, density, clustering, distance, etc. Interpret the results in relation to the research question and the network context.
  - Evaluation: Assess the validity, reliability, and generalizability of the network analysis. Compare the results with other studies or theories, and identify the limitations and implications of the analysis.
  - Presentation: Communicate the network analysis to the intended audience, using appropriate formats, such as tables, charts, graphs, maps, etc. Explain the main findings, conclusions, and recommendations of the analysis.

- Some of the challenges and issues in SNA are:
  - Data quality: Network data may be incomplete, inaccurate, or outdated, due to sampling errors, measurement errors, or data decay. Network data may also be sensitive or confidential, requiring ethical and legal considerations for data collection and storage.
  - Data integration: Network data may come from different sources, formats, and domains, requiring data integration techniques to combine and harmonize the data. Data integration may also involve data transformation, aggregation, or anonymization.
  - Data storage: Network data may be large or dynamic, requiring efficient and scalable data storage solutions, such as relational databases, graph databases, or distributed systems. Data storage may also involve data compression, indexing, or partitioning.
  - Data analysis: Network data may be complex or heterogeneous, requiring advanced and flexible data analysis methods and tools, such as graph algorithms, machine learning, or natural language processing. Data analysis may also involve data mining, visualization, or reasoning.
