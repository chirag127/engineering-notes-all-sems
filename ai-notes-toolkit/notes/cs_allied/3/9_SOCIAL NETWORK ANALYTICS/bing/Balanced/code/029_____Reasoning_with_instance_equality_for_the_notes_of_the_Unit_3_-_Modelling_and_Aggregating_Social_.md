### Reasoning with instance equality

- Reasoning is the inference of new statements (facts) that necessarily follow from the set of known statements.
- Instance equality is the assumption that two entities in a social network are the same if they have the same name or identifier.
- Reasoning with instance equality can help us to aggregate and analyze social network data by:
  - Reducing the complexity and ambiguity of the data by eliminating duplicate or inconsistent entities.
  - Enabling the application of logical rules and inference methods to derive new facts and relations from the existing data.
  - Enhancing the quality and accuracy of the social network analysis by ensuring that the data is consistent and coherent.
- Some examples of reasoning with instance equality are:
  - If Alice and Bob are friends on Facebook, and Alice and Bob are also friends on Twitter, then we can infer that the Alice and Bob on Facebook are the same as the Alice and Bob on Twitter.
  - If John is a follower of Mary on Instagram, and John is also a follower of Mary on LinkedIn, then we can infer that the John and Mary on Instagram are the same as the John and Mary on LinkedIn.
  - If David is a co-author of a paper with Emma, and David is also a co-author of a paper with Frank, then we can infer that the David and Emma in the first paper are the same as the David and Emma in the second paper.
