### Results for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of network and graph theories.
- SNA can be used to study various aspects of social networks, such as their structure, dynamics, evolution, diffusion, influence, and behavior.
- Evaluation of SNA is the process of assessing the quality, validity, reliability, and usefulness of SNA methods, models, and results.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done at different levels, such as data, network, model, and application level.
- Data level evaluation focuses on the quality and accuracy of the data used for SNA, such as the sources, sampling, representation, and measurement of the data.
- Network level evaluation focuses on the properties and characteristics of the network derived from the data, such as the size, density, degree distribution, centrality, clustering, and community structure of the network.
- Model level evaluation focuses on the validity and reliability of the models used for SNA, such as the assumptions, parameters, and fit of the models to the data and the network.
- Application level evaluation focuses on the usefulness and impact of the SNA results for the specific domain or problem, such as the insights, recommendations, and interventions derived from the SNA.
- Evaluation of SNA can be done using various methods and criteria, such as descriptive statistics, inferential statistics, hypothesis testing, goodness-of-fit, sensitivity analysis, robustness analysis, comparison, and benchmarking.
- Evaluation of SNA can also involve various stakeholders, such as the analysts, the clients, the users, and the experts, who can provide feedback, opinions, and judgments on the SNA process and outcomes.
- Evaluation of SNA is important for ensuring the quality and credibility of SNA, as well as for improving and enhancing the SNA practice and research.
