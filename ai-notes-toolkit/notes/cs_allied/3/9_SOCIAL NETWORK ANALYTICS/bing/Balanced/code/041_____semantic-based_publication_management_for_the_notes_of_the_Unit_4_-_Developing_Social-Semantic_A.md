### Semantic-based Publication Management

- Semantic-based publication management is the process of organizing, presenting, and sharing scientific publications using semantic web technologies, such as RDF, OWL, RSS, and FOAF  .
- Semantic web technologies enable the representation of the metadata and the content of the publications in a machine-readable and interoperable way, allowing for more efficient information retrieval, data integration, and knowledge discovery.
- Semantic-based publication management can be applied to different scenarios, such as:
  - Personal publication management: individual researchers can maintain and update their own publication lists using RSS feeds and FOAF profiles, and link them to other researchers and publications in the same domain .
  - Community publication management: research communities can aggregate and filter publications from different sources using semantic queries and reasoning, and provide customized views and recommendations based on user preferences and profiles .
  - Semantic publishing: publishers can enrich their publications with semantic annotations and links to external resources, such as datasets, ontologies, and related works, and make them available on the web as documents accompanied by semantic markup .
- Semantic-based publication management can provide several benefits, such as:
  - Improving the visibility and accessibility of publications on the web, by making them more searchable and discoverable by humans and machines .
  - Enhancing the quality and usability of publications, by providing more context and background information, and enabling interactive features, such as navigation, visualization, and annotation .
  - Facilitating the reuse and integration of publications, by enabling the linking and merging of data and knowledge from different sources and domains .
  - Supporting the evaluation and analysis of publications, by enabling the extraction and aggregation of metrics and indicators, such as citations, impact, and popularity .
