### The global structure of networks

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a general study of the social network using network and graph theory concepts  .
- A social network is a set of actors (nodes) and the relationships (edges) between them  .
- SNA examines the behavior of the individual actors at the micro level, the pattern of relationships (network structure) at the macro level, and the interactions between the two .
- SNA can be used to analyze various types of social structures, such as social media networks, information circulation, business networks, knowledge networks, collaboration graphs, kinship, disease transmission, and sexual relationships   .
- SNA can help understand the phenomena that emerge from the interaction of actors or institutions, such as social influence, diffusion, contagion, cohesion, centrality, brokerage, and power   .
- SNA can also help identify the opportunities and challenges for actors or institutions to achieve their goals, such as innovation, learning, collaboration, communication, and intervention   .
- SNA relies on the mathematical foundations of graph theory, which provides the tools and methods to represent, measure, and analyze the properties and patterns of networks  .
- Some of the key concepts and measures of graph theory that are relevant for SNA are: nodes, edges, degree, path, distance, shortest path, diameter, connectedness, component, cluster, density, centrality, betweenness, closeness, eigenvector, PageRank, modularity, community, and network visualization  .
- SNA can be performed using various methods and tools, such as surveys, interviews, observations, experiments, simulations, web scraping, social media APIs, network databases, and network analysis software   .
- SNA can be applied to various domains and disciplines, such as sociology, psychology, anthropology, history, economics, management, education, public health, political science, and computer science     .
