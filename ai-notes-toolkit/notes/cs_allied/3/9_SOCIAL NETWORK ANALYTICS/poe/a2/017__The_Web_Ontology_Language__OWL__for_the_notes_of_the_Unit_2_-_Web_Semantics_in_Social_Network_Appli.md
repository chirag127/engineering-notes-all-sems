### The Web Ontology Language (OWL)

1. OWL is a semantic markup language for publishing and sharing ontologies on the World Wide Web.
2. OWL ontologies provide a vocabulary for representing knowledge as a set of concepts within a domain and the relationships between those concepts.
3. OWL is designed for use by applications that need to process the content of information instead of just presenting information to humans.
4. OWL facilitates greater machine interpretability of Web content than that supported by XML, RDF, and RDF Schema (RDF-S) by providing additional vocabulary along with a formal semantics.
5. OWL comes in three increasingly expressive sublanguages: OWL Lite, OWL DL, and OWL Full.
6. OWL Lite supports those users primarily needing a classification hierarchy and simple constraints. OWL DL supports those users who need more expressivity while retaining computational completeness and decidability. OWL Full is meant for users who want maximum expressivity and the syntactic freedom of RDF with no computational guarantees.
7. The additional vocabulary and formal semantics of OWL provide:
- Explicit semantics for describing properties and classes: for example, the meaning of properties, the nature of classes, and the nature of their instances.
- Classification of classes and relations based on their properties and relations.
- Inference of new information based on the structure of the classes and properties and on instance data.
- Published mappings to other ontology languages and the RDF and RDF Schema vocabularies.
