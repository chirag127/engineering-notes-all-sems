### Representation for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

1. Centrality measures: Degree centrality, Betweenness centrality, Closeness centrality.
- Degree centrality: Number of direct connections a node has. Nodes with high degree centrality are more influential.
- Betweenness centrality: Ability of a node to act as a bridge along the shortest path between two other nodes. Nodes with high betweenness centrality control the flow of information.
- Closeness centrality: Average length of the shortest paths to access all other nodes from a given node. Nodes with high closeness centrality can reach other nodes quickly and have greater access to information.

2. Centralization: Measure of how centralized a network is around one or a few nodes. A highly centralized network depends on its central nodes while a decentralized network does not have key central nodes.
- Degree centralization: Centralization around nodes with highest degrees.
- Betweenness centralization: Centralization around nodes with highest betweenness.
- Closeness centralization: Centralization around nodes with highest closeness.

3. Network structure: Structure of a network refers to the pattern of connections between nodes. Different network structures have different properties.
- Scale-free network: Few nodes with very high degrees and many nodes with low degrees. Power law distribution.
- Small-world network: High local clustering and short average path lengths.
- Random network: Random connections between nodes with no discernible pattern. Low clustering and short path lengths.
- Regular network: Nodes have the same number of connections and are arranged in a regular pattern. High clustering and long path lengths.
