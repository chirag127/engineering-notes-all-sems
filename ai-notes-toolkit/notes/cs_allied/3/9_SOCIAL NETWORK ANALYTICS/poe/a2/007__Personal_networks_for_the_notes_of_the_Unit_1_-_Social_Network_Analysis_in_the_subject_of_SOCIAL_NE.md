### Personal networks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

1. Personal network: The web of social relationships that surround an individual person. It includes the specific people that someone interacts with regularly.
2. Network size: The number of people in a personal network. Larger networks provide access to more resources but can be harder to maintain.
3. Network diversity: The variety of people in a network in terms of characteristics such as age, gender, race, occupation, etc. More diverse networks can provide access to more perspectives and resources.
4. Network structure: The pattern of connections between people in a network. This could be dense with many connections or sparse with fewer connections. The structure affects how information and resources flow through a network.
5. Strong vs weak ties: Strong ties are close, frequent, and reciprocal relationships. Weak ties are looser, less frequent, and less reciprocal. Weak ties can be useful for accessing new information and opportunities, while strong ties are important for emotional support.
6. Centrality: A measure of a person's influence or importance in a network. People who are more central have more connections or shorter paths to others. Centrality affects access to information and resources.
