### The Global Structure of Networks

Networks can be defined as a set of nodes that are connected by edges. Social network analysis (SNA) is the study of these networks and how they evolve over time. The global structure of networks can be studied by analyzing their characteristics such as size, density, centrality, and clustering. In this unit, we will discuss the global structure of networks in detail.

Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- The size of a network refers to the number of nodes it contains. The larger the network, the more difficult it becomes to maintain and analyze.
- The density of a network refers to the proportion of connections that exist between nodes in the network. A network with high density is said to be tightly connected, whereas a network with low density is loosely connected.
- Centrality is a measure of the importance of a node in a network. This can be measured in terms of degree centrality, which is the number of connections a node has to other nodes in the network, or betweenness centrality, which is the number of times a node lies on the shortest path between two other nodes in the network.
- Clustering is a measure of the extent to which nodes in a network tend to cluster together. A network with high clustering is said to have tightly-knit communities, while a network with low clustering is more dispersed.

Understanding the global structure of networks is essential for analyzing how they function and evolve over time. By studying the characteristics of networks, we can gain insight into how information and resources flow through them, and how they can be used to facilitate the spread of ideas and influence within a community.

In conclusion, the global structure of networks is a complex and multifaceted subject that requires careful analysis and attention to detail. By studying the characteristics of networks, we can gain a deeper understanding of how they function, and how they can be used to promote social change and progress.
