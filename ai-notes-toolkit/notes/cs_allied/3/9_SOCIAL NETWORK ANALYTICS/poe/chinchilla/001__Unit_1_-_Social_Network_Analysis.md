## Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

Social Network Analysis (SNA) is a methodological approach for studying social structures by examining relationships between individuals, groups, and organizations. SNA is commonly used in sociology, anthropology, psychology, and other social sciences to study social phenomena such as social influence, information diffusion, and power dynamics.

### Key Concepts

- Nodes: Individuals, groups, or organizations that make up a social network.
- Edges: Connections or relationships between nodes in a social network.
- Degree: The number of connections or relationships that a node has in a social network.
- Centrality: A measure of the importance or influence of a node in a social network.
- Clustering: A measure of the degree to which nodes in a social network are connected to each other.
- Path: A sequence of nodes and edges that connects two nodes in a social network.

### Data Collection

- Data can be collected from various sources such as surveys, interviews, social media, and online communities.
- Data can be either qualitative or quantitative, and can include attributes such as demographics, interests, and behaviors.
- Data can be represented in different formats, including matrices, graphs, and networks.

### Network Metrics

- Degree centrality: The number of connections that a node has in a social network.
- Betweenness centrality: The degree to which a node acts as a bridge between other nodes in a social network.
- Closeness centrality: The degree to which a node is central to the overall network based on the shortest path to other nodes.
- Eigenvector centrality: The degree to which a node is connected to other important nodes in a social network.
- Clustering coefficient: The degree to which nodes in a social network are connected to each other.

### Visualization

- Network visualization is the process of representing social networks graphically.
- Visualization can help identify patterns and structures in social networks.
- Network visualization can be done using software such as Gephi or NodeXL.

### Applications

- Social Network Analysis can be used to study a wide range of social phenomena, including social influence, information diffusion, and power dynamics.
- SNA can be used in various fields such as marketing, public health, and political science.
- SNA can be used to identify key players in a network, predict behavior, and design interventions to influence network behavior.

### Limitations

- SNA is limited by the quality and quantity of data available.
- SNA can be time-consuming and complex, requiring specialized skills and software.
- SNA can be limited by the assumptions and limitations of network metrics used.
