### Neuron

A neuron is a fundamental unit of a neural network that is responsible for processing and transmitting information. It consists of three main parts:

1. Dendrites: These are the input terminals of the neuron that receive signals from other neurons or external stimuli.

2. Cell Body (Soma): The cell body processes the incoming signals and integrates them. It decides whether to generate an output or not.

3. Axon: The output signal generated by the cell body is transmitted through the axon, which acts as a wire-like structure to connect with other neurons or to transmit the output signal to a target.

The neuron model is based on the idea of threshold logic, where the neuron output is binary, i.e., it fires or does not fire. The firing of a neuron depends on the input signals it receives and a threshold value. The threshold value determines the minimum strength of the input signal required for the neuron to generate an output.

The mathematical model of a neuron is based on the concept of a weighted sum. The input signals received by the dendrites are multiplied by their respective weights, and the resulting products are summed up. The sum is then passed through an activation function, which determines the firing of the neuron.

The most commonly used activation function is the sigmoid function, which maps the weighted sum to a value between 0 and 1. If the sigmoid function output is greater than a threshold value, the neuron fires, otherwise, it does not.

In summary, a neuron is a basic unit of a neural network that receives input signals, processes them, and generates an output signal. The neuron model is based on the concept of threshold logic and a weighted sum, with the output determined by an activation function.
