# Various phases of data analytics lifecycle

Data analytics lifecycle is a framework that guides the process of transforming data into insights and actions that can help achieve business goals. It consists of several phases that may vary depending on the context and the methodology used, but generally include the following :

- **Understanding the business issue**: This phase involves defining the problem or opportunity that the data analytics project aims to address, as well as the objectives, scope, and expected outcomes. It also involves identifying the stakeholders, their needs, and their expectations.
- **Understanding the data set**: This phase involves collecting, exploring, and assessing the available data sources that are relevant to the business issue. It also involves understanding the data quality, structure, format, and limitations, as well as the ethical and legal implications of using the data.
- **Preparing the data**: This phase involves cleaning, transforming, integrating, and enriching the data to make it suitable for analysis. It also involves creating subsets, samples, or partitions of the data for different purposes, such as training, testing, or validation.
- **Exploratory analysis**: This phase involves applying various techniques, such as descriptive statistics, visualization, or clustering, to explore the data and discover patterns, trends, outliers, or anomalies. It also involves generating and testing hypotheses, as well as identifying the most relevant features or variables for the analysis.
- **Validation**: This phase involves applying various techniques, such as inferential statistics, machine learning, or optimization, to validate the findings and hypotheses from the exploratory analysis. It also involves evaluating the performance, accuracy, and reliability of the analytical models or methods, as well as their assumptions and limitations.
- **Communication and deployment**: This phase involves communicating the results and insights from the validation phase to the stakeholders, using appropriate formats, such as reports, dashboards, or presentations. It also involves deploying the analytical models or methods into production, monitoring their performance, and updating them as needed.
