### Real time sentiment analysis
- Real time sentiment analysis is a machine learning technique that automatically recognizes and extracts the sentiment in a text whenever it occurs .
- Sentiment is the subjective opinion or attitude expressed in a text, such as positive, negative, or neutral.
- Real time sentiment analysis is most commonly used to analyze brand and product mentions in live social comments and posts .
- Real time sentiment analysis can help businesses and organizations to monitor customer feedback, identify trends, improve customer service, and gain insights into their target audience .

#### Applications of real time sentiment analysis
- Some of the applications of real time sentiment analysis are :
  - Social media monitoring: analyzing the sentiment of tweets, posts, comments, reviews, etc. to understand how people feel about a brand, product, service, event, or topic.
  - Customer service: detecting the sentiment of customer queries, complaints, feedback, etc. to respond appropriately and improve customer satisfaction and loyalty.
  - Market research: measuring the sentiment of market surveys, focus groups, interviews, etc. to identify customer preferences, needs, expectations, and satisfaction.
  - Product development: analyzing the sentiment of product reviews, ratings, feedback, etc. to improve product features, quality, and usability.
  - Crisis management: identifying the sentiment of news articles, blogs, forums, etc. to assess the impact of a crisis, manage reputation, and mitigate risks.

#### Steps of real time sentiment analysis
- The process of real time sentiment analysis follows these four steps:
  - Breaking down the text into components: sentences, phrases, tokens, and parts of speech.
  - Identifying each phrase and component.
  - Assigning a sentiment score to each phrase with plus or minus points.
  - Combining scores for a final sentiment analysis.

#### Challenges of real time sentiment analysis
- Some of the challenges of real time sentiment analysis are:
  - Dealing with noisy, unstructured, and informal data from live social and online feeds.
  - Handling sarcasm, irony, humor, slang, emojis, and other nuances of natural language.
  - Accounting for different contexts, domains, cultures, and languages.
  - Balancing speed and accuracy of sentiment analysis models.
