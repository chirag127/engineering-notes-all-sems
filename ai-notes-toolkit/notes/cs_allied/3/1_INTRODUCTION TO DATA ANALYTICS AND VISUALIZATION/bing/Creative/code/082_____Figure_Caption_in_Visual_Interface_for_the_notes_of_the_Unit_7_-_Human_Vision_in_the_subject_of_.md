### Figure Caption in Visual Interface

- A figure caption is a text that accompanies a figure in a visual interface, such as a chart, a graph, a map, or an image.
- A figure caption serves several purposes, such as:
  - Providing a title or a summary of the figure
  - Explaining the data source, the variables, the units, and the scale of the figure
  - Highlighting the main findings, trends, patterns, or insights from the figure
  - Providing additional information or context that is not visible in the figure
  - Citing the original source or author of the figure, if applicable
- A figure caption should be concise, clear, accurate, and informative. It should not repeat the information that is already shown in the figure, but rather complement it and guide the reader's attention to the most important aspects of the figure.
- A figure caption should be placed below the figure, unless otherwise specified by the style guide or the instructor. It should be aligned with the left edge of the figure, and separated from the figure by a blank line. It should start with the word "Figure" followed by a number and a period, such as "Figure 1." or "Figure 2.".
- A figure caption should follow the formatting and citation rules of the style guide or the instructor, such as APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE, etc. For example, in APA style, a figure caption should end with a parenthetical citation of the source, such as "(Smith, 2020)" or "(Adapted from Jones, 2019)".
- Here is an example of a figure caption in a visual interface:

Figure 1. The relationship between GDP per capita and life expectancy in 2019. The data are from the World Bank (2020). The figure shows a positive correlation between the two variables, indicating that countries with higher income tend to have longer life spans. However, there are some outliers, such as Qatar, which has a very high GDP per capita but a relatively low life expectancy. (Adapted from Gapminder, 2020)
