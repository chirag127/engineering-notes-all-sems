### Evolution of analytic scalability

- Analytic scalability refers to the ability to handle large and complex data sets for analysis purposes.
- In the past, analytic scalability was limited by the separation of data and analytic environments, the lack of parallel processing, and the high cost of data storage and computation.
- With the emergence of big data, new paradigms and technologies have enabled greater analytic scalability, such as:
  - The convergence of data and analytic environments, which allows analysts to access and manipulate data directly in the data warehouse or data lake, without the need to extract, transform, and load (ETL) data into a separate analytic environment .
  - Massively parallel processing (MPP) architectures, which distribute data and computation across multiple nodes or servers, allowing for faster and more efficient processing of large data sets  .
  - The cloud, which offers scalable, on-demand, and pay-as-you-go data storage and computation services, reducing the upfront and operational costs of data analytics .
  - Grid computing, which leverages the idle resources of a network of computers to perform distributed and parallel computation, increasing the availability and reliability of data analytics .
  - MapReduce, which is a programming model and framework for processing large data sets in parallel using a cluster of computers, simplifying the development and execution of data analytics   .
- The evolution of analytic scalability has enabled new opportunities and challenges for data analytics, such as:
  - The ability to perform more complex and sophisticated analysis on larger and more diverse data sets, such as text, images, audio, video, social media, web logs, sensor data, etc.   .
  - The need to adopt new skills and tools for data analytics, such as programming languages, frameworks, libraries, platforms, etc.   .
  - The need to ensure the quality, security, privacy, and ethics of data and analytics, especially when dealing with sensitive, personal, or confidential data   .
