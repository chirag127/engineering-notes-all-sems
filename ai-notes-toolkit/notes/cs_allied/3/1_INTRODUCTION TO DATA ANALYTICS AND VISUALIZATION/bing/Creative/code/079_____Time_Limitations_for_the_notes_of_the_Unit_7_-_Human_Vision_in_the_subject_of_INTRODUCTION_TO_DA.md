### Time Limitations

- Human vision is limited by the speed of light and the processing power of the brain. The speed of light is about 300,000 km/s, which means that it takes about 0.000003 seconds for light to travel from one meter away to the eye. The brain, however, takes much longer to process the visual information and make sense of it. The brain has a limited capacity to store and retrieve information, and it also has to deal with other cognitive tasks such as attention, memory, and decision making. Therefore, human vision is subject to various time limitations that affect how we perceive and interact with data and visualizations.
- Some of the time limitations that affect human vision are:
  - **Persistence of vision**: This is the phenomenon that allows us to perceive smooth motion from a series of discrete images, such as in movies or animations. The human eye can retain an image for about 0.1 seconds after it disappears, which creates the illusion of continuity. However, this also means that we can miss some details or changes that occur in between the frames, especially if they are fast or subtle. Persistence of vision can be exploited to create visual effects such as motion blur, flicker, or stroboscopic motion.
  - **Change blindness**: This is the phenomenon that causes us to fail to notice changes in a visual scene, especially if they are gradual, unattended, or irrelevant. The human brain cannot process all the information in a complex scene at once, so it focuses on the most salient or important features and filters out the rest. However, this also means that we can overlook some changes that occur in the background, or when we are distracted or interrupted. Change blindness can affect our ability to detect errors, anomalies, or trends in data and visualizations.
  - **Attentional blink**: This is the phenomenon that causes us to miss a second target stimulus if it appears shortly after a first one, within a stream of distractors. The human brain has a limited capacity to process multiple stimuli at the same time, so it prioritizes the first one and suppresses the second one. However, this also means that we can miss some important information that is presented in rapid succession, such as in dashboards or alerts. Attentional blink can affect our ability to monitor and respond to data and visualizations.
  - **Visual short-term memory**: This is the part of the memory system that holds a limited amount of visual information for a short period of time, usually a few seconds. The human brain uses visual short-term memory to compare, integrate, and manipulate visual information, such as in mental imagery or reasoning. However, visual short-term memory is also prone to decay, interference, and distortion, which can affect the accuracy and reliability of our visual perception and cognition. Visual short-term memory can affect our ability to recall and communicate data and visualizations.
