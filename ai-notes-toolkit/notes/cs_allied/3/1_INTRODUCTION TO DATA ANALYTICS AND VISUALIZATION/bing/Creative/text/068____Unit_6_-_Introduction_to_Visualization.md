## Unit 6 - Introduction to Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information.
- Visualization can help users to explore, analyze, communicate, and understand data or information more effectively and efficiently.
- Visualization can be classified into different types based on the purpose, audience, data, and technique of the visualization.
- Some common types of visualization are:

  - **Descriptive visualization**: This type of visualization aims to summarize and present the main features or characteristics of a data set, such as its distribution, trends, outliers, or patterns. Examples of descriptive visualization are histograms, bar charts, line charts, pie charts, scatter plots, etc.
  - **Exploratory visualization**: This type of visualization aims to support users in discovering new insights or hypotheses from a data set, such as its relationships, correlations, clusters, or anomalies. Examples of exploratory visualization are parallel coordinates, scatter plot matrices, heat maps, treemaps, etc.
  - **Explanatory visualization**: This type of visualization aims to convey a specific message or argument based on a data set, such as its significance, causality, or implication. Examples of explanatory visualization are infographics, dashboards, maps, charts with annotations, etc.
  - **Predictive visualization**: This type of visualization aims to forecast or estimate the future outcomes or scenarios based on a data set, such as its trends, probabilities, or uncertainties. Examples of predictive visualization are time series, regression lines, confidence intervals, etc.

- Visualization can also be classified into different types based on the dimensionality, interactivity, and aesthetics of the visualization.
- Some common types of visualization are:

  - **2D visualization**: This type of visualization uses two spatial dimensions (x and y) to represent the data or information. Examples of 2D visualization are charts, maps, diagrams, etc.
  - **3D visualization**: This type of visualization uses three spatial dimensions (x, y, and z) to represent the data or information. Examples of 3D visualization are surface plots, volume rendering, virtual reality, etc.
  - **Static visualization**: This type of visualization does not change or update over time or in response to user actions. Examples of static visualization are printed charts, posters, etc.
  - **Dynamic visualization**: This type of visualization changes or updates over time or in response to user actions. Examples of dynamic visualization are animations, simulations, interactive charts, etc.
  - **Realistic visualization**: This type of visualization tries to mimic the appearance or behavior of the real world or the data source. Examples of realistic visualization are photographs, videos, maps, etc.
  - **Abstract visualization**: This type of visualization uses symbols, shapes, colors, or other visual elements to represent the data or information. Examples of abstract visualization are charts, diagrams, icons, etc.
