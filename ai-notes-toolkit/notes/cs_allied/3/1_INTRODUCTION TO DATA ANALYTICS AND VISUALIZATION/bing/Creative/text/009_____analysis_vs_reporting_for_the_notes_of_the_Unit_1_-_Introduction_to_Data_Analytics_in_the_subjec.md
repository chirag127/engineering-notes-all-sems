### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and summarized way, usually in the form of tables, charts, or dashboards.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and explaining data, usually with the aim of finding insights, patterns, or trends.
- Reporting answers the question of **what** is happening with the data, while analysis answers the question of **why** it is happening and **what** can be done about it.
- Reporting is often based on predefined metrics and dimensions, while analysis is more exploratory and flexible, allowing the user to drill down, slice and dice, and filter the data as needed.
- Reporting is usually done periodically, such as daily, weekly, or monthly, while analysis is done on demand, when there is a specific business problem or opportunity to address.
- Reporting is useful for monitoring the performance and status of a business, while analysis is useful for discovering the root causes and drivers of a business situation, and for generating recommendations and actions.
