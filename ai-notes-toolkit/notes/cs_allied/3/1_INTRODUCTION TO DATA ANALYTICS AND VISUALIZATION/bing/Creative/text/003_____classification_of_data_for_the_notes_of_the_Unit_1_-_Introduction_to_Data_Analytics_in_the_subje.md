### Classification of data for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Data Analytics in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Data is any collection of facts, figures, symbols, or values that can be processed by a computer or a human.
- Data can be classified into different types based on various criteria, such as the source, the format, the structure, the quality, the granularity, the dimensionality, and the purpose of the data.
- Some common types of data are:

  - **Primary data** and **secondary data**: Primary data is the data that is collected directly from the original source for a specific purpose, such as a survey, an experiment, or an observation. Secondary data is the data that is obtained from an existing source, such as a report, a database, or a website, for a different purpose than the original one.
  - **Qualitative data** and **quantitative data**: Qualitative data is the data that is descriptive, subjective, or non-numerical, such as text, images, audio, or video. Quantitative data is the data that is numerical, objective, or measurable, such as numbers, percentages, or statistics.
  - **Structured data** and **unstructured data**: Structured data is the data that has a predefined format, schema, or organization, such as a table, a spreadsheet, or a database. Unstructured data is the data that has no fixed format, schema, or organization, such as a document, an email, or a social media post.
  - **Clean data** and **dirty data**: Clean data is the data that is accurate, complete, consistent, and relevant, and that meets the quality standards and requirements for the intended use. Dirty data is the data that is inaccurate, incomplete, inconsistent, or irrelevant, and that does not meet the quality standards and requirements for the intended use.
  - **Atomic data** and **aggregated data**: Atomic data is the data that is at the lowest level of granularity, detail, or resolution, such as a single transaction, a record, or a measurement. Aggregated data is the data that is at a higher level of granularity, detail, or resolution, such as a summary, a group, or a statistic.
  - **Univariate data**, **bivariate data**, and **multivariate data**: Univariate data is the data that consists of only one variable or attribute, such as the height of students. Bivariate data is the data that consists of two variables or attributes, such as the height and weight of students. Multivariate data is the data that consists of more than two variables or attributes, such as the height, weight, age, and gender of students.
  - **Categorical data**, **ordinal data**, **interval data**, and **ratio data**: Categorical data is the data that can be divided into groups or categories, such as the color of cars. Ordinal data is the data that can be ordered or ranked, such as the grade of students. Interval data is the data that can be measured on a scale with equal intervals, but no absolute zero, such as the temperature in Celsius. Ratio data is the data that can be measured on a scale with equal intervals and an absolute zero, such as the height in centimeters.
