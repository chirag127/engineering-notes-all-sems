## Unit 2 - Data Analytics Lifecycle

The data analytics lifecycle is a framework for planning and executing data analysis projects. It consists of six phases:

1. Discovery: In this phase, the analyst identifies the business problem, the data sources, the stakeholders, and the project objectives. The analyst also performs a preliminary data exploration to understand the data quality, availability, and relevance.
2. Data preparation: In this phase, the analyst cleans, transforms, and integrates the data from various sources. The analyst also performs data profiling, data reduction, and feature engineering to prepare the data for analysis.
3. Model planning: In this phase, the analyst selects the appropriate analytical techniques, such as regression, classification, clustering, or association, to address the business problem. The analyst also defines the evaluation metrics, such as accuracy, precision, recall, or lift, to measure the performance of the models.
4. Model building: In this phase, the analyst applies the selected analytical techniques to the prepared data and builds the models. The analyst also validates and optimizes the models using techniques such as cross-validation, grid search, or hyperparameter tuning.
5. Communicating results: In this phase, the analyst interprets and communicates the results of the analysis to the stakeholders. The analyst also visualizes the results using charts, graphs, dashboards, or reports, and provides actionable insights and recommendations.
6. Operationalize: In this phase, the analyst deploys the models into production and monitors their performance and impact. The analyst also updates and maintains the models as needed, and documents the project outcomes and lessons learned.
