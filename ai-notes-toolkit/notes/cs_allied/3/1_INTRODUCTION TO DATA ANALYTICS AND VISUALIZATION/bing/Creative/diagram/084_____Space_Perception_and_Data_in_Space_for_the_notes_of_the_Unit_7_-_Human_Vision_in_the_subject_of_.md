### Space Perception and Data in Space

- Space perception is the process of becoming aware of the relative positions of one's own body and objects around them.
- Space perception provides cues, such as depth and distance, that are important for movement and orientation to the environment.
- Space perception is influenced by various factors, such as visual, auditory, haptic, vestibular, and proprioceptive information.
- Space perception can also be affected by the context, expectations, attention, and emotions of the perceiver.
- Space perception is not static, but dynamic and adaptive, as the perceiver interacts with the environment and learns from experience.
- Data in space refers to the information that is collected, stored, processed, and displayed in a spatial context, such as immersive interfaces, virtual reality, augmented reality, and geospatial applications.
- Data in space can enhance the perception of space by providing additional cues, such as labels, annotations, overlays, and visualizations, that help the user understand and navigate the spatial environment.
- Data in space can also challenge the perception of space by creating conflicts, distortions, illusions, and ambiguities, that may confuse or mislead the user or interfere with the natural cues of space.
- Data in space requires careful design and evaluation, as the user's perception of space may vary depending on the task, the interface, the display, and the individual differences.
- Data in space can be applied to various domains, such as architecture, education, entertainment, health, science, and engineering, that involve spatial reasoning, exploration, and communication  .
