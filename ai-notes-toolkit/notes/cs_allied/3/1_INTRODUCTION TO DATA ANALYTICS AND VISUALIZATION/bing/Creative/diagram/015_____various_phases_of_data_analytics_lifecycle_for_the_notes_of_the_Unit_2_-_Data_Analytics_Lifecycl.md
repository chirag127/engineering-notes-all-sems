### Various phases of data analytics lifecycle

- Data analytics lifecycle is a series of steps that guide the process of transforming data into insights and actions that can help achieve business or project goals  .
- The data analytics lifecycle is based on the CRISP-DM methodology, which is an open-standard analytics model developed by IBM.
- The data analytics lifecycle consists of six phases  :
  - **Business understanding**: This phase involves defining the problem or opportunity, identifying the objectives and requirements, and establishing the success criteria for the analytics project  .
  - **Data understanding**: This phase involves collecting, exploring, and describing the data that is relevant to the problem or opportunity, and assessing its quality, availability, and suitability for analysis  .
  - **Data preparation**: This phase involves cleaning, transforming, integrating, and formatting the data to make it ready for analysis, and creating subsets or samples of the data if needed  .
  - **Modeling**: This phase involves applying various analytical techniques and algorithms to the data, such as statistics, machine learning, or visualization, to discover patterns, trends, or relationships that can answer the questions or hypotheses related to the problem or opportunity  .
  - **Evaluation**: This phase involves assessing the validity, reliability, and accuracy of the results obtained from the modeling phase, and comparing them with the success criteria and the business objectives  .
  - **Deployment**: This phase involves communicating and presenting the results and insights to the stakeholders, and implementing the recommendations or actions derived from the analysis, such as changing a business process, launching a new product, or optimizing a strategy  .
- The data analytics lifecycle is not a linear or rigid process, but rather an iterative and flexible one, where feedback and learning from each phase can lead to revisiting or modifying previous phases  .
- The data analytics lifecycle can be applied to various types and domains of data, such as business, social, scientific, or personal data  .
