### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and summarized way, such as tables, charts, dashboards, etc. Reporting helps to answer questions like what, when, where, and how much.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and explaining data, such as finding patterns, trends, outliers, correlations, etc. Analysis helps to answer questions like why, how, and what if.
- Reporting and analysis are both important aspects of data analytics, but they have different purposes and audiences.
- Reporting is usually done for a wider and less technical audience, such as managers, stakeholders, customers, etc. Reporting aims to provide information that is clear, concise, and consistent.
- Analysis is usually done for a more specific and technical audience, such as analysts, researchers, data scientists, etc. Analysis aims to provide insights that are relevant, actionable, and novel.
- Reporting and analysis are not mutually exclusive, but rather complementary. Reporting can provide the context and background for analysis, and analysis can provide the depth and meaning for reporting.
