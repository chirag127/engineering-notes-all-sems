# Operationalization

- Operationalization is the final phase of the data analytics lifecycle, where the results of the analysis are delivered to the stakeholders and implemented in the business processes.
- Operationalization involves the following steps:

  - **Deployment**: This is the process of integrating the analytical models or solutions into the existing systems or platforms, such as dashboards, reports, applications, or workflows. Deployment can be done in various ways, such as batch processing, real-time processing, or on-demand processing, depending on the business needs and the technical capabilities.
  - **Monitoring**: This is the process of tracking the performance and the outcomes of the deployed analytical models or solutions, such as accuracy, reliability, scalability, and usability. Monitoring can be done using various metrics, such as key performance indicators (KPIs), return on investment (ROI), or customer satisfaction. Monitoring can also help identify any issues or errors that may arise in the deployment process or the analytical models or solutions.
  - **Maintenance**: This is the process of updating, modifying, or improving the deployed analytical models or solutions, based on the feedback from the monitoring process or the changing business requirements or environment. Maintenance can involve retraining, tuning, or validating the analytical models or solutions, or adding new features or functionalities to them.
  - **Evaluation**: This is the process of assessing the overall impact and value of the deployed analytical models or solutions, such as how they have helped achieve the business objectives, solve the business problems, or support the decision making. Evaluation can also help identify any gaps or limitations in the analytical models or solutions, or any new opportunities or challenges for further analysis.

- Operationalization is a critical phase of the data analytics lifecycle, as it ensures that the analytical models or solutions are effectively and efficiently applied in the real-world scenarios, and that they deliver the expected results and benefits to the stakeholders and the organization. Operationalization also requires continuous monitoring and maintenance, as the analytical models or solutions may need to adapt to the dynamic and evolving business context and data. Operationalization also enables the evaluation and feedback loop, which can help improve the quality and relevance of the analytical models or solutions, and foster a data-driven culture in the organization.
