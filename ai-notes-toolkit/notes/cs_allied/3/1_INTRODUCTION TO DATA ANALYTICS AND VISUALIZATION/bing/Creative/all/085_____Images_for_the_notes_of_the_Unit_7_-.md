- Unit 7 is about the structure and function of DNA and RNA, the processes of transcription and translation, and the regulation of gene expression.

- DNA is a double helix made of two strands of nucleotides, each with a phosphate group, a sugar, and a nitrogenous base. The bases are adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), and guanine (G). The strands are held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases: A with T, and C with G.

![DNA structure](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/DNA_Structure%2BKey%2BLabelled.pn_NoBB.png/1200px-DNA_Structure%2BKey%2BLabelled.pn_NoBB.png)

- RNA is a single-stranded nucleic acid that has a similar structure to DNA, except that it has a ribose sugar instead of a deoxyribose sugar, and it has a base called uracil (U) instead of thymine (T). RNA can have different functions, such as carrying genetic information (mRNA), forming part of the ribosome (rRNA), or bringing amino acids to the ribosome (tRNA).

![RNA structure](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/RNA_Structure.png/1200px-RNA_Structure.png)

- Transcription is the process of copying a segment of DNA into a complementary strand of RNA. The enzyme RNA polymerase binds to a specific sequence of DNA called the promoter and unwinds the DNA. It then adds RNA nucleotides that match the template strand of DNA, forming a pre-mRNA molecule. The pre-mRNA is then modified by adding a cap and a tail, and removing introns (non-coding regions) and splicing exons (coding regions) together.

![Transcription](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/Transcription_labelled.png/1200px-Transcription_labelled.png)

- Translation is the process of decoding the mRNA into a sequence of amino acids, which form a protein. The mRNA binds to a ribosome, which consists of two subunits: a large one and a small one. The tRNA molecules, each with an anticodon that matches a codon (three bases) on the mRNA, bring the corresponding amino acids to the ribosome. The ribosome catalyzes the formation of peptide bonds between the amino acids, creating a polypeptide chain. The translation ends when the ribosome reaches a stop codon on the mRNA.

![Translation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Peptide_syn.png/1200px-Peptide_syn.png)

- Gene expression is the process of turning on or off a gene, which affects the production of a protein and the phenotype of an organism. Gene expression can be regulated at different levels, such as transcription, translation, or post-translational modification. Some factors that can influence gene expression are environmental stimuli, hormones, transcription factors, epigenetic modifications, and mutations.

![Gene expression](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Gene_expression.svg/1200px-Gene_expression.svg.png)
