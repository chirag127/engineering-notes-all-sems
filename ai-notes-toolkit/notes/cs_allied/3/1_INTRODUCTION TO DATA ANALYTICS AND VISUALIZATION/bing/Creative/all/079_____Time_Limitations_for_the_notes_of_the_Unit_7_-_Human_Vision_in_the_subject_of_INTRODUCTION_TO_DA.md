# Time Limitations for the notes of the Unit 7 - Human Vision in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Human vision is the process of perceiving and interpreting visual information from the environment.
- Human vision has some limitations that affect how we perceive and understand data and visualizations.
- Some of the time limitations of human vision are:

  - **Persistence of vision**: The ability of the eye to retain an image for a fraction of a second after the stimulus is removed. This allows us to perceive motion and continuity in visual scenes, but also creates some illusions and artifacts, such as the wagon-wheel effect or the phi phenomenon.
  - **Saccadic suppression**: The reduction of visual sensitivity during rapid eye movements (saccades). This prevents us from perceiving the blur and instability of the visual input during saccades, but also makes us unaware of some details and changes in the scene, such as the saccadic omission or the change blindness.
  - **Attentional blink**: The temporary impairment of the ability to detect a second target stimulus after detecting a first one in a rapid serial visual presentation (RSVP). This limits our capacity to process multiple stimuli in a short time span, but also helps us to filter out irrelevant information and focus on the most salient ones.
  - **Inattentional blindness**: The failure to notice an unexpected stimulus that is in plain sight when attention is focused elsewhere. This shows that attention is a limited resource that can be easily distracted or overloaded, but also that we can selectively attend to the most relevant aspects of a complex scene.
