# Various phases of data analytics lifecycle

Data analytics lifecycle is a series of steps that guide the process of transforming data into insights and actions that can help achieve business goals. Different models of data analytics lifecycle may have different names and numbers of phases, but they generally share some common elements. One of the most widely used models is the CRISP-DM (Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining) methodology, which consists of six phases :

- **Business understanding**: This phase involves defining the problem or opportunity, identifying the objectives and success criteria, and understanding the business context and requirements.
- **Data understanding**: This phase involves collecting, exploring, and describing the data, as well as assessing its quality, relevance, and availability.
- **Data preparation**: This phase involves cleaning, transforming, integrating, and formatting the data to make it ready for analysis.
- **Modeling**: This phase involves applying various analytical techniques and algorithms to the data, such as statistics, machine learning, or visualization, to discover patterns, relationships, or insights.
- **Evaluation**: This phase involves assessing the validity, reliability, and usefulness of the results, as well as comparing them with the objectives and success criteria, and identifying any limitations or assumptions.
- **Deployment**: This phase involves communicating, presenting, or implementing the results, as well as monitoring, maintaining, and updating the analytical solution.

Another model of data analytics lifecycle is the data life cycle, which focuses more on the technical aspects of data management and governance. It consists of eight stages:

- **Generation**: This stage involves creating or capturing data from various sources, such as sensors, transactions, or user interactions.
- **Collection**: This stage involves acquiring, storing, and organizing the data in a centralized or distributed location, such as a database, a data warehouse, or a cloud platform.
- **Processing**: This stage involves transforming, enriching, and validating the data to ensure its quality, consistency, and completeness.
- **Analysis**: This stage involves applying various analytical techniques and tools to the data, such as statistics, machine learning, or visualization, to extract insights, knowledge, or value.
- **Publication**: This stage involves making the data and the results available and accessible to the intended audience, such as internal or external stakeholders, customers, or partners.
- **Archiving**: This stage involves preserving and protecting the data and the results for future use, reference, or compliance, such as by using backup, encryption, or retention policies.
- **Purging**: This stage involves deleting or destroying the data and the results that are no longer needed, relevant, or valid, such as by using deletion, anonymization, or shredding methods.
- **Usage**: This stage involves using the data and the results to support decision making, problem solving, or innovation, as well as measuring and evaluating the impact and value of the data and the results.

Both models of data analytics lifecycle aim to provide a structured and systematic way to manage data for converting it into information that can be used to fulfill organizational and project goals. However, they are not rigid or linear, and they may require iteration, adaptation, or customization depending on the specific context, scope, and complexity of the data analytics project.
