## Unit 7 - Human Vision

1. Human vision is the ability to interpret the surrounding environment using light in the visible spectrum reflected by the objects in the environment.
2. The human eye is the primary organ responsible for vision.
3. The eye is a complex organ that consists of several parts, including the cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina, and optic nerve.
4. Light enters the eye through the cornea, which is a clear, dome-shaped structure that covers the front of the eye.
5. The iris, which is the colored part of the eye, controls the amount of light that enters the eye by adjusting the size of the pupil.
6. The lens, which is located behind the pupil, focuses the light onto the retina, which is a layer of light-sensitive cells at the back of the eye.
7. The retina converts the light into electrical signals, which are then transmitted to the brain via the optic nerve.
8. The brain interprets these signals to create the images that we see.
9. Human vision is capable of perceiving a wide range of colors and shades, as well as depth and movement.
10. However, human vision is not perfect and can be affected by various conditions, such as nearsightedness, farsightedness, and color blindness.
