## Unit 7 - Human Vision

1. **Anatomy of the Eye**: The human eye is a complex organ that consists of several parts, including the cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina, and optic nerve. Each part plays a crucial role in the process of vision.

2. **The Cornea**: The cornea is the clear, dome-shaped tissue that covers the front of the eye. It helps to focus light onto the retina, the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye.

3. **The Iris and Pupil**: The iris is the colored part of the eye, and the pupil is the black circle in the center of the iris. The iris controls the size of the pupil, which regulates the amount of light that enters the eye.

4. **The Lens**: The lens is a transparent, biconvex structure located behind the iris. It changes shape to focus light onto the retina.

5. **The Retina**: The retina is a thin layer of tissue that lines the back of the eye. It contains photoreceptor cells that convert light into electrical signals, which are then sent to the brain via the optic nerve.

6. **The Optic Nerve**: The optic nerve is a bundle of nerve fibers that carries visual information from the retina to the brain.

7. **Visual Perception**: Visual perception is the process by which the brain interprets the visual information received from the eyes. This involves several stages, including the detection of light, the formation of an image, and the interpretation of the image.

8. **Color Vision**: Color vision is the ability to distinguish different wavelengths of light, which are perceived as different colors. This is made possible by the presence of three types of photoreceptor cells in the retina, called cones, which are sensitive to different wavelengths of light.

9. **Depth Perception**: Depth perception is the ability to perceive the distance of objects in the visual field. This is made possible by the fact that the eyes are separated by a small distance, which allows the brain to compare the slightly different images received from each eye and use this information to calculate the distance of objects.

10. **Visual Acuity**: Visual acuity is the ability to see fine details. It is determined by several factors, including the sharpness of the retinal image, the sensitivity of the retina, and the ability of the brain to interpret the visual information.

11. **Common Vision Problems**: Some common vision problems include nearsightedness, farsightedness, astigmatism, and presbyopia. These conditions can often be corrected with glasses or contact lenses.

12. **Eye Health**: Maintaining good eye health is important for preserving vision. This can be achieved by eating a healthy diet, protecting the eyes from UV radiation, and having regular eye exams.
