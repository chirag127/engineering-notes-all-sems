### Decaying Window

- A decaying window is a technique used in data stream mining to give more importance to recent data.
- It is based on the idea that the most recent data is more relevant than older data.
- This technique is used to handle concept drift, which is when the underlying distribution of the data changes over time.
- There are several methods to implement a decaying window, including exponential decay, sliding window, and landmark window.
- In exponential decay, the importance of data decreases exponentially as it gets older.
- In sliding window, only the most recent data within a fixed time window is considered.
- In landmark window, data is divided into fixed time intervals, and the importance of data decreases as the interval gets older.
- Decaying window is useful in applications such as stock market prediction, where recent trends are more important than older trends.
- It is also useful in sensor networks, where the most recent sensor readings are more relevant than older readings.
- Decaying window is a powerful technique for handling data streams, and is widely used in data analytics and visualization.
