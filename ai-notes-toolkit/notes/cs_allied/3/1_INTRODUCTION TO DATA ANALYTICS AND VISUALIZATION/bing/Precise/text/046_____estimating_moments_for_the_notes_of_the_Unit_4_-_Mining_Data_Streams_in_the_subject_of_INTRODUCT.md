### Estimating Moments for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams in the Subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Moments are statistical measures that describe the shape of a data distribution.
- The first moment is the mean, which measures the central tendency of the data.
- The second moment is the variance, which measures the spread of the data.
- Higher order moments, such as skewness (third moment) and kurtosis (fourth moment), measure the asymmetry and peakedness of the data distribution, respectively.
- In the context of mining data streams, it is often necessary to estimate moments from a sample of the data, rather than calculating them exactly from the entire data set.
- One common method for estimating moments is the method of moments, which involves matching the sample moments to the population moments to estimate the population parameters.
- Another method for estimating moments is the maximum likelihood method, which involves finding the population parameters that maximize the likelihood of observing the sample data.
- Both the method of moments and the maximum likelihood method have their advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of method may depend on the specific characteristics of the data and the goals of the analysis.
