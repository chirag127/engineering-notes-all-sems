### Analysis vs Reporting

Analysis and reporting are two important aspects of data analytics. They are often used together, but they serve different purposes.

1. **Analysis** refers to the process of examining data to uncover patterns, relationships, and trends. It involves using statistical and mathematical techniques to make sense of the data and draw conclusions. Analysis is often an iterative process, where the analyst will explore the data, generate hypotheses, test them, and refine their understanding of the data.

2. **Reporting**, on the other hand, refers to the process of presenting data and analysis results in a clear and concise manner. This can involve creating visualizations, such as charts and graphs, to help communicate the results. Reporting is often the final step in the data analytics process, where the results of the analysis are shared with others.

In summary, analysis is the process of exploring and making sense of data, while reporting is the process of communicating the results of the analysis. Both are important components of data analytics and are essential for making data-driven decisions.
