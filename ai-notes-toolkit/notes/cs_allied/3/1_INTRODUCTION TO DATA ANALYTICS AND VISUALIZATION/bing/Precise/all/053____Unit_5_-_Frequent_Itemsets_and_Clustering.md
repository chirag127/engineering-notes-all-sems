## Unit 5 - Frequent Itemsets and Clustering

1. **Frequent Itemsets**: Frequent itemsets are sets of items that appear together frequently in a dataset. They are commonly used in market basket analysis to identify items that are frequently purchased together.

2. **Association Rule Mining**: Association rule mining is the process of discovering interesting relationships between items in large datasets. It is commonly used to identify relationships between items in market basket analysis.

3. **Apriori Algorithm**: The Apriori algorithm is a popular algorithm for mining frequent itemsets. It uses a bottom-up approach, where frequent itemsets are extended one item at a time and groups of candidates are tested against the data.

4. **Clustering**: Clustering is the process of grouping similar objects together. It is commonly used in data analysis to identify patterns and relationships in large datasets.

5. **K-means Clustering**: K-means clustering is a popular clustering algorithm that partitions a dataset into k clusters, where k is a user-defined parameter. The algorithm iteratively assigns each data point to the nearest cluster center and updates the cluster centers based on the mean of the data points assigned to the cluster.

6. **Hierarchical Clustering**: Hierarchical clustering is a clustering algorithm that builds a hierarchy of clusters by either merging smaller clusters into larger clusters or by splitting larger clusters into smaller clusters. The result is a tree-like structure that represents the relationships between the clusters.

7. **DBSCAN**: DBSCAN is a density-based clustering algorithm that identifies clusters as dense regions of data points separated by low-density regions. It is particularly useful for identifying clusters of arbitrary shape and for handling noise and outliers.

8. **Evaluation of Clustering**: There are several methods for evaluating the quality of a clustering, including internal measures, such as the silhouette coefficient, and external measures, such as the Rand index. These measures can be used to compare different clustering algorithms and to select the best clustering for a given dataset.
