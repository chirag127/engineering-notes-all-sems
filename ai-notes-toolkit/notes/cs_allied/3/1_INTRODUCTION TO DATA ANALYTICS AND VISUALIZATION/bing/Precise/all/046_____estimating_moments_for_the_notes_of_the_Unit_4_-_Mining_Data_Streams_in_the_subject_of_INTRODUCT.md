# Estimating Moments for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams in the Subject of Introduction to Data Analytics and Visualization

- Moments are statistical measures that describe the shape of a data distribution.
- The first moment is the mean, which measures the central tendency of the data.
- The second moment is the variance, which measures the spread of the data around the mean.
- The third moment is the skewness, which measures the asymmetry of the data distribution.
- The fourth moment is the kurtosis, which measures the "peakedness" of the data distribution.
- In the context of mining data streams, estimating moments can be useful for summarizing the data and detecting changes in the data distribution over time.
- There are several algorithms for estimating moments in data streams, including the Alon-Matias-Szegedy algorithm for estimating the second moment and the Flajolet-Martin algorithm for estimating the number of distinct elements in a data stream.
- These algorithms use sketching and sampling techniques to provide approximate estimates of the moments with a small memory footprint.
- Estimating moments in data streams can be challenging due to the large volume and high velocity of the data, but these algorithms provide efficient and effective solutions for this problem.
