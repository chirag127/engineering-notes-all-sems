# Model Building

- Model building is the process of creating a mathematical representation of a real-world phenomenon or system based on data and assumptions.
- Model building is an important step in the data analytics lifecycle, as it allows the analyst to test hypotheses, explore relationships, make predictions, and optimize outcomes.
- Model building involves the following steps:

  - Selecting the appropriate modeling technique based on the type and purpose of the data analysis.
  - Preparing the data for modeling, such as cleaning, transforming, encoding, scaling, and splitting the data into training and testing sets.
  - Training the model using the selected technique and the training data, and evaluating the model performance using various metrics and methods.
  - Validating the model using the testing data, and comparing the model predictions with the actual outcomes.
  - Refining the model by tuning the parameters, adding or removing features, or changing the technique, and repeating the training and validation steps until the desired level of accuracy and generalization is achieved.
  - Deploying the model for operational use, such as making predictions for new data, or providing recommendations or insights to the stakeholders.

- Some of the common modeling techniques used in data analytics are:

  - Regression: A technique that models the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables, and predicts the value of the dependent variable based on the values of the independent variables. Examples of regression techniques are linear regression, logistic regression, and polynomial regression.
  - Classification: A technique that assigns a label or category to an observation based on its features, and predicts the label or category of a new observation based on its features. Examples of classification techniques are decision trees, k-nearest neighbors, and support vector machines.
  - Clustering: A technique that groups similar observations together based on their features, and identifies the characteristics of each group. Examples of clustering techniques are k-means, hierarchical clustering, and density-based clustering.
  - Association: A technique that discovers the patterns or rules that describe the relationships or co-occurrences among a set of items, and identifies the items that are frequently bought or used together. Examples of association techniques are apriori, eclat, and fp-growth.
  - Dimensionality Reduction: A technique that reduces the number of features or variables in a dataset, and preserves the most relevant or important information. Examples of dimensionality reduction techniques are principal component analysis, factor analysis, and singular value decomposition.
