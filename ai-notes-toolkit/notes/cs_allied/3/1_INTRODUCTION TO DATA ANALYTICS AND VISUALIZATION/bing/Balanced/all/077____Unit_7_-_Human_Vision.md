# Unit 7 - Human Vision

Human vision is the ability to perceive and interpret visual information from the environment. It involves the interaction of the eyes, the brain, and the nervous system. Human vision has the following features and limits:

- **Binocular vision**: Humans have two eyes that are located on the left and right sides of the face. Each eye forms a different image on the retina, which is the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. The brain combines these two images to create a single perception of vision that allows humans to see objects in three dimensions and estimate their distance and depth.
- **Eye structure**: The human eye is a complex organ that consists of several parts, such as the cornea, the pupil, the iris, the lens, the vitreous, the retina, the optic nerve, and the extraocular muscles. Each part has a specific function in the process of vision.
  - The **cornea** is the transparent, dome-shaped layer that covers the front of the eye. It bends and refracts light to help the eye focus.
  - The **pupil** is the black hole in the center of the iris, which is the colored part of the eye. The pupil controls the amount of light that enters the eye by dilating or constricting in response to light intensity.
  - The **iris** is the ring of muscle tissue that surrounds the pupil and regulates its size. The iris also gives the eye its color, which can vary from person to person.
  - The **lens** is the transparent, flexible structure behind the pupil that changes shape to focus light onto the retina. The lens can become cloudy or opaque due to aging or disease, causing a condition called cataract.
  - The **vitreous** is the clear, gel-like substance that fills the space between the lens and the retina. It helps maintain the shape of the eye and transmit light to the retina.
  - The **retina** is the thin layer of tissue that lines the back of the eye. It contains millions of photoreceptor cells that convert light into electrical signals. There are two types of photoreceptors: rods and cones. Rods are more sensitive to dim light and are responsible for night vision and peripheral vision. Cones are more sensitive to bright light and colors and are responsible for central vision and color vision.
  - The **optic nerve** is the bundle of nerve fibers that connects the retina to the brain. It carries the electrical signals from the photoreceptors to the visual cortex, which is the part of the brain that processes visual information.
  - The **extraocular muscles** are the six muscles that control the movement of the eye. They allow the eye to rotate in different directions and coordinate the eye movements of both eyes.
- **Color vision**: Humans can perceive a range of colors that are derived from the combination of three primary colors: red, green, and blue. These colors correspond to the three types of cones in the retina, which are sensitive to different wavelengths of light. The brain interprets the signals from the cones to create the perception of color. Humans can distinguish about 10 million different colors.
- **Visual acuity**: Humans can see objects clearly and sharply when they are in focus on the retina. The ability to see fine details is called visual acuity. Visual acuity is measured by the smallest size of letters or symbols that a person can read at a certain distance. The standard measure of visual acuity is 20/20, which means that a person can see the same size of letters at 20 feet as a normal person can see at 20 feet. A person with 20/40 vision can see the same size of letters at 20 feet as a normal person can see at 40 feet, and so on. Visual acuity can be affected by factors such as refractive errors, eye diseases, aging, or lighting conditions.
- **Visual field**: Humans can see a wide area of the environment without moving their eyes or head. This area is called the visual field. The visual field is divided into four quadrants: upper left, upper right, lower left, and lower right. The normal visual field for each eye is about 160 degrees horizontally and 135 degrees vertically.
