# Space Perception and Data in Space

- Space perception is the process through which humans and other organisms become aware of the relative positions of their own bodies and objects around them.
- Space perception provides cues, such as depth and distance, that are important for movement and orientation to the environment.
- Space perception can be affected by various factors, such as lighting, perspective, motion, and gravity.
- Space perception can also be influenced by the type of display and interface used to present data in space, such as immersive, augmented, or virtual reality.
- Data in space refers to the information that is collected, stored, analyzed, and visualized in the context of space exploration, such as astronomy, astrophysics, planetary science, and astronautics.
- Data in space can be used for various purposes, such as scientific discovery, engineering design, mission planning, education, and public outreach.
- Data in space can also pose various challenges, such as large volume, high dimensionality, complex structure, uncertainty, and heterogeneity.
- Data in space can be displayed and interacted with in different ways, such as 2D or 3D graphics, maps, charts, tables, animations, simulations, or narratives.
