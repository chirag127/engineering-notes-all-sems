### Different Types of Tasks

- In data visualization, a task is a goal or a question that a user wants to achieve or answer by using a visualization tool or technique.
- Tasks can be classified into different types based on various criteria, such as the level of abstraction, the type of data, the type of analysis, or the type of interaction.
- Some common types of tasks are:

  - **Exploratory tasks**: These are tasks that involve discovering new insights, patterns, trends, or relationships in the data, without having a specific hypothesis or question in mind. For example, exploring the distribution of income across different countries or regions, or finding outliers or anomalies in a dataset.
  - **Confirmatory tasks**: These are tasks that involve testing a specific hypothesis or question, or verifying a known fact or assumption, using the data. For example, confirming whether there is a correlation between two variables, or checking if a certain condition is satisfied by the data.
  - **Presentation tasks**: These are tasks that involve communicating or explaining the results of an analysis, or telling a story with the data, to an audience. For example, presenting the main findings of a research project, or illustrating the impact of a policy change with data.
  - **Data transformation tasks**: These are tasks that involve manipulating, transforming, or cleaning the data, in order to make it suitable for analysis or visualization. For example, filtering, sorting, aggregating, or normalizing the data, or dealing with missing values or errors in the data.
  - **Data mapping tasks**: These are tasks that involve choosing how to represent the data visually, by selecting appropriate visual variables, such as position, size, color, shape, or texture, and mapping them to the data attributes, such as categorical, ordinal, or quantitative. For example, mapping the population of a country to the area of a circle, or mapping the GDP of a country to the color of a bar.
  - **View manipulation tasks**: These are tasks that involve changing the appearance or the perspective of a visualization, by applying various operations, such as zooming, panning, rotating, filtering, or highlighting. For example, zooming in on a specific region of a map, or highlighting a subset of data points in a scatterplot.
  - **Data exploration tasks**: These are tasks that involve interacting with the data or the visualization, by performing various actions, such as selecting, querying, drilling-down, or linking. For example, selecting a data point to see more details, or querying the data to find the average or the maximum value of an attribute, or drilling-down to see a lower level of granularity, or linking multiple views to see the relationship between different variables.
