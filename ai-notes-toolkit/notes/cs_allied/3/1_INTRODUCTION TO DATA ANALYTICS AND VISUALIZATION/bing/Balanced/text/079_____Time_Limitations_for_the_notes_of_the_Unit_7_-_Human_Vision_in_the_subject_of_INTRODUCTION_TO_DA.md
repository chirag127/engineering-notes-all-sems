### Time Limitations for the notes of the Unit 7 - Human Vision in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Human vision is the process of perceiving and interpreting visual information from the environment.
- Human vision involves the interaction of the eye, the brain, and the visual system.
- Human vision has some limitations that affect how we perceive and process data and visualizations.
- Some of the time limitations of human vision are:

  - **Visual persistence**: The phenomenon of retaining an image on the retina for a brief period after the stimulus is removed. This can cause afterimages, motion blur, and flicker fusion.
  - **Saccadic suppression**: The reduction of visual sensitivity during rapid eye movements (saccades). This prevents us from perceiving the blurred images that would otherwise result from the saccades.
  - **Change blindness**: The failure to notice changes in a visual scene that occur during a brief interruption or distraction. This can affect our ability to detect errors, anomalies, or trends in data and visualizations.
  - **Inattentional blindness**: The failure to notice an unexpected stimulus that is in plain sight when our attention is focused elsewhere. This can affect our ability to perceive relevant information or features in data and visualizations.
  - **Visual crowding**: The impairment of object recognition when the target object is surrounded by other objects that are similar or close in proximity. This can affect our ability to distinguish individual data points or elements in visualizations.
  - **Visual working memory**: The temporary storage and manipulation of visual information in the brain. This has a limited capacity and duration, and can be affected by interference, decay, or forgetting. This can affect our ability to compare, contrast, or integrate data and visualizations.
