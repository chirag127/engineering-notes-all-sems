### Computational Support for Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and manipulating graphical representations of data to enhance human understanding and communication .
- Visualization can be classified into two main types: data visualization and computational visualization.
- Data visualization deals with abstract data, such as numbers, text, or symbols, that do not have a direct spatial correspondence. Examples of data visualization are charts, graphs, infographics, and dashboards.
- Computational visualization deals with data that have a spatial correspondence, such as images, volumes, surfaces, or meshes, that are derived from physical phenomena or simulations. Examples of computational visualization are medical imaging, fluid dynamics, molecular modeling, and computer graphics.
- Computational support for visualization refers to the hardware and software tools that enable the creation, manipulation, and interaction of visual representations of data.
- Computational support for visualization can be categorized into three levels: low-level, mid-level, and high-level.
- Low-level computational support consists of the basic hardware and software components that enable the display and rendering of graphics, such as monitors, graphics cards, drivers, and libraries.
- Mid-level computational support consists of the software applications and frameworks that provide specific functionality and features for visualization, such as data analysis, filtering, mapping, transformation, and interaction.
- High-level computational support consists of the software tools and environments that provide domain-specific or user-specific solutions for visualization, such as specialized packages, platforms, or systems.
- Computational support for visualization can vary depending on the type, size, and complexity of the data, the goals and tasks of the users, and the available resources and constraints.
- Computational support for visualization can also involve different modes of interaction, such as desktop, web, mobile, virtual reality, or augmented reality.
- Computational support for visualization can benefit from the advances in parallel and distributed computing, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and machine learning, which can enhance the performance, scalability, and intelligence of visualization systems.
