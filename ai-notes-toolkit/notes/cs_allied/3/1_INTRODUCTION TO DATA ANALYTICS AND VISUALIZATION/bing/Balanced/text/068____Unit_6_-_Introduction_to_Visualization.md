## Unit 6 - Introduction to Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information.
- Visualization can help to explore, analyze, communicate, and understand data or information in various domains and contexts.
- Visualization can be classified into different types based on the purpose, data, and audience of the visualization, such as:
  - Scientific visualization: focuses on visualizing physical phenomena or processes, such as weather, fluid dynamics, or medical imaging.
  - Information visualization: focuses on visualizing abstract or non-physical data, such as networks, hierarchies, or text.
  - Geovisualization: focuses on visualizing spatial or geographic data, such as maps, terrain, or trajectories.
  - Visual analytics: focuses on integrating visualization with analytical methods and interactive techniques, such as filtering, clustering, or machine learning.
- Visualization can also be classified into different types based on the visual encoding, interaction, and presentation of the visualization, such as:
  - Static visualization: uses a fixed image or graphic to represent the data or information, such as a chart, a diagram, or a table.
  - Dynamic visualization: uses animation, motion, or change to represent the data or information, such as a video, a simulation, or a timeline.
  - Interactive visualization: allows the user to manipulate, explore, or query the data or information, such as a dashboard, a map, or a graph.
  - Immersive visualization: uses virtual or augmented reality to create a realistic or enhanced experience of the data or information, such as a 3D model, a hologram, or a game.
- Visualization can be created and displayed using various tools, platforms, and formats, such as:
  - Software: uses specialized programs or applications to create and display visualizations, such as Excel, Tableau, or R.
  - Web: uses web browsers or online services to create and display visualizations, such as Google Charts, D3.js, or Plotly.
  - Hardware: uses physical devices or systems to create and display visualizations, such as monitors, projectors, or VR headsets.
  - Print: uses paper or other materials to create and display visualizations, such as books, posters, or magazines.
