### Introduction to streams concepts

- A data stream is an existing, continuous, ordered (implicitly by entrance time or explicitly by timestamp) chain of items.
- Data streams are often generated by sensors, web applications, social media, or other sources that produce large amounts of data in real time or near-real time.
- Data streams pose challenges for data analytics, such as:
  - High volume and velocity: Data streams can be very large and fast, requiring efficient and scalable methods to process them.
  - Unbounded and dynamic: Data streams have no predefined end or size, and can change over time in terms of distribution, patterns, or structure.
  - Limited memory and computation: Data streams cannot be stored or accessed randomly, and need to be processed with limited resources and minimal latency.
- Data streams can be processed in two main ways:
  - Batch processing: Data streams are divided into finite batches or windows, which are then processed periodically or on demand. This approach can handle large amounts of data, but introduces latency and complexity in managing the batches.
  - Stream processing: Data streams are processed as they arrive, without waiting for batches or windows. This approach can provide real-time or near-real-time results, but requires more efficient and robust methods to handle the data.
- Data streams can be analyzed for various purposes, such as:
  - Filtering: Removing irrelevant or redundant data from the stream.
  - Transformation: Changing the format or structure of the data to make it more suitable for further analysis.
  - Enhancement: Enriching the data with additional information from other sources or models.
  - Aggregation: Computing summary statistics or metrics from the data, such as count, sum, average, or variance.
  - Detection: Identifying patterns, trends, outliers, or anomalies in the data.
  - Prediction: Estimating future values or events based on the data.
  - Classification: Assigning labels or categories to the data based on predefined rules or models.
  - Clustering: Grouping similar or related data based on their features or distance.
- Data streams can be processed and analyzed using various techniques, such as:
  - Sampling: Selecting a subset of the data that represents the whole stream.
  - Sketching: Creating a compact summary or representation of the data that preserves its essential properties.
  - Hashing: Mapping the data to a fixed number of buckets or bins using a hash function.
  - Sliding windows: Maintaining a fixed-size buffer of the most recent data and updating it as new data arrives.
  - Landmarks: Defining reference points or timestamps in the stream and computing metrics or statistics from them.
  - Synopsis structures: Using data structures such as histograms, quantiles, bloom filters, or count-min sketches to store and query the data.
  - Stream mining algorithms: Applying machine learning or data mining methods that can handle data streams, such as online learning, incremental clustering, or adaptive classification.
- Data streams can be visualized using various methods, such as:
  - Line charts: Plotting the values of one or more variables over time.
  - Bar charts: Showing the frequency or proportion of different categories or groups in the data.
  - Pie charts: Representing the percentage or fraction of different segments or slices in the data.
  - Scatter plots: Displaying the relationship or correlation between two or more variables in the data.
  - Heat maps: Coloring the cells or regions of a matrix or a map based on the values or intensity of the data.
  - Sparklines: Drawing small line charts that show the variation or trend of the data.
  - Dashboards: Combining multiple charts or widgets that show different aspects or perspectives of the data.
