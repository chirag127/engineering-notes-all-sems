### Analytic Process and Tools

- The analytic process is a systematic approach to identify, collect, analyze, and communicate data and insights to support decision making and problem solving.
- The analytic process consists of four main steps: define, measure, analyze, and communicate.
- Define: This step involves clarifying the business problem or opportunity, defining the objectives and scope of the analysis, and identifying the stakeholders and their needs.
- Measure: This step involves collecting and preparing the relevant data for the analysis, ensuring its quality and validity, and exploring its characteristics and patterns.
- Analyze: This step involves applying appropriate methods and tools to analyze the data, test hypotheses, and generate insights and recommendations.
- Communicate: This step involves presenting and explaining the results of the analysis, highlighting the key findings and implications, and providing actionable recommendations and feedback.
- The analytic process is iterative and dynamic, meaning that it can be repeated and refined as new data and information become available, or as the business context and objectives change.
- The analytic process can be applied to different types of data, such as structured, unstructured, or semi-structured data, and different levels of analysis, such as descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, or prescriptive analytics.
- The analytic process requires various tools and techniques to support each step, such as data collection tools, data cleaning and transformation tools, data visualization tools, data analysis tools, and data communication tools.
- Some examples of data collection tools are web scraping, surveys, interviews, sensors, and databases.
- Some examples of data cleaning and transformation tools are Excel, Python, R, SQL, and Power Query.
- Some examples of data visualization tools are Tableau, Power BI, Excel, Python, and R.
- Some examples of data analysis tools are Excel, Python, R, SQL, and Power BI.
- Some examples of data communication tools are PowerPoint, Word, Excel, Tableau, and Power BI.
