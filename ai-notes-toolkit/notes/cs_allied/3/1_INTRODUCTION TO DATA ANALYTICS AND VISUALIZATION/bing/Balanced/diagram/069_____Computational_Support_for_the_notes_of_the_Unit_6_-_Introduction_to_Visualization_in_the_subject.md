### Computational Support for Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and manipulating graphical representations of data to enhance human understanding and communication .
- Visualization can be classified into two main types: data visualization and computational visualization.
- Data visualization deals with abstract data, such as numbers, text, or symbols, that do not have a direct spatial correspondence. Examples of data visualization are charts, graphs, infographics, and dashboards.
- Computational visualization deals with data that have a spatial correspondence, such as images, volumes, surfaces, or meshes, that are derived from physical phenomena or simulations. Examples of computational visualization are medical imaging, fluid dynamics, molecular modeling, and computer graphics.
- Computational support for visualization refers to the hardware and software tools that enable the creation, manipulation, and interaction of visual representations of data.
- Computational support for visualization can be divided into three main components: data sources, visualization systems, and display devices.
- Data sources are the origin of the data to be visualized, such as sensors, databases, files, or simulations. Data sources can provide different types of data, such as scalar, vector, tensor, or categorical.
- Visualization systems are the software applications or libraries that perform the visualization tasks, such as data processing, mapping, rendering, and interaction. Visualization systems can be general-purpose, such as MATLAB, Python, or R, or domain-specific, such as Paraview, VisIt, or VMD.
- Display devices are the hardware components that present the visual representations of data to the user, such as monitors, projectors, or virtual reality headsets. Display devices can vary in size, resolution, color, and dimensionality.
- Computational support for visualization can enable different types of visualization techniques, such as scalar, vector, or tensor visualization, contouring, slicing, volume rendering, surface rendering, glyph-based visualization, streamlines, or isosurfaces.
- Computational support for visualization can also enable different types of interaction techniques, such as zooming, panning, rotating, selecting, filtering, or brushing.
- Computational support for visualization can facilitate the exploration, analysis, and communication of data, as well as the discovery of new insights, patterns, and relationships .
