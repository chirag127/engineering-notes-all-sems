### Learning and Generalisation

- Learning analytics is the collection and analysis of data about learners and their environments for the purpose of understanding and improving learning outcomes.
- Learning analytics can be used to measure, monitor, and optimize various aspects of learning processes, such as engagement, motivation, performance, feedback, and collaboration.
- Generalisation is the ability of a machine learning model to classify or forecast new data that is not part of the training set.
- Generalisation is the ultimate goal of machine learning, as it indicates that the model has learned statistical patterns that are relevant and robust to unseen data.
- Generalisation can be influenced by several factors, such as the complexity of the model, the size and quality of the training data, the regularization techniques, and the evaluation methods .
- Generalisation can be measured by the difference between the training error and the test error of a model, also known as the generalisation gap or the generalisation error.
- Generalisation can be improved by using techniques such as cross-validation, holdout, bootstrapping, and information-theoretic analysis to reduce overfitting and bias-variance trade-off  .
- Generalisation can also be enhanced by using adaptive data analysis, which is a framework that accounts for the multiple uses of the same data and the feedback loops between data and analysis.
- Adaptive data analysis can prevent overfitting and improve the validity and reliability of the results by using mechanisms such as differential privacy, stability, and mutual information to limit the information leakage from the data.
