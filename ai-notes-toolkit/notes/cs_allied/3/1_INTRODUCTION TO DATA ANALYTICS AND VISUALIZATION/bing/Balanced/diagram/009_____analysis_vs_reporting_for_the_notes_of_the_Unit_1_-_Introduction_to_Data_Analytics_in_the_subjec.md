### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting and analysis are two different processes that involve data and information, but they have different purposes, tasks, outputs, and skills.
- Reporting is the process of organizing data into informational summaries in order to monitor how different areas of a business are performing. Reporting translates raw data into information.
- Analysis is the process of exploring data and reports in order to extract meaningful insights, which can be used to better understand and improve business performance. Analysis transforms data and information into insights.
- Some of the main differences between reporting and analysis are  :

| Reporting | Analysis |
|-----------|----------|
| Follows a push approach, where reports are pushed to users who are then expected to extract meaningful insights and take appropriate actions for themselves (i.e., self-serve) | Follows a pull approach, where analysts pull data and information from various sources and then provide insights and recommendations to users (i.e., serve) |
| Tells what is happening based on the data | Answers why something is happening based on the data |
| Organizes and summarizes data in a digestible manner | Reads between the data lines to drive conclusions |
| Is objective as it involves presenting accurate data | Is subjective as it involves interpreting data and information |
| Must happen before data analysis | Must happen after data reporting |
| Requires less time and skills | Requires more time and skills |
| Outputs are data tables, charts, graphs, dashboards, etc. | Outputs are insights, trends, patterns, anomalies, opportunities, threats, etc. |

- Reporting and analysis are both important for data-driven decision making, but they have different roles and functions. Reporting provides the data and information, while analysis provides the insights and recommendations. Reporting is more about what, while analysis is more about why and how. Reporting is more about the past and present, while analysis is more about the future and the actions. Reporting and analysis complement each other, but they are not interchangeable.
