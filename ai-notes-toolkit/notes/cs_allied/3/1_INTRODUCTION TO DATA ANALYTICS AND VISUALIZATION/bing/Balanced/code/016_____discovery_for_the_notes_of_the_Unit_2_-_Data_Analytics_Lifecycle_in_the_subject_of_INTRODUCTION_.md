### Discovery
- The first phase of the data analytics lifecycle is discovery, which involves identifying the business problem or opportunity, defining the objectives and scope of the analysis, and obtaining the data sources and resources needed for the project.
- The discovery phase helps to clarify the purpose and direction of the analysis, as well as the expected outcomes and benefits. It also helps to establish the feasibility and viability of the project, and to identify any potential risks or challenges that may arise during the process.
- The discovery phase consists of the following steps:

  - **Identify the business problem or opportunity**: This step involves understanding the context and background of the problem or opportunity, and defining the specific question or goal that the analysis aims to address. For example, a business problem could be to reduce customer churn, and a business opportunity could be to increase sales of a new product.
  - **Define the objectives and scope of the analysis**: This step involves specifying the desired outcomes and benefits of the analysis, as well as the scope and boundaries of the project. For example, an objective could be to identify the factors that influence customer loyalty, and the scope could be to focus on a specific segment of customers or a specific geographic region.
  - **Obtain the data sources and resources**: This step involves identifying and acquiring the data sources and resources that are relevant and necessary for the analysis. This may include internal or external data sources, such as databases, files, web pages, surveys, or social media. It may also include human or technical resources, such as analysts, stakeholders, tools, or platforms. This step also involves assessing the quality, availability, and accessibility of the data and resources, and ensuring that they meet the ethical and legal requirements of the project.
