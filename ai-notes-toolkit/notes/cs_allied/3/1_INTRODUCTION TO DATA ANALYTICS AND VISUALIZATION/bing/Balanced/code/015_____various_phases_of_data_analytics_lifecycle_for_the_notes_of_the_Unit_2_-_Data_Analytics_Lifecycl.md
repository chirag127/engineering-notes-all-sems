### Various phases of data analytics lifecycle

The data analytics lifecycle is a series of steps that guide the process of transforming data into insights and actions that can help achieve business or project goals. Different models of data analytics lifecycle exist, but one of the most widely used is the CRISP-DM (Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining) methodology . According to this model, the data analytics lifecycle consists of six phases:

- **Business understanding**: This phase involves defining the problem or opportunity, identifying the objectives and success criteria, and understanding the context and constraints of the data analytics project.
- **Data understanding**: This phase involves collecting, exploring, and describing the available data sources, as well as assessing their quality, relevance, and suitability for the analysis.
- **Data preparation**: This phase involves cleaning, transforming, integrating, and formatting the data to make it ready for analysis. This may include dealing with missing values, outliers, inconsistencies, duplicates, and errors in the data.
- **Modeling**: This phase involves applying various data analysis techniques, such as statistics, machine learning, or visualization, to the prepared data to discover patterns, trends, relationships, or insights. This may require selecting, testing, and comparing different methods, algorithms, or tools, as well as tuning their parameters and settings.
- **Evaluation**: This phase involves assessing the validity, reliability, and usefulness of the analysis results, as well as their alignment with the project objectives and success criteria. This may involve measuring the performance, accuracy, or impact of the analysis, as well as identifying the limitations, assumptions, or risks involved.
- **Deployment**: This phase involves communicating, presenting, or delivering the analysis results to the stakeholders, customers, or end-users, as well as implementing or integrating them into the business or project processes. This may involve creating reports, dashboards, or applications, as well as providing recommendations, feedback, or actions based on the analysis.

The data analytics lifecycle is not a linear or rigid process, but rather an iterative and flexible one, where the phases can be repeated, modified, or skipped depending on the needs and circumstances of each project. The data analytics lifecycle can help data analysts and data scientists to plan, execute, and manage their data analytics projects in a systematic and effective way.
