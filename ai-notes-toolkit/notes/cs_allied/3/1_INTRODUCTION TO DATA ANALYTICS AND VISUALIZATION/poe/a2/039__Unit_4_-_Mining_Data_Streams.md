## Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams

1. Data streams are continuous, rapid, time-variant flows of data. They arrive with a high volume and velocity. Data stream mining aims to extract knowledge structures in data streams.
2. Challenges in data stream mining:
- Limited memory as data arrives continuously and space is constrained.
- Limited time as data arrives rapidly and algorithms must produce results quickly.
- Concept drift as distributions change over time and patterns evolve. Models must adapt to reflect the current concept.
3. Types of patterns to find:
- Frequent items or associations: Identify items or itemsets that occur frequently.
- Clusters and anomalies: Group similar data instances or find anomalies.
- Sequential patterns: Find sequences of items that occur frequently.
- Classifications and predictions: Predict target variables or classify new data instances.
4. Approaches to data stream mining:
- Sliding window: Use a window of recent data to find patterns. The window slides over the data stream and patterns are found in the current window.
- Sketching: Use compressed summaries of data to estimate frequencies or patterns.
- Adapting and evolving: Models evolve over time to adapt to changes or concept drift.
- Ensemble methods: Combine multiple models for improved accuracy and adaptability.
