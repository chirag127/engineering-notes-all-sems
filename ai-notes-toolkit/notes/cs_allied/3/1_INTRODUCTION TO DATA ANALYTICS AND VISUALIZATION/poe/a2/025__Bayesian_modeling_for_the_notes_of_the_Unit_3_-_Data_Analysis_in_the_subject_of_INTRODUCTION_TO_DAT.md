### Bayesian modeling for the notes of the Unit 3 - Data Analysis in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

1. Bayesian modeling is a statistical modeling technique that uses Bayes' theorem in order to update the probability of a hypothesis as additional evidence or information becomes available.
2. Bayes' theorem is used to incorporate new evidence into existing beliefs and update probability estimates accordingly. It allows you to start with an initial belief about something (called a prior), get new information (called evidence or data), and then calculate a revised belief (called posterior).
3. The key steps in Bayesian modeling are:

- Specifying a prior distribution for the model parameters
- Collecting data/evidence and computing the likelihood of the data under different parameter values
- Applying Bayes' theorem to obtain the posterior distribution
- Comparing the relative probabilities of different hypotheses or models and drawing inferences from the posterior distributions

4. Advantages of Bayesian modeling:

- It is a coherent system for updating beliefs in the light of new evidence.
- It allows for incorporating prior knowledge and external information into the analysis.
- It provides the full posterior distribution over the quantities of interest, not just a single 'best' estimate.
- Hierarchical models can be built to share information and borrow strength across groups or parameters.

5. Some limitations of Bayesian modeling:

- Choice of prior distribution can significantly influence results.
- Computation can become intensive for complex models.
- Interpretation of results may be more difficult for those unfamiliar with Bayesian concepts.
