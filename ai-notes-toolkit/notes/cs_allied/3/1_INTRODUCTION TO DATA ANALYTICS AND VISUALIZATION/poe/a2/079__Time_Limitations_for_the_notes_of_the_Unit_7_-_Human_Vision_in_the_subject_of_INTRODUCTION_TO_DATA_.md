### Time Limitations for the notes of the Unit 7 - Human Vision

1. The human visual system has limitations in terms of how much information it can process at a time. Our visual working memory can only hold about 4 chunks of information at once. This limits how much data we can compare or reason about simultaneously.
2. We also have limitations in how quickly we can extract information from a visual display. It takes time to scan, locate, and interpret the meaning of visual elements. The more that is shown or the more complex the display, the longer it takes to comprehend.
3. Additionally, visualizations that require a lot of mental transformations or calculation to understand the information displayed may strain our cognitive capacities. The visual encoding should match the way we naturally think about and reason with the data.
4. Given the human visual system's constraints, visualizations should be designed to support the task and questions at hand without overloading the viewer or requiring an inordinate amount of effort. Simpler, well-structured displays that match the cognitive processes needed to understand the data tend to be more efficient and effective.
