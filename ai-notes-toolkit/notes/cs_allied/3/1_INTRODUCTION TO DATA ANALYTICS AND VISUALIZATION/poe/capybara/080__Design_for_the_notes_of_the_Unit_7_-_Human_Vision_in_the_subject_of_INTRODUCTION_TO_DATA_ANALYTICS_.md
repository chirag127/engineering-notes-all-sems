### Design for the notes of the Unit 7 - Human Vision in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Understand the basics of human vision before designing any visualizations. This includes knowing how the eye perceives color, brightness, contrast, and motion.
- Keep in mind that different people have different visual abilities, so it's important to design for accessibility. This means choosing appropriate colors, font sizes, and contrast levels.
- Use pre-attentive attributes, such as size, shape, and color, to draw attention to important information. However, be careful not to overuse them as it can lead to visual clutter.
- Choose an appropriate visual encoding technique for the data being presented. For example, use a bar chart for comparing discrete values or a line chart for showing trends over time.
- Avoid using 3D charts or excessive animations as they can be distracting and make it difficult for the user to interpret the data.
- Use appropriate labeling and annotations to provide context and help the user understand the data being presented.
- Test the design with different users to ensure that it is effective and easy to use. Make adjustments based on feedback to improve the design.
- Consider the medium in which the visualization will be presented. A design that works well on a computer screen may not be effective on a mobile device or in a print format.
- Always keep the user in mind and design with their needs and goals in mind. A good design should be intuitive and easy to use.
