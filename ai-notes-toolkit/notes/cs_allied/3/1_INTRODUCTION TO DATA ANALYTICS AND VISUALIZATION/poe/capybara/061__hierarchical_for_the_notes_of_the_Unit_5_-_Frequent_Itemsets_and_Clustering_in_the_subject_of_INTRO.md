### Hierarchical for the notes of the Unit 5 - Frequent Itemsets and Clustering in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Hierarchical clustering is a method of clustering data objects based on their similarity. 
- In hierarchical clustering, data objects are grouped together using a dendrogram, which is a tree-like structure that shows the relationships between the objects based on their similarity. 
- There are two types of hierarchical clustering: agglomerative and divisive. 
- Agglomerative clustering is a bottom-up approach where each data object is initially treated as a separate cluster and then merged together based on their similarity. 
- Divisive clustering is a top-down approach where all data objects are initially grouped together and then split into smaller clusters based on their dissimilarity. 
- In hierarchical clustering, the choice of similarity measure and linkage method can have a significant impact on the resulting clustering. 
- Common similarity measures include Euclidean distance, Manhattan distance, and cosine similarity. 
- Common linkage methods include single linkage, complete linkage, and average linkage. 
- Hierarchical clustering can be used for a variety of applications, such as image segmentation, gene expression analysis, and market segmentation. 
- One disadvantage of hierarchical clustering is that it can be computationally expensive and may not scale well to large datasets. 
- Overall, hierarchical clustering is a useful method for exploring the structure of complex datasets and identifying meaningful groups of similar objects.
