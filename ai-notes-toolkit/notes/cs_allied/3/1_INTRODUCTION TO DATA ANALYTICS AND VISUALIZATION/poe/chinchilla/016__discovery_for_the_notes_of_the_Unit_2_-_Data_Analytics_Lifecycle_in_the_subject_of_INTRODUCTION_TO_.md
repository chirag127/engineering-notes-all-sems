### Discovery

In the data analytics lifecycle, the discovery phase is the first step towards solving a problem. This phase involves identifying the question or problem that needs to be solved, and then gathering relevant data to answer that question. Below are some important points to remember about the discovery phase:

- The discovery phase is all about understanding the problem that needs to be solved. This involves talking to stakeholders, understanding the business context, and identifying the key questions that need to be answered.

- Once the problem has been identified, the next step is to gather relevant data. This may involve collecting data from internal sources, such as databases or spreadsheets, or external sources, such as publicly available datasets.

- It is important to ensure that the data being collected is relevant and of high quality. This means checking for missing values, outliers, and other data quality issues.

- During the discovery phase, it is also important to consider any ethical or legal issues that may arise from the use of the data. This may include issues related to privacy, confidentiality, or intellectual property.

- Once the data has been collected and cleaned, the next step is to explore the data to gain insights and identify patterns. This may involve using visualizations or statistical techniques to identify trends or outliers.

- The insights gained during the discovery phase can then be used to formulate hypotheses or develop models that can be tested in subsequent phases of the data analytics lifecycle.

- Finally, it is important to document the findings of the discovery phase, including any insights gained, data sources used, and any ethical or legal considerations. This documentation can then be used as a reference for subsequent phases of the data analytics lifecycle, and can help ensure that the insights gained are accurate and reliable.

Overall, the discovery phase is a critical first step in the data analytics lifecycle. By carefully identifying the problem to be solved and gathering relevant data, analysts can gain valuable insights that can inform subsequent phases of the lifecycle and ultimately lead to better decision-making.
