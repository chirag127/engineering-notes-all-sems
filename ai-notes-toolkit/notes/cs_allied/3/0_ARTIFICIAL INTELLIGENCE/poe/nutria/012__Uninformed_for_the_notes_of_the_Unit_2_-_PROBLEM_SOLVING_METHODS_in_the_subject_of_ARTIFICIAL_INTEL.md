
### Uninformed Search Methods
Uninformed search methods are search algorithms that do not use any additional information other than the problem definition to find a solution. These methods are also known as blind search algorithms, as they are blind to the problem they are trying to solve. Uninformed search methods are generally used to find a solution to a problem that is not known in advance. 

The most common uninformed search methods are:

* Breadth-first search: This method searches in a tree-like structure by expanding the most recently added node. It is considered to be the most efficient method for solving problems with large search spaces.

* Depth-first search: This method searches in a tree-like structure by expanding the deepest node. It is suitable for solving problems with small search spaces.

* Uniform-cost search: This method searches in a tree-like structure by expanding the node with the lowest cost. It is suitable for solving problems with large search spaces.

* Iterative deepening search: This method searches in a tree-like structure by expanding the deepest node. It is suitable for solving problems with large search spaces.
