### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

- Agents are software programs that perform tasks or make decisions in a particular domain.
- Intelligent agents have components that provide reasoning and learning capabilities.
- The architecture of an intelligent agent defines the various components that are integral to its functionality.
- The key components of an intelligent agent are:

1. Perception: The ability to take input from the environment via sensors or data feeds. The input is then processed into a suitable internal representation.
2. Reasoning: The process of thinking or inference that generates relevant beliefs and goals from the percepts. This could include automated reasoning, planning or machine learning techniques.
3. Memory: The component that persistently stores beliefs, goals and inference algorithms. The memory may be declarative or procedural in nature.
4. Communication: The ability to communicate with other agents or humans. Output is generated from the internal state and transmitted via effectors.
5. Effectors: The components that can perform physical actions in the environment as a result of decisions made by the agent.

- The overall aim of an intelligent agent's architecture is to perceive the environment, reason about it and then act appropriately to achieve the desired goals. The specific components and their complex interactions are designed based on the requirements and tasks of the agent.
