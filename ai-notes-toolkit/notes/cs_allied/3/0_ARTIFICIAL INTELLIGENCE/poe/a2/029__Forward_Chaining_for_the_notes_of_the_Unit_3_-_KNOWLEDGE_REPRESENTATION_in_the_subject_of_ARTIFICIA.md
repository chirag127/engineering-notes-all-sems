### Forward Chaining for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

1. Forward chaining is a inference method where inferences are made based on available data/facts.
2. It starts with the given facts and uses inference rules to derive new facts.
3. It continues this process of deriving new facts from the inferred facts until a goal is reached or no more new facts can be derived.
4. The process simulates a "data-driven" approach where inferences are made based on the data available, moving forward towards the goal.
5. Forward chaining is useful when there is some observable data but the goal is unknown. It can help in determining possible goals or consequences based on the available data.
6. In forward chaining, the inference engine tries to resolve a goal into subgoals and continue breaking down subgoals into simpler subgoals until it reaches some known facts.
7. In contrast, backward chaining starts with the goal and works backward to find out if there are facts/data that can support that goal.
