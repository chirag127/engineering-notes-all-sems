## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

Problem-solving is the process of finding a solution to an issue by using a systematic approach. There are several methods that can be used to solve problems, including:

1. **Trial and Error:** This method involves trying different solutions until the problem is solved. It is often used when there is no clear solution or when the problem is complex.

2. **Algorithm:** An algorithm is a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem. It is often used in mathematics and computer science.

3. **Heuristics:** Heuristics are rules of thumb or mental shortcuts that can be used to quickly find a solution to a problem. They are often used when time is limited or when a perfect solution is not necessary.

4. **Divide and Conquer:** This method involves breaking a problem down into smaller, more manageable parts and solving each part separately. It is often used when the problem is large or complex.

5. **Backtracking:** Backtracking is a method of problem-solving that involves trying different solutions and then backing up to a previous step if the solution does not work. It is often used in computer science and mathematics.

6. **Brainstorming:** Brainstorming is a group problem-solving method that involves generating a large number of ideas and then selecting the best one. It is often used in creative problem-solving.

7. **Root Cause Analysis:** Root cause analysis is a method of problem-solving that involves identifying the underlying cause of a problem and then addressing that cause. It is often used in engineering and quality control.

These are some of the common methods used for problem-solving. Each method has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the best method to use will depend on the specific problem and situation. It is important to choose the right method for the problem at hand in order to find an effective solution.
