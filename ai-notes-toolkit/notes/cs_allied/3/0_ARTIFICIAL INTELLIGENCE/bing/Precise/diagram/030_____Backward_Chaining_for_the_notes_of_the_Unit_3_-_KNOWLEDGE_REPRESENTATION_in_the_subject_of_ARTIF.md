### Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method used in artificial intelligence for automated reasoning and problem-solving. It is a form of inference that starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to determine the facts or premises that support it. This method is commonly used in expert systems and rule-based systems.

Here are some key points to remember about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining is a goal-driven approach, meaning it starts with the desired outcome and works backward to determine the necessary steps to achieve it.
2. It is commonly used in rule-based systems, where a set of rules is used to infer new information from existing facts.
3. Backward chaining can be used to solve problems by breaking them down into smaller sub-problems and solving them in reverse order.
4. It is an effective method for solving problems with multiple solutions, as it can explore different paths to the goal.
5. Backward chaining can be computationally expensive, as it may require exploring many different paths before finding a solution.
