### Forward Chaining

- Forward chaining is a popular inference method used in artificial intelligence and expert systems.
- It is a data-driven approach that starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data until a goal is reached.
- Forward chaining is also known as bottom-up reasoning, as it starts with the facts and works its way up to the conclusion.
- The process of forward chaining involves the application of modus ponens, a rule of inference that states that if a conditional statement is true and its antecedent is true, then its consequent must also be true.
- Forward chaining is used in rule-based systems, where a set of rules is used to derive new information from the given facts.
- It is an iterative process, where the rules are applied repeatedly until no new information can be derived or the goal is reached.
- Forward chaining is commonly used in expert systems, where the system uses a knowledge base of facts and rules to solve problems and provide recommendations.
- It is also used in planning and scheduling, where the system uses forward chaining to generate a sequence of actions to achieve a goal.
- Forward chaining is a powerful tool for knowledge representation and reasoning, and is widely used in artificial intelligence applications.
