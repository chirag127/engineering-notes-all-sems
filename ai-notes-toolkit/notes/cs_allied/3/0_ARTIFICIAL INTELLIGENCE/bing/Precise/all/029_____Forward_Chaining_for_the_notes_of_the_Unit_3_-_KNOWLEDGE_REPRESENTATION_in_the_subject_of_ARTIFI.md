# Forward Chaining

Forward chaining is a method of reasoning in artificial intelligence where the inference engine starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data until a goal is reached. It is a data-driven approach, also known as bottom-up reasoning.

Here are some key points to remember about forward chaining:

1. Forward chaining is used in rule-based expert systems to infer conclusions from the available data.
2. The inference engine iteratively applies the rules to the data, adding new data to the knowledge base as it is inferred.
3. The process continues until a goal is reached or no new data can be inferred.
4. Forward chaining is a data-driven approach, meaning that the reasoning process is driven by the available data.
5. It is also known as bottom-up reasoning, as the reasoning process starts with the available data and works its way up to higher-level conclusions.
