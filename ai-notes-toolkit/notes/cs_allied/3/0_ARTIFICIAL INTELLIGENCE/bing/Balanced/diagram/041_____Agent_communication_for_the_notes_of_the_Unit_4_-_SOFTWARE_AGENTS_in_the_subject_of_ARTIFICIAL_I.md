### Agent communication

- Agent communication is the process of exchanging information and knowledge between intelligent agents in a multi-agent system.
- Agent communication is an important aspect of artificial intelligence, as it enables agents to interact, cooperate, coordinate, and negotiate with each other.
- Agent communication is based on the use of agent communication languages (ACLs), which are formal languages that define the syntax and semantics of messages that agents can exchange.
- ACLs are often inspired by the speech act theory, which is a philosophical theory that analyzes the pragmatic aspects of human communication, such as the intentions, effects, and contexts of utterances.
- Some examples of ACLs are Knowledge Query and Manipulation Language (KQML), Knowledge Interchange Format (KIF), and Foundation for Intelligent Physical Agents (FIPA) ACL.
- Agent communication also requires the use of ontologies, which are explicit and shared specifications of the concepts, terms, and relations that are relevant for a domain of discourse.
- Ontologies provide a common vocabulary and a common understanding of the domain for the agents, which facilitates the exchange and interpretation of messages.
- Some examples of ontologies are Cyc, WordNet, and SUMO.
- Agent communication can be classified into different types, depending on the purpose, content, and direction of the messages.
- Some types of agent communication are:

  - Querying: asking for information or knowledge from another agent.
  - Informing: providing information or knowledge to another agent.
  - Requesting: asking another agent to perform an action or a task.
  - Committing: promising to perform an action or a task for another agent.
  - Proposing: offering to perform an action or a task for another agent.
  - Negotiating: engaging in a dialogue to reach an agreement or a compromise with another agent.
  - Contracting: establishing a formal and binding agreement with another agent.
  - Announcing: broadcasting information or knowledge to multiple agents.
  - Subscribing: expressing interest in receiving information or knowledge from another agent.
  - Notifying: sending information or knowledge to another agent who has subscribed to it.

- Agent communication can be modeled using different frameworks, such as:

  - Agent communication protocols: predefined and structured sequences of messages that specify the roles, rules, and goals of the communication.
  - Agent dialogue games: formal and interactive models of argumentation and persuasion that define the moves, strategies, and outcomes of the communication.
  - Agent conversation policies: flexible and dynamic models of communication that allow agents to adapt to the context and the state of the communication.

- Agent communication can be evaluated using different criteria, such as:

  - Effectiveness: the degree to which the communication achieves the intended goals and outcomes.
  - Efficiency: the amount of resources (such as time, bandwidth, or computation) that are consumed by the communication.
  - Robustness: the ability of the communication to cope with errors, failures, or uncertainties.
  - Scalability: the ability of the communication to handle an increasing number of agents or messages.
  - Security: the protection of the communication from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure.
  - Privacy: the respect of the confidentiality and anonymity of the agents and their messages.
  - Trust: the confidence and reliability of the agents and their messages.
  - Sociality: the degree to which the communication conforms to the norms, values, and expectations of the agent society.
