### Problem Solving Methods

- Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given problem that satisfies some criteria of optimality, efficiency, and correctness.
- In artificial intelligence, problem solving often involves investigating potential solutions to problems through reasoning techniques, making use of polynomial and differential equations, and carrying them out and use modelling frameworks.
- A same problem may have multiple solutions, that are all accomplished using a unique algorithm.
- Problem solving methods in AI can be classified into two broad categories: search-based and non-search-based.

#### Search-based methods

- Search-based methods are one of the common methods to solve problems in AI.
- The problem solving or rational agents with search algorithms are used to find the best optimal solution.
- This type of problem-solving technique is often called goal-based.
- Search algorithms in AI are universal problem-solving methods that explore a space of states, actions, and transitions from an initial state to a goal state.
- Search algorithms can be further divided into two types: uninformed search and informed search.
- Uninformed search algorithms do not use any domain-specific knowledge or heuristics to guide the search. They rely on brute-force exploration of the state space. Examples of uninformed search algorithms are breadth-first search, depth-first search, uniform-cost search, and iterative deepening search.
- Informed search algorithms use some domain-specific knowledge or heuristics to guide the search. They try to estimate the cost or distance to the goal state from each state and select the most promising one. Examples of informed search algorithms are greedy search, A* search, hill-climbing search, and beam search.

#### Non-search-based methods

- Non-search-based methods are problem-solving techniques that do not rely on exploring a state space, but rather use some other methods to find a solution.
- Non-search-based methods can be further divided into two types: evolutionary computation and knowledge representation.
- Evolutionary computation is a family of problem-solving techniques that are inspired by biological evolution and natural selection. They use a population of candidate solutions that are randomly generated and then improved through mutation, crossover, and selection. Examples of evolutionary computation techniques are genetic algorithms, genetic programming, evolutionary strategies, and particle swarm optimization.
- Knowledge representation is a problem-solving technique that involves encoding the relevant information about a problem domain and the problem itself in a formal language, such as logic, ontology, or semantic network. Then, the problem can be solved by applying inference rules, deduction, or reasoning mechanisms to the knowledge base. Examples of knowledge representation techniques are propositional logic, first-order logic, description logic, and production rules.
