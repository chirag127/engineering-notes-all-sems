## Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding and storing information in a form that can be used by an intelligent system, such as an AI agent.
- Knowledge representation aims to capture the meaning, structure, and context of the information, as well as the reasoning and inference capabilities of the system.
- Knowledge representation can be divided into two main categories: declarative and procedural.
  - Declarative knowledge represents facts, concepts, and relationships that are true or false in a given domain.
  - Procedural knowledge represents actions, procedures, and strategies that can be executed or applied in a given domain.
- Knowledge representation can also be classified based on the level of abstraction, the type of logic, and the degree of expressiveness.
  - The level of abstraction refers to how general or specific the knowledge is, and how it relates to the real world.
  - The type of logic refers to how the knowledge is structured and manipulated, and how it supports reasoning and inference.
  - The degree of expressiveness refers to how rich and complex the knowledge is, and how it captures the nuances and exceptions of the domain.
- Some of the common knowledge representation techniques are:
  - Propositional logic: a simple and precise form of logic that uses atomic propositions and logical connectives to represent declarative knowledge.
  - Predicate logic: an extension of propositional logic that uses predicates, variables, quantifiers, and functions to represent declarative knowledge with more expressiveness and generality.
  - Semantic networks: a graphical form of representation that uses nodes and links to represent concepts and relationships in a hierarchical and associative manner.
  - Frames: a structured form of representation that uses slots and values to represent concepts and attributes in a class-inheritance manner.
  - Scripts: a procedural form of representation that uses schemas and variables to represent typical sequences of events and actions in a given domain.
  - Production rules: a conditional form of representation that uses IF-THEN rules to represent procedural knowledge and control the flow of reasoning.
  - Ontologies: a formal and explicit form of representation that uses concepts, properties, and relations to define a common vocabulary and a shared understanding of a domain.
