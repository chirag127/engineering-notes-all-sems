### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors.
- The **architecture** of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together.
- The architecture of an agent determines how it processes the information from its sensors and how it selects the actions to perform with its effectors.
- There are different types of architectures for intelligent agents, depending on the level of complexity, rationality, and interaction they exhibit.
- Some of the common types of architectures are  :
  - **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They do not have any internal state or memory, and they act only based on the current percept. They do not have any goals or plans, and they do not reason about the consequences of their actions. Reactive agents are fast and robust, but they are limited in their capabilities and adaptability.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents are an improvement over reactive agents. They have an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment, and they update this state based on the percepts they receive. They also have a set of rules that map the state to the actions. Model-based reflex agents can handle partially observable and dynamic environments, but they still do not have any goals or plans, and they do not reason about the future.
  - **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents are more sophisticated than model-based reflex agents. They have an internal state, a set of rules, and also a set of goals that they want to achieve. They can select the actions that bring them closer to their goals, based on their state and the expected outcomes of their actions. Goal-based agents can act more flexibly and intelligently, but they still do not consider the preferences or utilities of their actions.
  - **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents are an extension of goal-based agents. They have an internal state, a set of rules, a set of goals, and also a utility function that assigns a numeric value to each state. They can select the actions that maximize their expected utility, based on their state and the probabilities of the outcomes of their actions. Utility-based agents can act more rationally and optimally, but they still do not interact with other agents or learn from their experience.
  - **Learning agents**: Learning agents are the most advanced type of AI agent. They have an internal state, a set of rules, a set of goals, a utility function, and also a learning component that allows them to improve their performance over time. They can learn from their own actions and feedback, as well as from other agents and sources of knowledge. Learning agents can adapt to changing environments and situations, and they can achieve complex and novel tasks.
