### Mental Events and Mental Objects

- Mental events are occurrences or happenings in the mind of an agent, such as perceiving, believing, reasoning, deciding, etc.
- Mental objects are entities or things that exist in the mind of an agent, such as concepts, propositions, images, schemas, etc.
- Mental events and mental objects are related by the notion of intentionality, which is the property of being about or directed at something.
- Intentionality is a key feature of mental representations, which are the ways that agents encode and manipulate information about the world in their minds.
- Mental representations can be classified into two main types: symbolic and subsymbolic.
- Symbolic representations use discrete and structured symbols, such as words, numbers, logic, etc., to express meaning and relations.
- Subsymbolic representations use continuous and distributed patterns, such as neural networks, fuzzy sets, analogies, etc., to capture similarity and association.
- Symbolic and subsymbolic representations have different strengths and weaknesses, and can be combined or integrated to achieve more robust and flexible knowledge representation.
