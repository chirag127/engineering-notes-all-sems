### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors.
- The **architecture** of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together.
- The architecture of an agent determines how it processes the information from its sensors and how it selects the actions to perform with its effectors.
- There are different types of architectures for intelligent agents, depending on their level of complexity, rationality, and interaction.
- Some of the common types of architectures are  :
  - **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They do not have any internal state or memory. They act only based on the current percept, without considering the past or the future. They are fast and robust, but they cannot handle complex or dynamic environments. An example of a reactive agent is a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents are more sophisticated than reactive agents. They have an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment. They use this state to keep track of the changes in the environment and to infer the hidden aspects that are not directly observable. They also have a set of rules that map the state and the percept to the action. They can handle partially observable and dynamic environments, but they still lack the ability to plan ahead or pursue goals. An example of a model-based reflex agent is a vacuum cleaner that keeps track of the locations it has cleaned and avoids obstacles.
  - **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents are more advanced than model-based reflex agents. They have a set of goals that they want to achieve, and they use their state and percept to decide which action will bring them closer to their goals. They can plan ahead and reason about the consequences of their actions. They can handle complex and dynamic environments, but they still lack the ability to measure the quality or utility of their actions. An example of a goal-based agent is a chess player that tries to checkmate the opponent.
  - **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents are the most sophisticated type of AI agent. They have a utility function that assigns a numerical value to each state of the environment. They use this function to evaluate the outcomes of their actions and to select the action that maximizes their expected utility. They can handle uncertain and stochastic environments, and they can balance between different and conflicting goals. An example of a utility-based agent is a stock trader that tries to maximize its profit while minimizing its risk.
  - **Interacting agents**: Interacting agents are agents that can communicate and cooperate with other agents, either human or artificial. They have the ability to exchange information, negotiate, coordinate, and collaborate with other agents. They can handle distributed and social environments, and they can achieve collective goals that are beyond the reach of individual agents. An example of an interacting agent is a chatbot that can converse with a human user.
