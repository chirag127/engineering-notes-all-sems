## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI).
- AI is the study of how to create machines and systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, perception, decision making, and natural language processing.
- AI can be divided into two main branches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI.
- Symbolic AI uses logic, rules, and symbols to represent and manipulate knowledge, and to perform inference and deduction. Examples of symbolic AI include expert systems, knowledge bases, and theorem provers.
- Sub-symbolic AI uses numerical and statistical methods to model and learn from data, and to perform pattern recognition and optimization. Examples of sub-symbolic AI include neural networks, evolutionary algorithms, and fuzzy logic.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the level of intelligence and the domain of application. Some common types are:
  - Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI): AI that can perform a specific task or a narrow range of tasks, but not general intelligence. Examples of ANI include speech recognition, face detection, and chess playing.
  - Artificial General Intelligence (AGI): AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can, and has human-like reasoning and learning abilities. Examples of AGI include HAL 9000 from 2001: A Space Odyssey, and Data from Star Trek.
  - Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI): AI that surpasses human intelligence in all aspects, and can create and understand knowledge that humans cannot. Examples of ASI include Skynet from The Terminator, and Ultron from The Avengers.
  - Weak AI: AI that only simulates human intelligence, but does not have the same underlying mechanisms or goals. Examples of weak AI include chatbots, virtual assistants, and video games.
  - Strong AI: AI that has the same cognitive and emotional capabilities as humans, and can understand and experience the world as humans do. Examples of strong AI include Samantha from Her, and Ava from Ex Machina.
  - Narrow AI: AI that is specialized in a particular domain or field, and has limited or no transferability to other domains. Examples of narrow AI include medical diagnosis, stock trading, and self-driving cars.
  - Broad AI: AI that is versatile and adaptable, and can perform well across different domains and tasks. Examples of broad AI include AlphaGo, which can play various board games, and GPT-3, which can generate natural language for various purposes.
