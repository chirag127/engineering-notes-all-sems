## Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding and storing information in a form that can be used by an intelligent system, such as an AI agent.
- Knowledge representation involves choosing a suitable representation language, defining the syntax and semantics of the language, and designing inference mechanisms that can manipulate and reason with the knowledge.
- Some of the goals of knowledge representation are:
  - Expressiveness: the ability to capture the relevant aspects of the domain of interest.
  - Efficiency: the ability to perform inference and computation with the knowledge in a reasonable time and space.
  - Understandability: the ability to communicate and explain the knowledge to humans and other agents.
  - Extensibility: the ability to accommodate new and changing knowledge without compromising the existing knowledge.
- Some of the common types of knowledge representation languages are:
  - Logic: a formal system of symbols and rules that can express facts, rules, and queries in a precise and unambiguous way. Examples of logic-based languages are propositional logic, first-order logic, and description logic.
  - Semantic networks: a graphical representation of concepts and their relationships, where nodes represent concepts and links represent relations. Examples of semantic network languages are frames, scripts, and conceptual graphs.
  - Production rules: a set of conditional statements that specify actions to be performed when certain conditions are met. Examples of production rule languages are OPS5, CLIPS, and Jess.
  - Ontologies: a hierarchical representation of concepts and their properties, where concepts are organized into classes and subclasses, and properties are defined as attributes and relations. Examples of ontology languages are RDF, OWL, and CycL.
