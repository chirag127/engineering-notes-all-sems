# Alpha for the notes of the Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Alpha is a deep learning model that can compete in real-world programming competitions, such as Codeforces.
- Alpha uses a neural network to generate code from natural language descriptions of problems, and a reinforcement learning agent to improve its performance by self-play.
- Alpha demonstrates the potential of artificial intelligence for problem solving, especially for tasks that require critical thinking, creativity, and generalization.
- Problem solving in artificial intelligence is the process of finding a sequence of actions that leads from an initial state to a goal state.
- Problem solving in artificial intelligence can be classified into three types: ignorable, recoverable, and irrecoverable, depending on whether the solution steps can be ignored, undone, or not.
- Problem solving in artificial intelligence can be achieved by using various techniques, such as search algorithms, evolutionary computations, knowledge representations, etc.
- Search algorithms in artificial intelligence are universal problem-solving methods that explore the state space of a problem and find a path to the goal.
- Search algorithms in artificial intelligence can be divided into two categories: uninformed search and informed search, depending on whether they use any domain-specific knowledge or not.
- Uninformed search algorithms in artificial intelligence include breadth-first search, depth-first search, uniform-cost search, iterative deepening search, etc.
- Informed search algorithms in artificial intelligence include greedy search, A* search, hill-climbing search, simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, etc.
- Search algorithms in artificial intelligence have various properties, such as completeness, optimality, time complexity, and space complexity, that measure their performance and suitability for different problems.
- Problem solving in artificial intelligence also involves various heuristics, which are rules of thumb or shortcuts that guide the search process and reduce the search space.
- Heuristics can be derived from domain knowledge, common sense, or experience, and can improve the efficiency and effectiveness of search algorithms.
- However, heuristics can also be misleading, inaccurate, or inconsistent, and can lead to suboptimal or incorrect solutions.
- Therefore, problem solving in artificial intelligence requires a careful balance between exploration and exploitation, and between generality and specificity.
