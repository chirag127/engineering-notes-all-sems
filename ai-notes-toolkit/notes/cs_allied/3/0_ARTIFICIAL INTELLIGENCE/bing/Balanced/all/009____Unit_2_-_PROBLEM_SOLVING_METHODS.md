## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- Problem solving methods are systematic approaches or strategies that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until finding one that works or eliminates the problem.
  - Algorithm: Following a set of predefined rules or steps that guarantee a solution if executed correctly.
  - Heuristic: Using a rule of thumb or a shortcut that may not guarantee a solution but can help narrow down the search space or guide the problem solver.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a large or complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved more easily.
  - Backtracking: Exploring different branches of a solution space and returning to a previous state if a dead end or a contradiction is encountered.
  - Hill climbing: Moving from one solution to another that is slightly better or closer to the goal until reaching the optimal or satisfactory solution.
  - Means-ends analysis: Comparing the current state and the goal state of a problem and identifying the actions or operations that can reduce the difference between them.
  - Analogy: Using a similar or familiar problem that has been solved before as a model or a source of inspiration for solving a new problem.
  - Brainstorming: Generating as many ideas or solutions as possible without judging or evaluating them until a later stage.
  - Lateral thinking: Applying creative or unconventional thinking to a problem to find novel or unexpected solutions.
