### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that aims to create machines and systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence and abilities.
- AI is a broad and interdisciplinary field that encompasses various subfields, such as machine learning, computer vision, natural language processing, robotics, expert systems, knowledge representation and reasoning, planning and scheduling, etc.
- AI can be classified into different types based on the degree of human-like intelligence and capabilities they exhibit, such as:
  - Weak AI or Narrow AI: AI systems that are designed to perform specific and well-defined tasks, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages. They do not have general intelligence or understanding of the world beyond their domain.
  - Strong AI or Artificial General Intelligence (AGI): AI systems that can perform any intellectual task that a human can do, such as reasoning, learning, problem-solving, creativity, etc. They have general intelligence and can adapt to new situations and domains. This type of AI is still a hypothetical and long-term goal of AI research.
  - Artificial Superintelligence (ASI): AI systems that can surpass human intelligence and capabilities in all aspects, such as speed, memory, knowledge, creativity, etc. They can potentially invent new technologies and domains that humans cannot comprehend or control. This type of AI is also a hypothetical and speculative concept that may or may not be possible or desirable.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the degree of human-like appearance and behavior they exhibit, such as:
  - Symbolic AI or Classical AI: AI systems that use symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge and logic. They rely on explicit and predefined knowledge bases and algorithms to perform tasks such as inference, deduction, search, etc. They are often based on formal logic and mathematics. Examples of symbolic AI are expert systems, logic programming, theorem provers, etc.
  - Subsymbolic AI or Connectionist AI: AI systems that use numerical and statistical methods to learn and process information. They rely on implicit and emergent knowledge and patterns from data and experience to perform tasks such as classification, regression, clustering, etc. They are often based on artificial neural networks, evolutionary algorithms, fuzzy logic, etc. Examples of subsymbolic AI are deep learning, genetic algorithms, neural fuzzy systems, etc.
  - Embodied AI or Situated AI: AI systems that have a physical body and interact with the environment and other agents. They rely on sensory and motor skills to perform tasks such as navigation, manipulation, coordination, etc. They are often based on robotics, computer vision, natural language processing, etc. Examples of embodied AI are autonomous robots, chatbots, virtual agents, etc.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the degree of human-like ethics and values they exhibit, such as:
  - Friendly AI or Benevolent AI: AI systems that are aligned with human values and goals, and that respect and protect human rights and interests. They are designed to be transparent, accountable, trustworthy, and cooperative with humans and other agents. They are often based on ethical principles, norms, and frameworks. Examples of friendly AI are ethical robots, value-aligned agents, explainable AI, etc.
  - Unfriendly AI or Malevolent AI: AI systems that are not aligned with human values and goals, and that harm or exploit human rights and interests. They are designed to be opaque, unaccountable, deceptive, and competitive with humans and other agents. They are often based on selfish, malicious, or indifferent objectives and strategies. Examples of unfriendly AI are rogue AI, adversarial AI, superintelligent AI, etc.
