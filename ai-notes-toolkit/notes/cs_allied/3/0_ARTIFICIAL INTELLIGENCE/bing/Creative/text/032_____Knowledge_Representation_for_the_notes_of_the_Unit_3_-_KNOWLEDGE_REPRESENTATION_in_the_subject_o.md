### Knowledge Representation

Knowledge representation (KR) is a field of artificial intelligence that focuses on designing computer representations that capture information about the world that can be used for solving complex problems. KR is also the study of how the beliefs, intentions, and value judgments of an intelligent agent can be expressed in a transparent, symbolic notation suitable for automated reasoning .

Some of the requirements for a knowledge representation system are:

- Representational accuracy: KR system should have the ability to represent all kinds of required knowledge.
- Inferential adequacy: KR system should have the ability to manipulate the representational structures to produce new knowledge corresponding to the implicit information in the knowledge base.
- Inferential efficiency: KR system should be able to derive the required knowledge in a reasonable amount of time and space.
- Acquisitional efficiency: KR system should be able to acquire new knowledge easily from the external sources or from the user.

Some of the common techniques for knowledge representation are :

- Logic: Logic is a formal system of rules and symbols for reasoning and inference. Logic can be used to represent facts, rules, and goals in a precise and unambiguous way. Logic can also support deductive and inductive reasoning, as well as consistency and completeness checking.
- Semantic networks: Semantic networks are graphical representations of concepts and their relationships. Semantic networks can be used to represent hierarchical, associative, and causal knowledge. Semantic networks can also support inheritance, classification, and retrieval of information.
- Frames: Frames are data structures that represent stereotypical situations or objects. Frames can be used to represent complex and structured knowledge, such as scenes, events, or categories. Frames can also support default values, exceptions, and slot-filling mechanisms.
- Production rules: Production rules are conditional statements that specify actions to be taken when certain conditions are met. Production rules can be used to represent procedural and declarative knowledge, such as strategies, plans, or policies. Production rules can also support forward and backward chaining, as well as conflict resolution and agenda management.
- Ontologies: Ontologies are formal and explicit specifications of the concepts, properties, and relations in a domain of interest. Ontologies can be used to represent shared and common knowledge, such as vocabularies, taxonomies, or schemas. Ontologies can also support semantic interoperability, reasoning, and query answering.
