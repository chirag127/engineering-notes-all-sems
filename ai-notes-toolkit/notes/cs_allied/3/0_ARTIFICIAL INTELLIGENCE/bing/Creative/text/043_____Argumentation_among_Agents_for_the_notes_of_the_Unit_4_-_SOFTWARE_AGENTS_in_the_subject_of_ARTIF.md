### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify decisions, explain actions, and coordinate plans.
- Argumentation can also be seen as a form of dialogue, where agents follow certain rules and protocols to engage in a cooperative or competitive exchange of arguments.
- Argumentation in multi-agent systems is a subfield of artificial intelligence that studies the theory and applications of argumentation for designing and implementing intelligent agents that can argue and interact with other agents.
- Argumentation in multi-agent systems draws on various disciplines, such as philosophy, logic, linguistics, psychology, and law, and uses various formalisms, such as abstract argumentation frameworks, logic-based argumentation systems, dialogue games, and argument schemes.
- Some of the main challenges and research topics in argumentation in multi-agent systems are:

  - How to represent and reason with arguments, counterarguments, preferences, and evidence in a computationally efficient and robust way.
  - How to design and evaluate argumentation protocols and strategies for different types of dialogues, such as persuasion, negotiation, deliberation, and inquiry.
  - How to integrate argumentation with other aspects of agent reasoning and communication, such as knowledge representation, planning, learning, and natural language processing.
  - How to apply argumentation to various domains and scenarios, such as e-commerce, e-government, e-health, e-democracy, e-learning, and e-entertainment.
