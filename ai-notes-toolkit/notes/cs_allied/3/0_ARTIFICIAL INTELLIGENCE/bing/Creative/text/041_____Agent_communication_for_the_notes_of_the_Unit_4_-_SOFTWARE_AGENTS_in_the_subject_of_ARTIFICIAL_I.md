### Agent communication

- Agent communication is the process of exchanging information, knowledge, and intentions between artificial agents that work in the field of artificial intelligence.
- Agent communication can enable coordination, cooperation, and collaboration among agents that have different goals, capabilities, and perspectives.
- Agent communication can also facilitate knowledge sharing and learning among agents that have different domains, languages, and ontologies.
- Agent communication can be performed using various methods, such as:
  - Agent communication languages (ACLs), which are formal languages that define the syntax and semantics of messages that agents can exchange. Examples of ACLs are KQML, FIPA-ACL, and COOL.
  - Agent communication protocols, which are rules that specify the sequence and content of messages that agents can exchange in a given context or scenario. Examples of protocols are Contract Net, Query-Ref, and Request-When.
  - Agent communication models, which are frameworks that describe the structure and dynamics of agent interactions, such as the roles, relationships, and norms that agents follow. Examples of models are BDI, Commitment, and Dialogue Games.
- Agent communication can be influenced by various factors, such as:
  - The type and characteristics of the agents involved, such as their rationality, autonomy, and intelligence.
  - The type and characteristics of the environment in which the agents operate, such as its complexity, uncertainty, and dynamism.
  - The type and characteristics of the task or problem that the agents are trying to solve, such as its difficulty, urgency, and interdependence.
  - The type and characteristics of the communication medium and channel, such as its bandwidth, reliability, and security.
