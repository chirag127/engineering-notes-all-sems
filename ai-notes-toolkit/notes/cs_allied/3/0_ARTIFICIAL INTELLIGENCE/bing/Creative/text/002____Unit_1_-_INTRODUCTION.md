## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI).
- AI is the study of how to create machines and software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, etc.
- AI can be classified into two main categories: weak AI and strong AI.
  - Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the type of AI that can only perform specific tasks or solve specific problems, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages. Weak AI does not have general intelligence or consciousness, and does not understand the meaning or context of its actions or inputs.
  - Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the type of AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can, such as understanding natural language, reasoning about complex situations, creating original art, or having emotions and self-awareness. Strong AI is still a hypothetical and controversial concept, and there is no consensus on whether it is possible or desirable to create it.
- AI can also be classified into two main approaches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI.
  - Symbolic AI, also known as classical AI or good old-fashioned AI (GOFAI), is the approach that relies on using symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge and logic. Symbolic AI uses techniques such as search, inference, planning, expert systems, knowledge representation, etc. Symbolic AI is good at solving well-defined and structured problems, but it has limitations in dealing with uncertainty, ambiguity, common sense, and learning from data.
  - Sub-symbolic AI, also known as connectionist AI or neural AI, is the approach that relies on using numerical values and networks to model and simulate the behavior and learning of natural systems, such as the brain, the immune system, or the evolution. Sub-symbolic AI uses techniques such as artificial neural networks, evolutionary algorithms, fuzzy logic, swarm intelligence, etc. Sub-symbolic AI is good at solving complex and dynamic problems, but it has limitations in explaining its reasoning, generalizing its knowledge, and integrating with symbolic AI.
- AI can also be classified into two main goals: applied AI and pure AI.
  - Applied AI, also known as pragmatic AI or engineering AI, is the goal of creating AI systems that can solve real-world problems and provide useful services, such as speech recognition, face detection, recommendation systems, self-driving cars, etc. Applied AI focuses on the practical aspects and applications of AI, such as performance, efficiency, reliability, scalability, etc.
  - Pure AI, also known as theoretical AI or cognitive AI, is the goal of creating AI systems that can understand and emulate the nature and mechanisms of intelligence, such as cognition, perception, learning, memory, etc. Pure AI focuses on the scientific and philosophical aspects and implications of AI, such as intelligence, consciousness, ethics, etc.
