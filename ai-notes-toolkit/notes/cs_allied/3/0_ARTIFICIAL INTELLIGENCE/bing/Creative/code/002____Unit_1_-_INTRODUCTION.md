## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

This unit provides an overview of the following topics:

- What is artificial intelligence (AI) and why is it important?
- What are the main subfields and applications of AI?
- What are the main challenges and limitations of AI?
- What are the ethical and social implications of AI?

### What is artificial intelligence (AI) and why is it important?

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the study and design of intelligent agents that can perceive, learn, reason, and act in complex environments.
- AI is important because it can enhance human capabilities, solve difficult problems, and create new opportunities for innovation and progress.
- AI can also pose risks and challenges, such as ethical dilemmas, social impacts, and technical limitations.

### What are the main subfields and applications of AI?

- AI is a broad and interdisciplinary field that encompasses many subfields and applications, such as:
  - Machine learning: the study of algorithms and systems that can learn from data and experience.
  - Computer vision: the study of methods and systems that can process and understand visual information, such as images and videos.
  - Natural language processing: the study of methods and systems that can process and understand natural language, such as text and speech.
  - Knowledge representation and reasoning: the study of methods and systems that can represent and manipulate knowledge, such as facts, rules, and concepts.
  - Planning and scheduling: the study of methods and systems that can generate and execute plans and schedules, such as for robots, agents, or projects.
  - Robotics: the study of methods and systems that can control and coordinate the actions of physical machines, such as robots, drones, or vehicles.
  - Artificial neural networks: the study of methods and systems that can model and simulate the structure and function of biological neural networks, such as the brain.
  - Expert systems: the study of methods and systems that can provide expert advice or solutions, such as for diagnosis, design, or decision making.
  - Game playing: the study of methods and systems that can play and win games, such as chess, go, or poker.
  - Computer-generated art: the study of methods and systems that can create and evaluate artistic works, such as music, paintings, or poems.

### What are the main challenges and limitations of AI?

- AI is a complex and evolving field that faces many challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Data quality and availability: the quality and availability of data can affect the performance and reliability of AI systems, such as for learning, reasoning, or decision making.
  - Computational complexity and scalability: the computational complexity and scalability of AI methods and systems can affect their efficiency and feasibility, such as for solving large or hard problems.
  - Explainability and transparency: the explainability and transparency of AI methods and systems can affect their trustworthiness and accountability, such as for providing justifications, interpretations, or feedback.
  - Robustness and security: the robustness and security of AI methods and systems can affect their resilience and safety, such as for handling uncertainty, errors, or attacks.
  - Generalization and transferability: the generalization and transferability of AI methods and systems can affect their adaptability and applicability, such as for handling new or different domains, tasks, or environments.
  - Human-AI interaction and collaboration: the human-AI interaction and collaboration can affect the usability and effectiveness of AI methods and systems, such as for providing user interfaces, communication, or coordination.

### What are the ethical and social implications of AI?

- AI is a powerful and influential field that has ethical and social implications, such as:
  - Fairness and bias: the fairness and bias of AI methods and systems can affect their impact and outcomes, such as for ensuring equality, diversity, and justice.
  - Privacy and security: the privacy and security of AI methods and systems can affect their respect and protection, such as for ensuring confidentiality, consent, and integrity.
  - Responsibility and accountability: the responsibility and accountability of AI methods and systems can affect their regulation and governance, such as for ensuring standards, laws, and policies.
  - Human dignity and autonomy: the human dignity and autonomy of AI methods and systems can affect their value and respect, such as for ensuring rights, freedoms, and choices.
  - Human well-being and flourishing: the human well-being and flourishing of AI methods and systems can affect their benefit and harm, such as for ensuring happiness, health, and prosperity.
