# Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding and storing information in a form that can be used by an intelligent system, such as an AI agent.
- Knowledge representation aims to capture the meaning, structure, and context of the information, as well as the reasoning and inference capabilities of the system.
- Knowledge representation can be divided into two main categories: declarative and procedural.
  - Declarative knowledge representation focuses on what is true or false in the world, such as facts, rules, concepts, and relations.
  - Procedural knowledge representation focuses on how to achieve a goal or perform a task, such as actions, plans, strategies, and algorithms.
- Knowledge representation can also be classified based on the level of abstraction, the degree of formalism, and the type of logic used.
  - The level of abstraction refers to how close the representation is to the natural language or the real world. Examples of different levels of abstraction are natural language, semi-structured language, structured language, and formal language.
  - The degree of formalism refers to how precise and rigorous the representation is. Examples of different degrees of formalism are informal, semi-formal, and formal.
  - The type of logic refers to the system of rules and principles that govern the reasoning and inference of the representation. Examples of different types of logic are propositional logic, predicate logic, modal logic, and fuzzy logic.
- Some of the common methods and techniques of knowledge representation are:
  - Semantic networks: A graphical representation of concepts and their relations, using nodes and links.
  - Frames: A hierarchical representation of concepts and their attributes, using slots and values.
  - Scripts: A representation of typical scenarios and events, using roles and actions.
  - Production rules: A representation of conditional actions, using if-then statements.
  - Ontologies: A representation of a domain of knowledge, using concepts, relations, axioms, and instances.
  - Description logics: A representation of concepts and their properties, using a subset of predicate logic.
  - Bayesian networks: A representation of probabilistic dependencies, using nodes and edges.
  - Neural networks: A representation of learning and generalization, using layers of artificial neurons and weights.
