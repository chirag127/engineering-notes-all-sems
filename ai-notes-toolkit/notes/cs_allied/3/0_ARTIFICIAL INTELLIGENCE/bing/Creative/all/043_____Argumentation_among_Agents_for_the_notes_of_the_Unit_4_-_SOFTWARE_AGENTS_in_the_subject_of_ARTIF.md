# Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, justify their actions, explain their opinions, or learn from each other.
- Argumentation can also provide a basis for designing and implementing multi-agent systems that can perform complex tasks such as negotiation, deliberation, planning, or coordination.

## Basic Concepts of Argumentation

- An argument is a pair of a claim and a set of premises that support the claim. A claim is a statement that the arguer wants to establish as true or acceptable. A premise is a statement that provides a reason or evidence for the claim.
- An argument can be attacked by another argument that challenges either the claim or one of the premises of the original argument. An attack can be of different types, such as rebuttal, undercut, or undermining.
- An argument can be defended by another argument that supports either the claim or one of the premises of the original argument. A defense can be of different types, such as reinforcement, backing, or strengthening.
- An argumentation framework is a structure that represents the arguments and the relations of attack and defense among them. An argumentation framework can be used to evaluate the acceptability of arguments based on different criteria or semantics.
- An argumentation system is a formal system that defines the syntax and semantics of arguments and argumentation frameworks, as well as the rules and procedures for constructing and evaluating arguments.

## Applications of Argumentation in Artificial Intelligence

- Argumentation can be used to specify the semantics of logic programs, by interpreting the rules as arguments and the negation as failure as attack. This allows for handling non-monotonic reasoning, defeasible inference, and inconsistency handling in logic programming.
- Argumentation can be used to generate natural language texts, by using arguments as the units of discourse and the relations of attack and defense as the coherence relations. This allows for producing persuasive, explanatory, or informative texts that can adapt to the audience and the context.
- Argumentation can be used to support legal reasoning, by using arguments as the representations of legal rules, cases, and evidence, and the relations of attack and defense as the modes of reasoning. This allows for modelling legal argumentation, case-based reasoning, and precedent-based reasoning in law.
- Argumentation can be used to support decision-making and conflict resolution, by using arguments as the representations of the alternatives, criteria, and preferences of the decision-makers, and the relations of attack and defense as the modes of comparison and evaluation. This allows for facilitating multi-criteria decision analysis, preference elicitation, and group decision support.
- Argumentation can be used to design and implement multi-agent systems, by using arguments as the messages that the agents exchange, and the relations of attack and defense as the protocols that the agents follow. This allows for enabling agent communication, negotiation, deliberation, planning, and coordination.
