## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- Problem solving methods are systematic approaches or strategies that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until finding one that works or eliminates the problem.
  - Algorithm: Following a set of rules or steps that guarantee a solution if applied correctly.
  - Heuristic: Using a rule of thumb or a shortcut that may not guarantee a solution but can help narrow down the search space or guide the problem solver.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a large or complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved more easily.
  - Backtracking: Retracing the steps or decisions made in the problem solving process and revising them if they lead to a dead end or an unsatisfactory solution.
  - Hill climbing: Moving from one solution to another that is slightly better or closer to the goal until reaching the optimal or best solution.
  - Means-ends analysis: Comparing the current state of the problem with the desired goal state and identifying the differences or gaps that need to be bridged by applying suitable actions or operators.
  - Working backwards: Starting from the goal state of the problem and working backwards to find the initial state or the steps that lead to the goal.
  - Analogy: Using a similar or familiar problem that has been solved before and applying the same or a modified solution to the current problem.
  - Brainstorming: Generating as many ideas or possible solutions as possible without judging or evaluating them until a later stage.
  - Lateral thinking: Thinking outside the box or using unconventional or creative ways to solve problems that may not be obvious or logical.
