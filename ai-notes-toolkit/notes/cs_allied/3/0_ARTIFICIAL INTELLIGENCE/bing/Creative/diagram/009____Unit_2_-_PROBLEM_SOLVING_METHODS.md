## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given situation or task that meets some criteria or constraints.
- Problem solving methods are systematic approaches or strategies that can be applied to different types of problems, such as mathematical, logical, scientific, engineering, etc.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different possible solutions until finding one that works or satisfies the criteria.
  - Algorithm: Following a set of predefined rules or steps that guarantee a correct solution or outcome.
  - Heuristic: Using a rule of thumb or a shortcut that may not guarantee a correct solution, but can help to reduce the search space or guide the problem solver towards a likely solution.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved independently or recursively.
  - Backtracking: Exploring different branches of a solution space and going back to a previous state or decision point if a dead end or a contradiction is encountered.
  - Hill climbing: Starting from an initial solution and iteratively improving it by making small changes that increase the value of an objective function or decrease the cost of a constraint.
  - Simulated annealing: A variation of hill climbing that allows occasional moves that worsen the solution in order to escape from local optima and explore the solution space more widely.
  - Genetic algorithm: A population-based method that mimics the process of natural selection and evolution by generating, evaluating, and combining candidate solutions using operators such as crossover, mutation, and selection.
  - Constraint satisfaction: Finding a solution that satisfies a set of constraints or conditions that limit the possible values or states of the problem variables or components.
  - Search: Finding a path or a sequence of actions that leads from an initial state to a goal state in a problem space that may be represented by a graph, a tree, or a network.
