### Heuristics

- Heuristics are techniques that are used to solve a problem or to make a decision, where traditional methods would be too time-consuming or impractical .
- Heuristics are often used as an approximation to an ideal solution, where the hope is that the approximate solution is good enough for the specific problem at hand.
- Heuristics are an inevitable and inseparable part of artificial intelligence, as they are a computer simulation of the human thinking process, used in situations no known algorithm can reach .
- Heuristics are generally used in conjunction with optimization algorithms to enhance the overall efficiency of the desired results .
- Heuristics can be classified into different types, such as:
  - **Constructive heuristics**: These heuristics build a solution incrementally, adding one element at a time, until a complete solution is obtained or a stopping criterion is met.
  - **Improvement heuristics**: These heuristics start with an initial solution and try to improve it by making local changes, such as swapping, inserting, or deleting elements.
  - **Metaheuristics**: These heuristics are higher-level strategies that guide and combine other heuristics, such as simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, or tabu search.
  - **Hybrid heuristics**: These heuristics combine different types of heuristics or other methods, such as exact algorithms, to exploit their strengths and overcome their weaknesses.
- Heuristics can be evaluated based on different criteria, such as:
  - **Effectiveness**: The quality of the solution obtained by the heuristic, measured by an objective function or a comparison with the optimal solution (if known).
  - **Efficiency**: The time and space complexity of the heuristic, measured by the number of iterations, evaluations, or operations performed by the heuristic.
  - **Robustness**: The ability of the heuristic to cope with different instances of the problem, varying in size, structure, or difficulty.
  - **Simplicity**: The ease of understanding, implementing, and modifying the heuristic, measured by the number of parameters, rules, or steps involved in the heuristic.
