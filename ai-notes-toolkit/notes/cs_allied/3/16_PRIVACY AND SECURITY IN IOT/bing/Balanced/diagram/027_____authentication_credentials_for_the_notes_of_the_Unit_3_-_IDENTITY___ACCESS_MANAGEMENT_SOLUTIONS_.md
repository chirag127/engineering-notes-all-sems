### Authentication credentials for IoT devices

Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a device or a user that wants to access a network, a system, or a service. Authentication credentials are the information that is used to prove the identity, such as passwords, tokens, certificates, or biometrics.

There are different types of authentication credentials that can be used for IoT devices, depending on the security requirements, the device capabilities, and the communication protocols. Some of the common authentication credentials for IoT devices are:

- **X.509 certificates**: These are digital certificates that follow the standard defined in IETF RFC 5280. They contain the public key of the device and other information, such as the device name, the issuer, the validity period, and the signature. X.509 certificates can be issued by a trusted certificate authority (CA) or self-signed by the device. X.509 certificates provide strong authentication and encryption for IoT devices, but they also require more resources and management  .
- **Trusted Platform Module (TPM)**: This is a hardware module that securely stores the private keys and other sensitive data of the device. TPM can also perform cryptographic operations, such as generating random numbers, encrypting and decrypting data, and signing and verifying signatures. TPM provides a high level of security and tamper-resistance for IoT devices, but it also requires additional hardware and software support .
- **Symmetric key**: This is a secret key that is shared between the device and the server. Symmetric key can be used to generate a shared access signature (SAS) token, which is a string that contains the device ID, the expiration time, and the signature. The device can use the SAS token to authenticate with the server, and the server can verify the signature using the symmetric key. Symmetric key provides a simple and lightweight authentication method for IoT devices, but it also has some drawbacks, such as the risk of key compromise, the need for key rotation, and the lack of encryption .
- **Other authentication credentials**: Depending on the specific IoT scenario, other authentication credentials may be used, such as biometrics, one-time passwords, QR codes, or NFC tags. These authentication credentials may provide more convenience, usability, or user experience for IoT devices, but they may also have some limitations, such as the accuracy, reliability, or availability of the authentication factors.
