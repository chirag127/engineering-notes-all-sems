## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION: SECURING THE INTERNET OF THINGS

- The Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical devices, sensors, actuators, and software that can communicate and exchange data over the internet.
- IoT devices can be used for various applications, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart agriculture, smart health, smart manufacturing, and smart transportation.
- IoT devices can provide benefits such as convenience, efficiency, productivity, and innovation, but they also pose challenges and risks, such as privacy, security, reliability, and interoperability.
- Securing the IoT is the process of protecting IoT devices, data, and networks from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction by malicious actors or accidental events.
- Securing the IoT involves multiple aspects, such as:
  - Device security: ensuring that IoT devices have secure hardware, software, firmware, and configuration, and can resist physical or logical attacks.
  - Data security: ensuring that IoT data is encrypted, authenticated, authorized, and integrity-protected during transmission, storage, and processing.
  - Network security: ensuring that IoT networks are isolated, segmented, monitored, and controlled, and can prevent or detect intrusions, anomalies, or malicious traffic.
  - Cloud security: ensuring that IoT cloud platforms and services are secure, compliant, and trustworthy, and can provide data protection, access control, and incident response.
  - Application security: ensuring that IoT applications are secure, robust, and resilient, and can prevent or mitigate common vulnerabilities, such as injection, broken authentication, or cross-site scripting.
  - User security: ensuring that IoT users are aware, educated, and responsible, and can follow best practices, such as using strong passwords, updating devices, or avoiding phishing.
- Securing the IoT requires a holistic and collaborative approach, involving multiple stakeholders, such as device manufacturers, service providers, network operators, cloud providers, application developers, and end users.
