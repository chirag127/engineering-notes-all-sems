### Trust and Trust Models for IoT

- Trust is a measure of confidence or belief that an entity or a system will behave as expected in a given context  .
- Trust management is the process of establishing, maintaining, and evaluating trust relationships among entities or systems in a network  .
- Trust models are frameworks or mechanisms that define how trust is computed, represented, and propagated in a network .
- Trust models for IoT aim to enhance the security, privacy, and reliability of IoT systems by enabling the identification and isolation of malicious or misbehaving nodes, and by facilitating the selection and collaboration of trustworthy nodes  .
- Trust models for IoT can be classified into different categories based on various criteria, such as:
  - The source of trust information: direct or indirect, subjective or objective, first-hand or second-hand, etc.
  - The type of trust information: binary or continuous, scalar or vector, qualitative or quantitative, etc.
  - The aggregation of trust information: centralized or distributed, hierarchical or flat, etc.
  - The update of trust information: static or dynamic, periodic or event-driven, etc.
  - The application of trust information: reputation-based or behavior-based, service-oriented or device-oriented, etc.
- Some examples of trust models for IoT are:
  - A human-centric trust model that considers the human factors and expectations in IoT systems, such as usability, transparency, accountability, and control .
  - A risk-based trust model that incorporates the uncertainty and consequences of trusting IoT nodes, and balances the trade-off between trust and risk .
  - A fuzzy logic-based trust model that uses fuzzy sets and rules to handle the ambiguity and imprecision of trust information in IoT systems .
  - A blockchain-based trust model that leverages the distributed ledger and consensus mechanisms of blockchain to ensure the integrity and verifiability of trust information in IoT systems .
