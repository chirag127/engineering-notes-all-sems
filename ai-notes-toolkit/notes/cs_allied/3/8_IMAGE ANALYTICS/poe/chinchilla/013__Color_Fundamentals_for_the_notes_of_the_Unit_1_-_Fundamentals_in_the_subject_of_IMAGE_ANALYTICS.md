### Color Fundamentals

Color is an important aspect of image analytics. It conveys information about the image and plays a vital role in image processing. In this section, we will cover the fundamentals of color in the context of image analytics.

Here are some important points to keep in mind when dealing with color in image analytics:

- Color is a result of the interaction between light and the human eye.
- The human eye can detect three primary colors: red, green, and blue. These are also known as the RGB colors.
- RGB colors are additive, meaning that when combined in equal amounts, they produce white light.
- The RGB color space is used in digital images, and each pixel in an image is represented by a combination of these three primary colors.
- The intensity of each primary color is represented by a value ranging from 0 to 255 in an 8-bit image.
- The combination of all three primary colors at full intensity (255, 255, 255) produces white, while the absence of all three (0, 0, 0) produces black.
- A color image can also be represented in other color spaces, such as the CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key) color space, which is used in printing.
- In addition to RGB and CMYK, there are other color spaces such as HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness) and HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) that are used in different applications.
- Color histograms are a useful tool in image analysis, as they provide information about the frequency of each color in the image.
- Color segmentation is a technique used in image processing to separate different regions of an image based on their color.

In conclusion, understanding the fundamentals of color is essential in image analytics. Being familiar with different color spaces, color histograms, and color segmentation techniques can help in analyzing and processing images effectively.
