### Sampling and Quantization

- Sampling is the process of converting a continuous signal into a discrete signal by taking samples at regular intervals.
- Quantization is the process of converting a continuous amplitude into a discrete amplitude by assigning a finite number of levels to the samples.
- Sampling and quantization are necessary steps for digital image processing, as they allow us to store, transmit, and manipulate images using binary numbers.
- The sampling rate and the quantization level determine the quality and the size of the digital image.
- The sampling rate is the number of samples taken per unit distance or time. It is measured in pixels per inch (ppi) or samples per second (sps).
- The quantization level is the number of bits used to represent each sample. It is measured in bits per pixel (bpp) or bits per sample (bps).
- The higher the sampling rate and the quantization level, the better the quality and the larger the size of the digital image.
- The lower the sampling rate and the quantization level, the worse the quality and the smaller the size of the digital image.
- Sampling and quantization introduce errors in the digital image, as they cause a loss of information from the original continuous signal.
- The sampling error is the difference between the original continuous signal and the sampled discrete signal. It is also called aliasing.
- The quantization error is the difference between the original continuous amplitude and the quantized discrete amplitude. It is also called quantization noise.
- Sampling and quantization errors can be reduced by using appropriate sampling and quantization techniques, such as anti-aliasing filters, dithering, and compression algorithms.
