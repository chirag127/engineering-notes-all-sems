### Topological and Texture Descriptors

- Topological and texture descriptors are methods to extract and represent the structural and statistical properties of an image or a region of interest.
- Topological descriptors capture the global shape and connectivity of an image, such as the number of components, holes, boundaries, and their relations.
- Texture descriptors capture the local variation and distribution of pixel intensities or colors, such as the frequency, orientation, contrast, and regularity of patterns.
- Topological and texture descriptors can be used for various applications, such as image quality assessment, image segmentation, image classification, object detection, and image forensics.

Some examples of topological and texture descriptors are:

- Local Binary Pattern (LBP): A texture descriptor that encodes the relative difference between a pixel and its neighbors into a binary code. LBP can be computed for different scales and orientations, and can be extended to capture uniform patterns and local binary count .
- Topological Attribute Pattern (TAP): A texture descriptor that generalizes LBP by using a family of numerical attributes that are calculated on the original LBP. TAP can capture rotation invariant and non-uniform patterns.
- Multifractal Analysis (MFA): A texture descriptor that measures the self-similarity and scaling properties of an image. MFA can be combined with topological features, such as the Euler characteristic, to form a class of topological textural-multifractal descriptors.
- Persistent Homology (PH): A topological descriptor that computes the birth and death of topological features, such as components and holes, as a function of a filtration parameter. PH can be represented by persistence diagrams or barcodes, and can be used to compare and classify images.
