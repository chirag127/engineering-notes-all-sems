### Representation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Feature Extraction in the subject of IMAGE ANALYTICS

1. Feature extraction is the process of extracting important and relevant information from an image, and transforming it into a set of features that can be easily understood and analyzed.
2. The goal of feature extraction is to reduce the dimensionality of the data by removing redundant or irrelevant information, while preserving the most important characteristics of the image.
3. Common techniques for feature extraction include edge detection, corner detection, blob detection, and texture analysis.
4. Edge detection is used to identify the boundaries between different regions in an image. This can be done using various methods such as the Sobel, Canny, or Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) operators.
5. Corner detection is used to identify points in an image where the intensity changes in two or more directions. The Harris corner detector is a commonly used method for corner detection.
6. Blob detection is used to identify regions in an image that are different from their surroundings in terms of intensity, color, or texture. The Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) and Difference of Gaussian (DoG) methods are commonly used for blob detection.
7. Texture analysis is used to extract information about the spatial arrangement of colors or intensities in an image. This can be done using methods such as gray-level co-occurrence matrices (GLCM), local binary patterns (LBP), or Gabor filters.
8. The extracted features can be used for various applications such as image classification, object recognition, and image retrieval.
