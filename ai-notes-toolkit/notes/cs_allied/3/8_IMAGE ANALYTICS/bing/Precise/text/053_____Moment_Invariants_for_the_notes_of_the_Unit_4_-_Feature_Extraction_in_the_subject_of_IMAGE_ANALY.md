### Moment Invariants

- Moment invariants are a set of features used in image analysis and pattern recognition.
- They are derived from the mathematical concept of moments, which describe the distribution of a function in space.
- Moment invariants are used to describe the shape of an object in an image, regardless of its position, orientation, and scale.
- The most commonly used moment invariants are Hu's seven moment invariants, which were introduced by Ming-Kuei Hu in 1962.
- These invariants are derived from the second and third order central moments of an image.
- They are widely used in image recognition and computer vision applications, due to their ability to provide a compact and robust representation of an object's shape.
- Moment invariants can be computed for both binary and grayscale images.
- They are typically used in combination with other features, such as texture and color, to provide a comprehensive description of an object in an image.
