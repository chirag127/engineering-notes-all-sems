# Unit 3 - Image Segmentation

- Image segmentation is the process of dividing a digital image into subgroups called image segments, reducing the complexity of the image and enabling further processing or analysis of each image segment.
- Image segmentation is typically used to locate objects and boundaries (lines, curves, etc.) in images.
- Image segmentation is the assignment of labels to pixels to identify objects, people, or other important elements in the image.
- Image segmentation can be done based on different criteria, such as color, intensity, texture, shape, or semantic meaning.
- Image segmentation can be classified into two main types: supervised and unsupervised.
  - Supervised image segmentation uses a training set of images with known labels to learn a model that can segment new images.
  - Unsupervised image segmentation does not use any prior information and relies on clustering or optimization techniques to group pixels based on their similarity or dissimilarity.
- Image segmentation can also be categorized into three levels: pixel-level, region-level, and object-level.
  - Pixel-level image segmentation assigns a label to each pixel in the image, resulting in a fine-grained segmentation.
  - Region-level image segmentation groups pixels into larger regions based on some criteria, such as homogeneity or connectivity.
  - Object-level image segmentation detects and segments individual objects in the image, such as cars, people, or animals.
- Image segmentation has many applications in various domains, such as medical imaging, autonomous driving, face recognition, image editing, and scene understanding .
