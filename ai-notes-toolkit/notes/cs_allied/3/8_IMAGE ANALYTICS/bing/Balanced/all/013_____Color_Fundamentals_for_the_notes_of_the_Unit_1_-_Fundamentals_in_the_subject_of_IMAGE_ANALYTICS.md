# Color Fundamentals

- Color is a property of electromagnetic (EM) radiation that is perceived by human vision or measured by color sensors.
- Color depends on the wavelength and intensity of the EM radiation, as well as the characteristics of the observer or the sensor.
- Color can be represented by different models or spaces, such as RGB, CMYK, HSV, HSL, CIE XYZ, CIE L*a*b*, etc.
- Each color model or space has its own advantages and disadvantages for different applications and purposes.
- Color image processing involves manipulating, enhancing, analyzing, and understanding color images using various techniques and algorithms.
- Color image processing can be performed in different color spaces, depending on the desired outcome and the properties of the color space.
- Some examples of color image processing applications are:

  - Color correction and enhancement: adjusting the brightness, contrast, saturation, hue, etc. of a color image to improve its quality or appearance.
  - Color segmentation: dividing a color image into regions or objects based on their color or color features.
  - Color feature extraction: extracting color information from a color image, such as color histograms, color moments, color descriptors, etc.
  - Color recognition and classification: identifying or labeling the color or color category of a region or object in a color image, such as skin color, flower color, traffic light color, etc.
  - Color conversion and transformation: changing the color space or model of a color image, such as from RGB to CMYK, from HSV to CIE L*a*b*, etc.
  - Color compression and coding: reducing the size or complexity of a color image, such as JPEG, PNG, GIF, etc.
