# Fundamental steps in Image Processing Systems

- An image processing system is a system that performs operations on images, such as enhancement, compression, segmentation, recognition, etc.
- An image processing system typically consists of the following steps:
  - Image acquisition: This is the process of capturing an image from a source, such as a camera, a scanner, a satellite, etc. The image can be in analog or digital form, depending on the device and the application.
  - Image preprocessing: This is the process of improving the quality of the image, such as reducing noise, correcting illumination, enhancing contrast, etc. The goal of this step is to make the image more suitable for further processing and analysis.
  - Image segmentation: This is the process of dividing the image into meaningful regions, such as objects, backgrounds, edges, etc. The goal of this step is to simplify the image and extract the features of interest.
  - Image representation: This is the process of describing the image or its regions using mathematical models, such as vectors, matrices, graphs, histograms, etc. The goal of this step is to reduce the dimensionality of the image and facilitate the comparison and classification of the image or its regions.
  - Image analysis: This is the process of extracting information from the image or its regions, such as shape, color, texture, motion, etc. The goal of this step is to interpret the image and understand its content and context.
  - Image synthesis: This is the process of creating a new image from the existing image or its regions, such as blending, morphing, warping, etc. The goal of this step is to modify the image and generate new visual effects.
  - Image output: This is the process of displaying or storing the image or its regions, such as on a screen, a printer, a file, etc. The goal of this step is to present the image or its regions to the user or another system.
