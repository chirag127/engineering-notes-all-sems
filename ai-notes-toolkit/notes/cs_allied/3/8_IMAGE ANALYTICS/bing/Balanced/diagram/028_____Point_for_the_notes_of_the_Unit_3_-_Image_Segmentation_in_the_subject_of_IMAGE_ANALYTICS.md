### Point for the notes of the Unit 3 - Image Segmentation in the subject of IMAGE ANALYTICS

- Image segmentation is the process of dividing a digital image into subgroups called image segments, reducing the complexity of the image and enabling further processing or analysis of each image segment.
- Image segmentation is typically used to locate objects and boundaries (lines, curves, etc.) in images .
- Image segmentation is the assignment of labels to pixels to identify objects, people, or other important elements in the image.
- Image segmentation can be done based on different criteria, such as color, intensity, texture, shape, or semantic meaning.
- Image segmentation can be classified into two main types: supervised and unsupervised.
  - Supervised image segmentation uses a pre-defined set of labels or classes to assign pixels to image segments, based on some training data or prior knowledge.
  - Unsupervised image segmentation does not use any pre-defined labels or classes, but instead groups pixels based on some similarity or dissimilarity measure, such as distance, density, or clustering.
- Image segmentation can be further categorized into different techniques, such as thresholding, region-based, edge-based, clustering, graph-based, or deep learning.
  - Thresholding image segmentation is the simplest and most common technique, which divides the image into two or more segments based on a single or multiple threshold values for the pixel intensity or color.
  - Region-based image segmentation is a technique that groups pixels into regions based on some homogeneity or connectivity criteria, such as region growing, region splitting, or region merging.
  - Edge-based image segmentation is a technique that detects the boundaries or edges of the objects or regions in the image, using operators such as Sobel, Canny, or Laplacian.
  - Clustering image segmentation is a technique that partitions the image into clusters or groups of pixels based on some similarity or dissimilarity measure, such as k-means, fuzzy c-means, or mean shift.
  - Graph-based image segmentation is a technique that represents the image as a graph, where the nodes are the pixels and the edges are the weights or distances between the pixels, and then applies graph algorithms such as minimum spanning tree, normalized cuts, or graph cuts to segment the image.
  - Deep learning image segmentation is a technique that uses neural networks or deep learning models to learn the features and labels of the image segments, such as convolutional neural networks, recurrent neural networks, or generative adversarial networks.
