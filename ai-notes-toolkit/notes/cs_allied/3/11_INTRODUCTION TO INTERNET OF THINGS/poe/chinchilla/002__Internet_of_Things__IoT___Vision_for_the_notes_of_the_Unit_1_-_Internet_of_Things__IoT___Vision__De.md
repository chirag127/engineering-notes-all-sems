### Internet of Things (IoT): Vision

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical devices, vehicles, home appliances, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and connectivity, enabling these objects to connect and exchange data.

#### Definition
- The IoT can be defined as a system of interrelated computing devices, machines, objects, and people that have unique identifiers and can transfer data over a network without requiring human-to-human or human-to-computer interaction.
- It is essentially a network of physical objects or "things" that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies that allow them to communicate and interact with each other.

#### Conceptual Framework
- The conceptual framework of IoT is based on the idea of connecting all physical objects to the internet, enabling them to communicate and interact with each other seamlessly.
- The IoT is designed to connect devices and systems that were previously not connected, allowing them to share data and work together to achieve a common goal.

#### Architectural View
- The IoT architecture consists of three layers: the perception layer, the network layer, and the application layer.
- The perception layer includes all the sensors and devices that collect data from the physical world.
- The network layer is responsible for transmitting the data collected by the perception layer to the application layer.
- The application layer is where the data is processed, analyzed, and acted upon.

#### Technology Behind IoT
- The technology behind IoT includes sensors, actuators, connectivity technologies like Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks, cloud computing, and big data analytics.
- These technologies work together to enable devices and systems to communicate, interact, and exchange data with each other.

#### Sources of IoT
- The sources of IoT include various industries such as healthcare, transportation, manufacturing, and logistics.
- IoT devices are also used in smart homes, wearables, and smart cities.

#### M2M Communication
- IoT is based on Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication, which involves the exchange of data between machines without human intervention.
- M2M communication enables devices to communicate and share data with each other, making it possible for them to work together in a connected system.

#### IoT Examples
- Some examples of IoT include smart homes, smart cities, connected cars, wearable technology, and industrial IoT.
- Smart homes use IoT devices to automate and control various household appliances and systems.
- Smart cities use IoT devices to monitor and manage various city services, such as traffic and energy usage.
- Connected cars use IoT devices to communicate with each other and with the surrounding infrastructure to improve safety and efficiency.

#### Design Principles for Connected Devices
- IoT/M2M systems should be designed with the following principles in mind:
  - IoT/M2M systems should be designed with a layered architecture that separates the perception, network, and application layers.
  - Communication technologies should be standardized to ensure interoperability and compatibility.
  - Data should be enriched and consolidated to improve the reliability and accuracy of the system.
  - IoT/M2M systems should be designed with ease of use and affordability in mind.
