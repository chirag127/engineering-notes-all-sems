### Architectural view for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a rapidly growing field that has the potential to revolutionize the way we live, work, and interact with our environment. In this unit, we will explore the vision, definition, conceptual framework, and architectural view of IoT, as well as the technology behind it, sources of IoT, M2M communication, and IoT examples. We will also discuss the design principles for connected devices, including IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing, and affordability.

#### Vision of IoT

- IoT is a vision of a world where everyday objects are connected to the internet, allowing them to communicate with each other and with us.
- The vision of IoT is to create a seamless and interconnected network of devices that can work together to improve our lives and make the world a better place.

#### Definition of IoT

- IoT is a network of physical objects that are connected to the internet and can communicate with each other and with other devices, sensors, and systems.
- IoT devices can range from simple sensors and actuators to complex machines and systems.

#### Conceptual Framework of IoT

- The conceptual framework of IoT includes three layers: perception, network, and application.
- The perception layer consists of sensors and actuators that collect and transmit data.
- The network layer is responsible for transmitting and processing data between devices and systems.
- The application layer is where data is analyzed and used to create value for users.

#### Architectural View of IoT

- The architectural view of IoT can be divided into four layers: devices, gateways, cloud, and application.
- Devices are the physical objects that collect and transmit data.
- Gateways are intermediary devices that collect data from devices and transmit it to the cloud.
- The cloud is where data is stored and processed.
- The application layer is where data is analyzed and used to create value for users.

#### Technology behind IoT

- The technology behind IoT includes sensors, wireless communication, cloud computing, and data analytics.
- Sensors are used to collect data from the environment and from devices.
- Wireless communication technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks are used to transmit data between devices and systems.
- Cloud computing is used to store and process data.
- Data analytics is used to analyze data and create value for users.

#### Sources of IoT

- The sources of IoT are diverse and include industries such as healthcare, transportation, agriculture, and manufacturing.
- IoT devices are used to monitor and control processes, improve efficiency, and create new products and services.

#### M2M Communication

- M2M communication is the exchange of data between machines without human intervention.
- M2M communication is an important part of IoT and is used to automate processes, improve efficiency, and reduce costs.

#### IoT Examples

- Examples of IoT include smart homes, smart cities, connected cars, and wearable devices.
- Smart homes use IoT devices to control lighting, heating, and security.
- Smart cities use IoT devices to monitor traffic, air quality, and energy consumption.
- Connected cars use IoT devices to provide real-time information on traffic, weather, and road conditions.
- Wearable devices use IoT technology to monitor health and fitness data.

#### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization are important for ensuring interoperability and scalability.
- Communication technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks should be selected based on the specific requirements of the application.
- Data enrichment and consolidation are important for ensuring that data is useful and meaningful.
- Ease of designing and affordability are important for ensuring that IoT devices can be developed and deployed quickly and at a reasonable cost.
