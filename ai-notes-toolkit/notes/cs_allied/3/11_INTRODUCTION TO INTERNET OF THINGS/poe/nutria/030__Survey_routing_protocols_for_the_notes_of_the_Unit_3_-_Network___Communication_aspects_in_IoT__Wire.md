
### Survey Routing Protocols

Routing protocols are an important part of the Internet of Things (IoT) as they enable communication between nodes and enable the sharing of data and information. There are several types of routing protocols that are used in IoT, but the most common are: 

1. Ad hoc On-Demand Distance Vector (AODV): AODV is a reactive routing protocol that is used for mobile ad-hoc networks. It is designed to handle dynamic topologies and requires very little bandwidth.

2. Dynamic Source Routing (DSR): DSR is a proactive routing protocol that is used for mobile ad-hoc networks. It is designed to adapt quickly to changes in the network topology and requires very little bandwidth.

3. Optimized Link State Routing (OLSR): OLSR is a proactive routing protocol that is used for mobile ad-hoc networks. It is designed to provide optimal paths for data transmission and requires very little bandwidth.

4. Zone Routing Protocol (ZRP): ZRP is a hybrid routing protocol that combines the features of proactive and reactive routing protocols. It is designed to provide fast and reliable routing and requires very little bandwidth.

5. Destination-Sequenced Distance Vector (DSDV): DSDV is a proactive routing protocol that is used for mobile ad-hoc networks. It is designed to provide reliable routing and requires very little bandwidth.

These routing protocols are used for a variety of purposes in the Internet of Things, such as:

- Establishing and maintaining routes between nodes
- Data aggregation and dissemination
- Sensor deployment and node discovery
- Wireless medium access issues
- MAC protocol survey

Each of these protocols has its own advantages and disadvantages and can be used in different scenarios depending on the requirements. It is important to understand the different protocols and how they work in order to make the best use of them in IoT applications.
