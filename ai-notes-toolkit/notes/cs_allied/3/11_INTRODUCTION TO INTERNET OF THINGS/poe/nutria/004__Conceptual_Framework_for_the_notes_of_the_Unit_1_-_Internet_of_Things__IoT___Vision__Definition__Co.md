
### Conceptual Framework for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT): 
- **Vision**: The Internet of Things (IoT) is a concept that envisions a world where everyday objects are connected to the internet and can communicate with each other.
- **Definition**: IoT is a network of physical objects—devices, vehicles, buildings and other items—embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and network connectivity that enables these objects to collect and exchange data.
- **Conceptual Framework**: IoT consists of four components: sensing and actuation, communication, data processing, and decision making. 
- **Architectural View**: IoT architecture consists of three layers – physical, network and application. 
- **Technology behind IoT**: IoT relies on a range of technologies, such as wireless and cellular networks, radio frequency identification (RFID), near field communication (NFC), Bluetooth, and ZigBee.
- **Sources of the IoT**: IoT data can come from a variety of sources, including sensors, wearables, and connected devices.
- **M2M Communication**: Machine-to-machine (M2M) communication is a type of communication in which two or more machines exchange data with each other.
- **IoT Examples**: Examples of IoT applications include smart homes, connected cars, connected healthcare, and industrial IoT. 
- **Design Principles for Connected Devices**: Design principles for connected devices include IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing, and affordability.
