

### Wireless Sensor Networks

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are networks of small electronic devices that are designed to collect and transmit data from a variety of sources. They are often used in the Internet of Things (IoT) to monitor and control physical environments.

#### Sensors

Sensors are small electronic devices that detect changes in their environment and then transmit that data to a central location. Sensors are used in WSNs to measure temperature, humidity, motion, sound, light, pressure, and other environmental conditions.

#### Digital Sensors

Digital sensors are sensors that can measure and transmit digital signals. These sensors are used in WSNs to measure and transmit data from a variety of sources, such as temperature, humidity, motion, sound, light, and pressure.

#### Actuators

Actuators are devices that can move or control a physical environment. They are used in WSNs to control devices such as lights, motors, and valves.

#### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

RFID technology is used in WSNs to identify and track objects. RFID tags are small electronic devices that are placed on objects and can be read by RFID readers.

#### Participatory Sensing Technology

Participatory sensing technology is a type of WSN that involves users actively participating in sensing activities. It is used to collect data from the environment in real-time and can be used to monitor air quality, traffic, and other environmental conditions.

#### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing is the use of small, low-power computers to control and monitor physical devices. These computers are often used in WSNs to process and transmit data from sensors to a central location.

#### Overview of IoT Supported Hardware Platforms

The following are some of the most popular hardware platforms used in IoT applications:

- Arduino
- Netduino
- Raspberry Pi
- Beagle Bone
- Intel Galileo Boards
- ARM Cortex

Each of these platforms is designed to provide a different set of features and capabilities, such as low power consumption, high performance, and ease of programming.
