### Embedded Computing Basics for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT

In this section, we will cover the basics of embedded computing for IoT and the hardware platforms that support IoT.

#### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing refers to the integration of computing systems into other devices, such as sensors, actuators, and other hardware components. These devices are designed to perform specific tasks and operate within predefined parameters. Some of the key features of embedded computing include:

- Small size and low power consumption
- Real-time operation and response
- Integration with sensors and other hardware components
- Limited processing and memory capabilities

#### IoT Supported Hardware Platforms

There are several hardware platforms that support IoT, including:

- Arduino: An open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software.
- NetArduino: An open-source platform that combines the simplicity of Arduino with the capabilities of networking.
- Raspberry Pi: A credit-card-sized computer that plugs into a TV or monitor and uses a standard keyboard and mouse.
- Beagle Bone: A low-cost, high-expansion hardware platform that runs full Linux and Android operating systems.
- Intel Galileo Boards: A series of development boards designed to be compatible with Arduino shields and software.
- ARM Cortex: A family of CPUs designed for use in embedded systems.

#### Hardware for IoT

In addition to the hardware platforms mentioned above, there are also several types of hardware components that are commonly used in IoT devices, including:

- Sensors: Devices that detect and measure physical quantities, such as temperature, pressure, and humidity.
- Digital Sensors: Sensors that use digital signals to communicate with other devices.
- Actuators: Devices that convert electrical signals into mechanical motion, such as motors and solenoids.
- Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology: A wireless communication technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects.
- Wireless Sensor Networks: Networks of sensors that communicate wirelessly with each other and with other devices.
- Participatory Sensing Technology: A form of crowdsourcing that uses mobile devices to collect and share data.

In conclusion, understanding the basics of embedded computing and the hardware platforms that support IoT is essential for anyone looking to develop or work with IoT devices. By familiarizing yourself with these concepts and components, you can gain the knowledge and skills needed to create innovative and effective IoT solutions.
