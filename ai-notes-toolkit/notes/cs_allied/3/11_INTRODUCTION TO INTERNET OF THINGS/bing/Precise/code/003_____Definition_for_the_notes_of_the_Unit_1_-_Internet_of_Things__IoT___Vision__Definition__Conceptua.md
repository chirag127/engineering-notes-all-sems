### Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

#### Definition
- The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of physical devices, vehicles, buildings, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and network connectivity, which enables these objects to collect and exchange data.
- IoT allows objects to be sensed and controlled remotely across existing network infrastructure, creating opportunities for more direct integration of the physical world into computer-based systems, and resulting in improved efficiency, accuracy, and economic benefit.

#### Vision
- The vision of IoT is to create a world where physical objects are seamlessly integrated into the information network, and where the physical objects can become active participants in business processes.
- Services are available to interact with these 'smart objects' over the Internet, query their state, and even control their operation.

#### Conceptual Framework
- The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components:
  - **Sensors/Actuators**: These are the devices that collect data from the environment or perform actions on the environment.
  - **Connectivity**: This refers to the communication technologies that enable the transfer of data between the sensors/actuators and the rest of the IoT system.
  - **Data Processing**: This includes the software and algorithms that process the data collected by the sensors/actuators.
  - **User Interface**: This is the interface through which the user interacts with the IoT system.

#### Architectural View
- The architectural view of IoT can be divided into three layers:
  - **Perception Layer**: This layer is responsible for collecting data from the environment through sensors and actuators.
  - **Network Layer**: This layer is responsible for transmitting the data collected by the perception layer to the application layer.
  - **Application Layer**: This layer is responsible for processing the data received from the network layer and providing services to the user.

#### Technology behind IoT
- The technology behind IoT includes a wide range of hardware and software components, including sensors, actuators, communication technologies, data processing algorithms, and user interfaces.

#### Sources of IoT
- The sources of IoT data can include a wide range of devices and systems, including sensors, actuators, smartphones, wearable devices, vehicles, and industrial equipment.

#### M2M Communication
- Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication refers to the direct communication between devices without the need for human intervention.
- M2M communication is a key enabler of IoT, allowing devices to exchange data and perform actions autonomously.

#### IoT Examples
- Some examples of IoT applications include smart homes, smart cities, connected cars, and industrial automation.

#### Design Principles for Connected Devices
- The design principles for connected devices in IoT/M2M systems include:
  - **Layers and Design Standardization**: IoT/M2M systems should be designed using a layered architecture, with standardized interfaces between the layers.
  - **Communication Technologies**: IoT/M2M systems should use appropriate communication technologies to ensure reliable and efficient data transfer.
  - **Data Enrichment and Consolidation**: IoT/M2M systems should include mechanisms for enriching and consolidating the data collected by the sensors/actuators.
  - **Ease of Designing and Affordability**: IoT/M2M systems should be easy to design and affordable to implement.
