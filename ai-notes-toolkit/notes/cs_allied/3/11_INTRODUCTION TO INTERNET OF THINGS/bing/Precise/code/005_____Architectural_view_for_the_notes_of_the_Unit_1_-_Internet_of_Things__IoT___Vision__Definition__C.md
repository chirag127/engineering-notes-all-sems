### Architectural view for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

1. **Vision:** The vision of the Internet of Things (IoT) is to create a world where physical objects are seamlessly integrated into the information network, and where they can become active participants in various processes and services.

2. **Definition:** The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of uniquely identifiable embedded computing devices within the existing Internet infrastructure.

3. **Conceptual Framework:** The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components: devices, connectivity, data processing, and user interface.

4. **Architectural view:** The architectural view of IoT includes the following layers: perception layer, network layer, and application layer.

5. **Technology behind IoT:** The technology behind IoT includes sensors, actuators, RFID, NFC, and other wireless communication technologies.

6. **Sources of the IoT:** The sources of the IoT include various sensors, devices, and systems that generate data.

7. **M2M Communication:** Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication refers to the direct communication between devices without the need for human intervention.

8. **IoT Examples:** Some examples of IoT include smart homes, smart cities, connected cars, and wearable devices.

9. **Design Principles for Connected Devices:** The design principles for connected devices include IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing and affordability.

10. **IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization:** The IoT/M2M systems layers include the device layer, the connectivity layer, the data processing layer, and the application layer. Design standardization refers to the use of common standards and protocols to ensure interoperability between different devices and systems.

11. **Communication technologies:** The communication technologies used in IoT include various wireless communication technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks.

12. **Data enrichment and consolidation:** Data enrichment refers to the process of enhancing the data collected from various sources by adding additional information. Data consolidation refers to the process of combining data from multiple sources into a single, consistent view.

13. **Ease of designing and affordability:** The ease of designing and affordability of IoT devices and systems is important to ensure their widespread adoption and use.
