### Architectural view for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

1. **Vision:** The vision of the Internet of Things (IoT) is to create a world where everyday objects are connected to the internet, allowing them to exchange data and interact with each other to improve our lives.

2. **Definition:** The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the network of physical objects or "things" embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet.

3. **Conceptual Framework:** The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components: 
    - Devices: Physical objects or "things" that are connected to the internet and can collect and exchange data.
    - Connectivity: The means by which devices communicate with each other and with the cloud.
    - Data Processing: The process of collecting, storing, and analyzing data generated by IoT devices.
    - User Interface: The means by which users interact with IoT devices and systems.

4. **Architectural view:** The architectural view of IoT includes the following layers:
    - Perception Layer: Responsible for collecting data from the physical world through sensors and other devices.
    - Network Layer: Responsible for transmitting data between devices and the cloud.
    - Middleware Layer: Responsible for data processing, storage, and management.
    - Application Layer: Responsible for providing services and applications to users.

5. **Technology behind IoT:** The technology behind IoT includes sensors, connectivity, data processing, and user interfaces. Some of the key technologies used in IoT include wireless communication, cloud computing, big data analytics, and machine learning.

6. **Sources of the IoT:** The sources of the IoT include sensors, devices, and other technologies that collect and transmit data.

7. **M2M Communication:** Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication refers to the direct communication between devices without the need for human intervention.

8. **IoT Examples:** Some examples of IoT applications include smart homes, smart cities, connected cars, and wearable devices.

9. **Design Principles for Connected Devices:** The design principles for connected devices include IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing and affordability.

10. **IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization:** IoT/M2M systems are typically designed using a layered architecture, with each layer responsible for a specific function. Standardization is important to ensure interoperability between devices and systems.

11. **Communication technologies:** Communication technologies used in IoT include wireless communication protocols such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks.

12. **Data enrichment and consolidation:** Data enrichment refers to the process of enhancing the quality of data by adding additional information. Data consolidation refers to the process of combining data from multiple sources into a single, consistent view.

13. **Ease of designing and affordability:** Ease of designing and affordability are important considerations when designing IoT systems, as they can impact the adoption and scalability of the technology.
