# Unit 4 - Programming the Arduino: Arduino Platform Boards Anatomy

The Arduino platform consists of a range of microcontroller boards that can be programmed to perform various tasks. These boards are based on the open-source hardware and software platform and are designed to be easy to use for beginners, while also being powerful and flexible enough for advanced users.

### Arduino Platform Boards Anatomy

- **Microcontroller:** The microcontroller is the brain of the Arduino board. It is responsible for executing the code that is uploaded to the board. The most commonly used microcontroller on Arduino boards is the Atmel AVR, but other microcontrollers are also used.

- **Digital and Analog Pins:** The digital and analog pins on the Arduino board are used to interface with external devices. The digital pins can be used for digital input (e.g. reading the state of a button) or digital output (e.g. controlling an LED). The analog pins can be used to read analog signals (e.g. from a sensor) or to generate analog output (e.g. to control the brightness of an LED).

- **Power Supply:** The Arduino board can be powered through the USB connection or through an external power supply. The board can operate on an external supply of 6 to 20 volts.

- **USB Connection:** The USB connection is used to upload code to the board and to power the board. It can also be used for serial communication between the board and a computer.

- **Reset Button:** The reset button is used to reset the microcontroller on the board. This can be useful when debugging code or when the board is not responding.

- **LEDs:** Most Arduino boards have one or more LEDs on the board. These can be used to indicate the status of the board or for debugging purposes.

- **Other Components:** Depending on the specific Arduino board, there may be other components such as voltage regulators, crystal oscillators, and other components that are necessary for the operation of the board.
