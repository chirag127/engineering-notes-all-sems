### Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

#### Definition
- The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of physical devices, vehicles, buildings, and other objects, embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and network connectivity, that enables these objects to collect and exchange data.
- IoT allows objects to be sensed and controlled remotely across existing network infrastructure, creating opportunities for more direct integration of the physical world into computer-based systems, and resulting in improved efficiency, accuracy, and economic benefit.

#### Vision
- The vision of IoT is to create a world where physical objects are seamlessly integrated into the information network, and where the physical objects can become active participants in business processes.
- Services are available to interact with these 'smart objects' over the Internet, query and change their state and any information associated with them, taking into account security and privacy issues.

#### Conceptual Framework
- The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components:
  - **Devices**: Physical objects that are connected to the network and can collect and transmit data.
  - **Network**: The communication infrastructure that enables the transmission of data between devices and the cloud.
  - **Cloud**: The storage and processing of data collected by devices.
  - **Applications**: The software that enables users to interact with devices and data.

#### Architectural View
- The architectural view of IoT includes the following layers:
  - **Perception Layer**: This layer is responsible for collecting data from the physical world through sensors and transmitting it to the network layer.
  - **Network Layer**: This layer is responsible for transmitting data between the perception layer and the application layer.
  - **Application Layer**: This layer is responsible for processing data and providing services to users.

#### Technology behind IoT
- The technology behind IoT includes sensors, actuators, communication technologies, and data processing and storage technologies.

#### Sources of IoT
- The sources of IoT data include sensors, devices, and other connected objects.

#### M2M Communication
- Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication refers to the automated exchange of data between machines, without the need for human intervention.

#### IoT Examples
- Some examples of IoT applications include smart homes, smart cities, connected cars, and wearable devices.

#### Design Principles for Connected Devices
- The design principles for connected devices include:
  - **IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization**: The design of IoT systems should follow a standardized layered architecture.
  - **Communication technologies**: IoT systems should use appropriate communication technologies to enable the transmission of data between devices and the cloud.
  - **Data enrichment and consolidation**: IoT systems should be able to enrich and consolidate data from multiple sources to provide more meaningful insights.
  - **Ease of designing and affordability**: IoT systems should be easy to design and affordable to implement.
