# Participatory Sensing Technology

- Participatory sensing is an approach to data collection and interpretation in which individuals, acting alone or in groups, use their personal mobile devices and web services to systematically explore interesting aspects of their worlds ranging from health to culture  .
- Participatory sensing differs from traditional sensor networks in that the sensors are owned and controlled by the users, who can decide when, where, and what to sense, and how to share and use the data.
- Participatory sensing can enable various applications such as environmental monitoring, urban planning, traffic management, public health, social networking, and citizen science.
- Participatory sensing involves four main components: sensors, devices, networks, and services.
  - Sensors are the hardware components that measure physical phenomena such as sound, light, temperature, humidity, air quality, etc. Sensors can be embedded in mobile devices, attached as external peripherals, or deployed as standalone devices.
  - Devices are the personal mobile devices that users carry or wear, such as smartphones, tablets, smartwatches, etc. Devices can act as sensor platforms, data processors, data storages, data visualizers, and data communicators.
  - Networks are the wireless communication infrastructures that enable data transmission and sharing among devices and services, such as cellular networks, Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth networks, etc.
  - Services are the web-based platforms that provide data aggregation, analysis, visualization, and feedback to the users and other stakeholders, such as cloud servers, web applications, databases, etc.
