## Unit 3 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT

- IoT devices communicate with other devices, applications, and services using various protocols and technologies, depending on the requirements and constraints of the scenario .
- Wireless Medium Access Issues
  - Wireless medium access is the process of coordinating the access of multiple devices to a shared wireless channel, such as radio frequency or optical spectrum.
  - Wireless medium access issues include interference, collision, hidden terminal, exposed terminal, fairness, energy efficiency, scalability, and security.
  - Wireless medium access protocols can be classified into two categories: contention-based and contention-free.
    - Contention-based protocols allow devices to compete for the channel access, such as CSMA/CA, ALOHA, and Slotted ALOHA.
    - Contention-free protocols assign the channel access to devices in a deterministic or random manner, such as TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and OFDMA.
- MAC Protocol Survey
  - MAC protocols are the rules that govern how devices access the wireless medium and exchange data frames.
  - MAC protocols for IoT devices should consider the characteristics and challenges of IoT networks, such as heterogeneity, mobility, scalability, reliability, latency, and energy efficiency.
  - MAC protocols for IoT can be classified into three categories: centralized, distributed, and hybrid.
    - Centralized protocols rely on a central controller or coordinator to manage the channel access, such as IEEE 802.15.4 and IEEE 802.11.
    - Distributed protocols allow devices to self-organize and cooperate to access the channel, such as B-MAC, X-MAC, and SMAC.
    - Hybrid protocols combine the advantages of both centralized and distributed protocols, such as Z-MAC and RI-MAC.
- Survey Routing Protocols
  - Routing protocols are the algorithms that determine the paths of data packets from the source to the destination in a network.
  - Routing protocols for IoT devices should consider the constraints and requirements of IoT networks, such as topology, traffic, energy, mobility, and scalability.
  - Routing protocols for IoT can be classified into three categories: proactive, reactive, and hybrid.
    - Proactive protocols maintain the routing information of the network periodically or periodically, such as OLSR, DSDV, and RIP.
    - Reactive protocols discover the routing information on demand, such as AODV, DSR, and RPL.
    - Hybrid protocols combine the features of both proactive and reactive protocols, such as ZRP, EIGRP, and CORMAN.
- Sensor Deployment & Node Discovery
  - Sensor deployment is the process of placing and configuring the sensor nodes in the target area to achieve the desired coverage and connectivity.
  - Sensor deployment can be classified into two types: deterministic and random.
    - Deterministic deployment follows a predefined pattern or strategy, such as grid, hexagonal, or triangular.
    - Random deployment relies on stochastic or probabilistic methods, such as uniform, Gaussian, or Poisson.
  - Node discovery is the process of identifying and locating the sensor nodes in the network.
  - Node discovery can be performed using various techniques, such as GPS, RSSI, TOA, TDOA, or AOA.
- Data Aggregation & Dissemination
  - Data aggregation is the process of collecting and processing the data from multiple sensor nodes to reduce the redundancy, enhance the quality, and save the energy.
  - Data aggregation can be performed using various methods, such as clustering, compression, fusion, or filtering.
  - Data dissemination is the process of distributing the data or queries from the sink or the source to the relevant sensor nodes in the network.
  - Data dissemination can be performed using various protocols, such as SPIN, Directed Diffusion, GBR, or TAG.
