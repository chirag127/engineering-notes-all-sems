# Design Principles for Connected Devices

Connected devices are physical objects that can communicate with other devices or systems over the internet or other networks. They are also known as Internet of Things (IoT) or Machine-to-Machine (M2M) devices. Connected devices can collect, process, and share data, as well as perform actions based on commands or rules. They can enable various applications and scenarios, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart agriculture, smart health, smart manufacturing, and more.

Designing connected devices requires considering various aspects, such as functionality, usability, security, scalability, interoperability, and serviceability. The following are some of the design principles that can help create successful connected devices:

- **Do your research**: Before designing a connected device, it is important to understand the problem it is trying to solve, the value it is providing to the users, and the context in which it will operate. Researching the user needs, preferences, expectations, and behaviors can help define the device's features, functions, and interactions. Researching the market, competitors, and trends can help identify the opportunities, challenges, and gaps in the existing solutions. Researching the technical, regulatory, and ethical requirements can help ensure the device's compliance, safety, and reliability.

- **Align features with user value**: A connected device should not have features just for the sake of having them, but rather focus on the features that deliver the most value to the users. Value can be measured in terms of utility, convenience, efficiency, cost, satisfaction, or other criteria. A connected device should also avoid feature creep, which is adding unnecessary or excessive features that can compromise the device's performance, usability, or security. A good way to align features with user value is to use the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) approach, which is to create a product with the minimum set of features that can satisfy the user needs and test it with real users to get feedback and iterate .

- **Look at the whole picture**: A connected device is not an isolated entity, but rather part of a larger system or network of devices, systems, and users. During IoT device design, it is important to consider everything the device could connect to, control, or communicate with in the network, and design accordingly. If designers understand the bigger picture, it helps create a device that is interoperable and can integrate into more use cases. It also helps to identify the potential risks, threats, and dependencies that can affect the device's functionality or security .

- **Consider the operating settings**: A connected device should be designed with the context and environment in which it will operate in mind. This includes the physical, social, and cultural aspects of the setting. For example, a connected device should be able to withstand the temperature, humidity, dust, or vibration of the environment. It should also be able to adapt to the network availability, bandwidth, or latency of the setting. Moreover, it should be able to respect the privacy, norms, and expectations of the users and stakeholders in the setting. A connected device should be tested and validated in the real or simulated operating settings to ensure its suitability and reliability .

- **Remember about the security**: Security is one of the most critical and challenging aspects of designing connected devices. A connected device should be able to protect the data it collects, processes, and shares from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure. It should also be able to prevent unauthorized or malicious actions on the device or the network. A connected device should follow the security best practices, such as encrypting the data, authenticating the users and devices, updating the software and firmware, and monitoring the activity and performance. A connected device should also be able to handle the possible security incidents, such as detecting, reporting, and recovering from the attacks .

- **Build with the context in mind**: A connected device should be designed with the user's perspective and experience in mind. This means that the device should be easy to use, understand, and interact with. It should also be intuitive, responsive, and consistent. A connected device should provide clear and timely feedback, guidance, and support to the users. It should also allow the users to customize, control, and manage the device and the data according to their preferences and needs. A connected device should be user-centered, user-friendly, and user-empowering .
