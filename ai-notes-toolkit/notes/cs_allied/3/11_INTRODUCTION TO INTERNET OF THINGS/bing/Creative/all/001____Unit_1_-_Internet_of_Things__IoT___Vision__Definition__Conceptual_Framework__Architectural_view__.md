## Internet of Things (IoT): Vision, Definition, Conceptual Framework, Architectural view, technology behind IoT, Sources of the IoT, M2M Communication, IoT Examples

- **IoT Vision**: The vision behind IoT is to have plug-n-play smart objects that can be deployed in any environment with an interoperable interconnection backbone that allows them to blend with other smart objects around them. Standardization of frequency bands and protocols plays a pivotal role in accomplishing this goal. IoT aims to provide the vision of connected world of Things. Devices, people and systems are connected with one another in such a way that they can communicate with each other over Internet so gives the illusion of web of things.
- **IoT Definition**: The Internet of Things, also called The Internet of Objects, refers to a wireless network between objects. By embedding short-range mobile transceivers into a wide array of additional gadgets and everyday items, enabling new forms of communication between people and things, and between things themselves. IoT is a paradigm based on the Internet that comprises many interconnected technologies like RFID (Radio Frequency IDentification) and WSAN (Wireless Sensor and Actor Networks) in order to exchange information.
- **IoT Conceptual Framework**: A conceptual framework for IoT can be described as follows:
  - **Things**: The physical objects that are connected to the Internet and can sense, actuate, and communicate with each other. They can be smart devices, sensors, actuators, RFID tags, etc.
  - **Network**: The communication infrastructure that enables data transmission and exchange among things. It can be wired or wireless, and can use different protocols and standards.
  - **Middleware**: The software layer that provides common services and functions for the things and the applications. It can include data management, device management, security, privacy, etc.
  - **Applications**: The software applications that provide specific functionalities and services for the end-users. They can be domain-specific or cross-domain, and can use different platforms and interfaces.
- **IoT Architectural view**: An architectural view of IoT can be described as follows:
  - **Perception layer**: The lowest layer that consists of the things that perceive the physical world and collect data. It can include sensors, RFID readers, cameras, etc.
  - **Network layer**: The middle layer that consists of the network devices and protocols that transmit and process the data. It can include routers, gateways, switches, etc.
  - **Application layer**: The highest layer that consists of the applications that provide services and intelligence for the users. It can include cloud computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, etc.
- **Technology behind IoT**: The technology behind IoT can be classified into three categories:
  - **Identification technologies**: The technologies that enable the unique identification and addressing of things. They can include RFID, NFC (Near Field Communication), QR (Quick Response) codes, etc.
  - **Communication technologies**: The technologies that enable the data transmission and exchange among things. They can include Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, 6LoWPAN, etc.
  - **Computation technologies**: The technologies that enable the data processing and analysis for the things and the applications. They can include cloud computing, fog computing, edge computing, etc.
- **Sources of the IoT**: The sources of the IoT can be divided into two types:
  - **Active sources**: The sources that can actively generate and send data to the network. They can include smart devices, sensors, actuators, etc.
  - **Passive sources**: The sources that can only be read by the network. They can include RFID tags, QR codes, barcodes, etc.
- **M2M Communication**: M2M (Machine-to-Machine) communication is the direct communication between devices without human intervention. It is one of the key features of IoT that enables the automation and coordination of things. M2M communication can use different protocols and standards, such as MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport), CoAP (Constrained Application Protocol), etc.
