# Embedded Platforms for IoT

## Embedded computing basics
- Embedded computing is the process of designing and implementing specialized hardware and software systems that perform specific functions within a larger system or device.
- Embedded systems typically have limited resources, such as memory, processing power, and battery life, and must meet strict requirements for reliability, security, and performance.
- Embedded systems are ubiquitous in IoT devices, such as sensors, actuators, gateways, and edge nodes, that collect, process, and communicate data over a network.
- Embedded systems can be classified into three categories based on their interaction with the environment: reactive, interactive, and hybrid.
  - Reactive systems respond to external events or stimuli without user intervention, such as a thermostat or a smoke detector.
  - Interactive systems communicate with users or other systems through a user interface or a network interface, such as a smartwatch or a smart speaker.
  - Hybrid systems combine both reactive and interactive features, such as a smart camera or a drone.

## Overview of IoT supported Hardware platforms
- There are many choices of development platforms for embedded IoT applications, each with its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the use case, the target device, and the developer's preferences.
- Some of the popular and widely used hardware platforms for IoT are:
  - Arduino: An open-source platform that consists of a range of microcontroller boards and a software development environment that supports various programming languages, such as C, C++, and Python. Arduino is suitable for beginners and hobbyists who want to prototype and experiment with IoT projects, as it offers a simple and intuitive programming interface, a large community of users and developers, and a variety of sensors, actuators, and shields that can be easily attached to the boards. Arduino boards are low-cost, low-power, and compatible with various operating systems, such as Windows, Linux, and Mac OS. However, Arduino boards have limited processing power, memory, and security features, and may not be suitable for complex or commercial IoT applications.
  - Raspberry Pi: A single-board computer that runs on a Linux-based operating system, such as Raspbian, and supports various programming languages, such as Python, Java, and C. Raspberry Pi is more powerful and versatile than Arduino, as it can perform complex computations, run multiple applications, and connect to various peripherals, such as cameras, keyboards, and monitors. Raspberry Pi can also act as a gateway or an edge node for IoT devices, as it can communicate with sensors and actuators through GPIO pins, and with other devices or cloud services through Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or Ethernet. Raspberry Pi is also low-cost, open-source, and has a large community of users and developers. However, Raspberry Pi consumes more power and requires more cooling than Arduino, and may not be suitable for battery-powered or harsh environments.
  - BeagleBone: A single-board computer that runs on a Linux-based operating system, such as Debian, and supports various programming languages, such as Python, C, and C++. BeagleBone is similar to Raspberry Pi in terms of performance and functionality, but it has more GPIO pins, analog inputs, and PWM outputs, which makes it more suitable for interfacing with sensors and actuators. BeagleBone also has a built-in microcontroller that can run real-time applications independently from the main processor, which can improve the reliability and responsiveness of the system. BeagleBone is also low-cost, open-source, and has a large community of users and developers. However, BeagleBone consumes more power and requires more cooling than Arduino, and may not be suitable for battery-powered or harsh environments.
  - Intel Galileo: A single-board computer that runs on a Linux-based operating system, such as Yocto, and supports various programming languages, such as C, C++, and Python. Intel Galileo is compatible with Arduino shields and software, which makes it easy to use for Arduino users and developers. Intel Galileo also has more processing power, memory, and security features than Arduino, which makes it more suitable for complex or commercial IoT applications. Intel Galileo can also communicate with sensors and actuators through GPIO pins, and with other devices or cloud services through Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or Ethernet. However, Intel Galileo is more expensive, consumes more power, and requires more cooling than Arduino, and may not be suitable for battery-powered or harsh environments.
