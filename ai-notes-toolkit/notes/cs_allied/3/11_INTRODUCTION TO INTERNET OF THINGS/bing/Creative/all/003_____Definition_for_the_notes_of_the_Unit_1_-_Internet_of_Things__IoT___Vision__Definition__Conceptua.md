## Vision
- The vision of the Internet of Things (IoT) is to create a network of physical objects that can communicate and exchange data with each other and with other devices and systems over the Internet or other communications networks.
- The IoT aims to enable new applications and services that can improve the quality of life, efficiency, productivity, and sustainability of various domains, such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart agriculture, smart industry, etc.
- The IoT also poses various challenges and opportunities for research and innovation, such as security, privacy, interoperability, scalability, reliability, intelligence, etc.

## Definition
- The Internet of Things (IoT) can be defined as the network of physical objects—“things”—that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the Internet.
- Alternatively, the IoT can be defined as the technology that allows us to add a device to an inert object (for example: vehicles, plant electronic systems, roofs, lighting, etc.) that can measure environmental parameters, generate associated data and transmit them through a communications network.
- The IoT can also be defined as the concept of connecting any device (so long as it has an on/off switch) to the Internet and to other connected devices.

## Conceptual Framework
- A conceptual framework for the IoT can be based on the following components:
  - Things: The physical objects that are connected to the IoT, such as sensors, actuators, RFID tags, smartphones, etc. They can have different capabilities, such as sensing, processing, communication, identification, etc.
  - Network: The communication infrastructure that enables the data transmission and exchange between the things and other devices and systems. It can consist of various technologies, such as wired, wireless, cellular, satellite, etc.
  - Middleware: The software layer that provides the common services and functions for the IoT, such as data management, device management, security, privacy, interoperability, etc. It can also support various applications and platforms for the IoT.
  - Applications: The software programs that use the data and services provided by the IoT to create value and benefits for the users and stakeholders. They can span various domains, such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart agriculture, smart industry, etc.

## Architectural view
- An architectural view of the IoT can be based on the following layers:
  - Perception layer: The layer that collects the data from the physical world through the things, such as sensors, RFID tags, etc. It can also perform some preprocessing and filtering of the data.
  - Network layer: The layer that transmits the data from the perception layer to the processing layer through the network, such as wired, wireless, cellular, satellite, etc. It can also perform some routing and aggregation of the data.
  - Processing layer: The layer that processes the data from the network layer and provides the services and functions for the IoT, such as data management, device management, security, privacy, interoperability, etc. It can also support various applications and platforms for the IoT.
  - Application layer: The layer that uses the data and services from the processing layer to create value and benefits for the users and stakeholders, such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart agriculture, smart industry, etc.

## Technology behind IoT
- The technology behind the IoT can be categorized into the following types:
  - Identification technology: The technology that enables the unique identification of the things, such as RFID, NFC, QR code, etc.
  - Sensing technology: The technology that enables the measurement of the physical parameters of the things and the environment, such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, etc.
  - Communication technology: The technology that enables the data transmission and exchange between the things and other devices and systems, such as wired, wireless, cellular, satellite, etc.
