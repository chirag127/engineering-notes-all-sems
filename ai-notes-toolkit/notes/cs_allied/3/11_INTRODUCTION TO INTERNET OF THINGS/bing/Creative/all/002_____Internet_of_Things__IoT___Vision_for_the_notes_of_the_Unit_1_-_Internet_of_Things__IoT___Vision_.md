# Internet of Things (IoT): Vision

- The vision behind IoT is to have plug-n-play smart objects that can be deployed in any environment with an interoperable interconnection backbone that allows them to blend with other smart objects around them.
- IoT aims to provide the vision of connected world of Things, where devices, people and systems are connected with one another in such a way that they can communicate with each other over Internet.
- IoT is a technological revolution that enables new forms of communication between people and things, and between things themselves, by embedding short-range mobile transceivers into a wide array of additional gadgets and everyday items.

# Internet of Things (IoT): Definition

- The Internet of Things, also called The Internet of Objects, refers to a wireless network between objects.
- IoT is a paradigm based on the Internet that comprises many interconnected technologies like RFID (Radio Frequency IDentification) and WSAN (Wireless Sensor and Actor Networks) in order to exchange information.
- IoT is a network of physical objects or things embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and network connectivity, which enables these objects to collect and exchange data.

# Internet of Things (IoT): Conceptual Framework

- A conceptual framework for IoT consists of four main layers: the device layer, the network layer, the service layer, and the application layer.
- The device layer consists of the physical objects or things that are equipped with sensing, actuating, processing, and communication capabilities.
- The network layer provides the connectivity and interoperability among the devices, as well as the access to the Internet and other networks.
- The service layer provides the functionalities and services for the devices, such as data storage, processing, analysis, management, and security.
- The application layer provides the end-user applications and services that utilize the data and services from the device and service layers, such as smart home, smart city, smart health, smart agriculture, etc.

# Internet of Things (IoT): Architectural View

- An architectural view of IoT can be described by three main components: the things, the Internet, and the cloud.
- The things are the physical objects or devices that have sensing, actuating, processing, and communication capabilities, and can interact with the environment and other things.
- The Internet is the global network that provides the connectivity and interoperability among the things, as well as the access to the cloud and other networks.
- The cloud is the distributed computing platform that provides the storage, processing, analysis, management, and security of the data and services for the things and the Internet.

# Internet of Things (IoT): Technology behind IoT

- The technology behind IoT can be classified into three categories: identification, communication, and computation.
- Identification is the process of assigning a unique identity to each thing, such as a serial number, a barcode, a QR code, or an RFID tag.
- Communication is the process of transmitting and receiving data among the things, the Internet, and the cloud, using various wired or wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, NFC, cellular, satellite, etc.
- Computation is the process of processing, analyzing, and managing the data and services for the things, the Internet, and the cloud, using various hardware and software technologies, such as microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, databases, cloud computing, big data, artificial intelligence, etc.

# Internet of Things (IoT): Sources of the IoT

- The sources of the IoT are the physical objects or devices that generate or consume data and services in the IoT paradigm, such as sensors, actuators, smartphones, tablets, laptops, cameras, wearables, etc.
- The sources of the IoT can be classified into three types: passive, active, and hybrid.
- Passive sources are the ones that do not have any processing or communication capabilities, and only provide identification information, such as RFID tags, barcodes, QR codes, etc.
- Active sources are the ones that have processing and communication capabilities, and can generate and consume data and services, such as sensors, actuators, smartphones, tablets, laptops, cameras, wearables, etc.
- Hybrid sources are the ones that have both passive and active features, such as smart cards, smart labels, smart posters, etc.
