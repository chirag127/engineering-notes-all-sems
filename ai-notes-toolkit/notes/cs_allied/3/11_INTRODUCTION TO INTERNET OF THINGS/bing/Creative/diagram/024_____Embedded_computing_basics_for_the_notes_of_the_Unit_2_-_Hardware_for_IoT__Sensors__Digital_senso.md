### Embedded Computing Basics

- An embedded system is a special-purpose computer system designed to perform a dedicated function.
- Unlike a general-purpose computer, such as a personal computer, an embedded system performs one or a few pre-defined tasks, usually with very specific requirements, and often includes task-specific hardware and mechanical parts not usually found in a general-purpose computer.
- Examples of embedded systems are an watch, washing machine, microwave oven, digital camera, etc.
- Embedded systems are typically characterized by low cost, low power, small size, high reliability, and real-time performance.
- Embedded systems can be classified into four types based on their functionality and connectivity:
  - Standalone embedded systems: These systems operate independently and do not require any network connection. They have their own user interface and memory. Examples are calculators, digital watches, etc.
  - Network embedded systems: These systems are connected to a network and communicate with other devices or systems. They can use wired or wireless protocols such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, etc. Examples are smart home devices, routers, printers, etc.
  - Mobile embedded systems: These systems are portable and battery-powered. They have limited resources and capabilities. They can use wireless technologies such as cellular, GPS, NFC, etc. Examples are smartphones, tablets, wearable devices, etc.
  - Real-time embedded systems: These systems have strict timing constraints and must respond to events within a specified deadline. They can be either hard or soft real-time systems. Hard real-time systems must meet the deadline at all costs, otherwise the system may fail. Soft real-time systems can tolerate some delay, but the quality of service may degrade. Examples are airbags, pacemakers, industrial controllers, etc.
- Embedded systems are designed and developed using a combination of hardware and software components. The hardware components include microcontrollers, microprocessors, sensors, actuators, memory, etc. The software components include operating systems, drivers, applications, etc.
- The development process of embedded systems involves several steps, such as requirement analysis, system design, hardware design, software design, testing, debugging, etc.
- Embedded systems are widely used in various domains, such as automotive, aerospace, medical, industrial, consumer, etc. They enable many applications, such as Internet of Things (IoT), smart cities, smart grids, smart agriculture, etc.
