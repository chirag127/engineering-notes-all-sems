# Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT

## Sensors, Digital sensors, actuators

- Sensors are devices that provide a usable output in response to a specified measurement.
- Sensors can be classified into analog and digital sensors based on the type of output signal they produce.
- Analog sensors produce a continuous output signal that varies proportionally to the measured quantity, such as temperature, pressure, or light intensity.
- Digital sensors produce a discrete output signal that is either on or off, such as a switch, a button, or a motion detector.
- Actuators are devices that convert an input signal into a physical action, such as a motor, a solenoid, or a speaker.
- Actuators can be controlled by analog or digital signals, depending on the type of input they require.
- Sensors and actuators are essential components of IoT systems, as they enable the interaction between the physical and the digital worlds.

## Radio frequency identification (RFID) technology, wireless sensor networks, participatory sensing technology

- RFID is a technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as tags, cards, or chips.
- RFID systems consist of three main components: a reader, a tag, and an antenna.
- The reader emits a radio signal that activates the tag, which then responds with its unique identifier or other data.
- The antenna facilitates the communication between the reader and the tag, and can be integrated with either of them or be a separate device.
- RFID technology can be used for various applications, such as inventory management, access control, asset tracking, or authentication.
- Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are networks of interconnected sensors that cooperate to gather and provide information from the environment.
- WSNs consist of sensor nodes, which are small devices that have sensing, processing, and communication capabilities.
- Sensor nodes can communicate with each other or with a central server, using wireless protocols such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, or LoRaWAN.
- WSNs can be used for various applications, such as environmental monitoring, smart agriculture, health care, or disaster management.
- Participatory sensing technology is a type of WSN that involves the participation of human users, who contribute data from their personal devices, such as smartphones, tablets, or wearables.
- Participatory sensing technology can leverage the sensors, cameras, GPS, or microphones that are embedded in the user devices, as well as the user input, feedback, or preferences.
- Participatory sensing technology can be used for various applications, such as traffic monitoring, urban planning, social networking, or citizen science.

## Embedded Platforms for IoT: Embedded computing basics, Overview of IOT supported Hardware platforms such as Arduino, NetArduino, Raspberry pi, Beagle Bone, Intel Galileo boards and ARM cortex

- Embedded computing is a type of computing that involves the integration of hardware and software components into a system that performs a specific function.
- Embedded computing systems are usually designed to be low-cost, low-power, and reliable, and to operate in real-time or near real-time.
- Embedded computing systems are widely used in IoT applications, as they enable the processing and communication of data from sensors and actuators.
- Embedded platforms for IoT are hardware devices that provide the necessary components and interfaces for developing and deploying embedded computing systems for IoT applications.
- Some of the most popular embedded platforms for IoT are:
  - Arduino: a family of open-source microcontroller boards that can be programmed using a simple C/C++ language and a user-friendly IDE. Arduino boards can be extended with various shields or modules that provide additional functionality, such as sensors, actuators, or wireless communication. Arduino boards are suitable for beginners and hobbyists, as well as for prototyping and testing IoT applications.
  - Netduino: a family of open-source microcontroller boards that are compatible with Arduino shields and modules, but can be programmed using the .NET Micro Framework and Visual Studio. Netduino boards can leverage the features and libraries of the .NET framework, such as networking, cryptography, or web services. Netduino boards are suitable for intermediate and advanced users, as well as for developing and deploying IoT applications that require more complex functionality or integration.
