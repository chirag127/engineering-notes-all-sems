## Definition

- The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet .
- The IoT can also be defined as the technology that allows us to add a device to an inert object that can measure environmental parameters, generate associated data and transmit them through a communications network.
- The IoT is a giant network of connected things and people that collect and share data about the way they are used and about the environment around them.

## Examples

- Some examples of IoT devices are:
  - Smart thermostats that can adjust the temperature based on the user's preferences and the weather conditions.
  - Smart cars that can communicate with each other and the infrastructure to avoid collisions and optimize traffic flow.
  - Smart refrigerators that can monitor the food items and order them online when they are running low.
  - Smart lights that can turn on and off automatically based on the presence of people and the ambient light.
  - Smart plant systems that can water and fertilize the plants based on the soil moisture and nutrient levels.

## Conceptual Framework

- A conceptual framework for the IoT can be based on the following components:
  - Things: The physical objects that are connected to the IoT network and can sense, actuate, compute, and communicate data.
  - Gateways: The devices that act as intermediaries between the things and the cloud, providing connectivity, security, and data processing functions.
  - Cloud: The platform that provides storage, computing, and analytics services for the IoT data, as well as applications and services for the end users and stakeholders.
  - Network: The infrastructure that enables the data transmission and communication among the things, the gateways, and the cloud, using various wired and wireless technologies.
  - Users: The human actors that interact with the IoT devices and systems, either directly or indirectly, and benefit from the IoT services and solutions.

## Architectural View

- An architectural view of the IoT can be based on the following layers:
  - Perception Layer: The layer that consists of the things and their sensors and actuators, which collect and control the physical data from the environment.
  - Network Layer: The layer that consists of the gateways and the network, which provide the connectivity and data transmission functions for the IoT.
  - Middleware Layer: The layer that consists of the cloud and its software components, which provide the data processing, storage, and analytics functions for the IoT.
  - Application Layer: The layer that consists of the various IoT applications and services that provide the functionality and value for the users and stakeholders.
  - Business Layer: The layer that consists of the business models, strategies, and policies that govern the IoT ecosystem and its stakeholders.

## Technology behind IoT

- The technology behind the IoT can be categorized into the following domains:
  - Sensing and Actuation: The domain that covers the hardware and software aspects of the things and their sensors and actuators, such as the types, characteristics, protocols, and standards of the devices.
  - Communication and Networking: The domain that covers the hardware and software aspects of the gateways and the network, such as the types, characteristics, protocols, and standards of the communication technologies and the network architectures.
  - Computing and Data Processing: The domain that covers the hardware and software aspects of the cloud and its components, such as the types, characteristics, protocols, and standards of the computing platforms, the data processing frameworks, and the data analytics techniques.
  - Application and Service: The domain that covers the software aspects of the IoT applications and services, such as the types, characteristics, protocols, and standards of the application development, the service delivery, and the user interface.
  - Security and Privacy: The domain that covers the hardware and software aspects of the security and privacy issues and solutions for the IoT, such as the types, characteristics, protocols, and standards of the security mechanisms, the privacy policies, and the trust management.
