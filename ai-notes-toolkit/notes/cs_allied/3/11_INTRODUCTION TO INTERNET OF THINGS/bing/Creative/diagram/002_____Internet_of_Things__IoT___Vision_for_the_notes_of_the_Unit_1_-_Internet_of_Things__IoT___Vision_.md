### Internet of Things (IoT): Vision

- The vision behind IoT is to have **plug-n-play smart objects** that can be deployed in any environment with an **interoperable interconnection backbone** that allows them to blend with other smart objects around them.
- IoT aims to provide the vision of **connected world of Things**. Devices, people and systems are connected with one another in such a way that they can communicate with each other over Internet so gives the illusion of **web of things**.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Definition

- The Internet of Things, also called The Internet of Objects, refers to a **wireless network between objects**. By embedding short-range mobile transceivers into a wide array of additional gadgets and everyday items, enabling new forms of communication between people and things, and between things themselves.
- The Internet of Things (IoT) is a paradigm based on the Internet that comprises many interconnected technologies like RFID (Radio Frequency IDentification) and WSAN (Wireless Sensor and Actor Networks) in order to **exchange information**.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Conceptual Framework

- A conceptual framework for IoT consists of three main layers: **perception layer**, **network layer** and **application layer**.
- The perception layer is responsible for **collecting data** from the physical world using sensors, RFID tags, cameras, etc. and **identifying objects** using unique identifiers.
- The network layer is responsible for **transmitting data** from the perception layer to the application layer using various communication technologies like Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, cellular networks, etc. and **processing data** using cloud computing, big data analytics, etc.
- The application layer is responsible for **providing services** to the users based on the data from the network layer using various applications like smart home, smart city, smart health, smart agriculture, etc.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Architectural view

- An architectural view of IoT consists of four main components: **things**, **gateways**, **networks** and **cloud**.
- Things are the **physical objects** that are embedded with sensors, actuators, processors, memory, etc. and can communicate with other things or gateways.
- Gateways are the **intermediary devices** that connect things to networks and provide data aggregation, protocol conversion, security, etc.
- Networks are the **communication infrastructures** that link gateways to cloud and provide data transmission, routing, addressing, etc.
- Cloud is the **computing platform** that provides data storage, processing, analysis, visualization, etc. and supports various applications and services.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Technology behind IoT

- Some of the key technologies that enable IoT are: **RFID**, **WSAN**, **Cloud Computing**, **Big Data**, **Machine Learning**, **Artificial Intelligence**, etc  .
- RFID is a technology that uses radio waves to **identify and track objects** using tags and readers.
- WSAN is a technology that uses wireless devices to **monitor and control physical phenomena** using sensors and actuators.
- Cloud Computing is a technology that provides **on-demand access to shared computing resources** over the Internet.
- Big Data is a technology that deals with **large and complex data sets** that require advanced techniques for storage, processing and analysis.
- Machine Learning is a technology that enables **systems to learn from data** and improve their performance without explicit programming.
- Artificial Intelligence is a technology that enables **systems to exhibit human-like intelligence** and perform tasks like reasoning, planning, decision making, etc.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Sources of the IoT

- Some of the main sources of the IoT are: **smart devices**, **smart environments**, **smart services** and **smart people**.
- Smart devices are the **objects that have sensing, computing and communication capabilities** and can interact with other devices or environments.
- Smart environments are the **spaces that have embedded smart devices** and can provide context-aware services
