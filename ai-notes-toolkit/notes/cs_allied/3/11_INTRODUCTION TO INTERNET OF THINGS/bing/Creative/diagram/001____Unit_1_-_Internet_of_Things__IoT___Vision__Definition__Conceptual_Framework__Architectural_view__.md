## Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT): Vision, Definition, Conceptual Framework, Architectural view, technology behind IoT, Sources of the IoT, M2M Communication, IoT Examples. Design Principles for Connected Devices: IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing and affordability

- **IoT Vision**: The vision behind IoT is to have plug-n-play smart objects that can be deployed in any environment with an interoperable interconnection backbone that allows them to blend with other smart objects around them. Standardization of frequency bands and protocols plays a pivotal role in accomplishing this goal. IoT aims to provide a connected world of things, where devices, people and systems can communicate with each other over the Internet.
- **IoT Definition**: The Internet of Things, also called The Internet of Objects, refers to a wireless network between objects. By embedding short-range mobile transceivers into a wide array of additional gadgets and everyday items, enabling new forms of communication between people and things, and between things themselves. IoT is a paradigm based on the Internet that comprises many interconnected technologies like RFID (Radio Frequency IDentification) and WSAN (Wireless Sensor and Actor Networks) in order to exchange information.
- **IoT Conceptual Framework**: A conceptual framework for IoT can be divided into four layers: the perception layer, the network layer, the middleware layer and the application layer. The perception layer is responsible for sensing the physical world and collecting data from various sources. The network layer is responsible for transmitting the data from the perception layer to the middleware layer using various communication technologies. The middleware layer is responsible for processing, storing and managing the data from the network layer and providing services to the application layer. The application layer is responsible for delivering the IoT services to the end users according to their needs and preferences.
- **IoT Architectural view**: An architectural view of IoT can be divided into three domains: the device domain, the information domain and the user domain. The device domain consists of the physical objects that are connected to the IoT network, such as sensors, actuators, RFID tags, smartphones, etc. The information domain consists of the data and services that are generated and provided by the device domain, such as location, temperature, identity, etc. The user domain consists of the human users and applications that consume and interact with the data and services from the information domain, such as smart home, smart city, smart health, etc.
- **Technology behind IoT**: The technology behind IoT can be classified into three categories: identification, communication and computation. Identification technologies are used to uniquely identify and address the objects in the IoT network, such as RFID, NFC (Near Field Communication), QR (Quick Response) codes, etc. Communication technologies are used to enable the data transmission and exchange between the objects in the IoT network, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, cellular, etc. Computation technologies are used to enable the data processing and analysis in the IoT network, such as cloud computing, edge computing, fog computing, etc.
- **Sources of the IoT**: The sources of the IoT are the objects that generate and provide data and services to the IoT network, such as sensors, actuators, RFID tags, smartphones, etc. These sources can be classified into three types: passive, active and hybrid. Passive sources are those that do not have any processing or communication capabilities, and rely on external readers or interrogators to access their data, such as RFID tags. Active sources are those that have their own processing and communication capabilities, and can initiate or respond to data requests, such as sensors and smartphones. Hybrid sources are those that have both passive and active modes, and can switch between them according to the context, such as NFC tags.
- **M2M Communication**: M2M (Machine-to-Machine) communication is a type of communication that occurs between two or more devices without human intervention. M2M communication can enable the automation, coordination and optimization of various processes and tasks in the IoT network, such as data collection, data transmission, data processing, data analysis, data visualization, etc. M2M communication can be based on various protocols and standards, such as MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport), CoAP (Constrained Application Protocol), HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol), etc.
