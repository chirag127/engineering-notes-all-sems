### Technology behind IoT

- IoT stands for Internet of Things, which is the concept of connecting any device with an on/off switch to the internet and to other connected devices.
- IoT devices can collect and share data about their usage and environment, enabling various applications such as smart homes, smart cities, smart factories, smart healthcare, etc.
- IoT works through a combination of wireless networking technology, physical devices, advanced data analytics and cloud computing.
- The basic process of how IoT works is as follows:
  - A group of physical devices is wired or wirelessly linked to each other and/or a central area.
  - The devices have sensors, actuators, and/or microcontrollers that enable them to collect data and perform actions.
  - The data is transmitted to a cloud platform or an edge device via a network protocol such as GSM, Wi-Fi, Ethernet, Bluetooth, Zigbee, etc.
  - The cloud platform or the edge device processes the data using software applications, machine learning, artificial intelligence, etc.
  - The processed data is sent back to the devices or to other systems for further action or analysis.
- Some of the technologies used in IoT systems are:
  - Edge computing: It increases the computing power at the edges of an IoT network, reducing communication latency and improving response time.
  - Cloud computing: It provides scalable, on-demand, and cost-effective storage and processing of IoT data in remote servers.
  - Security chips: They provide encryption, authentication, and tamper-proofing of IoT data and devices.
  - eSIM: It is a programmable SIM card that allows IoT devices to switch between different network operators without physical replacement.
  - Mesh networks: They are networks of interconnected nodes that can relay data to each other without a central hub, increasing reliability and coverage.
  - 5G: It is the fifth generation of cellular network technology that offers high speed, low latency, and massive connectivity for IoT devices.
  - Wi-Fi 6: It is the latest version of Wi-Fi technology that supports more devices, higher bandwidth, and lower power consumption for IoT devices.
  - FPGA: It is a programmable logic device that can be customized for specific IoT applications, offering flexibility and performance.
