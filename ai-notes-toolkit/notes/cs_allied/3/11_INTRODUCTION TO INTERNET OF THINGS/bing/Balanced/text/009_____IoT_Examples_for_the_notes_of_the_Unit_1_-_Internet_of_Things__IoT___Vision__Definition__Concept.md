### IoT Examples

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that can collect, process, exchange and utilize data interrelatedly, via the internet or other communications networks. IoT has many different applications in various domains, such as transportation, agriculture, retail, healthcare, manufacturing and urban development. Here are some examples of IoT devices and systems that illustrate the potential and benefits of IoT:

- **Connected cars**: IoT enables vehicles to communicate with each other, with the infrastructure, and with the cloud, to provide safety, efficiency, convenience and entertainment features. For example, Tesla's cars can receive over-the-air (OTA) software updates, monitor their performance and battery status, and access online services such as navigation, music and video streaming. Autonomous vehicles are another example of IoT in action, as they use artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled cameras, motion sensors and onboard computers to safely navigate roadways in all sorts of traffic and weather conditions.
- **Smart appliances**: IoT enables household appliances to be controlled remotely, to optimize their energy consumption, to perform self-diagnosis and maintenance, and to offer personalized services. For example, Samsung's Family Hub refrigerator can display the contents of the fridge, suggest recipes, play music and videos, and sync with other smart devices.
- **Connected security systems**: IoT enables home and business owners to monitor and protect their properties from intruders, fire, flood and other hazards. For example, Ring's video doorbell can send alerts and live video feed to the owner's smartphone, and allow them to communicate with visitors and deter unwanted guests.
- **Smart agriculture equipment**: IoT enables farmers to monitor and control their crops and livestock, to optimize their yield, quality and profitability. For example, John Deere's Field Connect system can measure soil moisture, temperature and salinity, and send the data to the cloud, where farmers can access it and adjust their irrigation and fertilization plans.
- **Connected retail**: IoT enables retailers to enhance their customer experience, inventory management, supply chain efficiency and marketing strategies. For example, Amazon Go is a cashierless store that uses sensors, cameras and AI to track the items that customers pick up and charge them automatically when they leave.
- **Connected healthcare monitors**: IoT enables patients and healthcare providers to monitor and manage various health conditions, such as diabetes, hypertension, asthma and heart disease. For example, Dexcom's G6 continuous glucose monitoring system can measure the blood glucose level of diabetic patients, send the data to their smartphone, and alert them when they need to take insulin or glucose.
- **Connected manufacturing equipment**: IoT enables manufacturers to improve their productivity, quality, safety and sustainability. For example, Siemens' MindSphere is a cloud-based platform that connects industrial machines and devices, and provides analytics and insights to optimize their performance and efficiency.
- **Connected cities**: IoT enables urban planners and managers to improve the livability, mobility, security and resilience of their cities. For example, Barcelona's smart city project uses IoT to manage traffic, parking, waste, lighting, water and energy.
