### Internet of Things (IoT): Vision

- The Internet of Things (IoT) is a vision of a world where physical objects, devices, and systems are connected to the Internet and can communicate, sense, and interact with each other and with humans.
- The IoT vision aims to create a smart, ubiquitous, and pervasive network of things that can provide value-added services, improve efficiency, and enhance quality of life.
- The IoT vision is driven by the convergence of several technologies, such as wireless communication, cloud computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, and embedded systems.
- The IoT vision is enabled by the availability of low-cost, low-power, and miniaturized sensors, actuators, and microcontrollers that can be embedded into objects and devices.
- The IoT vision is supported by the development of common standards, protocols, and platforms that can facilitate interoperability, security, and scalability of IoT systems.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Definition

- There is no universally agreed definition of IoT, but one possible definition is:

> The Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical and virtual objects that are connected to the Internet and can communicate, sense, and interact with each other and with other entities using standard protocols.

- Another possible definition is:

> The Internet of Things (IoT) is a paradigm that extends the Internet to the physical world of things, enabling anytime, anywhere, any device connectivity for anything and not only for information and communication.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Conceptual Framework

- A conceptual framework for IoT can be represented by the following diagram:

![IoT Conceptual Framework](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Internet_of_Things_Conceptual_Framework.svg/1200px-Internet_of_Things_Conceptual_Framework.svg.png)

- The framework consists of four main layers:

  - The **device layer** consists of the physical and virtual objects and devices that are connected to the Internet and can sense, actuate, and communicate.
  - The **network layer** consists of the communication technologies and protocols that enable data transmission and exchange between devices and other entities.
  - The **service layer** consists of the software applications and platforms that provide value-added services and functionalities to users and other entities based on the data collected and processed from devices.
  - The **application layer** consists of the domains and scenarios where IoT can be applied and can generate benefits and impacts for users and society.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Architectural View

- An architectural view of IoT can be represented by the following diagram:

![IoT Architectural View](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Internet_of_Things_Architecture.svg/1200px-Internet_of_Things_Architecture.svg.png)

- The architecture consists of three main components:

  - The **things** are the physical and virtual objects and devices that are connected to the Internet and can sense, actuate, and communicate.
  - The **Internet** is the global network of networks that provides connectivity and interoperability between things and other entities using standard protocols.
  - The **cloud** is the distributed system of servers and storage that provides computing and data processing capabilities for IoT applications and services.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Technology Behind IoT

- The technology behind IoT can be categorized into three main types:

  - The **hardware** technology includes the sensors, actuators, microcontrollers, and other components that are embedded into things and enable their sensing, actuation, and communication capabilities.
  - The **software** technology includes the operating systems, middleware, protocols, and applications that run on things and provide their functionality, intelligence, and security.
  - The **communication** technology includes the wireless and wired technologies and protocols that enable data transmission and exchange between things and other entities.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Sources of the IoT

- The sources of the IoT are the entities that generate, collect, and provide data for IoT applications and services. They can be classified into three main types:

  - The **sensors** are the devices that measure physical phenomena, such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, motion, etc., and convert them into digital signals.
  - The **actuators** are the devices that perform physical actions, such as switching, moving, rotating, etc., based on digital signals.
  - The **smart objects** are the devices that have both sensing and actuation capabilities, as well as computing and communication capabilities, and can perform complex tasks and behaviors.
