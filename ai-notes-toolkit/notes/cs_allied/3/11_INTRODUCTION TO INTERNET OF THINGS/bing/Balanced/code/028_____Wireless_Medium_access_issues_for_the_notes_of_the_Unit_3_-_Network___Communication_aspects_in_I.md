### Wireless Medium Access Issues for IoT

- Wireless medium access issues refer to the challenges and problems that arise when multiple IoT devices share the same wireless channel for data transmission and reception.
- Some of the common wireless medium access issues are:
  - Interference: Interference occurs when unwanted signals from other sources or devices interfere with the desired signal, causing degradation of performance, packet loss, and energy consumption. Interference can be caused by devices operating in the same or adjacent frequency bands, such as WLAN, Bluetooth, ZigBee, etc.
  - Hidden terminal problem: Hidden terminal problem occurs when two or more devices that are out of the range of each other try to communicate with a common device, resulting in collisions and retransmissions. For example, if device A and device B are both trying to send data to device C, but device A and device B cannot hear each other, they may transmit at the same time and collide at device C.
  - Exposed terminal problem: Exposed terminal problem occurs when a device is prevented from transmitting to another device because of a transmission by a third device, even though the transmission does not interfere with the intended receiver. For example, if device A is sending data to device B, and device C wants to send data to device D, device C may defer its transmission because it hears device A, even though device D is not affected by device A's transmission.
  - Near-far problem: Near-far problem occurs when a device with a stronger signal dominates the channel and prevents other devices with weaker signals from accessing the channel. For example, if device A is close to device B and device C is far from device B, device A may have a higher chance of transmitting successfully to device B than device C, even if device C has data to send.
  - Fading: Fading refers to the variation of signal strength over time and space due to multipath propagation, shadowing, and mobility. Fading can cause fluctuations in the received signal quality, leading to errors and retransmissions.

- To overcome these wireless medium access issues, various Medium Access Control (MAC) protocols have been proposed and designed for IoT applications. MAC protocols are responsible for coordinating and regulating the access of multiple IoT devices to the shared wireless channel.
- Some of the common objectives and features of MAC protocols for IoT are:
  - Energy efficiency: Energy efficiency is the ability to minimize the energy consumption of IoT devices, especially those that are battery-powered or energy-harvesting. Energy efficiency can be achieved by reducing the idle listening, collision, overhearing, and control overhead of the MAC protocol.
  - Scalability: Scalability is the ability to support a large number of IoT devices with diverse requirements and capabilities, without compromising the performance and reliability of the MAC protocol. Scalability can be achieved by using adaptive and distributed algorithms, avoiding synchronization and contention, and exploiting spatial reuse and diversity.
  - Reliability: Reliability is the ability to ensure the successful and timely delivery of data packets from the source to the destination, despite the wireless medium access issues and the dynamic network conditions. Reliability can be achieved by using error control, retransmission, acknowledgment, and feedback mechanisms, as well as prioritizing and scheduling the data packets according to their importance and urgency.
  - Throughput: Throughput is the amount of data that can be transmitted and received over the wireless channel per unit time, measured in bits per second (bps) or packets per second (pps). Throughput can be improved by increasing the channel utilization, reducing the collision and contention, and using multiple channels and antennas.
