### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT): Vision, Definition, Conceptual Framework, Architectural view, technology behind IoT, Sources of the IoT, M2M Communication, IoT Examples. ```
- Internet of Things (IoT) is a vision of a network of physical objects or things that are embedded with sensors, actuators, and communication capabilities to interact with each other and with other devices and services over the internet.
- IoT enables the collection, exchange, and analysis of data from the physical world, and the creation of new applications and services that can improve efficiency, productivity, safety, and quality of life.
- IoT is based on a conceptual framework that consists of four main components: things, communication, computation, and services.
  - Things are the physical objects or devices that can sense, actuate, and communicate with each other and with other entities.
  - Communication is the network infrastructure that enables the data transmission and exchange among things and other entities.
  - Computation is the cloud or edge computing resources that provide data processing, storage, and analytics capabilities for IoT applications and services.
  - Services are the software applications and platforms that provide functionality and value for the users and stakeholders of IoT systems.
- IoT can be viewed from different architectural perspectives, such as the three-layer architecture, the five-layer architecture, or the service-oriented architecture, depending on the level of abstraction and granularity of the system components and interactions.
- IoT relies on various technologies to enable the connectivity, interoperability, and intelligence of things, such as wireless sensor networks, radio-frequency identification (RFID), near-field communication (NFC), Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular, low-power wide-area networks (LPWAN), internet protocol (IP), web services, cloud computing, edge computing, artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), and big data analytics.
- IoT can be sourced from different domains and sectors, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart grids, smart health, smart agriculture, smart manufacturing, smart transportation, smart retail, smart environment, and smart education.
- Machine-to-machine (M2M) communication is a subset of IoT that refers to the direct communication and interaction between machines or devices without human intervention, using wired or wireless networks.
- IoT examples are numerous and diverse, ranging from smart thermostats, smart locks, smart lights, smart speakers, smart watches, smart cameras, smart meters, smart appliances, smart vehicles, smart drones, smart robots, smart wearables, smart implants, smart tags, smart sensors, smart actuators, and smart controllers.
- Design principles for connected devices are the guidelines and best practices that should be followed when developing IoT systems and solutions, to ensure their functionality, usability, reliability, security, scalability, and sustainability.
- IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization are the logical and physical divisions of the system components and functions, and the common protocols and interfaces that enable their integration and interoperability, such as the Open Systems Interconnection (OSI) model, the Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP), the Constrained Application Protocol (CoAP), the Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT), the Representational State Transfer (REST), and the oneM2M standard.
- Communication technologies are the hardware and software technologies that enable the data transmission and exchange among things and other entities, such as the wireless sensor networks, radio-frequency identification (RFID), near-field communication (NFC), Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular, low-power wide-area networks (LPWAN), internet protocol (IP), and web services.
- Data enrichment and consolidation are the processes of enhancing and integrating the data collected from the things, using techniques such as data cleaning, data transformation, data fusion, data aggregation, data compression, data encryption, and data anonymization, to improve the data quality, security, and usability for IoT applications and services.
- Ease of designing and affordability are the factors that influence the feasibility and attractiveness of IoT systems and solutions, such as the availability and cost of the hardware and software components, the simplicity and flexibility of the development and deployment processes, the compatibility and interoperability of the system architectures and protocols, the scalability and adaptability of the system performance and functionality, and the user and stakeholder satisfaction and acceptance.
