## Internet of Things (IoT): Vision, Definition, Conceptual Framework, Architectural view, technology behind IoT, Sources of the IoT, M2M Communication, IoT Examples

- The **vision** of IoT is to create a network of interconnected devices that can sense, communicate, and act on the physical and virtual world, enabling new applications and services that can improve the quality of life, efficiency, and sustainability of various domains such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart agriculture, smart industry, etc.
- The **definition** of IoT varies depending on the perspective and context, but a general one is: IoT is a system of interrelated computing devices, mechanical and digital machines, objects, animals or people that are provided with unique identifiers and the ability to transfer data over a network without requiring human-to-human or human-to-computer interaction.
- The **conceptual framework** of IoT consists of three main components: **things**, **networks**, and **applications**. Things are the physical or virtual entities that have identities, attributes, and capabilities. Networks are the communication infrastructures that enable data exchange and processing among things and applications. Applications are the software systems that provide value-added services to the end-users by exploiting the data and functionalities of things and networks.
- The **architectural view** of IoT can be divided into four layers: **perception layer**, **network layer**, **middleware layer**, and **application layer**. The perception layer is responsible for sensing, identifying, and collecting data from the physical world. The network layer is responsible for transmitting, routing, and processing data among things and other network nodes. The middleware layer is responsible for providing common services and functionalities such as data management, security, privacy, interoperability, etc. The application layer is responsible for delivering specific services and solutions to the end-users according to their needs and preferences.
- The **technology behind IoT** involves various hardware, software, and communication technologies that enable the realization of the IoT vision. Some of the key technologies are: **sensors and actuators**, **RFID and NFC**, **wireless sensor networks (WSNs)**, **cloud computing and fog computing**, **big data and data analytics**, **artificial intelligence and machine learning**, **blockchain and distributed ledger**, etc.
- The **sources of the IoT** are the different domains and sectors that generate and consume data and services through the IoT system. Some of the main sources are: **smart cities**, **smart homes**, **smart health**, **smart agriculture**, **smart industry**, **smart energy**, **smart transportation**, **smart environment**, etc.
- **M2M communication** is a type of communication that occurs between machines or devices without human intervention. It is an essential component of IoT that enables data exchange and coordination among things. M2M communication can be achieved through various protocols and standards such as **MQTT**, **CoAP**, **HTTP**, **AMQP**, **XMPP**, etc.
- **IoT examples** are the concrete instances of IoT applications and services that demonstrate the benefits and challenges of IoT in various domains and scenarios. Some of the common examples are: **smart lighting**, **smart metering**, **smart parking**, **smart waste management**, **smart irrigation**, **smart wearable devices**, **smart medical devices**, **smart manufacturing**, **smart logistics**, etc.

## Design Principles for Connected Devices: IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing and affordability

- **IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization** are the concepts and guidelines that aim to provide a common and consistent structure and methodology for designing and developing IoT/M2M systems. They help to ensure the interoperability, scalability, reliability, and security of IoT/M2M systems. Some of the main standards and frameworks are: **ISO/IEC 30141**, **ITU-T Y.2060**, **ETSI M2M**, **oneM2M**, **IETF**, **IEEE**, **OMA**, etc.
- **Communication technologies** are the technologies that enable the transmission and reception of data among things and networks in IoT/M2M systems. They can be classified into two categories: **wired** and **wireless**.
