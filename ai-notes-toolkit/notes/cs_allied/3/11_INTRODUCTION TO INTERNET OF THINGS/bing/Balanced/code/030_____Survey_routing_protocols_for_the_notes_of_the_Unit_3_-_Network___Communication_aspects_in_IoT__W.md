### Survey routing protocols for IoT

- Routing protocols are responsible for finding and maintaining routes between nodes in a network, especially in dynamic and resource-constrained environments like IoT.
- Routing protocols for IoT can be classified into three categories based on the network structure: flat, hierarchical and location-based.
- Flat routing protocols treat all nodes equally and use flooding or gossiping techniques to disseminate data. Examples of flat routing protocols are SPIN, Directed Diffusion, Flooding and Gossiping.
- Hierarchical routing protocols divide the network into clusters and assign different roles to nodes based on their energy or capabilities. Examples of hierarchical routing protocols are LEACH, PEGASIS, HEED and TEEN.
- Location-based routing protocols use the geographic information of nodes to make routing decisions. Examples of location-based routing protocols are GEAR, GPSR, GAF and ZRP.
- Routing protocols for IoT can also be classified based on the network layer they operate on: network layer, transport layer or application layer.
- Network layer routing protocols are designed to provide end-to-end connectivity and address the challenges of low-power and lossy networks (LLNs). Examples of network layer routing protocols are RPL, LOADng, AODV and DYMO .
- Transport layer routing protocols are designed to provide reliable and congestion-controlled data delivery and address the challenges of heterogeneous and constrained devices. Examples of transport layer routing protocols are CoAP, MQTT, AMQP and XMPP.
- Application layer routing protocols are designed to provide semantic interoperability and address the challenges of data representation and discovery. Examples of application layer routing protocols are LWM2M, OMA-DM, IPSO and oneM2M.
- Routing protocols for IoT must also consider the security and privacy issues that arise from the open and distributed nature of the network. Examples of secure routing protocols for IoT are S-RPL, S-LOADng, S-AODV and S-DYMO .
- Secure routing protocols for IoT must address the vulnerabilities to attacks such as sinkhole, wormhole, blackhole, Sybil, replay and selective forwarding.
- Secure routing protocols for IoT must also provide mechanisms for authentication, encryption, integrity, confidentiality, availability and non-repudiation.
