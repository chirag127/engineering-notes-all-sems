### Internet of Things (IoT): Vision

- The Internet of Things (IoT) is a vision of a world where physical objects, devices, and systems are connected to the Internet and can communicate, sense, and interact with each other and with humans.
- The IoT vision aims to create a smart, ubiquitous, and integrated network of things that can provide value-added services, improve efficiency, and enhance quality of life.
- The IoT vision is driven by the convergence of various technologies, such as wireless communication, cloud computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, and machine learning.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Definition

- There is no universally agreed definition of IoT, but one possible definition is:

> IoT is a network of physical objects that are embedded with sensors, actuators, and communication devices that enable them to collect, process, and exchange data with other objects, systems, and humans over the Internet.

- Another possible definition is:

> IoT is a paradigm that extends the Internet to the physical world, enabling things to be identified, sensed, controlled, and interacted with remotely.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Conceptual Framework

- A conceptual framework for IoT can be represented by a three-layer model, consisting of:

  - Perception layer: This layer consists of the physical objects or things that are equipped with sensors, actuators, and identifiers that enable them to sense, measure, and act on the environment, and to be uniquely identified.
  - Network layer: This layer consists of the communication devices and protocols that enable the data transmission and exchange between the things and the cloud or other systems.
  - Application layer: This layer consists of the software and services that provide the functionality and value for the users and stakeholders of the IoT system.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Architectural View

- An architectural view of IoT can be represented by a five-layer model, consisting of:

  - Device layer: This layer consists of the physical objects or things that are embedded with sensors, actuators, and communication devices that enable them to collect, process, and exchange data with other objects, systems, and humans over the Internet.
  - Edge layer: This layer consists of the devices and systems that are located at the edge of the network, such as gateways, routers, and servers, that provide data aggregation, filtering, processing, and storage capabilities, as well as security and privacy mechanisms.
  - Platform layer: This layer consists of the cloud-based platforms and services that provide data management, analytics, and visualization capabilities, as well as application development and deployment tools.
  - Service layer: This layer consists of the applications and services that provide the functionality and value for the users and stakeholders of the IoT system, such as smart home, smart city, smart health, smart agriculture, etc.
  - User layer: This layer consists of the end-users and stakeholders that interact with the IoT system, such as consumers, citizens, patients, farmers, etc.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Technology behind IoT

- The technology behind IoT can be categorized into four main domains, namely:

  - Identification: This domain consists of the technologies and standards that enable the unique identification of the things in the IoT system, such as RFID, NFC, QR codes, barcodes, etc.
  - Sensing: This domain consists of the technologies and standards that enable the sensing and measurement of the physical parameters and events in the environment, such as temperature, humidity, light, motion, sound, etc.
  - Communication: This domain consists of the technologies and standards that enable the data transmission and exchange between the things and the cloud or other systems, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, LoRa, 5G, MQTT, CoAP, etc.
  - Computation: This domain consists of the technologies and standards that enable the data processing, analysis, and decision making in the IoT system, such as cloud computing, edge computing, fog computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, machine learning, etc.

- The sources of the IoT can be classified into three main types, namely:

  - Existing devices: These are the devices that are already connected to the Internet, such as smartphones, laptops, tablets, smart TVs, etc., that can be integrated into the IoT system as data sources or controllers.
