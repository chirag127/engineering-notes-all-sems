# Wireless Sensor Networks for IoT

## Sensors, Digital Sensors, and Actuators

- Sensors are devices that measure physical quantities such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, pressure, etc. and convert them into electrical signals.
- Digital sensors are sensors that produce digital output signals, such as binary or pulse-width modulated (PWM) signals, that can be easily processed by computers or microcontrollers.
- Actuators are devices that convert electrical signals into physical actions, such as motors, valves, relays, LEDs, speakers, etc.
- Sensors and actuators are essential components of IoT systems, as they enable interaction with the physical world and data collection.

## Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- RFID is a technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as tags, cards, or chips, that are attached to or embedded in the objects.
- RFID systems consist of three main components: RFID tags, RFID readers, and RFID middleware.
- RFID tags are small devices that store a unique identifier and optionally some additional data. They can be passive (no battery), active (battery-powered), or semi-passive (battery-assisted).
- RFID readers are devices that emit radio signals and receive the responses from the RFID tags. They can be fixed or mobile, and can communicate with one or multiple tags at a time.
- RFID middleware is the software that connects the RFID readers with the backend systems, such as databases, applications, or cloud services. It performs functions such as data filtering, aggregation, processing, and analysis.

## Wireless Sensor Networks

- Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are networks of spatially distributed sensors that communicate wirelessly and cooperatively to monitor physical or environmental conditions, such as temperature, humidity, sound, vibration, etc.
- WSNs typically consist of three types of nodes: sensor nodes, sink nodes, and relay nodes.
- Sensor nodes are the basic units of WSNs, which perform sensing, processing, and communication functions. They are usually resource-constrained, such as having limited battery, memory, and computational power.
- Sink nodes are the nodes that collect the data from the sensor nodes and forward it to the external networks, such as the Internet or a cloud service. They are usually more powerful and reliable than sensor nodes, and can be wired or wireless.
- Relay nodes are the nodes that help to extend the communication range and network connectivity of the sensor nodes. They can be either dedicated nodes or sensor nodes that act as relays.

## Participatory Sensing Technology

- Participatory sensing is a technology that leverages the capabilities of mobile devices, such as smartphones, tablets, or wearable devices, to collect and share data about the environment, events, or activities, with the participation of the users or communities.
- Participatory sensing can be seen as a form of crowdsourcing, where the users voluntarily contribute data, feedback, or opinions, to a common goal or interest, such as environmental monitoring, traffic management, health care, social networking, etc.
- Participatory sensing can also be seen as a form of citizen science, where the users actively engage in scientific inquiry, such as data collection, analysis, or interpretation, to address real-world problems or questions, such as air quality, biodiversity, climate change, etc.
- Participatory sensing can enable new applications and services for IoT, such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart mobility, etc.

## Embedded Platforms for IoT

- Embedded platforms are hardware devices that are designed to perform specific functions or tasks, such as sensing, processing, or communication, with minimal or no user intervention.
- Embedded platforms for IoT are embedded platforms that are optimized for IoT applications, such as having low power consumption, high performance, wireless connectivity, security, and scalability.
- Some of the popular embedded platforms for IoT are:

  - Arduino: An open-source platform that consists of a microcontroller board and a software development environment. Arduino is easy to use, program, and extend, and supports a wide range of sensors, actuators, and shields (add-on modules).
  - Netduino: A platform that is similar to Arduino, but uses the .NET Micro Framework as the software development environment. Netduino supports the C# programming language and the Visual Studio IDE, and offers more memory and processing power than Arduino.
  - Raspberry Pi: A platform that consists of a single-board computer that runs a Linux-based operating system, such as Raspbian. Raspberry Pi can perform complex tasks, such as image processing, machine learning, or web hosting, and supports various peripherals, such as cameras, keyboards, or monitors.
  - BeagleBone: A platform that consists of a single-board computer that runs a Linux-based operating system, such as Debian. BeagleBone can perform similar tasks as Raspberry Pi
