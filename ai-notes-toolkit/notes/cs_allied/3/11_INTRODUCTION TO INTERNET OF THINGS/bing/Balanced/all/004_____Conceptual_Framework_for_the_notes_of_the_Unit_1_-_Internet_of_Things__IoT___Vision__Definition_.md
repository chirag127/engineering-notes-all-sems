- Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical objects or things that are embedded with sensors, actuators, controllers, and communication devices that enable them to exchange data and interact with other devices or systems through the internet .
- The vision of IoT is to create a smart, connected, and ubiquitous environment where humans, machines, and things can communicate and cooperate with each other to achieve various goals and applications.
- A conceptual framework for IoT is a way of describing the main components, functions, and relationships of an IoT system. It can help to understand the complexity and diversity of IoT, as well as to identify the challenges and opportunities for IoT development and implementation.
- One example of a simple conceptual framework for IoT is as follows:

  - Physical object + Controller, Sensor and Actuators + Internet = Internet of Things
  - IoT consisting of internetworked devices and objects: Gather + Enrich + Stream + Manage + Acquire + Organise and Analyse = Internet of Things with connectivity to data centre, enterprise or cloud server

- An architectural view of IoT is a way of representing the structure, components, and interactions of an IoT system. It can help to design, develop, and deploy IoT solutions, as well as to ensure interoperability, scalability, security, and reliability of IoT systems.
- One example of an architectural view of IoT is the three-layer model, which consists of the following layers:

  - Physical layer: This layer includes the physical devices or things that are connected to the internet, such as sensors, actuators, controllers, RFID tags, etc. This layer is responsible for collecting, processing, and transmitting data from the physical world to the network layer.
  - Network layer: This layer includes the communication devices and protocols that enable data transmission and exchange between the physical layer and the application layer, such as routers, gateways, switches, wireless networks, cellular networks, etc. This layer is responsible for providing connectivity, routing, addressing, and security for IoT data.
  - Application layer: This layer includes the software applications and services that provide value-added functions and features for IoT users, such as data analysis, visualization, storage, processing, etc. This layer is responsible for delivering IoT solutions for various domains and scenarios, such as smart home, smart city, smart health, smart agriculture, etc.

- The technology behind IoT is the combination of hardware, software, and communication technologies that enable the creation, operation, and management of IoT systems. Some of the key technologies behind IoT are:

  - Sensors and actuators: These are devices that can sense, measure, and control physical parameters or events, such as temperature, humidity, motion, light, sound, etc. They can convert physical signals into electrical signals, or vice versa, and communicate with other devices or systems.
  - Controllers: These are devices that can control the behavior or operation of other devices or systems, such as microcontrollers, microprocessors, embedded systems, etc. They can execute instructions, process data, and communicate with other devices or systems.
  - Communication devices and protocols: These are devices and protocols that can enable data transmission and exchange between devices or systems, such as wireless networks, cellular networks, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, ZigBee, MQTT, CoAP, etc. They can provide connectivity, routing, addressing, and security for IoT data.
  - Cloud computing and big data: These are technologies that can provide scalable, distributed, and on-demand computing and storage resources for IoT data and applications, such as cloud servers, databases, platforms, services, etc. They can enable data analysis, visualization, processing, and storage for IoT solutions.
  - Artificial intelligence and machine learning: These are technologies that can provide intelligent and adaptive functions and features for IoT data and applications, such as data mining, pattern recognition, natural language processing, computer vision, etc. They can enable data interpretation, prediction, decision making, and learning for IoT solutions.
