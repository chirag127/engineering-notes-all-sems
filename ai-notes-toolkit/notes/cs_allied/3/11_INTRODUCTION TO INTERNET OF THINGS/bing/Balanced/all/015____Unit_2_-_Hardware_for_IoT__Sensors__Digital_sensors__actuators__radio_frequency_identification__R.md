# Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT

## Sensors, Digital sensors, actuators

- Sensors are devices that provide a usable output in response to a specified measurement.
- Sensors can be classified into analog and digital sensors based on the type of output signal they produce.
- Analog sensors produce a continuous output signal that varies proportionally to the measured quantity, such as temperature, pressure, or light intensity.
- Digital sensors produce a discrete output signal that is either on or off, such as a switch, a button, or a motion detector.
- Actuators are devices that convert an input signal into a physical action, such as a motor, a solenoid, or a speaker.
- Actuators can be controlled by analog or digital signals, depending on the type of input they require.

## Radio frequency identification (RFID) technology, wireless sensor networks, participatory sensing technology

- RFID technology is a wireless communication system that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as tags, cards, or chips.
- RFID systems consist of three main components: a reader, a tag, and an antenna.
- The reader emits a radio signal that activates the tag, which then responds with its unique identification number or other data.
- The antenna transfers the radio signals between the reader and the tag.
- RFID technology can be used for various applications, such as inventory management, access control, asset tracking, or smart cards.
- Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are networks of interconnected sensors that cooperate to gather and provide information from the environment.
- WSNs consist of sensor nodes, which are small devices that have sensing, processing, and communication capabilities.
- Sensor nodes can communicate with each other or with a central base station, which collects and analyzes the data from the network.
- WSNs can be used for various applications, such as environmental monitoring, health care, smart homes, or industrial automation.
- Participatory sensing technology is a form of crowdsourcing that involves collecting and sharing data from mobile devices, such as smartphones, tablets, or wearables.
- Participatory sensing technology enables users to contribute to the sensing process by providing their location, context, or feedback.
- Participatory sensing technology can be used for various applications, such as traffic monitoring, air quality measurement, social networking, or citizen science.

## Embedded Platforms for IoT: Embedded computing basics, Overview of IOT supported Hardware platforms such as Arduino, NetArduino, Raspberry pi, Beagle Bone, Intel Galileo boards and ARM cortex

- Embedded computing is the process of designing and implementing computer systems that are integrated into a larger system, such as a device, a machine, or a vehicle.
- Embedded computing systems have specific requirements, such as low power consumption, limited memory, real-time performance, or reliability.
- Embedded computing systems typically use microcontrollers or microprocessors, which are small chips that contain a CPU, memory, and input/output interfaces.
- Embedded computing systems can be programmed using various languages, such as C, C++, Python, or Java.
- Arduino is an open-source hardware and software platform that consists of a microcontroller board and an integrated development environment (IDE).
- Arduino can be used to create interactive projects that involve sensors, actuators, LEDs, or displays.
- Arduino supports various types of boards, such as Uno, Nano, Mega, or Due, which differ in size, power, and functionality.
- Netduino is a similar platform to Arduino, but it uses the .NET Micro Framework, which allows users to program in C# or Visual Basic.
- Netduino also supports various types of boards, such as Netduino 2, Netduino 3, or Netduino Go, which differ in size, power, and functionality.
- Raspberry Pi is a single-board computer that runs on Linux and can be used as a general-purpose computer or as an embedded system.
- Raspberry Pi can be used to create projects that involve multimedia, gaming, networking, or artificial intelligence.
- Raspberry Pi supports various models, such as Model A, Model B, Model B+, or Model 4, which differ in size, power, and functionality.
