# Hardware for IoT

## Sensors
- Sensors are devices that measure physical quantities such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, pressure, etc. and convert them into electrical signals.
- Sensors are essential for IoT applications as they enable data collection from the physical world and provide feedback to the actuators.
- Sensors can be classified into two types: analog and digital.

## Digital sensors
- Digital sensors are sensors that produce discrete or binary signals, such as on/off, high/low, 0/1, etc.
- Digital sensors are easier to interface with microcontrollers and other digital devices, as they do not require analog-to-digital converters (ADCs).
- Examples of digital sensors are switches, buttons, infrared sensors, hall effect sensors, etc.

## Actuators
- Actuators are devices that convert electrical signals into physical actions, such as motion, sound, light, heat, etc.
- Actuators are essential for IoT applications as they enable physical control and manipulation of the environment and devices.
- Examples of actuators are motors, LEDs, speakers, heaters, relays, etc.

## Radio frequency identification (RFID) technology
- RFID is a technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as tags, cards, or chips, that are attached to or embedded in the objects.
- RFID consists of two components: a reader and a tag. The reader emits radio signals and the tag responds with its unique identifier or other data.
- RFID is widely used for IoT applications such as inventory management, access control, asset tracking, etc.

## Wireless sensor networks
- Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are networks of spatially distributed sensors that communicate wirelessly with each other and with a central node or gateway.
- WSNs are used for IoT applications such as environmental monitoring, smart agriculture, smart cities, etc.
- WSNs have several challenges such as limited power, bandwidth, memory, and security.

## Participatory sensing technology
- Participatory sensing is a technology that uses mobile devices, such as smartphones, tablets, or wearables, as sensors to collect and share data from the users and their surroundings.
- Participatory sensing is used for IoT applications such as crowd-sourcing, social networking, health care, etc.
- Participatory sensing has several advantages such as low cost, high availability, and user involvement.

## Embedded Platforms for IoT
- Embedded platforms are hardware devices that are designed to perform specific tasks or functions, such as sensing, processing, or communication, with minimal or no user intervention.
- Embedded platforms are suitable for IoT applications as they are small, low-power, and programmable.
- Some of the popular embedded platforms for IoT are:

### Arduino
- Arduino is an open-source platform that consists of a microcontroller board and a software development environment.
- Arduino is easy to use and supports a variety of sensors, actuators, and shields (expansion boards) for IoT applications.
- Arduino supports several programming languages, such as C, C++, and Python.

### Netduino
- Netduino is a platform that is similar to Arduino, but uses the .NET Micro Framework as the software development environment.
- Netduino is compatible with most Arduino shields and supports C# as the programming language.
- Netduino has more memory and processing power than Arduino, but is more expensive and less popular.

### Raspberry Pi
- Raspberry Pi is a platform that consists of a single-board computer that runs a Linux-based operating system, such as Raspbian, Ubuntu, or Windows 10 IoT Core.
- Raspberry Pi is more powerful and versatile than Arduino or Netduino, as it can run multiple applications and support various peripherals, such as cameras, keyboards, mice, etc.
- Raspberry Pi supports several programming languages, such as Python, Java, C, C++, etc.

### BeagleBone
- BeagleBone is a platform that is similar to Raspberry Pi, but has more input/output (I/O) pins and supports real-time processing.
- BeagleBone runs a Linux-based operating system, such as Debian, Ubuntu, or Android, and supports several programming languages, such as Python, C, C++, etc.
- BeagleBone is more expensive and less popular than Raspberry Pi, but is more suitable for industrial and robotics applications.

### Intel Galileo
- Intel Galileo is a platform that is based on the Intel Quark SoC X1000, a 32-bit, single-core, Pentium-class processor.
- Intel Galileo is compatible with Arduino shields and software development environment, and supports several programming languages, such as C, C++, and Python.
- Intel Galileo is more powerful and reliable than Arduino, but is more expensive and less available.
