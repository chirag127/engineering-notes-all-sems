# Architectural view for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

- **Internet of Things (IoT)** is a paradigm that envisions a network of interconnected objects that can sense, communicate, and act on the physical and virtual world.
- **Vision** of IoT is to create a smart, ubiquitous, and pervasive environment where objects can collaborate and offer services to humans and other objects.
- **Definition** of IoT is not universally agreed upon, but one possible definition is: "A dynamic global network infrastructure with self-configuring capabilities based on standard and interoperable communication protocols where physical and virtual 'things' have identities, physical attributes, and virtual personalities and use intelligent interfaces, and are seamlessly integrated into the information network" .
- **Conceptual Framework** of IoT consists of three main components: things, communication, and computation.
  - **Things** are the physical or virtual objects that have identities, attributes, and functionalities. They can be equipped with sensors, actuators, RFID tags, or other devices that enable them to interact with the environment and other things.
  - **Communication** is the process of exchanging data and information between things and other entities, such as humans, applications, or cloud services. It can be done through wired or wireless networks, using various protocols and technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, cellular, or 6LoWPAN.
  - **Computation** is the process of processing, analyzing, and storing the data and information collected from things and communication. It can be done at different levels of the IoT system, such as the edge, the fog, or the cloud, depending on the requirements and constraints of the application and the devices.
- **Architectural view** of IoT can vary depending on the perspective and the scope of the system, but one possible way to describe it is using a four-layered architecture :
  - **Media/Device layer**: This is the lowest layer of the architecture, where the things are located. It includes the devices, sensors, actuators, and RFID tags that enable the things to sense, actuate, and communicate with the environment and other things. It also includes the media, such as radio waves, light, or sound, that carry the signals and data between the things and the network layer.
  - **Network layer**: This is the layer that provides communication and connectivity between the things in the media/device layer and the entities in the service and application support layer. It includes the protocols and technologies that enable the things to connect and communicate with each other and with the wider internet. It also includes the gateways, routers, and switches that route and forward the data and information between the different networks and domains.
  - **Service and Application Support layer**: This is the layer that provides the computation and storage capabilities for the IoT system. It includes the servers, databases, cloud services, and platforms that process, analyze, and store the data and information collected from the things and the network layer. It also includes the middleware, APIs, and frameworks that enable the integration and interoperability of the different components and services of the IoT system.
  - **Application layer**: This is the highest layer of the architecture, where the end-users and applications are located. It includes the human-machine interfaces, such as smartphones, tablets, or web browsers, that enable the users to access and interact with the IoT system and its services. It also includes the applications, such as smart home, smart city, or smart health, that provide the functionality and value for the users and the things.
- **Technology behind IoT** is a combination of various hardware, software, and networking technologies that enable the realization of the IoT vision and framework. Some of the key technologies are:
  - **Sensors and Actuators**: These are the devices that enable the things to sense and actuate the physical and virtual world. Sensors can measure various parameters, such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, motion, or location, and convert them into electrical signals. Actuators can perform various actions, such as turning on/off a light, opening/closing a door, or playing a sound, and convert electrical signals into physical effects.
  - **RFID**: This stands for Radio Frequency IDentification, and it is a technology that enables the identification and tracking of things using radio waves. RFID tags are small and cheap devices that can be attached to things and store a unique identifier and some data. RFID readers are devices that can read the data from the tags using radio waves. RFID systems can be used for various applications, such as inventory management
