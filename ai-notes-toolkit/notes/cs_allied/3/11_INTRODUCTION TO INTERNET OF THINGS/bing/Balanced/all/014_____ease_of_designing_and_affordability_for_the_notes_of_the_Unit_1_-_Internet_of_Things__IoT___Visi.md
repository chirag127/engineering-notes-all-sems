# Ease of Designing and Affordability for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

- IoT is the network of physical objects that can communicate and interact with each other through the internet.
- IoT devices can collect, process, and exchange data with other devices, applications, and services.
- IoT devices can be classified into three categories: sensors, actuators, and gateways.
- Sensors are devices that measure physical phenomena, such as temperature, humidity, motion, etc.
- Actuators are devices that perform actions, such as switching on/off, opening/closing, etc.
- Gateways are devices that connect sensors and actuators to the internet, and provide data processing, security, and communication functions.

## Vision, Definition, Conceptual Framework, Architectural View, Technology Behind IoT, Sources of the IoT, M2M Communication, IoT Examples

- The vision of IoT is to create a smart and connected world, where physical objects can interact with each other and with humans, and provide value-added services and solutions.
- The definition of IoT varies depending on the perspective and context, but a common one is: "IoT is a global infrastructure for the information society, enabling advanced services by interconnecting (physical and virtual) things based on existing and evolving interoperable information and communication technologies" (ITU-T, 2012).
- The conceptual framework of IoT consists of four layers: device layer, network layer, service layer, and application layer.
- The device layer includes the sensors, actuators, and gateways that form the physical interface of the IoT.
- The network layer includes the communication protocols and standards that enable data transmission and exchange among the devices and the internet.
- The service layer includes the platforms and middleware that provide data management, security, and integration functions for the IoT.
- The application layer includes the software and applications that provide the end-user services and solutions based on the IoT data and functionality.
- The technology behind IoT includes various hardware, software, and communication technologies, such as microcontrollers, embedded systems, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, wireless networks, RFID, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, ZigBee, etc.
- The sources of the IoT include various domains and sectors that generate and consume IoT data, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart agriculture, smart health, smart industry, smart transportation, smart energy, etc.
- M2M communication is the direct exchange of data between machines or devices, without human intervention or intermediaries. M2M communication is a key enabler of IoT, as it allows devices to autonomously monitor, control, and optimize their operations and performance.
- IoT examples are numerous and diverse, ranging from smart thermostats, smart watches, smart speakers, smart locks, smart lights, smart meters, smart cameras, smart cars, smart drones, smart robots, smart factories, smart farms, smart hospitals, smart grids, etc.

## Design Principles for Connected Devices: IoT/M2M Systems Layers and Design Standardization, Communication Technologies, Data Enrichment and Consolidation, Ease of Designing and Affordability

- Design principles for connected devices are the guidelines and best practices that help to create effective, efficient, and user-friendly IoT devices and systems.
- Some of the common design principles for connected devices are:

  - Define the purpose and value proposition of the device or system, and align it with the user needs and expectations.
  - Choose the appropriate communication technology and protocol for the device or system, considering the factors such as data rate, range, power consumption, reliability, security, interoperability, etc.
  - Design the device or system to be scalable, modular, and adaptable, and support future upgrades and extensions.
  - Design the device or system to be secure, resilient, and trustworthy, and protect the data and privacy of the users and stakeholders.
  - Design the device or system to be user-friendly, intuitive, and accessible, and provide clear and consistent feedback and guidance to the users.
  - Design the device or system to be energy-efficient, environmentally-friendly, and sustainable, and minimize the negative impacts on the natural and social resources.
