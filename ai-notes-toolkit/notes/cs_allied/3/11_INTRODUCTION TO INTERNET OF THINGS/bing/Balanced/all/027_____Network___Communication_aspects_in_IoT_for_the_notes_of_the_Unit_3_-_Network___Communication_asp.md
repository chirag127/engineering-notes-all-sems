# Network & Communication aspects in IoT

- Network and communication aspects in IoT refer to the methods and protocols that enable IoT devices to communicate with each other, with applications, and with the cloud over the internet .
- IoT devices can use different types of communication, such as device-to-device, device-to-cloud, device-to-gateway, and device-to-application.
- IoT devices can also use different types of local communications, such as wired or wireless, short-range or long-range, and low-power or high-power.
- Some of the common wireless communication technologies for IoT devices are Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, Z-Wave, LoRaWAN, NB-IoT, and 5G .
- The choice of communication technology depends on various factors, such as data rate, range, power consumption, cost, security, and reliability .
- IoT devices also need to use application protocols that define how the information content is transported and formatted. Some of the common application protocols for IoT devices are MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, and AMQP .
- IoT devices may also need to use gateways that translate and re-transmit information, typically linking local device networks to the internet. Gateways can also provide additional functions, such as data processing, security, and device management.
- Some of the network and communication challenges in IoT are wireless medium access issues, MAC protocol survey, survey routing protocols, sensor deployment and node discovery, and data aggregation and dissemination.

## Wireless Medium Access Issues

- Wireless medium access issues refer to the problems and solutions related to the sharing of the wireless channel among multiple IoT devices.
- Wireless medium access control (MAC) protocols are responsible for coordinating the access of IoT devices to the wireless channel, avoiding collisions, and ensuring fair and efficient use of the channel.
- Wireless MAC protocols can be classified into two categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based MAC protocols allow IoT devices to compete for the channel access, using mechanisms such as carrier sense multiple access (CSMA), random access, or slotted access.
- Contention-free MAC protocols assign the channel access to IoT devices in a deterministic way, using mechanisms such as time division multiple access (TDMA), frequency division multiple access (FDMA), or code division multiple access (CDMA).
- The choice of MAC protocol depends on various factors, such as network size, traffic pattern, channel condition, energy efficiency, and latency requirement.
