## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

Bloom’s Taxonomy is a framework that helps in classifying educational goals and objectives based on the level of knowledge required to achieve them. The six levels of Bloom's Taxonomy are as follows:

1. Remembering (KL: Knowledge)
  - Recalling information from memory
  - Identifying facts, terms, and concepts
  - Listing information in a specific order
  - Describing events or processes

2. Understanding (KL: Comprehension)
  - Interpreting information in one's own words
  - Summarizing information
  - Explaining the meaning of concepts
  - Translating information from one form to another

3. Applying (KL: Application)
  - Using information in a new situation
  - Solving problems using acquired knowledge
  - Implementing a solution to a given problem
  - Demonstrating the use of a tool or technique

4. Analyzing (KL: Analysis)
  - Breaking down information into parts
  - Identifying relationships between parts
  - Comparing and contrasting information
  - Evaluating the evidence for a claim

5. Evaluating (KL: Evaluation)
  - Judging the value or quality of information
  - Assessing the validity of arguments
  - Making a decision based on evidence
  - Critiquing a work of art or literature

6. Creating (KL: Synthesis)
  - Combining information to form a new whole
  - Designing a new product or system
  - Generating new ideas or perspectives
  - Developing a solution to an unsolved problem

In conclusion, understanding the level of Bloom's Taxonomy associated with each course outcome can help students and educators better align their learning objectives, assessments, and teaching strategies. By focusing on higher levels of knowledge, students can develop critical thinking and problem-solving skills that are essential for success in their future careers.
