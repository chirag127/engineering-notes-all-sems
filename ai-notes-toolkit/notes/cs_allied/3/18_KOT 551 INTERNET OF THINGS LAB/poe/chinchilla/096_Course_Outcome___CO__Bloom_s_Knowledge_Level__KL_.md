# Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

Bloom's Taxonomy is a framework used to categorize different levels of learning objectives, from lower-order thinking skills to higher-order thinking skills. Each level in the taxonomy represents a different level of cognitive complexity that learners must master. In this article, we will discuss how course outcomes can be mapped to different levels in Bloom's Taxonomy.

## Remembering (KL: 1)

- Define key terms related to the course.
- Recall facts and information from readings and lectures.
- List key dates, events, and concepts related to the course.
- Identify basic principles and concepts.

## Understanding (KL: 2)

- Summarize key ideas and concepts from readings and lectures.
- Explain the meaning of key terms and concepts.
- Interpret data and charts related to the course.
- Compare and contrast different theories and perspectives.

## Applying (KL: 3)

- Solve problems related to the course using previously learned principles.
- Apply key concepts and theories to practical situations.
- Demonstrate the use of tools and techniques related to the course.
- Analyze and evaluate the effectiveness of different solutions to problems.

## Analyzing (KL: 4)

- Break down complex ideas and concepts into smaller parts.
- Identify patterns and relationships between different concepts and ideas.
- Compare and contrast different approaches to problem-solving.
- Analyze the effectiveness of different strategies and solutions.

## Evaluating (KL: 5)

- Assess the value and effectiveness of different ideas and concepts.
- Evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of different arguments and theories.
- Analyze the potential impact of different strategies and solutions.
- Formulate and defend an opinion based on evidence and reasoning.

## Creating (KL: 6)

- Generate new ideas and solutions to problems related to the course.
- Design and implement new tools and techniques related to the course.
- Synthesize different ideas and concepts to create new theories and perspectives.
- Construct and present a compelling argument based on evidence and reasoning.

In conclusion, mapping course outcomes to different levels in Bloom's Taxonomy can help instructors create more effective and engaging learning experiences for their students. By aligning course outcomes with specific cognitive skills, instructors can better assess their students' learning and provide targeted feedback to help them improve their understanding and mastery of the course material.
