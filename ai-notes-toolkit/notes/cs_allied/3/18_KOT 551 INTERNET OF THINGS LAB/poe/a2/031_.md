#Topic: Effects of Technology on Human Behavior

I. Increased connectivity

- Easier to stay connected with more people than ever before via social media, messaging, and cell phones.
- Always-on access to information and entertainment via the Internet.
- Bluetooth and wireless connectivity mean fewer plugged-in devices.

II. Information access

- Vast amount of information available on almost any topic at our fingertips via search engines and the Internet.
- Rise of self-publishing and user-generated content means more perspectives and voices are heard.
- Risk of 'filter bubbles' where people only see information with a particular slant.
- spread of misinformation and 'fake news' due to large volume of information available.

III. Shifting privacy and security

- More data about individuals is collected, stored, and shared than ever before via online services and devices.
- Many conveniences but loss of privacy as a tradeoff. Lack of understanding about how data may be collected or shared.
- Increased risk of hacking, identity theft, and scams as more of people's lives move online. Need for improved security measures and digital literacy.

IV. Distraction and addiction

- Concerns exist around dependency on technology leading to distraction, addiction, and less focus.
- 'Fear of missing out' and excessive social media use may negatively impact well-being and relationships.
- Need to develop healthy boundaries and habits around technology use to maximize benefits and minimize harms.

V. Access inequality

- Not all groups have equal access to or ability to use technology due to socioeconomic status, age, ability, and other factors.
- This 'digital divide' could exacerbate inequality if access to opportunities and resources becomes increasingly technology-dependent.
- More work is needed to provide access and technology literacy to disadvantaged populations to enable more equal participation in the digital world.
