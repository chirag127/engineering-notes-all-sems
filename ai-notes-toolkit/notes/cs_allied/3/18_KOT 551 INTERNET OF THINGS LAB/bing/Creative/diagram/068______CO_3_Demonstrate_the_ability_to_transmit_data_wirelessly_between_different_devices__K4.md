#### CO 3 Demonstrate the ability to transmit data wirelessly between different devices. K4

Wireless data transmission is the process of sending and receiving data without using physical wires or cables. Wireless data transmission can be classified into two main types: radio frequency (RF) and optical.

- Radio frequency (RF) transmission uses electromagnetic waves to carry data through the air, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks. RF signals are easily generated, ranging from 3 kHz to 300 GHz. RF transmission has the advantages of being able to penetrate walls and other obstacles, and being able to cover long distances. However, RF transmission also has some disadvantages, such as being susceptible to interference, noise, and security breaches, and requiring licenses for some frequency bands.
- Optical transmission uses light to send data, such as infrared, visible light, and laser. Optical transmission has the advantages of being immune to electromagnetic interference, having high bandwidth and data rates, and being secure and low-cost. However, optical transmission also has some disadvantages, such as being limited by line-of-sight, requiring direct alignment of the transmitter and receiver, and being affected by atmospheric conditions and obstacles.

To demonstrate the ability to transmit data wirelessly between different devices, one needs to understand the following concepts and skills:

- The basic principles of wireless communication, such as modulation, demodulation, encoding, decoding, multiplexing, and channel access methods.
- The characteristics and applications of different wireless transmission media, such as radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, and laser .
- The standards and protocols of different wireless networks, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, and satellite.
- The devices and components involved in wireless data transmission, such as wireless adapters, repeaters, routers, antennas, and transceivers .
- The advantages and disadvantages of wireless data transmission compared to wired data transmission, such as mobility, flexibility, scalability, reliability, security, and cost.
- The challenges and issues of wireless data transmission, such as interference, noise, attenuation, fading, multipath, shadowing, and security threats.
- The methods and tools for testing, troubleshooting, and optimizing wireless data transmission, such as signal strength, signal quality, throughput, latency, and error rate.
