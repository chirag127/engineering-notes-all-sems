# KOT 552 INTERNET OF THINGS LAB KCS

## Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that can communicate and interact with each other through the internet. IoT devices can collect, process, and share data, as well as perform actions based on predefined rules or commands. IoT devices can range from simple sensors and actuators to complex systems such as smart homes, smart cities, and smart factories. IoT devices can also be integrated with other technologies such as cloud computing, big data, artificial intelligence, and machine learning to enable new applications and services.

## Objectives

The main objectives of this lab course are:

- To understand the basic concepts and principles of IoT and its applications.
- To learn how to design, develop, and deploy IoT devices and systems using various hardware and software platforms and tools.
- To gain hands-on experience in working with different IoT protocols, standards, and architectures.
- To explore the challenges and opportunities of IoT in various domains such as healthcare, agriculture, transportation, and education.

## Course Outline

The course consists of the following topics and experiments:

- Introduction to IoT: Definition, characteristics, components, architecture, and applications of IoT. 
- IoT Hardware Platforms: Overview of various IoT hardware platforms such as Arduino, Raspberry Pi, ESP32, and NodeMCU. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 
- IoT Software Platforms: Overview of various IoT software platforms such as Arduino IDE, Raspberry Pi OS, Espressif IoT Development Framework, and Node-RED. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 
- IoT Protocols and Standards: Overview of various IoT protocols and standards such as MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, WebSocket, Bluetooth, Zigbee, and LoRaWAN. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 
- IoT Communication and Networking: Overview of various IoT communication and networking technologies such as Wi-Fi, Ethernet, Cellular, and LPWAN. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 
- IoT Cloud Services: Overview of various IoT cloud services such as AWS IoT, Google Cloud IoT, Microsoft Azure IoT, and IBM Watson IoT. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 
- IoT Data Analytics: Overview of various IoT data analytics techniques such as data preprocessing, data visualization, data mining, and machine learning. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 
- IoT Security and Privacy: Overview of various IoT security and privacy issues such as authentication, encryption, access control, and data protection. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 
- IoT Applications and Case Studies: Overview of various IoT applications and case studies in different domains such as healthcare, agriculture, transportation, and education. Comparison of their features, capabilities, and limitations. 

## Experiments

The experiments are designed to provide hands-on experience in working with different IoT hardware and software platforms, tools, protocols, standards, and architectures. The experiments are as follows:

- Experiment 1: Setting up and testing IoT hardware platforms such as Arduino, Raspberry Pi, ESP32, and NodeMCU. 
- Experiment 2: Setting up and testing IoT software platforms such as Arduino IDE, Raspberry Pi OS, Espressif IoT Development Framework, and Node-RED. 
- Experiment 3: Setting up and testing IoT communication and networking technologies such as Wi-Fi, Ethernet, Cellular, and LPWAN. 
- Experiment 4: Setting up and testing IoT protocols and standards such as MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, WebSocket, Bluetooth, Zigbee, and LoRaWAN. 
- Experiment 5: Setting up and testing IoT cloud services such as AWS IoT, Google Cloud IoT, Microsoft Azure IoT, and IBM Watson IoT. 
- Experiment 6: Setting up and testing IoT data analytics techniques such as data preprocessing, data visualization, data mining, and machine learning. 
- Experiment 7: Setting up and testing IoT security and privacy mechanisms such as authentication, encryption, access control, and data protection. 
- Experiment 8: Developing and deploying IoT applications and case studies in different domains such as healthcare, agriculture, transportation, and education.
