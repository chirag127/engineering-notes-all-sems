## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)

- A course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what a learner should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course or a unit of instruction.
- A course outcome should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART).
- A course outcome should align with the course objectives, the program outcomes, and the institutional mission and vision.
- A course outcome should also reflect the level of cognitive complexity or rigor that is expected from the learner.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a framework that classifies different levels of cognitive skills or knowledge, from lower-order to higher-order thinking.
- Bloom's taxonomy consists of six levels: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Bloom's knowledge level (KL) is a term that indicates the level of Bloom's taxonomy that corresponds to a course outcome.
- For example, a course outcome that states "The learner will be able to recall the main concepts of thermodynamics" has a KL of remember, which is the lowest level of Bloom's taxonomy.
- A course outcome that states "The learner will be able to design a heat engine that maximizes efficiency and minimizes environmental impact" has a KL of create, which is the highest level of Bloom's taxonomy.
- The KL of a course outcome can be determined by using verbs that indicate the level of cognitive skill or knowledge required, such as list, define, explain, compare, solve, critique, or synthesize.
- The KL of a course outcome can also be influenced by the context, the content, and the criteria of the outcome, such as the type of problem, the domain of knowledge, or the standards of performance.
- The KL of a course outcome should match the level of instruction, assessment, and feedback that is provided to the learner.
- The KL of a course outcome should also be appropriate for the level of the course, the program, and the discipline.
- The KL of a course outcome should be varied and balanced across the course and the program, to ensure that the learner develops a range of cognitive skills and knowledge.
