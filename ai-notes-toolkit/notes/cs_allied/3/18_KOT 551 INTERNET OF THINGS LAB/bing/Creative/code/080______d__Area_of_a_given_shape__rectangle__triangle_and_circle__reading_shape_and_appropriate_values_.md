#### d) Area of a given shape (rectangle, triangle and circle) reading shape and appropriate values from standard

The area of a given shape is the amount of space enclosed by the shape. Different shapes have different formulas for calculating their areas. To find the area of a given shape, we need to read the shape and the appropriate values from the standard input.

- **Rectangle**: A rectangle is a four-sided shape with opposite sides parallel and equal. The area of a rectangle is given by the formula:

  `Area = length * width`

  where length and width are the dimensions of the rectangle. To find the area of a rectangle, we need to read the length and width from the standard input and multiply them.

  For example, if the length is 10 units and the width is 5 units, then the area is:

  `Area = 10 * 5 = 50 square units`

- **Triangle**: A triangle is a three-sided shape with three angles. The area of a triangle is given by the formula:

  `Area = (base * height) / 2`

  where base and height are the dimensions of the triangle. To find the area of a triangle, we need to read the base and height from the standard input and multiply them by half.

  For example, if the base is 8 units and the height is 6 units, then the area is:

  `Area = (8 * 6) / 2 = 24 square units`

- **Circle**: A circle is a shape with all points equidistant from a fixed point called the center. The area of a circle is given by the formula:

  `Area = pi * radius * radius`

  where pi is a constant value approximately equal to 3.14 and radius is the distance from the center to any point on the circle. To find the area of a circle, we need to read the radius from the standard input and multiply it by itself and by pi.

  For example, if the radius is 4 units, then the area is:

  `Area = 3.14 * 4 * 4 = 50.24 square units`
