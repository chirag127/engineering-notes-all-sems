#### CO 3 Demonstrate the ability to transmit data wirelessly between different devices. K4

Wireless data transmission is the process of sending and receiving data without using physical wires or cables. Wireless data transmission can be classified into two main types: radio frequency (RF) and optical.

- Radio frequency (RF) transmission uses electromagnetic waves to carry data through the air, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks. RF signals are easily generated, ranging from 3 kHz to 300 GHz. RF transmission has the advantages of being able to penetrate walls and other obstacles, and being able to cover long distances. However, RF transmission also has some disadvantages, such as being susceptible to interference, noise, and security breaches, and requiring licenses for some frequency bands.
- Optical transmission uses light to send data, such as infrared, visible light, and laser. Optical transmission has the advantages of being immune to electromagnetic interference, having high bandwidth and low power consumption, and being secure and license-free. However, optical transmission also has some disadvantages, such as being limited by line-of-sight, being affected by atmospheric conditions, and requiring precise alignment and pointing of the transmitters and receivers.

To demonstrate the ability to transmit data wirelessly between different devices, one should be able to:

- Understand the basic principles and characteristics of wireless transmission media, such as frequency, wavelength, modulation, encoding, and multiplexing.
- Compare and contrast the advantages and disadvantages of different types of wireless transmission media, such as RF and optical .
- Identify and describe the common wireless transmission standards and protocols, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, NFC, RFID, and Li-Fi .
- Select and apply the appropriate wireless transmission media and methods for different scenarios and applications, such as home, office, industrial, medical, and military .
- Configure and test the wireless transmission devices and systems, such as routers, access points, adapters, repeaters, and antennas .
- Troubleshoot and solve the common wireless transmission problems and issues, such as interference, noise, attenuation, reflection, refraction, diffraction, and security .
