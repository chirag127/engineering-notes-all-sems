# Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

Course Outcome (CO) refers to the expected knowledge, skills, and attitudes that students should possess upon completion of a specific course. COs are derived from the course objectives and are used to assess the effectiveness of the course in achieving its intended goals.

Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL) is a classification system used to categorize the cognitive skills and abilities required for learning. The system was developed by Benjamin Bloom and his colleagues in the 1950s and is commonly used in education to design curricula, assessments, and instructional strategies.

The Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL) is divided into six levels, each representing a different type of cognitive skill:

1. **Remembering**: The ability to recall previously learned information.
2. **Understanding**: The ability to comprehend the meaning of the information.
3. **Applying**: The ability to use the information in a new situation.
4. **Analyzing**: The ability to break down complex information into its component parts.
5. **Evaluating**: The ability to make judgments about the value of the information.
6. **Creating**: The ability to combine information to form a new whole.

COs can be mapped to the different levels of Bloom’s KL to ensure that the course is designed to develop the appropriate cognitive skills in students. For example, a CO that requires students to apply a concept in a new situation would be mapped to the Applying level of Bloom’s KL.
