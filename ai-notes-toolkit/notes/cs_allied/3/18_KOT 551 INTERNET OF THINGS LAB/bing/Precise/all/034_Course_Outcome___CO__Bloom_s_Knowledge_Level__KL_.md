# Course Outcome (CO) and Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

Course Outcome (CO) refers to the expected knowledge, skills, and abilities that a student should possess upon completion of a specific course. These outcomes are used to assess the effectiveness of the course and to ensure that the course content aligns with the overall program goals.

Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL) is a framework for categorizing educational goals according to the level of cognitive complexity. The framework consists of six levels, ranging from simple recall of information to higher-order thinking skills such as analysis and evaluation. These levels are:

1. **Remembering:** The ability to recall previously learned information.
2. **Understanding:** The ability to comprehend the meaning of the material.
3. **Applying:** The ability to use learned material in new and concrete situations.
4. **Analyzing:** The ability to break down material into its component parts and understand its organizational structure.
5. **Evaluating:** The ability to make judgments about the value of material for a given purpose.
6. **Creating:** The ability to put parts together to form a new whole.

Course outcomes can be mapped to Bloom’s Knowledge Levels to ensure that the course content is appropriately challenging and promotes higher-order thinking skills. For example, a course outcome that states “Students will be able to analyze and evaluate arguments” would be mapped to the Analyzing and Evaluating levels of Bloom’s taxonomy.
