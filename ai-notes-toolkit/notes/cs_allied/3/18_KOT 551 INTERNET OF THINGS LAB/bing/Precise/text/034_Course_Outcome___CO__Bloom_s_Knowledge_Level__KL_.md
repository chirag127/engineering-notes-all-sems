# Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

Course Outcome (CO) refers to the expected knowledge, skills, and abilities that a student should possess upon completion of a specific course. COs are used to evaluate the effectiveness of a course and to ensure that the course content aligns with the overall program objectives.

Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL) is a classification system used to categorize the cognitive skills and abilities required for learning. The system was developed by Benjamin Bloom and his colleagues in the 1950s and is commonly used in education to design learning objectives and assessments.

The six levels of Bloom’s Knowledge Level are:

1. **Remembering**: The ability to recall previously learned information.
2. **Understanding**: The ability to comprehend the meaning of the information.
3. **Applying**: The ability to use the information in a new situation.
4. **Analyzing**: The ability to break down the information into parts and understand their relationships.
5. **Evaluating**: The ability to make judgments about the value of the information.
6. **Creating**: The ability to use the information to create something new.

COs can be mapped to Bloom’s Knowledge Levels to ensure that the course content and assessments are appropriately challenging and that students are developing the cognitive skills necessary to succeed in the course and in their future academic and professional pursuits.
