# CO 1 Understand the concept of Internet of Things K3

- The concept of Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of physical objects (or things) with sensors, processing ability, software and other technologies that enable them to communicate and exchange data with other devices and systems over the Internet or other networks .
- The term IoT was coined by Kevin Ashton in 1999, who defined it as "a system where the Internet is connected to the physical world via ubiquitous sensors" .
- The main components of IoT are:
  - Things: These are the physical objects that have embedded sensors, actuators, processors, memory and communication modules. They can sense, measure, control, store and transmit data. Examples of things are smart phones, wearables, smart home appliances, smart vehicles, industrial machines, etc.
  - Networks: These are the communication channels that connect the things to each other and to the Internet. They can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, radio frequency identification (RFID), near field communication (NFC), etc.
  - Platforms: These are the software systems that provide the services and applications for the IoT. They can include cloud computing, edge computing, fog computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, machine learning, etc. They can enable data collection, processing, storage, visualization, sharing, security, privacy, etc.
  - Users: These are the human or machine entities that interact with the IoT. They can use the IoT for various purposes, such as monitoring, control, automation, optimization, decision making, etc. They can access the IoT through various interfaces, such as web browsers, mobile apps, voice assistants, etc.
- The benefits of IoT are:
  - It can improve the efficiency, productivity, quality and safety of various processes and systems, such as manufacturing, transportation, energy, health care, agriculture, etc.
  - It can enhance the convenience, comfort, security and well-being of the users, such as smart home, smart city, smart health, smart education, etc.
  - It can create new opportunities, innovations and business models, such as smart services, smart products, smart markets, etc.
- The challenges of IoT are:
  - It can pose technical issues, such as scalability, interoperability, reliability, latency, bandwidth, power consumption, etc.
  - It can raise security and privacy risks, such as data breaches, cyberattacks, unauthorized access, identity theft, etc.
  - It can have social and ethical implications, such as data ownership, consent, transparency, accountability, trust, etc.
