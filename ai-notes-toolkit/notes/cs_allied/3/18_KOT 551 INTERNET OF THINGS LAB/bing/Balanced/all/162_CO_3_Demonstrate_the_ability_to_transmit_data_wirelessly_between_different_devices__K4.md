# CO 3 Demonstrate the ability to transmit data wirelessly between different devices. K4

- Wireless data transmission is the process of sending and receiving information without using physical wires or cables.
- Wireless data transmission can be achieved using various technologies, such as radio waves, infrared, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular networks, satellite, etc.
- Wireless data transmission has many advantages, such as mobility, convenience, scalability, cost-effectiveness, etc.
- Wireless data transmission also has some challenges, such as security, interference, range, reliability, power consumption, etc.
- To transmit data wirelessly between different devices, the following steps are required:

  - The sender device encodes the data into a suitable format, such as binary, text, audio, video, etc.
  - The sender device modulates the data onto a carrier signal, such as a radio wave, that has a specific frequency, amplitude, and phase.
  - The sender device transmits the modulated signal through an antenna or a transmitter, which converts the electrical signal into an electromagnetic wave.
  - The receiver device receives the electromagnetic wave through an antenna or a receiver, which converts the wave back into an electrical signal.
  - The receiver device demodulates the signal, which means separating the data from the carrier signal.
  - The receiver device decodes the data into the original format, such as binary, text, audio, video, etc.

- To ensure the quality and security of wireless data transmission, the following factors should be considered:

  - The bandwidth of the wireless channel, which determines the amount of data that can be transmitted per unit time.
  - The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the wireless channel, which measures the strength of the signal relative to the background noise.
  - The error correction and detection techniques, which can identify and correct the errors that occur during wireless data transmission, such as parity bits, checksums, cyclic redundancy checks (CRC), etc.
  - The encryption and decryption techniques, which can protect the data from unauthorized access or modification, such as symmetric-key encryption, public-key encryption, digital signatures, etc.
  - The authentication and authorization techniques, which can verify the identity and permissions of the sender and receiver devices, such as passwords, biometrics, certificates, etc.
