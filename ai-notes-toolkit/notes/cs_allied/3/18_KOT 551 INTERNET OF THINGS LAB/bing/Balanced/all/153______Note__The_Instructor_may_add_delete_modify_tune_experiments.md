# Note: The Instructor may add/delete/modify/tune experiments

- This is a statement that indicates that the instructor of a course or a lab has the authority and the flexibility to change the experiments that are part of the curriculum.
- This statement may be written in the syllabus, the lab manual, or the course website to inform the students about the possible changes in the experiments.
- The reasons for adding, deleting, modifying, or tuning experiments may vary depending on the instructor's objectives, preferences, resources, availability, feedback, or other factors.
- Some examples of adding, deleting, modifying, or tuning experiments are:

  - Adding a new experiment to introduce a new concept, technique, or tool that is relevant to the course or the lab.
  - Deleting an experiment that is outdated, redundant, or irrelevant to the course or the lab.
  - Modifying an experiment to update the instructions, the data, the methods, or the outcomes to reflect the current standards, practices, or expectations.
  - Tuning an experiment to adjust the difficulty, the duration, the scope, or the assessment to suit the level, the pace, or the goals of the students.

- The statement also implies that the students should be prepared for the changes in the experiments and should follow the instructor's guidance and instructions accordingly.
