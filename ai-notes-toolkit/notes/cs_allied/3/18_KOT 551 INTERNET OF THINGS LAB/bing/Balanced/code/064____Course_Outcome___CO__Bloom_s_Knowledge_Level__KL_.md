## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)

- Course Outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what a learner should be able to do at the end of a course or a unit of instruction.
- Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL) is a classification of the cognitive skills that learners need to demonstrate in order to achieve a CO.
- Bloom's KL consists of six levels: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, and Create. Each level represents a higher order of thinking and requires more complex cognitive processes than the previous one.
- The following table shows the definitions and examples of each level of Bloom's KL:

| Level | Definition | Example |
| ----- | ---------- | ------- |
| Remember | Recall facts, terms, concepts, or procedures from memory | List the steps of photosynthesis |
| Understand | Explain the meaning, interpretation, or summary of information | Describe the main idea of a story |
| Apply | Use information or skills in a new situation or context | Solve a math problem using a formula |
| Analyze | Break down information into parts and examine the relationships among them | Compare and contrast two characters in a novel |
| Evaluate | Make judgments or decisions based on criteria or standards | Assess the quality of a research paper |
| Create | Generate, plan, or produce something new or original | Design a poster for a social campaign |

- To write a CO, one should use an action verb that corresponds to the desired level of Bloom's KL, followed by the content or topic of the course or unit, and the conditions or criteria for performance. For example:

  - CO1: Apply the principles of object-oriented programming to design and implement a software project. (Apply level)
  - CO2: Analyze the ethical and social implications of artificial intelligence. (Analyze level)
  - CO3: Create a portfolio of digital artworks using various tools and techniques. (Create level)

- To assess the achievement of a CO, one should use appropriate assessment methods and tools that match the level of Bloom's KL. For example:

  - CO1: Project report, code review, presentation, or demonstration. (Apply level)
  - CO2: Essay, case study, debate, or quiz. (Analyze level)
  - CO3: Portfolio, exhibition, critique, or peer feedback. (Create level)
