## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)

- Course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what students are expected to know, understand, or be able to do at the end of a course.
- Bloom's knowledge level (KL) is a classification of the cognitive domain of learning objectives, based on the level of complexity and specificity of the knowledge required.
- Bloom's knowledge level (KL) consists of six categories: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create. Each category is further divided into subcategories.
- The table below shows the relationship between course outcome (CO) and Bloom's knowledge level (KL), with examples of verbs that can be used to write learning objectives for each category.

| CO | KL | Verbs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CO1 | Remember | Define, list, recall, identify, recognize, label, name, state, etc. |
| CO2 | Understand | Explain, describe, summarize, paraphrase, illustrate, interpret, classify, compare, etc. |
| CO3 | Apply | Use, apply, demonstrate, solve, calculate, perform, implement, etc. |
| CO4 | Analyze | Analyze, compare, contrast, differentiate, examine, relate, organize, etc. |
| CO5 | Evaluate | Evaluate, judge, critique, assess, justify, argue, appraise, etc. |
| CO6 | Create | Create, design, construct, produce, synthesize, compose, invent, etc. |

- For example, a course outcome for a mathematics course could be: CO1: Remember the basic concepts and formulas of calculus. CO2: Understand the applications and limitations of calculus. CO3: Apply calculus techniques to solve problems. CO4: Analyze the graphs and functions of calculus. CO5: Evaluate the accuracy and validity of calculus solutions. CO6: Create new problems and methods using calculus.
