#### d) Area of a given shape (rectangle, triangle and circle) reading shape and appropriate values from standard

- The area of a shape is the amount of space that it covers.
- Different shapes have different formulas for calculating their areas.
- To find the area of a given shape, we need to read the shape name and the appropriate values from the standard input, such as the length, width, base, height, or radius of the shape.
- Here are some common formulas for the area of a rectangle, triangle and circle:

  - Rectangle: A = lw, where l is the length and w is the width of the rectangle.
  - Triangle: A = (1/2)bh, where b is the base and h is the height of the triangle.
  - Circle: A = pi*r^2, where pi is approximately 3.14 and r is the radius of the circle.

- For example, if we read the shape name as "rectangle" and the length as 10 and the width as 5 from the standard input, we can find the area of the rectangle by multiplying 10 and 5, which gives us 50 square units.
- Similarly, if we read the shape name as "triangle" and the base as 8 and the height as 6 from the standard input, we can find the area of the triangle by multiplying 8 and 6 and dividing by 2, which gives us 24 square units.
- Likewise, if we read the shape name as "circle" and the radius as 4 from the standard input, we can find the area of the circle by multiplying 3.14 and 4^2, which gives us 50.24 square units.
