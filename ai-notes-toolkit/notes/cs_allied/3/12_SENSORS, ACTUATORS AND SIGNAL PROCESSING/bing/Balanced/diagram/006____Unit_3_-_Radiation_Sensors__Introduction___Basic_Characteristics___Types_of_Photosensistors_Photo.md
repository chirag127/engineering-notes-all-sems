## Unit 3 - Radiation Sensors

Radiation sensors are devices that can detect and measure different types of radiation, such as light, X-rays, gamma rays, neutrons, etc. Radiation sensors have various applications in fields such as astronomy, medicine, nuclear power, security, and communication.

### Basic Characteristics

Some of the basic characteristics of radiation sensors are:

- Sensitivity: The ratio of the output signal to the input radiation power or intensity.
- Responsivity: The ratio of the output signal to the input radiation energy or photon flux.
- Linearity: The degree to which the output signal is proportional to the input radiation over a certain range.
- Noise: The unwanted fluctuations or variations in the output signal that affect the accuracy and resolution of the sensor.
- Dynamic range: The ratio of the maximum to the minimum detectable input radiation.
- Bandwidth: The range of frequencies or wavelengths that the sensor can respond to.
- Speed: The time required for the sensor to respond to a change in the input radiation.

### Types of Photosensistors/Photo detectors

Photosensistors or photo detectors are radiation sensors that can convert light into electrical signals. There are different types of photosensistors or photo detectors, such as:

- Photodiodes: Semiconductor devices that generate a current or voltage when exposed to light. Photodiodes can be classified into p-n junction, p-i-n, avalanche, and Schottky photodiodes, depending on their structure and operation.
- Phototransistors: Semiconductor devices that amplify the current generated by a photodiode. Phototransistors have a base, a collector, and an emitter, similar to a normal transistor, but the base is exposed to light.
- Photoresistors: Resistive devices that change their resistance when exposed to light. Photoresistors are made of materials such as cadmium sulfide (CdS), lead sulfide (PbS), or selenium (Se) that have high resistance in the dark and low resistance in the light.
- Photovoltaic cells: Semiconductor devices that generate a voltage when exposed to light. Photovoltaic cells are composed of multiple p-n junctions that form a solar cell. Photovoltaic cells can be used to convert solar energy into electrical energy.

### X-ray and Nuclear Radiation Sensors

X-ray and nuclear radiation sensors are devices that can detect and measure high-energy radiation, such as X-rays, gamma rays, alpha particles, beta particles, or neutrons. X-ray and nuclear radiation sensors have various applications in fields such as medicine, industry, security, and research. Some of the common types of X-ray and nuclear radiation sensors are:

- Scintillation detectors: Devices that use a scintillator material that emits light when struck by high-energy radiation. The light is then converted into electrical signals by a photomultiplier tube or a photodiode. Scintillation detectors can be used to measure the energy and intensity of the radiation.
- Gas-filled detectors: Devices that use a gas-filled chamber that ionizes when high-energy radiation passes through it. The ionized gas then produces a current or voltage that is proportional to the radiation intensity. Gas-filled detectors can be classified into ionization chambers, proportional counters, and Geiger-Muller tubes, depending on their operation and gas pressure.
- Semiconductor detectors: Devices that use a semiconductor material that generates electron-hole pairs when high-energy radiation interacts with it. The electron-hole pairs then create a current or voltage that is proportional to the radiation energy. Semiconductor detectors can be made of materials such as silicon (Si), germanium (Ge), or cadmium telluride (CdTe).
