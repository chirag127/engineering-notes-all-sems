### Mechanical Actuation Systems

- Mechanical actuation systems are used to move or change the position of an object using energy, usually from a motor.
- Mechanical actuation systems can be classified into two types: linear and rotary.
- Linear actuation systems produce linear (back-and-forth) motion, such as pushing or pulling an object.
- Rotary actuation systems produce rotational (circular) motion, such as turning or spinning an object.
- Mechanical actuation systems can be composed of various components, such as:

  - Types of motion: the way an object moves, such as translation, rotation, oscillation, etc.
  - Kinematic chains: the arrangement of rigid bodies connected by joints, such as links, hinges, sliders, etc.
  - Cams: mechanical devices that convert rotary motion into linear or oscillating motion, such as lobes, followers, springs, etc.
  - Gears: mechanical devices that transmit rotary motion and torque between shafts, such as spur, helical, bevel, worm, etc.
  - Ratchet and pawl: mechanical devices that allow rotary motion in one direction only, such as toothed wheels, levers, springs, etc.
  - Belt and chain drives: mechanical devices that transmit rotary motion and torque between pulleys or sprockets, such as belts, chains, tensioners, etc.
  - Bearings: mechanical devices that reduce friction and wear between moving parts, such as ball, roller, plain, etc.
  - Mechanical aspects of motor selection: the factors that affect the choice of a motor for a mechanical actuation system, such as power, speed, torque, efficiency, size, weight, etc.

- Electrical Actuation Systems: actuation systems that use electricity to power and control the movement of an object, such as:

  - Electrical systems: the components and circuits that provide and regulate the electrical energy for the actuation system, such as power sources, converters, controllers, sensors, etc.
  - Mechanical switches: devices that open or close an electrical circuit by physical contact, such as push buttons, toggle switches, relays, etc.
  - Solid-state switches: devices that open or close an electrical circuit by electronic means, such as transistors, diodes, thyristors, etc.
  - Solenoids: devices that convert electrical energy into linear motion by using a coil of wire and a magnetic core, such as plungers, valves, relays, etc.
  - D.C. Motors: devices that convert electrical energy into rotary motion by using a coil of wire and a permanent magnet, such as brushed, brushless, servo, etc.
  - A.C. Motors: devices that convert electrical energy into rotary motion by using a coil of wire and an alternating magnetic field, such as induction, synchronous, stepper, etc.
  - Stepper motors: devices that convert electrical energy into discrete rotary motion by using a coil of wire and a permanent magnet, such as variable reluctance, permanent magnet, hybrid, etc.
