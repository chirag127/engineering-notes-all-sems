- Magnetic sensors are devices that convert the magnitude and variations of a magnetic field into electric signals.
- Magnetic sensors can be used to detect and measure the distance, speed, rotation, angle, and position of an object by converting magnetic information into electrical signals.
- Magnetic sensors can also be used to monitor the current flowing through a conductor by measuring the magnetic field generated by the current.
- Magnetic sensors can be classified into different types based on the principle of operation, the type of magnetic field, the output signal, and the application.
- Some of the common types of magnetic sensors are:

  - Magneto-resistive sensors: These sensors use the property of certain materials to change their electrical resistance when exposed to a magnetic field. There are two types of magneto-resistive sensors: anisotropic magneto-resistive (AMR) sensors and giant magneto-resistive (GMR) sensors.
    - Anisotropic magneto-resistive (AMR) sensors: These sensors use thin films of ferromagnetic materials, such as nickel-iron alloys, that have a preferred direction of magnetization. When a magnetic field is applied perpendicular to this direction, the resistance of the film increases. The change in resistance is proportional to the sine of the angle between the magnetic field and the preferred direction. AMR sensors can be used to measure the direction and strength of a magnetic field, as well as the position and speed of a magnetic object.
    - Giant magneto-resistive (GMR) sensors: These sensors use multilayer structures of ferromagnetic and non-magnetic materials, such as iron and copper, that exhibit a large change in resistance when exposed to a magnetic field. The resistance change is due to the alignment or misalignment of the magnetization of the ferromagnetic layers. GMR sensors can be used to measure the strength and direction of a magnetic field, as well as the position and speed of a magnetic object. GMR sensors have higher sensitivity and lower power consumption than AMR sensors.
  - Semiconductor magneto-resistors: These sensors use the property of certain semiconductors, such as silicon, germanium, or gallium arsenide, to change their electrical resistance when exposed to a magnetic field. The resistance change is due to the deflection of the charge carriers by the Lorentz force. Semiconductor magneto-resistors can be used to measure the strength and direction of a magnetic field, as well as the position and speed of a magnetic object. Semiconductor magneto-resistors have higher sensitivity and lower power consumption than magneto-resistive sensors, but they require higher operating voltages and temperatures.
  - Hall effect sensors: These sensors use the property of certain materials to generate a voltage across their width when a current flows through them and a magnetic field is applied perpendicular to the current. The voltage is proportional to the product of the current and the magnetic field. Hall effect sensors can be used to measure the strength and direction of a magnetic field, as well as the current flowing through a conductor. Hall effect sensors have lower sensitivity and higher power consumption than magneto-resistive sensors, but they have a linear output and can operate at higher frequencies.
  - Inductance and eddy current sensors: These sensors use the property of a coil of wire to generate an electromotive force (EMF) when the magnetic flux through the coil changes. The EMF is proportional to the rate of change of the magnetic flux. Inductance and eddy current sensors can be used to measure the distance, speed, rotation, and angle of a magnetic object. Inductance sensors use a permanent magnet or an alternating current (AC) source to generate a magnetic field, while eddy current sensors use an AC source to induce eddy currents in a conductive target.
