### Electro Analytical Sensors

- Electro analytical sensors are devices that measure the concentration or activity of an analyte in a solution by using an electrochemical reaction as the sensing principle .
- Electro analytical sensors can be classified into three main types based on the measurement technique: amperometric, potentiometric, and conductometric.
- Amperometric sensors measure the current generated by the oxidation or reduction of the analyte at an electrode surface. The current is proportional to the analyte concentration and can be detected by a potentiostat or a galvanostat circuit.
- Potentiometric sensors measure the potential difference between two electrodes in contact with the solution. The potential is related to the activity of the analyte by the Nernst equation and can be detected by a high-impedance voltmeter or a potentiometer circuit.
- Conductometric sensors measure the electrical conductivity or resistance of the solution, which depends on the concentration and mobility of the ions present. The conductivity or resistance can be detected by an alternating current (AC) bridge or a Wheatstone bridge circuit.

### The Electrochemical Cell

- An electrochemical cell is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy or vice versa by using redox reactions.
- An electrochemical cell consists of two electrodes (anode and cathode) immersed in an electrolyte solution and connected by an external circuit. The electrodes are usually made of metals or other conductive materials.
- An electrochemical cell can be classified into two main types based on the direction of the current flow: galvanic or voltaic cells and electrolytic cells.
- Galvanic or voltaic cells produce electrical energy from spontaneous redox reactions. The anode is the electrode where oxidation occurs and the cathode is the electrode where reduction occurs. The electrons flow from the anode to the cathode through the external circuit, generating a positive cell potential.
- Electrolytic cells consume electrical energy to drive non-spontaneous redox reactions. The anode is the electrode where reduction occurs and the cathode is the electrode where oxidation occurs. The electrons flow from the cathode to the anode through the external circuit, generating a negative cell potential.

### The Cell Potential

- The cell potential or electromotive force (EMF) of an electrochemical cell is the difference in electrical potential between the two electrodes. It is a measure of the driving force for the redox reaction and the amount of electrical energy that can be obtained or consumed by the cell.
- The cell potential can be calculated by using the standard electrode potentials of the two electrodes, which are the potentials of the electrodes when they are in equilibrium with their respective ions in a 1 M solution at 25°C and 1 atm.
- The standard cell potential (E°cell) is the cell potential when the cell operates under standard conditions. It can be calculated by using the following equation:

E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode

- The actual cell potential (Ecell) is the cell potential when the cell operates under non-standard conditions. It can be calculated by using the following equation, which is derived from the Nernst equation:

Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF) ln Q

where R is the universal gas constant, T is the absolute temperature, n is the number of electrons transferred in the redox reaction, F is the Faraday constant, and Q is the reaction quotient.

### Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

- The standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) is a reference electrode that is used to measure the standard electrode potentials of other electrodes. It is defined as a platinum electrode in contact with a 1 M H+ solution and a hydrogen gas at 1 atm.
- The standard hydrogen electrode has a standard electrode potential of 0 V by convention. It is the basis for the electrochemical series, which is a list of electrodes arranged in order of their standard electrode potentials.
- The standard hydrogen electrode is rarely used in practice because it is difficult to maintain and operate. Instead, other reference electrodes, such as the saturated calomel electrode (SCE) or the silver-silver chloride electrode (Ag/AgCl), are used to measure the electrode potentials of other electrodes.
