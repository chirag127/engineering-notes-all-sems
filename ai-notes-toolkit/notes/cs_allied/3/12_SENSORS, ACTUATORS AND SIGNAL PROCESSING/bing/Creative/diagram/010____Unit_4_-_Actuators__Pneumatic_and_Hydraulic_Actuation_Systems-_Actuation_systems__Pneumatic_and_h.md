## Unit 4 - Actuators: Pneumatic and Hydraulic Actuation Systems

- Actuation systems are devices that convert energy into mechanical motion to perform work on a load.
- Pneumatic and hydraulic systems are two types of actuation systems that use fluid power to generate force and movement.
- Directional control valves are components that control the direction of fluid flow in a pneumatic or hydraulic system.
- Pressure control valves are components that regulate the pressure of fluid in a pneumatic or hydraulic system.
- Cylinders are linear actuators that use fluid pressure to move a piston in a straight line.
- Servo and proportional control valves are valves that provide precise and variable control of fluid flow and pressure in a pneumatic or hydraulic system.
- Process control valves are valves that regulate the flow of fluid in industrial processes such as chemical, oil and gas, and power generation.
- Rotary actuators are actuators that use fluid pressure to rotate a shaft or a disc.

### Pneumatic Actuation Systems

- Pneumatic actuation systems use compressed air as the working fluid to generate force and movement.
- Pneumatic systems are simple, fast, reliable, and safe to operate.
- Pneumatic systems are suitable for applications that require low to medium force, high speed, and frequent cycling.
- Pneumatic systems are energy inefficient, noisy, and sensitive to air quality and moisture.

### Hydraulic Actuation Systems

- Hydraulic actuation systems use pressurized liquid, usually oil, as the working fluid to generate force and movement.
- Hydraulic systems are powerful, precise, smooth, and adaptable to various loads and conditions.
- Hydraulic systems are suitable for applications that require high force, low speed, and long stroke.
- Hydraulic systems are complex, expensive, heavy, and prone to leakage and contamination.
