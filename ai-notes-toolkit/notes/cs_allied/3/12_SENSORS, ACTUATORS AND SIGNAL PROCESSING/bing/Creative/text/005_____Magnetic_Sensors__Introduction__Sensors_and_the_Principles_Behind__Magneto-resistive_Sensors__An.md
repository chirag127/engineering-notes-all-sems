- Magnetic sensors are devices that convert the magnitude and variations of a magnetic field into electric signals .
- Magnetic sensors can be used to detect and measure the distance, speed, rotation, angle, and position of an object by converting magnetic information into electrical signals.
- Magnetic sensors can also be used to monitor the current flowing through a conductor by measuring the magnetic field generated by the current .
- Magnetic sensors can be classified into different types based on the principle of operation, the type of magnetic material, the output signal, and the application .
- Some of the common types of magnetic sensors are:

  - Magneto-resistive sensors: These sensors use the property of some materials to change their electrical resistance when subjected to a magnetic field . There are two types of magneto-resistive sensors: anisotropic magneto-resistive (AMR) sensors and giant magneto-resistive (GMR) sensors .
    - Anisotropic magneto-resistive (AMR) sensors: These sensors use thin films of ferromagnetic materials, such as nickel-iron alloys, that have a preferred direction of magnetization . When a magnetic field is applied perpendicular to this direction, the resistance of the film increases . The change in resistance is proportional to the sine of the angle between the magnetic field and the preferred direction . AMR sensors can be used to measure the direction and magnitude of a magnetic field, as well as the position, speed, and rotation of an object .
    - Giant magneto-resistive (GMR) sensors: These sensors use multilayer structures of ferromagnetic and non-magnetic materials, such as iron and copper, that have a large change in resistance when exposed to a magnetic field . The resistance change is due to the alignment or misalignment of the electron spins in the ferromagnetic layers . GMR sensors can have a higher sensitivity and lower power consumption than AMR sensors . GMR sensors can be used to measure the magnetic field, as well as the current, position, speed, and rotation of an object .
  - Semiconductor magneto-resistors: These sensors use the property of some semiconductors, such as silicon, germanium, and gallium arsenide, to change their electrical resistance when subjected to a magnetic field. The change in resistance is due to the Lorentz force acting on the charge carriers in the semiconductor. Semiconductor magneto-resistors can have a higher sensitivity and lower noise than magneto-resistive sensors, but they also require a higher power supply and a more complex circuitry. Semiconductor magneto-resistors can be used to measure the magnetic field, as well as the current, position, speed, and rotation of an object.
  - Hall effect sensors: These sensors use the property of some materials to generate a voltage difference across their width when subjected to a magnetic field perpendicular to the current flow . The voltage difference is proportional to the product of the current, the magnetic field, and the Hall coefficient of the material . Hall effect sensors can be used to measure the magnetic field, as well as the current, position, speed, and rotation of an object .
  - Inductance and eddy current sensors: These sensors use the property of a coil or a wire to generate an electromotive force (EMF) when the magnetic flux through it changes .
