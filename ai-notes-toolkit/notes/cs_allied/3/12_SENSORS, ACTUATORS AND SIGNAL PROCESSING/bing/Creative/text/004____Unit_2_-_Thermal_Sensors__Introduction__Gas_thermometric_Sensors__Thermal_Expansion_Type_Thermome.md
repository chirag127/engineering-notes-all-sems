## Unit 2 - Thermal Sensors: Introduction, Gas thermometric Sensors, Thermal Expansion Type Thermometric Sensors, Acoustic Temperature Sensor, Dielectric Constant and Refractive Index Thermo-sensors, Helium Low Temperature Thermometer, Nuclear Thermometer, Magnetic Thermometer, Resistance Change Type Thermometric Sensors, Thermo-EMF Sensors, Junction Semiconductor Types, Thermal Radiation Sensors, Quartz Crystal Thermo-electric Sensors, NQR Thermometry, Spectroscopic Thermometry, Noise Thermometry, Heat Flux Sensors.

- Thermal sensors are devices that measure temperature or heat flux by converting thermal energy into electrical signals.
- There are various types of thermal sensors based on different principles and applications, such as gas, expansion, acoustic, dielectric, nuclear, magnetic, resistance, thermo-electric, radiation, quartz, NQR, spectroscopic, noise, and heat flux sensors.
- Gas thermometric sensors use the change in pressure, volume, or density of a gas due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are constant volume gas thermometer, constant pressure gas thermometer, and thermocouple gas thermometer.
- Thermal expansion type thermometric sensors use the change in length, volume, or shape of a solid or liquid due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are bimetallic strip, liquid-in-glass thermometer, pressure thermometer, and Bourdon tube.
- Acoustic temperature sensor uses the change in speed of sound in a medium due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are acoustic pyrometer, acoustic thermometer, and acoustic resonator.
- Dielectric constant and refractive index thermo-sensors use the change in dielectric constant or refractive index of a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are capacitance thermometer, optical fiber thermometer, and interferometric thermometer.
- Helium low temperature thermometer uses the change in specific heat of helium gas near absolute zero as the sensing element. It can measure temperatures as low as 0.001 K.
- Nuclear thermometer uses the change in nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) or nuclear quadrupole resonance (NQR) of a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are NMR thermometer and NQR thermometer.
- Magnetic thermometer uses the change in magnetic properties of a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are paramagnetic thermometer, Curie-point thermometer, and superconducting thermometer.
- Resistance change type thermometric sensors use the change in electrical resistance of a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are resistance thermometer, thermistor, and bolometer.
- Thermo-EMF sensors use the change in electromotive force (EMF) generated by a thermocouple or a thermopile due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are thermocouple, thermopile, and Seebeck effect sensor.
- Junction semiconductor types use the change in voltage or current across a p-n junction or a metal-semiconductor junction due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are diode thermometer, transistor thermometer, and thermoelectric generator.
- Thermal radiation sensors use the change in intensity or wavelength of thermal radiation emitted by a body due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are pyrometer, radiometer, and infrared thermometer.
- Quartz crystal thermo-electric sensors use the change in frequency or phase of a quartz crystal oscillator due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are quartz crystal thermometer and quartz crystal microbalance.
- NQR thermometry uses the change in frequency of nuclear quadrupole resonance (NQR) of a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. It can measure temperatures as low as 0.01 K.
- Spectroscopic thermometry uses the change in spectral characteristics of a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are fluorescence thermometer, Raman thermometer, and laser thermometer.
- Noise thermometry uses the change in thermal noise or Johnson noise of a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. It can measure temperatures as low as 0.001 K.
- Heat flux sensors use the change in heat transfer rate or heat flux across a material due to temperature change as the sensing element. Examples are calorimeter, thermopile, and heat flux meter.
