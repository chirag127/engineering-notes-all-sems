### Digital Signal Processors: Introduction – Architecture – Features – Addressing Formats – Functional modes – Introduction to Commercial Processors

Digital Signal Processors (DSPs) are specialized microprocessors designed specifically for the fast and efficient processing of digital signals. These signals can include audio, video, speech, and other types of data that have been converted into a digital format.

#### Architecture

The architecture of a DSP is optimized for high-speed data processing. This is achieved through the use of specialized hardware, such as multiply-accumulate units, which can perform multiple mathematical operations simultaneously. DSPs also have large on-chip memory and fast data buses to facilitate the rapid transfer of data.

#### Features

Some common features of DSPs include:
- Real-time signal processing capabilities
- High-speed mathematical operations
- Low power consumption
- Specialized instruction sets for signal processing
- Large on-chip memory

#### Addressing Formats

DSPs use a variety of addressing formats to access data in memory. These can include direct, indirect, and indexed addressing modes. The specific addressing modes available will vary depending on the particular DSP being used.

#### Functional Modes

DSPs can operate in a variety of functional modes, including real-time processing, data transfer, and power-saving modes. The specific modes available will depend on the particular DSP being used.

#### Introduction to Commercial Processors

There are many commercial DSPs available from a variety of manufacturers. Some common examples include the Texas Instruments TMS320 series, the Analog Devices SHARC series, and the Freescale (now NXP) Symphony series. These processors are used in a wide range of applications, including audio and video processing, telecommunications, and control systems.
