## Unit 3 - Radiation Sensors

### Introduction
Radiation sensors are devices that detect and measure the presence of radiation. They are used in a variety of applications, including medical imaging, nuclear power plants, and environmental monitoring.

### Basic Characteristics
Radiation sensors have several basic characteristics that are important to consider when selecting a sensor for a particular application. These include sensitivity, accuracy, and response time. Sensitivity refers to the ability of the sensor to detect small amounts of radiation. Accuracy refers to the ability of the sensor to provide a correct measurement of the radiation present. Response time refers to the time it takes for the sensor to respond to a change in radiation levels.

### Types of Photosensistors/Photo detectors
There are several types of photosensors, including photodiodes, phototransistors, and photoresistors. Photodiodes are semiconductor devices that generate a current when exposed to light. Phototransistors are similar to photodiodes, but they have an additional layer of material that amplifies the current generated by the photodiode. Photoresistors are resistive devices that change their resistance when exposed to light.

### X-ray and Nuclear Radiation Sensors
X-ray and nuclear radiation sensors are used to detect and measure the presence of X-rays and nuclear radiation. These sensors are commonly used in medical imaging, nuclear power plants, and environmental monitoring.

### Fiber Optic Sensors
Fiber optic sensors use light to transmit information. They are commonly used in telecommunications, but they can also be used to measure temperature, pressure, and other physical quantities. Fiber optic sensors have several advantages over traditional sensors, including their small size, immunity to electromagnetic interference, and ability to transmit information over long distances.
