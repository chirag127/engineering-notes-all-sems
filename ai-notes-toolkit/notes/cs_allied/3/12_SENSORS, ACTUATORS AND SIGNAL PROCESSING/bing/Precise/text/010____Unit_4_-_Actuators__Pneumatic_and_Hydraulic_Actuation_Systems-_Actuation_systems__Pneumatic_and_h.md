## Unit 4 - Actuators: Pneumatic and Hydraulic Actuation Systems

Actuation systems are used to convert energy into mechanical motion. There are two main types of actuation systems: pneumatic and hydraulic.

Pneumatic systems use compressed air to generate mechanical motion. The air is compressed by a compressor and stored in a tank. The compressed air is then released through a series of valves to drive the actuator.

Hydraulic systems use pressurized fluid to generate mechanical motion. The fluid is pressurized by a pump and stored in a reservoir. The pressurized fluid is then released through a series of valves to drive the actuator.

Directional control valves are used to control the direction of flow of the compressed air or pressurized fluid. They can be either manually or automatically operated.

Pressure control valves are used to regulate the pressure of the compressed air or pressurized fluid. They can be either manually or automatically operated.

Cylinders are used to convert the energy of the compressed air or pressurized fluid into linear motion. They can be either single-acting or double-acting.

Servo and proportional control valves are used to precisely control the flow of the compressed air or pressurized fluid. They can be either manually or automatically operated.

Process control valves are used to control the flow of fluids in industrial processes. They can be either manually or automatically operated.

Rotary actuators are used to convert the energy of the compressed air or pressurized fluid into rotary motion. They can be either single-acting or double-acting.
