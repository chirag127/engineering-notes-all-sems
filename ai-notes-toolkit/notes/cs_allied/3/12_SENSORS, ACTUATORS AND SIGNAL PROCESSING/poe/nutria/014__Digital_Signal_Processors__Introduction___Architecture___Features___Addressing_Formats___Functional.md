
### Digital Signal Processors: Introduction

- Digital Signal Processors (DSPs) are specialized microprocessors designed for the efficient implementation of digital signal processing algorithms. 
- DSPs are used in a wide range of applications such as audio and video processing, communications, radar, sonar, medical imaging, and more.
- DSPs are characterized by their ability to execute complex mathematical operations quickly and efficiently, allowing for real-time processing of digital signals.

### Architecture

- DSPs typically consist of a CPU core, memory, and peripherals. 
- The CPU core is designed for the efficient execution of DSP algorithms, and is capable of executing a wide range of instructions tailored for digital signal processing.
- Memory is used to store data and instructions, while peripherals provide access to external devices.

### Features

- DSPs are typically designed with features that make them well-suited for digital signal processing applications. 
- These features include high-speed arithmetic operations, dedicated hardware for digital filters, and special instructions for efficient data manipulation.

### Addressing Formats

- DSPs use a variety of addressing formats to access memory and peripherals. 
- Common addressing formats include absolute, relative, indexed, and indirect addressing.

### Functional Modes

- DSPs typically support a variety of functional modes, such as single-cycle, multi-cycle, and pipelined. 
- These modes allow for the efficient execution of DSP algorithms, and can be tailored to the specific needs of the application.

### Introduction to Commercial Processors

- There are a variety of commercial DSPs available for use in a wide range of applications. 
- Popular DSPs include the Texas Instruments TMS320, Analog Devices ADSP-21xx, and Freescale DSP56000 series. 
- These processors offer a range of features and performance levels, and are widely used in a variety of applications.
