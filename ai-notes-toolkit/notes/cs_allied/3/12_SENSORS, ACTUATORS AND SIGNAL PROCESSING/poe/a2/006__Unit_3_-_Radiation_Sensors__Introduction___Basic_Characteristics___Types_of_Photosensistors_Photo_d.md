## Unit 3 - Radiation Sensors:

Introduction
- Radiation sensors detect various types of radiation such as light, X-rays, gamma rays, etc.
- They convert the detected radiation into electrical signals which can be measured and analyzed.

Basic Characteristics
- Sensitivity - Ability to detect small amounts of radiation
- Spectral response - Ability to detect specific ranges of radiation wavelengths
- Dynamic range - Ability to measure wide range of radiation intensities
- Noise - Level of unwanted signals which can affect measurement accuracy

Types of Photosensistors/Photo detectors
- Photodiodes - Semiconductor devices which produce current when exposed to light
- Phototransistors - Semiconductor devices which amplify the current produced by light
- Photoconductors - Materials which decrease electrical resistance when exposed to light
- Photovoltaic cells - Convert light energy into electrical energy

X-ray and Nuclear Radiation Sensors
- Use materials like silicon, germanium to absorb radiation and produce charge carriers
- Need high density and thickness to absorb enough radiation
- Often need cooling and shielding for high radiation fields

Fiber Optic Sensors
- Use optical fibers to transmit and detect light
- Immune to EMI and remote sensing possible
- Low loss cables enable long distance measurement
- Simple and low cost
