### Unit 3 - HARDWARE COMMUNICATION: Arduino Compatible Microcontrollers

1. **Arduino Uno:** The Arduino Uno is a microcontroller board based on the ATmega328P. It has 14 digital input/output pins, 6 analog inputs, a 16 MHz quartz crystal, a USB connection, a power jack, an ICSP header, and a reset button.

2. **Arduino Mega:** The Arduino Mega is a microcontroller board based on the ATmega2560. It has 54 digital input/output pins, 16 analog inputs, 4 UARTs, a 16 MHz crystal oscillator, a USB connection, a power jack, an ICSP header, and a reset button.

3. **Arduino Nano:** The Arduino Nano is a small, complete, and breadboard-friendly board based on the ATmega328. It has more or less the same functionality as the Arduino Uno, but in a different package. It lacks only a DC power jack and works with a Mini-B USB cable instead of a standard one.

4. **Arduino Pro Mini:** The Arduino Pro Mini is a microcontroller board based on the ATmega328. It has 14 digital input/output pins, 6 analog inputs, an on-board resonator, a reset button, and holes for mounting pin headers.

5. **Arduino Micro:** The Arduino Micro is a microcontroller board based on the ATmega32U4. It has 20 digital input/output pins, 7 of which can be used as PWM outputs and 12 as analog inputs, a 16 MHz crystal oscillator, a micro USB connection, an ICSP header, and a reset button.

6. **Arduino Leonardo:** The Arduino Leonardo is a microcontroller board based on the ATmega32U4. It has 20 digital input/output pins, 7 of which can be used as PWM outputs and 12 as analog inputs, a 16 MHz crystal oscillator, a micro USB connection, a power jack, an ICSP header, and a reset button.

7. **Arduino Due:** The Arduino Due is a microcontroller board based on the Atmel SAM3X8E ARM Cortex-M3 CPU. It is the first Arduino board based on a 32-bit ARM core microcontroller. It has 54 digital input/output pins, 12 analog inputs, 4 UARTs, a 84 MHz clock, a USB OTG capable connection, 2 DAC, 2 TWI, a power jack, an SPI header, a JTAG header, a reset button, and an erase button.

8. **Arduino Zero:** The Arduino Zero is a microcontroller board based on the Atmel SAMD21G18, a 32-bit ARM Cortex-M0+ core. It has 14 digital input/output pins, 6 of which can be used as PWM outputs, 6 analog inputs, a 32.768 kHz crystal oscillator, a micro USB connection, an ICSP header, 2 reset buttons, and an erase button.

9. **Arduino MKR1000:** The Arduino MKR1000 is a powerful board that combines the functionality of the Zero and the Wi-Fi Shield. It is based on the Atmel SAMD21 and a low power Wi-Fi module. It has 8 digital input/output pins, 7 of which can be used as PWM outputs, 7 analog inputs, a 32.768 kHz crystal oscillator, a micro USB connection, an ICSP header, a reset button, and an erase button.

10. **Arduino 101:** The Arduino 101 is a microcontroller board based on the Intel Curie module. It has 14 digital input/output pins, 6 of which can be used as PWM outputs, 6 analog inputs, a 32.768 kHz crystal oscillator, a micro USB connection, an ICSP header, a reset button, and an erase button.

These are some of the most popular Arduino compatible microcontrollers. They vary in size, processing power, and functionality, allowing you to choose the one that best fits your project's needs.
