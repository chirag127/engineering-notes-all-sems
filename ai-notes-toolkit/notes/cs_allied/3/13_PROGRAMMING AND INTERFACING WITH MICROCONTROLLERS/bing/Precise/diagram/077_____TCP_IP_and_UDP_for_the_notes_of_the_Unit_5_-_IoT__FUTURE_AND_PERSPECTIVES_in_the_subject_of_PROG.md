### TCP/IP and UDP for the notes of the Unit 5 - IoT, FUTURE AND PERSPECTIVES in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

TCP/IP (Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol) and UDP (User Datagram Protocol) are two of the core protocols in the Internet protocol suite. They are used to transmit data over the internet and other networks.

TCP/IP is a connection-oriented protocol, which means that it establishes a connection between two devices before transmitting data. This connection ensures that data is transmitted reliably and in the correct order. TCP/IP is used for applications that require reliable data transmission, such as email and file transfers.

UDP, on the other hand, is a connectionless protocol. It does not establish a connection before transmitting data, and it does not guarantee that data will be transmitted reliably or in the correct order. UDP is used for applications that do not require reliable data transmission, such as online gaming and video streaming.

Some key differences between TCP/IP and UDP include:
- TCP/IP is connection-oriented, while UDP is connectionless.
- TCP/IP guarantees reliable data transmission, while UDP does not.
- TCP/IP is used for applications that require reliable data transmission, while UDP is used for applications that do not require reliable data transmission.

In the context of IoT (Internet of Things), both TCP/IP and UDP can be used to transmit data between devices. The choice of protocol depends on the specific requirements of the application. For example, if reliable data transmission is required, TCP/IP would be the preferred choice. If speed is more important than reliability, UDP may be a better choice.
