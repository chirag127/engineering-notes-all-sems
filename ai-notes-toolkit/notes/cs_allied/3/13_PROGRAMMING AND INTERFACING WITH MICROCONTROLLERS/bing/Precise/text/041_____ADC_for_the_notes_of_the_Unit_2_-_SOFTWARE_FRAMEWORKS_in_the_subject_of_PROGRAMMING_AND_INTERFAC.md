### ADC

Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) is a device that converts an analog signal into a digital signal. It is used in microcontrollers to interface with analog sensors and devices. Here are some key points to remember about ADC:

1. ADC converts a continuous analog signal into a discrete digital signal.
2. The resolution of the ADC determines the number of discrete values it can produce over the range of analog values.
3. The accuracy of the ADC is determined by the number of bits it has. The more bits, the more accurate the conversion.
4. The sampling rate of the ADC determines how often the analog signal is sampled and converted into a digital value.
5. ADCs can be either internal or external to the microcontroller. Internal ADCs are integrated into the microcontroller, while external ADCs are separate devices that communicate with the microcontroller.
6. ADCs can use different conversion techniques, such as successive approximation, flash, and sigma-delta.
7. ADCs can have different input configurations, such as single-ended or differential.
8. ADCs can have different reference voltages, which determine the range of analog values that can be converted.
