### Analog to Digital Conversion

Analog to Digital Conversion (ADC) is the process of converting an analog signal into a digital signal. This is done by sampling the analog signal at regular intervals and quantizing the samples to the nearest digital value.

1. **Sampling**: The first step in ADC is to sample the analog signal at regular intervals. This is done using a sample and hold circuit, which captures the value of the analog signal at a specific point in time and holds it until the next sample is taken.

2. **Quantization**: The next step is to quantize the sampled values. This involves mapping the analog value to the nearest digital value. The number of possible digital values is determined by the number of bits used to represent the digital signal. For example, an 8-bit ADC can represent 256 different digital values.

3. **Encoding**: The final step is to encode the quantized values into a digital format. This is typically done using binary encoding, where each digital value is represented by a unique binary number.

ADC is an important process in many applications, including audio and video recording, data acquisition, and control systems. It allows analog signals to be processed and stored digitally, which can improve accuracy, reliability, and flexibility.
