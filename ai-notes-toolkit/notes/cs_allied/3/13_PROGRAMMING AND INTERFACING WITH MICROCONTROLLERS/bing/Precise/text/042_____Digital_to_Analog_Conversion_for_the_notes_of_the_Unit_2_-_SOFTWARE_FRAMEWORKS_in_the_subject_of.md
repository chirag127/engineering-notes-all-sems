### Digital to Analog Conversion

Digital to Analog Conversion (DAC) is the process of converting a digital signal into an analog signal. This is necessary because many real-world signals, such as sound and light, are analog in nature, while digital systems, such as computers and microcontrollers, can only process digital signals.

1. **DAC Techniques:** There are several techniques for performing digital to analog conversion, including the use of a resistor ladder network, a binary-weighted resistor network, and a R-2R ladder network.

2. **Resolution:** The resolution of a DAC is determined by the number of bits used to represent the digital signal. For example, an 8-bit DAC can represent 2^8 = 256 different levels, while a 16-bit DAC can represent 2^16 = 65,536 different levels.

3. **Accuracy:** The accuracy of a DAC is determined by how closely the output analog signal matches the desired analog signal. This can be affected by factors such as the linearity of the DAC, the stability of the reference voltage, and the presence of noise.

4. **Applications:** DACs are used in a wide range of applications, including audio and video playback, signal generation, and control systems.
