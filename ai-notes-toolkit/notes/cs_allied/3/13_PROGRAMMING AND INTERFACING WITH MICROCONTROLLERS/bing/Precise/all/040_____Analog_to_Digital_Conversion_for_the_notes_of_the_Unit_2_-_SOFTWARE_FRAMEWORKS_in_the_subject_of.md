### Analog to Digital Conversion

Analog to Digital Conversion (ADC) is the process of converting an analog signal into a digital signal. This is done by sampling the analog signal at regular intervals and converting the samples into digital values. The digital values can then be processed and stored by a digital system, such as a microcontroller.

1. **Sampling**: The first step in ADC is to sample the analog signal at regular intervals. This is done using a sample and hold circuit, which captures the value of the analog signal at a specific point in time and holds it until the next sample is taken.

2. **Quantization**: The next step is to quantize the sampled signal. This involves assigning a digital value to each sample based on its amplitude. The number of possible digital values is determined by the resolution of the ADC, which is typically measured in bits.

3. **Encoding**: The final step is to encode the quantized values into a digital format. This is typically done using binary encoding, where each digital value is represented by a binary number.

ADC is an important process in many applications, including audio and video recording, data acquisition, and control systems. It allows analog signals to be processed and stored by digital systems, which can provide many advantages such as increased accuracy, flexibility, and ease of use.
