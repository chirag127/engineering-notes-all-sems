# Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION: Measurements

- Measurements are an essential part of programming and interfacing with microcontrollers.
- They allow us to quantify and compare different physical quantities, such as voltage, current, and resistance.
- There are several standard units of measurement used in electronics, including volts (V) for voltage, amperes (A) for current, and ohms (Ω) for resistance.
- These units are part of the International System of Units (SI), which is the standard system of measurement used in science and engineering.
- In addition to these standard units, there are also several derived units that are commonly used in electronics, such as watts (W) for power and farads (F) for capacitance.
- It is important to use the correct units when making measurements and performing calculations, as this ensures that the results are accurate and meaningful.
- When working with microcontrollers, it is also important to understand the limitations of the measurement tools and sensors that are being used, as these can affect the accuracy and precision of the measurements.
- Proper calibration and error analysis can help to improve the accuracy and reliability of measurements when working with microcontrollers.
