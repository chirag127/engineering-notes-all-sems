### Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

1. **Microcontrollers** are small computers on a single integrated circuit that contain a processor core, memory, and programmable input/output peripherals.
2. They are designed for embedded applications, in contrast to the microprocessors used in personal computers or other general-purpose applications.
3. Microcontrollers are used in automatically controlled products and devices, such as automobile engine control systems, implantable medical devices, remote controls, office machines, appliances, power tools, toys, and other embedded systems.
4. By reducing the size and cost compared to a design that uses a separate microprocessor, memory, and input/output devices, microcontrollers make it economical to digitally control even more devices and processes.
5. Microcontrollers are typically programmed using a low-level language such as assembly language or a high-level language such as C or C++.
6. The use of microcontrollers in embedded systems allows for greater flexibility and control, as well as the ability to add new features and capabilities to a device through software updates.
7. Interfacing with microcontrollers involves connecting external devices and components to the microcontroller in order to expand its capabilities and functionality.
8. Common interfaces used with microcontrollers include digital and analog inputs and outputs, serial communication protocols such as UART, SPI, and I2C, and wireless communication protocols such as Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.
9. Programming and interfacing with microcontrollers requires knowledge of both hardware and software, as well as an understanding of the specific microcontroller being used and its capabilities and limitations.
10. The use of microcontrollers in embedded systems has revolutionized the way that devices and processes are controlled, allowing for greater efficiency, precision, and flexibility.
