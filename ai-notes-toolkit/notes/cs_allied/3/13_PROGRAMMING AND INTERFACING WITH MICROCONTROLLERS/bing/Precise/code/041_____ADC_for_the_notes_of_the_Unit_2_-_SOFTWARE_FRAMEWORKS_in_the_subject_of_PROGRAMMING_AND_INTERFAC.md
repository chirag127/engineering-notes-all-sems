### ADC

Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) is a device that converts an analog signal into a digital signal. It is an essential component in the field of electronics, particularly in the design of microcontroller-based systems.

Here are some key points to remember about ADC:

1. ADCs are used to convert continuous analog signals into discrete digital signals.
2. The resolution of an ADC is determined by the number of bits it uses to represent the digital output.
3. The accuracy of an ADC is affected by factors such as quantization error, non-linearity, and noise.
4. There are several types of ADCs, including successive approximation, flash, and sigma-delta.
5. ADCs are commonly used in applications such as data acquisition, audio and video processing, and control systems.

In the context of programming and interfacing with microcontrollers, ADCs play a crucial role in allowing the microcontroller to interact with the analog world. Microcontrollers typically have built-in ADCs that can be used to read analog sensor data and convert it into a digital format that can be processed by the microcontroller. This allows for the development of sophisticated control systems and data acquisition systems using microcontrollers.
