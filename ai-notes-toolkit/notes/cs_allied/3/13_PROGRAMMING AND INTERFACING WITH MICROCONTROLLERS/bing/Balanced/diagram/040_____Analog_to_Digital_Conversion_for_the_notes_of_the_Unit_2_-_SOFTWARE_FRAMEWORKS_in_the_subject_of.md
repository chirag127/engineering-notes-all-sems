### Analog to Digital Conversion

- Analog signals are continuous signals that vary in amplitude and frequency over time. They can represent physical quantities such as sound, light, temperature, pressure, etc.
- Digital signals are discrete signals that have only two possible values: 0 or 1. They can represent binary data such as text, images, audio, video, etc.
- Analog to digital conversion (ADC) is the process of converting an analog signal into a digital signal. It involves sampling, quantization, and encoding.
- Sampling is the process of taking periodic measurements of the analog signal at a fixed rate called the sampling frequency. The sampling frequency must be at least twice the highest frequency component of the analog signal, according to the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem, to avoid aliasing.
- Quantization is the process of approximating the sampled values of the analog signal with a finite number of discrete levels called the quantization levels. The difference between the actual value and the quantized value is called the quantization error. The number of quantization levels depends on the number of bits used to represent each sample, called the resolution. The higher the resolution, the lower the quantization error and the better the quality of the digital signal, but also the more memory and bandwidth required.
- Encoding is the process of assigning a binary code to each quantized level. The simplest encoding scheme is the binary encoding, where each bit corresponds to a power of two. Other encoding schemes include offset binary, sign-magnitude, ones' complement, twos' complement, gray code, etc. The choice of encoding scheme depends on the application and the hardware.
- ADCs are devices that perform analog to digital conversion. They can be classified into different types based on their architecture, such as successive approximation, flash, sigma-delta, dual-slope, etc. Each type has its own advantages and disadvantages in terms of speed, accuracy, power consumption, cost, etc.
- ADCs are widely used in microcontrollers to interface with analog sensors and devices. They can be configured and controlled by software frameworks that provide functions and libraries to access the ADC registers and settings, such as the ADC peripheral driver library (PDL) for the PSoC 6 microcontroller. The software frameworks also provide examples and documentation to help the user program and debug the ADC applications.
