### DAC

- A digital-to-analog converter (DAC) is a device that converts a digital signal into an analog signal, such as a voltage or a current.
- A DAC can be used to generate custom analog signals, such as waveforms, voice synthesis, or frequency or voltage generation.
- A DAC can be integrated on a microcontroller chip or used as an external peripheral.
- A DAC is defined by its bit resolution, which indicates how many selectable voltage levels are possible between the positive and negative voltage sources.
- A DAC requires a reference voltage, which can be derived from a dedicated analog pin or an external source. The reference voltage should be isolated from the digital logic to minimize noise and ripple.
- A DAC can be interfaced with a microcontroller using various protocols, such as SPI, I2C, or parallel.
- A DAC can have different output modes, such as buffered, unbuffered, or rail-to-rail.
- A DAC can have different performance parameters, such as speed, accuracy, linearity, noise, and power consumption.
