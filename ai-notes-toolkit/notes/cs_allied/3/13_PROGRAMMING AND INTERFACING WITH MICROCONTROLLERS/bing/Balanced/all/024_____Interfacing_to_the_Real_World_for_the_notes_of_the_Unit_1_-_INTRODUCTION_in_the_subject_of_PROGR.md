# Interfacing to the Real World

- Interfacing is the method of connecting or linking together one device, especially a computer or microcontroller, with another device or the real world.
- Interfacing allows us to design or adapt the output and input configurations of the two electronic devices so that they can work together.
- Interfacing can be done using various circuits, such as switches, LEDs, sensors, actuators, etc.
- Interfacing can also involve communication protocols, such as serial, parallel, I2C, SPI, etc.
- Interfacing can enable applications such as video, audio, robotics, internet of things, etc.
- Interfacing can be done using two methods: interrupts or polling.
- Interrupts are signals that notify the microcontroller that a device needs its service, and the microcontroller stops its current task and serves the device.
- Polling is a method where the microcontroller periodically checks the status of each device and serves the device if needed.
- Interrupts are more efficient and responsive than polling, but they require more hardware and software complexity.
- Interfacing can also involve semantic interoperability, which is the ability of different systems to exchange and use information in a meaningful way.
- Semantic interoperability can be achieved by using common ontologies, which are formal representations of the concepts and relations in a domain.
- Semantic interoperability can improve the quality and usability of data and information in various fields, such as medicine, biology, engineering, etc.
