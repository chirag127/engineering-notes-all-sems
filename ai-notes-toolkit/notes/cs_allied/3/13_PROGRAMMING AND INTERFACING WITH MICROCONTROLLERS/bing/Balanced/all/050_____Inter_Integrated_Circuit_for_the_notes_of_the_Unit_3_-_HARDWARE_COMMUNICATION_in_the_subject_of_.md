# Inter-Integrated Circuit

- Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) is a serial communication protocol that allows multiple devices to communicate with each other using only two wires: a clock line (SCL) and a data line (SDA)    .
- I2C was invented by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductors) in 1982 to connect low-speed peripherals to a motherboard, mobile phone, embedded system or other electronic devices  .
- I2C can support up to 1008 slave devices and multiple masters on the same bus, which makes it suitable for applications where low costs and ease-of-implementation take priority over high speed  .
- I2C uses a 7-bit or 10-bit address to identify each device on the bus, and transfers data in packets of 8 bits with an acknowledge bit after each byte  .
- I2C operates in four modes: standard mode (up to 100 kbit/s), fast mode (up to 400 kbit/s), fast mode plus (up to 1 Mbit/s) and high-speed mode (up to 3.4 Mbit/s) .
- I2C is widely used for connecting sensors, EEPROMs, LCDs, ADCs, DACs, RTCs and other devices to microcontrollers or microprocessors   .
- I2C is also known as a two-wire interface, I2C, or IIC   .
