### Circuit Theory

Circuit theory is a set of techniques used to describe the flow of energy around an electrical loop. The theory is comprised of a number of different laws, ideas, and definitions. These include:

- **Ohm’s law**: This law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it, and inversely proportional to the resistance of the conductor. The formula is `V = IR`, where `V` is the voltage, `I` is the current, and `R` is the resistance.
- **Kirchhoff’s laws**: These laws are based on the conservation of charge and energy in a circuit. The first law, also known as the current law, states that the algebraic sum of currents entering a node (a point where two or more branches meet) is zero. The second law, also known as the voltage law, states that the algebraic sum of voltages around a closed loop is zero.
- **Resistors**: These are components that oppose the flow of current and dissipate energy as heat. They are characterized by their resistance, which is measured in ohms. Resistors can be connected in series or parallel to create different combinations of resistance. The equivalent resistance of resistors in series is the sum of their individual resistances, while the equivalent resistance of resistors in parallel is the reciprocal of the sum of their reciprocals.
- **Capacitors**: These are components that store electric charge and energy in an electric field. They are characterized by their capacitance, which is measured in farads. Capacitors can be connected in series or parallel to create different combinations of capacitance. The equivalent capacitance of capacitors in series is the reciprocal of the sum of their reciprocals, while the equivalent capacitance of capacitors in parallel is the sum of their individual capacitances.
- **Inductors**: These are components that store energy in a magnetic field and oppose changes in current. They are characterized by their inductance, which is measured in henrys. Inductors can be connected in series or parallel to create different combinations of inductance. The equivalent inductance of inductors in series is the sum of their individual inductances, while the equivalent inductance of inductors in parallel is the reciprocal of the sum of their reciprocals.

These are some of the basic concepts of circuit theory that are relevant for the subject of programming and interfacing with microcontrollers. There are also other topics, such as AC and DC circuits, frequency response, filters, transformers, and more, that are covered in more advanced courses.
