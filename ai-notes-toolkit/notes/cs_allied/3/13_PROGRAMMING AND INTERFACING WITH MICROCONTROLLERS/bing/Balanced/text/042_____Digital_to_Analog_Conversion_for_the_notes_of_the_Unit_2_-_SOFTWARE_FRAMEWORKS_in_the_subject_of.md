### Digital to Analog Conversion

- Digital to analog conversion (DAC) is the process by which digital signals (which have a binary state) are converted to analog signals (which theoretically have an infinite number of states).
- DAC is necessary when we want to communicate digital data (such as from a computer or a microcontroller) to an analog device (such as a speaker or a motor).
- DAC can be achieved by using different techniques that modify one of the features of an analog signal (such as amplitude, frequency, or phase) depending on the data in a digital signal (such as 0s and 1s).
- Some of the common DAC techniques are:
  - Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK): The amplitude of an analog carrier signal is changed according to the digital data.
  - Frequency Shift Keying (FSK): The frequency of an analog carrier signal is changed according to the digital data.
  - Phase Shift Keying (PSK): The phase of an analog carrier signal is changed according to the digital data.
  - Pulse Code Modulation (PCM): The analog signal is sampled at regular intervals and each sample is quantized and encoded into a binary code.
- DAC can be implemented by using different architectures that vary in their complexity, accuracy, speed, and cost. Some of the common DAC architectures are:
  - Binary Weighted Resistor DAC: A network of resistors with binary weights is used to generate a voltage output proportional to the digital input.
  - R-2R Ladder DAC: A network of resistors with a constant ratio of 2 is used to generate a voltage output proportional to the digital input.
  - String DAC: A series of switches and resistors are used to divide a reference voltage into equal steps and select one of them according to the digital input.
  - Flash DAC: A parallel array of comparators and resistors are used to compare the digital input with a reference voltage and generate a corresponding output.
- DAC is an essential component of many applications that involve digital and analog signals, such as audio and video processing, data transmission, signal generation, and control systems.
- DAC can be evaluated by using different figures of merit, such as resolution, accuracy, linearity, speed, power consumption, and noise performance.
