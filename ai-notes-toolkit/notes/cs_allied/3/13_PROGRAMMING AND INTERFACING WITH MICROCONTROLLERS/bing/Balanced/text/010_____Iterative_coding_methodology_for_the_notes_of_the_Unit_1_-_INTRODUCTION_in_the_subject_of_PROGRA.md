### Iterative coding methodology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

- Iterative coding methodology is a process of developing software by repeating a cycle of planning, coding, testing, and evaluating the results.
- The main benefits of iterative coding methodology are:
  - It allows for early feedback and testing of the software functionality and quality.
  - It reduces the risk of errors and bugs by fixing them in small increments.
  - It enables the software to adapt to changing requirements and user needs.
  - It improves the collaboration and communication among the developers and the stakeholders.
- The main steps of iterative coding methodology are:
  - Define the scope and objectives of the software project.
  - Divide the project into smaller and manageable iterations, each with a specific goal and deliverable.
  - Plan the tasks and resources for each iteration, such as the design, coding, testing, and documentation.
  - Implement the tasks and produce the deliverable for each iteration, such as a prototype, a module, or a feature.
  - Test and evaluate the deliverable for each iteration, such as the functionality, performance, usability, and quality.
  - Review the feedback and results of each iteration, and identify the improvements and changes needed for the next iteration.
  - Repeat the cycle until the software meets the desired requirements and expectations.
- An example of iterative coding methodology is the agile software development, which is based on the principles of collaboration, flexibility, simplicity, and customer satisfaction. Agile software development uses various frameworks and methods, such as Scrum, Kanban, XP, and Lean.
