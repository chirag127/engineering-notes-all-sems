### Inter-Integrated Circuit

- Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) is a serial communication protocol that allows multiple devices to communicate with each other using only two wires: a clock line (SCL) and a data line (SDA)    .
- I2C was invented by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductors) in 1982 to connect low-speed peripherals to a motherboard, mobile phone, embedded system or other electronic devices   .
- I2C can support up to 1008 slave devices and multiple masters on the same bus, which can operate at different speeds ranging from 100 kbit/s to 5 Mbit/s   .
- I2C uses a packet-based data transfer mechanism, where each packet consists of a start condition, an address byte, one or more data bytes, and a stop condition  .
- I2C has several advantages over other communication protocols, such as low cost, simplicity, flexibility, and compatibility  .
- I2C also has some limitations, such as limited bus length, limited data rate, limited error detection, and arbitration issues  .
- I2C is widely used in applications such as sensors, EEPROMs, LCDs, RTCs, ADCs, DACs, and microcontrollers   .
