### Circuit Theory for the Notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- Circuit theory is a set of techniques used to describe the flow of energy around an electrical loop .
- Circuit theory is based on a number of different laws, ideas, and definitions that relate the electrical quantities of current, voltage, resistance, power, and energy .
- Some of the fundamental concepts of circuit theory are:
  - Ohm's law: It states that the current through a conductor is proportional to the voltage across it, and inversely proportional to the resistance .
  - Kirchhoff's laws: They are two rules that apply to any closed loop or junction in a circuit. The first law states that the sum of currents entering a junction is equal to the sum of currents leaving it. The second law states that the sum of voltage drops around a loop is equal to the sum of voltage sources .
  - Resistors: They are components that limit the flow of current in a circuit. They have a fixed or variable resistance value that determines how much voltage they drop  .
  - Series and parallel circuits: They are two ways of connecting resistors or other components in a circuit. In a series circuit, the components are connected end to end, and the same current flows through all of them. In a parallel circuit, the components are connected across the same voltage source, and the total current is divided among them.
  - Resistivity and conductivity: They are two properties of materials that affect how easily they allow current to flow. Resistivity is the measure of how much a material opposes the flow of current, and conductivity is the measure of how well a material conducts current. They are inversely related to each other.
  - Electric power: It is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred by a circuit. It is equal to the product of voltage and current, and it is measured in watts .
  - Electric energy: It is the capacity to do work by a circuit. It is equal to the product of power and time, and it is measured in joules.
