### Measurements

- Measurements are the process of obtaining quantitative information about physical quantities, such as voltage, current, resistance, temperature, etc.
- Measurements are essential for designing, testing, and debugging microcontroller-based systems, as well as for monitoring and controlling their performance and behavior.
- Measurements can be classified into two types: analog and digital.
  - Analog measurements are continuous and can have any value within a range. For example, the temperature of a room can vary continuously from 0°C to 40°C.
  - Digital measurements are discrete and can have only a finite number of values. For example, a digital thermometer can display the temperature in increments of 0.1°C, such as 25.3°C or 25.4°C.
- Measurements can also be classified into two categories: direct and indirect.
  - Direct measurements are obtained by using a device that directly measures the physical quantity of interest. For example, a voltmeter can directly measure the voltage across a resistor.
  - Indirect measurements are obtained by using a device that measures a related physical quantity and then converts it to the desired quantity using a mathematical formula. For example, an ammeter can measure the current through a resistor and then calculate the voltage using Ohm's law.
- Measurements can be affected by various factors, such as noise, interference, accuracy, precision, resolution, sensitivity, range, etc. These factors need to be considered and minimized to ensure reliable and valid measurements.
- Measurements can be performed using various instruments, such as multimeters, oscilloscopes, logic analyzers, frequency counters, etc. These instruments have different features, functions, and limitations that need to be understood and utilized properly.
