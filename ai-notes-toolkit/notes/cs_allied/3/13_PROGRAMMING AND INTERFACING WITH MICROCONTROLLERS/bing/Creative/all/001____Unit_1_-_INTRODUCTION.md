## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI).
- AI is the study of how to create machines and software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, perception, and natural language processing.
- AI can be divided into two main branches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI.
  - Symbolic AI uses symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge, such as logic, search, expert systems, and knowledge representation.
  - Sub-symbolic AI uses numerical and statistical methods to model and learn from data, such as neural networks, evolutionary algorithms, fuzzy logic, and machine learning.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the level of intelligence and the domain of application, such as weak AI, strong AI, narrow AI, general AI, and super AI.
  - Weak AI or narrow AI is the type of AI that can perform specific tasks or solve specific problems, but cannot generalize to other domains or exhibit human-like intelligence. Most of the current AI systems fall into this category, such as speech recognition, face recognition, chess playing, and self-driving cars.
  - Strong AI or general AI is the type of AI that can achieve human-level intelligence or beyond, and can understand and reason about any domain or problem. This is the ultimate goal of AI research, but it is still far from being realized.
  - Super AI is the type of AI that can surpass human intelligence in all aspects, and can potentially create and control other AI systems. This is a hypothetical and controversial concept that raises ethical and existential questions about the future of humanity and AI.
- AI can also be categorized into different paradigms based on the approach or methodology used to achieve intelligence, such as logic-based AI, search-based AI, knowledge-based AI, probabilistic AI, machine learning-based AI, and hybrid AI.
  - Logic-based AI uses formal logic and inference to represent and reason about knowledge, such as propositional logic, predicate logic, modal logic, and non-monotonic logic.
  - Search-based AI uses algorithms and heuristics to find solutions or optimal actions in a large or complex search space, such as uninformed search, informed search, local search, adversarial search, and constraint satisfaction.
  - Knowledge-based AI uses explicit and structured representations of knowledge and inference mechanisms to solve problems or provide advice, such as semantic networks, frames, scripts, ontologies, and expert systems.
  - Probabilistic AI uses probability theory and statistics to model and reason about uncertainty, such as Bayesian networks, Markov models, hidden Markov models, and Bayesian inference.
  - Machine learning-based AI uses data and algorithms to learn from experience and improve performance, such as supervised learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, and deep learning.
  - Hybrid AI uses a combination of different AI paradigms to achieve better results or overcome limitations, such as neural-symbolic integration, neuro-fuzzy systems, and multi-agent systems.
