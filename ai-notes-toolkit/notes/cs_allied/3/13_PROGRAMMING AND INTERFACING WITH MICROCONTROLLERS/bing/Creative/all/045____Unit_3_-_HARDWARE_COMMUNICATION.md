# Unit 3 - HARDWARE COMMUNICATION

Hardware communication refers to the process of transferring data or information from one device to another by using physical components and tangible media. Hardware communication can be classified into two types: wired and wireless.

## Wired communication
Wired communication uses cables, wires, or optical fibers to transmit signals between devices. Some examples of wired communication devices are:

- Modem: A device that converts digital signals from a computer to analog signals for transmission over a telephone line, and vice versa .
- Ethernet card: A device that connects a computer to a local area network (LAN) using an Ethernet cable.
- USB cable: A cable that connects a computer to a peripheral device, such as a printer, scanner, or flash drive, using the universal serial bus (USB) protocol.
- HDMI cable: A cable that connects a computer to a monitor, projector, or television, using the high-definition multimedia interface (HDMI) protocol.

## Wireless communication
Wireless communication uses electromagnetic waves, such as radio waves, infrared waves, or microwaves, to transmit signals between devices without cables or wires. Some examples of wireless communication devices are:

- Wi-Fi adapter: A device that allows a computer to connect to a wireless LAN using the wireless fidelity (Wi-Fi) protocol.
- Bluetooth adapter: A device that allows a computer to communicate with other Bluetooth-enabled devices, such as headphones, speakers, or keyboards, using the Bluetooth protocol.
- Cellular modem: A device that allows a computer to access the internet using the cellular network.
- Radio frequency identification (RFID) tag: A device that stores and transmits data using radio frequency waves, such as a smart card or a barcode.

## Hardware communication challenges
Hardware communication faces some challenges, such as:

- Compatibility: Different devices may use different protocols, standards, or formats for communication, which may cause incompatibility or interoperability issues.
- Security: Hardware communication may be vulnerable to unauthorized access, interception, or modification by malicious parties, which may compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the data or information.
- Reliability: Hardware communication may be affected by external factors, such as noise, interference, or attenuation, which may degrade the quality or performance of the communication.
