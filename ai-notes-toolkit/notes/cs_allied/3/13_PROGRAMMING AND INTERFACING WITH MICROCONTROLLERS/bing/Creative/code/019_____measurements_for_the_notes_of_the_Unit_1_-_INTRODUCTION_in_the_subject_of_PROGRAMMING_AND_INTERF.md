### Measurements
- Measurements are the process of obtaining quantitative information about physical quantities, such as length, mass, time, voltage, current, etc.
- Measurements are essential for scientific and engineering applications, as they allow us to describe, analyze, and control physical phenomena and systems.
- Measurements involve three main components: a **measuring instrument**, a **measurement standard**, and a **measurement unit**.

#### Measuring instrument
- A measuring instrument is a device that converts a physical quantity into a numerical value that can be displayed, recorded, or transmitted.
- Examples of measuring instruments are rulers, scales, thermometers, clocks, multimeters, oscilloscopes, etc.
- Measuring instruments have different characteristics, such as **accuracy**, **precision**, **resolution**, **range**, **sensitivity**, **linearity**, **repeatability**, **reproducibility**, etc.
- Accuracy is the degree of closeness of a measured value to the true value of the quantity.
- Precision is the degree of consistency or agreement among repeated measurements of the same quantity.
- Resolution is the smallest change in the quantity that can be detected by the instrument.
- Range is the interval of values that the instrument can measure.
- Sensitivity is the ratio of the change in the output of the instrument to the change in the input quantity.
- Linearity is the degree to which the output of the instrument is proportional to the input quantity.
- Repeatability is the ability of the instrument to give the same output for the same input under the same conditions.
- Reproducibility is the ability of the instrument to give the same output for the same input under different conditions.

#### Measurement standard
- A measurement standard is a physical object or phenomenon that defines a unit of measurement and serves as a reference for comparison of other measurements.
- Examples of measurement standards are the meter, the kilogram, the second, the ampere, the volt, etc.
- Measurement standards are established and maintained by national and international organizations, such as the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM), the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), etc.
- Measurement standards are classified into three categories: **primary standards**, **secondary standards**, and **working standards**.
- Primary standards are the highest quality and most accurate standards that are used to define the units of measurement and calibrate secondary standards.
- Secondary standards are the standards that are calibrated against primary standards and are used to calibrate working standards and measuring instruments.
- Working standards are the standards that are calibrated against secondary standards and are used for routine measurements and quality control.

#### Measurement unit
- A measurement unit is a fixed and agreed upon quantity of a physical quantity that is used as a basis for expressing other quantities of the same kind.
- Examples of measurement units are the meter, the kilogram, the second, the ampere, the volt, etc.
- Measurement units are organized into systems of units, such as the International System of Units (SI), the British Imperial System, the US Customary System, etc.
- The SI system is the most widely used and accepted system of units in the world. It consists of seven base units and several derived units.
- The seven base units of the SI system are the meter (m) for length, the kilogram (kg) for mass, the second (s) for time, the ampere (A) for electric current, the kelvin (K) for thermodynamic temperature, the mole (mol) for amount of substance, and the candela (cd) for luminous intensity.
- The derived units of the SI system are the units that are obtained by combining the base units using algebraic expressions, such as the newton (N) for force, the joule (J) for energy, the watt (W) for power, etc.
- The SI system also allows the use of prefixes to indicate multiples or fractions of the base units, such as the kilo (k) for 10^3, the milli (m) for 10^-3, the micro (µ) for 10^-6, etc.
