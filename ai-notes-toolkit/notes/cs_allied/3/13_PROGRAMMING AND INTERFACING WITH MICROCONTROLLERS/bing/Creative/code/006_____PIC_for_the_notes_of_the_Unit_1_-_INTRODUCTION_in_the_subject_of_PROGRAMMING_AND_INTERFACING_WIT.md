### PIC
- PIC stands for Peripheral Interface Controller, a family of microcontrollers developed by Microchip Technology.
- PIC microcontrollers are popular for embedded applications due to their low cost, wide availability, large user base, extensive collection of application notes, availability of low cost or free development tools, and serial programming capability.
- PIC microcontrollers have a RISC (Reduced Instruction Set Computer) architecture, which means they have a small and simple instruction set that can be executed in one or two clock cycles.
- PIC microcontrollers have a Harvard architecture, which means they have separate program and data memories, allowing faster access and more efficient use of memory space.
- PIC microcontrollers have various features, such as internal oscillators, timers, watchdogs, analog-to-digital converters, comparators, serial and parallel interfaces, PWM modules, capture/compare modules, and interrupt capabilities.
- PIC microcontrollers are classified into four families based on their architecture and memory size: PIC10, PIC12, PIC16, and PIC18. Each family has different variants and models with different specifications and capabilities.
