### Representing “reality” with computers

- Computers are devices that can process and store information in binary form, using sequences of 0s and 1s.
- To represent reality with computers, we need to find ways to convert real-world phenomena into binary data, and vice versa.
- This process involves several levels of abstraction and modeling, such as:
  - Physical conversion of input signals into digital signals (as well as converting digital signals into an output signal)
  - Encoding schemes for representing information in binary, such as ASCII, Unicode, JPEG, MP3, etc.
  - Algorithmic modeling of physical processes, such as simulation, animation, optimization, etc.
- Each level of modeling has its own advantages and limitations, such as:
  - Accuracy: how closely the model matches the reality
  - Efficiency: how fast and how much resources the model requires
  - Flexibility: how easily the model can be modified or adapted
  - Generality: how widely the model can be applied to different situations
  - Interpretability: how understandable the model is to humans
- Therefore, representing reality with computers is a trade-off between these factors, and depends on the purpose and context of the model.
