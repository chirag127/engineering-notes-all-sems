### ADC for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

- ADC stands for Analog to Digital Converter. It is a hardware device that converts analog signals to digital signals.
- The microcontroller can only understand digital data in the form of 0s and 1s. So, the analog signals from the real world have to be converted to digital signals before they can be processed by the microcontroller.
- The ADC converts the analog voltage to a corresponding digital number by sampling and quantizing.
- The resolution of the ADC determines the precision of the conversion. A higher resolution results in a more precise digital representation of the analog input.
- The ADC has an input voltage range and it converts values within this range to digital numbers. The values outside this range will be saturated to the maximum or minimum digital number.
- The microcontroller has an in-built ADC with a defined resolution and input voltage range. But, external ADCs with higher resolution and ranges can also be interfaced with the microcontroller.
- The software frameworks in the microcontroller have functions to initialize, trigger, and read the values from the ADC. These functions are used to interface the microcontroller with the analog world through the ADC.
