### Circuit Theory

1. Circuit: A complete path that allows electric charge to flow is known as an electric circuit. It consists of a voltage source, wires that provide a path and components that control the flow of charge.
2. Voltage Source: A device that provides electrical pressure to drive charge carriers through a circuit. Eg. batteries, power supplies, etc. It is measured in volts (V).
3. Current: The rate of flow of charge carriers through a circuit. It is measured in amperes (A).
4. Resistance: It opposes the flow of current and converts electrical energy into heat. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
5. Ohm's Law: States that current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage applied across it, as long as the temperature and other physical conditions remain constant. It is represented by the equation V=IR, where V is voltage, I is current and R is resistance.
6. Series and Parallel Circuits:
    - Series Circuit: Components are connected end-to-end. Same current flows through all components. Total resistance is equal to the sum of individual resistances.
    - Parallel Circuit: Components are connected across each other. The voltage across each component is the same. Total current is equal to the sum of currents through each branch and total resistance is less than any individual resistance.
