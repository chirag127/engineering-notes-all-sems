### DAC for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE FRAMEWORKS

1. DAC stands for Digital to Analog Converter. It is a system that converts a digital signal to an analog signal.
2. The microcontroller works on digital signals but the real world devices work on analog signals. So, a DAC is required to interface microcontrollers with analog devices like motors, speakers, sensors, etc.
3. The DAC takes a digital data and converts it into a continuous range of analog voltage levels. The resolution of a DAC defines the number of discrete voltage levels it can produce. Higher resolution leads to more accurate analog output.
4. The DAC has two main components:
- Decoder: It decodes the digital data and provides outputs to the resistor network.
- Resistor Network: It consists of resistors connected in a ladder network. The decoded outputs control the switches that control the flow of current through the resistors producing an analog voltage output.
5. Types of DAC:
- Binary weighted resistor DAC: Uses resistors of binary weighted values.
- R-2R ladder network DAC: Uses a ladder network of R-2R resistors. It is more accurate and efficient.
- Switched capacitor DAC: Uses a network of capacitors instead of resistors. It provides fast conversion and better linearity.
6. Applications of DAC:
- Audio systems
- Medical devices
- Process controls
- Automotive electronics
- Test and measurement equipment
