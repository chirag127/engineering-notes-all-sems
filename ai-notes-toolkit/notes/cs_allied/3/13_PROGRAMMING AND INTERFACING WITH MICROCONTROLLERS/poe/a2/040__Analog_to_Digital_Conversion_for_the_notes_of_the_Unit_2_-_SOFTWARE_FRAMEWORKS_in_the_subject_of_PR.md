### Analog to Digital Conversion

- Analog signals are continuous electrical signals that represent physical quantities like temperature, pressure, sound etc.
- Microcontrollers can only understand digital signals which are discrete values.
- Hence, analog signals need to be converted to digital signals before they can be processed by microcontrollers. This conversion is done using Analog to Digital Converters (ADCs).
- An ADC converts the analog signal to a corresponding digital number by sampling and quantizing the analog signal.
- The resolution of an ADC specifies the number of discrete values it can produce. Higher resolution leads to more accurate digital representation of the analog signal.
- The sampling rate refers to how frequently the ADC samples the analog signal. It must be at least twice the bandwidth of the analog signal as per the Nyquist sampling theorem.
- There are different types of ADCs like successive approximation, flash, sigma-delta, etc. The choice of ADC depends on resolution, speed, and accuracy requirements.
- ADCs are commonly used to read sensors that output analog signals like temperature, pressure, light intensity sensors, etc. The digital value can then be processed by the microcontroller.
