
### Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) 

I2C is a serial communication protocol that is used to connect devices to a microcontroller. It is widely used in embedded systems due to its low cost, small footprint, and ease of use.

- I2C is a two-wire serial communication protocol, with one wire used for data (SDA) and the other for clock (SCL). 
- It supports multi-master and multi-slave communication, meaning that multiple devices can be connected to the same bus. 
- It is capable of transferring data at speeds of up to 400 kbps.
- It is a synchronous protocol, meaning that the clock signal is used to synchronize data transfer.
- I2C devices have an address, which is used to identify them on the bus.
- I2C is used in many applications, such as sensors, LCD displays, and EEPROMs.
