
### I2C/IIC

I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit) is a serial communication protocol used for connecting electronic devices. It is used to communicate between microcontrollers, sensors, and other electronic components. It is also known as IIC (Inter-Integrated Circuit).

I2C is a two-wire protocol, meaning it uses two wires for communication: a clock (SCL) and a data (SDA) line. The clock line is used to synchronize data transfer between the two devices, while the data line is used to transfer the actual data.

I2C is used in a wide variety of applications, including embedded systems, automotive, medical, and industrial control. It is a low-cost, low-power protocol that can be used for short-distance communication.

The main advantages of I2C are its low cost, low power consumption, and ease of use. It is also a multi-master protocol, meaning multiple devices can be connected on the same bus.

I2C is a synchronous protocol, meaning the devices on the bus must be synchronized to the same clock signal. The clock signal is generated by the master device and is used to synchronize data transfer between the master and the slave devices.

I2C also supports multiple slave devices on the same bus. Each slave device must have a unique address, which is used by the master device to identify the slave device.

I2C is a simple and reliable protocol, and is widely used in embedded systems. It is also used in many other applications, such as automotive, medical, and industrial control.
