### Toward Web Enhanced Building Automation Systems

- Building automation systems (BAS) are systems that control and monitor the mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems in a building, such as heating, ventilation, air conditioning, lighting, security, fire alarms, etc.
- BAS can improve the comfort, safety and energy efficiency of buildings, as well as reduce the operational and maintenance costs.
- However, traditional BAS are often proprietary, isolated and incompatible with other systems and devices, which limits their interoperability, scalability and flexibility.
- Web enhanced building automation systems (WEBAS) are systems that leverage the web technologies and standards to enable the integration, communication and collaboration of BAS with other systems and devices, such as sensors, actuators, smart meters, smart appliances, etc.
- WEBAS can provide more functionalities, such as machine-to-machine communications, data analytics, remote control, user feedback, etc., and support the Internet-of-Things (IoT) paradigms, which aim to connect objects to the Internet and provide services to users and applications.
- WEBAS can also benefit from the web services and cloud computing, which can offer more resources, capabilities and functionalities to the BAS, such as data storage, processing, visualization, etc.
- Some of the challenges and requirements for WEBAS are:
  - Discovery and query: WEBAS need to be able to discover and query the available systems and devices in the building and on the web, and provide relevant information to the users and applications.
  - Security and privacy: WEBAS need to ensure the security and privacy of the data and communications, and prevent unauthorized access and attacks.
  - Interoperability and standardization: WEBAS need to adopt common protocols, formats and interfaces to enable the interoperability and compatibility of different systems and devices, and follow the web standards and best practices.
  - Performance and reliability: WEBAS need to provide high performance and reliability, and cope with the heterogeneity, complexity and dynamics of the systems and devices.
  - User interface and experience: WEBAS need to provide user-friendly and intuitive interfaces and experiences, and allow the users to interact with and control the systems and devices.
- Some of the examples of WEBAS are:
  - WebCTRL: A web-based platform that provides facility managers with software tools to keep occupants comfortable, manage energy conservation measures, identify key operational problems, and analyze the results.
  - APOGEE: A building automation system that enables the most cost-effective path to the latest technology to keep buildings upgraded and optimized, and provides flexible solutions for control, management, reporting and analytics.
  - BMS: A building management solution that uses a range of controllers, field devices and software to customize a system to fit the building and the future, and provides smart building performance.
