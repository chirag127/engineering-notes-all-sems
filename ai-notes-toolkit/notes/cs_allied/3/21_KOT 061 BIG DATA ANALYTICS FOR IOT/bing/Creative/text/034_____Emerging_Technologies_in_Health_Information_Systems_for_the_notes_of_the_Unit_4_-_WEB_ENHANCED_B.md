### Emerging Technologies in Health Information Systems

- Health information systems (HIS) are systems that collect, store, process, and communicate health-related data for decision-making, planning, and management of health services and programs.
- Emerging technologies are new or evolving technologies that have the potential to transform or disrupt existing practices, markets, or industries.
- Some of the emerging technologies in HIS are:

  - **Connected health devices**: These are devices that can monitor, measure, or transmit health data from patients to providers, caregivers, or other stakeholders. Examples include wearable sensors, smart watches, implantable devices, and remote patient monitoring systems. These devices can enable real-time data collection, personalized feedback, and improved patient engagement and adherence.
  - **Digital therapeutics**: These are software-based interventions that can deliver evidence-based therapies or treatments for various health conditions. Examples include apps, games, or online platforms that can help patients manage chronic diseases, mental health issues, or substance use disorders. These interventions can complement or replace traditional therapies, improve access and affordability, and enhance outcomes and quality of life.
  - **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: This is the technology that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, decision-making, or natural language processing. Examples include chatbots, voice assistants, image recognition, natural language generation, and machine learning. AI can help automate and optimize various processes in HIS, such as diagnosis, triage, treatment, documentation, billing, and research.
  - **Telehealth**: This is the delivery of health care services and information using telecommunications technologies, such as phone, video, or internet. Examples include teleconsultation, telemonitoring, telediagnosis, teletherapy, and teleeducation. Telehealth can increase access and convenience, reduce costs and travel time, and improve patient satisfaction and outcomes.
  - **Virtual reality (VR)**: This is the technology that can create immersive and interactive simulations of real or imagined environments using computer-generated images, sounds, and motions. Examples include headsets, gloves, or controllers that can provide sensory feedback and user interaction. VR can be used for various purposes in HIS, such as education, training, therapy, rehabilitation, pain management, and entertainment.

- These emerging technologies can offer various benefits for HIS, such as:

  - Enhancing the quality, efficiency, and effectiveness of health care delivery and management.
  - Empowering patients and providers with more information, choices, and control over their health and well-being.
  - Improving the accessibility, affordability, and equity of health care services and programs.
  - Supporting the prevention, detection, and treatment of various health conditions and diseases.
  - Facilitating the innovation, collaboration, and learning among health care stakeholders and communities.

- However, these emerging technologies also pose various challenges and risks for HIS, such as:

  - Ensuring the safety, security, privacy, and ethics of health data and systems.
  - Addressing the technical, regulatory, and legal barriers and standards for the adoption and integration of new technologies.
  - Evaluating the effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, and impact of new technologies on health outcomes and behaviors.
  - Managing the change, resistance, and expectations of health care stakeholders and users.
  - Developing the skills, competencies, and capacities of health care workforce and leaders.
