### Unit 3 - FOG COMPUTING

- Fog computing is a decentralized computing infrastructure that uses edge devices to carry out computation, storage, and communication locally and routed over the Internet backbone .
- Fog computing is also known as edge computing or fogging .
- Fog computing is intended for distributed computing where numerous "peripheral" devices connect to a cloud.
- Fog computing brings the advantages and power of the cloud closer to where data is created and acted upon.
- Fog computing is more energy-efficient than cloud computing.
- Fog computing is a medium weight and intermediate level of computing power.
- Fog computing often serves as a complement to cloud computing, rather than a substitute.
- Fog computing can improve the performance, security, and reliability of IoT applications by reducing latency, bandwidth, and data processing costs  .
- Fog computing can also enable real-time analytics, local decision making, and context-aware services .
- Fog computing requires a standardization of protocols, interfaces, and architectures to ensure interoperability and scalability .
- IEEE adopted the fog computing standards proposed by OpenFog Consortium.
- Some of the challenges and issues of fog computing are resource management, security and privacy, quality of service, and network heterogeneity .
