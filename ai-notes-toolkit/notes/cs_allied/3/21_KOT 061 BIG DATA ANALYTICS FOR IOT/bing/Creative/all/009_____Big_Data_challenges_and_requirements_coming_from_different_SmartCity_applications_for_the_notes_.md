# Big Data Challenges and Requirements Coming from Different Smart City Applications

- A smart city is a city that uses information and communication technologies (ICT) to enhance the quality of life, efficiency of urban services, and sustainability of the environment .
- A smart city generates and consumes large amounts of data from various sources, such as sensors, cameras, mobile devices, social media, and open data platforms .
- Big data is a term that refers to the collection, processing, analysis, and visualization of large and complex data sets that exceed the capabilities of traditional data management systems .
- Big data can enable smart city applications to provide better services, optimize resources, improve decision making, and foster innovation .
- However, big data also poses many challenges and requirements for smart city applications, such as:

  - Data quality: The data collected from various sources may be incomplete, inconsistent, inaccurate, noisy, or outdated, which can affect the reliability and validity of the analysis and results . Therefore, smart city applications need to ensure the quality of the data by applying techniques such as data cleaning, integration, validation, and verification .
  - Data security and privacy: The data collected from various sources may contain sensitive or personal information that can be exploited by malicious actors or exposed by unauthorized access . Therefore, smart city applications need to ensure the security and privacy of the data by applying techniques such as encryption, anonymization, authentication, and authorization .
  - Data storage and processing: The data collected from various sources may be heterogeneous, unstructured, or streaming, which can pose challenges for storing and processing the data efficiently and effectively . Therefore, smart city applications need to leverage technologies such as cloud computing, edge computing, and distributed systems to store and process the data in a scalable, flexible, and cost-effective manner .
  - Data analysis and visualization: The data collected from various sources may be complex, multidimensional, or dynamic, which can pose challenges for analyzing and visualizing the data in a meaningful and actionable way . Therefore, smart city applications need to leverage technologies such as artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data mining to analyze and visualize the data in a smart, adaptive, and interactive manner .
  - Data sharing and governance: The data collected from various sources may be owned or controlled by different stakeholders, such as the city, the citizens, the service providers, or the regulators, which can pose challenges for sharing and governing the data in a transparent and accountable way. Therefore, smart city applications need to establish policies and mechanisms for data sharing and governance that balance the interests and rights of the stakeholders, such as data ownership, access, usage, and monetization.
