### Self-healing systems for the notes of the Unit 2 - RFID False Authentications

- Self-healing systems are systems that can detect and correct faults or errors in their operation without human intervention.
- RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is a technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as products, animals, or people.
- RFID false authentication is a type of attack that exploits the weaknesses of RFID systems to impersonate or clone legitimate RFID tags or readers, or to modify or tamper with the data transmitted by them.
- RFID false authentication can have serious consequences, especially in healthcare, where RFID is used to monitor patients, track medical equipment, and ensure patient safety and quality of care.
- Some of the challenges and barriers to RFID technology in healthcare are:
  - Interference: RFID readers can provide false reads caused by interference in the electromagnetic field by other medical equipment, metallic objects, liquid, glass, and moist environments.
  - Read rates and accuracy: The read rates and accuracy of RFID systems depend on the type, size, orientation, and placement of the RFID tags and readers, as well as the environmental conditions and the presence of other RFID systems.
  - Security and privacy: RFID systems are vulnerable to eavesdropping, spoofing, jamming, replay, and denial-of-service attacks, as well as unauthorized access, modification, or deletion of the data stored or transmitted by the RFID tags and readers.
  - Cost and complexity: RFID systems require a significant investment in hardware, software, and infrastructure, as well as maintenance and management. They also need to be integrated with other information systems and comply with standards and regulations.
- Self-healing systems can help to overcome some of these challenges and barriers by :
  - Detecting and diagnosing faults or errors in the RFID systems, such as hardware failures, software bugs, network congestion, or malicious attacks.
  - Isolating and containing the faulty or erroneous components or processes, and preventing them from affecting the rest of the system or causing further damage.
  - Recovering and restoring the normal operation of the RFID systems, by repairing, replacing, or reconfiguring the faulty or erroneous components or processes, or by switching to alternative or backup modes or resources.
  - Learning and adapting to the changing conditions and requirements of the RFID systems, by updating, optimizing, or reconfiguring the system parameters, policies, or rules, or by applying feedback or reinforcement mechanisms.
