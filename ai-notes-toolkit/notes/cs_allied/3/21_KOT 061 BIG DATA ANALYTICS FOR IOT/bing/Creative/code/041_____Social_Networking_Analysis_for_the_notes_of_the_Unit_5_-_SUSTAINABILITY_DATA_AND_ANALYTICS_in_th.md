### Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA measures and maps the flow of relationships and relationship changes between knowledge-possessing entities.
- SNA is based on theoretical constructs of sociology and mathematical foundations of graph theory.
- SNA is useful for examining the social structure and interdependencies (or work patterns) of individuals or organizations.
- SNA can be applied to various domains, such as health, education, business, politics, and the internet.
- SNA can help to identify influential actors, key connectors, subgroups, communities, and network properties, such as density, centrality, and cohesion.
- SNA can also help to understand how information, resources, behaviors, and opinions spread through networks, and how network interventions can affect network outcomes.
- SNA requires data collection, network representation, network analysis, and network visualization.
- SNA can use different types of data, such as surveys, interviews, observations, digital traces, and archival records.
- SNA can represent networks using nodes (entities) and ties (relationships) in various forms, such as matrices, graphs, or sociograms.
- SNA can analyze networks using various methods, such as descriptive statistics, network models, network algorithms, and network simulations.
- SNA can visualize networks using various tools, such as graphs, maps, charts, or animations.
