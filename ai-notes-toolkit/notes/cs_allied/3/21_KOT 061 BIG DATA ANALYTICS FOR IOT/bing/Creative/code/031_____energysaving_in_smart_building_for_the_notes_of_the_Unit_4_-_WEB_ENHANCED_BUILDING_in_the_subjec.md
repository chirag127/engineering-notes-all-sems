### Energy saving in smart building

- A smart building is a building that uses modern technologies such as sensors, IoT devices, automation, and data analytics to optimize its performance, comfort, safety, and sustainability.
- Smart buildings can improve energy efficiency by reducing energy consumption, incorporating renewable energy sources, managing office space, maximizing heating and cooling, recycling waste, and reducing travel footprint    .
- Some examples of energy saving technologies in smart buildings are:
  - Smart HVAC systems that adjust the temperature and ventilation according to the occupancy, weather, and preferences of the occupants  .
  - Smart lighting that dims or turns off the lights when there is enough natural light or no occupancy  .
  - Smart windows that change their transparency or tint to control the amount of heat and light entering the building .
  - Smart meters that monitor and display the energy consumption and cost of the building and its appliances  .
  - Smart grids that connect the building to the public network and allow it to sell or buy electricity depending on the demand and supply .
  - Photovoltaic panels or solar water heaters that generate electricity or hot water from the sun .
  - Smart waste management systems that sort and recycle the waste generated by the building .
  - Smart mobility solutions that reduce the need for travel or use low-carbon modes of transportation .
- Smart buildings can save up to 50% of a building’s energy consumption and lower their carbon footprint .
- Smart buildings can also improve the occupant comfort, productivity, health, and safety by providing optimal indoor environment quality, personalized settings, real-time feedback, and emergency alerts  .
