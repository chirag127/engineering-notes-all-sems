### Emerging Technologies in Health Information Systems

- Health information systems (HIS) are systems that collect, store, process, and communicate health-related data for decision-making, planning, and management of health services and programs.
- Emerging technologies are new or evolving technologies that have the potential to transform or disrupt existing practices, processes, or systems in various domains, including health care.
- Some of the emerging technologies that are influencing HIS are:

  - **Connected health devices**: These are devices that can monitor, measure, or transmit health data from patients to providers, caregivers, or other stakeholders, using wireless or wired connections. Examples include wearable sensors, smart watches, blood pressure monitors, glucose meters, etc. These devices can enable remote patient monitoring, chronic disease management, and personalized care.
  - **Digital therapeutics**: These are software-based interventions that can deliver evidence-based therapies or treatments for various health conditions, such as mental health, diabetes, obesity, etc. Examples include mobile apps, web platforms, chatbots, etc. These interventions can complement or replace traditional therapies, improve patient engagement and adherence, and reduce costs and side effects.
  - **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: This is the technology that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, decision-making, etc. Examples include machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, etc. AI can enhance HIS by providing data analysis, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment recommendations, clinical decision support, etc.
  - **Telehealth**: This is the delivery of health care services and information using telecommunication technologies, such as phone, video, email, etc. Examples include teleconsultation, telemedicine, telediagnosis, telemonitoring, etc. Telehealth can improve access to care, especially for rural and remote areas, reduce travel and waiting time, and lower costs and risks of infection.
  - **Virtual reality (VR)**: This is the technology that can create immersive and interactive simulations of real or imagined environments, using devices such as headsets, gloves, etc. Examples include VR games, VR therapy, VR training, etc. VR can enhance HIS by providing education, training, rehabilitation, pain management, distraction, etc.

- These emerging technologies can offer various benefits for HIS, such as:

  - Improving the quality, efficiency, and effectiveness of health care delivery and outcomes.
  - Empowering patients and providers with more information, choices, and control over their health and care.
  - Reducing the costs, errors, and disparities in health care provision and utilization.
  - Enabling innovation, collaboration, and integration across different levels and sectors of health care.

- However, these emerging technologies also pose various challenges and risks for HIS, such as:

  - Ensuring the reliability, validity, and security of the data and devices.
  - Protecting the privacy, confidentiality, and consent of the users and stakeholders.
  - Addressing the ethical, legal, and social implications and responsibilities of the technologies and their applications.
  - Developing the standards, regulations, and policies to govern the technologies and their use.
  - Enhancing the skills, competencies, and readiness of the health workforce and the public to adopt and use the technologies.

- Therefore, it is important to evaluate, monitor, and manage the emerging technologies and their impact on HIS, using a multidisciplinary and participatory approach, involving various stakeholders, such as patients, providers, policymakers, researchers, developers, etc.
