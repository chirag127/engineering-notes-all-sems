### YA-TRAP

- YA-TRAP is a reader/tag authentication protocol for RFID systems  .
- It aims to prevent unauthorized readers from accessing tag data and unauthorized tags from impersonating legitimate ones  .
- It uses a hash function and a pseudo-random number generator to generate challenges and responses between the reader and the tag  .
- It consists of four steps:  
  - Step 1: Reader -> Tag: T_r, where T_r is a random number generated by the reader.
  - Step 2: Tag -> Reader: T_t, where T_t is a random number generated by the tag.
  - Step 3: Tag -> Reader: H_r, where H_r is the hash of T_r, T_t and the tag's secret key K_t.
  - Step 4: Reader -> Tag: H_t, where H_t is the hash of T_r, T_t and the reader's secret key K_r.
- The reader and the tag authenticate each other by verifying the received hashes  .
- However, YA-TRAP may suffer from false authentications, which means that a legitimate tag could be wrongly rejected by a reader  .
- This may happen when the tag's memory is corrupted by external factors, such as noise, interference, or power loss  .
- If the tag's memory is corrupted, it may not be able to generate or store the correct values of T_t, H_r, or K_t  .
- As a result, the tag may send an incorrect response H_r to the reader, or fail to verify the reader's response H_t  .
- This may lead to the reader rejecting the tag as an unauthorized one, even though the tag is legitimate  .
- False authentications may cause serious problems for RFID applications, such as inventory management, access control, or payment systems  .
- Therefore, it is important to design RFID authentication protocols that can prevent or reduce false authentications  .
