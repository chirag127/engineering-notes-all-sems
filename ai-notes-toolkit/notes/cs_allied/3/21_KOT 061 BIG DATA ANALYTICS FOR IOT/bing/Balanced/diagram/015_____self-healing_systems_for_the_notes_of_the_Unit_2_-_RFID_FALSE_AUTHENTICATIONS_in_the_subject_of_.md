### Self-healing systems for the notes of the Unit 2 - RFID False Authentication in the subject of KOT 061 Big Data Analytics for IoT KCS

- Self-healing systems are systems that can detect and correct faults or errors automatically without human intervention.
- Self-healing systems can improve the reliability, availability, and security of IT systems, especially in complex and dynamic environments such as IoT.
- RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is a technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as products, assets, or people.
- RFID systems consist of RFID tags, which are attached to the objects, and RFID readers, which communicate with the tags and collect their data.
- RFID false authentication is a type of attack that exploits the vulnerabilities of RFID systems, such as weak encryption, lack of authentication, or poor design, to impersonate or clone RFID tags, or to eavesdrop or modify the data transmitted between the tags and the readers.
- RFID false authentication can compromise the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of the RFID data, and cause serious consequences, such as theft, fraud, privacy breach, or sabotage.
- Self-healing systems can help prevent or mitigate RFID false authentication by applying various techniques, such as:

  - Monitoring and analyzing the RFID data and events to detect anomalies or suspicious activities, such as unexpected tag readings, duplicate tags, or data tampering.
  - Isolating or blocking the affected RFID tags or readers to prevent further damage or propagation of the attack.
  - Recovering or restoring the normal operation of the RFID system by replacing or reconfiguring the faulty or compromised RFID tags or readers, or by applying patches or updates to fix the vulnerabilities.
  - Learning and adapting to the changing RFID environment and threats by updating the self-healing rules or policies, or by using machine learning or artificial intelligence to improve the self-healing capabilities.
