# Big Data Challenges and Requirements Coming from Different Smart City Applications

- A smart city is a city that uses information and communication technologies (ICT) to enhance the quality of life, efficiency of urban services, and sustainability of the environment.
- A smart city generates and consumes large amounts of data from various sources, such as sensors, cameras, mobile devices, social media, and open data platforms.
- Big data is a term that refers to the collection, processing, analysis, and visualization of large and complex data sets that exceed the capabilities of traditional data management systems.
- Big data can provide valuable insights and solutions for various smart city applications, such as transportation, energy, health, education, security, and governance.
- However, big data also poses many challenges and requirements for smart city applications, such as:

  - Data quality: The data collected from different sources may be incomplete, inconsistent, noisy, or inaccurate, which affects the reliability and validity of the analysis and decision making.
  - Data integration: The data from different sources may have different formats, structures, semantics, and granularities, which requires effective methods to integrate and harmonize the data for a unified view.
  - Data security and privacy: The data collected from smart city applications may contain sensitive or personal information, which requires appropriate mechanisms to protect the data from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure.
  - Data storage and processing: The data generated and consumed by smart city applications may be massive, heterogeneous, and dynamic, which requires scalable and efficient systems to store and process the data in a timely manner.
  - Data analysis and visualization: The data from smart city applications may be complex, multidimensional, and high-dimensional, which requires advanced techniques to extract meaningful patterns, trends, and insights from the data and present them in an intuitive and interactive way.
  - Data governance and ethics: The data from smart city applications may have social, economic, and environmental impacts, which requires clear and transparent policies and regulations to ensure the ethical and responsible use of the data for the public good.
