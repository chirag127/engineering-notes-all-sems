# Building a useful understanding of a social network

- A social network is a set of actors (such as individuals, groups, or organizations) and the relationships or interactions among them.
- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA can help to understand how social networks influence health, behavior, communication, innovation, and other outcomes.
- SNA can also be applied to networks outside of the societal realm, such as the web, biological systems, or transportation networks.
- To conduct SNA, one needs to collect data on the nodes (actors) and the ties (relationships) of a network, and represent them as a graph or a matrix.
- SNA can use various methods and metrics to analyze the properties and patterns of a network, such as centrality, density, clustering, reciprocity, homophily, and community detection.
- SNA can provide useful insights for designing interventions, evaluating policies, or optimizing network performance.
- SNA can also help to identify the key actors, groups, or positions in a network, and how they affect the network structure and function.
- SNA can be performed using various tools and software, such as UCINET, Gephi, R, or Python.
- SNA is an interdisciplinary and dynamic field that can benefit from the advances in big data, machine learning, and complex systems.
