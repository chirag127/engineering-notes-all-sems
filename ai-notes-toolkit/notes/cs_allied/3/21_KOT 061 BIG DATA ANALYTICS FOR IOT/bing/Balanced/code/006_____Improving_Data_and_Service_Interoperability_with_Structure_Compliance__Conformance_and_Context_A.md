### Improving Data and Service Interoperability with Structure, Compliance, Conformance and Context Awareness

- Interoperability is the ability of different systems, devices, or applications to exchange and use data or services without requiring human intervention or extra effort.
- Interoperability is essential for the Internet of Things (IoT), which involves a large number of heterogeneous and distributed entities that need to communicate and cooperate to achieve common goals.
- Interoperability can be achieved at different levels, such as syntactic, semantic, or pragmatic.
- Syntactic interoperability refers to the common format and structure of the data or messages exchanged.
- Semantic interoperability refers to the common meaning and interpretation of the data or messages exchanged.
- Pragmatic interoperability refers to the common understanding and expectations of the behavior and functionality of the services or applications exchanged.
- Interoperability can be improved by using the following concepts:
  - Structure: the organization and representation of the data or services, which can be based on standards, schemas, or formats that facilitate the exchange and processing of information.
  - Compliance: the adherence to a set of rules or specifications that define the structure, syntax, or semantics of the data or services, which can be verified by validation or verification mechanisms.
  - Conformance: the degree of similarity or compatibility between the data or services and a given standard, schema, or format, which can be measured by metrics or indicators.
  - Context awareness: the ability to adapt the data or services to the specific situation, environment, or preferences of the users or applications, which can be achieved by using contextual information or metadata.
- This chapter presents an architectural style, Structural Services, which combines the service modelling capabilities of Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) with the flexibility of structured resources in Representational State Transfer (REST).
- Structural Services aim to enhance interoperability, at the data and service levels, by using the following features:
  - Partial interoperability: the possibility of achieving interoperability even when the data or services do not fully comply or conform to a given standard, schema, or format, by using compliance and conformance levels that indicate the degree of interoperability achieved.
  - Context model: the representation and management of the contextual information that influences the data or services, which includes forward and backward context, that is, the context that precedes and follows the data or service exchange.
- Structural Services can address some of the challenges of IoT, such as heterogeneity, scalability, or dynamism, by using compliance and conformance (less restrictive than schema sharing) and context awareness (which increases the range of applicability).
