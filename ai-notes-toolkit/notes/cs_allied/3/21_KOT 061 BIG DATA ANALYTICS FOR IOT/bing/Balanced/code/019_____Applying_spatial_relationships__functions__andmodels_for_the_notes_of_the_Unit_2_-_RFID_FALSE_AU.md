# Applying spatial relationships, functions, and models for the notes of the Unit 2 - RFID FALSE AUTHENTICATIONS in the subject of KOT 061 BIG DATA ANALYTICS FOR IOT KCS

- Spatial relationships, functions, and models are methods of data analytics that are used to analyze geographic patterns and determine the spatial relationship between physical objects or events.
- Spatial data are the common thread for data and information that is keyed or tagged based on space and time, such as location, distance, direction, and movement.
- Spatial data can be collected from various sources, such as sensors, RFID tags, GPS devices, social media feeds, and IoT systems.
- Spatial data can be stored, processed, and visualized using distributed architectures, such as cloud computing, big data frameworks, and geospatial platforms.
- Spatial data can be used to perform various types of analysis, such as:
  - Descriptive analysis: to summarize and display the characteristics and distribution of spatial data, such as heat maps, density maps, and choropleth maps.
  - Exploratory analysis: to discover and identify patterns, clusters, outliers, and trends in spatial data, such as spatial autocorrelation, spatial regression, and spatial interpolation.
  - Predictive analysis: to forecast and estimate future outcomes and scenarios based on spatial data, such as spatial machine learning, spatial simulation, and spatial optimization.
  - Prescriptive analysis: to recommend and optimize actions and decisions based on spatial data, such as spatial decision support systems, spatial multi-criteria analysis, and spatial optimization.
- RFID false authentication is a type of attack that aims to impersonate or clone a legitimate RFID tag or reader, or to modify or forge the data transmitted between them.
- RFID false authentication can pose serious threats to the security and privacy of IoT applications, such as inventory management, asset tracking, access control, and smart payment.
- RFID false authentication can be prevented or detected by using various techniques, such as:
  - Cryptographic techniques: to encrypt and authenticate the data exchanged between RFID tags and readers, such as symmetric-key encryption, public-key encryption, hash functions, and digital signatures.
  - Physical techniques: to enhance the security and robustness of RFID tags and readers, such as physical unclonable functions, tamper-resistant hardware, and distance-bounding protocols.
  - Statistical techniques: to analyze and monitor the behavior and performance of RFID systems, such as anomaly detection, data mining, and machine learning.
