### Applying spatial relationships, functions, and models for the notes of the Unit 2 - RFID FALSE AUTHENTICATIONS in the subject of KOT 061 BIG DATA ANALYTICS FOR IOT KCS

- Spatial relationships, functions, and models are methods of data analytics that are used to analyze geographic patterns and determine the spatial relationship between physical objects, such as RFID tags and readers.
- RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is a technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as products, vehicles, or people, by attaching a small electronic device called a tag to them. The tag contains a unique identifier that can be read by a device called a reader, which can be fixed or mobile.
- RFID false authentication is a type of attack that aims to impersonate a legitimate tag or reader, or to modify or forge the data exchanged between them, in order to gain unauthorized access, steal information, or cause damage.
- Big data analytics for IoT (Internet of Things) is the process of collecting, storing, processing, and analyzing large volumes of data generated by various sensors and devices connected to the internet, such as RFID systems, in order to extract useful insights and enable smart applications.
- Spatial analysis and data science are key components of big data analytics for IoT, as they can help to transform massive spatial data into manageable information, ingest real-time data from sensors and IoT systems, display and visualize data on maps, and apply machine learning and artificial intelligence techniques to discover patterns, relationships, anomalies, and incidents in spatial data.
- Some examples of spatial analysis and data science applications for RFID false authentication detection and prevention are:

  - Location-based authentication: This is a technique that verifies the identity and location of a tag or reader by comparing the received signal strength (RSS) or the time of arrival (TOA) of the radio signals with the expected values based on the distance and direction between them. This can help to detect and reject false tags or readers that are too far away or in the wrong direction.
  - Clustering analysis: This is a technique that groups similar tags or readers based on their spatial and temporal attributes, such as location, movement, frequency, or duration of communication. This can help to identify and isolate abnormal or suspicious tags or readers that deviate from the normal behavior or pattern of their cluster.
  - Anomaly detection: This is a technique that detects and flags unusual or unexpected events or patterns in the spatial data, such as sudden changes in location, speed, direction, or signal strength of a tag or reader. This can help to alert and investigate potential false authentication attempts or attacks.
