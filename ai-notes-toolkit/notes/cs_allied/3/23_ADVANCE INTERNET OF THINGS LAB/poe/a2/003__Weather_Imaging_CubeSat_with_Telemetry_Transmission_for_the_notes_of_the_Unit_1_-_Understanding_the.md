### Weather Imaging CubeSat with Telemetry Transmission for the notes of the Unit 1 - Understanding the implementation of IOT in the subject of ADVANCE INTERNET OF THINGS LAB.

1. CubeSats are a class of nanosatellites that use a standard size and form factor. The standard CubeSat size uses a "one unit" or "1U" that is 10 cm x 10 cm x 10 cm and a maximum mass of no more than 1.33 kg.

2. CubeSats are used for a variety of space missions including weather monitoring, Earth observation, communications, and technology demonstration.

3. For weather monitoring, CubeSats can carry instruments like cameras, radiometers, and spectrometers to capture weather data and images. The data can be transmitted to Earth via radio signals and received by ground stations for analysis.

4. For this weather imaging CubeSat project, the key components include:
    - Imaging payload: Camera and spectrometer to capture weather data and images
    - On-board computer: To control the CubeSat and imaging payload
    - Communications system: To transmit the data and images to ground stations
    - Power system: Solar panels and batteries to power the CubeSat
    - Structural system: To protect the internal components from launch and space environment

5. The data and images from the weather imaging CubeSat will be transmitted to ground stations as telemetry. The telemetry is data sent by the satellite to the ground station and contains information on the status and measurements of the satellite. The ground stations can then download and analyze the weather data and images sent via telemetry.

6. Weather imaging CubeSats can help with weather monitoring and forecasting by providing high-resolution data and images about conditions and events from low Earth orbit. The data from multiple CubeSats can provide better global weather coverage and observation of weather events like storms. However, the limited size, weight, and power of CubeSats place constraints on the sophistication of the instruments and accuracy of measurements.
