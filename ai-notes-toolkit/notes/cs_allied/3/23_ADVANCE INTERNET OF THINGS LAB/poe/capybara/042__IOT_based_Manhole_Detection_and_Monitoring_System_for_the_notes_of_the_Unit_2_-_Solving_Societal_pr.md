### IOT based Manhole Detection and Monitoring System

In today's society, manholes are an essential part of urban infrastructure. They allow access to underground utilities such as sewer lines, water supply pipes, and communication cables. However, manholes can also pose a significant danger to the public if left unsecured or uncovered. Accidents and fatalities have occurred due to open or uncovered manholes, making it a critical societal problem that needs to be addressed.

With the help of IoT, an intelligent system can be developed to detect and monitor manholes' status. Here are some of the key features of an IoT-based manhole detection and monitoring system:

1. **Sensors:** Sensors are installed on the manhole covers to detect their status, whether they are open, closed, or partially open.

2. **Connectivity:** The sensors are connected to a central server through the internet. The server receives the sensor data and processes it to determine the manhole's status.

3. **Real-time monitoring:** The system provides real-time monitoring of manholes, enabling authorities to take immediate action if a manhole is found to be open.

4. **Alerts and notifications:** The system sends alerts and notifications to the concerned authorities if a manhole is found to be open. This ensures that action is taken promptly to prevent accidents.

5. **Data analysis:** The system collects and analyzes data on manhole status, providing insights into the frequency of manhole openings, locations, and times of day when they are most likely to occur.

6. **Maintenance:** The system enables authorities to prioritize maintenance based on the data collected, reducing the risk of accidents due to faulty manhole covers.

In conclusion, an IoT-based manhole detection and monitoring system can help solve the societal problem of open and uncovered manholes. It provides real-time monitoring, alerts and notifications, data analysis, and maintenance prioritization. With the help of IoT, we can make our cities safer and more secure for everyone.
