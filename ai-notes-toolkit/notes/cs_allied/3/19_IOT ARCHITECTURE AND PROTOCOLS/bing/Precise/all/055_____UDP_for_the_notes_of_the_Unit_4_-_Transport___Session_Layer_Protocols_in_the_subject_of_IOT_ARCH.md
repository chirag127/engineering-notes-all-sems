### UDP for the notes of the Unit 4 - Transport & Session Layer Protocols in the subject of IOT ARCHITECTURE AND PROTOCOLS

- UDP stands for User Datagram Protocol.
- It is a transport layer protocol that is used for transmitting data over the internet.
- UDP is a connectionless protocol, meaning that it does not establish a connection between the sender and the receiver before transmitting data.
- It is a simple protocol that does not provide any reliability, flow control, or error recovery mechanisms.
- UDP is used for applications that require fast transmission of data and can tolerate some loss of data, such as online gaming, video streaming, and voice over IP (VoIP).
- UDP packets are called datagrams and have a fixed size header of 8 bytes.
- The header contains the source and destination port numbers, the length of the datagram, and a checksum for error detection.
- UDP is faster than TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) because it does not have the overhead of establishing a connection, error recovery, and flow control.
- However, the lack of reliability and error recovery mechanisms can result in lost or corrupted data.
- UDP is not suitable for applications that require reliable transmission of data, such as file transfers or financial transactions.
