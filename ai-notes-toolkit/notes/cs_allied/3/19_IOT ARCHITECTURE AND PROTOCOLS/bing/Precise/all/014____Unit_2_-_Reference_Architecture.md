## Unit 2 - Reference Architecture

1. A reference architecture is a document or set of documents that provides a common framework and vocabulary for designing and implementing systems within a specific domain.
2. It provides a high-level view of the system, including its components, their interactions, and the principles and guidelines that should be followed when designing and implementing the system.
3. Reference architectures are often used in large organizations or industries where there is a need for standardization and consistency across multiple systems.
4. They can help to reduce complexity, improve interoperability, and increase the speed and efficiency of system development.
5. A reference architecture typically includes a description of the system's components, their interfaces, and their interactions, as well as a set of principles and guidelines for designing and implementing the system.
6. It may also include reference implementations, which are concrete examples of how the architecture can be implemented.
7. Reference architectures are often developed by standards organizations, industry consortia, or large vendors, and are widely adopted within the relevant domain.
8. They are typically updated periodically to reflect changes in technology and best practices.
