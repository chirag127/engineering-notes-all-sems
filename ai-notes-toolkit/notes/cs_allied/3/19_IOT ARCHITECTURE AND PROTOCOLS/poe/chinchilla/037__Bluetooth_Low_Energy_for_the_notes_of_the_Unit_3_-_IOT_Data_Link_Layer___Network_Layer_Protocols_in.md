### Bluetooth Low Energy

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) is a wireless communication technology that is commonly used in Internet of Things (IoT) devices. It is designed to consume less power and have a shorter range than traditional Bluetooth technology.

Here are some key points to remember about BLE:

- BLE operates in the 2.4 GHz ISM band and uses GFSK modulation.
- It has a maximum data rate of 1 Mbps.
- BLE devices are classified as either central or peripheral devices. Central devices typically have more processing power and are responsible for initiating and managing connections with peripheral devices.
- BLE uses a connection-oriented protocol. Once a connection is established between two devices, they can exchange data in a reliable manner.
- BLE uses advertising packets to broadcast information about a device's capabilities and to initiate connections with other devices.
- BLE supports a range of different profiles, which define how devices communicate with each other. Examples of profiles include the Heart Rate Profile and the Proximity Profile.
- BLE includes built-in security features, such as encryption and authentication, to protect against unauthorized access and data interception.
- BLE has a shorter range than traditional Bluetooth, typically up to 100 meters in open space.
- BLE is commonly used in IoT devices such as smart home sensors, wearables, and medical devices, where low power consumption is important.

Overall, BLE is a popular choice for IoT devices due to its low power consumption, reliability, and security features. By understanding the key features and capabilities of BLE, developers can design more efficient and effective IoT solutions.
