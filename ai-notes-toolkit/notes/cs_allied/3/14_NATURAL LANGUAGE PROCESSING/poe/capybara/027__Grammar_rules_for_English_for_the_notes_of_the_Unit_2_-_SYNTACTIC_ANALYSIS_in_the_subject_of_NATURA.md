### Grammar Rules for English

In the study of natural language processing, it is essential to understand the grammar rules of the English language. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- English follows a Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order in a declarative sentence. For example, "John ate the apple."
- In interrogative sentences, the verb comes before the subject. For example, "Did John eat the apple?"
- Proper nouns, such as names of people, places, and things, are always capitalized.
- English has eight parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, and interjection.
- Nouns can be classified as common, proper, countable, or uncountable.
- Pronouns take the place of a noun in a sentence. Examples include "he," "she," "it," "they," and "we."
- Verbs can be classified as transitive or intransitive. Transitive verbs require an object, while intransitive verbs do not.
- Adjectives modify nouns and pronouns. They can be used to describe size, shape, color, age, etc.
- Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs. They can be used to describe time, place, manner, etc.
- Prepositions show a relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. Examples include "in," "on," "at," "to," and "from."
- Conjunctions connect words, phrases, or clauses. Examples include "and," "but," "or," and "so."
- Interjections are words that express emotions or feelings. Examples include "wow," "ouch," and "yikes."

Understanding these grammar rules is essential for analyzing and processing natural language. By applying these concepts, we can better understand the structure of sentences and the meaning behind them.
