### Acoustic Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 4 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Acoustic phonetics is the study of the physical aspects of speech sounds. It is concerned with the relationship between the physical characteristics of speech sounds and their perception by human beings. Here are some key points about acoustic phonetics:

- Sound waves: Speech sounds are produced by the movement of air molecules. These movements create sound waves that can be measured and analyzed. Acoustic phonetics is concerned with the properties of these sound waves.

- Frequency: The frequency of a sound wave determines its pitch. High-frequency waves produce high-pitched sounds, while low-frequency waves produce low-pitched sounds. In speech, the frequency of a sound wave corresponds to the perceived pitch of the sound.

- Amplitude: The amplitude of a sound wave determines its loudness. High-amplitude waves produce loud sounds, while low-amplitude waves produce soft sounds. In speech, the amplitude of a sound wave corresponds to the perceived loudness of the sound.

- Spectrogram: A spectrogram is a visual representation of a sound wave. It shows the frequency and amplitude of the wave over time. Spectrograms are often used in acoustic phonetics to analyze speech sounds.

- Formants: Formants are the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract. They are the frequencies at which the air inside the vocal tract vibrates most strongly. Formants are important for distinguishing between different vowel sounds.

- Coarticulation: Coarticulation is the phenomenon in which the articulation of one sound affects the articulation of another sound. For example, the position of the tongue for one vowel sound may affect the position of the tongue for the following consonant sound. Coarticulation is an important aspect of speech production that is studied in acoustic phonetics.

- Phonemes: Phonemes are the smallest units of sound in a language that can distinguish meaning. Different languages have different sets of phonemes. Acoustic phonetics is concerned with the acoustic properties of phonemes and how they are perceived by listeners.

In conclusion, acoustic phonetics is a crucial component of speech processing and natural language processing. Understanding the physical characteristics of speech sounds is essential for developing effective speech recognition systems and improving our understanding of human language.
