### WSD using Supervised for the notes of the Unit 3 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

In the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP), Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD) is an essential task. It involves identifying the correct sense of a word in a given context. WSD has numerous applications in NLP, such as machine translation, text summarization, and information retrieval.

One of the popular approaches to perform WSD is by using supervised learning techniques. In this approach, a machine learning model is trained on a labeled dataset, where each instance consists of a word and its corresponding sense in a particular context. The model then uses this training data to predict the sense of a word in a new context.

Here are some key points to consider when using the supervised approach for WSD:

- The quality of the labeled dataset plays a crucial role in the performance of the model. It is essential to ensure that the dataset is comprehensive, diverse, and accurately labeled.
- The choice of features is critical in determining the performance of the model. Features can be lexical, syntactic, or semantic, and their selection depends on the task and the language being used.
- The selection of an appropriate algorithm is also important. Some common algorithms used for WSD include Naive Bayes, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Decision Trees.
- The evaluation of the model is necessary to assess its performance. The commonly used evaluation metrics for WSD include precision, recall, and F1-score.
- It is essential to keep in mind the limitations of the supervised approach, such as the need for labeled data, the possibility of overfitting, and the difficulty in handling the nuances of language.

In conclusion, WSD using supervised learning is an effective approach for identifying the correct sense of a word in a given context. However, it requires careful consideration of the labeled dataset, features, algorithm, and evaluation metrics to achieve optimal performance.
