### Selectional Restrictions

Selectional restrictions are a set of constraints that determine the type of arguments that a predicate can take. These restrictions are important for understanding the meaning of a sentence and for disambiguating between different interpretations of a sentence.

#### What are Selectional Restrictions?

Selectional restrictions are constraints on the type of arguments that a predicate can take. These constraints are based on the meaning of the predicate and the properties of the arguments that it can take.

For example, the predicate "eat" has a selectional restriction that it can only take objects that are edible. This means that "eat" can take objects like "apple" and "hamburger", but not objects like "book" or "car".

- Selectional Restrictions on Modifiers: These are based on the type of modifier that can be used with a predicate. For example, the predicate "happy" has a selectional restriction that it can only take adverbial modifiers that indicate a state of being, such as "very" or "extremely".

#### Importance of Selectional Restrictions

Selectional restrictions are important for understanding the meaning of a sentence and for disambiguating between different interpretations of a sentence. They help to narrow down the possible meanings of a sentence and to ensure that the arguments used with a predicate are appropriate.
