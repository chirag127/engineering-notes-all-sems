## Unit 3 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics and pragmatics are two important branches of linguistics that deal with the meaning of language. Semantics is concerned with the study of meaning in language, while pragmatics is concerned with the study of how language is used in context. In this unit, we will explore the key concepts and theories in semantics and pragmatics.

### Semantics

Semantics is the study of meaning in language. It focuses on how words, phrases, and sentences convey meaning in different contexts. Some key concepts in semantics include:

- **Lexical semantics:** This is the study of how individual words and their meanings contribute to the overall meaning of a sentence.
- **Structural semantics:** This is the study of how the structure of a sentence contributes to its meaning.
- **Principle of compositionality:** This principle states that the meaning of a sentence is determined by the meanings of its individual parts and how they are combined.

### Pragmatics

Pragmatics is the study of how language is used in context. It focuses on the ways in which speakers use language to communicate meaning. Some key concepts in pragmatics include:

- **Speech acts:** These are the different types of utterances that speakers use to perform different functions, such as making requests, giving orders, or expressing opinions.
- **Deixis:** This is the use of words that depend on the context in which they are used. Examples include words like "here," "there," "this," and "that."
- **Implicature:** This refers to the meaning that is implied by a speaker, rather than the literal meaning of their words.

### Semantics vs. Pragmatics

While semantics and pragmatics are closely related, they are distinct areas of study. Semantics focuses on the meaning of language, while pragmatics focuses on how language is used in context. Some key differences between semantics and pragmatics include:

- Semantics is concerned with the literal meaning of language, while pragmatics is concerned with the implied meaning of language.
- Semantics focuses on the meaning of individual words and sentences, while pragmatics focuses on the meaning of whole conversations or interactions.
- Semantics is concerned with the relationship between language and the world, while pragmatics is concerned with the relationship between language and the people who use it.

In conclusion, the study of semantics and pragmatics is essential for understanding the meaning of language and how it is used in context. By exploring the key concepts and theories in these areas, we can gain a deeper understanding of how language works and how we use it to communicate with one another.
