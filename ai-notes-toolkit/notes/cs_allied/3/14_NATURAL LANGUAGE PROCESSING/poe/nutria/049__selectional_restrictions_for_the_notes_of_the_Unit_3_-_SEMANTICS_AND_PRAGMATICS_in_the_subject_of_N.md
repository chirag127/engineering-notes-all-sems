1. Selectional restrictions are a type of syntactic constraint that limit the types of words that can appear in a particular syntactic slot. 
2. They are based on the idea that the meaning of a phrase is determined by the meaning of its individual words. 
3. For example, the verb "eat" can only take a noun phrase that refers to something edible as its direct object. 
4. Selectional restrictions are important for natural language processing because they help to constrain the possible interpretations of a sentence. 
5. In addition, they can help to identify errors in a sentence, as when a verb is used with an inappropriate argument. 
6. Selectional restrictions can also be used to generate paraphrases of a sentence by replacing an argument with a synonym that satisfies the selectional restrictions of the verb. 
7. Selectional restrictions can be implemented in natural language processing systems by using a lexical database that contains information about the types of arguments that a verb can take.
