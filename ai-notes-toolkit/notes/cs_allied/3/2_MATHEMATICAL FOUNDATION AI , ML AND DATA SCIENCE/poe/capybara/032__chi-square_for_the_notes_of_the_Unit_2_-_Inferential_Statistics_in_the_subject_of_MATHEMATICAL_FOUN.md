### Chi-Square for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics in the Subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI, ML, and Data Science

Chi-Square is a statistical test used to determine whether there is a significant difference between the expected frequencies and the observed frequencies in one or more categories. This test is used to analyze categorical data and is commonly used in research studies.

Here are some important points to remember about Chi-Square:

- Chi-Square is used to test the hypothesis that there is no significant difference between the expected frequencies and the observed frequencies in one or more categories.
- The test is performed by calculating the Chi-Square statistic, which measures the difference between the expected and observed frequencies.
- The Chi-Square statistic follows a distribution called the Chi-Square distribution, which has a specific number of degrees of freedom.
- The number of degrees of freedom is calculated by subtracting 1 from the number of categories being analyzed.
- The p-value, which is the probability of obtaining the observed results by chance, is calculated using the Chi-Square distribution and the degrees of freedom.
- If the p-value is less than the significance level (usually 0.05), the null hypothesis is rejected, and it is concluded that there is a significant difference between the expected and observed frequencies.
- Chi-Square can be used to analyze data from a variety of sources, including surveys, experiments, and observational studies.

In summary, Chi-Square is a powerful statistical tool used to analyze categorical data and determine whether there is a significant difference between the expected and observed frequencies. Understanding how to apply this test is crucial for researchers and data analysts in the field of AI, ML, and Data Science.
