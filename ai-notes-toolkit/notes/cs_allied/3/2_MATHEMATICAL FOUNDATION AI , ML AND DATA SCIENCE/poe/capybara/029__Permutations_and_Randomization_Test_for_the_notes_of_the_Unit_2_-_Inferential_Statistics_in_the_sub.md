### Permutations and Randomization Test for the notes of the Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI , ML AND DATA SCIENCE

Inferential statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with drawing conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. Permutations and randomization test are two important techniques used in inferential statistics.

Permutations:
- Permutations refer to the arrangement of objects in a specific order. 
- In statistics, permutations are used to calculate the number of ways a set of objects can be arranged in a specific order. 
- A permutation test is a non-parametric test that is used to test the significance of observed data. 
- In a permutation test, the null hypothesis is that there is no difference between the observed data and the expected data. 
- The permutation test involves randomly permuting the observed data and calculating a test statistic. 
- This process is repeated many times to obtain a null distribution of the test statistic. 
- The p-value is calculated as the proportion of test statistics from the null distribution that are equal to or more extreme than the observed test statistic. 
- If the p-value is less than the significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected.

Randomization test:
- Randomization test is a statistical technique that is used to test the significance of an observed difference between two groups. 
- In a randomization test, the null hypothesis is that there is no difference between the two groups. 
- The randomization test involves randomly assigning the observations to the two groups and calculating a test statistic. 
- This process is repeated many times to obtain a null distribution of the test statistic. 
- The p-value is calculated as the proportion of test statistics from the null distribution that are equal to or more extreme than the observed test statistic. 
- If the p-value is less than the significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected.

Conclusion:
Permutations and randomization test are two important techniques used in inferential statistics. These techniques are used when the assumptions of parametric tests are not met or when the data is non-normal or skewed. It is important to understand the concepts and applications of these tests to draw valid conclusions about the population.
