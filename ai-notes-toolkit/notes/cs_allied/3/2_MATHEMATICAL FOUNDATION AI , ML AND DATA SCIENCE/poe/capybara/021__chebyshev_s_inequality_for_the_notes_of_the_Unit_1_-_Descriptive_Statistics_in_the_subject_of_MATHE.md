### Chebyshev’s Inequality for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics in the Subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML, and Data Science

In probability theory, Chebyshev’s inequality is a fundamental theorem that provides a bound on the probability that a random variable deviates from its expected value. The inequality is applicable to any probability distribution, whether discrete or continuous. Let's dive into the key points of Chebyshev’s inequality:

- Chebyshev’s inequality states that, for any random variable X with finite mean μ and finite variance σ^2, the probability that X deviates from its mean by more than k standard deviations is at most 1/k^2, where k is any positive number greater than 1.
- This theorem can be mathematically expressed as P(|X - μ| ≥ kσ) ≤ 1/k^2.
- The inequality holds for any distribution with finite mean and variance. This includes normal, Poisson, and binomial distributions, among others.
- Chebyshev’s inequality provides an upper bound on the probability of a deviation from the mean. It does not provide a precise probability of the deviation.
- The inequality is useful for calculating the range of values where a certain percentage of values lie. For example, if we want to know the range of values that contain 95% of all values, we can use Chebyshev’s inequality to calculate the bounds.
- Chebyshev’s inequality is a powerful tool in statistics, as it enables us to make statements about the distribution of data without making any assumptions about its shape.

In conclusion, Chebyshev’s inequality is an important tool in probability theory and statistics. It provides a bound on the probability of a deviation from the mean, which is useful for understanding the distribution of data. By using Chebyshev’s inequality, we can make statements about the distribution of data without making any assumptions about its shape.
