### Measures of Dispersion for the Notes of Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics in the Subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML, and Data Science

Descriptive statistics is the branch of statistics that deals with the organization, analysis, interpretation, and presentation of data. Measures of dispersion are a set of statistical measures that describe the variability or spread of a dataset. In this section, we will discuss the most commonly used measures of dispersion.

1. Range: The range is the difference between the maximum and minimum values in a dataset. It is a simple and easy-to-calculate measure of dispersion, but it is sensitive to outliers.

2. Interquartile Range (IQR): The IQR is the range of the middle 50% of the data. It is less sensitive to outliers than the range and provides a better measure of the spread of the central portion of the dataset.

3. Variance: Variance is the average of the squared differences from the mean. It measures the amount of variability or dispersion of the data points from the mean. It is a widely used measure of dispersion, but it is sensitive to outliers.

4. Standard Deviation: The standard deviation is the square root of the variance. It measures the spread of the data points around the mean. It is a widely used measure of dispersion because it is easy to interpret and is less sensitive to outliers than the variance.

5. Coefficient of Variation (CV): The CV is the ratio of the standard deviation to the mean. It is a relative measure of dispersion and is useful when comparing datasets with different units or scales.

In conclusion, measures of dispersion provide valuable information about the variability or spread of a dataset. Different measures of dispersion are used in different situations, depending on the nature of the data and the research question. It is important to choose the appropriate measure of dispersion to accurately describe the variability in the dataset.
