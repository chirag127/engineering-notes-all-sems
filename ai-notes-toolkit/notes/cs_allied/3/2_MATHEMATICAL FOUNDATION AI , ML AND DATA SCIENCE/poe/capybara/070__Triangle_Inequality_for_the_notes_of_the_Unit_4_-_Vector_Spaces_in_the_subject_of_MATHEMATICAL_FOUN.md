### Triangle Inequality

The Triangle Inequality is an important concept in the study of Vector Spaces. It states that for any vectors **u** and **v** in a Vector Space, the following inequality holds:

|**u** + **v**| ≤ |**u**| + |**v**|

Here are some key points to remember about the Triangle Inequality:

- The Triangle Inequality is a fundamental property of Vector Spaces, and is used in many different areas of mathematics and science.

- The inequality states that the length of the sum of two vectors is always less than or equal to the sum of the lengths of the individual vectors.

- This concept can be extended to any number of vectors. For example, for three vectors **u**, **v**, and **w**, the inequality becomes:

    |**u** + **v** + **w**| ≤ |**u**| + |**v**| + |**w**|

- The Triangle Inequality is closely related to the concept of the Euclidean norm or length of a vector, which is defined as:

    |**u**| = √(u1^2 + u2^2 + ... + un^2)

- The Triangle Inequality can be used to prove other important theorems in Vector Spaces, such as the Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality and the Triangle Inequality for integrals.
