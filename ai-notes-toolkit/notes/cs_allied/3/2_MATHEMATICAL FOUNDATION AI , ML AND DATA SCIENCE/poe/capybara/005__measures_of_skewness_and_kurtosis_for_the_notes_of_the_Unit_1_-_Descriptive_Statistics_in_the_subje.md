### Measures of Skewness and Kurtosis

In the field of statistics, measures of skewness and kurtosis play a vital role in analyzing a distribution. These measures help to understand the shape and symmetry of the distribution. Here are some important points related to measures of skewness and kurtosis:

- Skewness is a measure of the asymmetry of a distribution. It tells us about the degree to which the data deviates from the normal distribution.
- Skewness can be positive, negative or zero. Positive skewness indicates that the tail of the distribution is longer on the right side. Negative skewness indicates that the tail of the distribution is longer on the left side. Zero skewness indicates that the distribution is symmetric.
- The formula for skewness is given by:

  Skewness = (3 * Mean - Mode) / Standard Deviation

- Kurtosis measures the degree of peakedness or flatness of a distribution. It tells us about the fatness of the tails of the distribution.
- Kurtosis can be positive, negative or zero. Positive kurtosis indicates that the distribution is more peaked than the normal distribution. Negative kurtosis indicates that the distribution is flatter than the normal distribution. Zero kurtosis indicates that the distribution is mesokurtic, i.e., has the same degree of peakedness as the normal distribution.
- The formula for kurtosis is given by:

  Kurtosis = (Sum of (x - Mean)^4 / n) / (Standard Deviation)^4 - 3

- A distribution with positive skewness and positive kurtosis is called leptokurtic. A distribution with negative skewness and negative kurtosis is called platykurtic. A distribution with zero skewness and zero kurtosis is called mesokurtic.
- Skewness and kurtosis can be computed using statistical software packages such as Excel, R, Python, etc.

Understanding the measures of skewness and kurtosis is important for data analysts and scientists as it helps in making data-driven decisions. These measures provide valuable insights into the underlying distribution of the data and help in identifying outliers and anomalies.
