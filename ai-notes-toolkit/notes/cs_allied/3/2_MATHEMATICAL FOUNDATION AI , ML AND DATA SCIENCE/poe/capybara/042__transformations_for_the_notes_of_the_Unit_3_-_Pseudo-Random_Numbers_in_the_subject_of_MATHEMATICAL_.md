### Transformations for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers in the Subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML and Data Science

In the Unit 3 of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML and Data Science, we will be discussing Pseudo-Random Numbers. This unit covers various topics, including transformations. Transformations are essential in generating random numbers that can pass as true random numbers.

Here are the transformations that will be discussed in this unit:

1. Linear Congruential Generator (LCG)
    - This is the most popular method of generating Pseudo-Random Numbers.
    - In this method, a sequence of numbers is generated by multiplying and adding a constant value to the previous number.
    - It is easy to implement and requires less memory.

2. Blum Blum Shub (BBS)
    - This method generates Pseudo-Random Numbers by computing modular exponentiation.
    - It is considered secure because it is hard to predict the next number in the sequence.
    - However, it is slow and requires a large amount of memory.

3. Mersenne Twister (MT)
    - This is a widely used method of generating Pseudo-Random Numbers.
    - It generates a sequence of 32-bit or 64-bit numbers using a mathematical formula.
    - It is fast and has a long period, making it suitable for simulations and games.

4. Additive Feedback (AF)
    - This method generates a sequence of numbers by adding a previous number to a fixed value and then taking the result modulo a prime number.
    - It is easy to implement and has a long period.
    - However, it is not as fast as other methods.

5. XORshift
    - This method generates Pseudo-Random Numbers by performing bitwise exclusive OR operations.
    - It is fast and requires less memory.
    - However, it has a shorter period than other methods.

In conclusion, transformations are essential in generating Pseudo-Random Numbers. In this unit, we have discussed the most popular methods of generating Pseudo-Random Numbers, including Linear Congruential Generator, Blum Blum Shub, Mersenne Twister, Additive Feedback, and XORshift. Understanding these methods is crucial for anyone working with simulations or games.
