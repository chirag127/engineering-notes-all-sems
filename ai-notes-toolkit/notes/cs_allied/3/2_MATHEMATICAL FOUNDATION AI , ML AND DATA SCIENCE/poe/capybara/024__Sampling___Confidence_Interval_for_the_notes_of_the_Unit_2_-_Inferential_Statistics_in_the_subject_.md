### Sampling & Confidence Interval

In inferential statistics, we use a sample to draw conclusions about a population. However, we must ensure that the sample is representative of the population to avoid biased results. Sampling helps us achieve this objective. Here are some important points to remember about sampling:

- A sample is a subset of a population that is selected to represent the population.
- The process of selecting a sample is known as sampling.
- The size of the sample is an important consideration. A larger sample size leads to more accurate results, but it also incurs higher costs and requires more time and resources.
- Random sampling is a common method of selecting a sample. In this method, every member of the population has an equal chance of being selected for the sample.
- Stratified sampling is another method of selecting a sample. In this method, the population is divided into strata (subgroups), and members are randomly selected from each stratum.
- Systematic sampling involves selecting members of the population at regular intervals.
- Cluster sampling involves selecting groups (clusters) of members from the population.

Once we have selected a sample, we can use it to estimate population parameters such as mean, variance, and proportion. However, we need to account for the variability in the sample. The confidence interval is a statistical technique that helps us estimate the range of values within which the true population parameter is likely to lie. Here are some important points to remember about confidence intervals:

- A confidence interval is a range of values within which the true population parameter is likely to lie.
- The level of confidence is the probability that the true population parameter lies within the confidence interval. The most common level of confidence is 95%.
- The width of the confidence interval depends on the sample size and the variability in the sample.
- A larger sample size leads to a narrower confidence interval, whereas a smaller sample size leads to a wider confidence interval.
- A higher level of confidence leads to a wider confidence interval, whereas a lower level of confidence leads to a narrower confidence interval.

In conclusion, sampling and confidence intervals are important tools in inferential statistics. They help us draw conclusions about a population based on a sample while accounting for the variability in the sample. By understanding these concepts, we can make more informed decisions based on statistical analysis.
