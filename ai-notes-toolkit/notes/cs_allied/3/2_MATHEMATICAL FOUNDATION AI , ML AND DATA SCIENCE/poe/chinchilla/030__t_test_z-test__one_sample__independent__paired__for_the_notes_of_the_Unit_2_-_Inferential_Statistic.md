### t-test/z-test (one sample, independent, paired) for the notes of the Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI , ML AND DATA SCIENCE

In inferential statistics, t-tests and z-tests are commonly used to test hypotheses about population means. They are used to determine whether the difference between sample means is statistically significant or not. In this section, we will discuss one sample t-test, independent t-test, paired t-test, and z-test.

#### One sample t-test
A one sample t-test is used to compare the mean of a sample to a known or hypothesized population mean. The null hypothesis (H0) is that the mean of the sample is equal to the population mean, while the alternative hypothesis (Ha) is that the mean of the sample is not equal to the population mean. The test statistic is calculated as:

t = (x̄ - μ) / (s / √n)

where x̄ is the sample mean, μ is the population mean, s is the sample standard deviation, and n is the sample size. If the calculated t-value is greater than the critical t-value, we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the sample mean is significantly different from the population mean.

#### Independent t-test
An independent t-test is used to compare the means of two independent samples. The null hypothesis (H0) is that the means of the two samples are equal, while the alternative hypothesis (Ha) is that the means of the two samples are not equal. The test statistic is calculated as:

t = (x̄1 - x̄2) / (s_p √(1/n1 + 1/n2))

where x̄1 and x̄2 are the means of the two samples, s_p is the pooled standard deviation, n1 and n2 are the sample sizes of the two samples. If the calculated t-value is greater than the critical t-value, we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the means of the two samples are significantly different.

#### Paired t-test
A paired t-test is used to compare the means of two dependent samples. The null hypothesis (H0) is that the means of the two samples are equal, while the alternative hypothesis (Ha) is that the means of the two samples are not equal. The test statistic is calculated as:

t = (x̄d - μd) / (s_d / √n)

where x̄d is the mean difference between the two samples, μd is the population mean difference, s_d is the standard deviation of the differences, and n is the sample size. If the calculated t-value is greater than the critical t-value, we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the means of the two samples are significantly different.

#### Z-test
A z-test is used to test hypotheses about population means when the population standard deviation is known. The null hypothesis (H0) is that the mean of the sample is equal to the population mean, while the alternative hypothesis (Ha) is that the mean of the sample is not equal to the population mean. The test statistic is calculated as:

z = (x̄ - μ) / (σ / √n)

where x̄ is the sample mean, μ is the population mean, σ is the population standard deviation, and n is the sample size. If the calculated z-value is greater than the critical z-value, we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the sample mean is significantly different from the population mean.

In conclusion, t-tests and z-tests are powerful statistical tools for testing hypotheses about population means. One sample t-test, independent t-test, paired t-test, and z-test are used to compare means of samples and population means. Understanding these tests and their applications is essential in inferential statistics.
