### Stratified Sampling

Stratified sampling is a sampling technique used in statistical analysis. It is a method of dividing a population into smaller subgroups, or strata, and then selecting samples from each stratum. This technique is used to ensure that the sample is representative of the population as a whole.

#### Advantages of Stratified Sampling

- It ensures that the sample is representative of the population as a whole.
- It reduces the sampling error.
- It can provide more precise estimates of population parameters than simple random sampling.
- It allows for comparisons between subgroups within the population.

#### Procedure for Stratified Sampling

1. Divide the population into smaller subgroups or strata based on some relevant characteristic.
2. Determine the sample size for each stratum based on its proportion of the population.
3. Randomly select samples from each stratum.
4. Combine the samples to form the final sample.

#### Example of Stratified Sampling

Suppose we want to conduct a survey of students in a college. The population consists of all students in the college. We can divide the population into smaller subgroups based on their year of study: first-year, second-year, third-year, and fourth-year students. We can then randomly select samples from each stratum based on the proportion of students in each year group. By doing this, we can ensure that the sample is representative of the population as a whole and can provide more precise estimates of population parameters than simple random sampling.

#### Conclusion

Stratified sampling is a useful technique for sampling a population. It ensures that the sample is representative of the population as a whole, reduces the sampling error, and provides more precise estimates of population parameters. By following the procedure of stratified sampling, we can select samples from each stratum and combine them to form the final sample.
