### Orthogonal Vectors

Orthogonal vectors play a crucial role in many areas of mathematics, including vector spaces, linear algebra, and geometry. In this section, we will define what it means for vectors to be orthogonal, and explore some of their properties and applications.

#### Definition

Two vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ in a vector space are said to be orthogonal (or perpendicular) if their dot product is zero, i.e.,

$$\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0.$$

#### Properties

Here are some important properties of orthogonal vectors:

- The zero vector is orthogonal to every vector in the vector space.
- If two vectors are orthogonal, then their magnitudes are related by the Pythagorean theorem:

$$\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{u}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{v}\|^2.$$

- If two non-zero vectors are orthogonal, then they are linearly independent. This means that neither vector can be expressed as a linear combination of the other.

#### Applications

Orthogonal vectors have several applications in mathematics and related fields:

- In linear algebra, orthogonal vectors are used to define orthogonal bases for vector spaces. These bases have many useful properties, such as simplifying computations and making it easier to find projections.
- In geometry, orthogonal vectors are used to define perpendicular lines and planes. They are also used to find the angle between two vectors.
- In signal processing and data analysis, orthogonal vectors are used to decompose signals or data into components that are easier to analyze. This is known as orthogonal decomposition or principal component analysis.

#### Conclusion

Orthogonal vectors are an important concept in mathematics and have many applications in various fields. Understanding orthogonal vectors is crucial for further studies in linear algebra, vector spaces, and geometry.
