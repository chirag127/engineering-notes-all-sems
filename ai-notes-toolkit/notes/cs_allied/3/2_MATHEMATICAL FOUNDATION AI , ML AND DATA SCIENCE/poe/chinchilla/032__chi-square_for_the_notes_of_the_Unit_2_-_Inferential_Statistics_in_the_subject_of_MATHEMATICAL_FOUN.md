### Chi-Square

Chi-square is a statistical test used to determine if there is a significant difference between the expected frequencies and the observed frequencies in one or more categories. It is used to test the goodness of fit of a distribution or to test for independence between two variables.

#### Assumptions

To use chi-square, the following assumptions must be met:

- The data is categorical.
- The sample is random.
- The expected frequencies are not too small.

#### Calculation

The calculation of chi-square involves the following steps:

1. Determine the expected frequencies for each category.
2. Calculate the difference between the observed and expected frequencies for each category.
3. Square the differences.
4. Divide each squared difference by the expected frequency.
5. Add up the results from step 4 to get the chi-square statistic.

#### Degrees of Freedom

The degrees of freedom for chi-square is calculated as:

df = (number of rows - 1) x (number of columns - 1)

#### Interpretation

The chi-square statistic can be compared to a chi-square distribution table to determine the p-value. If the p-value is less than the significance level (usually 0.05), then there is significant evidence to reject the null hypothesis and conclude that there is a difference between the expected and observed frequencies.

#### Applications

Chi-square can be used in a variety of applications, including:

- Testing for goodness of fit of a distribution.
- Testing for independence between two variables.
- Comparing observed frequencies to expected frequencies in a contingency table.
- Testing for homogeneity of proportions across different groups.

#### Conclusion

Chi-square is a powerful statistical tool that can be used to test for significant differences between expected and observed frequencies in categorical data. Understanding the assumptions, calculation, degrees of freedom, and interpretation of chi-square is essential for conducting inferential statistics in AI, ML, and data science.
