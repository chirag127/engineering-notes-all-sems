### Expectation and Variance for the Notes of Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics in the Subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML and Data Science

In the field of data science and machine learning, descriptive statistics play a crucial role in analyzing and interpreting data. The concepts of expectation and variance are fundamental components of descriptive statistics that you must understand to make sense of data. In this section, we will discuss the concept of expectation and variance and their significance in data analysis.

#### Expectation

- Expectation is a measure of the central tendency of a probability distribution. 
- It is calculated as the weighted average of all possible outcomes of a random variable, where the weights are the probabilities of each outcome. 
- In simpler terms, it represents the "average" value that we would expect to obtain over many trials of a random experiment. 
- The expectation is denoted by E(X), where X is a random variable.

#### Variance

- Variance is a measure of how spread out or dispersed a probability distribution is. 
- It is calculated as the average of the squared deviations of each outcome from the mean, where the deviations are weighted by their probabilities. 
- In other words, variance measures how far a set of numbers is spread out from their average value. 
- The variance is denoted by Var(X) or σ², where X is a random variable.

#### Significance of Expectation and Variance

- Expectation and variance are crucial measures in probability theory and statistics that help us understand the properties of a random variable. 
- They provide useful information about the central tendency and dispersion of the data, which is essential for making decisions based on the data. 
- Expectation and variance are widely used in data analysis, machine learning, and statistical inference.

#### Properties of Expectation and Variance

- Linearity: E(aX + bY) = aE(X) + bE(Y) and Var(aX + bY) = a²Var(X) + b²Var(Y) + 2abCov(X,Y), where a and b are constants and Cov(X,Y) is the covariance between X and Y.
- Non-negativity: E(X) ≥ 0 and Var(X) ≥ 0.
- Additivity: E(X + Y) = E(X) + E(Y) and Var(X + Y) = Var(X) + Var(Y) + 2Cov(X,Y), where Cov(X,Y) is the covariance between X and Y.
- Independence: If X and Y are independent, then Cov(X,Y) = 0, and Var(X + Y) = Var(X) + Var(Y).

#### Conclusion

Expectation and variance are essential concepts in descriptive statistics that help us understand the properties of probability distributions. They provide valuable information about the central tendency and dispersion of the data, which is crucial for data analysis, machine learning, and statistical inference. By understanding the concepts of expectation and variance and their properties, you will be better equipped to analyze and interpret data in the field of data science and machine learning.
