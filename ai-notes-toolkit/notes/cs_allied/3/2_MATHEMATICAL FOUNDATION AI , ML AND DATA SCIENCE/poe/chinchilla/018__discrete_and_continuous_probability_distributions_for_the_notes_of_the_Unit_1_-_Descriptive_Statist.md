### Discrete and Continuous Probability Distributions

Probability distributions are an essential part of descriptive statistics, which help us understand the behavior of random variables. In this section, we will explore two types of probability distributions: discrete and continuous.

#### Discrete Probability Distribution

A discrete probability distribution is a distribution of probability for a finite number of outcomes or values. This means that each possible outcome has a specific probability assigned to it, and the sum of all probabilities is equal to 1. Some common examples of discrete probability distributions are the binomial distribution and the Poisson distribution.

##### Binomial Distribution

The binomial distribution is used when we have a fixed number of trials, and each trial has only two possible outcomes (success or failure). #### Continuous Probability Distribution

A continuous probability distribution is a distribution of probability for an infinite number of outcomes or values. This means that the probability of any specific value is zero, and we have to use intervals to calculate probabilities. Some common examples of continuous probability distributions are the normal distribution and the exponential distribution.

#### Conclusion

Probability distributions are an essential tool for understanding the behavior of random variables. In this section, we covered two types of probability distributions: discrete and continuous.
