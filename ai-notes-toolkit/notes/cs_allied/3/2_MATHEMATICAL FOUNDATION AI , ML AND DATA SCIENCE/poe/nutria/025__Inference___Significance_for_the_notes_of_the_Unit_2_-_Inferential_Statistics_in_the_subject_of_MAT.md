
### Inference & Significance 

* Inference is the process of making decisions or drawing conclusions based on data or evidence. It is an important part of the scientific process and is used to make predictions and draw conclusions from data. 
* Significance is the degree to which an effect or result is meaningful or important. In statistics, significance refers to the probability that a result or relationship is not due to random chance. 
* In inferential statistics, the goal is to draw conclusions about a population based on a sample. To do this, statistical tests are used to determine if the results of the sample are statistically significant. 
* Statistical tests measure the probability that the results of a sample are due to random chance, rather than a real effect or relationship. If the probability is low (less than 5%), the results are considered statistically significant and can be used to make inferences about the population. 
* Common tests used for inferential statistics include the t-test, ANOVA, and chi-square test. These tests are used to compare means, analyze variance, and test relationships between variables. 
* Inferential statistics is an important tool for understanding data and making decisions based on evidence. By understanding the principles of inference and significance, researchers can make more informed decisions and draw more accurate conclusions from their data.
