1. Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that appear to be random, but are actually generated according to a deterministic algorithm.
2. Pseudo-random numbers are used in many applications such as cryptography, simulations, and games.
3. The most common pseudo-random number generator is the linear congruential generator (LCG).
4. LCGs use a linear equation to generate a sequence of numbers that appear random but are actually predictable.
5. Another common pseudo-random number generator is the Mersenne Twister.
6. The Mersenne Twister is a pseudorandom number generator based on a deterministic algorithm that produces a sequence of numbers that are statistically indistinguishable from truly random numbers.
7. Transformations can be used to transform the output of a pseudo-random number generator into a more desirable distribution.
8. Common transformations include the Box-Muller transform, the Ziggurat algorithm, and the inverse cumulative distribution function.
9. Transformations can also be used to increase the speed of the pseudo-random number generator.
10. Finally, transformations can be used to improve the quality of the pseudo-random numbers generated by the algorithm.
