
### Central Limit Theorem

The Central Limit Theorem is an important concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that the sum of a large number of independent random variables will tend towards a normal distribution, regardless of the underlying distribution of the individual variables. This theorem is useful for understanding the behavior of complex systems, such as markets and other large-scale phenomena.

The theorem can be expressed mathematically as follows:

If X1, X2, ..., Xn are independent random variables with mean μ and standard deviation σ, then the sum of the variables, Y = X1 + X2 + ... + Xn, will tend towards a normal distribution with mean μn and standard deviation σ/√n, where n is the number of variables.

In practice, this means that the sum of a large number of independent random variables will tend to be normally distributed, even if the individual variables are not. This is useful for understanding the behavior of complex systems, such as stock markets, where the individual components may not follow any particular distribution.
