
### Inference Procedure for Correlation Coefficient

1. The correlation coefficient is a measure of linear association between two variables.
2. The correlation coefficient can be calculated using the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient formula.
3. The formula for the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient is:
   $$r = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}$$
4. The correlation coefficient ranges from -1 to 1.
5. A correlation coefficient of -1 indicates a perfect negative linear relationship between two variables.
6. A correlation coefficient of 0 indicates no linear relationship between two variables.
7. A correlation coefficient of 1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship between two variables.
8. The correlation coefficient can be used to test the hypothesis of whether two variables are linearly related or not.
9. The null hypothesis is that there is no linear relationship between two variables.
10. The alternative hypothesis is that there is a linear relationship between two variables.
11. The decision rule is that if the correlation coefficient is greater than or equal to the critical value, then reject the null hypothesis and accept the alternative hypothesis.
12. The critical value can be found in a table of critical values for the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient.
