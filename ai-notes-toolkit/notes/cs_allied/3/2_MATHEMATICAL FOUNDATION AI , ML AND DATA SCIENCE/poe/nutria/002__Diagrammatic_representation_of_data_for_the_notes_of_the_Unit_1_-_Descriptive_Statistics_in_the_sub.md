
### Diagrammatic Representation of Data for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics

1. **Bar Graphs**: Bar graphs are used to compare different categories of data. The length of each bar represents the magnitude of the data, and the bars are arranged in order of magnitude. Bar graphs are useful for comparing data across different categories, such as population size in different countries or the number of people in different age groups.

2. **Pie Charts**: Pie charts are used to represent data as a proportion of the whole. Each slice of the pie chart represents a different category of data, and the size of each slice is proportional to the magnitude of the data in that category. Pie charts are useful for comparing the relative sizes of different categories of data.

3. **Line Graphs**: Line graphs are used to show the relationship between two sets of data. The x-axis of the graph is used to represent one set of data, and the y-axis is used to represent the other set of data. Line graphs are useful for visualizing trends over time, such as the population growth of a city or the performance of a stock over time.

4. **Scatter Plots**: Scatter plots are used to show the relationship between two sets of data. The x-axis of the graph is used to represent one set of data, and the y-axis is used to represent the other set of data. Scatter plots are useful for visualizing the relationship between two variables, such as the relationship between age and income or the relationship between temperature and pressure.
