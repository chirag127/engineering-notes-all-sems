### Multivariate Probability Calculations

- Multivariate probability distributions deal with random variables that have multiple variables. For example, the joint probability distribution of two random variables X and Y.
- The joint probability function f(x,y) gives the probability that X=x and Y=y simultaneously. It must satisfy the conditions:
- f(x,y) >= 0 for all x and y
- Sum over all possible x and y of f(x,y) = 1
- Marginal probability distributions can be obtained by summing the joint probability distribution over one of the variables. For example, the marginal distribution of X is:

fX(x) = Sum[y: f(x,y)]

- Conditional probability distributions describe the probability of one random variable given the other. For example, the conditional distribution of X given Y = y is:

fX|Y(x|y) = f(x,y) / fY(y)     (if fY(y) > 0)

- Probability density functions can also be defined for multivariate distributions and must satisfy analogous conditions to joint probability distribution functions.
- Multivariate distributions have uses in many applications such as estimating correlations between variables and generating samples from complex distributions.
