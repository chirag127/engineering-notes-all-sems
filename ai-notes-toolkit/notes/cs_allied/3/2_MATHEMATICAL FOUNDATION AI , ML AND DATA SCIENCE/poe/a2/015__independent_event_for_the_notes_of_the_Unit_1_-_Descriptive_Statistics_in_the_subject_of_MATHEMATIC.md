### Independent Event for the notes of the Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics

- An independent event is an event whose occurrence/non-occurrence has no effect on the probability of occurrence/non-occurrence of another event.
- The probability of an independent event is not affected by the outcome of another event.
- For example, the probability of rain tomorrow is independent of whether or not it rained today.
- To determine if two events A and B are independent, check if P(A|B) = P(A) and P(B|A) = P(B). If yes, then the events are independent.
- For independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) x P(B). This is not true for dependent events.
- Some examples of independent events:
    - Toss of a fair coin and roll of a fair die
    - Winning a lottery and rainfall tomorrow
    - Gender of a baby and day of birth
- Independent events have no effect on each other and do not influence each other's probabilities. The occurrence of one event does not change the probability of the other event.
