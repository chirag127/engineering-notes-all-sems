### Chebyshev's Inequality

Chebyshev's inequality is a result which gives an upper bound on the probability that a random variable deviates from its mean by more than a certain amount.

- Let X be a random variable with mean μ and variance σ2. Then for any real number k > 0,

P(|X − μ| > kσ) ≤ 1/k2

- In other words, the probability that X deviates from its mean by more than k standard deviations is at most 1/k2.
- As k increases, the upper bound decreases. So the inequality gives a worst-case bound on the probability of a large deviation from the mean.
- The inequality is weak but the bound is easy to calculate since it only depends on k and not on the underlying distribution of X.
- The inequality is named after Pafnuty Chebyshev who proved a more general version of it in 1867. It is a fundamental result in probability with many applications in statistics and other fields.
