### 1. Vector Space
- A vector space is a mathematical structure that consists of a set of vectors along with operations for combining vectors such as addition, scalar multiplication, and linear combinations.
- Vectors are quantities that have magnitude and direction such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, etc.
- The vectors in a vector space can be added, subtracted, and scaled (multiplied by a scalar).
- The set of all vectors in the vector space and the operations defined on them must satisfy a set of requirements known as the axioms of a vector space.

### 2. Spanning and Linear Independence
- A subset of vectors in a vector space is said to span the vector space if every vector in the space can be written as a linear combination of the spanning set vectors.
- A set of vectors is linearly independent if none of the vectors can be written as a linear combination of the other vectors in the set.
- A basis is a minimal spanning set, i.e. it is a linearly independent spanning set with no redundant vectors.
- Every vector space has a basis, and all bases for a vector space have the same number of elements, called the dimension of the vector space.

### 3. Subspaces and Quotient Spaces
- A subspace is a subset of vectors in a vector space that is also a vector space when the vector space operations are restricted to the subset.
- The quotient space is constructed by identifying vectors in a vector space that differ by a vector in some subspace. It results in a vector space of a smaller dimension than the original space.
- Quotient spaces are useful for categorizing or clustering vectors in a vector space based on their differences lying within a particular subspace.
