## Unit 4 - Vector Spaces

1. Vector - A quantity that has magnitude and direction is called a vector. It is represented by an arrow drawn from the origin.
2. Vector Addition - Two vectors can be added using the parallelogram law or triangle law. The resultant vector is the sum of the two vectors.
3. Scalar Multiplication - A vector can be multiplied by a scalar (real number) to produce another vector that is in the same direction but has a magnitude that is scaled up or down.
4. Equal Vectors - Two vectors are equal if they have the same magnitude and same direction.
5. Unit Vector - A vector with magnitude 1 is called a unit vector. It is obtained by dividing a vector by its magnitude.
6. Basis and Span - A set of vectors that can be linearly combined to produce any vector in a vector space is called a basis. The set of all vectors produced by a basis is called the span of the basis.
7. Linear Independence - A set of vectors are linearly independent if none can be expressed as a linear combination of the others. A basis must consist of linearly independent vectors.
8. Dimension - The minimum number of vectors required to generate a basis is called the dimension of the vector space. A vector space is finite-dimensional if it has a finite basis.
