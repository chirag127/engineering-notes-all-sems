## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

1. Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by algorithms to simulate randomness. They are not truly random but appear random for most practical purposes.
2. Pseudo-random number generators use a seed value or initial state to generate a sequence of numbers. A different seed will produce a different sequence.
3. The quality of a PRNG depends on how random the numbers it produces appear to be. This is measured using statistical tests. Good PRNGs should pass a range of such tests, e.g. for uniformity, independence, correlations, etc.
4. Examples of PRNG algorithms include:
- Linear congruential generator
- Mersenne twister
- Blum Blum Shub

5. Applications of pseudo-random numbers include:
- Simulations (e.g. Monte Carlo)
- Cryptography
- Sampling
- Computer games

6. It's important that the period (length of sequence) of a PRNG is very long to avoid repeats. The numbers should also be uniformly distributed and uncorrelated. Otherwise, there are security and applicability risks.
