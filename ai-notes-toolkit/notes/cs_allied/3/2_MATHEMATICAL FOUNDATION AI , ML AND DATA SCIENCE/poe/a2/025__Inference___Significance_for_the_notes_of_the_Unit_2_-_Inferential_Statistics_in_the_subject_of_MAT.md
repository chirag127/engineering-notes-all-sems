### Inference & Significance

- Inferential statistics is used to infer properties of a population from a sample. It allows us to draw conclusions about a large group based on a subset of the data.
- A significance test is used to assess whether an observed effect is "real" or just due to chance. It calculates a p-value, which is the probability of observing the given effect (or an even more extreme effect) if the null hypothesis is true.
- If the p-value is very small (typically less than 0.05), we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the observed effect is statistically significant. This means it is unlikely to have occurred by chance alone.
- Confidence intervals provide a range of plausible values for a population parameter. For example, a 95% confidence interval means we are 95% confident that the true population parameter lies within the given interval.
- Wide confidence intervals indicate more uncertainty, while narrow intervals indicate the estimate is more precise. The margin of error depends on the sample size and confidence level.
- Statistical inference makes assumptions about the data and population to arrive at conclusions. The validity of inferences depends on whether these assumptions approximately hold true. If assumptions are violated, inferences may be misleading.
