### Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)

- Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is a class of algorithms for sampling from a probability distribution that is difficult to sample from directly.
- MCMC works by constructing a Markov chain that has the target distribution as its equilibrium or stationary distribution, and then running the chain for a long enough time to reach the equilibrium.
- A Markov chain is a stochastic process that moves from one state to another with some probability that depends only on the current state and not on the previous states. The equilibrium distribution of a Markov chain is the probability distribution of the states when the chain has reached a steady state, where the probabilities of the states do not change over time.
- MCMC can be used to estimate various quantities of interest from the target distribution, such as the mean, variance, mode, quantiles, etc. by taking the average, variance, maximum, etc. of the samples generated by the chain.
- MCMC can also be used to perform Bayesian inference, where the target distribution is the posterior distribution of some unknown parameters given some observed data and a prior distribution. MCMC can provide samples from the posterior distribution that can be used to calculate the posterior mean, credible intervals, posterior predictive distributions, etc.
- There are different types of MCMC algorithms, such as Gibbs sampling, Metropolis-Hastings algorithm, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, etc. They differ in how they construct and update the Markov chain, but they all share the same goal of converging to the target distribution.
- MCMC algorithms have some advantages and disadvantages. Some advantages are that they can handle complex and high-dimensional distributions, they do not require the knowledge of the normalization constant of the target distribution, and they can explore the whole support of the target distribution. Some disadvantages are that they can be slow to converge, they can get stuck in local modes, and they can be sensitive to the choice of the initial state and the transition probabilities of the chain.
