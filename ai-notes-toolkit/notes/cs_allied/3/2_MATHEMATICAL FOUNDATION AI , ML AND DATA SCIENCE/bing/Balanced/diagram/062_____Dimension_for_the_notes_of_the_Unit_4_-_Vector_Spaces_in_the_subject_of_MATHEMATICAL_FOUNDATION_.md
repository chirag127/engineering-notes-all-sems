### Dimension

- The dimension of a vector space is the number of vectors in a basis of the vector space .
- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the vector space.
- The dimension of a vector space is denoted by dim(V), where V is the vector space.
- The dimension of a vector space is a measure of its size and complexity.
- The dimension of a vector space is unique, meaning that any two bases of the same vector space have the same number of vectors.
- The dimension of a vector space can be zero, finite, or infinite.

#### Examples

- The trivial vector space {0}, which contains only the zero vector, has dimension zero.
- The vector space R^n, which consists of all n-tuples of real numbers, has dimension n.
- The vector space P_n, which consists of all polynomials of degree at most n with real coefficients, has dimension n+1.
- The vector space M_mn, which consists of all m x n matrices with real entries, has dimension mn.
- The vector space C([0,1]), which consists of all continuous functions from [0,1] to R, has infinite dimension.
