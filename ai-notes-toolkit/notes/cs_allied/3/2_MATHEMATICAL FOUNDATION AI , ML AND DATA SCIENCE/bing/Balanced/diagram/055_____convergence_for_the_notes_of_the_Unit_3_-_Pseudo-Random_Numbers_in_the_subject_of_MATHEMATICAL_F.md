### Convergence for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI, ML AND DATA SCIENCE

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that simulates randomness, but are not truly random.
- A pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) is a function that takes a short random seed and outputs a longer bit sequence that “appears random.”
- A common PRNG technique is called the linear congruential method. The pseudo-random numbers are generated using following equation:

`Xn+1 = (aXn + c) mod m`

where,

`Xn` is the previous pseudo number

`a` is a constant multiplier

`c` is a constant increment

`m` is a constant modulus

- The choice of `a`, `c`, and `m` affects the quality and period of the pseudo-random numbers. For example, for `a = 1`, it will be the additive congruence method.
- A PRNG is said to converge if the distribution of the generated numbers approaches a desired probability distribution as the number of samples increases.
- Convergence is important for applications that use pseudo-random numbers for simulation, estimation, or optimization, such as Monte Carlo methods, cryptography, or machine learning.
- Convergence can be measured by various statistical tests, such as the chi-square test, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, or the runs test.
- Pseudo-random numbers are less uniform than quasirandom numbers and may be more appropriate for applications that require greater randomness. Quasirandom numbers are generated by a different approach that avoids clustering and can speed up convergence, but they are generally too uniform to pass randomness tests.
