### Chi-square test

- A chi-square test is a statistical hypothesis test used to compare observed and expected frequencies of categorical data.
- It can be used to test the independence or association of two or more categorical variables, the goodness of fit of a theoretical distribution to observed data, or the homogeneity or difference of population variances .
- The test statistic, chi-square (Χ²), is calculated by summing the squared differences between the observed and expected frequencies, divided by the expected frequencies:

- The test statistic follows a chi-square distribution with degrees of freedom (df) equal to the number of categories minus one.
- The null hypothesis (H0) of a chi-square test is that the observed and expected frequencies are equal, or that the variables are independent, or that the population variance is equal to a specified value .
- The alternative hypothesis (H1) is that the observed and expected frequencies are not equal, or that the variables are dependent, or that the population variance is not equal to a specified value .
- To perform a chi-square test, the following steps are followed:

  - Define the null and alternative hypotheses.
  - Choose a significance level (α), usually 0.05 or 0.01.
  - Calculate the expected frequencies based on the null hypothesis.
  - Calculate the test statistic using the formula above.
  - Find the critical value of chi-square from a table or a calculator, using the df and α.
  - Compare the test statistic and the critical value, and reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.
  - Interpret the results in terms of the research question.

- A chi-square test can be used for various applications, such as testing the association between gender and political preference, testing the goodness of fit of a dice or a coin, or testing the homogeneity of variances of different groups  .
