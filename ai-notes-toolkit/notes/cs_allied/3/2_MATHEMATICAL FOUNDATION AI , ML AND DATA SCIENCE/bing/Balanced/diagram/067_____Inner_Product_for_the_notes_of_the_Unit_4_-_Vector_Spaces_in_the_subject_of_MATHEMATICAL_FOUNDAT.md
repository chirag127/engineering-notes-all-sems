### Inner Product

- An inner product is a way to multiply vectors together, with the result being a scalar.
- An inner product is also called a dot product or a scalar product.
- An inner product can be used to define geometric notions, such as lengths, angles, and orthogonality of vectors.
- An inner product space is a vector space with an inner product defined on it.
- An inner product must satisfy the following four properties for any vectors u, v, w and scalar a in a real vector space :

  1. Commutativity: (u, v) = (v, u)
  2. Linearity: (u + v, w) = (u, w) + (v, w) and (au, v) = a(u, v)
  3. Positivity: (u, u) ≥ 0 and (u, u) = 0 if and only if u = 0
  4. Symmetry: (u, v) = (v, u)

- An example of an inner product is the standard inner product on R^n^, given by (u, v) = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + ... + u_n v_n for any vectors u = (u_1, u_2, ..., u_n) and v = (v_1, v_2, ..., v_n) in R^n^ .
- Another example of an inner product is the complex inner product on C^n^, given by (u, v) = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + ... + u_n v_n for any vectors u = (u_1, u_2, ..., u_n) and v = (v_1, v_2, ..., v_n) in C^n^, where v_i is the complex conjugate of v_i for each i. Note that this inner product is anti-linear in the second slot, that is, (u, v + w) = (u, v) + (u, w) and (u, av) = a(u, v) for any vectors u, v, w and scalar a in C^n^.
