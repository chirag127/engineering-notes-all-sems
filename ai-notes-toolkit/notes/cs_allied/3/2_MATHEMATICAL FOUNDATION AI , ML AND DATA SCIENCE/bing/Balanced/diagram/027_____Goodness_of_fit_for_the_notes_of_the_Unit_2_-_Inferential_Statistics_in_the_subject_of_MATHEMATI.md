### Goodness of fit

- Goodness of fit is a concept that measures how well a statistical model fits a set of observed data  .
- It is used to test whether the sample data is representative of the population data, or whether there is a significant difference between them.
- It can also be used to compare different models and select the one that best describes the data.
- There are different methods and tests to assess the goodness of fit, depending on the type and distribution of the data, such as chi-square test, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, Anderson-Darling test, etc.
- The null hypothesis of a goodness of fit test is usually that the sample data follows the expected distribution or model, and the alternative hypothesis is that it does not.
- The test statistic is calculated based on the discrepancy between the observed and expected frequencies or values, and compared with a critical value or a p-value to determine whether to reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.
- A low test statistic or a high p-value indicates a good fit, while a high test statistic or a low p-value indicates a poor fit.
- The level of significance or alpha is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true, and it is usually set at 0.05 or 0.01.
- The goodness of fit test is useful for inferential statistics, as it allows us to test hypotheses and make inferences about the population parameters based on the sample data.
