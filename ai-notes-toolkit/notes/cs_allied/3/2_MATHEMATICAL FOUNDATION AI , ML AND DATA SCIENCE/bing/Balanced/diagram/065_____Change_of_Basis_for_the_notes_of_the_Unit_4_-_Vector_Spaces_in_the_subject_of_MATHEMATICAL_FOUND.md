### Change of Basis

- Change of basis is a technique applied to finite-dimensional vector spaces in order to rewrite vectors in terms of a different set of basis elements .
- A basis is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the vector space.
- The coordinates of a vector depend on the choice of basis. Different bases may have different numbers of elements, different lengths, and different orientations.
- To change the basis of a vector, we need to find a linear transformation that maps the old basis to the new basis .
- The matrix of this linear transformation is called the change of basis matrix. It has the property that multiplying a vector by this matrix gives the coordinates of the vector in the new basis .
- The change of basis matrix can be found by writing the new basis vectors as linear combinations of the old basis vectors, and then arranging the coefficients as columns of the matrix .
- Alternatively, the change of basis matrix can be found by writing the old basis vectors as linear combinations of the new basis vectors, and then taking the inverse of the matrix formed by the coefficients .
- The change of basis matrix is unique for a given pair of bases, and it is invertible. The inverse of the change of basis matrix is the matrix that changes the basis in the opposite direction .
- The change of basis matrix can be used to rewrite a linear transformation in terms of a different basis. This can simplify the computation and interpretation of the linear transformation .
- The change of basis matrix can also be used to find the similarity between two matrices. Two matrices are similar if they represent the same linear transformation in different bases. The similarity transformation is given by multiplying the matrix by the change of basis matrix on the left and its inverse on the right .
