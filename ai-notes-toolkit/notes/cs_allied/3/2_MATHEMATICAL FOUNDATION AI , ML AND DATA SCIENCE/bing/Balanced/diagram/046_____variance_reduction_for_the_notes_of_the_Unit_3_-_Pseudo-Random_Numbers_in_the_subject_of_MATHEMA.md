### Variance Reduction

Variance reduction is a set of techniques that aim to improve the accuracy and efficiency of Monte Carlo simulations by reducing the variance of the estimator without changing its expected value. Variance reduction can help to achieve a desired level of precision with fewer simulations, saving computational time and resources.

Some of the common variance reduction techniques are:

- **Common random numbers (CRN)**: This technique applies when comparing two or more alternative configurations of a system, such as different policies or designs. CRN uses the same random numbers to generate the inputs for each configuration, so that the outputs are more correlated and the difference between them has less variance. CRN can also be called correlated sampling, matched streams or matched pairs.
- **Control variates (CV)**: This technique uses a known quantity that is correlated with the quantity of interest to reduce the variance of the estimator. CV involves subtracting a multiple of the control variate from the original estimator and adding back the expected value of the control variate. The multiple is chosen to minimize the variance of the new estimator. CV is useful when a crude version of the problem can be solved explicitly, such as the Black-Scholes formula for option pricing.
- **Partial integration (PI)**: This technique reduces the variance by replacing some integrals over random variables or regions of space by their exact values. PI can also be called Rao-Blackwellization, and it exploits the conditional expectation property of the estimator. PI can be applied when some parts of the problem can be solved analytically or numerically.
- **Systematic sampling (SS)**: This technique uses a more structured way of generating random numbers to reduce the variance of the estimator. SS can include methods such as antithetic variates, stratified sampling, and quasi-Monte Carlo integration. Antithetic variates use pairs of random numbers that are negatively correlated, such as 1-x and x, to cancel out some of the variation. Stratified sampling divides the sample space into subregions and allocates a fixed number of samples to each subregion, ensuring a more uniform coverage. Quasi-Monte Carlo integration uses low-discrepancy sequences of numbers that are more evenly distributed than random numbers, such as the Halton or Sobol sequences.
- **Importance sampling (IS)**: This technique changes the probability distribution of the random variables to make the estimator more sensitive to the quantity of interest. IS involves sampling from a different distribution than the original one, and weighting the estimator by the ratio of the original and the new probabilities. IS can also be used for sensitivity analysis and rare event simulation. IS is effective when the new distribution is close to the optimal distribution that minimizes the variance of the estimator .

These techniques can be used individually or in combination, depending on the problem and the available information. Variance reduction can help to make Monte Carlo simulations more reliable and efficient, and to obtain more accurate estimates of the quantities of interest.
