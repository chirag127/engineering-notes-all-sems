### Probability

- Probability is a measure of how likely an event is to occur in a random experiment.
- An event is a subset of the sample space, which is the set of all possible outcomes of the experiment.
- The probability of an event A is denoted by P(A) and satisfies the following properties:
  - 0 ≤ P(A) ≤ 1 for any event A
  - P(S) = 1, where S is the sample space
  - P(∅) = 0, where ∅ is the empty set
  - If A and B are mutually exclusive events, then P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)
  - P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B), if A and B are independent events
  - P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B), for any events A and B
  - P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B|A), where P(B|A) is the conditional probability of B given A
  - P(A) = P(A ∩ B) + P(A ∩ B'), where B' is the complement of B
- There are different types of probability, such as:
  - Classical probability: based on the assumption of equally likely outcomes
  - Empirical probability: based on the relative frequency of observed outcomes
  - Subjective probability: based on the personal judgment or belief of the individual
  - Axiomatic probability: based on the axioms of probability theory
- Some common probability distributions are:
  - Binomial distribution: models the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials with a constant probability of success
  - Poisson distribution: models the number of occurrences of a rare event in a fixed interval of time or space
  - Normal distribution: models the distribution of continuous variables that are influenced by many factors
  - Exponential distribution: models the time between successive occurrences of a rare event
  - Uniform distribution: models the distribution of discrete or continuous variables that are equally likely in a given range
- Some important concepts and formulas in probability are:
  - Expected value: the weighted average of all possible values of a random variable
  - Variance: the measure of how spread out the values of a random variable are around the expected value
  - Standard deviation: the square root of the variance
  - Covariance: the measure of how two random variables vary together
  - Correlation: the normalized measure of the linear relationship between two random variables
  - Bayes' theorem: a formula that relates the conditional probabilities of two events
  - Law of large numbers: a theorem that states that the sample mean of a large number of independent trials converges to the population mean
  - Central limit theorem: a theorem that states that the sampling distribution of the sample mean of a large number of independent trials is approximately normal
