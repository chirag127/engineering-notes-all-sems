# Measures of Central Tendency

- Measures of central tendency are summary statistics that attempt to describe a whole set of data with a single value that represents the middle or centre of its distribution.
- Colloquially, measures of central tendency are often called averages.
- The most common measures of central tendency are the arithmetic mean, the median, and the mode    .
- The mean is the sum of all values divided by the total number of values . It is also known as the arithmetic average.
- The median is the middle number in an ordered dataset . It is also known as the 50th percentile.
- The mode is the most frequent value in a dataset . It is also known as the modal value.
- Different measures of central tendency may be more appropriate for different types of data or different purposes of analysis .
- For example, the mean is sensitive to outliers (extreme values) and may not reflect the typical value of a skewed distribution, while the median is more robust and resistant to outliers .
- The mode can be used for any type of data, including nominal (categorical) data, but it may not be unique or meaningful if there are multiple modes or no mode in a dataset .
- Measures of central tendency are often used in conjunction with measures of dispersion (such as range, variance, and standard deviation) to describe the shape and spread of a distribution .
