# Diagrammatic Representation of Data

- Diagrammatic representation of data is the use of diagrams to display and summarize numerical data in a clear and effective way .
- Diagrams are geometrical figures, such as lines, bars, and circles, that can show trends, outliers, and patterns in data .
- Diagrams can make the study of data more interesting and easy to understand, as well as provide visual aid to the reader .
- Diagrams can also help in presenting data to non-technical audiences and communicating the main findings or insights from the data .
- Some of the common types of diagrams used in data representation are:

  - Line diagrams: These are diagrams that use straight or curved lines to show the changes in a variable over time or across categories . For example, a line diagram can show the sales of a product over different months or the population of a country over different years.
  - Bar diagrams: These are diagrams that use rectangular bars of equal width to show the magnitude or frequency of a variable for different categories . For example, a bar diagram can show the number of students in different grades or the market share of different brands.
  - Pie diagrams: These are diagrams that use circular sectors to show the relative proportions of a variable for different categories . For example, a pie diagram can show the percentage of votes for different candidates or the distribution of income for different groups.
  - Histograms: These are diagrams that use rectangular bars of varying width to show the frequency distribution of a continuous variable . For example, a histogram can show the number of students with different heights or the number of cars with different speeds.
  - Frequency polygons: These are diagrams that use connected dots to show the frequency distribution of a continuous variable . For example, a frequency polygon can show the number of students with different marks or the number of houses with different prices.
  - Ogives: These are diagrams that use connected dots to show the cumulative frequency distribution of a continuous variable . For example, an ogive can show the percentage of students with marks less than or equal to a given value or the percentage of houses with prices less than or equal to a given value.
  - Scatter diagrams: These are diagrams that use dots to show the relationship between two variables . For example, a scatter diagram can show the correlation between the height and weight of students or the demand and supply of a product.
  - Box plots: These are diagrams that use a box and whiskers to show the summary statistics of a variable, such as the minimum, maximum, median, quartiles, and outliers . For example, a box plot can show the variation in the marks of students in different subjects or the variation in the prices of houses in different areas.
