## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that appear to be random but are generated by a deterministic algorithm.
- Pseudo-random numbers are useful for applications such as cryptography, simulation, gaming, and statistical analysis.
- Pseudo-random numbers are not truly random because they can be reproduced by knowing the algorithm and the initial state or seed.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that produce pseudo-random numbers from a seed.
- PRNGs can be classified into two types: linear and nonlinear.
- Linear PRNGs use arithmetic operations such as addition, multiplication, and modulo to generate pseudo-random numbers. Examples of linear PRNGs are linear congruential generators (LCGs) and linear feedback shift registers (LFSRs).
- Nonlinear PRNGs use more complex operations such as bitwise operations, exponentiation, and hashing to generate pseudo-random numbers. Examples of nonlinear PRNGs are Blum Blum Shub (BBS), Mersenne Twister (MT), and Secure Hash Algorithm (SHA).
- PRNGs can be evaluated by various criteria such as period, uniformity, independence, and unpredictability.
- Period is the number of pseudo-random numbers that can be generated before the sequence repeats. A longer period is desirable to avoid repetition and correlation.
- Uniformity is the degree to which the pseudo-random numbers are evenly distributed over the range of possible values. A uniform distribution is desirable to avoid bias and clustering.
- Independence is the degree to which the pseudo-random numbers are not influenced by previous or future values. An independent sequence is desirable to avoid patterns and cycles.
- Unpredictability is the degree to which the pseudo-random numbers are hard to guess or reproduce by an adversary. An unpredictable sequence is desirable to ensure security and privacy.
