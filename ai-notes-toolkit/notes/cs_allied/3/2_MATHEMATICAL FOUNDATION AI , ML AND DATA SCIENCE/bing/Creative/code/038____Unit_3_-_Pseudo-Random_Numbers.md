## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that appear to be random but are actually generated by a deterministic algorithm.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that produce pseudo-random numbers from a seed value, which is usually a small integer or a string.
- PRNGs are useful for applications that require randomness, such as cryptography, simulations, games, and statistical analysis.
- PRNGs have different properties and characteristics, such as period, uniformity, independence, and unpredictability, that affect their quality and suitability for different purposes.
- Some common types of PRNGs are:
  - Linear congruential generators (LCGs), which use a simple recurrence relation to generate numbers.
  - Mersenne Twister (MT), which is a widely used PRNG that has a very long period and good statistical properties.
  - Blum Blum Shub (BBS), which is a cryptographically secure PRNG that is based on the hardness of factoring large numbers.
  - Xorshift, which is a fast and simple PRNG that uses bitwise operations to generate numbers.
- PRNGs are not truly random, and they can be predictable or biased if the seed value or the algorithm is known or compromised. Therefore, they should not be used for applications that require high security or unpredictability, such as generating cryptographic keys or passwords.
- True random number generators (TRNGs) are devices or methods that produce random numbers from a physical or natural source of randomness, such as atmospheric noise, quantum phenomena, or human input.
- TRNGs are more secure and unpredictable than PRNGs, but they are also slower, more expensive, and harder to obtain or verify. Therefore, they are often used to generate seed values for PRNGs, or to supplement PRNGs for critical applications.
