### Orthonormal (Orthogonal) Basis

- A set of vectors is **orthonormal** if each vector is a **unit vector** (length or norm is equal to 1) and all vectors in the set are **orthogonal** to each other.
- A **basis** is a set of linearly independent vectors that span a vector space.
- An **orthonormal basis** is a basis that is also orthonormal.
- The simplest example of an orthonormal basis is the **standard basis** for Euclidean space, which consists of the unit vectors along each coordinate axis.
- Every finite-dimensional inner product space has an orthonormal basis, which may be obtained from an arbitrary basis using the **Gram–Schmidt process**.
- In functional analysis, the concept of an orthonormal basis can be generalized to arbitrary (infinite-dimensional) inner product spaces.

Some properties of orthonormal bases are:

- The inner product of any two basis vectors is zero, i.e. <u,v> = 0 for u ≠ v.
- The inner product of any basis vector with itself is one, i.e. <u,u> = 1 for any u.
- The norm of any basis vector is one, i.e. ||u|| = 1 for any u.
- The number of basis vectors is equal to the dimension of the vector space.
- The matrix representation of a linear transformation with respect to an orthonormal basis is **orthogonal**, i.e. its transpose is equal to its inverse.
- The coordinates of a vector with respect to an orthonormal basis are equal to its inner products with the basis vectors, i.e. x = [<x,u1>, <x,u2>, ..., <x,un>] for an orthonormal basis {u1, u2, ..., un}.
