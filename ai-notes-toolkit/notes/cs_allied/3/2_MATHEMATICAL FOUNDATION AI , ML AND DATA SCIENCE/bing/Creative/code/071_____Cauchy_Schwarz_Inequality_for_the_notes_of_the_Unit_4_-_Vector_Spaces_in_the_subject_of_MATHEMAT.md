### Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality

- The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is one of the most important and widely used inequalities in mathematics. It states that for any two vectors **u** and **v** in an inner product space, the following inequality holds:

  `|<u, v>| ≤ ||u|| ||v||`

  where `<u, v>` is the inner product of **u** and **v**, and `||u||` and `||v||` are their norms.

- The equality holds if and only if **u** and **v** are linearly dependent, that is, one is a scalar multiple of the other.

- The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality can be used to prove many other results in linear algebra, such as the triangle inequality, the parallelogram law, the angle between two vectors, and the orthogonality of eigenvectors.

- The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality can also be generalized to other settings, such as sums, integrals, and functions. For example, for any two real-valued functions f and g on a set X, the following inequality holds:

  `|∫X f(x) g(x) dx| ≤ (∫X f(x)^2 dx)^1/2 (∫X g(x)^2 dx)^1/2`

  where the integrals are taken with respect to some measure on X.

- The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality can also be derived from the more general Hölder's inequality, which states that for any two functions f and g on a set X, and any positive numbers p and q such that 1/p + 1/q = 1, the following inequality holds:

  `|∫X f(x) g(x) dx| ≤ (∫X |f(x)|^p dx)^1/p (∫X |g(x)|^q dx)^1/q`

  By setting p = q = 2, we obtain the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for functions.

- The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality was first published by Augustin-Louis Cauchy in 1821 for sums, and later generalized by Hermann Schwarz in 1888 for inner product spaces. It is also known as the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality, as Viktor Bunyakovsky independently discovered it in 1859 for integrals.
