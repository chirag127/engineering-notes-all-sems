### Stratified Sampling

Stratified sampling is a method of sampling from a population that can be divided into subgroups or strata that share some common characteristics. The purpose of stratified sampling is to ensure that each subgroup is adequately represented in the sample, and to reduce the sampling error and increase the precision of the estimates.

Some examples of stratified sampling are:

- Sampling students from different grades or majors in a school.
- Sampling voters from different regions or political parties in a country.
- Sampling customers from different age groups or income levels in a market.

The steps to perform stratified sampling are:

- Define the population and the subgroups of interest.
- Separate the population into non-overlapping strata based on the subgroups.
- Select a random sample from each stratum, either proportionally or equally.
- Combine the samples from each stratum to form the final sample.

There are two types of stratified sampling:

- Proportional stratified sampling: The sample size of each stratum is proportional to the population size of the stratum. For example, if the population consists of 40% males and 60% females, and the sample size is 100, then the sample should have 40 males and 60 females.
- Equal stratified sampling: The sample size of each stratum is equal, regardless of the population size of the stratum. For example, if the population consists of 40% males and 60% females, and the sample size is 100, then the sample should have 50 males and 50 females.

Stratified sampling has some advantages and disadvantages:

- Advantages: It can improve the accuracy and representativeness of the sample, it can reduce the variability and bias of the estimates, it can allow for comparisons and analysis of different subgroups, and it can be more efficient and cost-effective than simple random sampling.
- Disadvantages: It can be difficult and time-consuming to identify and separate the strata, it can require a larger sample size than simple random sampling, it can be affected by the choice of the strata and the sample size of each stratum, and it can be more complex and require more calculations than simple random sampling.
