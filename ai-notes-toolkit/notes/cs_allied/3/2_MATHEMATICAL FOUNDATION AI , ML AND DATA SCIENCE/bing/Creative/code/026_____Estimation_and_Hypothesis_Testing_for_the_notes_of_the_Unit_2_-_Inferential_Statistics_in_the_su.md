### Estimation and Hypothesis Testing

- Estimation is the process of using sample data to estimate a population parameter, such as the mean, the proportion, the variance, etc.
- Hypothesis testing is the process of using sample data to test a claim or an assumption about a population parameter, such as whether the mean is equal to a certain value, whether the proportion is greater than a certain value, whether the variance is different between two groups, etc.
- Both estimation and hypothesis testing are based on the concept of sampling distribution, which is the distribution of a sample statistic over repeated samples from the same population.
- Estimation can be divided into two types: point estimation and interval estimation.
  - Point estimation is the process of finding a single value that best represents the population parameter, such as the sample mean, the sample proportion, the sample variance, etc.
  - Interval estimation is the process of finding a range of values that contains the population parameter with a certain level of confidence, such as the confidence interval, the prediction interval, the tolerance interval, etc.
- Hypothesis testing can be divided into two types: parametric and nonparametric.
  - Parametric hypothesis testing is the process of testing a claim or an assumption about a population parameter using a sample statistic that follows a known distribution, such as the normal distribution, the t-distribution, the chi-square distribution, etc.
  - Nonparametric hypothesis testing is the process of testing a claim or an assumption about a population parameter using a sample statistic that does not follow a known distribution, such as the rank-sum test, the sign test, the Wilcoxon test, etc.
- The general steps of hypothesis testing are as follows:
  - Step 1: State the null and alternative hypotheses, which are the opposite claims or assumptions about the population parameter.
  - Step 2: Collect the sample data and calculate the sample statistic and the standard error.
  - Step 3: Perform a statistical test to find the test statistic and the p-value, which are the measures of how likely the sample data are under the null hypothesis.
  - Step 4: Decide whether to reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis based on the p-value and the significance level, which is the threshold of how much evidence is needed to reject the null hypothesis.
  - Step 5: Present the findings and interpret the results in the context of the problem.
