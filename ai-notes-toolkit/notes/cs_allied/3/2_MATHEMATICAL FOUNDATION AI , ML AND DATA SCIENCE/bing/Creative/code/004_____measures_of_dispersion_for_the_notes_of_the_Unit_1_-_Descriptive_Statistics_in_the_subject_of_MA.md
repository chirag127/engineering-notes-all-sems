### Measures of Dispersion

- Measures of dispersion are numerical values that describe how the data values are spread out or vary around the central tendency (mean, median or mode) of the data set.
- Measures of dispersion are also called measures of variability or measures of spread.
- Measures of dispersion are useful for comparing the variability of two or more data sets, or for assessing the reliability or accuracy of a statistic.
- Some common measures of dispersion are:

  - **Range:** The difference between the maximum and minimum values in the data set. It is the simplest measure of dispersion, but it is sensitive to outliers and does not consider the distribution of the data.
  - **Interquartile Range (IQR):** The difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1) of the data set. It is the range of the middle 50% of the data values. It is more robust than the range, as it is not affected by outliers or extreme values.
  - **Variance:** The average of the squared deviations of the data values from the mean. It measures how far the data values are from the mean on average. It is a widely used measure of dispersion, but it is not easy to interpret, as it is in squared units.
  - **Standard Deviation:** The square root of the variance. It measures how far the data values are from the mean on average. It is the most common measure of dispersion, as it is in the same units as the data and the mean.
  - **Coefficient of Variation (CV):** The ratio of the standard deviation to the mean, expressed as a percentage. It measures the relative variability of the data values. It is useful for comparing the dispersion of two or more data sets with different units or scales.
