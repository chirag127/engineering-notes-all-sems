# Markov Inequality

- Markov inequality is a result in probability theory that gives an upper bound for the probability that a non-negative function of a random variable is greater than or equal to some positive constant.
- Formally, if X is a non-negative random variable and a > 0, then

$$
P(X \geq a) \leq \frac{E[X]}{a}
$$

where E[X] is the expected value of X .

- Markov inequality is useful because it holds for any distribution with positive values, regardless of its shape or other features.
- Markov inequality can be extended to non-decreasing functions of random variables, such as powers or exponentials.
- Markov inequality can be used to derive other inequalities, such as Chebyshev's inequality and Chernoff's bound .
- Markov inequality can be applied to real-world problems, such as estimating the fraction of a population that has more than a certain income level.
