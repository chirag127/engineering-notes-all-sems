### Chi-square
- Chi-square is a statistical test that measures how well the observed data fit the expected data according to a certain hypothesis.
- Chi-square can be used to test the independence of two categorical variables, the goodness of fit of a categorical variable to a theoretical distribution, or the homogeneity of proportions of a categorical variable across different groups.
- Chi-square is calculated as the sum of the squared differences between the observed and expected frequencies, divided by the expected frequencies, for each cell in a contingency table.
- Chi-square has a sampling distribution that follows the chi-square distribution, which depends on the degrees of freedom of the test. The degrees of freedom are equal to the number of categories minus one, or the product of the number of rows minus one and the number of columns minus one, depending on the type of test.
- Chi-square can be compared to a critical value from the chi-square distribution table, or a p-value can be calculated using the chi-square distribution function, to determine the significance of the test result.
- Chi-square has some assumptions and limitations, such as the requirement of large sample sizes, the sensitivity to outliers, and the inability to handle ordinal or continuous variables.
