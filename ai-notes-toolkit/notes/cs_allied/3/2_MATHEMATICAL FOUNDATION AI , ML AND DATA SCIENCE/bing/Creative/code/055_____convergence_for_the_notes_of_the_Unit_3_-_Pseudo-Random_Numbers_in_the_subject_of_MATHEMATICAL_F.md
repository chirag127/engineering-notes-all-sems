### Convergence for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI, ML AND DATA SCIENCE

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that simulates a random process.
- Pseudo-random numbers are not truly random, but they can pass statistical tests of randomness and can be used for various applications that require randomness, such as cryptography, simulation, sampling, etc.
- A pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) is a function that takes a short random seed and outputs a longer bit sequence that “appears random.”
- A common PRNG technique is called the linear congruential method, which generates pseudo-random numbers using the following equation:

```
Xn+1 = (aXn + c) mod m
```

where,

- Xn is the nth pseudo-random number in the sequence
- a is the multiplier (0 < a < m)
- c is the increment (0 <= c < m)
- m is the modulus (m > 0)
- X0 is the seed (0 <= X0 < m)

- The choice of a, c, m, and X0 affects the quality and the period of the pseudo-random sequence. The period is the number of pseudo-random numbers generated before the sequence repeats itself.
- A good PRNG should have a large period, a uniform distribution, and a high degree of unpredictability.
- Convergence is a property of a sequence of numbers that indicates how close the sequence gets to a certain value or a certain distribution as the number of terms increases.
- Convergence is important for pseudo-random numbers because it measures how well they approximate the desired randomness and how fast they achieve it.
- There are different types of convergence, such as pointwise convergence, uniform convergence, almost sure convergence, etc. Each type has a different definition and a different criterion to test for convergence.
- For example, pointwise convergence means that for every fixed point x in the domain, the sequence of functions fn(x) converges to a limit function f(x) as n goes to infinity. Uniform convergence means that the sequence of functions fn(x) converges to f(x) uniformly, that is, the difference between fn(x) and f(x) can be made arbitrarily small for all x in the domain by choosing a sufficiently large n.
- One way to test for convergence of pseudo-random numbers is to use statistical tests, such as the chi-square test, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, the runs test, etc. These tests compare the observed frequency or distribution of the pseudo-random numbers with the expected frequency or distribution of a true random sequence, and calculate a test statistic and a p-value that indicate the likelihood of observing such a difference by chance.
- Another way to test for convergence of pseudo-random numbers is to use graphical methods, such as histograms, scatter plots, autocorrelation plots, etc. These methods visualize the pseudo-random sequence and reveal any patterns, trends, or anomalies that may indicate a lack of randomness or convergence.
