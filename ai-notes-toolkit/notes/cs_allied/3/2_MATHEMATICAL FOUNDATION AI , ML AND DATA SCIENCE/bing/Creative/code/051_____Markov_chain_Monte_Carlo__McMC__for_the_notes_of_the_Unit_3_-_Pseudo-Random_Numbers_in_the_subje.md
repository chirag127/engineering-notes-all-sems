### Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)

- Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is a class of algorithms for sampling from a probability distribution that is difficult to sample from directly.
- MCMC works by constructing a Markov chain that has the desired distribution as its equilibrium or stationary distribution, and then running the chain for a long enough time to reach equilibrium.
- A Markov chain is a stochastic process that moves from one state to another according to some transition probabilities that depend only on the current state and not on the past history of the process.
- The equilibrium distribution of a Markov chain is the limiting distribution of the state probabilities as the number of transitions goes to infinity, assuming that the chain is ergodic (i.e., it can reach any state from any other state with positive probability).
- MCMC methods can be used to approximate integrals, expectations, and other quantities of interest that involve the probability distribution, by using the samples generated by the Markov chain.
- MCMC methods can also be used to perform Bayesian inference, by sampling from the posterior distribution of the parameters given the data and the prior distribution, using Bayes' theorem.
- The two most common MCMC algorithms are Gibbs sampling and Metropolis-Hastings algorithm, which are special cases of a more general framework called the Metropolis algorithm.
- Gibbs sampling is a MCMC algorithm that updates one variable at a time, conditional on the current values of the other variables, using the full conditional distributions of the variables.
- Metropolis-Hastings algorithm is a MCMC algorithm that proposes a new state from a proposal distribution, and then accepts or rejects it based on an acceptance ratio that ensures the detailed balance condition of the Markov chain.
- The choice of the proposal distribution and the initial state of the Markov chain can affect the convergence and efficiency of the MCMC algorithm, and various diagnostic tools and criteria can be used to assess the quality of the samples and the convergence of the chain.
