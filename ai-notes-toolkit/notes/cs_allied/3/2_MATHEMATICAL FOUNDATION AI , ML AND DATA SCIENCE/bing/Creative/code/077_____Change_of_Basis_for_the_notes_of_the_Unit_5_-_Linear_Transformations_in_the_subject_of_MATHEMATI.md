### Change of Basis

- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the whole space.
- A vector can be represented as a linear combination of the basis vectors using unique coefficients called coordinates or components.
- The coordinates of a vector depend on the choice of the basis. Different bases may have different numbers, types and orientations of the basis vectors.
- To compare or transform vectors in different bases, we need to find a way to convert the coordinates from one basis to another. This is called a change of basis.
- A change of basis can be done by using a matrix that maps the old basis vectors to the new basis vectors. This matrix is called the change of basis matrix.
- The change of basis matrix can be found by writing the new basis vectors as column vectors and then multiplying them by the inverse of the matrix formed by writing the old basis vectors as column vectors.
- The change of basis matrix can be used to transform any vector from the old basis to the new basis by multiplying the vector by the matrix.
- The change of basis matrix can also be used to transform a linear transformation from the old basis to the new basis by multiplying the matrix of the linear transformation by the change of basis matrix on the left and its inverse on the right.
- A change of basis preserves the properties of vectors and linear transformations, such as length, angle, rank, determinant, eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
