### Probability

- Probability is a measure of how likely an event is to occur in a random experiment.
- An event is a subset of the sample space, which is the set of all possible outcomes of the experiment.
- The probability of an event A is denoted by P(A) and satisfies the following properties:
  - P(A) is a number between 0 and 1, inclusive. 0 means impossible and 1 means certain.
  - P(S) = 1, where S is the sample space.
  - If A and B are mutually exclusive events, meaning they cannot occur at the same time, then P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).
  - P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B), if A and B are independent events, meaning the occurrence of one does not affect the occurrence of the other.
  - P(A) = 1 - P(A'), where A' is the complement of A, meaning the event that A does not occur.
- There are different types of probability, such as classical, empirical, subjective, and conditional.
  - Classical probability is based on the assumption that all outcomes are equally likely, and is calculated by dividing the number of favorable outcomes by the number of possible outcomes.
  - Empirical probability is based on observed data or experiments, and is calculated by dividing the number of times the event occurred by the number of trials.
  - Subjective probability is based on personal judgment or belief, and may vary from person to person.
  - Conditional probability is the probability of an event given that another event has occurred, and is calculated by dividing the joint probability of both events by the probability of the given event. It is denoted by P(A|B), which means the probability of A given B.
