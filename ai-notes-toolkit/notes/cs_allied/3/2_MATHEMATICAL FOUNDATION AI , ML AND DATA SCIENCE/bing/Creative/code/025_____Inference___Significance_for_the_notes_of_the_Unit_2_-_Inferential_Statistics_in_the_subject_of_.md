### Inference & Significance

- Inference is the process of drawing conclusions from data that are subject to random variation, such as observational errors or sampling variation.
- Significance is the degree of confidence that the results of an inference are not due to chance, but reflect a true relationship or difference in the population.
- Inference and significance are based on the following concepts:
  - Population: the entire group of individuals or objects that we want to study.
  - Sample: a subset of the population that we actually observe or measure.
  - Parameter: a numerical characteristic of the population, such as the mean, the standard deviation, or the proportion.
  - Statistic: a numerical characteristic of the sample, such as the sample mean, the sample standard deviation, or the sample proportion.
  - Sampling distribution: the distribution of a statistic over all possible samples of the same size from the same population.
  - Standard error: the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of a statistic, which measures the variability of the statistic due to sampling.
  - Confidence interval: an interval estimate of a population parameter, based on a sample statistic and a level of confidence, which indicates the degree of certainty that the interval contains the true parameter value.
  - Hypothesis testing: a method of making decisions or judgments about a population parameter, based on a sample statistic and a significance level, which indicates the probability of rejecting a true null hypothesis.
  - Null hypothesis: a statement that assumes no difference or relationship between the population parameter and a specified value or another parameter.
  - Alternative hypothesis: a statement that contradicts the null hypothesis and expresses the researcher's claim or expectation about the population parameter.
  - Test statistic: a statistic that measures the discrepancy between the sample statistic and the null hypothesis, in standardized units.
  - P-value: the probability of obtaining a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed one, assuming that the null hypothesis is true, which measures the strength of the evidence against the null hypothesis.
  - Significance level: a predetermined threshold for the p-value, below which the null hypothesis is rejected and the alternative hypothesis is accepted, which indicates the risk of making a type I error (rejecting a true null hypothesis).
  - Type I error: the error of rejecting a true null hypothesis, also known as a false positive.
  - Type II error: the error of failing to reject a false null hypothesis, also known as a false negative.
  - Power: the probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis, which measures the sensitivity of a test to detect a true alternative hypothesis.
