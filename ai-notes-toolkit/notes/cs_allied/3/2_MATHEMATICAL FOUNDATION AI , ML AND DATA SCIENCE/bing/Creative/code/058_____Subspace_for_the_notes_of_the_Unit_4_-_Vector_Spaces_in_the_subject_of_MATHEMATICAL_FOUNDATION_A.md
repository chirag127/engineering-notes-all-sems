# Subspace

- A subspace is a subset of a vector space that is also a vector space itself.
- A subspace must satisfy three conditions:
  - It must contain the zero vector.
  - It must be closed under scalar multiplication, meaning that if a vector is in the subspace, then any scalar multiple of that vector is also in the subspace.
  - It must be closed under vector addition, meaning that if two vectors are in the subspace, then their sum is also in the subspace.
- Examples of subspaces are :
  - The zero subspace, which consists of only the zero vector.
  - Any line passing through the origin in R^2 or R^3.
  - Any plane passing through the origin in R^3 or higher dimensions.
  - The entire vector space itself.
- A subspace can be described by a set of linearly independent vectors that span it, called a basis.
- The dimension of a subspace is the number of vectors in its basis, and it is always less than or equal to the dimension of the original vector space.
- A subspace can also be defined as the solution set of a homogeneous system of linear equations, where the coefficients form the matrix A and the right-hand side is the zero vector.
- A subspace can also be defined as the column space or the row space of a matrix, which are the sets of all possible linear combinations of the columns or the rows of the matrix, respectively.
