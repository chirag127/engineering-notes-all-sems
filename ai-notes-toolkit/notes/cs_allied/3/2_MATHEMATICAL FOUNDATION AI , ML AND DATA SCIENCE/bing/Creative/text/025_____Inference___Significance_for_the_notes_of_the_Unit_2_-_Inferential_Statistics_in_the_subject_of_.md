### Inference & Significance

- Inference is the process of drawing conclusions about a population based on a sample of data from that population.
- Significance is the measure of how likely a sample result is to occur by random chance under a null hypothesis.
- The null hypothesis is a statement that assumes there is no difference or relationship between the variables of interest.
- The alternative hypothesis is a statement that contradicts the null hypothesis and claims there is a difference or relationship between the variables of interest.
- A significance test is a method of comparing the sample result with the null hypothesis and calculating a p-value.
- A p-value is the probability of obtaining a sample result as extreme or more extreme than the observed one, assuming the null hypothesis is true.
- A significance level, or alpha, is a threshold for rejecting the null hypothesis. It is usually set at 0.05 or 0.01, meaning that there is a 5% or 1% chance of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true.
- If the p-value is less than or equal to the significance level, then the sample result is statistically significant and the null hypothesis is rejected.
- If the p-value is greater than the significance level, then the sample result is not statistically significant and the null hypothesis is not rejected.
- Statistical significance does not imply practical significance, which means that the result has a meaningful impact or relevance in the real world.
- There are different types of significance tests for different types of data and hypotheses, such as z-tests, t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square tests, etc.
- The conditions for inference vary depending on the type of test, but generally they include the following:
  - The sample is random and representative of the population of interest.
  - The sample size is large enough to ensure the normality of the sampling distribution or the test statistic.
  - The population parameters, such as the mean or the proportion, are unknown and need to be estimated from the sample.
  - The variables are measured at an appropriate level of measurement, such as nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio.
