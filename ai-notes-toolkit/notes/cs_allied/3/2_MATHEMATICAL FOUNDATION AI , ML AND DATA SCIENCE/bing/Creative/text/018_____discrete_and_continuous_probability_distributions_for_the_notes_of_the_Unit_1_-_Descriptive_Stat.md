### Discrete and Continuous Probability Distributions

- A **probability distribution** is a function that describes all possible values of a random variable as well as the associated probabilities.
- A **random variable** is a variable whose value is determined by the outcome of a random experiment.
- A **discrete random variable** is a random variable that can take only a finite or countable number of values, such as the number of heads in a coin toss, the number of students in a class, or the number of defects in a product.
- A **continuous random variable** is a random variable that can take any value in a continuous range, such as the height of a person, the weight of a fruit, or the temperature of a room.
- A **discrete probability distribution** is a probability distribution that defines the probabilities of a discrete random variable. It can be represented by a table, a formula, or a graph.
- A **continuous probability distribution** is a probability distribution that defines the probabilities of a continuous random variable. It can be represented by a formula or a graph, but not by a table.
- The **probability density function (PDF)** is a function that gives the probability of a continuous random variable falling within a certain interval. The area under the curve of the PDF between two values is equal to the probability of the random variable taking a value in that interval.
- The **cumulative distribution function (CDF)** is a function that gives the probability of a continuous random variable being less than or equal to a certain value. It is the integral of the PDF from negative infinity to that value.
- Some examples of discrete probability distributions are the **binomial distribution**, the **Poisson distribution**, and the **Bernoulli distribution**.
- Some examples of continuous probability distributions are the **normal distribution**, the **exponential distribution**, and the **uniform distribution**.
- The main difference between discrete and continuous probability distributions is that discrete distributions have countable or finite outcomes, while continuous distributions have uncountable or infinite outcomes.
