### Variance Reduction

- Variance reduction is a technique to improve the accuracy and efficiency of Monte Carlo simulations, which use pseudo-random numbers to approximate the solutions of complex mathematical problems.
- Variance reduction aims to reduce the variance of the estimator, which is a measure of how much the estimator fluctuates around its expected value. A lower variance means a more stable and precise estimator.
- Variance reduction can be achieved by using different methods, such as:

  - **Antithetic variates**: This method uses pairs of negatively correlated random variables to cancel out some of the variance. For example, if X is a random variable with mean μ and variance σ^2, then -X is an antithetic variate of X with the same mean and variance. The estimator based on the average of X and -X has a lower variance than the estimator based on X alone.
  - **Control variates**: This method uses a known function of a random variable that is correlated with the function of interest, and adjusts the estimator by subtracting the difference between the known function and its expected value. For example, if X is a random variable with mean μ and variance σ^2, and f(X) is the function of interest, then g(X) is a control variate of f(X) if g(X) is a known function of X that is correlated with f(X). The estimator based on f(X) - b(g(X) - E[g(X)]) has a lower variance than the estimator based on f(X) alone, where b is a constant that depends on the correlation coefficient between f(X) and g(X).
  - **Stratified sampling**: This method divides the sample space into disjoint strata, and samples from each stratum proportionally to its size and importance. For example, if X is a random variable with mean μ and variance σ^2, and f(X) is the function of interest, then the sample space can be partitioned into n strata, such that X_i is the random variable corresponding to the i-th stratum, with mean μ_i and variance σ_i^2. The estimator based on the weighted average of f(X_i) over the n strata has a lower variance than the estimator based on f(X) over the whole sample space, if the strata are chosen such that the variance within each stratum is small and the difference between the means of different strata is large.
  - **Importance sampling**: This method samples from a different distribution than the original one, and adjusts the estimator by multiplying by a ratio of the probabilities of the two distributions. For example, if X is a random variable with mean μ and variance σ^2, and f(X) is the function of interest, then Y is an importance sampling variable of X if Y has a different distribution than X, but the support of Y contains the support of X. The estimator based on f(Y) * p(X) / p(Y) has a lower variance than the estimator based on f(X), if the distribution of Y is chosen such that it assigns more probability to the regions where f(X) is large and p(X) is small.
