### Measures of Dispersion

- Measures of dispersion are numerical values that indicate the variability or spread of a data set.
- They complement the measures of central tendency, such as mean, median and mode, which describe the average or typical value of a data set.
- Measures of dispersion help to understand how much the data values deviate from the central value, and how consistent or inconsistent the data values are.
- Some common measures of dispersion are:

  - **Range**: The difference between the maximum and minimum values in a data set. It is the simplest measure of dispersion, but it is sensitive to outliers and does not consider the distribution of the data.
  - **Interquartile Range (IQR)**: The difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1) in a data set. It is the range of the middle 50% of the data values. It is more robust than the range, as it is not affected by extreme values, but it still does not consider the distribution of the data.
  - **Variance**: The average of the squared deviations of the data values from the mean. It is a measure of the total variation of the data values around the mean. It is more comprehensive than the range and the IQR, as it considers all the data values, but it is difficult to interpret, as it is not in the same unit as the data values.
  - **Standard Deviation**: The square root of the variance. It is a measure of the typical deviation of the data values from the mean. It is more interpretable than the variance, as it is in the same unit as the data values, but it is still sensitive to outliers and skewed distributions.
  - **Coefficient of Variation (CV)**: The ratio of the standard deviation to the mean, expressed as a percentage. It is a measure of the relative variation of the data values around the mean. It is useful for comparing the dispersion of data sets with different units or scales, but it is not meaningful for data sets with negative or zero values.

- Measures of dispersion are important for statistical analysis, as they provide information about the reliability, accuracy and precision of the data values, and the shape and characteristics of the data distribution.
