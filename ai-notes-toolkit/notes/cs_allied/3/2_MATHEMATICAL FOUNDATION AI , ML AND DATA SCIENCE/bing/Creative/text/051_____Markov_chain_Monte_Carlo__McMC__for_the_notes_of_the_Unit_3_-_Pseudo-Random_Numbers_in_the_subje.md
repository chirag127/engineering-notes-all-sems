### Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)

- Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is a class of algorithms for sampling from a probability distribution that is difficult to sample from directly.
- MCMC works by constructing a Markov chain that has the target distribution as its equilibrium or stationary distribution, and then running the chain for a long enough time to reach the equilibrium.
- A Markov chain is a stochastic process that moves from one state to another according to some transition probabilities that depend only on the current state and not on the past history of the process.
- The equilibrium or stationary distribution of a Markov chain is the probability distribution that remains unchanged as the chain evolves over time. It is also the limiting distribution of the chain as the number of steps goes to infinity.
- MCMC methods can be used to estimate various quantities of interest from the target distribution, such as means, variances, quantiles, or probabilities of events. These estimates are based on the samples generated by the Markov chain, and their accuracy depends on the convergence and mixing properties of the chain.
- MCMC methods can also be used to perform Bayesian inference, where the target distribution is the posterior distribution of some unknown parameters given some observed data and a prior distribution. MCMC allows for exploring the posterior distribution and computing posterior summaries and credible intervals.
- There are many variants of MCMC algorithms, but two of the most common and general ones are the Gibbs sampling and the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm.
- Gibbs sampling is a special case of MCMC where the Markov chain updates one component of the state vector at a time, conditional on the rest of the components. This requires knowing the full conditional distributions of each component given the others, which may not always be available or easy to sample from.
- Metropolis-Hastings algorithm is a more general MCMC method that can be applied to any target distribution, as long as a proposal distribution is available that can generate candidate states. The algorithm accepts or rejects the candidate states based on a ratio of the target and proposal densities, which ensures that the equilibrium distribution of the chain is the target distribution. The choice of the proposal distribution affects the efficiency and convergence of the algorithm.
