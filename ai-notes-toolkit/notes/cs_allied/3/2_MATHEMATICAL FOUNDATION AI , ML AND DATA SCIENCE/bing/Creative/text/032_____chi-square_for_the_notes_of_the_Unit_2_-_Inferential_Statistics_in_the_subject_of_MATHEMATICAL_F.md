### Chi-square

- Chi-square is a statistical test that measures how well the observed data fit the expected data according to a certain hypothesis.
- Chi-square can be used for different purposes, such as testing the independence of two categorical variables, testing the goodness of fit of a distribution, or testing the homogeneity of proportions across groups.
- Chi-square is calculated by summing the squared differences between the observed and expected frequencies, divided by the expected frequencies, for each cell in a contingency table.
- Chi-square has a distribution with k-1 degrees of freedom, where k is the number of categories or cells in the table.
- Chi-square can be compared with a critical value from the chi-square distribution table, or with a p-value from a chi-square calculator, to determine the significance of the test result.
- Chi-square has some assumptions and limitations, such as the requirement of large sample size, the sensitivity to outliers, and the inability to handle ordinal or continuous data.
