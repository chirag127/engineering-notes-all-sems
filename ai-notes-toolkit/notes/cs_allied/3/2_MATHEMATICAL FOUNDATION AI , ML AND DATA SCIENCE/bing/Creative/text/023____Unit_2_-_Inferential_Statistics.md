## Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics

- Inferential statistics are methods of drawing conclusions about a population based on a sample of data from that population.
- Inferential statistics involve two steps: estimating a parameter and testing a hypothesis.
- A parameter is a numerical characteristic of a population, such as the mean, the standard deviation, or the proportion.
- An estimate is a numerical value obtained from a sample that approximates a parameter.
- A point estimate is a single value that estimates a parameter, such as the sample mean or the sample proportion.
- An interval estimate is a range of values that estimates a parameter, such as a confidence interval.
- A confidence interval is an interval estimate that has a specified level of confidence, such as 95% or 99%, that the interval contains the true parameter value.
- A hypothesis is a statement or claim about a parameter, such as the mean is equal to a certain value, or the proportion is greater than a certain value.
- A hypothesis test is a procedure of using sample data to evaluate a hypothesis and make a decision to accept or reject it.
- A hypothesis test involves four steps: stating the null and alternative hypotheses, choosing a significance level, calculating a test statistic and a p-value, and making a conclusion.
- The null hypothesis is the hypothesis that is assumed to be true unless there is strong evidence against it. It is usually denoted by H0.
- The alternative hypothesis is the hypothesis that is contrary to the null hypothesis and is what the researcher wants to prove. It is usually denoted by Ha or H1.
- The significance level is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true. It is usually denoted by alpha and is often set at 0.05 or 0.01.
- The test statistic is a numerical value that measures how far the sample data are from the null hypothesis. It is usually calculated by using a formula that depends on the type of test and the sample size.
- The p-value is the probability of obtaining a test statistic as extreme or more extreme than the one observed, assuming the null hypothesis is true. It is a measure of the strength of the evidence against the null hypothesis.
- The conclusion is the final decision to accept or reject the null hypothesis based on the p-value and the significance level. If the p-value is less than or equal to the significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected and the alternative hypothesis is accepted. If the p-value is greater than the significance level, the null hypothesis is not rejected and the alternative hypothesis is not accepted.
