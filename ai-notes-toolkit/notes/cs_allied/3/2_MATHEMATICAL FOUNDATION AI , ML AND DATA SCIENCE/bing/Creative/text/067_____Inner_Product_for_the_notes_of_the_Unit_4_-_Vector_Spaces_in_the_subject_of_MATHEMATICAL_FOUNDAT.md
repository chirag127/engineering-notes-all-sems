### Inner Product

- An inner product is a function that assigns a real number to every pair of vectors in a vector space.
- An inner product satisfies the following properties for any vectors u, v, w and any scalar c:
  - Symmetry: <u, v> = <v, u>
  - Linearity: <u + v, w> = <u, w> + <v, w> and <cu, v> = c<u, v>
  - Positivity: <u, u> >= 0 and <u, u> = 0 if and only if u = 0
- An inner product can be used to define the length or norm of a vector as ||u|| = sqrt(<u, u>)
- An inner product can also be used to define the angle or cosine similarity between two vectors as cos(u, v) = <u, v> / (||u|| ||v||)
- An inner product can be generalized to complex vector spaces by using conjugate symmetry instead of symmetry, i.e. <u, v> = conj(<v, u>)
- An example of an inner product is the dot product of two vectors in R^n, i.e. <u, v> = u1v1 + u2v2 + ... + unvn
- Another example of an inner product is the matrix product of two matrices in M(m, n), i.e. <A, B> = trace(B^T A)
