### Transformations for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that simulates randomness, but are not truly random.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are functions that take a seed (a short random value) and produce a longer sequence of pseudo-random numbers that appear random to an observer.
- PRNGs are useful for many applications that require randomness, such as cryptography, simulation, gaming, etc.
- However, not all PRNGs are equally good. Some PRNGs may have patterns, biases, correlations, or other weaknesses that make them unsuitable for certain purposes.
- Therefore, it is important to evaluate the quality of a PRNG and choose one that meets the desired criteria for randomness and security.
- There are many methods for generating pseudo-random numbers from different probability distributions, such as uniform, normal, exponential, etc. Some of the common methods are:

  - Linear congruential generator (LCG): This is one of the oldest and simplest PRNGs. It is defined by the recurrence relation: Xn+1 = (aXn + c) mod m, where Xn is the nth pseudo-random number, a, c, and m are constants, and mod is the modulo operation. LCGs are fast and easy to implement, but they have some drawbacks, such as low period, poor statistical properties, and non-uniform distribution.
  - Inverse transform sampling: This is a basic method for generating pseudo-random numbers from any probability distribution given its cumulative distribution function (CDF). The idea is to generate a uniform pseudo-random number U in [0, 1] and then apply the inverse of the CDF F to get a pseudo-random number X from the desired distribution: X = F^-1(U). This method is simple and general, but it requires the CDF to be invertible and computable.
  - Summation method: This is a method for generating pseudo-random numbers from a distribution that can be expressed as a sum of other distributions. For example, a normal distribution can be approximated by summing 12 uniform distributions and subtracting 6. This method is easy and fast, but it may introduce some errors and deviations from the true distribution.
  - Transformation method: This is a method for generating pseudo-random numbers from a distribution that can be obtained by applying a transformation to another distribution. For example, an exponential distribution can be generated by taking the negative logarithm of a uniform distribution. This method is convenient and efficient, but it requires the transformation to be monotonic and bijective.
