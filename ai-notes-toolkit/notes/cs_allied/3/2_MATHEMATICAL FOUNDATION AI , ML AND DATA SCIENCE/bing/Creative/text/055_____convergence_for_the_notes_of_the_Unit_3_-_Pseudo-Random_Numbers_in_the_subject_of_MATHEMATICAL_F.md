### Convergence for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI, ML AND DATA SCIENCE

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that simulates randomness, but are not truly random.
- Pseudo-random numbers are useful for applications that require randomness, such as cryptography, simulation, sampling, etc.
- A pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) is a function that takes a short random seed and outputs a longer bit sequence that “appears random.”
- A common PRNG technique is called the linear congruential method, which generates pseudo-random numbers using the following equation: X_n = (aX_n-1 + c) mod m, where a, c, and m are constants and X_0 is the seed. 
- The quality of a PRNG depends on its statistical properties, such as uniformity, independence, and periodicity. A good PRNG should produce a sequence of pseudo-random numbers that are uniformly distributed, independent of each other, and have a long period (the length of the sequence before it repeats).
- Convergence is a property of a sequence of numbers that measures how close the sequence gets to a certain value or limit as the number of terms increases. For example, the sequence 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, ... converges to 0 as the number of terms goes to infinity.
- Convergence is important for PRNGs because it indicates how well the pseudo-random numbers approximate the true random numbers. For example, if a PRNG produces a sequence of pseudo-random numbers that converges to the mean of the true random distribution, then the PRNG is unbiased and has a good uniformity property.
- Convergence can be measured by various methods, such as the mean square error, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, the chi-square test, etc. These methods compare the empirical distribution of the pseudo-random numbers with the theoretical distribution of the true random numbers and calculate a statistic that reflects the difference between them. The smaller the statistic, the better the convergence.
- Quasi-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that produces a low-discrepancy sequence, which means that the sequence covers the sample space evenly and avoids clustering. Quasi-random numbers are more uniform than pseudo-random numbers and may be more appropriate for applications that require greater accuracy and faster convergence, such as numerical integration, optimization, etc.
