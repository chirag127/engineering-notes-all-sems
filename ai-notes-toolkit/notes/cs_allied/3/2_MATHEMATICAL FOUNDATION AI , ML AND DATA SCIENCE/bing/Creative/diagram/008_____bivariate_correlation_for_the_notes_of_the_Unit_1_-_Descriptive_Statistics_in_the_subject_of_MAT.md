### Bivariate Correlation

- Bivariate correlation is a statistical technique that measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables .
- Bivariate correlation can be calculated using different methods, depending on the type and level of measurement of the variables. The most common methods are:
  - Pearson's correlation coefficient (r): This method is used when both variables are continuous and have a linear relationship. It ranges from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative correlation, 0 indicates no correlation, and 1 indicates a perfect positive correlation  .
  - Spearman's rank correlation coefficient (rho): This method is used when one or both variables are ordinal (ranked) or when the relationship is nonlinear. It also ranges from -1 to 1, and it measures the degree of monotonic association between the variables, meaning how well they can be described by a monotone function .
  - Kendall's rank correlation coefficient (tau): This method is also used when one or both variables are ordinal or when the relationship is nonlinear. It ranges from -1 to 1, and it measures the degree of concordance between the variables, meaning how often they agree or disagree in their relative rankings .
- Bivariate correlation can be used to explore the data, test hypotheses, or build predictive models. However, correlation does not imply causation, meaning that a high or low correlation does not necessarily mean that one variable causes or influences the other. Other factors, such as confounding variables, may affect the relationship between the variables .
- Bivariate correlation can be visualized using a scatterplot, which shows the values of the two variables for each observation. The shape and direction of the scatterplot can indicate the type and strength of the correlation. A line of best fit can also be drawn to show the linear trend of the data .
- Bivariate correlation can be tested for statistical significance using a hypothesis test, which compares the observed correlation coefficient to a null hypothesis of no correlation. The p-value of the test indicates the probability of obtaining the observed correlation or more extreme by chance, assuming the null hypothesis is true. A small p-value (usually less than 0.05) means that the correlation is unlikely to be due to chance and is statistically significant .
