### Multivariate Probability Calculations

- Multivariate probability is the study of random variables that have more than one dimension or outcome. For example, the height and weight of a person are two random variables that are related to each other. Multivariate probability can be used to model the joint behavior of such variables and their correlations. 
- A multivariate probability distribution is a function that assigns probabilities to every possible outcome of a multivariate experiment. For example, the multinomial distribution is a multivariate distribution that describes the probability of obtaining a certain number of successes in a fixed number of trials, each with a finite number of possible outcomes.  
- A multivariate probability distribution can be characterized by its joint probability mass function (PMF) or joint probability density function (PDF), depending on whether the variables are discrete or continuous. The joint PMF or PDF gives the probability of a particular combination of values for the variables. For example, the joint PMF of two discrete variables X and Y is P(X = x, Y = y), and the joint PDF of two continuous variables X and Y is f(x, y). 
- A multivariate probability distribution can also be characterized by its marginal and conditional distributions, which give the probabilities of individual variables or subsets of variables. The marginal distribution of a variable is obtained by summing or integrating over the other variables in the joint distribution. The conditional distribution of a variable given another variable is obtained by dividing the joint distribution by the marginal distribution of the given variable. For example, the marginal PMF of X is P(X = x) = ∑y P(X = x, Y = y), and the conditional PMF of X given Y is P(X = x | Y = y) = P(X = x, Y = y) / P(Y = y). 
- A multivariate probability distribution can also be characterized by its moments, such as the mean, variance, covariance, and correlation. The mean of a variable is the expected value of that variable, which is the weighted average of its possible values. The variance of a variable is the measure of its dispersion or variability, which is the expected value of the squared deviation from the mean. The covariance of two variables is the measure of their linear relationship, which is the expected value of the product of their deviations from their means. The correlation of two variables is the normalized covariance, which is the ratio of the covariance to the product of the standard deviations. For example, the mean of X is E(X) = ∑x x P(X = x), the variance of X is Var(X) = E(X^2) - E(X)^2, the covariance of X and Y is Cov(X, Y) = E(XY) - E(X)E(Y), and the correlation of X and Y is Corr(X, Y) = Cov(X, Y) / (SD(X)SD(Y)).  
- A multivariate probability distribution can also be characterized by its independence and dependence properties, which describe how the variables affect each other. Two variables are independent if their joint distribution is equal to the product of their marginal distributions, or equivalently, if their conditional distribution is equal to their marginal distribution. Two variables are dependent if their joint distribution is not equal to the product of their marginal distributions, or equivalently, if their conditional distribution is not equal to their marginal distribution. For example, X and Y are independent if P(X = x, Y = y) = P(X = x)P(Y = y), or P(X = x | Y = y) = P(X = x). X and Y are dependent if P(X = x, Y = y) ≠ P(X = x)P(Y = y), or P(X = x | Y = y) ≠ P(X = x).
