### Orthogonal Vectors

- Two vectors are orthogonal if they are perpendicular to each other, that is, if the angle between them is 90 degrees.
- Mathematically, two vectors **u** and **v** are orthogonal if their dot product is zero, that is, **u** · **v** = 0.
- For example, the vectors (1, 0) and (0, 1) are orthogonal because (1, 0) · (0, 1) = 0.
- Orthogonal vectors have some important properties and applications in linear algebra, such as:
  - Orthogonal vectors are linearly independent, that is, they cannot be written as a linear combination of each other.
  - Orthogonal vectors can be used to form an orthogonal basis for a vector space, that is, a set of vectors that span the space and are mutually orthogonal.
  - Orthogonal vectors can be used to find the distance between a point and a line or a plane, by using the Pythagorean theorem or the projection formula.
  - Orthogonal vectors can be used to find the best approximation of a vector by another vector in a subspace, by using the least squares method or the Gram-Schmidt process.
  - Orthogonal vectors can be used to perform orthogonal transformations, such as rotations, reflections, or projections, that preserve the length and angle of vectors.
  - Orthogonal vectors can be used to perform principal component analysis, a technique that reduces the dimensionality of data by finding the directions of maximum variance.
