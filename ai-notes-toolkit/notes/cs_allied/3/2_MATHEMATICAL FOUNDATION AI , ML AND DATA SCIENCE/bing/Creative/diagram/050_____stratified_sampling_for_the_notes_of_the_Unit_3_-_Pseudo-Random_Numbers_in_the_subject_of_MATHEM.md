### Stratified sampling

Stratified sampling is a method of sampling from a population that can be divided into subpopulations or strata that share some common characteristics. The purpose of stratified sampling is to ensure that the sample is representative of the population and that each stratum is adequately represented in the sample. Stratified sampling can reduce the sampling error and increase the precision of the estimates.

Some examples of stratified sampling are:

- In a survey of voters, the population can be stratified by age, gender, education, income, etc. and a random sample can be drawn from each stratum.
- In a clinical trial, the population can be stratified by health status, treatment group, risk factors, etc. and a random sample can be drawn from each stratum.
- In a Monte Carlo simulation, the population can be stratified by the values of some variables, and a random sample can be drawn from each stratum.

Some advantages of stratified sampling are:

- It can ensure that the sample reflects the diversity and heterogeneity of the population.
- It can improve the accuracy and efficiency of the estimates by reducing the variance within each stratum.
- It can facilitate the analysis and comparison of the results across different strata.

Some disadvantages of stratified sampling are:

- It requires prior knowledge of the population and the relevant strata.
- It can be complex and costly to implement, especially when there are many strata or the strata are not easily identifiable.
- It can introduce bias if the strata are not mutually exclusive or collectively exhaustive, or if the sampling fractions are not proportional to the population proportions.
