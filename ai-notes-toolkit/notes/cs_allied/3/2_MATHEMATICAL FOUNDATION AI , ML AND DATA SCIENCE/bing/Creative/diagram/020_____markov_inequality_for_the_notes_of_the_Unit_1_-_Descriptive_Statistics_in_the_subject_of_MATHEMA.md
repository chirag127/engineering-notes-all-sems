### Markov Inequality

- Markov inequality is a result in probability theory that gives an upper bound for the probability that a non-negative function of a random variable is greater than or equal to some positive constant.
- The inequality can be stated as follows: Let X be a non-negative random variable and a be a positive constant. Then, P(X >= a) <= E[X] / a, where E[X] is the expected value of X .
- The inequality holds for any distribution with positive values, no matter what other features that it has.
- The inequality can be used to derive other inequalities, such as Chebyshev's inequality and Chernoff's bound.
- The inequality can also be applied to measure-theoretic settings, where X is a measurable extended real-valued function and μ is a measure.
- The inequality can be extended to non-decreasing functions of random variables, such as the absolute value or the square.
- The inequality can be used to show that no more than a fraction of a population can have more than a multiple of the average value of a positive quantity, such as income.
