## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that appear to be random but are actually generated by a deterministic algorithm.
- Pseudo-random numbers are useful for applications such as cryptography, simulation, gaming, and statistical analysis.
- Pseudo-random numbers are not truly random because they can be reproduced by using the same algorithm and the same initial value, called a seed.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that produce pseudo-random numbers from a seed.
- PRNGs have different properties and characteristics, such as period, uniformity, independence, and unpredictability.
- Period is the length of the sequence of pseudo-random numbers before it repeats itself. A PRNG with a longer period is preferable to avoid repetition.
- Uniformity is the property that each possible value in the range of the PRNG has an equal probability of being generated. A PRNG with uniformity produces a fair distribution of pseudo-random numbers.
- Independence is the property that each pseudo-random number is not influenced by the previous or the next ones. A PRNG with independence produces a random-looking sequence of pseudo-random numbers.
- Unpredictability is the property that it is hard to guess the next pseudo-random number given the previous ones or the seed. A PRNG with unpredictability is desirable for security and privacy purposes.
