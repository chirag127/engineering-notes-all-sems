### Discrete and Continuous Probability Distributions

- A **probability distribution** is a function that describes all possible values of a random variable as well as the associated probabilities.
- A **random variable** is a variable whose value is determined by the outcome of a random experiment.
- A **discrete random variable** is a random variable that can take only a countable (usually finite) number of values .
- A **continuous random variable** is a random variable that can take any value within a specified range (which may be infinite and uncountable) .
- A **discrete probability distribution** is a probability distribution that counts occurrences that have countable or finite outcomes.
- A **continuous probability distribution** is a probability distribution that measures occurrences that have continuous or infinite outcomes.
- Examples of discrete random variables are the number of heads in a coin toss, the number of students in a class, the number of defects in a product, etc.
- Examples of continuous random variables are the height of a person, the weight of a fruit, the time taken to complete a task, etc.
- Examples of discrete probability distributions are the binomial, Poisson, and Bernoulli distributions.
- Examples of continuous probability distributions are the normal, exponential, and uniform distributions.
- For a discrete probability distribution, probabilities can be assigned to the values in the distribution - for example, "the probability that the web page will have 12 clicks in an hour is 0.15".
- For a continuous probability distribution, probabilities can be assigned to the intervals of values in the distribution - for example, "the probability that the temperature will be between 20 and 25 degrees Celsius is 0.4".
- For a discrete probability distribution, the sum of the probabilities of all the values in the distribution is equal to 1.
- For a continuous probability distribution, the area under the curve of its probability density function (PDF) is equal to 1.
- A **probability density function (PDF)** is a function that gives the probability of a continuous random variable falling within a given interval of values.
- A **cumulative distribution function (CDF)** is a function that gives the probability of a random variable (discrete or continuous) being less than or equal to a given value.
