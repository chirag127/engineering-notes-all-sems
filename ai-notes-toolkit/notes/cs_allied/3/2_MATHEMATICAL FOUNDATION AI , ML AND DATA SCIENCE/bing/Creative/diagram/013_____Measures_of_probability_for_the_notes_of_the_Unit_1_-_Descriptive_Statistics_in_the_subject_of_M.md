### Measures of probability

- Probability is a numerical measure of the likelihood of an event occurring.
- Probability can range from 0 to 1, where 0 means the event is impossible and 1 means the event is certain.
- There are different ways to assign probabilities to events, depending on the available information and assumptions.
- Some of the common methods are:

  - **Classical probability**: This method assumes that all possible outcomes of an experiment are equally likely, and assigns probabilities based on the ratio of favorable outcomes to total outcomes. For example, the classical probability of rolling a 6 on a fair die is 1/6, because there is one favorable outcome out of six possible outcomes.
  - **Empirical probability**: This method estimates probabilities based on the relative frequency of observed outcomes in a large number of trials. For example, the empirical probability of getting heads on a coin toss can be estimated by dividing the number of times heads occurred by the total number of tosses.
  - **Subjective probability**: This method assigns probabilities based on personal judgment, belief, or opinion. For example, the subjective probability of rain tomorrow can be based on one's own experience, weather forecast, or intuition.

- Some of the important properties and rules of probability are:

  - **Complement rule**: The probability of an event not occurring is equal to 1 minus the probability of the event occurring. For example, the probability of not rolling a 6 on a die is 1 - 1/6 = 5/6.
  - **Addition rule**: The probability of the union of two events is equal to the sum of their individual probabilities minus the probability of their intersection. For example, the probability of rolling a 6 or an even number on a die is 1/6 + 3/6 - 1/6 = 3/6.
  - **Multiplication rule**: The probability of the intersection of two events is equal to the product of their individual probabilities if they are independent, or the product of one probability and the conditional probability of the other given the first if they are dependent. For example, the probability of rolling two 6s on two dice is 1/6 * 1/6 = 1/36 if the dice are independent, or 1/6 * 1/5 = 1/30 if the dice are dependent.
