### Singular value decomposition (SVD)

- SVD is a factorization of a real or complex matrix into three matrices: A = UDV^T, where U and V are orthonormal matrices and D is a diagonal matrix with positive real entries .
- SVD generalizes the eigendecomposition of a square normal matrix with an orthonormal eigenbasis to any matrix.
- SVD has some interesting algebraic properties and conveys important geometrical and theoretical insights about linear transformations .
- SVD is related to the polar decomposition, which expresses a matrix as the product of a unitary matrix and a positive semidefinite matrix.
- SVD is useful in many applications in data science, such as dimensionality reduction, data compression, noise reduction, image processing, latent semantic analysis, and more .
- SVD can be computed using various numerical algorithms, such as the power method, the QR algorithm, the Jacobi algorithm, and the Golub-Reinsch algorithm .
- SVD can be extended to tensors, which are higher-order generalizations of matrices.
