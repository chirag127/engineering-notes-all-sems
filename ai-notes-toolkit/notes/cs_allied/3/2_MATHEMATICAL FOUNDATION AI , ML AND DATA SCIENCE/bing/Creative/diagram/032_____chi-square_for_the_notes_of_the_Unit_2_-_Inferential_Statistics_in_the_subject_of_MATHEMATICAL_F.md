### Chi-square test

- A chi-square test is a statistical hypothesis test used to compare observed frequencies with expected frequencies under a null hypothesis.
- A chi-square test can be used to test the independence of two categorical variables, the goodness of fit of a theoretical distribution to observed data, or the homogeneity of variances across groups .
- A chi-square test is a non-parametric test, meaning that it does not assume that the data is normally distributed, but rather that it follows a chi-square distribution.

- A chi-square test can be performed using a contingency table, which shows the frequencies of observations in each category of two or more variables.
- A chi-square test can be interpreted using a p-value, which is the probability of obtaining a chi-square statistic as extreme or more extreme than the one observed, assuming that the null hypothesis is true.
- A chi-square test can be used for various applications, such as testing the association between gender and voting preference, testing the fit of a dice to a fair distribution, or testing the equality of variances across different treatments  .
