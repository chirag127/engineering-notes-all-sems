### Estimation and Hypothesis Testing

- Estimation and hypothesis testing are two methods of inferential statistics that use sample data to make inferences about a population parameter.
- Estimation is the process of finding an interval or a point estimate for a population parameter based on sample data. An interval estimate gives a range of plausible values for the parameter, while a point estimate gives a single value.
- Hypothesis testing is the process of testing a claim or an assumption about a population parameter using sample data. A hypothesis test consists of two competing statements: the null hypothesis and the alternative hypothesis. The null hypothesis is the default or status quo statement, while the alternative hypothesis is the statement that the researcher wants to prove or support.
- The steps of hypothesis testing are:
  - Set up the null and alternative hypotheses and select the level of significance (alpha).
  - Collect the sample data and calculate the test statistic.
  - Determine the critical value or the p-value based on the test statistic and the chosen significance level.
  - Compare the test statistic with the critical value or the p-value with the significance level and decide whether to reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.
  - Present the conclusion in the context of the problem.
- There are different types of hypothesis tests depending on the parameter of interest, the distribution of the data, the number of samples, and the direction of the alternative hypothesis. Some common types of hypothesis tests are:
  - Z-test: used to test the mean of a population when the population standard deviation is known and the sample size is large.
  - T-test: used to test the mean of a population when the population standard deviation is unknown and the sample size is small.
  - Chi-square test: used to test the independence or the homogeneity of two categorical variables or the goodness-of-fit of a categorical variable to a theoretical distribution.
  - F-test: used to test the equality of two population variances or the equality of two population means when the variances are unknown and assumed to be equal.
  - ANOVA: used to test the equality of three or more population means.
  - Regression: used to test the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables.
