### Simulation and Monte Carlo integration

- Simulation is a technique of creating a virtual representation of a real-world system or phenomenon that is too complex, costly, or impractical to study directly.
- Simulation can be used to analyze the behavior, performance, or outcomes of a system under different scenarios or conditions, such as uncertainty, randomness, or variability.
- Simulation can also be used to test or optimize the design, operation, or control of a system, such as a manufacturing process, a traffic network, or a financial portfolio.
- Monte Carlo simulation is a specific type of simulation that uses random sampling of inputs or parameters to generate a large number of possible outcomes or scenarios.
- Monte Carlo simulation can be used to estimate the probability distribution, the mean, the variance, or other statistics of a quantity of interest, such as a performance measure, a cost, or a risk.
- Monte Carlo simulation can also be used to approximate the value of a definite integral that cannot be easily solved by analytical methods, such as a multidimensional or nonlinear integral.
- Monte Carlo integration is based on the idea that the expected value of a function can be approximated by the sample average of the function evaluated at random points in the domain of integration.
- Monte Carlo integration can be performed by generating a large number of random points in the domain of integration, evaluating the function at each point, and computing the average of the function values.
- Monte Carlo integration can be improved by using a better sampling method, such as stratified sampling, importance sampling, or quasi-Monte Carlo methods, that reduces the variance of the estimator or increases the efficiency of the algorithm.
