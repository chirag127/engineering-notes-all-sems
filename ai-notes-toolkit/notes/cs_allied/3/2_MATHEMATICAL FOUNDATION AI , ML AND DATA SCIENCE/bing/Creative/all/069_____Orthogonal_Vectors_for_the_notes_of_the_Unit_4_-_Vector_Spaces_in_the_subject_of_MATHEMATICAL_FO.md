# Orthogonal Vectors

- Two vectors are orthogonal if they are perpendicular to each other, that is, if their angle is 90 degrees or pi/2 radians.
- Orthogonal vectors have a zero dot product, that is, u.v = 0, where u and v are orthogonal vectors.
- Orthogonal vectors are linearly independent, that is, they cannot be written as a linear combination of each other.
- A set of vectors is orthogonal if every pair of vectors in the set is orthogonal.
- An orthogonal set of vectors can be used to form an orthogonal basis for a vector space, that is, a set of linearly independent vectors that span the vector space.
- An orthogonal basis has the property that the length of a vector can be computed as the square root of the sum of the squares of its coordinates with respect to the basis, that is, ||v|| = sqrt(v1^2 + v2^2 + ... + vn^2), where v1, v2, ..., vn are the coordinates of v in the orthogonal basis.
- An orthogonal basis can be normalized to form an orthonormal basis, that is, a set of orthogonal vectors with unit length. An orthonormal basis has the property that the dot product of two vectors is equal to their cosine of the angle between them, that is, u.v = cos(theta), where u and v are orthonormal vectors and theta is the angle between them.
- An orthonormal basis can be used to simplify the computation of projections, distances, and angles between vectors. For example, the projection of a vector v onto a vector u in an orthonormal basis is given by (u.v)u, the distance between two vectors u and v is given by ||u-v||, and the angle between two vectors u and v is given by arccos(u.v).
