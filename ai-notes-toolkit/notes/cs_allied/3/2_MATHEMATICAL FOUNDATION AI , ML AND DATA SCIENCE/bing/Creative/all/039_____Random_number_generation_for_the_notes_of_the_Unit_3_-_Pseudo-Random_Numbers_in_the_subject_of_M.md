# Random number generation

- Random number generation is a process by which, often by means of a random number generator (RNG), a sequence of numbers or symbols that cannot be reasonably predicted better than by random chance is generated.
- Random numbers are useful for many applications, such as cryptography, simulation, gaming, statistical sampling, and scientific experiments.
- There are two main types of random number generators: hardware-based and pseudo-random.
- Hardware-based random number generators use physical sources of randomness, such as dice, coins, radioactive decay, quantum phenomena, or atmospheric noise . These sources are considered to be truly random, as they are unpredictable and independent of any deterministic algorithm.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) use mathematical algorithms to produce sequences of numbers that appear to be random, but are actually deterministic and reproducible. PRNGs are faster and easier to implement than hardware-based RNGs, but they have some limitations and drawbacks.
- PRNGs require an initial value, called a seed, to start the generation process. The seed determines the entire sequence of numbers that will be produced by the PRNG. If the same seed is used, the same sequence will be generated. Therefore, the seed should be chosen randomly or unpredictably, otherwise the PRNG will be vulnerable to attacks or biases.
- PRNGs also have a finite period, which is the number of numbers that can be generated before the sequence repeats itself. The period should be large enough to avoid repetition or correlation in the applications that use the PRNG. Some PRNGs have very large periods, such as the Mersenne Twister, which has a period of 2^19937-1.
- PRNGs are not suitable for applications that require high levels of security or unpredictability, such as cryptography. For these applications, hardware-based RNGs or cryptographically secure PRNGs (CSPRNGs) are preferred. CSPRNGs are PRNGs that pass certain statistical tests and are resistant to attacks that try to predict or manipulate their output.
- There are many methods and algorithms for generating pseudo-random numbers, such as linear congruential generators, lagged Fibonacci generators, linear feedback shift registers, cellular automata, and chaotic maps. Each method has its own advantages and disadvantages, such as speed, quality, complexity, and memory requirements.
- To evaluate the quality of a PRNG, various statistical tests can be applied to its output, such as frequency tests, runs tests, autocorrelation tests, entropy tests, and randomness tests. These tests can measure how well the output approximates the properties of a true random sequence, such as uniformity, independence, and unpredictability. However, passing these tests does not guarantee that the PRNG is suitable for all applications or that it is secure against attacks.
