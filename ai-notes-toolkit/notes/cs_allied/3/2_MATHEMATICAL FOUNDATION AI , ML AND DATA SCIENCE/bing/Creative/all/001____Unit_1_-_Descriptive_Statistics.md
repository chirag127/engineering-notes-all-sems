## Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics are methods of summarizing and displaying data in a meaningful way. They help us to understand the main features and patterns of a data set, such as its center, spread, shape, and outliers.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- **Types of data**: Data can be classified into different types based on their nature and level of measurement. The main types of data are categorical (nominal or ordinal) and numerical (discrete or continuous).
- **Frequency distributions**: A frequency distribution is a table that shows how often each value or category of a variable occurs in a data set. It can be displayed as a relative frequency (proportion or percentage) or a cumulative frequency (running total).
- **Graphical displays**: Graphical displays are visual representations of data that help us to see patterns and trends. Some of the common graphical displays are bar charts, pie charts, histograms, stem-and-leaf plots, box plots, and scatter plots.
- **Measures of central tendency**: Measures of central tendency are numerical values that describe the center or typical value of a data set. The main measures of central tendency are mean, median, and mode.
- **Measures of variability**: Measures of variability are numerical values that describe the spread or variation of a data set. The main measures of variability are range, interquartile range, variance, and standard deviation.
- **Measures of relative standing**: Measures of relative standing are numerical values that describe the position of a value or a group of values within a data set. The main measures of relative standing are percentiles, quartiles, and z-scores.
- **Measures of association**: Measures of association are numerical values that describe the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables. The main measures of association are correlation coefficient and coefficient of determination.
