# Inference & Significance

- Inference is the process of drawing conclusions from data that are subject to random variation, such as observational errors or sampling variation.
- Significance is the degree of confidence that the results of an inference are not due to chance, but reflect a true relationship or difference in the population.
- Inference and significance are based on the following concepts:
  - Population: the entire group of individuals or objects of interest.
  - Sample: a subset of the population that is selected for data collection.
  - Parameter: a numerical characteristic of the population, such as the mean or the proportion.
  - Statistic: a numerical characteristic of the sample, such as the sample mean or the sample proportion.
  - Sampling distribution: the distribution of the possible values of a statistic for all possible samples of the same size from the same population.
  - Standard error: a measure of the variability of a statistic, calculated as the standard deviation of the sampling distribution.
  - Confidence interval: an interval estimate of a population parameter, based on a sample statistic and a level of confidence.
  - Hypothesis testing: a method of testing a claim or a hypothesis about a population parameter, based on a sample statistic and a significance level.
  - Null hypothesis: the hypothesis that there is no difference or no relationship between the population parameter and a specified value or another parameter.
  - Alternative hypothesis: the hypothesis that there is a difference or a relationship between the population parameter and a specified value or another parameter, contrary to the null hypothesis.
  - Test statistic: a statistic that measures the strength of the evidence against the null hypothesis, based on the sample data.
  - P-value: the probability of obtaining a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed one, assuming that the null hypothesis is true.
  - Significance level: the threshold for rejecting the null hypothesis, usually denoted by alpha (α).
  - Type I error: the error of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true, with a probability equal to the significance level.
  - Type II error: the error of failing to reject the null hypothesis when it is false, with a probability equal to the power of the test.
  - Power: the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is false, equal to one minus the probability of a type II error.
