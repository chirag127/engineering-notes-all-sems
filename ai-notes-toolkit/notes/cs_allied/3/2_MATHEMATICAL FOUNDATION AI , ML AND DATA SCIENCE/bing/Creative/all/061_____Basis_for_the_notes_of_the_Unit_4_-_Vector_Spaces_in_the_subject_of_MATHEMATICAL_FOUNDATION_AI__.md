- A vector space is a set of objects called vectors that can be added and multiplied by scalars (usually real or complex numbers) in a way that satisfies certain rules or axioms .
- The basic example of a vector space is the Euclidean space mathbb {R}^n Rn, where every element is represented by a list of n real numbers, and the operations of addition and scalar multiplication are defined componentwise .
- Other examples of vector spaces include the set of polynomials of a given degree or less, the set of matrices of a fixed size, the set of functions from a domain to a field, and the set of solutions of a homogeneous linear system .
- The main properties of a vector space are:
  - The set is closed under addition and scalar multiplication, meaning that the sum of any two vectors and the product of any scalar and vector are also vectors in the set .
  - The operations of addition and scalar multiplication are associative and commutative, meaning that the order and grouping of the operands do not affect the result .
  - There exists a zero vector in the set, denoted by mathbf {0} 0, such that adding it to any vector does not change the vector .
  - There exists an additive inverse for every vector in the set, denoted by -mathbf {v} -v, such that adding it to the vector gives the zero vector .
  - There exists a multiplicative identity in the field of scalars, denoted by 1, such that multiplying any vector by it does not change the vector .
  - The operations of addition and scalar multiplication satisfy the distributive property, meaning that a(b+c) = ab + ac and (a+b)c = ac + bc for any scalars a, b, c and any vector c .
- A vector space is an abstract concept that allows us to study the geometry and algebra of linear equations and transformations. It is the central object of study in linear algebra .
