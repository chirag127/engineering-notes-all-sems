# Change of Basis

- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the space.
- Any vector in the space can be written as a unique linear combination of the basis vectors.
- The coefficients of the linear combination are called the coordinates or components of the vector with respect to the basis.
- Different bases can be used to represent the same vector space, as long as they satisfy the conditions of linear independence and spanning.
- Changing the basis of a vector space means finding a new set of basis vectors that are related to the old ones by a linear transformation.
- The linear transformation that maps the old basis vectors to the new ones is called the change of basis matrix.
- The change of basis matrix is invertible and its inverse is the matrix that maps the new basis vectors to the old ones.
- To find the change of basis matrix, we can write the new basis vectors as linear combinations of the old ones and arrange the coefficients in a matrix.
- To find the coordinates of a vector with respect to the new basis, we can multiply the vector by the change of basis matrix.
- To find the vector from its coordinates with respect to the new basis, we can multiply the coordinates by the inverse of the change of basis matrix.
- Changing the basis of a vector space does not change the properties of the space, such as its dimension, rank, nullity, etc. It only changes the way the vectors are represented.
