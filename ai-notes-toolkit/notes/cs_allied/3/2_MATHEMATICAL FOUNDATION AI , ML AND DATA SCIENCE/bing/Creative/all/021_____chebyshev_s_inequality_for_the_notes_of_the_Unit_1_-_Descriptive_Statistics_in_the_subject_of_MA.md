# Chebyshev’s Inequality

- Chebyshev’s inequality is a theorem in probability theory that guarantees only a certain fraction of values will be found within a specific distance from the mean of a distribution .
- The fraction for which no more than a certain number of values can exceed is represented by 1/K2, where K is any positive number.
- The inequality can be stated as follows: For any random variable X with mean μ and standard deviation σ, and for any positive number K, the probability that X is more than K standard deviations away from the mean is at most 1/K2. Mathematically, this can be written as:

    P(|X - μ| ≥ Kσ) ≤ 1/K2

- Chebyshev’s inequality holds for any probability distribution, not only the normal distribution .
- Chebyshev’s inequality can be used to estimate the proportion of values that lie within a certain number of standard deviations from the mean. For example, Chebyshev’s inequality states that within two standard deviations away from the mean contains at least 75% of the values, and within three standard deviations away from the mean contains at least 88.9% of the values .
- Chebyshev’s inequality can also be used to compare different probability distributions and measure how dispersed they are. For example, Chebyshev’s inequality states that at most approximately 11.11% of the distribution will lie at least three standard deviations away from the mean. However, for the normal distribution, the value is just under 5%. This means that the normal distribution is more concentrated around the mean than a general distribution .
- Chebyshev’s inequality is named after the Russian mathematician Pafnuty Chebyshev, who proved it in the 19th century. It is also sometimes called the Bienaymé-Chebyshev inequality, after the French mathematician Irénée-Jules Bienaymé, who independently discovered it .
