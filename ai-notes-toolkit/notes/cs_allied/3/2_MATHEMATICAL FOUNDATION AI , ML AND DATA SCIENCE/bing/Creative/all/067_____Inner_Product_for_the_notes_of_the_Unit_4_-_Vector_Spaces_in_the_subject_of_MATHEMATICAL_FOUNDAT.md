# Inner Product

- An inner product is a generalization of the dot product. In a vector space, it is a way to multiply vectors together, with the result of this multiplication being a scalar .
- An inner product satisfies the following four properties for a real vector space:
  - **Linearity**: For any vectors **u**, **v**, **w** and any scalar **a**, we have (**u** + **v**, **w**) = (**u**, **w**) + (**v**, **w**) and (**a** **u**, **v**) = **a** (**u**, **v**).
  - **Symmetry**: For any vectors **u** and **v**, we have (**u**, **v**) = (**v**, **u**).
  - **Positive-definiteness**: For any vector **u**, we have (**u**, **u**) ≥ 0 and (**u**, **u**) = 0 if and only if **u** = **0**.
  - **Non-degeneracy**: For any vector **u**, if (**u**, **v**) = 0 for all **v**, then **u** = **0**.
- For a complex vector space, the inner product is anti-linear in the second slot, that is, (**u**, **v** + **w**) = (**u**, **v**) + (**u**, **w**) and (**u**, **a** **v**) = **a**̅ (**u**, **v**) for all **u**, **v**, **w** and any complex scalar **a**, where **a**̅ is the complex conjugate of **a**.
- Inner products allow formal definitions of intuitive geometric notions, such as lengths, angles, and orthogonality (zero inner product) of vectors. Inner product spaces generalize Euclidean vector spaces, in which the inner product is the dot product or scalar product of Cartesian coordinates.
