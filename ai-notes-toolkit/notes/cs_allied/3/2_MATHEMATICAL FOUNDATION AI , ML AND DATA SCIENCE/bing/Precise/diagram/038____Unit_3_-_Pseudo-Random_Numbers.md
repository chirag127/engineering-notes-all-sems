## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm, but appear to be random. They are commonly used in computer simulations, cryptography, and other applications where the use of true random numbers is impractical.

1. **Generation of Pseudo-Random Numbers**: Pseudo-random numbers can be generated using various algorithms, such as the linear congruential generator, the Mersenne Twister, and the Blum Blum Shub generator. These algorithms use a seed value to generate a sequence of numbers that are statistically random.

2. **Properties of Pseudo-Random Numbers**: Pseudo-random numbers should have certain statistical properties, such as uniform distribution, independence, and a long period. These properties ensure that the numbers generated by the algorithm are suitable for use in simulations and other applications.

3. **Applications of Pseudo-Random Numbers**: Pseudo-random numbers are used in a wide range of applications, including computer simulations, cryptography, and statistical sampling. In simulations, pseudo-random numbers are used to model random events, such as the roll of a die or the movement of a particle. In cryptography, pseudo-random numbers are used to generate keys for encryption and decryption.

4. **Limitations of Pseudo-Random Numbers**: Pseudo-random numbers are not truly random, as they are generated by a deterministic algorithm. This means that, given the same seed value, the same sequence of numbers will be generated. As a result, pseudo-random numbers are not suitable for all applications, such as gambling, where true randomness is required.

5. **True Random Numbers**: True random numbers are generated by a non-deterministic process, such as the decay of radioactive isotopes or atmospheric noise. These numbers are truly random and cannot be predicted. However, generating true random numbers can be difficult and time-consuming, which is why pseudo-random numbers are often used instead.
