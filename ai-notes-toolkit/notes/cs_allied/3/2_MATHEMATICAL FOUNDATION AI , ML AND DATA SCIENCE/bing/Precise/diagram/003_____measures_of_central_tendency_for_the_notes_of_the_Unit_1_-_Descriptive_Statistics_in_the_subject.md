### Measures of Central Tendency

Measures of central tendency are statistical measures that represent the central or typical value of a dataset. These measures are used to describe the central location of the data. There are three main measures of central tendency: mean, median, and mode.

1. **Mean**: The mean is the arithmetic average of a set of values. It is calculated by adding all the values in the dataset and dividing by the number of values in the dataset. The mean is sensitive to outliers, which can affect its value.

2. **Median**: The median is the middle value of a dataset when the values are arranged in ascending or descending order. If the dataset has an odd number of values, the median is the middle value. If the dataset has an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values. The median is not affected by outliers.

3. **Mode**: The mode is the value that appears most frequently in a dataset. A dataset can have more than one mode if there is more than one value that appears with the same frequency. The mode is not affected by outliers.

These measures of central tendency are used in descriptive statistics to summarize and describe data. They are also used in inferential statistics to make inferences about a population based on a sample of data.
