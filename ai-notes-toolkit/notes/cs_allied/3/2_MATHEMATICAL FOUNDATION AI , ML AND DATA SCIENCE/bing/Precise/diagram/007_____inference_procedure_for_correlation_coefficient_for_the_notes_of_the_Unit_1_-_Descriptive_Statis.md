### Inference Procedure for Correlation Coefficient

1. The correlation coefficient is a measure of the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two variables.
2. The correlation coefficient is denoted by the letter 'r' and takes on values between -1 and 1.
3. A correlation coefficient of 1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship, while a correlation coefficient of -1 indicates a perfect negative linear relationship.
4. A correlation coefficient of 0 indicates no linear relationship between the two variables.
5. To test the significance of the correlation coefficient, a t-test can be used.
6. The null hypothesis for the test is that the population correlation coefficient is equal to 0 (i.e. there is no linear relationship between the two variables).
7. The alternative hypothesis is that the population correlation coefficient is not equal to 0 (i.e. there is a linear relationship between the two variables).
8. The test statistic for the t-test is calculated as: t = r * sqrt((n-2)/(1-r^2)), where n is the sample size and r is the sample correlation coefficient.
9. The degrees of freedom for the t-test is n-2.
10. The p-value for the test can be calculated using a t-distribution table or using statistical software.
11. If the p-value is less than the chosen significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected and it can be concluded that there is a significant linear relationship between the two variables.
12. If the p-value is greater than the chosen significance level, the null hypothesis is not rejected and it cannot be concluded that there is a significant linear relationship between the two variables.
