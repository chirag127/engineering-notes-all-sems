### Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics
#### t-test/z-test (one sample, independent, paired)

- A t-test is a statistical test used to determine whether two sample means are significantly different from each other.
- A z-test is similar to a t-test, but it is used when the sample size is large (n > 30) or the population standard deviation is known.
- There are three main types of t-tests: one sample, independent, and paired.
- A one sample t-test is used to compare the mean of a sample to a known population mean.
- An independent t-test is used to compare the means of two independent samples.
- A paired t-test is used to compare the means of two related samples, such as before and after measurements on the same subjects.
- The choice between a t-test and a z-test depends on the sample size and whether the population standard deviation is known.
- If the sample size is large or the population standard deviation is known, a z-test is used. Otherwise, a t-test is used.
- The null hypothesis for a t-test or z-test is that there is no significant difference between the means of the two samples.
- The alternative hypothesis is that there is a significant difference between the means of the two samples.
- The test statistic for a t-test or z-test is calculated and compared to a critical value to determine whether to reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.
- The p-value can also be calculated to determine the probability of obtaining a test statistic as extreme as the one observed, assuming the null hypothesis is true.
- A small p-value (typically less than 0.05) indicates strong evidence against the null hypothesis, so it is rejected in favor of the alternative hypothesis.
- A large p-value (typically greater than 0.05) indicates weak evidence against the null hypothesis, so it is not rejected.
