The Triangle Inequality is a fundamental concept in the study of Vector Spaces. It states that for any vectors u and v in a normed vector space, the following inequality holds:

||u + v|| ≤ ||u|| + ||v||

This inequality can be visualized geometrically by considering the vectors u and v as sides of a triangle. The third side of the triangle, represented by the vector u + v, must be shorter than or equal to the sum of the lengths of the other two sides.

The Triangle Inequality has several important implications in the study of Vector Spaces. For example, it can be used to prove the Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality, which is a fundamental result in the study of inner product spaces.
