### Inference & Significance

Inferential statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with drawing conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. It is used to make inferences about the characteristics of a population by analyzing the data from a sample of that population.

- **Inference:** Inference is the process of drawing conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. This is done by using statistical methods to analyze the data and make predictions about the population.

- **Significance:** Significance refers to the likelihood that the results of a statistical analysis are not due to chance. A result is considered statistically significant if the probability of it occurring by chance is very low.

- **Hypothesis Testing:** Hypothesis testing is a method used to determine the significance of a result. It involves stating a null hypothesis and an alternative hypothesis, and then using statistical methods to determine whether the data supports the null hypothesis or the alternative hypothesis.

- **P-value:** The p-value is the probability of obtaining a result as extreme as the observed result, assuming that the null hypothesis is true. A low p-value indicates that the result is unlikely to have occurred by chance, and therefore provides evidence against the null hypothesis.

- **Confidence Intervals:** A confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the true population parameter with a certain level of confidence. It is calculated from the sample data and provides an estimate of the range of values that the population parameter could take.

In summary, inferential statistics is used to make inferences about a population based on a sample of data. Significance is used to determine the likelihood that the results are not due to chance, and hypothesis testing, p-values, and confidence intervals are common methods used in inferential statistics. These concepts are important for understanding and interpreting the results of statistical analyses.
