### Stratified Sampling

Stratified sampling is a sampling method used in statistics. It is used when the population is divided into different subgroups or strata, and a sample is taken from each stratum. The goal of stratified sampling is to ensure that the sample is representative of the population.

Here are some key points to remember about stratified sampling:

1. The population is divided into different subgroups or strata based on some characteristic.
2. A sample is taken from each stratum using a simple random sampling method.
3. The sample size for each stratum is determined based on the size of the stratum and the desired level of precision.
4. The samples from each stratum are combined to form the final sample.

Stratified sampling can be useful when the population is heterogeneous and the different subgroups have different characteristics. By taking a sample from each stratum, it is possible to ensure that the sample is representative of the population.

This method can also help to reduce sampling error and increase the precision of the estimates. However, it requires more effort and resources than simple random sampling, as the population must be divided into strata and a sample must be taken from each stratum.

In conclusion, stratified sampling is a useful sampling method when the population is divided into different subgroups or strata. It can help to ensure that the sample is representative of the population and can increase the precision of the estimates. However, it requires more effort and resources than simple random sampling.
