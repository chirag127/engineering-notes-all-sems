### Chebyshev’s Inequality

Chebyshev’s Inequality is a theorem in probability theory that states that for any given distribution, the proportion of observations that fall within k standard deviations of the mean is at least 1 - 1/k^2, where k is any positive real number greater than 1.

This theorem can be useful in understanding the spread of a distribution and in making predictions about the likelihood of certain observations. It is particularly useful when dealing with distributions that do not have a well-defined shape, such as those that are skewed or have heavy tails.

Some key points to remember about Chebyshev’s Inequality include:

1. The theorem applies to any distribution, regardless of its shape.
2. The value of k must be greater than 1 for the theorem to hold.
3. The theorem provides a lower bound on the proportion of observations that fall within k standard deviations of the mean.
4. The theorem can be used to make predictions about the likelihood of certain observations, but it does not provide exact probabilities.

In summary, Chebyshev’s Inequality is a powerful tool for understanding the spread of a distribution and making predictions about the likelihood of certain observations. It is particularly useful when dealing with distributions that do not have a well-defined shape.
