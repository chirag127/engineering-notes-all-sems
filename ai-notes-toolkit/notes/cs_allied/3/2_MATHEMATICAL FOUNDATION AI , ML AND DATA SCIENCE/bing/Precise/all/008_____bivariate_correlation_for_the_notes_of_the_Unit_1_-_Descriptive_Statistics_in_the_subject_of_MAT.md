# Bivariate Correlation

Bivariate correlation is a statistical measure that represents the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables. It is used in the field of Descriptive Statistics, which is a part of the subject of Mathematical Foundation for AI, ML, and Data Science.

Here are some key points to remember about bivariate correlation:

1. Bivariate correlation measures the linear relationship between two variables. It does not imply causation.
2. The correlation coefficient, denoted by 'r', ranges from -1 to 1. A value of -1 indicates a perfect negative linear relationship, a value of 1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship, and a value of 0 indicates no linear relationship.
3. The correlation coefficient can be calculated using the formula: r = Σ[(x - x̄)(y - ȳ)] / √[Σ(x - x̄)² * Σ(y - ȳ)²], where x and y are the two variables, x̄ and ȳ are their respective means, and Σ denotes the sum of all the values.
4. A scatter plot can be used to visually represent the relationship between two variables and to determine if a linear relationship exists.
5. Bivariate correlation is sensitive to outliers, which can greatly affect the value of the correlation coefficient.
6. It is important to note that correlation does not imply causation. Just because two variables are correlated does not mean that one causes the other.
