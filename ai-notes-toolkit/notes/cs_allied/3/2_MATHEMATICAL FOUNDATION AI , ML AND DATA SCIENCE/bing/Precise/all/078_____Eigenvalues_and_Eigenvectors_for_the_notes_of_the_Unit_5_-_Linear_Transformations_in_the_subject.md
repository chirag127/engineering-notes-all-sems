# Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors are important concepts in the study of linear transformations in the subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML, and Data Science. They are used to understand the behavior of linear transformations and to solve problems involving them.

- **Eigenvalue**: An eigenvalue of a square matrix A is a scalar λ such that there exists a non-zero vector v satisfying the equation Av = λv. In other words, an eigenvalue is a scalar that, when multiplied by a non-zero vector, results in the same vector as when the matrix is multiplied by that vector.

- **Eigenvector**: An eigenvector of a square matrix A is a non-zero vector v that satisfies the equation Av = λv for some scalar λ. In other words, an eigenvector is a vector that, when multiplied by the matrix, results in a scalar multiple of itself.

- **Characteristic polynomial**: The characteristic polynomial of a square matrix A is defined as the polynomial p(λ) = det(A - λI), where I is the identity matrix of the same size as A. The roots of the characteristic polynomial are the eigenvalues of the matrix A.

- **Diagonalization**: A square matrix A is diagonalizable if there exists an invertible matrix P such that P^(-1)AP is a diagonal matrix. The columns of P are the eigenvectors of A, and the diagonal entries of the diagonal matrix are the corresponding eigenvalues.

- **Applications**: Eigenvalues and eigenvectors have many applications in the field of AI, ML, and Data Science. They are used in principal component analysis, which is a technique for reducing the dimensionality of data. They are also used in the power method, which is an algorithm for finding the dominant eigenvalue and eigenvector of a matrix.

These are some of the key points to remember when studying eigenvalues and eigenvectors in the context of linear transformations. It is important to understand these concepts and their applications in order to effectively use them in the field of AI, ML, and Data Science.
