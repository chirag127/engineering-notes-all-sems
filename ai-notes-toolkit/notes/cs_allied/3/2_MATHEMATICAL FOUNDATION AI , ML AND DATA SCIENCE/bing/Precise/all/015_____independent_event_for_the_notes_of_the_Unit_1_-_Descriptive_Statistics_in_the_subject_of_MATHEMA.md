### Independent Events

In probability theory, an independent event is an event that is not affected by the occurrence of another event. In other words, the probability of an event occurring is not influenced by whether or not another event has occurred.

Here are some key points to remember about independent events:

1. Two events are independent if the occurrence of one event does not affect the probability of the other event occurring.
2. If two events are independent, then the probability of both events occurring is the product of the probabilities of each event occurring.
3. The probability of at least one of two independent events occurring is the sum of the probabilities of each event occurring, minus the probability of both events occurring.

For example, consider the toss of two fair coins. The outcome of the first toss does not affect the outcome of the second toss. Therefore, the events "first toss is heads" and "second toss is heads" are independent events. The probability of both events occurring is 0.5 * 0.5 = 0.25. The probability of at least one of the events occurring is 0.5 + 0.5 - 0.25 = 0.75.

In the context of descriptive statistics, understanding independent events is important when analyzing data and making predictions. Knowing whether events are independent or not can help in making accurate predictions and drawing valid conclusions from data.
