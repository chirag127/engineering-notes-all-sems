## Unit 5 - Linear Transformations

1. **Definition**: A linear transformation is a function between two vector spaces that preserves the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication.
2. **Properties**: Linear transformations have several important properties, including:
    - Additivity: T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v) for all vectors u and v in the domain of T.
    - Homogeneity: T(cu) = cT(u) for all scalars c and vectors u in the domain of T.
3. **Matrix representation**: Every linear transformation can be represented by a matrix. The matrix of a linear transformation is unique with respect to a given choice of bases for the domain and codomain.
4. **Kernel and range**: The kernel of a linear transformation is the set of all vectors in the domain that are mapped to the zero vector in the codomain. The range of a linear transformation is the set of all vectors in the codomain that are images of vectors in the domain.
5. **Inverse**: A linear transformation is invertible if and only if it is bijective, meaning that it is both injective (one-to-one) and surjective (onto). The inverse of a linear transformation is also a linear transformation.
6. **Composition**: The composition of two linear transformations is also a linear transformation. The matrix of the composition of two linear transformations is the product of the matrices of the individual transformations.
7. **Change of basis**: A change of basis is a linear transformation that relates the coordinates of a vector with respect to one basis to its coordinates with respect to another basis. The matrix of a change of basis transformation is called a transition matrix.
