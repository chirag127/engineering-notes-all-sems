# Markov Inequality

Markov Inequality is a statement about the probability distribution of a non-negative random variable. It is a fundamental concept in the field of probability and statistics, and is commonly used in the study of Descriptive Statistics, which is a part of the Mathematical Foundation for AI, ML, and Data Science.

The inequality states that for any non-negative random variable X and any positive number a, the probability that X is greater than or equal to a is less than or equal to the expected value of X divided by a. Mathematically, this can be expressed as:

P(X >= a) <= E(X) / a

Where P(X >= a) represents the probability that the random variable X is greater than or equal to a, E(X) represents the expected value of X, and a is a positive number.

Markov Inequality is useful in providing an upper bound on the probability that a random variable will take on a large value. It is often used in the analysis of algorithms, where it can provide a bound on the probability that an algorithm will take a long time to run.

Some key points to remember about Markov Inequality are:

- It applies only to non-negative random variables.
- It provides an upper bound on the probability that a random variable will take on a large value.
- It is commonly used in the analysis of algorithms.
