### Measures of Central Tendency

Measures of central tendency are statistical measures that describe the central point or the typical value of a dataset. These measures indicate where most values in a distribution fall and are also referred to as the central location of a distribution. There are three main measures of central tendency: the mean, the median, and the mode.

1. **Mean**: The mean is the sum of all the values in the dataset divided by the total number of values in the dataset. It is commonly referred to as the average.

2. **Median**: The median is the middle value when the data is arranged in ascending order. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values.

3. **Mode**: The mode is the value that occurs most frequently in the dataset. There can be more than one mode if several values occur with the same frequency.

These measures of central tendency are used in descriptive statistics to summarize and describe data. They are also used in inferential statistics to make inferences about a population based on a sample of data. It is important to note that the mean, median, and mode may not always be the same value and can provide different information about the dataset. It is useful to consider all three measures when analyzing data.
