### Chebyshev’s Inequality

Chebyshev’s Inequality is a theorem in probability theory that states that for any given probability distribution, the proportion of observations that fall within k standard deviations of the mean is at least 1 - 1/k^2, where k is any positive real number greater than 1.

In other words, Chebyshev’s Inequality provides a lower bound on the probability that a random variable will fall within a certain number of standard deviations from its mean.

Some key points to remember about Chebyshev’s Inequality are:

- It applies to any probability distribution, regardless of its shape.
- It provides a lower bound, meaning that the actual probability could be higher than the value given by the inequality.
- The value of k must be greater than 1 for the inequality to hold.
- As the value of k increases, the lower bound provided by the inequality becomes less restrictive.

Chebyshev’s Inequality can be useful in situations where the shape of the probability distribution is unknown or difficult to determine. It provides a way to estimate the probability of an observation falling within a certain range without having to know the exact details of the distribution.

In the context of descriptive statistics, Chebyshev’s Inequality can be used to provide a rough estimate of the spread of a dataset. For example, if we know the mean and standard deviation of a dataset, we can use the inequality to estimate the proportion of observations that fall within a certain number of standard deviations from the mean. This can be useful when trying to understand the overall distribution of the data.
