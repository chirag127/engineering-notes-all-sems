### Inference & Significance

Inferential statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with drawing conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. It is used to make inferences about the characteristics of a population by analyzing the data from a sample of that population.

Some key concepts in inferential statistics include:

1. **Sampling**: The process of selecting a subset of the population to represent the entire population.
2. **Sample distribution**: The distribution of a statistic calculated from a sample of data.
3. **Central Limit Theorem**: A theorem that states that the distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution as the sample size increases, regardless of the shape of the population distribution.
4. **Confidence interval**: An interval estimate of a population parameter, calculated from a sample of data, that is likely to contain the true value of the parameter with a certain level of confidence.
5. **Hypothesis testing**: A statistical method used to test a claim or hypothesis about a population parameter, based on a sample of data.
6. **P-value**: The probability of observing a test statistic as extreme or more extreme than the one observed, assuming the null hypothesis is true.
7. **Type I and Type II errors**: Type I error occurs when the null hypothesis is rejected when it is true, while Type II error occurs when the null hypothesis is not rejected when it is false.
8. **Statistical significance**: A result is statistically significant if the p-value is less than the chosen significance level, indicating that the observed result is unlikely to have occurred by chance.

These concepts are important for understanding how to draw conclusions about a population based on a sample of data, and for evaluating the strength of evidence for or against a claim or hypothesis. They are commonly used in research and data analysis to make informed decisions based on data.
