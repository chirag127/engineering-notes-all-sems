### Expectation and Variance

Expectation and variance are two important concepts in descriptive statistics. They are used to describe the central tendency and dispersion of a random variable.

#### Expectation

The expectation, also known as the expected value or mean, is a measure of the central tendency of a random variable. It is defined as the weighted average of all possible values that the random variable can take on, where the weights are the probabilities of the respective values.

For a discrete random variable X with probability mass function p(x), the expectation is defined as:

E(X) = ∑x * p(x)

For a continuous random variable X with probability density function f(x), the expectation is defined as:

E(X) = ∫x * f(x) dx

#### Variance

The variance is a measure of the dispersion of a random variable. It is defined as the expected value of the squared deviation of the random variable from its mean.

For a random variable X with mean μ, the variance is defined as:

Var(X) = E[(X - μ)²]

The standard deviation is the square root of the variance and is another commonly used measure of dispersion.

These concepts are important in the study of mathematical foundations of AI, ML, and data science as they provide a way to describe and understand the behavior of random variables, which are often used to model uncertainty in these fields.
