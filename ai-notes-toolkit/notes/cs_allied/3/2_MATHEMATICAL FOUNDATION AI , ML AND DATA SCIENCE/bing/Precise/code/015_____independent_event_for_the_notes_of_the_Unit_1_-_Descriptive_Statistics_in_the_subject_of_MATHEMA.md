### Independent Event

An independent event is an event that is not affected by the outcome of another event. In other words, the probability of one event occurring is not influenced by the occurrence of another event.

Here are some key points to remember about independent events:

1. Two events are independent if the occurrence of one event does not change the probability of the other event occurring.
2. If two events are independent, then the probability of both events occurring is the product of the probabilities of each event occurring.
3. The probability of at least one of two independent events occurring is the sum of their individual probabilities minus the probability of both events occurring.

For example, consider the toss of two fair coins. The outcome of the first coin toss does not affect the outcome of the second coin toss. Therefore, the events are independent. The probability of getting heads on the first coin toss is 1/2, and the probability of getting heads on the second coin toss is also 1/2. The probability of getting heads on both coin tosses is (1/2) * (1/2) = 1/4.
