### Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)

Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is a matrix factorization technique used in many applications of statistics and signal processing. SVD is used to decompose a matrix into three matrices, where the original matrix is equal to the product of these three matrices.

Some key points to remember about SVD are:

1. SVD can be applied to any matrix, regardless of its shape or the field over which it is defined.
2. The three matrices resulting from the SVD decomposition have specific properties. The first matrix is an orthogonal matrix, the second is a diagonal matrix, and the third is also an orthogonal matrix.
3. The diagonal entries of the second matrix are known as the singular values of the original matrix. These values are always non-negative and are arranged in descending order.
4. The columns of the first matrix are known as the left singular vectors, while the columns of the third matrix are known as the right singular vectors.
5. SVD has many applications, including data compression, noise reduction, and the computation of the pseudo-inverse of a matrix.

SVD is an important concept in the field of inferential statistics and is a key topic in the study of mathematical foundations for AI, ML, and data science. It is important to have a good understanding of SVD and its properties in order to effectively apply it in these fields.
