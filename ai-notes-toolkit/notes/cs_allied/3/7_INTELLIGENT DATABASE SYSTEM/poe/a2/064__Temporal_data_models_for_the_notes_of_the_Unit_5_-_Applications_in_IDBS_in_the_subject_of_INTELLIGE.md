### Temporal data models for the notes of the Unit 5 - Applications in IDBS

1. Temporal databases store data with time stamps to capture the valid time of facts. This allows queries to consider time in their conditions and results.
2. The key aspects of temporal data models are:
- Valid time: The time when a fact is true in the real world.
- Transaction time: The time when a fact was recorded in the database.
3. The two most common temporal data models are:
- Standard temporal model: Valid and transaction times are combined in one timestamp.
- Bitemporal model: Valid and transaction times are stored separately, allowing queries based on either one or both.
4. Queries on temporal data may consider time in multiple ways:
- Time slices: Retrieve data that was valid/recorded at a specific time.
- Time intervals: Retrieve data that was valid/recorded during a specific time range.
- Time sequences: Retrieve data organized by time, e.g. "all locations of an object over time".
5. Applications of temporal databases include:
- Tracking status changes of objects (e.g. location of moving objects).
- Keeping and querying historical records.
- Analysis of data over time (e.g. trends).
- Reconstruction of past states.
- Auditing and database version control.
