### A language for implementing very large 'integral approach' systems

- Such languages provide a more global viewpoint, allowing the specification of large systems as a whole.
- They support abstraction mechanisms to deal with complexity.
- They are based on the concept of modules or components with well-defined interfaces.
- Examples are languages like LISP, Prolog, etc. which take an "integral approach".
- An integral approach system consists of a set of modules with explicit interfaces. The system can be viewed as the interconnection of the modules.
- Each module may be an application or may be implemented in terms of other modules.
- This modular approach aims to enhance comprehensibility, flexibility, and reuse.
- The integral languages emphasize symbolic processing and are suitable for knowledge-intensive applications.
- They are useful for implementing expert systems, symbolic processing systems, etc.
