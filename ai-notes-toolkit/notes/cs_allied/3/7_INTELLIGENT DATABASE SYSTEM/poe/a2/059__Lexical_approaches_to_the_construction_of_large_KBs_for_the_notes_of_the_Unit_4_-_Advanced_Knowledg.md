### Lexical approaches to the construction of large KBs for the notes of the Unit 4 - Advanced Knowledge-Based Systems in the subject of INTELLIGENT DATABASE SYSTEM

1. Dictionary-Based Approach: In this approach, domain terms are extracted from online dictionaries. The dictionaries provide lexical information such as synonyms, hypernyms, hyponyms, meronyms, etc. which can be used to construct taxonomies and ontologies. However, dictionary-based approaches have some limitations such as lack of context-sensitivity and domain-specific terminology.

2. Corpus-Based Approach: In this approach, a domain corpus is analyzed to extract relevant terms and relationships. Statistical methods are used to identify important terms and patterns of term co-occurrences are used to identify semantic relationships. However, large amounts of data and significant preprocessing and pattern analysis are required in corpus-based approaches.

3. Web-Based Approach: This approach utilizes the vast information available on the World Wide Web to extract domain knowledge. Web search engines are used to retrieve relevant pages and data mining techniques are applied to extract domain terms, taxonomies and relationships. The key advantage is the huge amount of information but the lack of structure and reliability are potential challenges.

4. Hybrid Approach: This approach combines multiple lexical approaches to overcome individual limitations and harness complementary strengths. For example, dictionary definitions can be used to validate terms extracted from corpora or web while corpus co-occurrence analysis can provide contextual information to dictionary-based approaches. Hybrid approaches tend to produce more robust knowledge bases but also tend to be more complex to implement.

The above points cover the key lexical approaches to constructing large knowledge bases. An appropriate approach can be selected based on the scale and type of the knowledge base as well as availability of resources. Combining multiple approaches in a hybrid strategy is a promising direction for more comprehensive knowledge acquisition.
