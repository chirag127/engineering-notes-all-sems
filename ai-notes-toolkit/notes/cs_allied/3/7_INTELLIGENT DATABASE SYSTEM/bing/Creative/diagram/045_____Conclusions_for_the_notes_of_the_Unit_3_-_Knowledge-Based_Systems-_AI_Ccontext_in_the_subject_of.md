### Conclusions

- Knowledge-based systems (KBS) are computer systems that use artificial intelligence techniques to solve complex problems that normally require human expertise.
- KBS consist of a knowledge base, which stores the domain knowledge, and an inference engine, which applies logical rules to the knowledge base to derive new information or solutions.
- KBS can be classified into different types based on the nature of the knowledge, the reasoning method, or the application domain. Some common types are expert systems, rule-based systems, case-based systems, neural networks, fuzzy systems, and ontologies.
- KBS can be integrated with database systems to enhance their functionality and performance. Some benefits of KBS-database integration are:
  - KBS can provide intelligent query processing, data analysis, and decision support for database users.
  - Database systems can provide efficient storage, retrieval, and management of large-scale and heterogeneous knowledge bases for KBS.
  - KBS and database systems can share common data and knowledge models, languages, and interfaces to facilitate interoperability and communication.
- KBS-database integration can be achieved at different levels, such as the data level, the schema level, the language level, or the interface level. Each level has its own advantages and challenges.
- KBS-database integration can also be realized by using different architectures, such as the tight coupling, the loose coupling, or the hybrid coupling. Each architecture has its own trade-offs between performance, flexibility, and complexity.
