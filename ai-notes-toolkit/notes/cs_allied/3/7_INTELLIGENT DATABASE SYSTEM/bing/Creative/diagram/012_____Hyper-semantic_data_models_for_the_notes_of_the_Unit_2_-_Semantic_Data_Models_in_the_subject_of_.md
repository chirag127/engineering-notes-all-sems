### Hyper-semantic data models

- Hyper-semantic data models are a new class of models that extend the semantic data models by incorporating knowledge in the form of heuristics, uncertainty, constraints and other artificial intelligence concepts .
- Semantic data models are high-level models that capture the meaning and structure of an application environment using object-oriented concepts such as entities, attributes, relationships, inheritance and aggregation.
- Hyper-semantic data models aim to enhance the effectiveness and usability of database systems by allowing more expressive and flexible representation and manipulation of data and knowledge .
- Hyper-semantic data models can be classified into three categories based on the type of knowledge they incorporate:
  - **Traditional** hyper-semantic data models: These models include knowledge in the form of integrity constraints, derived attributes, triggers and stored procedures. Examples are SQL, Oracle and DB2.
  - **Semantic** hyper-semantic data models: These models include knowledge in the form of rules, facts, frames, semantic networks and ontologies. Examples are KDM, KDL and KL-ONE.
  - **Hypersemantic** hyper-semantic data models: These models include knowledge in the form of uncertainty, fuzziness, probability, possibility and evidence. Examples are U-KDM, F-KDM and E-KDM.
- Hyper-semantic data models have several advantages over conventional data models, such as  :
  - They can capture the semantics and context of the application domain more accurately and completely.
  - They can support complex queries and reasoning over data and knowledge.
  - They can handle incomplete, inconsistent and uncertain data and knowledge.
  - They can facilitate data integration and interoperability among heterogeneous data sources.
  - They can improve data quality and reliability by enforcing consistency and validity rules.
