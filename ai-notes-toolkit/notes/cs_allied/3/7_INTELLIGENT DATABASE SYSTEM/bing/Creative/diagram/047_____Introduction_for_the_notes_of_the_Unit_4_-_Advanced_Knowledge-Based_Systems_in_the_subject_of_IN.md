### Introduction

- A knowledge-based system (KBS) is a computer system that uses artificial intelligence techniques to solve problems that normally require human expertise.
- A KBS consists of two main components: a knowledge base and an inference engine.
- A knowledge base is a collection of facts and rules that represent the domain knowledge of a specific problem area.
- An inference engine is a software module that applies logical reasoning to the knowledge base to derive new facts and conclusions.
- A KBS can also have other components, such as a user interface, a knowledge acquisition module, a knowledge representation language, and a knowledge validation module.
- A KBS can be classified into different types, such as rule-based systems, case-based systems, model-based systems, and hybrid systems, depending on the structure and content of the knowledge base and the inference engine.
- A KBS can be applied to various domains, such as diagnosis, planning, scheduling, design, configuration, and decision support.
- A KBS can provide several benefits, such as improving the quality and consistency of solutions, reducing the cost and time of problem solving, enhancing the learning and training of human experts, and facilitating the communication and collaboration among different stakeholders.
- A KBS can also face several challenges, such as acquiring and maintaining the domain knowledge, ensuring the validity and reliability of the knowledge base, dealing with uncertainty and incompleteness of information, and adapting to changing environments and user needs.
