### Characteristics and classification of the knowledge-based systems

A knowledge-based system (KBS) is a computer program that reasons and uses a knowledge base to solve complex problems. The term is broad and refers to many different kinds of systems. The one common theme that unites all knowledge based systems is an attempt to represent knowledge explicitly and a reasoning system that allows it to derive new knowledge from the existing one.

A knowledge-based system has two distinguishing features: a knowledge base and an inference engine . The first part, the knowledge base, represents facts about the world, often in some form of subsumption ontology (rather than implicitly embedded in procedural code, in the way a conventional computer program does) . The second part, the inference engine, applies logical rules to the knowledge base to deduce new information and provide solutions .

Knowledge-based systems can be classified into different types based on various criteria, such as the type of knowledge, the type of reasoning, the type of application, or the type of user interface. Here are some examples of such classifications :

- Based on the type of knowledge, knowledge-based systems can be divided into declarative, procedural, or hybrid systems. Declarative systems represent knowledge as facts, rules, or constraints, while procedural systems represent knowledge as actions, plans, or strategies. Hybrid systems combine both types of knowledge in a single system.
- Based on the type of reasoning, knowledge-based systems can be divided into deductive, inductive, or abductive systems. Deductive systems use logical rules to draw conclusions from given premises, while inductive systems use examples or observations to infer general rules or patterns. Abductive systems use incomplete or uncertain information to generate plausible explanations or hypotheses.
- Based on the type of application, knowledge-based systems can be divided into expert systems, case-based systems, hypertext manipulation systems, intelligent tutoring systems, or rule-based systems. Expert systems emulate the reasoning of human experts in a specific domain, while case-based systems use past cases or experiences to solve new problems. Hypertext manipulation systems allow users to access and manipulate large amounts of information in a non-linear way, while intelligent tutoring systems provide personalized instruction and feedback to learners. Rule-based systems use a set of rules to perform tasks or make decisions.
- Based on the type of user interface, knowledge-based systems can be divided into natural language systems, graphical systems, or mixed systems. Natural language systems allow users to communicate with the system using natural language, such as English or Spanish, while graphical systems use visual elements, such as icons, menus, or diagrams, to interact with the system. Mixed systems combine both natural language and graphical elements in the user interface.

Knowledge-based systems have many uses and benefits, such as enhancing human performance, providing consistent and reliable solutions, facilitating knowledge sharing and reuse, and enabling complex problem solving . However, they also face some challenges and limitations, such as acquiring and maintaining knowledge, ensuring the validity and quality of knowledge, dealing with uncertainty and inconsistency, and explaining the reasoning process .
