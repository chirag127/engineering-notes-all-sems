### A taxonomy of intelligent database systems

- An intelligent database system (IDBS) is a database system that integrates artificial intelligence (AI) techniques with database management systems (DBMSs) to provide enhanced functionality, such as data quality, data mining, knowledge representation, reasoning, and learning  .
- A taxonomy is a system of classification that organizes and labels concepts or entities into categories and subcategories based on their characteristics and relationships  .
- A taxonomy of IDBSs can help to understand the different types, features, and applications of IDBSs, as well as to compare and evaluate them.
- One possible taxonomy of IDBSs is based on the origin and the level of integration of AI and DB techniques . This taxonomy divides IDBSs into two main categories: efforts originated in a DB context and efforts originated in an AI context.
- Efforts originated in a DB context are those that extend the traditional DBMSs with AI features, such as data quality, data mining, and reasoning. These efforts can be further classified into static extensions and dynamic extensions.
  - Static extensions are those that enhance the data model of the DBMS with more expressive and flexible constructs, such as object-oriented, semantic, or deductive models .
  - Dynamic extensions are those that introduce some form of reasoning or inference inside the DBMS, such as rule-based systems, active databases, or constraint databases .
- Efforts originated in an AI context are those that use AI systems to manage and manipulate data, such as knowledge-based systems, expert systems, or neural networks. These efforts can be further classified into basic solutions and advanced solutions.
  - Basic solutions are those that couple AI systems and DBMSs using external interfaces, such as query languages, communication protocols, or middleware .
  - Advanced solutions are those that attempt to use AI systems as the core of the IDBS, such as logic programming, ontologies, or hybrid systems .
