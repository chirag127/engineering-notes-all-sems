### Ontology theoretical foundations

- Ontology is the philosophical study of being, as well as related concepts such as existence, becoming, and reality.
- Ontology addresses questions like how entities are grouped into categories and which of these entities exist on the most fundamental level.
- Ontology is also a branch of knowledge engineering, artificial intelligence and computer science, where it refers to a formal representation of a domain of interest, using concepts, relations, axioms, and instances.
- Ontology engineering is the process of developing, maintaining, and using ontologies for various purposes, such as knowledge management, natural language processing, e-commerce, and intelligent information integration.
- Ontology engineering relies on some theoretical foundations, such as:
  - Foundational ontologies, which provide a high-level categorization of the kinds of things that will be represented in the ontology, such as process, physical object, quality, event, etc. Foundational ontologies also define relations that are useful across subject domains, such as parthood, participation, causation, etc. Foundational ontologies aim to capture the most general and universal aspects of reality, and to serve as a common reference for domain-specific ontologies .
  - Conceptual modelling, which is the activity of designing and constructing an abstract representation of a domain of interest, using concepts, relations, constraints, and rules. Conceptual modelling can be based on ontological principles, such as clarity, coherence, consistency, and completeness, to ensure the quality and usability of the model.
  - Logic, which is the study of valid reasoning and inference, as well as the formal languages and systems that can express and manipulate such reasoning. Logic is the main tool for specifying and reasoning with ontologies, as it allows to define the syntax and semantics of the ontology language, to check the consistency and validity of the ontology, to derive new facts from the ontology, and to query and manipulate the ontology.
  - Ontology languages, which are formal languages that can be used to represent and communicate ontologies. Ontology languages have different levels of expressiveness, depending on the complexity and richness of the concepts and relations that they can capture. Some examples of ontology languages are RDF, RDFS, OWL, and Common Logic.
