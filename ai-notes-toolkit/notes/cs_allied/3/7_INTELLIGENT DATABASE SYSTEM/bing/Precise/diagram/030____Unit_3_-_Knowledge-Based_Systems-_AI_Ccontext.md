## Unit 3 - Knowledge-Based Systems- AI Context

1. **Introduction:** Knowledge-based systems (KBS) are computer programs that reason and use a knowledge base to solve complex problems. They are a branch of artificial intelligence (AI) that aims to provide intelligent behavior by capturing and using human expertise.

2. **Knowledge Representation:** Knowledge representation is the process of encoding information about the world in a form that a computer can understand and reason with. Common knowledge representation techniques include rule-based systems, semantic networks, frames, and ontologies.

3. **Inference:** Inference is the process of deriving new information from existing knowledge. Inference engines are used in KBS to apply rules and reasoning to the knowledge base to derive new information and make decisions.

4. **Expert Systems:** Expert systems are a type of KBS that use a knowledge base and inference engine to provide expert-level advice or make decisions in a specific domain. They are often used in fields such as medicine, law, and finance to provide expert advice and decision-making support.

5. **Applications:** KBS have a wide range of applications, including decision support, diagnosis, planning, scheduling, and design. They are used in many industries, including healthcare, finance, manufacturing, and transportation.

6. **Challenges:** Developing and maintaining a KBS can be challenging, as it requires capturing and representing complex and often changing knowledge. Ensuring the accuracy and completeness of the knowledge base is also important, as errors or omissions can lead to incorrect decisions or advice.

7. **Future Directions:** Research in KBS continues to focus on improving knowledge representation and reasoning techniques, as well as developing new applications for KBS. Advances in machine learning and natural language processing are also expected to play a role in the future development of KBS.
