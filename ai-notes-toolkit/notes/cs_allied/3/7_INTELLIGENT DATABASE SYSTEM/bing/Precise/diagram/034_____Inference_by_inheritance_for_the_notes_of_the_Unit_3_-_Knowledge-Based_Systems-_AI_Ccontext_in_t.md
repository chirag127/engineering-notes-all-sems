### Inference by inheritance

Inference by inheritance is a technique used in knowledge-based systems, specifically in the context of artificial intelligence (AI) and intelligent databases. It is a part of Unit 3 - Knowledge-Based Systems in the subject of Intelligent Database Systems.

Inference by inheritance involves the use of hierarchical relationships between concepts to derive new information. This is done by inferring that if a certain property or characteristic holds true for a general concept, it must also hold true for all of its sub-concepts or instances.

For example, consider a knowledge base that contains information about different types of animals. If the knowledge base contains the information that all mammals have fur, then it can be inferred that all dogs, being a type of mammal, also have fur. This is an example of inference by inheritance, where the property of having fur is inherited from the general concept of mammals to the specific instance of dogs.

Inference by inheritance can be a powerful tool in knowledge-based systems, allowing for the efficient derivation of new information from existing knowledge. However, it is important to ensure that the hierarchical relationships between concepts are accurately represented in the knowledge base, as incorrect relationships can lead to incorrect inferences.

In summary, inference by inheritance is a technique used in knowledge-based systems to derive new information from existing knowledge by leveraging hierarchical relationships between concepts. It is a part of Unit 3 - Knowledge-Based Systems in the subject of Intelligent Database Systems.
