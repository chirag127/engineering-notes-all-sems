### Energy sources for energy harvesting

Energy harvesting is the process of capturing and converting ambient energy from various sources into electrical energy for powering small, wireless autonomous devices. Energy harvesting can enable self-powered devices that do not require batteries or external power sources, which can reduce maintenance costs and environmental impact. Some of the common energy sources for energy harvesting are:

- **Thermal energy**: Thermal energy is the energy associated with heat and temperature differences. Thermal energy can be harvested from various sources, such as waste heat from industrial processes, human body heat, geothermal energy, and solar radiation. Thermal energy can be converted into electrical energy using thermoelectric generators (TEGs), which are devices that generate voltage based on the Seebeck effect. TEGs consist of two dissimilar materials connected in a loop, which create a temperature gradient across the junctions. The temperature difference induces an electric current in the loop, which can be used to power a load. TEGs can also work in reverse, using electrical energy to create a temperature difference, which can be used for cooling or heating applications.  

- **RF energy**: RF energy is the energy carried by electromagnetic waves in the radio frequency range. RF energy can be harvested from various sources, such as broadcast signals, cellular networks, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and RFID. RF energy can be converted into electrical energy using antennas, rectifiers, and capacitors, which form a rectenna. A rectenna is a device that captures RF waves and converts them into direct current (DC) voltage. The rectified voltage can be used to power a load or stored in a battery or capacitor. RF energy harvesting can enable wireless power transfer and wireless charging for devices that are within the range of the RF source.  

- **Solar energy**: Solar energy is the energy from the sun that reaches the earth in the form of visible light, infrared radiation, and ultraviolet radiation. Solar energy can be harvested using photovoltaic (PV) cells, which are devices that convert light into electrical energy. PV cells are made of materials that have the property of photoelectric effect, which is the emission of electrons when light hits a metal surface. The emitted electrons create a voltage difference between the two sides of the PV cell, which can be used to power a load or stored in a battery or capacitor. PV cells can be arranged in arrays or modules to increase the output power and voltage. Solar energy harvesting can enable renewable and clean energy generation for devices that are exposed to sunlight.  

- **Mechanical energy**: Mechanical energy is the energy associated with motion and force. Mechanical energy can be harvested from various sources, such as human or animal movements, vibrations, wind, water, and sound. Mechanical energy can be converted into electrical energy using different mechanisms, such as piezoelectric, electromagnetic, and electrostatic. Piezoelectric materials are materials that generate voltage when subjected to mechanical stress or strain, such as pressure, bending, or stretching. Piezoelectric materials can be used to make sensors, actuators, and generators that can harvest mechanical energy from vibrations, impacts, or deformations. Electromagnetic generators are devices that use magnets and coils to induce voltage based on Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction. Electromagnetic generators can harvest mechanical energy from rotational or linear motion, such as spinning, shaking, or swinging. Electrostatic generators are devices that use variable capacitors to generate voltage based on the principle of electrostatic induction. Electrostatic generators can harvest mechanical energy from vibrations or fluctuations, such as tapping, flapping, or fluttering.
