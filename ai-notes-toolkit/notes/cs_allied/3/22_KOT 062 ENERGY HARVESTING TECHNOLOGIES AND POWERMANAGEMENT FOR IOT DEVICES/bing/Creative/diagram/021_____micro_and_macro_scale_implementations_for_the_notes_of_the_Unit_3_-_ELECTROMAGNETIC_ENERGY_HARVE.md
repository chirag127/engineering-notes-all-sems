# Micro and Macro Scale Implementations for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting and Non-Linear Techniques

Electromagnetic energy harvesting (EMEH) is a technique that converts ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction. EMEH devices consist of a coil and a magnet that move relative to each other and generate an electromotive force (EMF) across the coil terminals. EMEH devices can be classified into linear and nonlinear types, depending on the restoring force of the system.

## Linear EMEH Devices

Linear EMEH devices have a constant restoring force that is proportional to the displacement of the magnet. Linear EMEH devices have a fixed natural frequency that depends on the mass and stiffness of the system. Linear EMEH devices can be designed for micro or macro scale applications, depending on the size and power requirements of the device.

### Micro Scale Linear EMEH Devices

Micro scale linear EMEH devices are typically based on microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) technology, which allows the fabrication of miniaturized components with high precision and low cost. Micro scale linear EMEH devices can be used to power wireless sensor nodes, biomedical implants, or wearable electronics. Some examples of micro scale linear EMEH devices are:

- Cantilever-based EMEH devices, which consist of a thin beam with a coil attached to one end and a magnet attached to the other end. The beam can vibrate in response to external excitations and induce an EMF in the coil. The power output of cantilever-based EMEH devices can be improved by using multiple beams, multiple coils, or multiple magnets.
- Spring-mass EMEH devices, which consist of a mass attached to a spring and a coil. The mass can oscillate along the spring axis and induce an EMF in the coil. The power output of spring-mass EMEH devices can be improved by using multiple springs, multiple masses, or multiple coils.
- Bridge-based EMEH devices, which consist of a bridge structure with a coil and a magnet attached to opposite sides. The bridge can bend or twist in response to external excitations and induce an EMF in the coil. The power output of bridge-based EMEH devices can be improved by using multiple bridges, multiple coils, or multiple magnets.

### Macro Scale Linear EMEH Devices

Macro scale linear EMEH devices are typically based on conventional mechanical components, such as springs, magnets, coils, gears, or flywheels. Macro scale linear EMEH devices can be used to power large-scale systems, such as buildings, vehicles, or industrial machines. Some examples of macro scale linear EMEH devices are:

- Vibration-based EMEH devices, which consist of a mass-spring-damper system with a coil and a magnet. The system can vibrate in response to ambient vibrations and induce an EMF in the coil. The power output of vibration-based EMEH devices can be improved by using multiple masses, multiple springs, multiple dampers, multiple coils, or multiple magnets.
- Rotation-based EMEH devices, which consist of a rotating shaft with a coil and a magnet. The shaft can rotate in response to external torques and induce an EMF in the coil. The power output of rotation-based EMEH devices can be improved by using multiple shafts, multiple coils, or multiple magnets.
- Translation-based EMEH devices, which consist of a linear actuator with a coil and a magnet. The actuator can move in response to external forces and induce an EMF in the coil. The power output of translation-based EMEH devices can be improved by using multiple actuators, multiple coils, or multiple magnets.

Nonlinear EMEH devices have a frequency-dependent natural frequency that depends on the amplitude and shape of the vibration.
