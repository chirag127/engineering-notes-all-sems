### Powering Wireless SHM Sensor Nodes through Energy Harvesting

- Wireless sensor nodes and sensor networks are widely used for structural health monitoring (SHM) applications, such as detecting damage, cracks, corrosion, or deformation in buildings, bridges, pipelines, aircraft, etc.
- However, one of the main challenges is how to power the sensor nodes, which typically consist of sensors, microcontrollers, memory, and wireless transceivers, without requiring frequent battery replacement or maintenance.
- Energy harvesting is a promising technique to provide a sustainable and autonomous power source for wireless sensor nodes by converting ambient energy sources, such as solar, thermal, vibration, wind, or electromagnetic radiation, into electrical energy.
- Energy harvesting systems usually consist of four components: an energy transducer, a power conditioning circuit, an energy storage device, and a power management unit.
- The energy transducer converts the ambient energy into electrical energy, such as a photovoltaic cell, a thermoelectric generator, a piezoelectric element, or an antenna.
- The power conditioning circuit adjusts the output voltage and current of the energy transducer to match the requirements of the sensor node, such as a DC-DC converter, a rectifier, or a regulator.
- The energy storage device stores the excess energy harvested when the ambient energy is abundant and provides energy when the ambient energy is scarce, such as a battery, a capacitor, or a supercapacitor.
- The power management unit controls the charging and discharging of the energy storage device and optimizes the power consumption of the sensor node, such as a microcontroller, a switch, or a sensor.
- Different energy harvesting techniques have different advantages and disadvantages, depending on the availability, intensity, and variability of the ambient energy sources, as well as the size, weight, cost, and efficiency of the energy transducers and power conditioning circuits.
- Some examples of energy harvesting techniques for wireless SHM sensor nodes are:

  - Solar energy harvesting: using photovoltaic cells to convert sunlight into electrical energy. This technique is suitable for outdoor applications where sunlight is abundant and predictable, but it requires large surface area, high cost, and low efficiency of the photovoltaic cells, and it cannot work at night or in cloudy conditions.
  - Thermal energy harvesting: using thermoelectric generators to convert temperature gradients into electrical energy. This technique is suitable for applications where there is a large and stable temperature difference between the sensor node and the environment, such as in industrial processes, but it requires high thermal conductivity, low electrical conductivity, and high Seebeck coefficient of the thermoelectric materials, and it has low efficiency and power density.
  - Vibration energy harvesting: using piezoelectric elements to convert mechanical vibrations into electrical energy. This technique is suitable for applications where there is a high and periodic vibration source, such as in machinery, vehicles, or human motion, but it requires high mechanical stress, high resonant frequency, and high coupling coefficient of the piezoelectric materials, and it has low power density and bandwidth.
  - Wind energy harvesting: using micro wind turbines to convert air flow into electrical energy. This technique is suitable for applications where there is a sufficient and steady wind speed, such as in coastal or mountainous areas, but it requires large size, high cost, and high maintenance of the wind turbines, and it is affected by turbulence and noise.
  - Electromagnetic energy harvesting: using antennas to convert electromagnetic radiation into electrical energy. This technique is suitable for applications where there is a strong and constant electromagnetic source, such as in radio frequency (RF) or microwave wireless energy transmission systems, but it requires high gain, high efficiency, and high impedance matching of the antennas, and it is limited by the distance and direction of the electromagnetic source.
