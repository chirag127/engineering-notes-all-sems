### Non-linear techniques for electromagnetic energy harvesting

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is the process of converting ambient electromagnetic radiation into electrical energy for powering low-power devices.
- Non-linear techniques are used to optimize the harvested energy from electromagnetic generators by increasing the output voltage or current, or by adapting the impedance to the load.
- Some examples of non-linear techniques are:
  - Synchronous electric charge extraction (SECE) and synchronous induced current extraction (SICE) : These techniques use switches to transfer the charge or current from the generator to the load at optimal instants, resulting in voltage or current amplification and load independence.
  - Synchronized switch harvesting on inductor (SSHI)  : This technique uses a switch and an inductor to invert the voltage across the generator at the optimal time, resulting in voltage amplification and impedance matching.
  - Magnetic levitation : This technique uses a permanent magnet to levitate the moving part of the generator, resulting in a non-linear restoring force and a wide bandwidth of operation.
  - Non-linear multi-stable systems : These systems use multiple magnets to create multiple stable positions for the moving part of the generator, resulting in a non-linear restoring force and a high sensitivity to ambient vibrations.
