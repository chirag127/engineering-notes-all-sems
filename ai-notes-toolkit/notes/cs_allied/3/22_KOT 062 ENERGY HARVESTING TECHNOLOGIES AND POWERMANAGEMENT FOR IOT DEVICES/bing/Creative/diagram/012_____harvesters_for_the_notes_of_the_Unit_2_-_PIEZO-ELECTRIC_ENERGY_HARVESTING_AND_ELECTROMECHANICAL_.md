### Harvesters
- Harvesters are devices that convert ambient mechanical energy into electrical energy using piezoelectric materials.
- Piezoelectric materials are materials that generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or strain, and vice versa.
- Harvesters can be used to power low-power electronic devices, such as sensors, wireless transmitters, or microcontrollers, without the need for batteries or wires.
- Harvesters can be classified into two types: resonant and non-resonant.
  - Resonant harvesters are designed to operate at a specific frequency that matches the natural frequency of the piezoelectric structure. They can achieve high power output, but are sensitive to frequency variations and require precise tuning.
  - Non-resonant harvesters are designed to operate over a wide range of frequencies, but have lower power output. They can adapt to different excitation sources, such as random vibrations, human motion, or fluid flow.

### Electromechanical Modeling
- Electromechanical modeling is the process of developing mathematical models that describe the coupled mechanical and electrical behavior of piezoelectric harvesters.
- Electromechanical models can be used to analyze the performance, optimize the design, and predict the output of piezoelectric harvesters under various conditions.
- Electromechanical models can be classified into two types: lumped-parameter and distributed-parameter.
  - Lumped-parameter models are models that assume the piezoelectric structure as a single mass-spring-damper system with equivalent electrical components. They are simple and easy to implement, but have limited accuracy and applicability.
  - Distributed-parameter models are models that account for the spatial variation of the mechanical and electrical properties of the piezoelectric structure. They are more accurate and general, but require more complex mathematical tools and numerical methods.
