### Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags

- RF sensors and ID tags are devices that use radio frequency (RF) signals to communicate data, such as identification, location, temperature, or motion, to a reader or a network.
- RF sensors and ID tags can be classified into passive, semi-passive, or active, depending on their power source and functionality.
- Passive RF sensors and ID tags have no battery and rely on the RF energy from the reader to power up their circuitry and transmit their data. They have a limited range and performance, but a long lifetime and low cost.
- Semi-passive RF sensors and ID tags have a battery that powers their circuitry, but not their RF communication. They use the RF energy from the reader to transmit their data. They have a longer range and better performance than passive tags, but a shorter lifetime and higher cost.
- Active RF sensors and ID tags have a battery that powers both their circuitry and their RF communication. They can initiate their own transmission and communicate with other tags or networks. They have the longest range and the best performance, but the shortest lifetime and the highest cost.
- Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags is the process of collecting ambient energy from the environment, such as light, heat, vibration, or RF waves, and converting it into electrical energy to power the devices.
- Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags can extend their lifetime, reduce their cost, and enhance their functionality, especially for active and semi-passive devices.
- Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags can be done using various techniques, such as solar cells, thermoelectric generators, piezoelectric transducers, or RF rectifiers.
- Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags can also be done using the RF signal from the reader or other sources, such as TV or cellular towers, as the energy source. This is called RF energy harvesting or RF power harvesting.
- RF energy harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags can be done using different methods, such as direct power transfer, backscatter modulation, or harmonic power harvesting.
- Direct power transfer is the method of using the RF signal from the reader to directly power the tag circuitry and communication. This is the conventional method used by passive tags.
- Backscatter modulation is the method of using the RF signal from the reader to power the tag circuitry, and then modulating the reflected signal to transmit the tag data. This is the method used by semi-passive tags and some passive tags.
- Harmonic power harvesting is the method of using the RF signal from the reader or other sources to power the tag circuitry, and then generating a harmonic signal to transmit the tag data. This is a novel method that can enable passive tags to have longer range and higher data rate.
