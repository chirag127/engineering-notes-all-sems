### Power sources for WSN

Wireless sensor networks (WSN) are composed of small, low-power devices that can sense, process, and communicate data wirelessly. WSN have many applications in environmental monitoring, industrial automation, health care, security, etc. However, one of the main challenges of WSN is the limited energy supply of the sensor nodes, which affects their lifetime and performance. Therefore, finding suitable power sources for WSN is an important research topic.

According to , power sources for WSN can be classified into three categories:

- **Energy reservoirs**: These are devices that store energy on the node, such as batteries, capacitors, or fuel cells. They have the advantage of being simple and reliable, but they have the drawback of being finite and bulky. The lifetime of the node depends on the energy capacity and the discharge rate of the reservoir. Examples of energy reservoirs are:

  - **Batteries**: They are the most common power source for WSN, as they provide high energy density and low cost. However, they have limitations such as self-discharge, leakage, environmental impact, and difficulty of replacement or recharging. Batteries can be primary (non-rechargeable) or secondary (rechargeable). The most common types of batteries for WSN are alkaline, lithium, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, and lithium-ion.

  - **Capacitors**: They are devices that store energy in an electric field. They have the advantage of being fast and efficient in charging and discharging, and having a long cycle life. However, they have the drawback of having low energy density and high leakage. Capacitors can be used as a backup or a buffer for batteries, or as the main power source for low-power applications. The most common types of capacitors for WSN are electrostatic, electrolytic, and supercapacitors.

  - **Fuel cells**: They are devices that convert chemical energy into electrical energy. They have the advantage of having high energy density and low environmental impact. However, they have the drawback of being complex, expensive, and requiring fuel replenishment. Fuel cells can be classified by the type of fuel and the type of electrolyte. The most common types of fuel cells for WSN are hydrogen, methanol, and microbial.

- **Power distribution**: These are methods that deliver power to the node from an external source, such as a wire, a laser, or a radio frequency. They have the advantage of providing continuous and unlimited power, but they have the drawback of being costly, complex, and intrusive. The power distribution depends on the distance, the efficiency, and the safety of the transmission. Examples of power distribution are:

  - **Wires**: They are the simplest and most reliable way of delivering power to the node. However, they have limitations such as being bulky, inflexible, and vulnerable to damage or interference. Wires can be used for fixed or mobile nodes, depending on the length and the routing of the cable.

  - **Lasers**: They are beams of light that can carry power to the node through a photovoltaic cell. They have the advantage of being wireless, directional, and efficient. However, they have limitations such as being expensive, complex, and requiring line-of-sight. Lasers can be used for long-distance or short-distance transmission, depending on the power and the divergence of the beam.

  - **Radio frequencies**: They are electromagnetic waves that can carry power to the node through an antenna. They have the advantage of being wireless, omnidirectional, and scalable. However, they have limitations such as being inefficient, noisy, and regulated. Radio frequencies can be used for near-field or far-field transmission, depending on the frequency and the power of the wave.

- **Power scavenging**: These are techniques that harvest ambient energy from the environment, such as solar, thermal, mechanical, or electromagnetic. They have the advantage of being self-sustaining, renewable, and ubiquitous. However, they have the drawback of being intermittent, unpredictable, and low-power.
