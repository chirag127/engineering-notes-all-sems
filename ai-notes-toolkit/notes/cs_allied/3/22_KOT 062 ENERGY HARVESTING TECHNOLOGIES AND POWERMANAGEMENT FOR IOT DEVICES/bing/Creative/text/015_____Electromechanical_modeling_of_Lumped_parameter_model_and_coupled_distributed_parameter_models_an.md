### Electromechanical modeling of Lumped parameter model and coupled distributed parameter models and closed-form solutions

- Electromechanical modeling is the process of describing the dynamic behavior of systems that involve both electrical and mechanical components, such as piezoelectric devices, using mathematical equations and parameters.
- Lumped parameter model is a type of electromechanical model that assumes that the system can be represented by a finite number of discrete elements, such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, masses, springs, and dampers, that are connected at nodes with zero electrical length and capacitance to ground. Lumped parameter models are often simpler and easier to analyze than distributed parameter models, but they may not capture the effects of spatial variations and wave propagation in the system .
- Distributed parameter model is a type of electromechanical model that considers the system as a continuous medium, such as a beam or a plate, that is characterized by spatially varying physical properties, such as density, stiffness, and piezoelectric coefficients. Distributed parameter models are often more accurate and realistic than lumped parameter models, but they require solving partial differential equations and boundary conditions, which can be challenging and computationally intensive .
- Coupled distributed parameter models are distributed parameter models that account for the interactions between different physical domains, such as mechanical, electrical, thermal, and fluidic, in the system. Coupled distributed parameter models can capture the complex phenomena and nonlinearities that arise from the coupling effects, such as hysteresis, creep, and feedback, but they also increase the complexity and dimensionality of the model .
- Closed-form solutions are analytical solutions that can be expressed in terms of a finite number of known functions, such as trigonometric, exponential, or polynomial functions, without the need for numerical methods or approximations. Closed-form solutions are desirable because they can provide exact and explicit expressions for the system variables, such as displacement, voltage, and power, as functions of the system parameters, such as frequency, load, and geometry. However, closed-form solutions are not always possible or easy to obtain, especially for nonlinear and coupled distributed parameter models .
