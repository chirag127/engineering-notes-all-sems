### Power conditioning and losses for energy harvesting wireless sensors

- Power conditioning is the process of converting the harvested energy from various sources (such as solar, thermal, vibration, etc.) into a suitable form for the wireless sensor nodes (WSNs)  .
- Power conditioning typically involves voltage regulation, rectification, filtering, and storage .
- Power conditioning is essential for achieving self-powered WSNs that can operate autonomously and reliably for machine condition monitoring (MCM) and other applications    .
- Power losses can occur in different stages of power conditioning, such as in the transducer, the interface circuit, the storage device, and the load .
- Power losses can reduce the efficiency and performance of the energy harvesting system and the WSN .
- Some of the factors that can cause power losses are:
  - Mismatch between the source impedance and the load impedance .
  - Nonlinear and time-varying characteristics of the energy source and the load .
  - Parasitic elements and losses in the components and wires .
  - Leakage currents and self-discharge in the storage device .
- Some of the techniques that can reduce power losses are:
  - Impedance matching and maximum power point tracking (MPPT) .
  - Adaptive and intelligent control of the power conditioning circuit .
  - Low-power and high-efficiency components and devices .
  - Optimal sizing and placement of the storage device .
