### Energy Harvesting Based Sensor Networks

- Energy harvesting based sensor networks are wireless sensor networks that use ambient energy sources such as solar, wind, vibration, thermal, etc. to power the sensor nodes and extend their lifetime .
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks have several advantages over battery-powered sensor networks, such as:
  - They can reduce the maintenance cost and environmental impact of replacing batteries.
  - They can enable the deployment of sensor networks in remote, inaccessible, or hazardous environments.
  - They can enhance the reliability and robustness of sensor networks by providing continuous and stable power supply.
  - They can support the scalability and adaptability of sensor networks by allowing dynamic reconfiguration and self-organization  .
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks also face several challenges, such as:
  - The energy sources are often intermittent, unpredictable, and variable, which requires efficient energy management and storage techniques.
  - The energy harvesting devices are often bulky, costly, and complex, which limits the miniaturization and integration of sensor nodes.
  - The energy harvesting performance is often affected by environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, noise, etc., which requires robust and adaptive design and optimization methods  .
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks can be classified into two types based on the energy harvesting mode:
  - Dedicated energy harvesting: The sensor nodes have dedicated energy harvesting devices that are separate from the sensing and communication components. This mode can provide higher energy efficiency and flexibility, but also increases the size and cost of the sensor nodes.
  - Parasitic energy harvesting: The sensor nodes use the same components for both energy harvesting and sensing or communication. This mode can reduce the size and cost of the sensor nodes, but also introduces trade-offs and interferences between the functions .
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks can be applied to various domains and applications, such as:
  - Environmental monitoring: The sensor nodes can harvest energy from solar, wind, water, or temperature gradients to monitor the air quality, water quality, soil moisture, etc.
  - Structural health monitoring: The sensor nodes can harvest energy from vibrations, strain, or acoustic waves to monitor the integrity, damage, or fatigue of buildings, bridges, pipelines, etc.
  - Biomedical and healthcare: The sensor nodes can harvest energy from body heat, motion, or biochemical reactions to monitor the vital signs, activity, or health status of humans or animals.
  - Internet of things: The sensor nodes can harvest energy from radio frequency, light, or magnetic fields to enable the connectivity, interoperability, and intelligence of various devices and objects  .
