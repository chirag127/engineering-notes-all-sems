## Unit 3 - ELECTROMAGNETIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND NON-LINEAR TECHNIQUES

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is the process of converting ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using electromagnetic transducers.
- Electromagnetic transducers consist of a coil and a magnet that move relative to each other and induce a voltage across the coil terminals.
- Non-linear techniques are methods that enhance the performance of electromagnetic energy harvesters by exploiting the non-linear behavior of the system, such as bistability, magnetic levitation, snap-through, etc.
- Non-linear techniques can increase the bandwidth, power output, and robustness of the energy harvesters, as well as enable self-powered operation and adaptive tuning.
- Some examples of non-linear techniques are:

  - Bistable energy harvesting: A system that has two stable equilibrium positions and can switch between them under external excitation. This can result in large-amplitude oscillations and high power output .
  - Magnetic levitation energy harvesting: A system that uses magnetic forces to suspend a magnet above a coil, creating a negative stiffness effect. This can reduce the damping and increase the frequency range of the system .
  - Snap-through energy harvesting: A system that uses a buckled beam or a curved membrane that can snap from one configuration to another under external force. This can generate high power pulses and widen the frequency bandwidth.
