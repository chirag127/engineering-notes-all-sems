### Energy sources for energy harvesting

Energy harvesting is the process of capturing and converting ambient energy from various sources into electrical energy for powering small, wireless autonomous devices. Some of the common energy sources for energy harvesting are:

- **Thermal energy**: Thermal energy is the energy associated with temperature variations and heat flows. It can be harvested from sources such as body heat, industrial waste heat, geothermal heat, solar radiation, etc. Thermal energy can be converted into electrical energy using thermoelectric generators (TEGs), which are devices that generate voltage based on the Seebeck effect .
- **RF energy**: RF energy is the electromagnetic energy that propagates through space as radio waves. It can be harvested from sources such as TV and radio broadcasts, cellular networks, Wi-Fi routers, etc. RF energy can be converted into electrical energy using RF energy harvesters, which are devices that capture and rectify RF signals into DC voltage .
- **Solar energy**: Solar energy is the radiant energy emitted by the sun. It can be harvested from sources such as natural or artificial light. Solar energy can be converted into electrical energy using photovoltaic (PV) cells, which are devices that use the photovoltaic effect to generate current from light .
- **Mechanical energy**: Mechanical energy is the energy associated with motion and deformation. It can be harvested from sources such as human and animal movements, vibrations, wind, water, etc. Mechanical energy can be converted into electrical energy using various mechanisms, such as piezoelectric, electromagnetic, electrostatic, etc. Piezoelectric devices generate voltage based on the piezoelectric effect, which is the property of some materials to produce electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress. Electromagnetic devices generate voltage based on the Faraday's law of induction, which is the phenomenon of inducing electric current in a conductor by changing magnetic flux. Electrostatic devices generate voltage based on the variable capacitance effect, which is the change in capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor due to the variation of the distance between the plates  .
