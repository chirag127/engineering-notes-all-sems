### Harvesting Microelectronic Circuits

- Harvesting microelectronic circuits are circuits that can extract and convert ambient energy sources, such as light, heat, vibration, or radio waves, into electrical energy for powering low-power devices.
- Harvesting microelectronic circuits can enable wireless sensors, wearable devices, implantable devices, and other applications that require long-term operation without batteries or wires.
- Harvesting microelectronic circuits typically consist of four main components: a transducer, a rectifier, a regulator, and a storage element.
  - A transducer is a device that converts one form of energy into another, such as a photovoltaic cell, a thermoelectric generator, a piezoelectric element, or an antenna.
  - A rectifier is a circuit that converts an alternating current (AC) into a direct current (DC), such as a diode bridge, a voltage multiplier, or a synchronous rectifier.
  - A regulator is a circuit that maintains a constant output voltage or current, regardless of the input variations, such as a linear regulator, a switching regulator, or a maximum power point tracking (MPPT) circuit.
  - A storage element is a device that stores electrical energy for later use, such as a capacitor, a battery, or a supercapacitor.
- Harvesting microelectronic circuits face several challenges and trade-offs, such as low input power, variable input conditions, high conversion efficiency, low quiescent power, small size, and low cost .
- Harvesting microelectronic circuits require careful design and optimization of the transducer, the rectifier, the regulator, and the storage element, as well as the interface and communication between them .
- Harvesting microelectronic circuits can benefit from new methods and technologies for energy conversion, such as nanomaterials, microelectromechanical systems (MEMS), and organic electronics .
