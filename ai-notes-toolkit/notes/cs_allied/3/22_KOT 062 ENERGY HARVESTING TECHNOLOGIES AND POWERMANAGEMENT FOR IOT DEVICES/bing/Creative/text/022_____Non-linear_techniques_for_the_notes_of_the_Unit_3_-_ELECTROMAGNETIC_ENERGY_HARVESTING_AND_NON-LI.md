### Non-linear techniques for electromagnetic energy harvesting

- Non-linear techniques are methods that exploit the non-linear behavior of some electromagnetic energy harvesters to enhance the power output or the bandwidth of operation.
- Non-linear techniques can be classified into two categories: passive and active.
- Passive non-linear techniques use the inherent non-linearity of the harvester structure or the magnetic field to create a non-linear restoring force or a bi-stable or multi-stable potential.
- Active non-linear techniques use external circuits or switches to modify the electrical load or the magnetic field of the harvester in a synchronized manner.
- Some examples of passive non-linear techniques are:
  - Magnetic levitation-based electromagnetic energy harvesting, which uses a magnetically levitated coil to induce a voltage across a fixed coil.
  - Non-linear multi-stable electromagnetic energy harvesting, which uses a piezoelectric element attached to a cantilever beam with two repelling magnets at the tip.
  - Non-linear electromagnetic system, which uses a small magnet moving in a tube surrounded by a coil and two fixed magnets at the ends  .
- Some examples of active non-linear techniques are:
  - Synchronized switching harvesting on inductor (SSHI), which uses a switch to periodically short-circuit the piezoelectric element and store the energy in an inductor.
  - Synchronized electric charge extraction (SECE), which uses a switch to periodically transfer the charge from the piezoelectric element to a capacitor.
  - Variable reluctance energy harvester (VREH), which uses a switch to periodically change the magnetic flux linkage of a coil.
