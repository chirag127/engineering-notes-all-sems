### Conversion for the notes of the Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS in the subject of KOT 062 ENERGY HARVESTING TECHNOLOGIES AND POWERMANAGEMENT FOR IOT DEVICES KCS

- Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can collect and convert ambient energy from their environment into electrical energy for powering themselves and transmitting data .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can use various sources of energy, such as solar, thermal, kinetic, electromagnetic, or piezoelectric .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors have several advantages over battery-powered sensors, such as:
  - They are maintenance-free, flexible, and inexpensive to install .
  - They can operate in harsh or inaccessible environments where battery replacement is difficult or impossible .
  - They can reduce the environmental impact of battery disposal and recycling .
  - They can enable new applications and functionalities that require continuous or frequent sensing and communication .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors also have some challenges and limitations, such as:
  - They depend on the availability and intensity of the ambient energy sources, which can be variable and unpredictable .
  - They have to balance the trade-off between energy consumption and performance, such as data rate, reliability, and latency .
  - They have to deal with the mismatch between the harvested energy and the required power, such as voltage, current, and frequency .
  - They have to cope with the interference and noise from other wireless devices and sources .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can be classified into different categories based on the type of energy source, the type of energy conversion, the type of energy storage, and the type of communication protocol .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can be applied to various domains and scenarios, such as smart buildings, industrial automation, environmental monitoring, healthcare, agriculture, and security  .
