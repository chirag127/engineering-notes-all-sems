### Types of Energy Harvesting Systems

Energy harvesting systems are devices or systems that are used to convert ambient energy, which is derived from external resources existing in the atmosphere, into electrical energy. Energy harvesting systems can be classified into different types based on the source of ambient energy, the conversion mechanism, and the application. Some of the common types of energy harvesting systems are:

- **Solar energy harvesting systems**: These systems use photovoltaic cells to convert solar radiation into electrical energy. Solar energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as wireless sensor networks, wearable devices, and remote monitoring systems. Solar energy harvesting systems can be further divided into subtypes such as organic, inorganic, and hybrid solar cells.

- **Thermal energy harvesting systems**: These systems use thermoelectric generators to convert temperature gradients or heat flows into electrical energy. Thermal energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as waste heat recovery, body heat harvesting, and environmental monitoring. Thermal energy harvesting systems can be further divided into subtypes such as bulk, thin-film, and nanostructured thermoelectric generators.

- **Mechanical energy harvesting systems**: These systems use piezoelectric, electrostatic, or electromagnetic transducers to convert mechanical vibrations, motions, or forces into electrical energy. Mechanical energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as structural health monitoring, human motion harvesting, and vehicle energy harvesting. Mechanical energy harvesting systems can be further divided into subtypes such as resonant, non-resonant, and broadband mechanical energy harvesters.

- **Electromagnetic energy harvesting systems**: These systems use antennas, coils, or rectifiers to convert electromagnetic waves or fields into electrical energy. Electromagnetic energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as radio frequency identification, wireless power transfer, and ambient radio frequency harvesting. Electromagnetic energy harvesting systems can be further divided into subtypes such as near-field, far-field, and hybrid electromagnetic energy harvesters.

- **Other types of energy harvesting systems**: These systems use other sources of ambient energy such as chemical, biological, or acoustic energy to generate electrical energy. Some examples of these systems are fuel cells, microbial fuel cells, and acoustic energy harvesters  .
