# Unit 1 - Energy Harvesting Systems

## Introduction

Energy harvesting systems are devices or systems that are used to convert ambient energy, which is derived from external resources existing in the atmosphere, into electrical energy. Energy harvesting systems can provide a sustainable and low-cost alternative to battery-operated systems, especially for long-term, low-power, and self-sustaining electronic systems. Energy harvesting systems can also enable wireless communication and sensing in remote and inaccessible locations, such as body sensor networks, oceanographic monitoring sensors, and structural health monitoring systems .

## Types of Energy Harvesting Systems

There are different types of energy harvesting systems, depending on the source of ambient energy and the conversion mechanism. Some of the common types of energy harvesting systems are:

- **Solar energy harvesting systems**: These systems use photovoltaic cells to convert solar radiation into electrical energy. Solar energy harvesting systems can provide high power output, but they are dependent on the availability and intensity of sunlight, which can vary with time, location, and weather conditions.

- **Thermoelectric energy harvesting systems**: These systems use thermoelectric generators to convert temperature gradients into electrical energy. Thermoelectric energy harvesting systems can exploit waste heat from industrial processes, human body, or environmental sources, such as fire, geothermal, or solar heat.

- **Piezoelectric energy harvesting systems**: These systems use piezoelectric materials to convert mechanical stress or strain into electrical energy. Piezoelectric energy harvesting systems can harvest energy from vibrations, acoustic waves, or human motion, such as walking, running, or breathing.

- **Electromagnetic energy harvesting systems**: These systems use electromagnetic induction to convert magnetic flux variations into electrical energy. Electromagnetic energy harvesting systems can harvest energy from rotating machines, such as motors, generators, or turbines, or from ambient electromagnetic fields, such as radio frequency, microwave, or wireless power transfer.

- **Electrostatic energy harvesting systems**: These systems use electrostatic or capacitive transducers to convert mechanical displacement into electrical energy. Electrostatic energy harvesting systems can harvest energy from vibrations, human motion, or wind.

- **Levitation based energy harvesting systems**: These systems use a levitating magnet to convert kinetic energy into electrical energy. Levitation based energy harvesting systems can harvest energy from vibrations or motion, such as vehicle suspension, railway track, or bridge.
