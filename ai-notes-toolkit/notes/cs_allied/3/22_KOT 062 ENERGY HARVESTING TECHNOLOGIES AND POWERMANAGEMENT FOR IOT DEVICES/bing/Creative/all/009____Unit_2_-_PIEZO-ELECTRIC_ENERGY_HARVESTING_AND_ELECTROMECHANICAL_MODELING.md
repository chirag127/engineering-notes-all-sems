# Unit 2 - Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting and Electromechanical Modeling

- Piezoelectric energy harvesting is the process of converting mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using piezoelectric materials, which are materials that generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or strain.
- Piezoelectric energy harvesters are devices that utilize piezoelectric materials to scavenge energy from ambient vibrations, such as those caused by wind, traffic, human motion, etc. They can be used to power low-power electronic devices, such as wireless sensors, microcontrollers, or LEDs, without the need for batteries or wires.
- Electromechanical modeling of piezoelectric energy harvesters is the mathematical description of the coupled dynamics of the mechanical and electrical domains of the harvesters, taking into account the geometry, material properties, boundary conditions, and excitation sources of the harvesters.
- Electromechanical modeling can help to understand the behavior, performance, and optimization of piezoelectric energy harvesters, as well as to design and test new configurations and applications of the harvesters.
- There are different approaches to electromechanical modeling of piezoelectric energy harvesters, such as lumped-parameter models, distributed-parameter models, finite element models, and analytical models. Each approach has its own advantages and limitations, depending on the complexity, accuracy, and computational cost required for the analysis.
- Lumped-parameter models are the simplest and most common models, which assume that the harvester can be represented by a single degree of freedom (DOF) mechanical system coupled to an equivalent electrical circuit. They are suitable for linear and symmetric harvesters with simple geometries and uniform material properties, but they cannot capture the effects of higher modes, shear deformation, or non-uniform stress distribution.
- Distributed-parameter models are more general and accurate models, which consider the harvester as a continuous system with infinite DOF. They are based on the partial differential equations (PDEs) that govern the electromechanical coupling of the piezoelectric material, such as the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation or the Timoshenko beam equation. They can account for the effects of higher modes, shear deformation, and non-uniform stress distribution, but they require more mathematical and numerical skills to solve and analyze.
- Finite element models are numerical models that discretize the harvester into a finite number of elements and nodes, and apply the finite element method (FEM) to solve the PDEs of the electromechanical coupling. They can handle complex geometries, material properties, boundary conditions, and excitation sources, but they are computationally expensive and require specialized software and tools.
- Analytical models are exact or approximate solutions of the PDEs of the electromechanical coupling, obtained by using mathematical techniques such as separation of variables, modal analysis, or perturbation methods. They can provide insight into the physical phenomena and parameters that affect the harvester performance, but they are often limited to idealized or simplified cases and assumptions.
