# Harvesters

- Harvesters are devices that convert ambient energy sources, such as mechanical vibrations, into electrical energy.
- Harvesters can be used to power low-power electronic devices, such as sensors, wireless transmitters, or microcontrollers, without the need for batteries or wires.
- Harvesters can enable self-powered and autonomous systems for various applications, such as structural health monitoring, environmental monitoring, wearable devices, or implantable devices.

# Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting

- Piezoelectric energy harvesting is a type of harvester that utilizes the piezoelectric effect, which is the ability of certain materials to generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or strain.
- Piezoelectric materials can be classified into two categories: piezoceramics, such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT), and piezopolymers, such as polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF).
- Piezoelectric energy harvesters can be designed in various configurations, such as cantilever beams, diaphragms, plates, or shells, depending on the type and direction of the mechanical excitation.

# Electromechanical Modeling of Piezoelectric Energy Harvesters

- Electromechanical modeling of piezoelectric energy harvesters is the process of deriving mathematical equations that describe the coupled mechanical and electrical behavior of the harvester system.
- Electromechanical modeling can be used to analyze the performance of the harvester, such as the output voltage, power, or efficiency, and to optimize the design parameters, such as the geometry, material, or load resistance.
- Electromechanical modeling can be performed at different levels of complexity, such as lumped-parameter models, distributed-parameter models, or finite element models, depending on the accuracy and computational cost required.
