# Harvesting for RF Sensors and ID Tags

- RF sensors and ID tags are devices that use radio frequency (RF) signals to communicate data, such as identification, location, temperature, humidity, etc.
- RF sensors and ID tags can be classified into passive, semi-passive, and active, depending on their power source and functionality.
- Passive RF sensors and ID tags have no battery and rely on the RF energy from a reader or interrogator to power up their circuitry and transmit their data. They have a limited range and functionality, but are low-cost and maintenance-free.
- Semi-passive RF sensors and ID tags have a battery that powers their sensor and logic circuitry, but use the RF energy from a reader or interrogator to transmit their data. They have a longer range and functionality than passive tags, but are more expensive and require battery replacement.
- Active RF sensors and ID tags have a battery that powers their entire circuitry, including the RF transmitter. They have the longest range and functionality, but are the most expensive and require frequent battery replacement.
- Energy harvesting is a technique that enables RF sensors and ID tags to collect energy from the environment, such as solar, thermal, mechanical, or electromagnetic sources, and use it to power their circuitry or recharge their battery.
- Energy harvesting can extend the lifetime, range, and functionality of RF sensors and ID tags, as well as reduce the environmental impact and maintenance cost of batteries.
- Energy harvesting can be implemented in different ways, such as using the RF reader signal, the third harmonic signal generated by the RFID chip, or ambient RF sources, to deliver energy to the RF sensors and ID tags.
- Energy harvesting can also be combined with other techniques, such as duty cycling, power management, and adaptive modulation, to optimize the performance and efficiency of RF sensors and ID tags.
