# Piezoelectric materials

- Piezoelectric materials are materials that can convert mechanical stress into electrical charge, or vice versa. This phenomenon is called the **piezoelectric effect**.
- Piezoelectric materials can be classified into the following categories:
  - **Crystalline materials**: These are natural or synthetic materials that have a regular and ordered arrangement of atoms. Examples are quartz, tourmaline, and gallium nitride.
  - **Ceramic materials**: These are polycrystalline materials that are formed by sintering powders of different compounds. Examples are lead zirconate titanate (PZT), barium titanate, and zinc oxide.
  - **Polymer materials**: These are organic materials that consist of long chains of molecules. Examples are polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), polyurethane, and polyimide.
  - **Composite materials**: These are materials that combine two or more different types of materials to enhance their properties. Examples are PZT-polymer, PZT-ceramic, and PZT-epoxy composites.
  - **Glass-ceramic materials**: These are materials that are formed by controlled crystallization of glass. Examples are lithium silicate and barium titanate silicate glass-ceramics.
- Piezoelectric materials have many applications in various fields, such as :
  - **Sensors and actuators**: These are devices that can measure or generate physical quantities, such as force, pressure, displacement, vibration, sound, etc. Examples are microphones, speakers, accelerometers, ultrasonic transducers, etc.
  - **Energy harvesting**: This is the process of converting ambient mechanical energy into electrical energy for powering low-power devices, such as wireless sensors, wearable electronics, etc. Examples are piezoelectric shoes, backpacks, floor tiles, etc.
  - **Power monitoring**: This is the process of measuring or controlling high-power applications, such as medical treatment, sonochemistry, industrial processing, etc. Examples are piezoelectric transformers, switches, relays, etc.
  - **Chemical and biological sensors**: These are devices that can detect or quantify the presence of specific molecules, such as gases, liquids, biomolecules, etc. Examples are piezoelectric microbalances, biosensors, gas sensors, etc.
  - **Strain gauges**: These are devices that can measure the deformation or strain of a material or structure under external forces. Examples are piezoelectric strain gauges, load cells, etc.
