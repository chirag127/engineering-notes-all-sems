### Power sources for WSN

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are composed of small, low-power devices that can sense, process, and communicate data wirelessly. WSNs have many applications in environmental monitoring, industrial automation, health care, smart buildings, and military operations. However, one of the main challenges of WSNs is how to provide adequate and reliable power for the sensor nodes, which are often deployed in remote or inaccessible locations.

There are three main categories of power sources for WSNs :

- **Energy reservoirs**: These are devices that store energy on the node, such as batteries, capacitors, or fuel cells. They have the advantage of being simple, compact, and widely available, but they have the disadvantage of having limited energy capacity, finite lifetime, and environmental impact.
- **Power distribution**: These are methods that deliver power to the node from an external source, such as wires, electromagnetic induction, or radio frequency. They have the advantage of providing continuous and unlimited power, but they have the disadvantage of being costly, complex, and intrusive, and may not be feasible for some applications.
- **Power scavenging**: These are techniques that harvest ambient energy from the environment, such as solar, thermal, vibration, or wind. They have the advantage of being self-sustaining, renewable, and scalable, but they have the disadvantage of being intermittent, unpredictable, and dependent on the availability and intensity of the energy source.

Some examples of power sources for WSNs in each category are:

- **Energy reservoirs**: Alkaline batteries, lithium-ion batteries, supercapacitors, micro fuel cells, nuclear batteries.
- **Power distribution**: Power over Ethernet, wireless power transfer, resonant coupling, RF energy harvesting.
- **Power scavenging**: Photovoltaic cells, thermoelectric generators, piezoelectric generators, electromagnetic generators, electrostatic generators.

The choice of the best power source for a WSN depends on several factors, such as the power consumption and duty cycle of the sensor node, the size and weight constraints, the cost and maintenance requirements, the environmental conditions and impact, and the desired lifetime and reliability of the network. A trade-off analysis and a system-level optimization are needed to select the most suitable power source for a given application.
