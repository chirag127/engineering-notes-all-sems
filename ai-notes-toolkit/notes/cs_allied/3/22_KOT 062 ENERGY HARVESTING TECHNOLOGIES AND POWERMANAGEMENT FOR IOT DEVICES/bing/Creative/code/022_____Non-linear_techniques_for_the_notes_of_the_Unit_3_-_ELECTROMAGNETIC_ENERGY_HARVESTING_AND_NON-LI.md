# Non-linear techniques for electromagnetic energy harvesting

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting (EMEH) is a technique that converts ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction.
- Non-linear techniques are used to enhance the performance of EMEH systems by increasing the bandwidth, output voltage, and power density of the harvesters.
- Some of the non-linear techniques for EMEH are:
  - Magnetic levitation: This technique uses a permanent magnet to suspend a movable magnet in a coil, creating a non-linear restoring force that allows the harvester to operate over a wide frequency range.
  - Non-linear interface circuit: This technique uses a diode bridge and a capacitor to rectify and store the alternating current (AC) generated by the harvester, creating a non-linear load that optimizes the power transfer .
  - Multi-stable configuration: This technique uses multiple magnets or springs to create multiple equilibrium positions for the harvester, resulting in snap-through or bi-stable behavior that enhances the output voltage and power.
  - Synchronization: This technique uses a feedback control or a coupling mechanism to synchronize the motion of multiple harvesters, increasing the coherence and amplitude of the output voltage.
