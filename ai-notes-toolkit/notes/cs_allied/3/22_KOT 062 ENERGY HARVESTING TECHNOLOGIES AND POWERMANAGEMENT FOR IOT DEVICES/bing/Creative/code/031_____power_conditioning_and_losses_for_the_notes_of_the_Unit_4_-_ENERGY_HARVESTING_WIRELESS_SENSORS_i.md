# Power conditioning and losses for energy harvesting wireless sensors

- Power conditioning is the process of converting the harvested energy from various sources (such as solar, thermal, vibration, etc.) into a suitable form for the wireless sensor nodes (WSNs)   .
- Power conditioning typically involves voltage regulation, rectification, filtering, and storage of the harvested energy  .
- Power conditioning is essential for ensuring the reliability, efficiency, and functionality of the WSNs, as the harvested energy is often intermittent, unstable, and incompatible with the WSNs' requirements  .
- Power losses can occur at various stages of the power conditioning process, such as during conversion, transmission, storage, and consumption of the harvested energy  .
- Power losses can reduce the amount of available energy for the WSNs, and thus affect their performance, lifetime, and quality of service  .
- Some of the factors that can cause power losses are:

  - The mismatch between the impedance of the energy source and the power conditioning circuit  .
  - The non-ideal characteristics of the power conditioning components, such as resistance, capacitance, inductance, leakage, etc.  .
  - The parasitic effects of the power conditioning circuit, such as noise, interference, distortion, etc.  .
  - The inefficiency of the energy storage devices, such as batteries, capacitors, etc.  .
  - The variation of the environmental conditions, such as temperature, humidity, light, etc.  .
  - The dynamic behavior of the WSNs, such as duty cycle, data rate, transmission power, etc.  .

- Some of the techniques that can reduce power losses are:

  - The use of maximum power point tracking (MPPT) algorithms to optimize the power transfer from the energy source to the power conditioning circuit  .
  - The use of adaptive power management schemes to adjust the power conditioning parameters according to the WSNs' needs and the energy availability  .
  - The use of low-power and high-efficiency power conditioning components, such as converters, regulators, filters, etc.  .
  - The use of energy-aware and cooperative protocols for the WSNs, such as routing, clustering, data aggregation, etc.  .
  - The use of hybrid energy harvesting systems that can exploit multiple energy sources and diversify the energy supply  .
  - The use of energy harvesting techniques that can match the characteristics of the WSNs, such as piezoelectric, thermoelectric, etc.   .
