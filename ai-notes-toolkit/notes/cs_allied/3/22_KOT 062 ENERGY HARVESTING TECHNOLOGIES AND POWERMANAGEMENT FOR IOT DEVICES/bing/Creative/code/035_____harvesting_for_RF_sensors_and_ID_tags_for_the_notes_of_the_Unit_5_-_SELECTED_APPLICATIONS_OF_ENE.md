### Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags

- RF sensors and ID tags are devices that use radio frequency (RF) signals to communicate data, such as identification, location, temperature, humidity, etc.
- RF sensors and ID tags can be classified into two types: passive and active.
- Passive RF sensors and ID tags do not have a battery or a power source. They rely on the RF reader signal to deliver energy and power up their circuitry. They can operate at a distance of up to 10 meters from the reader .
- Active RF sensors and ID tags have a battery or a power source. They can transmit data at a longer distance and at a higher rate than passive ones. They can also perform more complex functions, such as sensing, processing, and storing data. However, they have a limited lifetime due to battery depletion .
- Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags is a technique that enables them to collect energy from the environment, such as RF, motion, heat, light, etc. This can extend their lifetime, reduce their maintenance cost, and enable new applications  .
- Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags can be done in different ways, depending on the type and source of energy available. Some examples are:
  - Using the RF reader signal to power up passive RFID tags or to recharge active RFID tags. This can be done by using a rectifier circuit that converts the AC signal to DC voltage, and a capacitor or a battery that stores the energy  .
  - Using the third harmonic signal generated by the RFID chip to power up an associated sensor to the RFID tag. This can be done by using a harmonic power harvesting system that consists of a matching network, a rectifier, and a storage element.
  - Using the motion of the RFID tag or the sensor to generate electrical energy. This can be done by using a piezoelectric, electromagnetic, or electrostatic transducer that converts mechanical vibrations to electrical voltage, and a storage element that stores the energy .
  - Using the heat difference between the RFID tag or the sensor and the environment to generate electrical energy. This can be done by using a thermoelectric generator that converts heat to electrical voltage, and a storage element that stores the energy .
  - Using the ambient light to generate electrical energy. This can be done by using a photovoltaic cell that converts light to electrical voltage, and a storage element that stores the energy .

- Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags can improve their performance, reliability, and functionality. Some benefits are:
  - Extending the lifetime and range of active RFID tags and sensors by reducing the battery consumption or recharging the battery .
  - Enabling passive RFID tags and sensors to operate at a higher data rate, a lower threshold power, and a wider frequency band by increasing the available energy  .
  - Enabling new applications that require continuous or periodic sensing, processing, and communication of data, such as environmental monitoring, health care, smart buildings, etc .
