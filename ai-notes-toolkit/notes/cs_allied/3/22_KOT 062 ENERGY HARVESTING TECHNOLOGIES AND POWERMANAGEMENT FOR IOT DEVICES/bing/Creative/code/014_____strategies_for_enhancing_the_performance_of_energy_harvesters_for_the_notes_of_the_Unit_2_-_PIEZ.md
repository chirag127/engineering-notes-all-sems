# Strategies for enhancing the performance of energy harvesters

Energy harvesters are devices that can convert ambient energy sources, such as vibration, wind, light, heat, etc., into electrical energy. Energy harvesting is a promising technology for powering low-power consumption devices, such as sensors, wireless networks, wearable electronics, etc., without the need for batteries or external power supply. However, the performance of energy harvesters is often limited by the low and variable energy density of the ambient sources, the mismatch between the frequency and amplitude of the input and the output, the nonlinear and complex dynamics of the harvesting systems, and the losses in the conversion process. Therefore, various strategies have been proposed to enhance the performance of energy harvesters, such as increasing the power output, widening the frequency bandwidth, improving the efficiency, and optimizing the design and materials.

Some of the common strategies for enhancing the performance of energy harvesters are:

- **Adopting new materials**: The choice of materials for energy harvesters affects their output voltage, current, power, and durability. For example, piezoelectric materials can convert mechanical strain into electric charge, and vice versa. The piezoelectric coefficient, which measures the amount of charge generated per unit strain, is an important parameter for piezoelectric energy harvesters. Therefore, researchers have explored various piezoelectric materials, such as ceramics, polymers, composites, nanomaterials, etc., to increase the piezoelectric coefficient and the output power . Similarly, other materials, such as electrostatic, electromagnetic, thermoelectric, triboelectric, etc., have been investigated for different types of energy harvesters.

- **Optimizing the design and structure**: The design and structure of energy harvesters affect their resonance frequency, mode shape, damping, coupling, and output impedance. For example, cantilevered beams are widely used for vibration energy harvesting, as they can resonate at a certain frequency and generate electric charge by piezoelectric, electrostatic, or electromagnetic effects. However, the resonance frequency of cantilevered beams is usually fixed and narrow, which limits their performance under variable and broadband vibrations. Therefore, researchers have proposed various design and structure modifications, such as adding mass, springs, magnets, nonlinear elements, etc., to tune the resonance frequency, widen the frequency bandwidth, induce bi-stable or multi-stable oscillations, and enhance the power output  .

- **Power management and hybridization**: The output power of energy harvesters is usually low and fluctuating, which makes it difficult to directly power the load devices. Therefore, power management circuits, such as rectifiers, regulators, converters, storage units, etc., are often used to condition the output power and match the load impedance. However, power management circuits also introduce losses and parasitic effects, which reduce the efficiency of energy harvesting. Therefore, researchers have developed various power management techniques, such as maximum power point tracking, impedance matching, adaptive control, etc., to optimize the power transfer and minimize the losses. Moreover, hybridization of different energy harvesting mechanisms, such as piezoelectric and electromagnetic, piezoelectric and triboelectric, etc., can also improve the performance of energy harvesters by exploiting multiple energy sources and increasing the output power.

These are some of the strategies for enhancing the performance of energy harvesters. However, there is no universal strategy that can suit all types of energy harvesters and ambient sources. Therefore, the performance enhancement of energy harvesters requires a comprehensive understanding of the energy harvesting principles, the characteristics of the ambient sources, the requirements of the load devices, and the trade-offs between different design parameters and optimization objectives.
