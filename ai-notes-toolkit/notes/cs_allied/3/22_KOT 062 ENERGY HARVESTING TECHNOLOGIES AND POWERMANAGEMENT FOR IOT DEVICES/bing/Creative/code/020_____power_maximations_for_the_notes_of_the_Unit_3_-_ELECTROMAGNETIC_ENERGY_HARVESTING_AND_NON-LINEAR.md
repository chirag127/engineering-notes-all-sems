### Power maximization for the notes of the Unit 3 - ELECTROMAGNETIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND NON-LINEAR TECHNIQUES

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting (EMEH) is a technique to convert ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction.
- EMEH devices typically consist of a coil and a magnet, which can be arranged in different configurations to achieve different performance characteristics.
- Non-linear techniques are used to enhance the power output and bandwidth of EMEH devices, by introducing non-linearities in the mechanical or electrical domain.
- Some examples of non-linear techniques are:
  - Magnetic levitation: using repulsive magnetic forces to suspend the magnet and reduce the damping effect of the coil .
  - Non-linear springs: using springs with variable stiffness to adjust the natural frequency of the system according to the excitation frequency .
  - Non-linear interface circuits: using circuits with diodes, capacitors, inductors and switches to optimize the power transfer between the coil and the load.
- The power maximization of EMEH devices depends on several factors, such as the design parameters, the excitation frequency and amplitude, the load resistance and the interface circuit.
- Some general principles for power maximization are:
  - Matching the natural frequency of the system to the excitation frequency, or using a wideband system to harvest energy from a broad frequency range.
  - Maximizing the relative motion between the coil and the magnet, or using multiple coils and magnets to increase the flux linkage.
  - Minimizing the losses due to friction, eddy currents, hysteresis and parasitic capacitance.
  - Optimizing the load resistance and the interface circuit to match the internal impedance of the coil and maximize the power transfer efficiency.
