### Case studies for Implanted medical devices

Implanted medical devices are electronic devices that are inserted into the human body for various purposes, such as monitoring, diagnosis, therapy, or enhancement. Some examples of implanted medical devices are pacemakers, cochlear implants, neurostimulators, drug delivery systems, and biosensors. 

One of the main challenges for implanted medical devices is to provide a reliable and long-lasting power source that does not require frequent replacement or recharging. Energy harvesting is a promising technique that can convert ambient energy sources, such as mechanical, thermal, or chemical energy, into electrical energy for powering implanted medical devices. Energy harvesting can reduce the size and weight of the devices, eliminate the need for wires or batteries, and improve the biocompatibility and safety of the devices.

Some case studies of implanted medical devices that use energy harvesting are:

- **Heartbeat-powered pacemaker**: A pacemaker is a device that regulates the heart rate by delivering electrical pulses to the heart. A conventional pacemaker requires a battery that needs to be replaced every 5 to 10 years, which involves a surgical procedure and poses a risk of infection. A heartbeat-powered pacemaker is a self-powered device that harvests energy from the mechanical motion of the heart. The device consists of a piezoelectric generator that converts the heart's contraction and relaxation into electrical energy, a power management circuit that regulates the voltage and current, and a capacitor that stores the energy and delivers the pulses to the heart. The device can generate enough energy to power the pacemaker without affecting the heart function .

- **Respiration-powered drug delivery system**: A drug delivery system is a device that administers a controlled amount of medication to a specific site in the body. A conventional drug delivery system requires a battery or an external power source, which limits the mobility and comfort of the patient. A respiration-powered drug delivery system is a self-powered device that harvests energy from the airflow in the lungs. The device consists of a microturbine that rotates by the inhalation and exhalation of the patient, a generator that converts the mechanical energy into electrical energy, a power management circuit that regulates the voltage and current, and a microvalve that controls the release of the drug. The device can deliver the drug in a precise and programmable manner according to the patient's breathing pattern.

- **Glucose-powered biosensor**: A biosensor is a device that detects and measures a biological or chemical substance in the body. A conventional biosensor requires a battery or an external power source, which limits the lifetime and biocompatibility of the device. A glucose-powered biosensor is a self-powered device that harvests energy from the glucose and oxygen in the blood. The device consists of a biofuel cell that catalyzes the oxidation of glucose and the reduction of oxygen, generating electrical energy and water as byproducts, a power management circuit that regulates the voltage and current, and a sensor that measures the concentration of the target substance. The device can monitor the glucose level, the pH, the oxygen saturation, and other biomarkers in the blood without consuming the glucose or oxygen .
