- Electromagnetic energy harvesting (EMEH) is the process of converting ambient electromagnetic waves into electrical energy for powering wireless devices or sensors .
- EMEH can be classified into two types: far-field and near-field. Far-field EMEH captures the radiated electromagnetic waves from distant sources such as radio, TV, or cellular signals. Near-field EMEH exploits the magnetic or electric fields generated by nearby sources such as power lines, transformers, or RFID readers .
- EMEH devices typically consist of three main components: an antenna or a coil, a rectifier, and a power management circuit. The antenna or coil captures the electromagnetic waves and converts them into alternating current (AC). The rectifier converts the AC into direct current (DC). The power management circuit regulates the DC voltage and current to match the load requirements .
- Non-linear techniques are used to enhance the performance of EMEH devices by increasing the output power, widening the bandwidth, or improving the efficiency. Non-linear techniques can be applied to the antenna or coil, the rectifier, or the power management circuit  .
- Some examples of non-linear techniques are:

  - Magnetic levitation: This technique uses permanent magnets to create a non-linear restoring force on a movable coil. The coil can vibrate at a wide range of frequencies and generate high output voltage. The coil can also be coupled with a resonant circuit to increase the power density .
  - Non-linear interface circuit: This technique uses a diode or a transistor to create a non-linear relationship between the input voltage and the output current of the rectifier. The non-linear interface circuit can optimize the power transfer efficiency and the output power of the rectifier.
  - Buck-boost converter: This technique uses a switch-mode DC-DC converter to adjust the output voltage and current of the power management circuit. The buck-boost converter can match the load resistance and the input impedance of the EMEH device, and also provide a constant output voltage for the wireless device or sensor.

- EMEH devices with non-linear techniques have advantages such as low cost, maintenance-free, and autonomous operation. They can also harvest energy from multiple sources and frequencies, and adapt to different environments and load conditions  .
