# Unit 3 - ELECTROMAGNETIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND NON-LINEAR TECHNIQUES

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is the process of converting ambient electromagnetic waves into electrical energy for powering low-power devices, such as wireless sensors, wearable electronics, and implantable devices.
- Non-linear techniques are methods that exploit the non-linear behavior of some components or systems to enhance the performance of energy harvesting devices, such as increasing the output power, widening the bandwidth, or improving the efficiency.
- Some examples of non-linear techniques are:
  - Magnetic levitation: using permanent magnets to create a repulsive force that suspends a movable magnet above a fixed magnet, creating a non-linear spring-mass system that can be excited by ambient vibrations and generate electrical energy through a coil .
  - Non-linear interface circuit: using a diode or a transistor to create a non-linear relationship between the voltage and current of an electromagnetic energy harvester, which can increase the power transfer and optimize the load resistance.
  - Non-linear springs: using springs with non-linear stiffness or damping to modify the dynamic response of an electromagnetic energy harvester, which can increase the frequency bandwidth or the output power .
- The advantages of non-linear techniques are:
  - They can improve the performance of energy harvesting devices in terms of power, bandwidth, or efficiency, which are important parameters for low-power applications.
  - They can enable energy harvesting from a wider range of ambient sources, such as low-frequency vibrations, weak electromagnetic fields, or irregular motions.
  - They can reduce the cost and maintenance of energy harvesting devices, by eliminating the need for complex mechanical or electrical components, such as gears, transformers, or batteries.
- The challenges of non-linear techniques are:
  - They can introduce complexity and uncertainty in the modeling and analysis of energy harvesting devices, due to the non-linear behavior of some components or systems, which can affect the stability, reliability, and predictability of the devices.
  - They can require careful design and optimization of the parameters and components of the energy harvesting devices, such as the geometry, material, and configuration of the magnets, springs, coils, and circuits, to achieve the desired performance and avoid unwanted effects, such as saturation, hysteresis, or chaos.
  - They can depend on the characteristics and variations of the ambient sources, such as the frequency, amplitude, and direction of the vibrations or electromagnetic fields, which can limit the applicability and adaptability of the devices.
