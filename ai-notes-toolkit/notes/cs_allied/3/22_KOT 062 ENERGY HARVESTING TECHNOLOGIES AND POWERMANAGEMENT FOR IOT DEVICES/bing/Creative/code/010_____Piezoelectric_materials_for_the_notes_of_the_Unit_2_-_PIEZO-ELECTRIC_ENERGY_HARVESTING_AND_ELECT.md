### Piezoelectric materials

- Piezoelectric materials are materials that can convert mechanical stress into electrical charge, or vice versa, by the piezoelectric effect  .
- Piezoelectric materials can be classified into the following categories:
  - Single crystalline materials, such as quartz (SiO2), which is the best known natural piezoelectric material.
  - Piezoceramics, such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT), barium titanate, and lead titanate, which are the most commonly produced and widely used synthetic piezoelectric materials  .
  - Piezoelectric semiconductors, such as zinc oxide (ZnO), which have both piezoelectric and semiconducting properties.
  - Piezopolymers, such as polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), which are flexible and lightweight piezoelectric materials.
  - Piezoelectric composites, which are combinations of piezoelectric materials and other materials, such as polymers, metals, or ceramics, to enhance the performance or functionality of the piezoelectric materials.
  - Glass ceramics, such as lithium disilicate (Li2Si2O5) and barium titanate silicate (Ba2TiSiO6), which are transparent and have high piezoelectric coefficients.
- Piezoelectric materials have useful applications in many industries, such as :
  - Sensors and actuators, which can measure or generate force, pressure, acceleration, vibration, sound, etc.
  - Energy harvesting, which can convert ambient mechanical energy into electrical energy for powering low-power devices.
  - Medical imaging and therapy, which can use ultrasound waves for diagnosis or treatment of various diseases and conditions.
  - Sonochemistry and industrial processing, which can use high-power ultrasound waves for enhancing chemical reactions or physical processes.
  - Strain gauges, which can measure the deformation or strain of a material or structure.
  - Tilt sensing, which can detect the orientation or inclination of a device or surface.
