## Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

Energy harvesting systems are devices that convert ambient energy into usable electrical energy. These systems can be used to power low-power electronic devices, such as sensors, without the need for batteries or other external power sources.

1. **Types of Energy Harvesting Systems**: There are several types of energy harvesting systems, including solar, thermal, wind, and kinetic energy harvesting systems. Each type of system converts a different form of ambient energy into electrical energy.

2. **Solar Energy Harvesting**: Solar energy harvesting systems convert sunlight into electrical energy using photovoltaic cells. These systems are commonly used in remote locations where access to the power grid is limited.

3. **Thermal Energy Harvesting**: Thermal energy harvesting systems convert temperature differences into electrical energy using thermoelectric generators. These systems can be used to power devices in environments where temperature differences are present, such as in industrial processes or in the human body.

4. **Wind Energy Harvesting**: Wind energy harvesting systems convert the kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy using wind turbines. These systems are commonly used in remote locations where access to the power grid is limited.

5. **Kinetic Energy Harvesting**: Kinetic energy harvesting systems convert the kinetic energy of motion into electrical energy using piezoelectric or electromagnetic generators. These systems can be used to power devices in environments where motion is present, such as in vehicles or in the human body.

6. **Applications of Energy Harvesting Systems**: Energy harvesting systems have a wide range of applications, including powering remote sensors, wireless sensor networks, and wearable devices. These systems can also be used to reduce the reliance on batteries and other external power sources, reducing the environmental impact of electronic devices.

7. **Challenges and Limitations**: Despite the potential benefits of energy harvesting systems, there are several challenges and limitations that must be addressed. These include the low power output of some systems, the need for energy storage, and the need for efficient power management. Additionally, the cost and complexity of some energy harvesting systems can limit their widespread adoption.

8. **Future Developments**: Research and development in the field of energy harvesting systems is ongoing, with the goal of improving the efficiency, power output, and cost-effectiveness of these systems. Advances in materials science, nanotechnology, and power management are expected to drive future developments in this field.
