# Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

## Energy sources

Energy harvesting, also known as power harvesting or energy scavenging, is the process of capturing and storing small amounts of energy from external sources such as solar, wind, thermal, and kinetic energy. This energy can then be used to power electronic devices such as sensors, wireless communication devices, and wearable technology.

1. **Solar energy:** Solar energy is the most widely used form of renewable energy. Photovoltaic (PV) cells convert sunlight into electricity, which can be used to power electronic devices. Solar panels can be installed on rooftops, walls, or on the ground to capture sunlight and generate electricity.

2. **Wind energy:** Wind energy is another widely used form of renewable energy. Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of the wind into mechanical energy, which is then converted into electricity. Wind turbines can be installed on land or offshore to capture the energy of the wind.

3. **Thermal energy:** Thermal energy harvesting captures heat energy from sources such as the sun, industrial processes, or the human body. This heat energy can be converted into electricity using thermoelectric generators (TEGs), which generate electricity when there is a temperature difference between two sides of the device.

4. **Kinetic energy:** Kinetic energy harvesting captures the energy of motion from sources such as human movement, vibrations, or water flow. This energy can be converted into electricity using piezoelectric materials, which generate an electric charge when they are subjected to mechanical stress.

These are some of the common energy sources used in energy harvesting systems. Each source has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of energy source depends on factors such as the location, availability, and cost of the energy source.
