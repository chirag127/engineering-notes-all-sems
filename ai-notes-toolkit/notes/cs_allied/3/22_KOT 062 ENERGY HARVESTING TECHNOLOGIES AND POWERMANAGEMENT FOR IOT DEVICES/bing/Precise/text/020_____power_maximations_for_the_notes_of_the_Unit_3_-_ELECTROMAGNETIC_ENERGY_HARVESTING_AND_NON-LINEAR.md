### Power Maximizations for Unit 3 - Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting and Non-Linear Techniques

1. To maximize the harvested energy, vibrations at different frequencies need to be included in the harvesting system. Some designs have tried to increase the bandwidth of energy harvesting by placing an array of harvesters with different resonance frequencies. However, these are bulky and have a low power density .
2. Electromagnetic induction effect can be used to maximize the harvested energy. For stronger magnetic field, it is necessary that the maximum area of the coil should cut the magnetic line produced by NdFeB magnet. Hence a square shaped coil structure can be deposited on the cantilever beam .
3. Energy Harvesting is a technique used to collect energy from the environment and then convert it into an electrical source to power a load or recharge a battery. This conversion takes advantage of several energy sources, including light, heat, radio frequency (RF), and vibrational sources .
4. Over the last decade, scientists have looked for ways to harvest and convert ambient energy into usable electrical energy. They have done so mainly through rectifiers, devices that are designed to convert electromagnetic waves from their oscillating (alternating) current to direct current .
5. Electromagnetic energy harvesting holds potential for small and large-scale devices. Electric power densities of up to 8 mW/cm 3 (8 kW/m 3) have already been achieved .
6. The usual methods of analyzing such physical mechanisms are FEA simulations and linear parametric models, such as ANSYS simulations of an electromagnetic micro-power generator, COMSOL simulations of a self-powered thermal sensor, and linear models for several harvesting architectures .
