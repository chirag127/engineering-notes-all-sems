## Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS

1. Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can convert ambient energy into electrical energy to power themselves.
2. These sensors can harvest energy from various sources such as light, heat, vibration, and radio frequency.
3. Energy harvesting wireless sensors have the potential to reduce the need for battery replacement and maintenance, making them ideal for remote or hard-to-reach locations.
4. These sensors can be used in a variety of applications, including environmental monitoring, structural health monitoring, and industrial automation.
5. Energy harvesting technologies used in wireless sensors include photovoltaic, thermoelectric, piezoelectric, and electromagnetic.
6. The selection of the energy harvesting technology depends on the specific application and the availability of the energy source.
7. Energy storage is an important aspect of energy harvesting wireless sensors, as it allows the sensor to continue functioning even when the energy source is not available.
8. Supercapacitors and rechargeable batteries are commonly used for energy storage in energy harvesting wireless sensors.
9. Energy management is also crucial in energy harvesting wireless sensors to ensure efficient use of the harvested energy.
10. Energy harvesting wireless sensors have the potential to revolutionize the way we collect and use data, enabling the development of smarter and more sustainable systems.
