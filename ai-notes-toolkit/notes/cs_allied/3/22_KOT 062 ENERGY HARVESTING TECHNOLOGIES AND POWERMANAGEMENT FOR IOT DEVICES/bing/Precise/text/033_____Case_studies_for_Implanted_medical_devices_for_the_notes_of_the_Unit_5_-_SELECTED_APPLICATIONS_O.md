### Case studies for Implanted medical devices

Implanted medical devices are electronic devices that are placed inside the human body to monitor, diagnose, or treat medical conditions. These devices require a power source to function, and energy harvesting systems are being developed to provide a sustainable and reliable power source for these devices.

1. **Implantable Energy Harvesters (IEHs)**: These devices harvest energy from the human body, such as heartbeat, respiration, and chemical energy from the redox reaction of glucose, to power implantable medical electronics.

2. **Ultrasound-driven Implantable Wireless Energy Harvesting System**: This device is designed to be implanted in the subcutaneous tissue of the human body to collect mechanical energy carried through ultrasound that is transmitted through the skin. This energy can be used to power implanted devices such as pacemakers, neurostimulators, vascular robots, implantable chips, or sensors.

3. **Self-Powered Implantable Medical Devices**: There are a variety of approaches for harvesting energy from the subcutaneous environment using photovoltaic (PV) cells, radio frequency (RF) harvesters, piezoelectric generators (PEGs), thermal electric generators (TEGs), biofuel cells (BC), as well as other hybrid energy harvesting techniques.

These are just a few examples of the many case studies and developments in the field of energy harvesting systems for implanted medical devices. These systems have the potential to provide a sustainable and reliable power source for these devices, improving their functionality and longevity.
