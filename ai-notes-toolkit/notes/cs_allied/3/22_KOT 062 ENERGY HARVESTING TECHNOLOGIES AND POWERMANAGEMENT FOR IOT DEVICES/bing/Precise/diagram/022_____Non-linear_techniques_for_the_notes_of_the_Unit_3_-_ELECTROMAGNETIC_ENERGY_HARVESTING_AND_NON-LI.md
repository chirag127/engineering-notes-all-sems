### Non-linear techniques for electromagnetic energy harvesting

Non-linear techniques are used to optimize the harvested energy from electromagnetic generators. These techniques can be used to increase the output voltage of an electromagnetic energy harvester (EMEH) . A power management circuit (PMC) can be designed based on the model of a nonlinear interface circuit derived from the EMEH. The proposed PMC uses a buck–boost direct current-direct current (DC–DC) converter to match the load resistance of the nonlinear interface circuit.

One example of a nonlinear electromagnetic energy harvester is a small magnet moving in an isolating tube surrounded by a coil attached to an electrical circuit. In the nonlinear case, the magnet vibrates between two fixed magnets attached to both ends of the tube. Additionally, two springs are used to limit the movement of the small magnet.

A semi-analytical, non-linear model can be introduced to enable accurate and efficient analysis of energy transduction. This model can predict the transient and steady-state response of the harvester.

In summary, non-linear techniques can be used to optimize the harvested energy from electromagnetic generators, increase the output voltage of an EMEH, and design a power management circuit to match the load resistance of the nonlinear interface circuit. These techniques can be applied to various types of nonlinear electromagnetic energy harvesters.
