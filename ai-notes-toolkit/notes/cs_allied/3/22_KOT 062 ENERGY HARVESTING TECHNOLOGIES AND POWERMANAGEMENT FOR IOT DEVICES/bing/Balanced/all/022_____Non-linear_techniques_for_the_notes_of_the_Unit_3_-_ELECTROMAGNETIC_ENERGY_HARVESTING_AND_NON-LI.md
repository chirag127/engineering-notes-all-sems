# Non-linear techniques for electromagnetic energy harvesting

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting (EMEH) is a technique that converts ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using a coil and a magnet.
- Non-linear techniques are used to optimize the harvested energy from EMEH by exploiting the non-linear dynamics of the system, such as bistability, snap-through, or magnetic levitation.
- Non-linear techniques can enhance the bandwidth, output voltage, and power density of EMEH, as well as reduce the dependency on the frequency and amplitude of the vibrations.
- Some examples of non-linear techniques for EMEH are:

  - Non-linear interface circuit: A circuit that uses a diode bridge and a capacitor to rectify and store the alternating current (AC) generated by the EMEH. The circuit has a non-linear voltage-current relationship that can match the load resistance to the optimal value for maximum power transfer .
  - Non-linear magnetic spring: A spring that consists of a small magnet moving in a tube surrounded by a coil and two fixed magnets at the ends. The magnetic force between the magnets creates a non-linear restoring force that can induce large displacements and high voltages in the coil .
  - Non-linear multi-stable harvester: A harvester that has multiple stable equilibrium positions due to the non-linear magnetic force between the coil and the magnet. The harvester can snap from one position to another under external excitations, generating high voltage spikes in the coil.
