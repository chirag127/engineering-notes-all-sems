# Photovoltaic Cell Technologies

- Photovoltaic (PV) cells are devices that absorb energy from sunlight and convert it into electrical energy through semiconducting materials .
- PV cells are based on the photovoltaic effect, which is the process of generating voltage and electric current in a material upon exposure to light.
- PV cells are usually made of different semiconductor materials, such as silicon, cadmium telluride, copper indium gallium selenide, etc.
- PV cells are connected together in chains to form larger power-generating units known as modules or panels .
- PV modules or panels can be installed on rooftops, ground-mounted systems, or integrated into building materials or vehicles.
- PV technologies can be classified into three main categories: crystalline silicon (c-Si), thin-film, and emerging technologies.
- Crystalline silicon (c-Si) is the most widely used PV technology, accounting for about 90% of the global PV market. It has high efficiency, durability, and reliability, but also high manufacturing cost and energy consumption.
- Thin-film PV technologies use thin layers of semiconducting materials deposited on a substrate, such as glass, metal, or plastic. They have lower efficiency, but also lower cost, weight, and material usage than c-Si. They can also be flexible and adaptable to different surfaces and applications.
- Emerging PV technologies include perovskite, organic, quantum dot, and dye-sensitized solar cells, which are still in the research and development stage. They have the potential to achieve higher efficiency, lower cost, and novel functionalities, such as transparency, color, and tunability.
