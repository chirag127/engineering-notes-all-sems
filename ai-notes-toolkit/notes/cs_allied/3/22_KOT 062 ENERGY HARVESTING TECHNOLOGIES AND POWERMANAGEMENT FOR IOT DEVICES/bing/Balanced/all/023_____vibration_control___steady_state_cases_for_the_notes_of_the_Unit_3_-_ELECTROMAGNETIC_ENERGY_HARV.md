# Vibration Control and Steady State Cases for Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is the concept of converting vibration energy into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction.
- Electromagnetic energy harvesters consist of a coil and a magnet that move relative to each other under external vibrations, generating an induced voltage across the coil terminals.
- Vibration control is the process of reducing or eliminating unwanted vibrations in a system, such as a structure, a machine, or a device.
- Vibration control can be achieved by passive or active methods, or a combination of both.
- Passive vibration control methods use mechanical elements, such as springs, dampers, or isolators, to absorb or isolate the vibration energy.
- Active vibration control methods use sensors, actuators, and controllers to measure, cancel, or modify the vibration signals.
- Vibration control and energy harvesting can be integrated in a single system, such as a Stewart platform, a GMM energy harvesting device, or a tri-stable vibration energy harvester  .
- The integration of vibration control and energy harvesting can provide benefits such as improved performance, reduced weight, and increased functionality  .
- The steady state cases for electromagnetic energy harvesting are the conditions where the vibration amplitude, frequency, and phase are constant or periodic.
- The steady state cases can be analyzed using linear or nonlinear models, depending on the characteristics of the system and the vibration source.
- The steady state cases can be used to evaluate the output power, efficiency, and bandwidth of the electromagnetic energy harvester.
