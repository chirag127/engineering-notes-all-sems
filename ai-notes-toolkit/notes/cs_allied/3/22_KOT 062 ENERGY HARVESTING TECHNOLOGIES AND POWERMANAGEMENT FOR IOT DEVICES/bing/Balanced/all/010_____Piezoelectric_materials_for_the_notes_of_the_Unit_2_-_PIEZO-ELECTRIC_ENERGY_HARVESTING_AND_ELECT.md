# Piezoelectric materials

- Piezoelectric materials are materials that can convert mechanical stress into electrical charge, or vice versa. This phenomenon is called the piezoelectric effect .
- Piezoelectric materials can be broadly classified into the following categories:
  - Crystalline materials, such as quartz, SiO2, which is the best known natural piezoelectric material.
  - Ceramic materials, such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT), barium titanate, and lead titanate, which are the most commonly produced and widely used synthetic piezoelectric materials . They have high piezoelectric coefficients and can be easily shaped and polarized.
  - Semiconductor materials, such as zinc oxide (ZnO) and gallium nitride (GaN), which can also be regarded as ceramics due to their relatively wide band gaps . They have high piezoelectric coefficients and can be used for high-frequency applications.
  - Polymer materials, such as polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) and its copolymers, which are flexible, lightweight, and biocompatible piezoelectric materials. They have low piezoelectric coefficients and can be used for low-frequency applications.
  - Composite materials, which are combinations of different piezoelectric materials, such as PZT and PVDF, to achieve improved properties and performance. They can be tailored to specific applications and have various geometries and configurations.
  - Glass-ceramic materials, such as lithium disilicate (Li2Si2O5) and barium titanate silicate (Ba2TiSiO6), which are polycrystalline materials with a glassy phase and a crystalline phase. They have moderate piezoelectric coefficients and can be used for high-temperature applications.
- Piezoelectric materials have useful applications in many industries, such as  :
  - Sensors and actuators, such as pressure sensors, accelerometers, microphones, ultrasonic transducers, and piezoelectric motors.
  - Energy harvesting and storage, such as piezoelectric generators, batteries, and capacitors, which can convert ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy, or store electrical energy for later use.
  - Medical and biological devices, such as piezoelectric implants, drug delivery systems, biosensors, and tissue engineering scaffolds, which can interact with the human body and provide diagnostic or therapeutic functions.
  - Information and communication technologies, such as piezoelectric displays, speakers, printers, scanners, and optical modulators, which can process and transmit signals and data.
  - Industrial and environmental applications, such as piezoelectric igniters, cleaners, welders, mixers, and filters, which can provide mechanical or electrical functions for various processes and systems.
