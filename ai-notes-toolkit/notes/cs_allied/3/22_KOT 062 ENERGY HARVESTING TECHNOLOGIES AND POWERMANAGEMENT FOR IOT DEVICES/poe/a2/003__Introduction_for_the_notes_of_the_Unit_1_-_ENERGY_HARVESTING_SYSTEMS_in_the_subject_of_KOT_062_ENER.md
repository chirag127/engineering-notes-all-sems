### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

- Energy harvesting is the process by which energy is derived from external sources (e.g. solar power, thermal, wind energy, salinity gradients, and kinetic energy etc.), captured and stored for small, wireless autonomous devices, like those used in wearable electronics and wireless sensor networks.
- The energy harvesting technologies are increasingly being utilized to power the Internet of Things (IoT) devices and more generally low-power electronics to enable their wireless, portable and self-sustaining operation.
- The energy harvesting technologies can eliminate the need for batteries in low-power electronics and reduce the maintenance costs of replacing or recharging batteries. They are eco-friendly and sustainable solutions for powering the increasing number of IoT devices and wireless sensor networks.
- The most common energy sources for energy harvesting are:
    - Solar energy using photovoltaic cells
    - Kinetic energy using piezoelectric, electromagnetic or electrostatic transducers
    - Thermal energy using thermoelectric generators
    - Radio frequency energy using rectenna
- The harvested energy is often stored in energy storage elements like rechargeable batteries or supercapacitors before it is used to power the devices.
- The key characteristics of energy harvesting systems are: low power consumption of devices, efficient energy transducers, maximum power extraction from the ambient energy source and efficient energy storage.
