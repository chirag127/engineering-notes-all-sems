### Powering Wireless SHM Sensor Nodes

* Wireless SHM (Structural Health Monitoring) sensor nodes are deployed on infrastructure to monitor structural integrity.
* These sensor nodes are typically battery-powered and expected to last for years.
* Due to limited power capacity of batteries, energy harvesting is essential to enable perpetual operation of these wireless SHM sensor nodes.
* Some potential energy sources for harvesting to power wireless SHM sensor nodes:
    * Vibrations - From machinery, vehicles, wind, etc.
    * Solar - Outdoor deployments in sunlight
    * Thermal - Temperature gradients
    * RF - Ambient RF signals
* Challenges in using energy harvesting for wireless SHM sensor nodes:
    * Intermittent and variable nature of energy sources - Energy harvesting system must handle energy fluctuations.
    * Multiple energy sources may be present - System must handle multiple inputs and optimize use of available energy.
    * Capacity of energy storage elements - Determined by size, cost, and power requirements of sensor node.
* Approaches to address challenges:
    * Energy management systems with MPPT and battery charging circuits tuned to application
    * Use of supercapacitors in addition to or instead of batteries to handle intermittent high-power pulses
    * Low-power components and optimized efficiency of sensor node components
    * Wirelessly transmitting only essential data to minimize power use for wireless communication
