### Microgenerators for the notes of the Unit 2 - PIEZO-ELECTRIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND ELECTROMECHANICAL MODELING

1. Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting
- Piezoelectric materials produce charge when subjected to mechanical stress
- The direct piezoelectric effect is used for energy harvesting
- Piezoelectric harvesters convert ambient vibrations into electrical energy
- Commonly used piezoelectric materials: Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT), Polyvinylidene Fluoride (PVDF)
- Advantages: high energy density, customizable for different vibration frequencies
- Disadvantages: limited bandwidth, can be fragile

2. Electromechanical Modeling
- The dynamic response of a piezoelectric energy harvester can be modeled using coupled electromechanical equations
- The coupled equations represent the electrical and mechanical domains and their interactions
- Key parameters: mass, stiffness, damping, load resistance, piezoelectric coefficients
- The coupled equations can be solved to determine the output power and other performance metrics
- The model can be used to optimize the harvester design for maximum power output

3. Applications
- Piezoelectric energy harvesters are used to power wireless sensors and low-power IoT devices
- They can harvest energy from ambient vibrations in the environment, enabling self-powered devices
- Possible vibration sources include: machinery, human motion, wind, water waves, building vibrations
- The harvested energy can be stored in batteries or supercapacitors until needed
- Piezoelectric harvesters are a promising solution for powering devices in remote, inaccessible locations where battery replacement is difficult.
