### Harvesters

In this unit, we will be discussing piezo-electric energy harvesting and electromechanical modeling. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Piezo-electric energy harvesting is the process of converting mechanical energy into electrical energy using piezoelectric materials.
- Piezoelectric materials generate an electrical charge when subjected to mechanical stress, such as pressure or vibration.
- Electromechanical modeling is the process of creating mathematical models to predict the behavior of piezoelectric materials and devices.
- These models can help engineers design more efficient and effective energy harvesting devices.
- There are several different types of piezoelectric energy harvesting devices, including cantilever beams, bimorphs, and unimorphs.
- Cantilever beams are used for low-frequency applications, while bimorphs and unimorphs are better suited for higher frequencies.
- The output voltage and power of a piezoelectric energy harvesting device depend on several factors, including the mechanical properties of the piezoelectric material and the design of the device.
- In order to maximize the output of a piezoelectric energy harvester, it is important to carefully select the piezoelectric material and optimize the design of the device.
- Electromechanical modeling can help identify the best piezoelectric material and device design for a given application, as well as predict the output voltage and power of the device.
- The efficiency of a piezoelectric energy harvesting device can be improved by using multiple devices in parallel or series, or by using a resonant circuit to match the impedance of the device to the load.
- Piezoelectric energy harvesting has several potential applications, including powering wireless sensor networks and wearable devices, as well as harvesting energy from sources such as ambient vibrations and human motion.
