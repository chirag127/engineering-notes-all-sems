

### Case Studies for Implanted Medical Devices

1. **Pacemakers:** Pacemakers are implanted devices that use electrical pulses to regulate the heart rate of patients with arrhythmia. They require a reliable and long-term energy source, which is usually provided by a battery. Recent advances in energy harvesting technologies have enabled pacemakers to be powered by energy harvested from the body's motion, temperature, and other sources.

2. **Cochlear Implants:** Cochlear implants are devices that can restore hearing to deaf people. They consist of an external microphone and a receiver-stimulator implanted into the skull. The receiver-stimulator is powered by an internal power source, which is usually provided by a battery. Energy harvesting technologies can be used to power these implants and reduce the need for frequent battery replacement.

3. **Retinal Implants:** Retinal implants are devices that can restore vision to people with retinal degeneration. They consist of an external camera and a receiver-stimulator implanted into the eye. The receiver-stimulator is powered by an internal power source, which is usually provided by a battery. Energy harvesting technologies can be used to power these implants and reduce the need for frequent battery replacement.

4. **Neurostimulators:** Neurostimulators are devices that can provide electrical stimulation to the brain to treat various neurological disorders. They consist of an external controller and a receiver-stimulator implanted into the skull. The receiver-stimulator is powered by an internal power source, which is usually provided by a battery. Energy harvesting technologies can be used to power these implants and reduce the need for frequent battery replacement.
