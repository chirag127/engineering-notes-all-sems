
## Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS

1. Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that collect energy from the environment and convert it into electrical power.
2. They are often used in remote or difficult-to-access locations, where traditional power sources are not available.
3. Energy harvesting wireless sensors can collect energy from a variety of sources, such as solar, wind, vibration, thermal, and radio frequency.
4. These sensors are typically self-contained, with no need for external power sources.
5. The energy collected by the sensor is then used to power the sensor’s electronics, as well as any other connected devices.
6. The energy harvesting wireless sensor can be used for a variety of applications, such as monitoring environmental conditions, tracking objects, and providing remote control.
7. The technology is also being used in medical devices, such as pacemakers and hearing aids, to enable them to run without the need for batteries.
8. Energy harvesting wireless sensors are becoming increasingly popular due to their low cost, long life span, and ease of installation.
