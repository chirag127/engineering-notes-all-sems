### Introduction

In this unit, we will be studying energy harvesting systems, which are becoming increasingly important in the world of IoT devices. Energy harvesting systems enable devices to operate using renewable energy sources, thus reducing their dependence on batteries and other non-renewable sources of energy. In this way, energy harvesting systems can help to reduce the environmental impact of IoT devices and make them more sustainable.

Here are some key points to keep in mind as we delve into this topic:

- Energy harvesting systems are designed to extract energy from the environment and convert it into usable electrical power.
- The most common sources of energy for harvesting systems include solar, thermal, kinetic, and electromagnetic energy.
- Energy harvesting systems typically consist of three main components: an energy source, an energy converter, and an energy storage device.
- The energy source provides the initial energy input, which is then converted into electrical power by the energy converter.
- The energy storage device is used to store the electrical power generated by the energy converter, which can then be used to power the IoT device.
- There are a number of different types of energy converters that can be used in energy harvesting systems, including solar cells, thermoelectric generators, piezoelectric generators, and electromagnetic generators.
- The choice of energy converter will depend on a number of factors, including the type of energy source being used, the amount of power required, and the size and weight constraints of the IoT device.
- In addition to energy harvesting systems, there are a number of other techniques that can be used to reduce the energy consumption of IoT devices, including power management techniques and energy-efficient hardware and software design.
- Overall, the development of energy harvesting systems and other energy-saving technologies is essential for making IoT devices more sustainable and reducing their impact on the environment.
