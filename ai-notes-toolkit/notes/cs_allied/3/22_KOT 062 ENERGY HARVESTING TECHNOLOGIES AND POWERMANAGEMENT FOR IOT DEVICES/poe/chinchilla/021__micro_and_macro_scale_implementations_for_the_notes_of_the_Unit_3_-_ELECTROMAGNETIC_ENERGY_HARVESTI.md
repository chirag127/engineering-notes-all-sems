### Micro and Macro Scale Implementations for Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting and Non-Linear Techniques

In the field of energy harvesting, there are various techniques used to generate power from the surrounding environment. One such technique is electromagnetic energy harvesting, which involves converting the energy from electromagnetic waves into usable electrical power. This technique has both micro and macro scale implementations, which are discussed below:

#### Micro Scale Implementations

1. Micro-scale electromagnetic energy harvesting involves harvesting energy from small electromagnetic sources, such as radio waves. It is commonly used in low-power electronic devices, such as sensors, where the energy requirements are low.

2. The micro-scale implementation of electromagnetic energy harvesting involves the use of a small antenna, typically in the range of a few millimeters, that captures the electromagnetic energy. The energy captured is then rectified and stored in a capacitor or battery for later use.

3. The efficiency of micro-scale electromagnetic energy harvesting is typically low, ranging from a few percent to around 30%. However, as the energy requirements of low-power electronic devices are also low, the harvested energy is usually sufficient to power these devices.

#### Macro Scale Implementations

1. Macro-scale electromagnetic energy harvesting involves harvesting energy from larger electromagnetic sources, such as Wi-Fi signals, cell phone towers, and other wireless communication networks. It is commonly used in applications where the energy requirements are higher, such as in powering small electronic devices or charging batteries.

2. The macro-scale implementation of electromagnetic energy harvesting involves the use of a larger antenna, typically in the range of a few centimeters to a few meters, that captures the electromagnetic energy. The energy captured is then rectified and stored in a capacitor or battery for later use.

3. The efficiency of macro-scale electromagnetic energy harvesting is typically higher than that of micro-scale implementations, ranging from a few percent to around 50%. However, the harvested energy is still limited, and it may not be sufficient to power larger electronic devices.

4. Non-linear techniques, such as rectenna and Schottky diodes, can be used to improve the efficiency of macro-scale electromagnetic energy harvesting. These techniques involve using non-linear elements in the energy harvesting circuit to improve the rectification efficiency and reduce losses.

In conclusion, electromagnetic energy harvesting is a promising technique for generating power for low-power electronic devices. Both micro and macro-scale implementations have their advantages and limitations, and the choice of implementation depends on the specific application requirements. Non-linear techniques can be used to improve the efficiency of macro-scale implementations, making them more suitable for powering larger electronic devices.
