### History of Wearable Computing

- Wearable computing is the field of technology that involves devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, glasses, headsets, rings, etc.
- The concept of wearable computing can be traced back to ancient times, when people used devices such as abacuses, rings, and necklaces to perform calculations or store information.
- The modern era of wearable computing began in the late 1970s, when Steve Mann, a professor at the University of Toronto, invented the first wearable computer that could record and process video and audio signals .
- In the 1980s and 1990s, wearable computing research and development expanded to various domains, such as military, medical, entertainment, and education. Some examples of wearable devices from this period are:
  - The Trekker, a head-mounted display and speech recognition system for blind people, developed by Thad Starner and others at MIT .
  - The AiRScouter, a retinal scanning display that projects images directly onto the user's retina, developed by Brother Industries.
  - The Zypad, a wrist-mounted computer that can run various operating systems and applications, developed by Eurotech.
- In the 2000s and 2010s, wearable computing became more mainstream and accessible, thanks to the advances in miniaturization, wireless communication, and user interface design. Some examples of wearable devices from this period are:
  - The Fitbit, a fitness tracker that can monitor the user's physical activity, heart rate, sleep quality, and calories burned, developed by Fitbit Inc.
  - The Nike+, a system that can measure the user's running distance, speed, and performance, using a sensor in the shoe and an app on the smartphone, developed by Nike and Apple.
  - The Looxie, a wearable camera that can capture and share photos and videos, developed by Looxie Inc.
- The current and future trends of wearable computing include:
  - The integration of wearable devices with other technologies, such as cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and the Internet of Things, to create more personalized, context-aware, and intelligent services and applications.
  - The development of new forms and functions of wearable devices, such as smart clothing, jewelry, tattoos, implants, and contact lenses, to enhance the user's appearance, health, and capabilities.
  - The exploration of the social, ethical, and legal implications of wearable computing, such as privacy, security, identity, and human-computer interaction, to ensure the responsible and beneficial use of wearable technology.
