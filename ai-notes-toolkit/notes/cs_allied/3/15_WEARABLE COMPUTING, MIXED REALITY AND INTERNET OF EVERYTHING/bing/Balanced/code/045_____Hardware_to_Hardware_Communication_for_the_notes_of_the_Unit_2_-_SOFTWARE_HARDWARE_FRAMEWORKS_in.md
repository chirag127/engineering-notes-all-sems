### Hardware to Hardware Communication for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Hardware to hardware communication refers to the process of exchanging data between different devices or components of a wearable computing system.
- Hardware to hardware communication can be achieved by using various methods, such as wired or wireless connections, serial or parallel ports, interrupt requests, or bus architectures.
- Some examples of hardware to hardware communication in wearable computing are:

  - A head mounted display (HMD) that receives video signals from a belt or backpack PC via a wireless link.
  - A one-handed chordic keyboard that sends keystrokes to a wearable computer via a serial port.
  - A sensor that triggers an interrupt request (IRQ) to the CPU when it detects a change in the environment.
  - A personal wireless local area network (PWL) that connects multiple wearable devices and allows data sharing and synchronization.
  - A smart wearable device that adapts its processing speed and power consumption according to the available resources and the user's context.

- Hardware to hardware communication is essential for wearable computing because it enables:

  - High performance and functionality of the wearable system by allowing parallel and distributed processing of data and tasks.
  - Low power consumption and longer battery life of the wearable devices by reducing the need for data transmission and storage.
  - User convenience and comfort by minimizing the size and weight of the wearable components and providing unobtrusive and natural interfaces.
  - Context awareness and personalization of the wearable system by sensing and responding to the user's needs and preferences.
  - Interoperability and compatibility of the wearable devices with other systems and platforms by following common standards and protocols.
