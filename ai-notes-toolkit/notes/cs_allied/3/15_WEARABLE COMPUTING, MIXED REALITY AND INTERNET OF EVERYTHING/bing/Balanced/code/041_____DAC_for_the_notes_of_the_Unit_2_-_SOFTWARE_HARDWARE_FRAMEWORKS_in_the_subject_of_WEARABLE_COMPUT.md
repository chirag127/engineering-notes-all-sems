- DAC stands for **Digital-to-Analog Converter**, a device that converts digital audio signals into analog audio signals that can be output to speakers, headphones or other analog devices .
- DACs are essential for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, as they enable the communication and interaction between digital devices and human senses, such as hearing and touch .
- DACs can be classified into different types based on their architecture, performance, features and applications, such as:
  - **USB DACs**: DACs that connect to a computer or a mobile device via a USB port and act as an external sound card, bypassing the internal DAC of the device . USB DACs can offer better sound quality, portability and compatibility than the built-in DACs of some devices .
  - **Portable DACs**: DACs that are battery-powered and can be carried around with a mobile device or a portable music player, providing high-quality audio output on the go . Portable DACs can also include headphone amplifiers, which boost the signal and drive the headphones more effectively .
  - **Desktop DACs**: DACs that are designed to be used at home or in the office, usually connected to a computer, a CD player, a streamer or a turntable, and outputting to a pair of speakers or headphones  . Desktop DACs can offer more features, such as volume control, multiple inputs and outputs, wireless connectivity and digital filters  .
  - **DAC HATs**: DACs that are designed to be used with Raspberry Pi, a single-board computer that can be used for various projects, such as data acquisition, internet of things, robotics and gaming. DAC HATs are attached to the GPIO pins of the Raspberry Pi and provide analog output channels, digital input/output channels, analog input channels and temperature sensors.
- DACs can be evaluated based on their specifications, such as:
  - **Resolution**: The number of bits used to represent each sample of the digital audio signal, which determines the dynamic range and the signal-to-noise ratio of the analog output . Higher resolution means more accurate and detailed sound reproduction, but also more data and processing power required . Common resolutions are 16-bit, 24-bit and 32-bit .
  - **Sampling rate**: The number of times per second that the digital audio signal is sampled, which determines the frequency range and the bandwidth of the analog output . Higher sampling rate means more information and higher fidelity, but also more data and processing power required . Common sampling rates are 44.1 kHz, 48 kHz, 96 kHz and 192 kHz .
  - **DSD**: Direct Stream Digital, a format of digital audio that uses a single bit and a very high sampling rate (usually 2.8 MHz or 5.6 MHz) to encode the audio signal, which is different from the conventional PCM (Pulse Code Modulation) format that uses multiple bits and a lower sampling rate . DSD is claimed to offer more natural and realistic sound quality, but also more data and processing power required . Not all DACs support DSD format .
  - **MQA**: Master Quality Authenticated, a format of digital audio that uses a proprietary compression and encoding technique to deliver high-resolution audio in a smaller file size, which is suitable for streaming services and portable devices . MQA is claimed to offer the exact sound of the original master recording, but also requires a compatible DAC and a subscription to a streaming service that supports MQA . Not all DACs support MQA format .
