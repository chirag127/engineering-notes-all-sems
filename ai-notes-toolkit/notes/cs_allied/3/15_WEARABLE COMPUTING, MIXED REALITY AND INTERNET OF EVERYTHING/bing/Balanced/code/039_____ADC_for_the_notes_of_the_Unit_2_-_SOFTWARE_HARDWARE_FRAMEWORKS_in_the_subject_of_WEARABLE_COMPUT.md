### ADC

- ADC stands for Analog to Digital Converter. It is a device that converts analog signals, such as sound, light, temperature, etc., into digital signals that can be processed by a computer or a microcontroller.
- ADCs are essential components of many wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, as they enable the interaction between the physical world and the digital world.
- ADCs can have different characteristics, such as resolution, sampling rate, accuracy, power consumption, etc., depending on the requirements of the application and the hardware platform.
- Resolution is the number of bits that the ADC uses to represent the analog signal. Higher resolution means more precision, but also more memory and processing power needed.
- Sampling rate is the frequency at which the ADC captures the analog signal. Higher sampling rate means more information, but also more bandwidth and power consumption needed.
- Accuracy is the degree of conformity between the digital output of the ADC and the actual analog input. Accuracy can be affected by various factors, such as noise, distortion, offset, etc.
- Power consumption is the amount of energy that the ADC uses to perform the conversion. Lower power consumption means longer battery life, but also lower performance and quality.
