# Smart Cities and Wearable Computing

- Smart cities are urban environments that use information and communication technologies (ICT) to enhance the quality of life, efficiency of services, and sustainability of resources for their inhabitants .
- Wearable computing is a form of human-computer interaction that involves devices worn on or attached to the body, such as smart watches, glasses, clothing, or implants, that can sense, process, and communicate data .
- Smart cities and wearable computing are related in several ways, such as:
  - Wearable devices can collect and transmit data about the user's health, location, activity, and preferences, which can be used by smart city applications to provide personalized and context-aware services, such as health monitoring, navigation, transportation, or entertainment .
  - Wearable devices can also receive and display information from smart city infrastructure, such as sensors, cameras, or networks, which can enhance the user's awareness, safety, and convenience in the urban space .
  - Wearable devices can enable new forms of social interaction and civic engagement in smart cities, such as participatory sensing, crowdsourcing, or gamification, which can foster collaboration, innovation, and empowerment among citizens .
- Smart cities and wearable computing can also pose some challenges, such as:
  - Wearable devices can generate large amounts of data, which require efficient and secure storage, processing, and transmission in smart city networks, as well as appropriate data governance and privacy policies to protect the user's rights and interests .
  - Wearable devices can also create new risks and vulnerabilities for the user, such as cyberattacks, hacking, or malfunction, which can compromise the user's safety, security, and trust in smart city systems .
  - Wearable devices can also have social and ethical implications, such as digital divide, discrimination, or surveillance, which can affect the user's identity, autonomy, and dignity in smart city environments .
- Smart cities and wearable computing can be seen as a form of urban design that aims to create more human-centric, inclusive, and resilient cities, by leveraging the potential of ICT to enhance the interaction and integration of people, places, and processes .
