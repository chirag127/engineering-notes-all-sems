# Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

## Wearable Computing

- Wearable computing is the use of devices that can be worn on the body to perform various functions such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, or interacting with the environment or other devices.
- Wearable devices can be classified into different categories based on their form factor, functionality, and application domain. Some examples are smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, head-mounted displays, smart clothing, smart jewelry, and implantable devices.
- Wearable computing can provide various benefits such as convenience, personalization, health monitoring, entertainment, education, and social interaction.
- Wearable computing also poses some challenges such as privacy, security, usability, battery life, compatibility, and social acceptance.

## Mixed Reality

- Mixed reality (MR) is a type of immersive technology that combines elements of virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) to create a hybrid reality where physical and digital objects coexist and interact in real time.
- MR can be achieved by using devices such as head-mounted displays, smart glasses, or projection systems that overlay digital information onto the physical environment or vice versa.
- MR can enable various applications such as gaming, education, training, entertainment, tourism, art, and collaboration.
- MR also faces some issues such as technical limitations, user comfort, content creation, and ethical implications.

## Internet of Everything

- Internet of Everything (IoE) is a concept that extends the Internet of Things (IoT) by incorporating not only things, but also people, data, and processes into a network of connections that provide general intelligence and improved cognition across the networked environment.
- IoE can be enabled by technologies such as cloud computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, machine learning, and blockchain that allow the collection, processing, and sharing of large amounts of data from various sources and devices.
- IoE can offer various opportunities such as smart cities, smart homes, smart healthcare, smart agriculture, smart transportation, and smart industry.
- IoE also presents some challenges such as security, privacy, interoperability, scalability, and governance.
