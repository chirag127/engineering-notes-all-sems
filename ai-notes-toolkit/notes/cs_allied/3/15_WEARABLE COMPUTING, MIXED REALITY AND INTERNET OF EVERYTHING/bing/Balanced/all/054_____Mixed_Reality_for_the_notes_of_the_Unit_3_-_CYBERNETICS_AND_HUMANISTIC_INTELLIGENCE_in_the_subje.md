# Mixed Reality

- Mixed reality is a medium consisting of immersive computer-generated environments in which elements of a physical and virtual environment are combined.
- Mixed reality is a blend of physical and digital worlds, unlocking natural and intuitive 3D human, computer, and environmental interactions.
- Mixed reality is the next wave in computing followed by mainframes, PCs, and smartphones. Mixed reality is going mainstream for consumers and businesses. It liberates us from screen-bound experiences by offering instinctual interactions with data in our living spaces and with our friends.
- Mixed reality that incorporates haptics has sometimes been referred to as Visuo-haptic mixed reality.
- Mixed reality can be classified into two types: augmented reality and virtual reality.
  - Augmented reality (AR) is a type of mixed reality that enhances the real-world environment with computer-generated information or objects. AR can be experienced through devices such as smartphones, tablets, glasses, or headsets. AR can be used for various purposes such as education, entertainment, navigation, gaming, and social media.
  - Virtual reality (VR) is a type of mixed reality that creates a fully immersive and simulated environment that replaces the real-world environment. VR can be experienced through devices such as headsets, gloves, or suits. VR can be used for various purposes such as training, simulation, therapy, gaming, and art.
- Mixed reality can be measured by two dimensions: the virtuality continuum and the reality-virtuality continuum.
  - The virtuality continuum is a scale that measures the degree of realism of the computer-generated environment. It ranges from photorealistic to non-realistic. Photorealistic environments are those that mimic the real world in terms of appearance, behavior, and physics. Non-realistic environments are those that deviate from the real world in terms of style, logic, or fantasy.
  - The reality-virtuality continuum is a scale that measures the degree of immersion of the user in the mixed reality environment. It ranges from fully immersive to non-immersive. Fully immersive environments are those that block out the real world and provide a sense of presence and agency to the user. Non-immersive environments are those that allow the user to perceive and interact with the real world while experiencing the mixed reality environment.
- Mixed reality can be enabled by various technologies such as sensors, cameras, displays, processors, networks, and software.
  - Sensors are devices that detect and measure physical phenomena such as motion, orientation, position, light, sound, temperature, and pressure. Sensors can be used to track the user's movements, gestures, eye gaze, and facial expressions, as well as the environmental conditions and changes.
  - Cameras are devices that capture and transmit images or videos of the real world or the mixed reality environment. Cameras can be used to recognize objects, faces, scenes, and gestures, as well as to provide feedback and guidance to the user.
  - Displays are devices that present the mixed reality environment to the user's senses such as vision, hearing, touch, and smell. Displays can be classified into three types: head-mounted, handheld, and spatial. Head-mounted displays are those that are worn on the user's head and provide a stereoscopic view of the mixed reality environment. Handheld displays are those that are held by the user's hand and provide a monoscopic view of the mixed reality environment. Spatial displays are those that are projected or embedded in the physical environment and provide a shared view of the mixed reality environment.
  - Processors are devices that perform computations and operations on the data and information from the sensors, cameras, and displays. Processors can be classified into two types: local and remote. Local processors are those that are embedded in the mixed reality devices and provide fast and low-latency processing. Remote processors are those that are located in the cloud or the edge and provide high-performance and scalable processing.
  - Networks are devices that connect the mixed reality devices and processors and enable data and information exchange. Networks can be classified into two types: wired and wireless. Wired networks are those that use physical cables or wires to transmit data and information. Wireless networks are those that use radio waves or optical signals to transmit data and information.
  - Software are programs or applications that run on the mixed reality devices and processors and provide the functionality and logic of the mixed reality environment. Software can be classified into two types: system and application. System software are those that manage the hardware and software resources and provide the basic services and interfaces for the mixed reality environment. Application software are those that provide the specific features and functions for the mixed reality environment.
