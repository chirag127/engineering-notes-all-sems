# WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the use of devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, or virtual reality headsets, to provide various functions and services to the user .
- Mixed reality is the blending of the physical and digital worlds, where virtual objects can interact with real environments and vice versa, creating immersive and interactive experiences for the user .
- Internet of everything is the concept of connecting any device, object, or entity to the internet and to each other, enabling data collection, analysis, and communication across various domains and applications .

Some of the benefits and challenges of these technologies are:

- Benefits:
  - They can enhance the user's perception, cognition, and interaction with the environment and other people, providing new possibilities for education, entertainment, health, and socialization .
  - They can enable the user to access information, services, and resources anytime and anywhere, improving convenience, productivity, and efficiency .
  - They can support the creation of a user-generated, non-verbal language, allowing the user to express emotions, intentions, and preferences through light, sound, and vibration patterns on their wearable devices.
- Challenges:
  - They can raise ethical, legal, and social issues, such as privacy, security, trust, ownership, and regulation, as the user's data and activities are exposed to various stakeholders and platforms .
  - They can pose technical and design challenges, such as interoperability, compatibility, usability, and accessibility, as the user's devices and applications need to work seamlessly and intuitively across different contexts and scenarios .
  - They can affect the user's psychological and physiological well-being, such as attention, memory, mood, and stress, as the user's senses and cognition are constantly stimulated and influenced by the devices and applications .
