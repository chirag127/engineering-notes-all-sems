# Microcontrollers for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- A microcontroller is a small and low-cost computer that can execute a program and control devices such as sensors, LEDs, motors, etc.
- A microcontroller consists of a processor, memory, input/output ports, timers, and other peripherals integrated on a single chip.
- A microcontroller can be programmed using a high-level language such as C or Python, or a low-level language such as assembly or machine code.
- A microcontroller can be embedded in a wearable device, such as a smartwatch, a fitness tracker, a smart clothing, etc., to provide functionality, interactivity, and connectivity.
- A wearable device is a device that can be worn on the body or attached to the clothing, and that can sense, process, and communicate data.
- A wearable device can have various applications, such as health monitoring, fitness tracking, entertainment, gaming, education, etc.
- A wearable device can communicate with other devices, such as smartphones, computers, or cloud servers, using wireless technologies, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, etc.
- A wearable device can have a user interface, such as a display, a speaker, a microphone, a touch sensor, etc., to provide feedback and interaction to the user.
- A wearable device can have a power source, such as a battery, a solar cell, a kinetic generator, etc., to provide energy for the operation of the device.
- A wearable device can have a form factor, such as a watch, a bracelet, a ring, a necklace, a badge, etc., to suit the user's preference and comfort.

Some examples of microcontrollers that are suitable for wearable devices are:

- Arduino: Arduino is an open-source platform that consists of a hardware board and a software environment. Arduino boards are based on various microcontrollers, such as ATmega, SAMD, ESP32, etc. Arduino boards are easy to use, program, and connect with various sensors and actuators. Arduino boards can be sewn or soldered to the wearable device. Arduino boards have a large community of users and developers, and many tutorials and projects are available online. 
- FLORA: FLORA is a wearable electronic platform developed by Adafruit. FLORA is a round, sewable, Arduino-compatible microcontroller that can drive various wearable components, such as LEDs, sensors, GPS, etc. FLORA has a built-in USB port, a reset button, and an on/off switch. FLORA has a 3.3V logic level and can be powered by a 3.7V LiPo battery or a USB cable. FLORA comes with Adafruit's support, tutorials, and projects. 
- Microchip: Microchip is a company that produces various microcontrollers and microprocessors, such as PIC, AVR, SAM, etc. Microchip microcontrollers have low power consumption, small footprint, and high performance. Microchip microcontrollers can be used for wearable devices that require ultra-low active and static power consumption, small-form design, and consumer-grade user interface. Microchip microcontrollers can support various wireless technologies, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, etc. Microchip microcontrollers can be programmed using MPLAB X IDE, Arduino IDE, or other tools.
