### Sensors and Software for Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything

- Wearable computing is the use of electronic devices that can be worn on the body, either as accessories or as part of clothing, to perform various functions such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, and interacting with the environment.
- Mixed reality is the blending of the physical and digital worlds, where virtual and augmented reality elements can coexist and interact with each other in real time.
- Internet of Everything is the concept of connecting people, processes, data, and things through the internet, enabling new levels of intelligence, efficiency, and innovation.
- Sensors are devices that can detect and measure physical phenomena, such as light, temperature, motion, sound, pressure, etc., and convert them into electrical signals that can be processed by software.
- Software is the set of instructions and data that control the operation of hardware devices, such as sensors, microprocessors, and communication modules.
- Sensors and software are essential components of wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything, as they enable the collection, analysis, and transmission of data from the environment, the user, and other devices, and provide feedback and interaction capabilities to the user and other devices  .
- Some examples of sensors and software for wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything are:

  - Artificial intelligence (AI) hearing aids, which use sensors to detect the user's environment and adjust the sound quality and volume accordingly, and software to learn from the user's preferences and behavior.
  - Google Glass and Microsoft's HoloLens, which use sensors to capture the user's head movements and gestures, and software to project holographic images and information on the user's field of view.
  - Fitness trackers, which use sensors to monitor the user's heart rate, steps, calories, sleep, and other health indicators, and software to display and analyze the data on a smartphone app or a web dashboard.
  - Smart Sensoria® socks, which use sensors to measure the user's foot pressure, gait, and balance, and software to provide real-time feedback and alerts to the user, caregivers, and clinicians, and to store and visualize the data on a cloud platform.
  - Motus Smart, which uses sensors to monitor the user's fracture healing and range of motion, and software to provide personalized rehabilitation exercises and progress reports to the user, caregivers, and clinicians, and to store and visualize the data on a cloud platform.
  - Smart sensors with self-diagnostics and repair capabilities, which use sensors to detect and measure hazardous gas, fire, intruders, and other safety-critical events, and software to perform self-checks, self-calibration, and self-repair, and to communicate the data and alerts to other devices and systems.
