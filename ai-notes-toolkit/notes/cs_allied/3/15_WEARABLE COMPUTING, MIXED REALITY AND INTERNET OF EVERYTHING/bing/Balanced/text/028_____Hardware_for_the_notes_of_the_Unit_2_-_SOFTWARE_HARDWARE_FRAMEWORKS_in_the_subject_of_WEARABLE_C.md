### Hardware for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Hardware refers to the physical components of a computing system that enable the execution of software and the interaction with users and the environment.
- Hardware for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything (IoE) applications must meet certain requirements, such as:
  - Low power consumption and long battery life
  - Small size and weight
  - Wireless connectivity and data transmission
  - Sensing and actuating capabilities
  - User comfort and safety
  - Robustness and reliability
- Hardware for wearable computing, mixed reality and IoE applications can be classified into four categories, based on their functionality and location:
  - Body-worn devices: These are devices that are attached to or worn on the human body, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, head-mounted displays, earbuds, etc. They can provide various functions, such as biometric monitoring, notification, navigation, communication, entertainment, etc. They can also serve as input or output devices for mixed reality applications, such as augmented reality (AR) or virtual reality (VR).
  - Environmental devices: These are devices that are embedded in or attached to the physical environment, such as smart home appliances, sensors, cameras, speakers, etc. They can provide various functions, such as environmental monitoring, control, automation, security, etc. They can also serve as input or output devices for mixed reality applications, such as mixed reality (MR) or extended reality (XR).
  - Network devices: These are devices that enable the wireless communication and data transmission between the body-worn and environmental devices, such as routers, gateways, hubs, etc. They can provide various functions, such as network access, data aggregation, data processing, data storage, etc. They can also serve as intermediaries for mixed reality applications, such as cloud computing or edge computing.
  - Hybrid devices: These are devices that combine the features of two or more of the above categories, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, etc. They can provide various functions, such as computing, display, input, output, etc. They can also serve as bridges for mixed reality applications, such as mobile AR or VR.
