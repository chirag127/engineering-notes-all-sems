### Sketch for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the use of devices that are worn on the body or attached to clothing, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, and virtual reality headsets. These devices can collect and analyse data, communicate with other devices, and provide various services to the user.
- Mixed reality is a type of immersive technology that combines the physical and digital worlds, creating a hybrid environment where virtual and real objects coexist and interact. Mixed reality devices, such as Magic Leap One or HoloLens 2, can project digital content into the user's field of view, allowing them to manipulate and interact with it.
- Internet of everything (IoE) is a concept that extends the internet of things (IoT) by including not only devices and sensors, but also people, data, and processes. IoE aims to provide general intelligence and improved cognition across the networked environment, enabling new applications and opportunities in various domains.
- The main objectives of this unit are to:
  - Understand the basic concepts and definitions of wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything.
  - Learn about the history and evolution of these technologies and their current trends and challenges.
  - Explore the applications and use cases of these technologies in different domains, such as health, education, entertainment, and industry.
  - Analyse the benefits and drawbacks of these technologies for the users and the society, and the ethical and social implications they raise.
  - Compare and contrast the different types of wearable computing and mixed reality devices, and their features and functionalities.
  - Identify the main components and architectures of these technologies, and the standards and protocols they use.
  - Evaluate the performance and quality of these technologies, and the factors that affect them, such as usability, user experience, security, privacy, and reliability.
