### Communication for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Communication is the process of exchanging information and data between different devices, systems, and users.
- Communication in wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything involves the use of various technologies and protocols to enable seamless and ubiquitous interaction and collaboration across different domains and contexts.
- Some of the key aspects of communication in these fields are:

  - **Wearable communication space**: A wearable communication space is a virtual environment that allows multiple remote users to communicate with a wearable computer user through audio, video, and graphical cues. The wearable communication space can be body-stabilized, meaning that it moves with the user's head orientation, or world-stabilized, meaning that it is fixed in the physical world. The wearable communication space can also provide context-awareness, meaning that it adapts to the user's location, activity, and preferences.
  - **Internet of Things (IoT)**: IoT is the network of physical objects that are embedded with sensors, actuators, and communication capabilities that enable them to collect, process, and exchange data with other devices and systems over the internet. IoT can enable wearables and mixed reality devices to interact with other smart objects and environments, creating new possibilities for personalization, automation, and optimization. IoT can also enable data sharing and analytics, enhancing the user's experience and understanding of the world.
  - **Mixed reality (MR)**: MR is the blending of the real world and the virtual world, creating immersive experiences where 3D digital images can be represented alongside physical objects. MR can be achieved through various devices, such as head-mounted displays, smart glasses, smartphones, and projectors. MR can enable users to augment their perception, cognition, and interaction with the world, as well as to create and manipulate virtual content. MR can also enable users to collaborate with other users and devices across different locations and contexts, creating a sense of presence and co-presence.
  - **Communication protocols and standards**: Communication protocols and standards are the rules and formats that govern the exchange of information and data between different devices and systems. Communication protocols and standards can vary depending on the type, purpose, and quality of the communication, as well as the characteristics of the devices and systems involved. Some of the common communication protocols and standards for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything are:

    - **Bluetooth**: Bluetooth is a wireless technology that enables short-range communication between devices, such as wearables, smartphones, and computers. Bluetooth can support data transfer, audio streaming, and device pairing, among other functions. Bluetooth can also enable low-energy communication for IoT devices, such as sensors and actuators, through the Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) protocol.
    - **Wi-Fi**: Wi-Fi is a wireless technology that enables high-speed communication between devices and networks, such as wearables, smartphones, computers, and routers. Wi-Fi can support data transfer, internet access, and device discovery, among other functions. Wi-Fi can also enable long-range communication for IoT devices, such as cameras and smart appliances, through the Wi-Fi Direct protocol.
    - **5G**: 5G is the fifth generation of cellular network technology that enables high-speed, low-latency, and high-reliability communication between devices and networks, such as wearables, smartphones, computers, and base stations. 5G can support data transfer, internet access, and device connectivity, among other functions. 5G can also enable massive-scale communication for IoT devices, such as vehicles and drones, through the 5G New Radio (NR) protocol.
    - **WebRTC**: WebRTC is a web-based technology that enables real-time communication between browsers and applications, such as wearables, smartphones, computers, and web servers. WebRTC can support audio, video, and data streaming, as well as device synchronization, among other functions. WebRTC can also enable peer-to-peer communication for MR devices, such as head-mounted displays and smart glasses, through the WebRTC Data Channel protocol.
