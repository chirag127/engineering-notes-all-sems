### Inter-Integrated Circuit

- Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) is a serial communication protocol that allows multiple devices to communicate with each other using only two wires: a clock line (SCL) and a data line (SDA)     .
- I2C was invented by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductors) in 1982 and is widely used in applications where low cost and ease of implementation are more important than high speed  .
- I2C can support up to 1008 slave devices and multiple masters on the same bus, which can be dynamically configured by software  .
- I2C uses a 7-bit or 10-bit address to identify each device on the bus and transfers data in packets of 8 bits with an acknowledge bit after each byte  .
- I2C has four modes of operation: standard mode (up to 100 kbit/s), fast mode (up to 400 kbit/s), fast mode plus (up to 1 Mbit/s), and high-speed mode (up to 3.4 Mbit/s)  .
- I2C is suitable for connecting sensors, EEPROMs, LCDs, RTCs, and other low-speed peripherals to microcontrollers, embedded systems, or mobile phones    .
