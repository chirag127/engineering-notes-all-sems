### Iterative coding methodology

- Iterative coding methodology is a way of developing a product or service in phases, with each phase building on the previous one.
- The basic idea behind it is that development takes place through repeated cycles (iterations) and in small parts at a time.
- The iterative methodology is commonly used in software development but is often applied in other fields.
- The key advantage of this method is that you can test your ideas early and often. This means less wasted time, money and resources later on when it comes to implementing your product or service.
- The iterative methodology also allows you to adapt to changing requirements and feedback from users or stakeholders.
- The iterative methodology consists of four main phases: planning, design, implementation and evaluation.
- Planning: This phase involves defining the scope, objectives and deliverables of the project. It also involves identifying the risks, assumptions and constraints that may affect the project.
- Design: This phase involves creating a prototype or a mock-up of the product or service, based on the requirements and specifications defined in the planning phase. The design phase also involves testing the prototype and getting feedback from users or stakeholders.
- Implementation: This phase involves developing the actual product or service, based on the design and feedback from the previous phase. The implementation phase also involves testing the product or service and fixing any bugs or errors.
- Evaluation: This phase involves assessing the quality and effectiveness of the product or service, based on the objectives and criteria defined in the planning phase. The evaluation phase also involves collecting feedback from users or stakeholders and identifying any areas for improvement.
- The iterative methodology is not a linear process, but a cyclical one. This means that after each phase, you can go back to the previous phase and make changes or refinements, based on the feedback and results from the current phase.
- The iterative methodology is also flexible and dynamic, as it allows you to adjust the scope, objectives and deliverables of the project as you go along, based on the changing needs and expectations of the users or stakeholders.
- Iterative coding is a process where you code a small piece of your project and then test it, then code the next piece and test it, so on and so forth.
- Iterative coding helps you to avoid coding errors, bugs or inconsistencies, as you can catch them early and fix them before they affect the whole project.
- Iterative coding also helps you to improve your code quality, readability and maintainability, as you can refactor and optimize your code as you go along.
- Iterative coding also helps you to learn from your mistakes and improve your coding skills, as you can see the results and feedback of your code in action.
