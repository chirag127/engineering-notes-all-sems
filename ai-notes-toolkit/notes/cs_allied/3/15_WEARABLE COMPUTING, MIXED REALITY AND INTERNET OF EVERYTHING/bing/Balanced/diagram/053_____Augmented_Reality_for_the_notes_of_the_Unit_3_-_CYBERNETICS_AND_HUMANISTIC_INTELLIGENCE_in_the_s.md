### Augmented Reality

- Augmented reality (AR) is a technology that superimposes a computer-generated image on a user's view of the real world, thus providing a composite view.
- AR can span multiple sensory modalities, including visual, auditory, haptic, somatosensory and olfactory.
- AR can enhance the user's perception of reality by adding artificial or real information that is not normally visible or audible.
- AR can be used for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, navigation, gaming, art, medicine, tourism, etc.
- AR can be delivered via different devices, such as smartphones, tablets, head-mounted displays, smart glasses, projectors, etc.
- AR can be classified into different types, such as marker-based, markerless, projection-based, superimposition-based, etc.
- AR can be distinguished from other related technologies, such as virtual reality (VR), mixed reality (MR), and extended reality (XR).
- VR creates a fully immersive and simulated environment that replaces the real world.
- MR combines the real and virtual worlds and allows interaction between them.
- XR is a term that encompasses all the technologies that create immersive and interactive experiences that augment or replace the real world.
