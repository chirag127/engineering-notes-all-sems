### Digital vs. Analog circuits

- Analog circuits operate on analog signals, which are continuous valued signals that vary smoothly over time and can have any magnitude within a certain range. Examples of analog signals are sound waves, light waves, temperature, voltage, etc. Analog circuits can perform operations such as amplification, filtering, modulation, demodulation, etc. on analog signals. Analog circuits are often used for applications that require high precision, accuracy, and fidelity, such as audio and video processing, radio communication, sensor interfacing, etc. Analog circuits are usually composed of passive components (such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.) and active components (such as transistors, diodes, op-amps, etc.).
- Digital circuits operate on digital signals, which are discrete valued signals that exist at only two levels, usually denoted as 0 and 1. Examples of digital signals are binary numbers, logic states, bits, etc. Digital circuits can perform operations such as arithmetic, logic, encoding, decoding, multiplexing, demultiplexing, etc. on digital signals. Digital circuits are often used for applications that require high speed, reliability, and scalability, such as data processing, computation, encryption, communication, etc. Digital circuits are usually composed of logic gates, flip-flops, registers, counters, etc.
- The main differences between analog and digital circuits are:

  - Analog circuits are more difficult to design, test, and manufacture than digital circuits, since every component must be carefully selected and positioned to achieve the desired functionality and performance. Digital circuits are easier to design, test, and manufacture, since they can be implemented using standard modules and techniques.
  - Analog circuits are more susceptible to noise, interference, and distortion than digital circuits, since any variation in the signal can affect the output. Digital circuits are more immune to noise, interference, and distortion, since they only depend on the logic level of the signal and not its exact magnitude.
  - Analog circuits are more power-efficient than digital circuits, since they consume power only when the signal changes. Digital circuits consume power constantly, since they switch between 0 and 1 levels frequently.
  - Analog circuits have a limited bandwidth and dynamic range than digital circuits, since they are constrained by the physical characteristics of the components and the medium. Digital circuits have a higher bandwidth and dynamic range than analog circuits, since they can use multiple bits to represent a signal and can use different encoding schemes to optimize the transmission and storage of the signal.
