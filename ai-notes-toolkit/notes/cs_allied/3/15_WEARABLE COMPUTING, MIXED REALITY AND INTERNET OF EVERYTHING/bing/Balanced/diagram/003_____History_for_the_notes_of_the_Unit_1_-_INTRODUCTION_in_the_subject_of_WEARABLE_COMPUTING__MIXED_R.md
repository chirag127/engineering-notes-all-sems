### History

- Wearable computing is the field of technology that involves devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, glasses, headsets, rings, etc.
- The concept of wearable computing has been around for centuries, but the term was coined by Steve Mann in the 1980s.
- Some of the earliest examples of wearable computing are:
  - The abacus ring, a finger-sized device that could perform simple calculations, dating back to the 16th century.
  - The covert timing devices hidden in shoes by Edward Thorp and Claude Shannon, to cheat at roulette in the 1960s and 1970s.
  - Steve Mann's rig, a backpack-sized computer with a head-mounted display and a camera, that he wore in the 1980s.
- Some of the milestones in the development of wearable computing are:
  - The Trekker, a wearable computer system with a Private Eye display and a Twiddler chording keyboard, developed by Thad Starner and others at MIT in 1993.
  - The WearCam, a wearable camera that could transmit live video over the internet, developed by Steve Mann in 1994.
  - The Google Glass, a smart eyewear that could display information, take photos, record videos, and access the internet, launched by Google in 2013.
- Some of the current trends and challenges in wearable computing are:
  - The integration of wearable devices with the internet of things, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and big data, to create smart and personalized services.
  - The improvement of the battery life, performance, design, and usability of wearable devices, to make them more appealing and convenient for users.
  - The protection of the privacy, security, and ethics of the data collected and processed by wearable devices, to avoid misuse and abuse by third parties.
