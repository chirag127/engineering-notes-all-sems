# Microcontrollers

- A microcontroller is a small computer on a single VLSI integrated circuit (IC) chip that can govern a specific operation in an embedded system.
- A microcontroller contains one or more CPUs (processor cores) along with memory and input/output (I/O) peripherals.
- Microcontrollers are used in automatically controlled products and devices, such as automobile engine control systems, implantable medical devices, remote controls, office machines, appliances, power tools, toys and other embedded systems.
- Microcontrollers can be classified by their architecture, memory size, instruction set, clock speed, power consumption, and peripheral features.
- Some common microcontroller architectures are 8-bit, 16-bit and 32-bit, which refer to the size of the data bus and the registers.
- Some common microcontroller families are PIC, AVR, MSP430, ARM, and Arduino, which have different features and capabilities.
- Microcontrollers can be programmed using various languages, such as assembly, C, C++, Python, and Arduino.
- Microcontrollers can communicate with other devices or sensors using various protocols, such as UART, SPI, I2C, CAN, USB, and Bluetooth.
- Microcontrollers can perform various tasks, such as analog-to-digital conversion, pulse-width modulation, timers, interrupts, and watchdogs.
- Microcontrollers can be powered by batteries, solar cells, or external power sources, depending on their voltage and current requirements.
