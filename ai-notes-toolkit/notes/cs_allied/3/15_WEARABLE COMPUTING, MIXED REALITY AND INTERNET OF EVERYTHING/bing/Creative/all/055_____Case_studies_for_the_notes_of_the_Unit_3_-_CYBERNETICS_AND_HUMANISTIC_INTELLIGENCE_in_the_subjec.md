# Case studies for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Cybernetics is the science of communication and control in living beings and machines. It studies the feedback loops that regulate the behavior of complex systems and the information flows that enable adaptation and learning. Cybernetics has applications in various fields, such as engineering, biology, psychology, sociology, and philosophy.
- Humanistic intelligence (HI) is a concept proposed by Steve Mann, a pioneer of wearable computing. HI is intelligence that arises when a human is part of the feedback loop of a computational process in which the human and computer are inextricably intertwined. HI recognizes that the human brain is perhaps the best neural network of its kind, and that there are many new signal processing applications, within the domain of personal cybernetics, that can make use of this excellent but often overlooked processor.
- Wearable computing is a form of technology that integrates with the human body, form, and function. Wearable computing devices can augment the human senses, enhance the human capabilities, and facilitate new forms of communication and interaction. Wearable computing can embody HI by creating a symbiotic relationship between the human and the computer, where the human provides the context, creativity, and intuition, and the computer provides the speed, accuracy, and memory.
- Mixed reality (MR) is a term that encompasses both augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR). MR is the merging of real and virtual worlds to produce new environments and visualizations where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time. MR can create immersive and engaging experiences that can enhance the human perception, cognition, and emotion. MR can also embody HI by creating a feedback loop between the human and the virtual environment, where the human can manipulate and modify the virtual objects, and the virtual objects can influence and affect the human.
- Internet of everything (IoE) is a concept that extends the internet of things (IoT) by incorporating people, processes, data, and things into a networked system that can communicate, collaborate, and create value. IoE can enable new applications and services that can improve the quality of life, efficiency, and productivity of individuals, organizations, and societies. IoE can also embody HI by creating a feedback loop between the human and the networked system, where the human can provide the goals, values, and preferences, and the networked system can provide the data, analytics, and recommendations.

Some case studies that illustrate the concepts of cybernetics, HI, wearable computing, MR, and IoE are:

- The Digital Eye Glass, a wearable computing device developed by Steve Mann, that can augment the human vision, memory, and communication. The Digital Eye Glass can capture, process, and display images and videos in real time, and can also connect to the internet and other devices. The Digital Eye Glass can create a feedback loop between the human and the computer, where the human can control the device with gestures, voice, or eye movements, and the device can provide visual information and feedback to the human.
- The Augmediated Reality, a form of MR that uses the Digital Eye Glass to modify the human perception of reality. The Augmediated Reality can enhance, diminish, or replace the visual stimuli that the human receives from the environment, and can also create new visual stimuli that are not present in the environment. The Augmediated Reality can create a feedback loop between the human and the virtual environment, where the human can manipulate and modify the virtual stimuli, and the virtual stimuli can influence and affect the human.
- The Personal Area Network (PAN), a wearable computing device developed by Thomas Zimmerman, that can transmit data and power through the human body. The PAN can connect the human to other devices, such as sensors, actuators, displays, and computers, without the need for wires or batteries. The PAN can create a feedback loop between the human and the computer, where the human can provide the data and power, and the computer can provide the functionality and feedback.
- The SixthSense, a wearable computing device developed by Pranav Mistry, that can project interactive information and interfaces onto any surface. The SixthSense can recognize the human gestures, objects, and faces, and can provide relevant information and feedback. The SixthSense can create a feedback loop between the human and the computer, where the human can control the device with gestures, and the device can provide visual information and feedback to the human.
