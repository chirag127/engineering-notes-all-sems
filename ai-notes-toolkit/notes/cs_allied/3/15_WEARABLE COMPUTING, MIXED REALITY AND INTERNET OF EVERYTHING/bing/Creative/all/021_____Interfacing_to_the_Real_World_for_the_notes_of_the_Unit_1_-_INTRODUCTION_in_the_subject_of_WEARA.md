# Interfacing to the Real World for Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything

- Wearable computing is a field of computer science that aims to create devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, glasses, headsets, etc.
- Mixed reality (MR) is a type of immersive technology that combines elements of virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) to create a hybrid environment where digital and physical objects coexist and interact.
- Internet of Everything (IoE) is a concept that refers to the interconnection of people, processes, data and things through the internet, enabling new forms of communication, collaboration and innovation.
- Interfacing to the real world is a key challenge for wearable computing, MR and IoE, as it requires designing and developing devices and systems that can sense, process and display information from and to the user and the environment in a natural and intuitive way.
- Some of the topics and techniques that are relevant for interfacing to the real world are:

  - Wireless communication: This enables data transmission and reception between wearable devices and other devices or networks, such as smartphones, computers, sensors, cloud services, etc. Wireless communication can be based on different standards and protocols, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, 5G, etc. Wireless communication can also enable collaborative MR experiences, where multiple users can share and interact with the same virtual content.
  - Computer vision: This is a branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the analysis and understanding of visual data, such as images and videos. Computer vision can be used for various purposes, such as face recognition, object detection, gesture recognition, scene reconstruction, etc. Computer vision can also enable tracking and registration of virtual objects in MR, where the position and orientation of the device and the environment are estimated and aligned .
  - Haptic feedback: This is a type of feedback that stimulates the sense of touch, such as vibration, pressure, temperature, etc. Haptic feedback can be used to enhance the realism and interactivity of MR experiences, where the user can feel the virtual objects or events. Haptic feedback can also be used to provide cues and guidance to the user, such as navigation, alerts, notifications, etc.
  - Audio feedback: This is a type of feedback that stimulates the sense of hearing, such as sound, music, speech, etc. Audio feedback can be used to create immersive and engaging MR experiences, where the user can hear the virtual objects or events. Audio feedback can also be used to communicate information and instructions to the user, such as voice commands, text-to-speech, etc.
  - User interface design: This is the process of designing and developing the visual and interactive elements of a system or application, such as menus, buttons, icons, etc. User interface design can be used to create intuitive and user-friendly interfaces for wearable devices and MR applications, where the user can access and control the functions and features of the system. User interface design can also be influenced by the characteristics and limitations of the device and the environment, such as screen size, resolution, battery life, lighting conditions, etc.
