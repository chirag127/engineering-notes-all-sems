# Push and Pull for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Push and pull are two different modes of communication and interaction between systems, agents, or users in cybernetics and related fields.
- Push mode is when a system or an agent initiates an action or a message to another system or agent, without waiting for a request or a demand from the receiver. For example, a push notification is when an app sends a message to a user's device, without the user asking for it.
- Pull mode is when a system or an agent responds to an action or a message from another system or agent, based on a request or a demand from the sender. For example, a pull request is when a developer asks for a code review from another developer, before merging their changes to the main branch.
- Push and pull modes have different advantages and disadvantages, depending on the context and the goal of the communication or interaction. Some of the factors that influence the choice of mode are:
  - The availability and reliability of the systems or agents involved. Push mode may be more suitable when the sender is more available or reliable than the receiver, or when the receiver may not be aware of the sender's existence or updates. Pull mode may be more suitable when the receiver is more available or reliable than the sender, or when the sender may not have the latest information or status of the receiver.
  - The urgency and importance of the information or action. Push mode may be more suitable when the information or action is urgent or important, and the sender wants to ensure that the receiver gets it as soon as possible. Pull mode may be more suitable when the information or action is not urgent or important, and the sender wants to avoid interrupting or overwhelming the receiver.
  - The complexity and diversity of the information or action. Push mode may be more suitable when the information or action is simple or homogeneous, and the sender knows what the receiver needs or wants. Pull mode may be more suitable when the information or action is complex or diverse, and the sender does not know what the receiver needs or wants, or the receiver has different preferences or options.
- Push and pull modes can also be combined or hybridized, depending on the situation and the desired outcome. For example, a system or an agent can use push mode to notify another system or agent of an event or a change, and then use pull mode to provide more details or options upon request. Alternatively, a system or an agent can use pull mode to request information or action from another system or agent, and then use push mode to confirm or acknowledge the response.
- Push and pull modes can have different implications and effects on the systems or agents involved, as well as on the overall system or network. Some of the possible implications and effects are:
  - Push mode can increase the efficiency and effectiveness of the communication or interaction, by reducing the latency and increasing the relevance of the information or action. However, push mode can also increase the risk and cost of the communication or interaction, by increasing the dependency and vulnerability of the systems or agents, and by consuming more resources and bandwidth.
  - Pull mode can increase the autonomy and flexibility of the systems or agents, by allowing them to control the timing and the content of the information or action. However, pull mode can also decrease the coherence and consistency of the communication or interaction, by introducing more variability and uncertainty in the information or action, and by requiring more coordination and synchronization.
- Push and pull modes can also have different impacts and influences on the humanistic aspects of the systems or agents, such as their motivation, satisfaction, trust, and engagement. Some of the possible impacts and influences are:
  - Push mode can increase the motivation and satisfaction of the systems or agents, by providing them with timely and relevant feedback and guidance. However, push mode can also decrease the trust and engagement of the systems or agents, by reducing their agency and choice, and by creating more noise and distraction.
  - Pull mode can increase the trust and engagement of the systems or agents, by enhancing their agency and choice, and by creating more curiosity and interest. However, pull mode can also decrease the motivation and satisfaction of the systems or agents, by requiring more effort and attention, and by creating more frustration and confusion.
