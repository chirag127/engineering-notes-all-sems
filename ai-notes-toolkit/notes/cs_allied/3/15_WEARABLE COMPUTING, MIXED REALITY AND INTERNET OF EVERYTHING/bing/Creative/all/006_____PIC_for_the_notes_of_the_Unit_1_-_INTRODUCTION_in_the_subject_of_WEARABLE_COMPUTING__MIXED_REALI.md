# PIC for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is a field of technology that involves devices that can be worn on the user's body and provide some form of computing or communication functionality .
- Examples of wearable devices include smart watches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, head-mounted displays, smart clothing, medical devices, and more .
- Wearable devices can be classified into three categories based on their interaction modes: passive, active, and proactive.
  - Passive devices are those that do not require any user input or feedback, such as sensors or monitors.
  - Active devices are those that require user input or feedback, such as touchscreens or buttons.
  - Proactive devices are those that initiate interaction with the user or the environment, such as notifications or gestures.
- Wearable devices can communicate with other devices or networks using various wireless technologies, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular, NFC, or RFID.
- Wearable devices can enable various applications and services, such as health and fitness, entertainment, education, social networking, gaming, augmented reality, virtual reality, and more .
- Mixed reality is a field of technology that involves the blending of real and virtual environments, where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time.
- Examples of mixed reality devices include mixed reality headsets, such as Microsoft HoloLens, Magic Leap One, or Meta 2, that can project holograms onto the user's field of view.
- Mixed reality can be classified into four categories based on the degree of immersion and realism: augmented reality, augmented virtuality, virtual reality, and real reality.
  - Augmented reality is the enhancement of the real environment with virtual objects or information, such as navigation or annotation.
  - Augmented virtuality is the enhancement of the virtual environment with real objects or information, such as video or audio.
  - Virtual reality is the replacement of the real environment with a fully synthetic one, such as a simulation or a game.
  - Real reality is the natural perception of the real environment, without any artificial modification.
- Mixed reality can communicate with other devices or networks using various wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, 5G, or Li-Fi.
- Mixed reality can enable various applications and services, such as education, entertainment, gaming, tourism, art, medicine, engineering, and more.
- Internet of Everything is a field of technology that involves the interconnection of people, processes, data, and things, using various devices, sensors, networks, and platforms, to enable new capabilities, experiences, and opportunities.
- Examples of Internet of Everything devices include smartphones, tablets, laptops, wearables, smart home appliances, smart vehicles, smart cities, smart grids, and more.
- Internet of Everything can be classified into four categories based on the type of connection: machine-to-machine, machine-to-people, people-to-people, and people-to-machine.
  - Machine-to-machine is the connection of devices or sensors without human intervention, such as smart meters or smart locks.
  - Machine-to-people is the connection of devices or sensors with human input or output, such as smart watches or smart speakers.
  - People-to-people is the connection of people with each other using devices or platforms, such as social media or video conferencing.
  - People-to-machine is the connection of people with devices or sensors using commands or gestures, such as voice assistants or smart glasses.
- Internet of Everything can communicate with other devices or networks using various wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, NFC, or RFID.
- Internet of Everything can enable various applications and services, such as health and wellness, education, entertainment, security, transportation, energy, and more.
