# Representing “reality” with computers

- Computers can model some aspects of reality by using data, algorithms and simulations.
- Data is a collection of facts or measurements that can be processed by a computer.
- Algorithms are a set of rules or instructions that tell the computer how to manipulate data and perform calculations.
- Simulations are the imitation of a real-world process or system by using data and algorithms on a computer.
- Computers can represent reality at different levels of abstraction and complexity, depending on the purpose and the limitations of the computer system.
- Some examples of representing reality with computers are:

  - Physical conversion of input signals into digital signals (as well as converting digital signals into an output signal) . This involves using sensors, transducers, analog-to-digital converters, digital-to-analog converters, etc. to capture and reproduce physical phenomena such as sound, light, temperature, etc. in a binary format that the computer can understand and manipulate.
  - Encoding schemes for representing information in binary . This involves using different methods to encode data such as text, numbers, images, etc. into sequences of bits (0s and 1s) that the computer can store and process. Some examples of encoding schemes are ASCII, Unicode, JPEG, MP3, etc.
  - Algorithmic modeling of physical processes . This involves using mathematical equations, rules, and logic to describe and predict the behavior of natural or artificial systems such as weather, physics, chemistry, biology, etc. on a computer. Some examples of algorithmic modeling are differential equations, cellular automata, neural networks, etc.
  - Data models for representing spatial and temporal aspects of reality . This involves using different ways to organize and structure data that reflect the spatial and temporal relationships among objects or events in the real world. Some examples of data models are raster, vector, network, etc.
  - Computer simulations for experimenting with reality . This involves using data and algorithms to create virtual scenarios that mimic or modify the real-world conditions and observe the outcomes or effects of different actions or interventions on a computer. Some examples of computer simulations are flight simulators, video games, virtual reality, etc.
