# ADC for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- ADC stands for **analog-to-digital converter**, a circuit that converts a continuous voltage value (analog) to a binary value (digital) that can be understood by a digital device.
- ADCs are essential for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, as they enable the processing and communication of analog signals such as sound, light, temperature, pressure, etc. that are captured by sensors.
- ADCs can be classified into different types based on their architecture, such as **successive approximation**, **pipelined**, **delta-sigma**, **flash**, **integrating**, **ramp**, etc. Each type has its own advantages and disadvantages in terms of speed, resolution, power consumption, complexity, etc.
- ADCs require **ADC drivers**, which are specialty amplifiers that are designed specifically to work alongside ADCs, to provide an instantaneous charge to the sampling node, to level shift the common mode voltage, and to isolate the ADC from the signal source.
- ADCs also require **sampling and quantization**, which are the processes of converting a continuous-time signal to a discrete-time signal and assigning a discrete value to each sample, respectively. Sampling and quantization introduce errors such as **aliasing** and **quantization noise**, which can be reduced by using appropriate sampling frequency and number of bits.
- ADCs can be designed to save power by using **non-uniform sampling** techniques, such as **delta-modulation** or **level-crossing**, which only sample the signal when there is a significant change in its value, rather than at a fixed rate. This reduces the number of samples and the data rate, making it suitable for wearable devices .
