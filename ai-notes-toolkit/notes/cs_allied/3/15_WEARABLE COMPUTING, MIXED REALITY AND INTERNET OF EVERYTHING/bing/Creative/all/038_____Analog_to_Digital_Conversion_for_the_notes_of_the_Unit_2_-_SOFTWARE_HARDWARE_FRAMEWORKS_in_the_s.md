# Analog to Digital Conversion

- Analog to digital conversion (ADC) is an electronic process in which a continuously variable, or analog, signal is changed into a multilevel digital signal without altering its essential content.
- ADC is a system that converts an analog signal, such as a sound picked up by a microphone or light entering a digital camera, into a digital signal.
- ADC allows digital circuits to interface with the real world by encoding an analog signal into a binary code.
- ADC is a very useful feature that converts an analog voltage on a pin to a digital number.
- ADC allows micro-processor controlled circuits, Arduinos, Raspberry Pi, and other such digital logic circuits to communicate with the analog world around us.
- ADC is used for digitizing pixels, radar systems, in situ and remote sensing systems, and many other applications .
- ADC consists of three main components: a sample and hold circuit, a quantizer, and an encoder.
- The sample and hold circuit samples the analog input signal at a fixed rate and holds it constant until the next sample is taken.
- The quantizer assigns a discrete value to the sampled signal based on a predefined scale or resolution.
- The encoder converts the discrete value into a binary code that can be processed by digital circuits.
- ADC can be classified into different types based on the method of conversion, such as successive approximation, flash, dual slope, sigma-delta, and pipeline.
- ADC can also be characterized by various parameters, such as resolution, accuracy, speed, power consumption, and noise.
