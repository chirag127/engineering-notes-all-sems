# Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

## Wearable Computing

- Wearable computing is the use of devices that can be worn on the body to perform various functions such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, or interacting with the environment or other devices.
- Wearable devices can be classified into different categories based on their form factor, functionality, and application domain. Some examples are smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, head-mounted displays, smart clothing, smart jewelry, and implantable devices.
- Wearable computing can provide various benefits such as convenience, personalization, health monitoring, entertainment, education, and social interaction.
- Wearable computing also poses some challenges such as privacy, security, usability, battery life, interoperability, and social acceptance.

## Mixed Reality

- Mixed reality (MR) is a type of immersive technology that combines the real and virtual worlds in a seamless way, allowing users to interact with both physical and digital objects in a natural manner.
- MR can be achieved by using different devices such as head-mounted displays (HMDs), smart glasses, projection systems, or mobile devices that can overlay digital information onto the real environment or vice versa.
- MR can be used for various purposes such as gaming, entertainment, education, training, simulation, collaboration, navigation, and visualization.
- MR can also enable new forms of interaction such as gesture, voice, eye-tracking, haptics, and brain-computer interfaces.

## Internet of Everything

- Internet of Everything (IoE) is a concept that extends the Internet of Things (IoT) by incorporating not only devices and sensors, but also people, data, and processes into a network of connections that provide general intelligence and improved cognition across the networked environment.
- IoE can be seen as a convergence of four dimensions: people, things, data, and processes. People can connect to the network through wearable devices, smartphones, or social media. Things can include any physical or virtual object that can be identified, sensed, or controlled. Data can be generated, collected, analyzed, or shared by people or things. Processes can be the methods or rules that govern how people, things, data, and processes interact and create value.
- IoE can enable various applications and opportunities such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart agriculture, smart education, smart manufacturing, and smart transportation.
- IoE can also pose some challenges such as scalability, security, privacy, reliability, ethics, and governance.
