### AR versus VR for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- AR stands for Augmented Reality, which is a technology that overlays digital information or objects onto the real world, enhancing the user's perception of reality.
- VR stands for Virtual Reality, which is a technology that immerses the user in a simulated environment, creating the illusion of a different reality.
- Both AR and VR are examples of Mixed Reality, which is a spectrum of technologies that combine the real and virtual worlds in various ways.
- Some of the differences between AR and VR are:

  - AR enhances the real world, while VR replaces it.
  - AR requires a transparent display or a camera to show the real world, while VR requires a closed display or a headset to block out the real world.
  - AR allows the user to interact with both the real and virtual objects, while VR limits the user to the virtual objects.
  - AR can be used in various contexts and scenarios, while VR is more suitable for specific applications and experiences.
  - AR can be more accessible and affordable, while VR can be more immersive and realistic.

- Some of the similarities between AR and VR are:

  - Both AR and VR use computer graphics and sensors to create and manipulate virtual objects.
  - Both AR and VR aim to provide the user with a sense of presence and engagement in the virtual environment.
  - Both AR and VR can be used for entertainment, education, training, communication, and other purposes.
  - Both AR and VR can benefit from the advances in wearable computing, internet of everything, and humanistic intelligence.

- Some of the challenges and opportunities for AR and VR are:

  - Technical challenges, such as improving the quality, performance, and compatibility of the hardware and software, reducing the latency, and increasing the battery life and the field of view.
  - User experience challenges, such as designing intuitive and natural interfaces, avoiding motion sickness and cybersickness, and ensuring the safety and privacy of the user.
  - Ethical and social challenges, such as addressing the potential negative impacts of AR and VR on the user's health, behavior, and identity, as well as on the society and the environment.
  - Creative and innovative opportunities, such as exploring new forms of expression, storytelling, and interaction, enhancing the user's learning and skills, and creating new possibilities for collaboration and co-creation.
