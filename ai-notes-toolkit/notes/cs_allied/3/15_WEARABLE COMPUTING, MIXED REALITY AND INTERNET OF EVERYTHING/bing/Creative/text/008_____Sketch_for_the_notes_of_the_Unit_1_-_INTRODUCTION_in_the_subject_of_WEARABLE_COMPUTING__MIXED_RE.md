### Sketch for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the use of devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, or virtual reality headsets, to collect and analyse data, provide feedback, or enhance the user's experience.
- Mixed reality is the blending of the physical and digital worlds, where virtual objects can interact with real environments, and users can manipulate both through spatial computing. Spatial computing is the ability of devices to sense and map the surrounding space, and project digital content onto it.
- Internet of Everything (IoE) is a network of connections between people, things, data, and processes that provide general intelligence and improved cognition across the networked environment. IoE encompasses the Internet of Things (IoT), which is the interconnection of devices and sensors that collect and exchange data.
- The main applications of wearable computing, mixed reality, and IoE are in the domains of health, education, entertainment, gaming, social media, and productivity. Some examples are:
  - Health: Wearable devices can monitor vital signs, track physical activity, provide alerts, or deliver medication. Mixed reality can provide immersive simulations for training, diagnosis, or therapy. IoE can enable remote health care, personalized medicine, and data-driven insights.
  - Education: Wearable devices can enhance learning outcomes, engagement, and accessibility. Mixed reality can create interactive and immersive learning environments, such as virtual classrooms, labs, or field trips. IoE can enable collaborative and adaptive learning, as well as access to rich and diverse data sources.
  - Entertainment: Wearable devices can provide immersive and personalized experiences, such as virtual reality movies, games, or concerts. Mixed reality can create new forms of storytelling, art, and expression, such as augmented reality graffiti, comics, or sculptures. IoE can enable social and interactive entertainment, such as multiplayer games, live streaming, or content sharing.
- The main challenges of wearable computing, mixed reality, and IoE are in the areas of technology, usability, privacy, security, and ethics. Some examples are:
  - Technology: Wearable devices, mixed reality devices, and IoE devices need to overcome limitations in battery life, processing power, storage capacity, connectivity, and compatibility. They also need to ensure reliability, accuracy, and robustness of the data and feedback they provide.
  - Usability: Wearable devices, mixed reality devices, and IoE devices need to ensure user comfort, convenience, and satisfaction. They also need to consider user preferences, needs, and contexts, as well as provide intuitive and natural interfaces and interactions.
  - Privacy: Wearable devices, mixed reality devices, and IoE devices need to protect the user's personal data and identity from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure. They also need to respect the user's consent, control, and awareness of the data collection and processing.
  - Security: Wearable devices, mixed reality devices, and IoE devices need to prevent malicious attacks, such as hacking, spoofing, or tampering, that could compromise the device's functionality, data, or user's safety. They also need to ensure the device's compliance with relevant standards, regulations, and policies.
  - Ethics: Wearable devices, mixed reality devices, and IoE devices need to consider the potential social, cultural, and environmental impacts of their use and development. They also need to ensure the device's alignment with the user's values, rights, and responsibilities.
