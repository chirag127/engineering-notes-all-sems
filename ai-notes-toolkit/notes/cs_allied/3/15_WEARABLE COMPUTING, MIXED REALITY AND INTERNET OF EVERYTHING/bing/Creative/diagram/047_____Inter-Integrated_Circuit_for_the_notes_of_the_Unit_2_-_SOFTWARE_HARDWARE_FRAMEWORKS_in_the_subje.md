### Inter-Integrated Circuit

- Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) is a serial communication protocol that allows multiple devices to communicate with each other over a two-wire bus    .
- I2C was invented by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductors) in 1982 for low-speed and low-cost applications .
- I2C can support up to 1008 slave devices and multiple masters on the same bus .
- I2C uses two wires: Serial Data Line (SDA) and Serial Clock Line (SCL). SDA carries the data bits and SCL provides the clock signal to synchronize the data transfer    .
- I2C is a packet-switched protocol, which means that each data transfer consists of a start condition, an address byte, one or more data bytes, and a stop condition    .
- I2C supports different data transfer modes, such as standard mode (up to 100 kbit/s), fast mode (up to 400 kbit/s), fast mode plus (up to 1 Mbit/s), high-speed mode (up to 3.4 Mbit/s), and ultra-fast mode (up to 5 Mbit/s)  .
- I2C is widely used in applications such as sensors, microcontrollers, EEPROMs, LCDs, and other peripheral devices    .
