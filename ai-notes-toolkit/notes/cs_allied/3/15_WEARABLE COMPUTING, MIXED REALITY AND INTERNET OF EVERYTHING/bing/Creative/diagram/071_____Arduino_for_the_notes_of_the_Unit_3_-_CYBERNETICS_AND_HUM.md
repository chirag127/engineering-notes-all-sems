### Arduino

Arduino is an open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software. Arduino boards are able to read inputs from sensors, buttons, or online sources, and turn them into outputs such as activating motors, turning on LEDs, or publishing data online .

Some basic concepts of Arduino are:

- Microcontroller: This is the brain of an Arduino, and is the component that we load programs into. It can process digital and analog signals, and communicate with other devices.
- USB port: This is used to connect your Arduino board to a computer. You can use it to upload your programs, power your board, or send and receive data.
- USB to Serial chip: This is an important component, as it helps translating data that comes from the computer to the microcontroller, and vice versa.
- Circuit: This is the arrangement of components and wires that connect your Arduino board to the external world. Circuits consist of at least one active electronic component, such as a LED, a motor, or a sensor, and a conductive material, such as wires, so that current can pass through.
- Sketch: This is the name of the program that you write for your Arduino board. It is written in a language based on C/C++, and consists of two main functions: setup() and loop(). The setup() function runs once when the board is powered on or reset, and the loop() function runs repeatedly until the board is turned off.
- Library: This is a collection of code that provides extra functionality to your sketches. For example, you can use libraries to control servos, communicate with other devices, or access online services. Arduino comes with a number of built-in libraries, and you can also install or create your own.

Some examples of Arduino projects are:

- Blinking an LED: This is the simplest Arduino project, and it involves connecting an LED to a digital pin on the board, and turning it on and off with a sketch.
- Reading a potentiometer: This is a project that involves connecting a potentiometer, which is a variable resistor, to an analog pin on the board, and reading its value with a sketch. You can use this value to control the brightness of an LED, the speed of a motor, or the pitch of a sound.
- Making a temperature sensor: This is a project that involves connecting a thermistor, which is a resistor that changes its resistance according to the temperature, to an analog pin on the board, and reading its value with a sketch. You can use this value to display the temperature on an LCD screen, or send it to an online service.
- Making a musical instrument: This is a project that involves connecting a piezo buzzer, which is a device that can produce sounds, to a digital pin on the board, and playing different notes with a sketch. You can also use buttons, potentiometers, or sensors to control the pitch, volume, or rhythm of the sound.
