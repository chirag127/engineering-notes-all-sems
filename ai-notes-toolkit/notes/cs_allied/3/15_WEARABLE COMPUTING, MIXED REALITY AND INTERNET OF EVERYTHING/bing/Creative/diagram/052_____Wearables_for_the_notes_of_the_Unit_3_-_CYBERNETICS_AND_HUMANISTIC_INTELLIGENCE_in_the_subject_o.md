### Wearables
- Wearables are devices that can be worn on the body or integrated into clothing or accessories, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, smart rings, etc.
- Wearables can collect data from sensors, provide feedback to the user, communicate with other devices or networks, and perform various functions, such as health monitoring, navigation, entertainment, etc.
- Wearables can be classified into different categories, such as passive or active, standalone or tethered, discreet or conspicuous, etc.

### Cybernetics
- Cybernetics is the study of communication and control systems in living organisms and machines, especially the feedback mechanisms that regulate their behavior and adaptation.
- Cybernetics can be applied to various fields, such as engineering, biology, psychology, sociology, etc.
- Cybernetics can be used to model and design complex systems, such as robots, artificial intelligence, neural networks, etc.

### Humanistic Intelligence
- Humanistic Intelligence (HI) is a concept proposed by Steve Mann, Marvin Minsky, and Ray Kurzweil, that defines intelligence as the result of a human being in the feedback loop of a computational process, where the human and computer are inextricably intertwined.
- HI can be embodied by wearable computing, which allows the user to augment their senses, memory, cognition, and communication through a constant and intimate interaction with the computer.
- HI can be contrasted with Artificial Intelligence (AI), which aims to create machines that can perform tasks that require human intelligence, and Natural Intelligence (NI), which refers to the innate intelligence of living organisms.
