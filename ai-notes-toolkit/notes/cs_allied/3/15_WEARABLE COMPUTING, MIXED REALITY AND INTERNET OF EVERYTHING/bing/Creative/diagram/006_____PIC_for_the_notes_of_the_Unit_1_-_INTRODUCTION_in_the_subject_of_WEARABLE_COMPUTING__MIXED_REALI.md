- Wearable computing is the use of devices that are worn on the body or attached to clothing, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, or virtual reality headsets. These devices can collect and analyze data, communicate with other devices, and provide feedback to the user.
- Mixed reality is a form of augmented reality that blends the physical and digital worlds, creating a hybrid environment where virtual objects can interact with real ones. Mixed reality devices, such as Magic Leap One or HoloLens 2, can project digital content into the user's field of view, allowing them to multitask and interact with their surroundings in new ways.
- Internet of everything is a concept that extends the internet of things (IoT) to include not only devices, but also people, data, and processes. Internet of everything aims to create a network of connections that provide intelligence and value across the entire system, enabling new applications and services in various domains, such as health, education, entertainment, or security.

Some examples of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything are:

- A smartwatch that tracks the user's heart rate, steps, calories, and sleep, and syncs with a smartphone app that provides personalized insights and recommendations.
- A pair of smart glasses that displays information, such as directions, notifications, or social media updates, on the user's eye, and allows them to control the device with voice or gestures.
- A virtual reality headset that immerses the user in a realistic simulation of a different environment, such as a game, a movie, or a training scenario, and responds to the user's movements and actions.
- A wearable sensor that monitors the user's vital signs, such as blood pressure, glucose, or oxygen levels, and sends the data to a cloud platform that analyzes the data and alerts the user or a doctor in case of any anomalies.
- A mixed reality device that overlays digital content, such as holograms, animations, or videos, on the user's physical environment, and allows them to interact with the content and the environment in real time.
- A network of smart devices, such as cameras, lights, thermostats, or speakers, that communicate with each other and with the user, and can be controlled remotely or automatically, creating a smart home or a smart city.
