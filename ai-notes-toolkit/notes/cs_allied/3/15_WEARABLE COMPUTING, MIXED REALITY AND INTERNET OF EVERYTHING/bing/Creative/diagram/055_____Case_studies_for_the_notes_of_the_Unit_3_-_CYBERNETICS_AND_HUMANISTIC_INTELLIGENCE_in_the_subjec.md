### Case studies for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Case study 1: Digital Eye Glass
  - A wearable computing device that augments the user's vision with digital information  .
  - The device consists of a head-mounted display, a camera, a microphone, a speaker, and a computer that runs the software .
  - The device can perform various functions, such as:
    - Augmediated reality: altering the appearance of the physical world by adding, removing, or modifying visual elements .
    - Mediated reality: enhancing or diminishing the user's perception of reality by applying filters, effects, or transformations to the visual input .
    - Computer vision: recognizing objects, faces, gestures, or scenes in the camera input and providing relevant information or feedback .
    - Personal cybernetics: monitoring and influencing the user's physiological, psychological, or behavioral states by using sensors, actuators, or feedback loops .
  - The device embodies humanistic intelligence, as it creates a symbiotic relationship between the human and the computer, where the human is part of the feedback loop of the computational process, and the computer is inextricably intertwined with the natural capabilities of the human mind and body  .

- Case study 2: Norbert Wiener and Cybernetics
  - Norbert Wiener was a mathematician and philosopher who coined the term cybernetics, which is the study of control and communication in the animal and the machine.
  - Wiener was interested in the similarities and differences between biological and artificial systems, and how they can interact and influence each other.
  - Wiener proposed that cybernetics can be applied to various fields, such as engineering, biology, psychology, sociology, and ethics, and that it can help us understand and improve the human condition.
  - Wiener also warned about the potential dangers of cybernetics, such as the loss of human autonomy, dignity, and responsibility, and the emergence of new forms of oppression, manipulation, and warfare.
  - Wiener's work is relevant to the topics of wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything, as it provides a theoretical framework and a moral perspective for analyzing and designing systems that involve human-machine interaction, feedback, and adaptation.
