### Case studies for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Case study 1: Digital Eye Glass
  - A wearable computing device that augments the user's vision with digital information  .
  - The device consists of a head-mounted display, a camera, a computer, and a wireless connection to the internet.
  - The device can perform various functions, such as face recognition, text translation, navigation, image enhancement, and social media interaction .
  - The device can also modify the user's perception of reality by adding, deleting, or modifying visual elements, such as logos, advertisements, or people. This is called Augmediated Reality (AR).
  - The device can be used for personal, educational, artistic, or social purposes, such as enhancing memory, learning languages, creating art, or communicating with others .
  - The device embodies humanistic intelligence (HI), which is intelligence that arises when a human is part of the feedback loop of a computational process in which the human and computer are inextricably intertwined   .
  - The device challenges the traditional notions of privacy, identity, and reality, as it allows the user to access, manipulate, and share information in unprecedented ways .
- Case study 2: Norbert Wiener and Cybernetics
  - Norbert Wiener was a mathematician and philosopher who coined the term cybernetics, which is the study of control and communication in the animal and the machine.
  - Wiener developed the theory of cybernetics during and after World War II, when he worked on projects such as anti-aircraft systems, radar, and computing.
  - Wiener applied the principles of cybernetics to various fields, such as biology, psychology, sociology, and ethics, and explored the implications of feedback, information, and communication for human society.
  - Wiener was concerned about the potential misuse of cybernetics by authoritarian regimes, corporations, or machines, and advocated for a humanistic approach to technology that respects human dignity, freedom, and creativity.
  - Wiener's ideas influenced many disciplines and movements, such as artificial intelligence, systems theory, cognitive science, cyberculture, and transhumanism.
  - Wiener's work is relevant today, as we face the challenges and opportunities of the internet of everything, which is the interconnection of people, devices, and data through the internet.
