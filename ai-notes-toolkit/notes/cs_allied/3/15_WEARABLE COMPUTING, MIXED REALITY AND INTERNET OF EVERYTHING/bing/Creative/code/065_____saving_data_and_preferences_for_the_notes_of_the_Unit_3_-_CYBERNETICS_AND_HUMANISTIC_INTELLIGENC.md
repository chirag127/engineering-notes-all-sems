### Saving data and preferences

- Saving data and preferences is an important aspect of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything, as it allows users to customize their experiences, access their information across devices and platforms, and preserve their privacy and security.
- There are different methods and technologies for saving data and preferences, depending on the type and amount of data, the level of security and privacy required, the availability and cost of storage and network resources, and the user's preferences and needs.
- Some of the common methods and technologies for saving data and preferences are:

  - Local storage: This involves storing data and preferences on the device itself, such as in the memory, disk, or flash drive. This method is fast, reliable, and does not require network connectivity, but it has limited storage capacity, may not be compatible with other devices or platforms, and may be vulnerable to loss, theft, or damage of the device.
  - Cloud storage: This involves storing data and preferences on a remote server or service, such as Google Drive, Dropbox, or iCloud. This method is scalable, compatible, and accessible from anywhere, but it requires network connectivity, may incur costs or fees, and may be subject to security breaches or data loss.
  - Hybrid storage: This involves storing data and preferences on both the device and the cloud, such as using a local cache or backup and syncing with the cloud. This method combines the advantages of both local and cloud storage, but it also inherits their disadvantages and may introduce complexity and inconsistency.
  - Blockchain storage: This involves storing data and preferences on a distributed ledger or network, such as Bitcoin, Ethereum, or IPFS. This method is decentralized, secure, and transparent, but it is also slow, expensive, and requires high computational power and network bandwidth.

- Saving data and preferences also involves choosing the appropriate format, structure, and encryption of the data, as well as the frequency, timing, and triggers of the data synchronization or backup. These choices depend on the type and purpose of the data, the user's preferences and needs, and the trade-offs between performance, security, and usability.
- Saving data and preferences is not only a technical challenge, but also a humanistic and ethical one, as it involves respecting the user's autonomy, agency, and consent, as well as protecting the user's identity, privacy, and security. Therefore, saving data and preferences should follow the principles of user-centered design, human-computer interaction, and cyberethics, such as:

  - Providing clear and meaningful information and feedback to the user about the data and preferences being saved, the methods and technologies being used, and the risks and benefits involved.
  - Allowing the user to control and customize the data and preferences being saved, the methods and technologies being used, and the access and sharing of the data and preferences with others.
  - Obtaining the user's informed and voluntary consent before saving, accessing, or sharing the user's data and preferences, and respecting the user's right to withdraw or revoke the consent at any time.
  - Ensuring the security, integrity, and availability of the user's data and preferences, and preventing unauthorized or malicious access, modification, or deletion of the data and preferences.
  - Minimizing the collection, storage, and use of the user's data and preferences, and deleting or anonymizing the data and preferences when they are no longer needed or requested by the user.
