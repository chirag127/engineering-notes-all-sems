### Sensors and Software for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the integration of sensors, computing devices, and communication interfaces into clothing or accessories that can be worn on the body.
- Wearable sensors are devices that can measure, monitor, and record physiological or environmental parameters, such as heart rate, temperature, motion, sound, etc.
- Wearable software is the application layer that processes, analyzes, and displays the data collected by wearable sensors, and provides user interaction and feedback.
- Some examples of wearable sensors and software are:
  - Dry EEG sensors and DSI Streamer software for brain-computer interface, neurofeedback, and neuromarketing applications.
  - Accelerometers, gyroscopes, and magnetometers and BHI260AB software for motion tracking, gesture recognition, and activity recognition applications.
  - Electrocardiogram (ECG) sensors and edge computing framework for cardiac arrhythmia detection and diagnosis applications.
- Wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything are related concepts that aim to enhance human capabilities, augment reality, and connect everything through the internet.
- Mixed reality is the combination of real and virtual worlds, where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time.
- Internet of everything is the network of physical objects, people, processes, and data that are interconnected through the internet and can communicate and exchange information.
- Some examples of mixed reality and internet of everything are:
  - Smart glasses and head-mounted displays that overlay digital information on the user's field of view, such as Google Glass, Microsoft HoloLens, and Oculus Rift.
  - Smart watches and fitness trackers that monitor the user's health and fitness, and sync with the user's smartphone and cloud services, such as Apple Watch, Fitbit, and Garmin.
  - Smart home and smart city devices that control and optimize the user's environment, such as smart thermostats, smart lights, smart locks, and smart meters.
