### Hardware to Hardware Communication for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Hardware to hardware communication refers to the process of exchanging data and signals between different hardware devices, such as sensors, actuators, displays, input devices, and processors, that are part of a wearable computing system.
- Hardware to hardware communication can be achieved by using various methods, such as wired or wireless connections, serial or parallel ports, buses, protocols, and standards.
- Some examples of hardware to hardware communication in wearable computing are:
  - A head mounted display (HMD) that receives video and audio signals from a belt or backpack PC via a wireless connection.
  - A touchpad or a chording keyboard that sends input commands to a wearable computer via a serial port.
  - A personal wireless local area network (PWLAN) that enables data exchange between different wearable devices, such as sensors, smartwatches, and smartphones.
  - A wearable device that adapts its power consumption, processing speed, and system configuration based on the feedback from other wearable devices, such as heart rate monitors, accelerometers, and thermometers.
- Hardware to hardware communication is essential for wearable computing because it enables:
  - Enhanced user experience and interaction by providing multimodal feedback, context awareness, and personalization.
  - Improved performance and efficiency by optimizing the use of resources, such as battery, memory, and bandwidth.
  - Increased functionality and reliability by supporting a variety of applications, such as healthcare, entertainment, education, and navigation.
  - Collaborative and distributed computing by forming intellectual collectives, augmented realities, and reflexive systems .
