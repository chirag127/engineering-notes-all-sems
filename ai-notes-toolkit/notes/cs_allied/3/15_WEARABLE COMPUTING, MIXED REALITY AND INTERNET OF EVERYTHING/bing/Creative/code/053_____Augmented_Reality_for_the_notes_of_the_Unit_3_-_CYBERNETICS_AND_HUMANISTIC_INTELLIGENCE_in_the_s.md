### Augmented Reality

- Augmented Reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the perception of reality by overlaying digital information or objects onto the physical world.
- AR can be experienced through various devices, such as head-mounted displays (HMDs), smartphones, tablets, smart glasses, or projectors.
- AR can be classified into three types based on the degree of immersion and interaction: 
  - Marker-based AR: uses a predefined image or pattern (marker) as a trigger to display digital content on the device's screen or HMD.
  - Markerless AR: uses the device's sensors, such as camera, GPS, accelerometer, or gyroscope, to track the user's position and orientation and display digital content accordingly.
  - Projection-based AR: uses a projector to cast digital content onto a physical surface, such as a wall, a table, or a human body.
- AR can be used for various purposes, such as education, entertainment, gaming, tourism, navigation, medical, industrial, military, or art.
- AR can provide benefits, such as enhancing the user's learning, engagement, creativity, collaboration, and productivity, as well as reducing errors, costs, and risks.
- AR can also pose challenges, such as technical limitations, privacy and security issues, ethical and social implications, and user acceptance and satisfaction.
