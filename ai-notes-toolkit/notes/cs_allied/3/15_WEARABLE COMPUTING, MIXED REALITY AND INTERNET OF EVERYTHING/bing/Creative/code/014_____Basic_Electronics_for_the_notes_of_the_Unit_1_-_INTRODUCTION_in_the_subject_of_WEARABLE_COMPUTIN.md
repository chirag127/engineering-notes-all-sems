### Basic Electronics for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the field of designing and developing electronic devices that can be worn on the body, such as smart watches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, etc. 
- Wearable devices can collect and analyze data, communicate with other devices, and provide feedback or services to the user, such as health monitoring, navigation, entertainment, etc. 
- Wearable devices are an example of the Internet of Things (IoT), which is the network of physical objects that can interact with each other and the internet through sensors, software, and connectivity. 
- IoT enables the exchange of data and services among devices, users, and cloud platforms, without requiring human intervention. IoT can enable applications such as smart homes, smart cities, smart agriculture, smart healthcare, etc. 
- Mixed reality (MR) is the combination of real and virtual worlds, where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time. MR can create immersive and interactive experiences for the user, such as augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR). 
- MR can be enabled by wearable devices, such as head-mounted displays (HMDs), smart glasses, or haptic gloves, that can sense the user's environment, track the user's movements, and display or manipulate virtual content. 
- MR can have applications such as education, entertainment, gaming, tourism, art, etc. 
- Basic electronics is the study of the principles and components of electronic circuits, such as resistors, capacitors, transistors, diodes, LEDs, etc. 
- Basic electronics can help understand the design and functionality of wearable devices, IoT devices, and MR devices, as they are composed of electronic circuits that perform various tasks, such as sensing, processing, communication, and display. 
- Basic electronics can also help develop and prototype new wearable devices, IoT devices, and MR devices, by using tools such as breadboards, Arduino, Raspberry Pi, etc.
