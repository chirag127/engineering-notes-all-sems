### Interfacing to the Real World

Interfacing is the method of connecting or linking together one device, especially a computer or micro-controller, with another device or the real world. Interfacing allows us to design or adapt the output and input configurations of the two electronic devices so that they can work together.

Some of the topics covered in this section are:

- Input interfacing circuits: These are circuits that allow us to use mechanical switches, sensors, or other devices as inputs to the computer or micro-controller. Input interfacing circuits can use different techniques such as polling, interrupts, or analog-to-digital conversion to read the input signals and convert them to digital data .
- Output interfacing circuits: These are circuits that allow us to use LEDs, displays, motors, or other devices as outputs from the computer or micro-controller. Output interfacing circuits can use different techniques such as parallel, serial, or pulse-width modulation to send digital data and control the output devices.
- Communication interfacing circuits: These are circuits that allow us to use various protocols and standards to communicate between different devices or the real world. Communication interfacing circuits can use different techniques such as UART, SPI, I2C, Ethernet, or wireless to transmit and receive data over different media .
- Real-world applications: These are examples of how interfacing can be used to create various projects that involve video, audio, sensors, actuators, or other real-world interactions. Real-world applications can use different platforms such as Raspberry Pi, Arduino, or other embedded systems to interface with the real world .
