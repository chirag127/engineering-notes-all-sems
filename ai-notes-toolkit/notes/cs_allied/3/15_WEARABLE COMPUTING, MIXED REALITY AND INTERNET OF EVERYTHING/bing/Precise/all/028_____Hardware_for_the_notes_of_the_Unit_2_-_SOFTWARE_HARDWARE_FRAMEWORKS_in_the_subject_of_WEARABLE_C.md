# Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS

### Hardware

Hardware refers to the physical components of a computer or other electronic system. In the context of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything, hardware can include a wide range of devices and components, such as:

1. Sensors: These are devices that detect and respond to changes in the environment, such as temperature, light, motion, and sound. In wearable computing, sensors can be used to track physical activity, monitor health, and provide feedback to the user.

2. Processors: These are the "brains" of the device, responsible for executing instructions and performing calculations. In wearable computing, processors can range from simple microcontrollers to powerful multi-core CPUs.

3. Memory: This refers to the storage of data and instructions. In wearable computing, memory can include both volatile memory (such as RAM) and non-volatile memory (such as flash storage).

4. Displays: These are used to present information to the user. In wearable computing, displays can range from simple LED indicators to high-resolution screens.

5. Input/output devices: These allow the user to interact with the device. In wearable computing, input/output devices can include buttons, touchscreens, microphones, and speakers.

6. Wireless communication: This allows the device to communicate with other devices and networks. In wearable computing, wireless communication can include technologies such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and cellular networks.

7. Power sources: These provide the energy needed to operate the device. In wearable computing, power sources can include batteries, solar cells, and kinetic energy harvesters.

These hardware components work together to provide the functionality of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything devices. The specific hardware used can vary depending on the application and the requirements of the device.
