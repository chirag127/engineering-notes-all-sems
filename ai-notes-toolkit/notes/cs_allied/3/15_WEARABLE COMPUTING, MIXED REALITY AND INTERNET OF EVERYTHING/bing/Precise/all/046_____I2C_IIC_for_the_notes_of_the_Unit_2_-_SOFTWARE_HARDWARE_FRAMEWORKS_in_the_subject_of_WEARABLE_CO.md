### I2C/IIC for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit), also known as IIC, is a multi-master, multi-slave, packet-switched, single-ended, serial computer bus invented by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductors).

Some of the key features of I2C include:
- It is used for attaching lower-speed peripheral ICs to processors and microcontrollers in short-distance, intra-board communication.
- It uses only two bidirectional open-drain lines, Serial Data Line (SDA) and Serial Clock Line (SCL), pulled up with resistors.
- Data transfer may be initiated only when the bus is not busy.
- It is a half-duplex protocol, meaning that data can be transmitted in both directions, but not at the same time.
- It supports multiple masters and slaves, with arbitration and collision detection.
- It supports 7-bit and 10-bit addressing, allowing up to 112 or 1008 devices on the bus, respectively.
- It supports clock stretching, where a slave device can hold the clock line low to delay the master and slow down the communication.

I2C is widely used in various applications, including wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything. It is a simple and efficient protocol for communication between devices, making it a popular choice for designers and developers.
