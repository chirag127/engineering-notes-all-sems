### ADC

Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) is a device that converts an analog signal, such as a sound picked up by a microphone or light entering a digital camera, into a digital signal. ADCs are used in a wide range of applications, including audio and video processing, data acquisition, and control systems.

Here are some key points to remember about ADCs:

1. ADCs convert continuous analog signals into discrete digital signals.
2. The resolution of an ADC is determined by the number of bits it uses to represent the digital output.
3. The sampling rate of an ADC is the rate at which it takes samples of the analog signal.
4. The accuracy of an ADC is determined by how closely its digital output matches the true value of the analog input.
5. ADCs can be implemented using various techniques, including successive approximation, flash, and sigma-delta conversion.
