### Wearables

Wearables are electronic devices that are worn on the body, either as an accessory or as part of the clothing. They are designed to perform a specific set of functions, such as tracking fitness, monitoring health, or providing entertainment. Wearables are part of the larger trend of the Internet of Things (IoT), where everyday objects are connected to the internet and can communicate with other devices.

Some common types of wearables include:

1. **Smartwatches**: These are wrist-worn devices that can perform many of the functions of a smartphone, such as making calls, sending messages, and accessing the internet. They also often have fitness tracking features, such as heart rate monitoring and step counting.

2. **Fitness trackers**: These are devices that are specifically designed to track physical activity and monitor health. They can measure things like steps taken, calories burned, and heart rate.

3. **Smart glasses**: These are glasses that have a small display and can provide information to the wearer, such as directions or notifications. They can also have a camera and can be used to take photos or record videos.

4. **Smart clothing**: This is clothing that has sensors and other technology embedded in it. It can be used to track physical activity, monitor health, or provide other functions.

Wearables are part of the field of cybernetics and humanistic intelligence, as they are designed to enhance human capabilities and provide new ways for people to interact with technology. They are also part of the larger trend of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything, where the physical and digital worlds are increasingly merging.
