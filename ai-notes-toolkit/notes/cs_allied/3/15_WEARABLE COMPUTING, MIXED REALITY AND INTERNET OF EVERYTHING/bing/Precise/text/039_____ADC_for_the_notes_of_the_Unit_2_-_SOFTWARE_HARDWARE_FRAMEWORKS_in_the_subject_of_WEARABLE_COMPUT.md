### ADC

Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) is a device that converts an analog signal, such as a sound picked up by a microphone or light entering a digital camera, into a digital signal. ADCs are used in various applications, including audio and video systems, measurement and control systems, and digital communication systems.

Here are some key points to note about ADCs:

1. ADCs work by sampling the analog signal at regular intervals and converting each sample into a digital value.
2. The accuracy of the conversion depends on the number of bits used to represent the digital value. The more bits used, the higher the resolution of the conversion.
3. The sampling rate of the ADC must be at least twice the highest frequency component of the analog signal to avoid aliasing.
4. There are several types of ADCs, including flash ADCs, successive approximation ADCs, and sigma-delta ADCs. Each type has its own advantages and disadvantages.
5. ADCs can be integrated into microcontrollers or used as standalone devices.
