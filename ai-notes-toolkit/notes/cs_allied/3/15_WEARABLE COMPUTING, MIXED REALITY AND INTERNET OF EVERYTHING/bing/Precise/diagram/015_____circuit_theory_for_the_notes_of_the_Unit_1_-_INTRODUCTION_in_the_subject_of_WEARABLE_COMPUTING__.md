## Circuit Theory

Circuit theory is a branch of electrical engineering that deals with the analysis and design of electric circuits. It is used to understand the behavior of electric circuits and to predict their performance.

Some key concepts in circuit theory include:

1. **Ohm's Law**: This law states that the current flowing through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points.
2. **Kirchhoff's Laws**: These laws deal with the conservation of charge and energy in a circuit. Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) states that the algebraic sum of currents entering a node is zero. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) states that the algebraic sum of voltages around a closed loop is zero.
3. **Series and Parallel Circuits**: In a series circuit, components are connected end-to-end such that the same current flows through all of them. In a parallel circuit, components are connected such that the voltage across each component is the same.
4. **Thevenin's and Norton's Theorems**: These theorems are used to simplify complex circuits by replacing them with an equivalent circuit consisting of a single voltage or current source and a single resistor.
5. **Superposition Theorem**: This theorem states that the response in a linear circuit with multiple independent sources can be found by considering the effect of each source separately and then adding the individual responses.

Circuit theory is an essential tool for understanding and designing electronic systems, including those used in wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything. It provides a foundation for analyzing the behavior of circuits and predicting their performance. Understanding circuit theory is crucial for anyone working in these fields.
