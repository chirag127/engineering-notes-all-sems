### Operating Systems and Software Frameworks for Wearable and IoT Devices
- Lightweight operating systems: Operating systems for wearable and IoT devices are typically lightweight, real-time operating systems (RTOS) that have a small memory footprint and can run efficiently on low-power hardware. Examples include FreeRTOS, TinyOS, Contiki, etc.
- Constrained software environments: The software environments for these devices are highly constrained in terms of memory, storage, CPU, and power. This requires rethinking traditional software architectures and developing new compact data formats and network protocols.
- Event-driven frameworks: Software frameworks are typically event-driven to handle sensing, inference, and communication events efficiently while minimizing resource usage. Examples include TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers and Arduino.

### Hardware Components and System Design for Wearable and IoT Devices
- Low-power chips: These devices use chips and systems-on-chip (SoCs) that are specifically designed for low-power operation, such as ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers, Intel Edison, and chips from companies like Ambiq Micro and Eta Compute.
- Tiny sensors and displays: They incorporate various tiny sensors and displays, such as accelerometers, gyroscopes, heart rate sensors, force sensors, LEDs, and low-resolution displays. New types of tiny, efficient sensors and displays are being actively developed.
- Wireless communication: Most devices use wireless communications via technologies like Bluetooth Low Energy, Zigbee, NFC, and various IEEE 802.15.4 and cellular protocols to connect to other devices and networks. Antennas and peripherals are integrated in small form factors.
- Energy harvesting: Some devices can harvest energy from the environment using technologies like solar power, thermal gradients, radio frequencies, or body heat and movements to enable longer battery life or eliminate the need for batteries.
