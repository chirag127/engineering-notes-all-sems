1. Cybernetics: The study of self-governing mechanisms in machines and natural organizations. It is a transdisciplinary approach for exploring regulatory systems-their structures, constraints, and possibilities. Present-day cybernetics primarily involves the study of systems that evolve and adapt, with applications in scientific fields as diverse as information theory, thermodynamics, engineering, biology and psychology.

2. Humanistic Intelligence: Humanistic intelligence is focused on human experiences, values, creativity, cultural differences, and wisdom. It is interested in what makes human life meaningful, worthwhile, and flourishing. Humanistic intelligence seeks to cultivate qualities such as empathy, compassion, resilience, creativity, mindfulness, and well-being.

3. Wearable Computing: Wearable computing refers to incorporating computing technologies into wearable accessories or clothing to create constant and continuous computing interfaces. This allows technology to become more embedded in our day-to-day lives. Wearable devices are becoming increasingly sophisticated and capable of tracking performance metrics, biometrics, and augmented/virtual reality interfaces.

4. Mixed Reality: Mixed reality (MR) is the merging of real and virtual worlds to produce new environments and visualizations where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time. MR takes place not just in physical space but in 3D augmented space, and enables seamless interactions of digital information over a physical world.

5. Internet of Everything: The Internet of Everything (IoE) refers to the interconnection of everyday physical objects and integration into computer-based systems via the internet. This includes the connection of everyday objects and appliances ranging from wearable devices to household products. The IoE enables these devices to send and receive data, which can then be analyzed and allow for a smarter and more efficient way of living.
