### Smart Cities and Wearable Computing as a form of urban design

- Wearable computing enables smart cities: Wearable devices with sensors and computing capabilities can be used to collect data about a city's infrastructure and operations. This data can be analyzed to gain insights and enable smart city applications.
- Wearables for infrastructure monitoring: Wearables with sensors can be used by city workers to monitor infrastructure like roads, bridges, buildings, utilities, etc. The data from these sensors can help identify issues early for efficient maintenance and repair.
- Wearables for citizen services: Wearable devices can enable smart services for citizens such as mobile payments, access control, wayfinding, etc. The data from these services can help cities improve experiences and operations.
- Privacy and security considerations: While wearables enable new smart city capabilities, they also introduce new privacy and security risks. Cities must implement strong safeguards and data policies to protect citizens' personal information and ensure data is used properly.
- Equity of services: As smart cities adopt new wearable-enabled services, they must ensure that the benefits are available to all citizens equally. Cities must avoid marginalizing disadvantaged groups or creating technology divides.
- Infrastructure for wearable data: Cities must implement networks, servers, platforms, and other technologies to handle the data from many wearable sensors and devices. This infrastructure is necessary to gain insights from and act on the data to enable smart city applications.

The above content summarizes some of the key ways in which wearable computing can enable smart cities as a form of urban design. The points cover wearables for infrastructure monitoring and citizen services as well as important considerations around privacy, security, equity, and data infrastructure.
