### Hardware to Hardware Communication

- Near Field Communication (NFC): It is a set of standards for smartphones and similar devices to establish radio communication with each other by touching them together or bringing them into close proximity, usually no more than a few centimeters.
- Bluetooth: It is a wireless technology standard for exchanging data over short distances (using UHF radio waves in the frequency range of 2.4 to 2.485 gigahertz) from fixed and mobile devices, and building personal area networks (PANs).
- Wi-Fi Direct: It is a Wi-Fi standard that allows devices to easily connect directly to each other without a wireless access point. It is capable of one-to-one or one-to-many communications and data transmission speeds of up to 250 Mbit/s.
- Infrared Data Association (IrDA): It is a standard that defines physical interfaces, protocols and framed data formats for short-range infrared wireless communications, primarily for remote control, messaging and wireless syncing/transfer applications.
- ZigBee: It is a low-power wireless technology standard built on top of the IEEE 802.15.4 personal area network standard for monitoring and control of devices which need to run on batteries for years and transmit small amounts of data over short to medium ranges.

The above points cover the major hardware to hardware communication technologies which can be used for communication in wearable devices, IoT devices and mixed reality devices. The selection of a particular technology depends on the requirements of range, speed, power, security, cost, etc. of the application.
