### Desktop

- A desktop computer is a personal computer designed for regular use at a single location on or near a desk or table due to its size and power requirements.
- Desktop computers come in a variety of styles ranging from large vertical tower cases to small form factor models that can be tucked behind an LCD monitor.
- The most common configuration has a case that has the power supply, motherboard, hard drive, and numerous slots and ports to which components can be connected externally such as keyboard, mouse, display, etc.
- Desktop computers typically have more power and capabilities than laptops or tablets as they have more internal space and are not constrained by portability requirements.
- Key components of a desktop computer include:

- CPU: The brain of the computer which executes program instructions.
- Memory: Temporary storage for running programs and active data.
- Storage: Permanent storage for data and programs. Often a hard disk drive or solid-state drive.
- Graphics card: Provides dedicated graphics processing power.
- Power supply: Provides power to all the internal components.
- Optical drive: Reads and writes CDs or DVDs.
- Operating system: The primary software that runs on the computer. Examples include Windows, macOS, and Linux.

- The main advantages of desktop computers are their power, upgradability, and price compared to laptops. The main disadvantages are that they are not portable and require a dedicated workspace.
