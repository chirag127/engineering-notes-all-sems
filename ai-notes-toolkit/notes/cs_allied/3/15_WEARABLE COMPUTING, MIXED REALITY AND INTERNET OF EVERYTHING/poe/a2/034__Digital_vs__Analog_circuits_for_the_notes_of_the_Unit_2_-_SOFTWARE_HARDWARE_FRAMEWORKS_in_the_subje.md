### Digital vs. Analog circuits

- Digital circuits operate on discrete electrical signals that represent binary data. They use voltage levels to represent binary 1s and 0s.
- Analog circuits operate on continuous electrical signals that can take on any value within a range. They are used to process signals that can change over time without discrete steps or levels.
- Digital circuits are more robust to noise but can not represent fractional or continuous values directly. Analog circuits can represent a range of values but are more susceptible to noise and interference.
- Many systems use a combination of both digital and analog circuits. The analog signals are converted to digital, processed digitally, then converted back to analog. This is known as analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog conversion.
- Examples of digital circuits include processors, memory, and logic gates. Examples of analog circuits include amplifiers, oscillators, and filters.
- The choice between digital vs. analog circuits depends on the requirements and goals of the specific system or application. Analog may be better for capturing detailed continuous data while digital may be better for robust processing and storage.
