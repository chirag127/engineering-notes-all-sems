1. Google Glass: Google Glass is a head-mounted display designed in the shape of a pair of eyeglasses. It was developed by Google X engineers with the mission of producing a ubiquitous computer. The Google Glass displays information in a smartphone-like hands-free format. Wearers communicated with the device via voice commands. While the initial consumer version was discontinued, Glass is still used for business and professional applications where hands-free access to information is useful.

2. Snapchat Spectacles: Snapchat Spectacles are sunglasses that can record video snippets and synchronize them with a user's Snapchat account. The glasses can record 10-second video clips with a tap of a button. The video is then wirelessly transferred to the user's Snapchat account where it can be viewed or shared. Spectacles highlight the potential popularity and style of wearable cameras despite the discontinuation of Google Glass.

3. Virtual reality headsets: Virtual reality headsets or head-mounted displays are wearable devices that provide immersive virtual reality experiences. The most common types are the Oculus Rift, the HTC Vive, and Sony's PlayStation VR. These headsets use internal displays and motion tracking sensors to simulate a user's physical presence in a virtual environment. VR headsets have significant applications for gaming, entertainment, business, and social connection. As the technology develops further, VR promises to become increasingly integrated into daily life.
