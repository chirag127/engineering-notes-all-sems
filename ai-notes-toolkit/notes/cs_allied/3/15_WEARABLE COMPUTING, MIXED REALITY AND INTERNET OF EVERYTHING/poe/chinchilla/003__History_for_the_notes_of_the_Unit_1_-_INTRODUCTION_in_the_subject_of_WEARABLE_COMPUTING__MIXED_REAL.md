### History for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

In this unit, we will explore the history of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the internet of everything. Here are some important points to keep in mind:

- Wearable computing has been around for decades. In the 1960s, researchers at MIT developed a wearable computer called the "Wearable Motherboard." This device was worn on the body and included a camera, display, and input devices.
- In the 1980s and 1990s, wearable devices became more popular. The first commercial smartwatch, the Seiko RC-1000, was released in 1984. In 1991, Steve Mann developed the "WearComp" – a wearable computer that included a camera, display, and input devices.
- In the early 2000s, wearable computing became more mainstream with the release of the first fitness trackers. These devices tracked steps taken, calories burned, and other health metrics.
- Mixed reality has its roots in virtual reality and augmented reality. Virtual reality was first explored in the 1960s, and augmented reality was first used in the 1990s.
- The term "mixed reality" was coined by Paul Milgram and Fumio Kishino in 1994. They defined mixed reality as a continuum between virtual reality and the real world, with augmented reality in the middle.
- In recent years, mixed reality has become more popular with the release of devices like the Microsoft HoloLens and the Magic Leap One.
- The internet of everything (IoE) is a relatively new concept that refers to the interconnectivity of devices, people, and things. The term "internet of everything" was coined by Cisco in 2013.
- The IoE builds on the concept of the internet of things (IoT), which refers to the interconnectivity of devices. The difference between the two is that the IoE includes people and things in addition to devices.
- The IoE has the potential to revolutionize many industries, including healthcare, transportation, and manufacturing.

In conclusion, wearable computing, mixed reality, and the internet of everything have a rich history that has led to the development of many exciting technologies. Understanding this history is important for understanding the current state of these fields and their potential for the future.
