### Analog to Digital Conversion

Analog to Digital Conversion (ADC) is a process of converting analog signals into digital signals. It is an important process in the field of electronics, especially in the context of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the internet of everything. In this unit, we will focus on understanding the basics of ADC, its importance, and applications.

Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Analog signals are continuous signals whereas digital signals are discrete signals. Analog signals can take any value within a range whereas digital signals can take only a finite set of values.
- ADC is used to convert analog signals into digital signals so that they can be processed by digital systems. This conversion involves sampling, quantization, and encoding of the analog signal.
- Sampling is the process of taking a continuous analog signal and converting it into a discrete signal by taking samples at regular intervals. The frequency at which the samples are taken is called the sampling rate.
- Quantization is the process of converting the continuous range of the analog signal into a finite set of values. This is done by dividing the range of the analog signal into equal intervals and assigning a digital value to each interval.
- Encoding is the process of assigning digital codes to each quantized value. The most common encoding technique used is the binary encoding, where each quantized value is assigned a binary code.
- The accuracy of ADC depends on the resolution of the ADC. The resolution is determined by the number of bits used to represent each sample. The higher the resolution, the more accurate the ADC.
- ADC is used in various applications such as digital audio recording, digital image processing, and measurement systems. In wearable computing, ADC is used to convert the analog signals from sensors such as heart rate sensors, temperature sensors, and motion sensors into digital signals that can be processed by the wearable device.
- There are various types of ADC such as successive approximation ADC, flash ADC, and delta-sigma ADC. The choice of ADC depends on the application and the required accuracy.

In conclusion, ADC is a crucial process in the field of electronics, especially in wearable computing, mixed reality, and the internet of everything. It involves converting continuous analog signals into discrete digital signals that can be processed by digital systems. Understanding the basics of ADC and its applications is important for anyone working in these fields.
