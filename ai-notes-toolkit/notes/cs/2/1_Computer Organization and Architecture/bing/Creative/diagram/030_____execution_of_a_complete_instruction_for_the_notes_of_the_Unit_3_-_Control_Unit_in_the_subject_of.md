### Execution of a complete instruction

- The execution of a complete instruction involves two main phases: instruction fetch and instruction execution.
- Instruction fetch: The control unit fetches the instruction from the memory location pointed by the program counter (PC) and stores it in the instruction register (IR). The PC is then incremented by the size of the instruction to point to the next instruction. The control unit also decodes the instruction in the IR to determine the operation code (opcode) and the operands.
- Instruction execution: The control unit executes the instruction according to the opcode and the operands. The operands may be in the registers, in the memory, or in the instruction itself. The control unit may also generate control signals to the arithmetic logic unit (ALU), the memory, and the input/output devices to perform the required operations. The result of the execution may be stored in a register, in the memory, or sent to an output device. The control unit may also update the condition code register (CCR) to reflect the status of the execution, such as zero, negative, overflow, or carry flags.
- The instruction execution may be further divided into sub-phases, depending on the type and complexity of the instruction. For example, a typical instruction execution may consist of the following sub-phases: operand fetch, operand processing, result store, and branch completion.
- Operand fetch: The control unit fetches the operands from the registers, the memory, or the instruction itself, depending on the addressing mode of the instruction. The operands are stored in temporary registers for further processing.
- Operand processing: The control unit performs the required operation on the operands using the ALU, the memory, or the input/output devices. The operation may be arithmetic, logical, shift, rotate, compare, or transfer. The result of the operation is stored in another temporary register.
- Result store: The control unit stores the result of the operation in a register, in the memory, or sends it to an output device, depending on the destination of the instruction. The control unit may also update the CCR to reflect the status of the result.
- Branch completion: The control unit checks the CCR and the opcode to determine if the instruction is a branch instruction, and if the branch condition is satisfied. If so, the control unit updates the PC to the target address of the branch instruction. Otherwise, the PC remains unchanged and points to the next instruction.
