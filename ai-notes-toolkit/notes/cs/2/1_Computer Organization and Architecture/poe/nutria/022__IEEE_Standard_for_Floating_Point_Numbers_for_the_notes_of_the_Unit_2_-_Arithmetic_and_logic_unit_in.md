
### IEEE Standard for Floating Point Numbers

- Floating point numbers are a way of representing real numbers in a finite amount of space. 
- The IEEE 754 standard defines a 32-bit and a 64-bit floating point representation. 
- The 32-bit representation consists of three fields: a sign bit, an 8-bit exponent field, and a 23-bit mantissa field. 
- The 64-bit representation consists of four fields: a sign bit, an 11-bit exponent field, and a 52-bit mantissa field. 
- The sign bit indicates the sign of the number, with 0 indicating a positive number and 1 indicating a negative number. 
- The exponent field indicates the magnitude of the number, with the most significant bit indicating the sign of the exponent. 
- The mantissa field contains the actual number, with the most significant bit being implicit and always assumed to be 1. 
- The IEEE 754 standard also defines special values for NaN (not a number) and infinity. 
- This standard is used in most modern computers and is the basis for most programming languages' floating point arithmetic.
