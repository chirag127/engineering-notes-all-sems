### Optical Disks

Optical disks are a type of storage media that use laser technology to read and write data. These disks are commonly used for storing large amounts of data, such as music, videos, and software.

Here are some key points to understand about optical disks:

- Optical disks are made of a polycarbonate plastic substrate, a reflective layer, and a protective layer.
- There are several types of optical disks, including CD-ROM, DVD-ROM, Blu-ray, and HD-DVD.
- CD-ROMs can hold up to 700 MB of data, while DVDs can hold up to 4.7 GB of data.
- Blu-ray disks are capable of storing up to 25 GB of data on a single layer and up to 50 GB on a dual-layer disk.
- Optical disks are read by using a laser beam to read the reflective layer on the disk. The laser reads the data by detecting changes in the reflective layer caused by the presence or absence of pits or bumps on the disk surface.
- Optical disks are generally more durable than other types of media, such as magnetic disks, because they are not affected by magnetic fields.
- Optical disks are not suitable for high-speed access to data, as they have slower read and write speeds compared to other types of storage media.
- Optical disks are commonly used for distributing software, music, and videos, as they are a cost-effective and convenient way to distribute large amounts of data.

In conclusion, optical disks are an important type of storage media that are commonly used for storing large amounts of data. Understanding how they work and their limitations is essential for anyone studying computer organization and architecture.
