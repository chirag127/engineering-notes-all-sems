### Execution of a Complete Instruction - Unit 3: Control Unit 

In computer architecture and organization, the Control Unit (CU) is responsible for directing the flow of data and instructions within the Central Processing Unit (CPU). The execution of a complete instruction involves a series of steps that are performed by the CU. Let's take a look at the steps involved in executing a complete instruction:

1. **Fetch**: The first step in executing a complete instruction is to fetch the instruction from memory. The CU sends a request to the memory to fetch the instruction, which is then transferred to the CPU.

2. **Decode**: Once the instruction has been fetched, the CU decodes it to determine what operation needs to be performed. The CU uses the instruction's opcode to determine the operation and its operands to determine the data that needs to be used.

3. **Fetch Operands**: After decoding the instruction, the CU fetches any necessary operands from memory or registers. The operands are the data that the instruction will operate on.

4. **Execute**: With the instruction and operands in hand, the CU now executes the instruction. This involves performing the necessary operation on the operands.

5. **Store Result**: Once the instruction has been executed, the result is stored back into memory or a register. This result could be a value, an address, or a status flag.

6. **Next Instruction**: The final step in executing a complete instruction is to fetch the next instruction from memory and begin the process all over again. This continues until the program has completed or the CPU is halted.

In conclusion, the execution of a complete instruction involves a series of steps performed by the CU, including fetching the instruction, decoding it, fetching any necessary operands, executing the instruction, storing the result, and fetching the next instruction. Understanding these steps is crucial for understanding how a CPU operates and how programs are executed.
