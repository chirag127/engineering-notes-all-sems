### Multithreaded Systems

- A multithreaded system is a system that can execute multiple threads concurrently, where a thread is a unit of execution within a process  .
- A thread has its own program counter, stack, and registers, but shares the code, data, and resources of the process with other threads  .
- A multithreaded system can be of two types: user-level multithreading and kernel-level multithreading  .
- User-level multithreading is when the threads are managed by the user-level library, without the involvement of the kernel  .
- Kernel-level multithreading is when the threads are managed by the operating system, which can schedule them on different processors or cores  .
- The benefits of multithreading include increased responsiveness, resource sharing, economy, scalability, and parallelism   .
- The challenges of multithreading include synchronization, deadlock, starvation, race conditions, and security   .
- Multithreading is widely used in modern operating systems, such as Windows, Linux, macOS, Android, iOS, etc   .
