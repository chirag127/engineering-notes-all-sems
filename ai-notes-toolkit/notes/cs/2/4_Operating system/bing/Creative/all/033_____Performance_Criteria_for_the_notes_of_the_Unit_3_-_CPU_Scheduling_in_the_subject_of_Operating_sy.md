# Performance Criteria for CPU Scheduling

CPU scheduling is the process of selecting a process from the ready queue and allocating the CPU to it. CPU scheduling aims to optimize the performance of the system by maximizing the utilization of the CPU and minimizing the waiting time of the processes. There are different types of CPU scheduling algorithms, such as first-come first-served (FCFS), shortest job first (SJF), priority, round robin (RR), and multilevel queue (MLQ).

To compare and evaluate the performance of different CPU scheduling algorithms, we need to define some criteria that measure the effectiveness and efficiency of the algorithms. The criteria include the following :

- **CPU utilization**: The main objective of any CPU scheduling algorithm is to keep the CPU as busy as possible. CPU utilization is the percentage of time that the CPU is executing a process. Theoretically, CPU utilization can range from 0 to 100%, but in a real system, it varies from 40 to 90% depending on the load on the system .
- **Throughput**: A measure of the work done by the CPU is the number of processes that complete their execution per unit of time. Throughput is also known as the CPU service rate. Higher throughput means higher productivity of the system .
- **Turnaround time**: For a particular process, the turnaround time is the amount of time it takes from the moment the process arrives in the system to the moment it finishes its execution. Turnaround time includes the waiting time in the ready queue, the execution time on the CPU, and the I/O time. The average turnaround time is the average of the turnaround times of all the processes in the system. Lower average turnaround time means faster completion of the processes .
- **Waiting time**: For a particular process, the waiting time is the amount of time the process has been waiting in the ready queue before being allocated to the CPU. The average waiting time is the average of the waiting times of all the processes in the system. Lower average waiting time means less idle time for the processes .
- **Response time**: For a particular process, the response time is the amount of time it takes from the moment the process arrives in the system to the moment it gets its first response from the CPU. The response time is relevant for interactive processes that require immediate feedback from the system. The average response time is the average of the response times of all the interactive processes in the system. Lower average response time means better user satisfaction .

Different CPU scheduling algorithms may have different impacts on these criteria. For example, FCFS is simple and fair, but it may have high average waiting time and low CPU utilization. SJF is optimal for minimizing the average waiting time, but it may cause starvation for long processes. RR is suitable for achieving low average response time, but it may have high average turnaround time and context switching overhead. MLQ can handle different types of processes with different priorities, but it may be complex and difficult to implement. Therefore, there is no single best CPU scheduling algorithm for all situations, and the choice of the algorithm depends on the goals and requirements of the system.
