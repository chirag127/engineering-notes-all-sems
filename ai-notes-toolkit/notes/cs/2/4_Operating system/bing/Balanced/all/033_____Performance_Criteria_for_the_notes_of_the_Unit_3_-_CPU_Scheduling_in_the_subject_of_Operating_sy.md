# Performance Criteria for CPU Scheduling

CPU scheduling is the process of selecting a process from the ready queue and allocating the CPU to it. CPU scheduling algorithms aim to optimize the performance of the system by maximizing the CPU utilization, minimizing the waiting time, and increasing the throughput. Different CPU scheduling algorithms have different advantages and disadvantages, and they can be evaluated based on the following criteria   :

- **CPU utilization**: This is the percentage of time the CPU is busy executing processes. A high CPU utilization means that the CPU is not idle and is efficiently using its resources. A low CPU utilization means that the CPU is wasting time and is underutilized. The CPU utilization can range from 0% to 100%, but in a real system, it varies from 40% to 90% depending on the system load.
- **Throughput**: This is the number of processes that complete their execution per unit of time. A high throughput means that the CPU is able to process more processes in a given time interval. A low throughput means that the CPU is slow and is not able to handle the workload. The throughput can be measured in processes per second, processes per hour, or processes per day.
- **Turnaround time**: This is the amount of time it takes for a process to finish its execution, from the time it is submitted to the system until the time it is terminated. The turnaround time includes the waiting time, the CPU time, and the I/O time of the process. A low turnaround time means that the process is completed quickly and the system is responsive. A high turnaround time means that the process is delayed and the system is sluggish. The turnaround time can be measured in seconds, minutes, or hours.
- **Waiting time**: This is the amount of time a process spends in the ready queue, waiting for its turn to be executed by the CPU. The waiting time does not include the CPU time or the I/O time of the process. A low waiting time means that the process does not have to wait long and the system is fair. A high waiting time means that the process has to wait long and the system is unfair. The waiting time can be measured in seconds, minutes, or hours.
- **Response time**: This is the amount of time it takes for the system to respond to a request from a process, from the time the request is made until the time the first response is produced. The response time does not include the time it takes to complete the request. The response time is important for interactive processes that need immediate feedback from the system. A low response time means that the system is responsive and the user is satisfied. A high response time means that the system is unresponsive and the user is frustrated. The response time can be measured in seconds, milliseconds, or microseconds.

These criteria are often conflicting, and there is no single CPU scheduling algorithm that can optimize all of them. Therefore, the choice of a CPU scheduling algorithm depends on the goals and requirements of the system and the processes. Some systems may prioritize CPU utilization and throughput, while others may prioritize turnaround time and response time. Some processes may be CPU-bound, while others may be I/O-bound. Some processes may be long, while others may be short. Some processes may be periodic, while others may be aperiodic. Some processes may be preemptive, while others may be non-preemptive. All these factors affect the performance of the CPU scheduling algorithm and the system.
