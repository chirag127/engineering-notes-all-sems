### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that deals with creating machines or software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, etc.
- AI can be classified into different types based on the degree of human-like intelligence, the scope of the problem domain, and the approach or technique used to solve the problem.
- Some of the types of AI are:
  - Weak AI or Narrow AI: This type of AI can only perform a specific task or a narrow range of tasks within a predefined domain. It does not have general intelligence or understanding of the world. Examples of weak AI are chatbots, virtual assistants, self-driving cars, etc.
  - Strong AI or General AI: This type of AI can perform any intellectual task that a human can do. It has general intelligence and can understand and reason about the world. Examples of strong AI are hypothetical or fictional, such as HAL from 2001: A Space Odyssey, Skynet from Terminator, etc.
  - Artificial Superintelligence (ASI): This type of AI can surpass human intelligence and capabilities in all domains. It can create and improve itself without human intervention. Examples of ASI are also hypothetical or fictional, such as The Matrix, Transcendence, etc.
  - Narrow Domain AI: This type of AI can solve problems within a specific domain or area of knowledge, such as chess, medicine, finance, etc. It may use different techniques or methods depending on the domain. Examples of narrow domain AI are IBM Watson, AlphaGo, etc.
  - Broad Domain AI: This type of AI can solve problems across multiple domains or areas of knowledge, such as common sense, creativity, social skills, etc. It may use a combination of techniques or methods from different domains. Examples of broad domain AI are OpenAI GPT-3, Google Duplex, etc.
  - Symbolic AI: This type of AI uses symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge. It relies on logic, search, and inference to solve problems. It is also known as classical AI or rule-based AI. Examples of symbolic AI are expert systems, logic programming, etc.
  - Subsymbolic AI: This type of AI uses numerical or statistical methods to represent and manipulate knowledge. It relies on learning, adaptation, and emergence to solve problems. It is also known as connectionist AI or neural network AI. Examples of subsymbolic AI are artificial neural networks, deep learning, evolutionary algorithms, etc.
  - Hybrid AI: This type of AI uses a combination of symbolic and subsymbolic methods to represent and manipulate knowledge. It tries to overcome the limitations of both approaches and achieve the best of both worlds. Examples of hybrid AI are neural-symbolic systems, cognitive architectures, etc.
