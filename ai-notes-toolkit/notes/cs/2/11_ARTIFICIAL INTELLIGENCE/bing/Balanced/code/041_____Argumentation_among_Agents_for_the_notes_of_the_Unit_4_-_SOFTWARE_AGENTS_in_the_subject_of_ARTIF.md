### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify decisions, explain actions, and learn from each other.
- Argumentation can be formalized using various logical frameworks, such as abstract argumentation, structured argumentation, or probabilistic argumentation, that define the structure, semantics, and dynamics of arguments and their relations.
- Argumentation can be implemented using various computational techniques, such as argumentation-based dialogue protocols, argumentation schemes, argument mining, or argument visualization, that enable agents to construct, exchange, and evaluate arguments in different domains and contexts.
- Argumentation is an interdisciplinary area of research that draws on insights from philosophy, logic, linguistics, psychology, and computer science, and has applications in artificial intelligence, multi-agent systems, natural language processing, decision support, and education.
