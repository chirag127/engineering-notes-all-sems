# Trust and Reputation in Multi-agent systems

- Trust and reputation are important concepts for effective interactions in open multi-agent systems (MAS), where agents may belong to different owners and have different goals and behaviors.
- Trust is the degree of belief that an agent has about the reliability, honesty, or competence of another agent, based on its own direct experiences or observations.
- Reputation is the collective opinion or evaluation of an agent by a group of other agents, based on their indirect experiences or reports.
- Trust and reputation can help agents to select suitable partners, avoid malicious or incompetent agents, and cope with uncertainty and risk in dynamic and complex environments.
- Several models and mechanisms have been proposed in the literature to represent, compute, and update trust and reputation in MAS, based on different assumptions, criteria, and methods.
- Some of the well-known models are:
  - Marsh's model: A formal model of trust that defines trust as a function of basic, general, and situational trust, and uses a calculus of trust to reason about trust relationships.
  - Sen et al.'s model: A computational model of trust that uses a Bayesian network to estimate the probability of an agent being trustworthy, based on its past actions and outcomes.
  - SPORAS: A reputation system that uses a simple scoring algorithm to assign reputation scores to agents, based on the feedback from other agents, and adjusts the scores according to the frequency and recency of interactions.
  - eBay's system: A reputation system that uses a feedback mechanism to collect positive, negative, or neutral ratings from buyers and sellers, and computes the net reputation score as the difference between positive and negative ratings.
  - Certified reputation: A reputation system that uses a third-party certification authority to verify and certify the reputation of agents, based on their credentials and performance.
  - REGRET: An integrated trust and reputation model that combines individual, social, and ontological aspects of trust and reputation, and uses a fuzzy logic approach to aggregate and evaluate different sources of information.
  - FIRE: A flexible and interoperable trust and reputation model that allows agents to use different trust and reputation models and mechanisms, depending on their preferences and contexts, and provides a common framework for interoperability and communication.
