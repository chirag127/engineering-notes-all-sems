## Unit 5 - APPLICATIONS

- This unit covers some of the practical applications of artificial intelligence in various domains and industries.
- The applications include natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, robotics, expert systems, gaming, and machine learning.
- The unit also discusses some of the ethical, social, and legal implications of artificial intelligence, such as privacy, bias, accountability, and human dignity.

### Natural Language Processing
- Natural language processing (NLP) is the branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the analysis, understanding, and generation of natural languages, such as English, Chinese, or Hindi.
- NLP enables computers to perform tasks such as translation, summarization, sentiment analysis, question answering, dialogue systems, and information extraction.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in NLP are parsing, tokenization, lemmatization, stemming, part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, word embeddings, n-grams, bag-of-words, tf-idf, and neural networks.

### Computer Vision
- Computer vision is the branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the acquisition, processing, and understanding of visual information, such as images and videos.
- Computer vision enables computers to perform tasks such as face recognition, object detection, scene segmentation, optical character recognition, image synthesis, and video analysis.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in computer vision are edge detection, feature extraction, convolutional neural networks, deep learning, generative adversarial networks, and transfer learning.

### Speech Recognition
- Speech recognition is the branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the recognition and conversion of spoken language into text or commands.
- Speech recognition enables computers to perform tasks such as voice assistants, speech-to-text, voice search, voice authentication, and speech synthesis.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in speech recognition are acoustic modeling, language modeling, hidden Markov models, neural networks, and natural language understanding.

### Robotics
- Robotics is the branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the design, construction, and operation of machines that can perform physical tasks, such as manipulation, locomotion, and interaction.
- Robotics enables computers to perform tasks such as industrial automation, autonomous vehicles, household robots, medical robots, and social robots.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in robotics are sensors, actuators, kinematics, dynamics, control, planning, navigation, and reinforcement learning.

### Expert Systems
- Expert systems are computer programs that can provide expert-level knowledge and advice in a specific domain, such as medicine, law, or engineering.
- Expert systems enable computers to perform tasks such as diagnosis, prescription, consultation, and decision making.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in expert systems are knowledge base, inference engine, rule-based reasoning, forward chaining, backward chaining, and uncertainty handling.

### Gaming
- Gaming is the branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the creation and evaluation of computer games, such as chess, checkers, or Go.
- Gaming enables computers to perform tasks such as game design, game playing, game analysis, and game generation.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in gaming are search algorithms, minimax, alpha-beta pruning, Monte Carlo tree search, reinforcement learning, and procedural content generation.

### Machine Learning
- Machine learning is the branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the learning and improvement of computer programs from data and experience, without explicit programming.
- Machine learning enables computers to perform tasks such as classification, regression, clustering, anomaly detection, recommendation, and natural language generation.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in machine learning are supervised learning, unsupervised learning, semi-supervised learning, reinforcement learning, linear regression, logistic regression, k-means, support vector machines, decision trees, random forests, neural networks, and deep learning.
