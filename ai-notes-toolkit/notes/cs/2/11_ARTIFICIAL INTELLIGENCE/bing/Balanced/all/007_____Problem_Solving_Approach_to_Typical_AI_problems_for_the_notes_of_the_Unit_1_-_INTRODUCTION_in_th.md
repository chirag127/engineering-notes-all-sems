# Problem Solving Approach to Typical AI Problems

- Problem solving in AI is the process of finding solutions to complex or ill-defined problems by applying knowledge, heuristics, algorithms, and reasoning techniques.
- Problem solving in AI can be classified into two types: goal-based and data-driven.
  - Goal-based problem solving involves defining a goal state and finding a sequence of actions that can transform the initial state into the goal state. Examples of goal-based problems are path finding, planning, and game playing.
  - Data-driven problem solving involves analyzing a given data set and finding patterns, insights, or predictions from it. Examples of data-driven problems are classification, clustering, regression, and recommendation.
- Problem solving in AI can also be categorized based on the characteristics of the problem domain, such as deterministic or stochastic, fully observable or partially observable, discrete or continuous, static or dynamic, single-agent or multi-agent, and so on.
- Problem solving in AI requires the following steps:
  - Problem formulation: This step involves defining the problem in terms of states, actions, and costs. A state is a representation of the situation or the environment. An action is a transition from one state to another. A cost is a measure of the desirability of an action or a state.
  - Problem representation: This step involves choosing a suitable data structure or a model to represent the problem and its solution. Some common representations are graphs, trees, matrices, logic, and neural networks.
  - Problem solving method: This step involves selecting and applying an appropriate algorithm or a technique to solve the problem. Some common methods are search, optimization, inference, learning, and simulation.
  - Solution evaluation: This step involves assessing the quality and the validity of the solution. Some common criteria are optimality, completeness, correctness, efficiency, and robustness.
