# Forward Chaining

- Forward chaining is a method of reasoning in artificial intelligence in which inference rules are applied to existing data to extract additional data until an endpoint (goal) is achieved .
- In this type of chaining, the inference engine starts by evaluating existing facts, derivations, and conditions before deducing new information .
- Forward chaining is also known as bottom-up reasoning or data-driven reasoning, as it starts from the bottom (data) and moves up to the top (conclusion)  .
- Forward chaining is often used in expert systems, which are AI programs that simulate the knowledge and reasoning of human experts in a specific domain .
- An example of forward chaining is a medical diagnosis system that uses rules such as "if the patient has a fever and a cough, then they may have a cold" to infer possible diagnoses from the symptoms of the patient .
- The advantages of forward chaining are that it can handle incomplete or uncertain data, and that it can generate new knowledge that was not explicitly stated in the rules .
- The disadvantages of forward chaining are that it can be inefficient, as it may explore irrelevant or redundant paths, and that it may not find a solution if the goal is not well-defined or reachable .
