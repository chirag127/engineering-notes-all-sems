# Information Extraction

Information extraction (IE) is the process of automatically extracting structured information from unstructured or semi-structured text documents. It is an important application of artificial intelligence (AI) that can enable various tasks such as data analysis, decision making, knowledge discovery, and information retrieval.

Some of the main components of information extraction are:

- **Document analysis**: This involves preprocessing the documents to identify the format, layout, language, and structure of the text. For example, optical character recognition (OCR) can be used to convert scanned images of documents into machine-readable text.
- **Named entity recognition (NER)**: This involves identifying and classifying the entities mentioned in the text, such as persons, organizations, locations, dates, etc. For example, NER can be used to extract the names of the authors and the publication date from a research paper.
- **Relation extraction (RE)**: This involves identifying and classifying the relations between the entities in the text, such as affiliation, ownership, causation, etc. For example, RE can be used to extract the relation between a company and its subsidiaries from a business report.
- **Event extraction (EE)**: This involves identifying and classifying the events that occur in the text, such as transactions, meetings, conflicts, etc. For example, EE can be used to extract the events related to a stock market crash from a news article.
- **Template filling (TF)**: This involves filling a predefined template or schema with the extracted information, such as a database table, a spreadsheet, or a graph. For example, TF can be used to fill a table with the information extracted from a resume.

Some of the applications of information extraction are:

- **Banking**: Information extraction can be used to digitize and process paper-based records, such as invoices, receipts, contracts, etc. It can also be used to analyze and interpret the information and make decisions, such as fraud detection, credit scoring, risk assessment, etc.  
- **Healthcare**: Information extraction can be used to extract and analyze information from medical records, such as diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results, etc. It can also be used to support clinical decision making, such as diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, etc. 
- **Legal**: Information extraction can be used to extract and analyze information from legal documents, such as contracts, agreements, patents, etc. It can also be used to support legal tasks, such as contract review, compliance checking, litigation support, etc. 
- **Education**: Information extraction can be used to extract and analyze information from educational documents, such as textbooks, lectures, assignments, etc. It can also be used to support learning tasks, such as summarization, question answering, feedback, etc. 
- **Media**: Information extraction can be used to extract and analyze information from media documents, such as news articles, social media posts, podcasts, etc. It can also be used to support media tasks, such as sentiment analysis, topic detection, recommendation, etc.
