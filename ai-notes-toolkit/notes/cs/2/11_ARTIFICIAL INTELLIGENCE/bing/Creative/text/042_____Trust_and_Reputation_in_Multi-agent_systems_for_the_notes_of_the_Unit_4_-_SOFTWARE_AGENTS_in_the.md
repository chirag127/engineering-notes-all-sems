### Trust and Reputation in Multi-agent systems

- Trust and reputation are important concepts for effective interactions in open multi-agent systems (MAS), where agents may have different goals, preferences, and behaviors, and may enter and leave the system dynamically .
- Trust is the subjective belief that an agent has about the expected behavior of another agent in a given context . Trust can be based on direct experiences, recommendations from other agents, or default values.
- Reputation is the collective opinion that a group of agents has about the behavior of another agent, based on their own or others' experiences . Reputation can be used to infer the trustworthiness of an agent that has not been encountered before, or to update the trust level after an interaction.
- Trust and reputation models aim to provide mechanisms for agents to evaluate, maintain, and share information about the trustworthiness and reputation of other agents, and to use this information to guide their decisions and actions .
- There are many different trust and reputation models proposed in the literature, each with different assumptions, definitions, and methods  . Some of the factors that influence the design of trust and reputation models are:
  - The type and source of information used to compute trust and reputation, such as direct observations, third-party reports, or social networks.
  - The representation and aggregation of trust and reputation values, such as numerical, probabilistic, or fuzzy.
  - The dynamics and update of trust and reputation values, such as how to handle uncertainty, inconsistency, and change over time.
  - The dissemination and communication of trust and reputation information, such as how to share, verify, and protect the information from manipulation or deception.
  - The application and use of trust and reputation information, such as how to select partners, negotiate contracts, or coordinate actions.
- Trust and reputation models can enhance the performance, robustness, and resilience of multi-agent systems, by enabling agents to cope with uncertainty, risk, and diversity, and to avoid or mitigate malicious or faulty behaviors .
