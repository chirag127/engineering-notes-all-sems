### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify decisions, explain actions, and coordinate plans.
- Argumentation can also provide a basis for defining the semantics of logic programs, natural language, and legal reasoning.
- Argumentation in multi-agent systems is a subfield of artificial intelligence that studies how agents can engage in argumentative dialogues, following different protocols, strategies, and formats.
- Argumentation in multi-agent systems can be classified into three main types: abstract, structured, and bipolar.
  - Abstract argumentation focuses on the relations of attack and defeat among arguments, without considering their internal structure or content.
  - Structured argumentation considers the logical structure and content of arguments, and how they can be constructed, compared, and evaluated.
  - Bipolar argumentation extends structured argumentation by distinguishing between two types of relations among arguments: support and attack.
- Argumentation in multi-agent systems can be applied to various domains and scenarios, such as e-commerce, e-government, e-health, e-democracy, e-learning, and e-science.
