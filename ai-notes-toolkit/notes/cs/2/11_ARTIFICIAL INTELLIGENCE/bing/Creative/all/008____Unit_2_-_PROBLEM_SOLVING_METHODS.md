## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- Problem solving methods are strategies or techniques that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until finding one that works.
  - Algorithm: Following a step-by-step procedure that guarantees a correct solution.
  - Heuristic: Using a rule of thumb or a shortcut that may not guarantee a correct solution, but can help to find one quickly.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a large problem into smaller and simpler subproblems, and solving them recursively.
  - Backtracking: Exploring different paths in a search space, and going back to a previous state if a dead end is reached.
  - Hill climbing: Moving from one solution to another that is slightly better, until reaching a local or global optimum.
  - Simulated annealing: Combining hill climbing with random jumps to escape local optima and explore the search space more widely.
  - Genetic algorithm: Using a population of candidate solutions that evolve through selection, crossover, and mutation, to find an optimal or near-optimal solution.
  - Constraint satisfaction: Finding a solution that satisfies a set of constraints or conditions.
  - Means-ends analysis: Comparing the current state with the goal state, and finding an action that reduces the difference between them.
  - Analogical reasoning: Using a similar problem or situation that has been solved before, and applying its solution to the current problem.
  - Expert system: Using a knowledge base and an inference engine that can reason and provide solutions based on the domain expertise.
