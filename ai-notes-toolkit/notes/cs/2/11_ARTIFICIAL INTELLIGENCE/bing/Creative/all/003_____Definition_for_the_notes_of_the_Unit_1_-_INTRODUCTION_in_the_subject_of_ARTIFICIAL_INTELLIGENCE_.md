# Definition

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that deals with creating machines or software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, etc.
- AI can be classified into two main categories: weak AI and strong AI. Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the type of AI that is designed to perform a specific task or function, such as playing chess, driving a car, or diagnosing a disease. Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the type of AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can do, such as understanding natural language, generating novel ideas, or exhibiting self-awareness. Weak AI is currently more prevalent and achievable than strong AI, which is still a hypothetical and elusive goal.
- AI can also be classified into four main types based on the degree of human-like intelligence and rationality they exhibit: systems that think like humans, systems that act like humans, systems that think rationally, and systems that act rationally. Systems that think like humans are the ones that try to mimic the cognitive processes and structures of the human mind, such as neural networks, expert systems, or natural language processing. Systems that act like humans are the ones that try to behave in a way that is indistinguishable from a human, such as robots, chatbots, or speech recognition. Systems that think rationally are the ones that try to follow the principles of logic, mathematics, or optimization, such as theorem provers, decision support systems, or machine learning. Systems that act rationally are the ones that try to achieve the best possible outcome given the available information and resources, such as planning systems, game-playing systems, or autonomous agents.
