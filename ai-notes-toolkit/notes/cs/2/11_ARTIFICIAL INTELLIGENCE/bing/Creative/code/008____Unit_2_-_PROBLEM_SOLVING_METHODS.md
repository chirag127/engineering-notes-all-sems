## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given situation or task that meets some criteria or constraints.
- Problem solving methods are systematic ways of approaching and solving problems, often involving some steps or stages.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until one works or the problem is solved.
  - Heuristics: Using rules of thumb, intuition, or experience to guide the search for solutions.
  - Algorithms: Following a precise and well-defined sequence of steps or instructions to solve the problem.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved more easily.
  - Backtracking: Retracing the steps or decisions made in the problem solving process and trying alternative paths or choices.
  - Recursive: Solving a problem by reducing it to a simpler or smaller version of itself, until a base case is reached that can be solved directly.
  - Brute force: Searching exhaustively through all possible solutions or combinations until the optimal one is found.
  - Greedy: Choosing the best or most promising option at each step or stage of the problem solving process, without considering the long-term consequences or outcomes.
  - Dynamic programming: Solving a problem by storing and reusing the solutions to overlapping subproblems that have been solved before, to avoid recomputation and save time.
  - Branch and bound: Pruning or eliminating the search space of possible solutions by using some criteria or bounds to discard suboptimal or infeasible ones.
