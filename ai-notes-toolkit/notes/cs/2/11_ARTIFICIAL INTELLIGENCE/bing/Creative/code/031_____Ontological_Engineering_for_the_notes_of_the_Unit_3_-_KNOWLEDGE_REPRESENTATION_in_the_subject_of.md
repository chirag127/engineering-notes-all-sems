# Ontological Engineering

- Ontological Engineering is a field of study that deals with the creation and management of ontologies, which are formal and explicit specifications of the concepts, properties and relations in a domain of interest .
- Ontologies are used to provide a common vocabulary and a shared understanding of a domain, to enable interoperability and integration of heterogeneous data and systems, and to support reasoning and inference over the domain knowledge .
- Ontological Engineering involves the following activities:
  - Ontology development: the process of defining the ontology components, such as classes, properties, axioms, rules, annotations, etc., using a suitable ontology language and tool.
  - Ontology evaluation: the process of assessing the quality, correctness, completeness, consistency, and usability of an ontology, using various criteria and methods, such as logical analysis, empirical testing, peer review, etc.
  - Ontology alignment: the process of finding correspondences and mappings between different ontologies that cover the same or related domains, to enable data exchange and integration.
  - Ontology evolution: the process of updating and maintaining an ontology over time, to reflect changes in the domain or the requirements of the users, using methods such as versioning, change management, etc.
  - Ontology reuse: the process of reusing existing ontologies or parts of them, to avoid duplication of effort and to leverage existing knowledge, using methods such as ontology libraries, modules, patterns, etc.
  - Ontology learning: the process of extracting ontological knowledge from various sources, such as natural language texts, databases, schemas, etc., using methods such as natural language processing, machine learning, etc.
- Ontological Engineering requires the use of methods and methodologies that guide the ontology development process, such as the following:
  - Ontology design principles: the best practices and recommendations for building ontologies, such as clarity, coherence, extendibility, minimal encoding bias, minimal ontological commitment, etc.
  - Ontology development methodologies: the systematic and structured approaches for planning, executing, and managing the ontology development process, such as METHONTOLOGY, NeOn, etc.
  - Ontology languages: the formal and standardized languages for representing and exchanging ontologies, such as RDF, OWL, etc.
  - Ontology tools: the software applications that support the ontology development activities, such as ontology editors, reasoners, validators, etc.
