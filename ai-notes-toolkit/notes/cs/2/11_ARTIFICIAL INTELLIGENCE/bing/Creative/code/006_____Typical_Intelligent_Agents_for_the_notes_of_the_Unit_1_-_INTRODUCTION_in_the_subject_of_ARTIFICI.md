# Typical Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is an autonomous entity that acts upon an environment using sensors and actuators to achieve its goals .
- An intelligent agent may also learn from the environment to improve its performance.
- Examples of intelligent agents are driverless cars, virtual assistants, chatbots, robots, etc .
- There are different types of intelligent agents based on their degree of rationality, observability, learning ability, and goal-directedness  .
- The main types of intelligent agents are:
  - **Simple reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, without considering the history of percepts or the consequences of their actions. They are the simplest and most limited type of agents. For example, a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents maintain an internal state of the environment based on the history of percepts and actions. They use this state to choose the best action for the current situation. They are more flexible and adaptive than simple reflex agents. For example, a driverless car that uses a map and sensors to navigate the road.
  - **Goal-based agents**: These agents have a set of goals that they want to achieve, and they act based on the expected outcomes of their actions. They are more intelligent and proactive than reflex agents. For example, a chess-playing agent that chooses the best move to win the game.
  - **Utility-based agents**: These agents have a utility function that measures how desirable a state is, and they act to maximize their expected utility. They are more rational and optimal than goal-based agents. For example, a stock-trading agent that buys and sells shares to maximize its profit.
  - **Learning agents**: These agents have the ability to learn from their experience and improve their performance over time. They consist of four components: a learning element, a performance element, a critic, and a problem generator. They are the most advanced and complex type of agents. For example, a virtual assistant that learns from user feedback and adapts to user preferences.
