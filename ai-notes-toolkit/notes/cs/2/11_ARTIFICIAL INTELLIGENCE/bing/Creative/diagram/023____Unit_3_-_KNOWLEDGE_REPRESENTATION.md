## Unit 3 - Knowledge Representation

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding and storing information in a form that can be used by an intelligent system, such as an AI agent.
- Knowledge representation aims to capture the meaning, structure, and context of the information, as well as the reasoning and inference capabilities of the system.
- Knowledge representation can be divided into two main categories: declarative and procedural.
  - Declarative knowledge representation focuses on what is true or false in the world, such as facts, rules, concepts, and categories. Examples of declarative knowledge representation are logic, semantic networks, frames, and ontologies.
  - Procedural knowledge representation focuses on how to do something or achieve a goal, such as actions, plans, strategies, and skills. Examples of procedural knowledge representation are production systems, scripts, and planning systems.
- Knowledge representation can also be classified based on the level of abstraction, granularity, and expressiveness of the information, such as propositional, first-order, second-order, and higher-order logic.
- Knowledge representation can also be evaluated based on the criteria of adequacy, efficiency, and flexibility.
  - Adequacy refers to how well the representation captures the essential features and relationships of the domain.
  - Efficiency refers to how fast and easy the representation can be processed and manipulated by the system.
  - Flexibility refers to how adaptable and extendable the representation is to new information and changes in the domain.
- Knowledge representation is an important and challenging task in AI, as it requires a balance between the complexity and the usefulness of the information, as well as the trade-offs between the different types and criteria of representation.
