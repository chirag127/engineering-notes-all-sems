## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- Problem solving methods are strategies or techniques that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until finding one that works.
  - Algorithm: Following a step-by-step procedure that guarantees a correct solution.
  - Heuristic: Using a rule of thumb or a shortcut that may not guarantee a correct solution, but can speed up the search process.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a large problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved separately.
  - Backtracking: Going back to a previous state or decision point when the current solution path leads to a dead end or a suboptimal result.
  - Recursion: Solving a problem by using the same method to solve a smaller or simpler version of the same problem.
  - Brute force: Searching exhaustively through all possible solutions until finding the optimal one.
  - Greedy: Choosing the best option available at each step, without considering the long-term consequences.
  - Dynamic programming: Storing and reusing the results of subproblems that have been solved before, to avoid recomputation.
  - Branch and bound: Pruning the search space by eliminating suboptimal or infeasible solutions based on some criteria or bounds.

- Problem solving methods can be classified into two categories:

  - Deterministic: The methods that always produce the same output for a given input, and guarantee a correct solution if one exists.
  - Non-deterministic: The methods that may produce different outputs for a given input, and do not guarantee a correct solution, but may find a good approximation or a satisfactory solution.

- Problem solving methods can be applied to different types of problems, such as:

  - Well-defined: The problems that have a clear goal, a known initial state, and a set of rules or constraints that define the possible actions and solutions.
  - Ill-defined: The problems that have a vague or ambiguous goal, an unknown or uncertain initial state, and no clear rules or constraints that define the possible actions and solutions.
  - Static: The problems that do not change over time, and the actions and solutions are independent of the time factor.
  - Dynamic: The problems that change over time, and the actions and solutions depend on the time factor.
  - Single-agent: The problems that involve only one entity that can perform actions and seek solutions.
  - Multi-agent: The problems that involve more than one entity that can perform actions and seek solutions, and may cooperate or compete with each other.
