# Typical Intelligent Agents

An intelligent agent is a system that can perceive its environment, reason, learn, and act autonomously to achieve its goals. There are several types of intelligent agents, including:

1. **Simple reflex agents**: These agents select actions based on the current percept, ignoring the rest of the percept history. They operate using condition-action rules, also known as production rules or "if-then" rules.

2. **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents maintain an internal model of the world, which is used to choose actions based on how the world is expected to evolve. The internal model is updated based on the agent's percepts.

3. **Goal-based agents**: These agents choose actions based on their goals, which are desired states of the world. They use a process called goal formulation to generate specific goals based on their high-level desires.

4. **Utility-based agents**: These agents choose actions based on a utility function, which assigns a numerical value to each possible state of the world. The agent's goal is to maximize its utility.

5. **Learning agents**: These agents can improve their performance over time by learning from their experiences. They use techniques such as reinforcement learning, supervised learning, and unsupervised learning to adapt to their environment.

These are some of the typical intelligent agents that are studied in the field of artificial intelligence. Each type of agent has its strengths and limitations, and the choice of agent depends on the specific problem at hand.
