### Forward Chaining

- Forward chaining is a popular inference method used in artificial intelligence and expert systems.
- It is a data-driven approach that starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data until a goal is reached.
- Forward chaining is also known as bottom-up reasoning, as it starts with the facts and works its way up to the conclusion.
- In forward chaining, the system iteratively applies the rules to the known facts to derive new facts.
- The process continues until no new facts can be derived or the goal is reached.
- Forward chaining is commonly used in rule-based systems, where the rules are represented as if-then statements.
- The if part of the rule is called the antecedent and the then part is called the consequent.
- In forward chaining, the system checks if the antecedent of a rule is satisfied by the known facts. If it is, the consequent is added to the list of known facts.
- Forward chaining is a complete and sound inference method, meaning that it will find all the possible solutions and the solutions it finds are correct.
- However, it can be inefficient in some cases, as it may generate a large number of irrelevant facts before reaching the goal.
