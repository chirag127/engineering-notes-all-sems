### Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method used in artificial intelligence for knowledge representation and reasoning. It is a type of inference engine that starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to find the evidence or facts that support it. This method is commonly used in expert systems and rule-based systems.

Here are some key points to remember about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to find the evidence or facts that support it.
2. It is a type of inference engine commonly used in expert systems and rule-based systems.
3. Backward chaining is useful when the goal is known but the path to reach it is not clear.
4. It is a depth-first search algorithm that explores all possible paths until a solution is found.
5. Backward chaining can be computationally expensive, as it may require exploring many possible paths before finding a solution.
