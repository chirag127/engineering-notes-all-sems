### Backward Chaining
Backward chaining is a method used in artificial intelligence for automated reasoning and problem-solving. It is a type of inference engine that works by starting with the goal or conclusion and working backward to determine the facts or evidence that support it. This method is commonly used in expert systems and rule-based systems.

Here are some key points to remember about backward chaining:
- Backward chaining starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to determine the facts or evidence that support it.
- It is a type of inference engine commonly used in expert systems and rule-based systems.
- Backward chaining is useful when the goal is known but the path to achieving it is not clear.
- It can be used to solve problems by breaking them down into smaller subgoals and working backward to determine the necessary steps to achieve the overall goal.
- Backward chaining can be contrasted with forward chaining, which starts with the known facts and works forward to determine the conclusions that can be drawn from them.
