### Optimal Decisions in Games

- Games involve making decisions to achieve a goal. The decisions are made to maximize the reward or minimize the cost.
- The optimal decision is the one that leads to the best possible outcome. To find the optimal decision, we evaluate all possible decisions and their outcomes and choose the one with the maximum reward or minimum cost.
- Games can be classified into two types:
    - Zero-sum games: The reward for one player is the cost for the other player. The sum of all rewards is zero. For example, chess is a zero-sum game.
    - Non-zero-sum games: The reward or cost for one player does not necessarily lead to the equal and opposite reward or cost for the other player. The sum of all rewards can be non-zero. For example, collaborative games are non-zero-sum games.
- To find the optimal decision in games, we can use:
    - Minimax algorithm: For zero-sum games, to find the decision that minimizes the maximum possible loss.
    - Alpha-beta pruning: An optimization technique for the minimax algorithm to reduce the search space.
    - Expectiminimax algorithm: For non-zero-sum games, to find the decision that maximizes the minimum expected utility.
    - Recursive algorithms: As the number of decisions and outcomes increase, the algorithms become computationally expensive. Pruning techniques and approximations are used to overcome this.
