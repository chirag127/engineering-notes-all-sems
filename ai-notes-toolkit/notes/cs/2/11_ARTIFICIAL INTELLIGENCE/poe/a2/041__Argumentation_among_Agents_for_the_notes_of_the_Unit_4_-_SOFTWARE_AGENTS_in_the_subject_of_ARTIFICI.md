### Argumentation among Agents

- Software agents may have conflicting goals and constraints, leading to disagreement on the best course of action.
- Agents need to resolve such conflicts through rational argumentation and negotiation.
- Argumentation is a process where agents exchange reasons and counter-reasons to persuade each other and reach an agreement.
- Dung's framework for argumentation is a popular approach wherein arguments are structured in a graph, and acceptable/unacceptable arguments are deduced using properties like consistency, attackedness, and conflict-freeness.
- Arguments can be based on priorities, probabilities, beliefs, moral values, etc. The key is to provide justifications for arguments and counter-arguments.
- Challenges in agent argumentation include efficiency of the process, handling imperfect/incomplete information, dealing with rhetorical/emotional arguments, managing complexity, etc.
- Applications include decision support systems, coordinating autonomous systems, etc. Automated argument assistants can help evaluate user arguments and generate counter-arguments.
