### Forward Chaining for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS.

1. Forward chaining is a inference method where inferences are made from the known facts to reach a goal. It starts with the known facts and moves forward to derive new conclusions.
2. It is also known as data-driven or deductive inference. The inference engine matches the conditions of the IF part of the rules with the known facts to find applicable rules.
3. The inferred facts are derived as conclusions and added to the knowledge base. This process is repeated until a goal is reached or no more applicable rules can be fired.
4. Forward chaining is useful when the goal is not precisely defined. It searches through the inference space and derives all possible conclusions. It is used in expert systems, automated reasoning, etc.
5. The disadvantages are that it may take a long time for the inferences to complete and it may derive incorrect conclusions if the knowledge base contains incorrect information or inappropriate rules.
