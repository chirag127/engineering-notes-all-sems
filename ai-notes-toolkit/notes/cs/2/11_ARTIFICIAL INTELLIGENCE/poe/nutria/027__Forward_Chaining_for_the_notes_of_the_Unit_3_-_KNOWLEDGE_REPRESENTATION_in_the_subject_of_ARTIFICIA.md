
### Forward Chaining

Forward chaining is a type of logical reasoning used in Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems to draw inferences from given facts and rules. It is a form of data-driven reasoning, where the AI system starts with a set of facts and rules and then works its way forward to draw conclusions.

In forward chaining, the AI system begins with a set of known facts and then uses the available rules to draw inferences from them. It then adds new facts to the knowledge base, which it can then use to draw further conclusions. This process continues until the AI system has reached a conclusion or until it has exhausted all possible rules to apply.

Forward chaining is often used in expert systems, which are AI systems that make decisions based on a set of rules and facts. By using forward chaining, the AI system can quickly and accurately come to the right conclusion.

Forward chaining is also used in natural language processing (NLP), where the AI system can use the available facts and rules to interpret and understand natural language. It is also used in robotics, where the AI system can use the available facts and rules to make decisions and take action.
