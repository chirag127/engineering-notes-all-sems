
### Knowledge Representation 

Knowledge Representation (KR) is a field of Artificial Intelligence (AI) concerned with representing knowledge in a form that can be used by computers. KR is used to represent facts, rules, and other types of information, and to reason with that information. 

KR techniques can be used to solve problems, generate plans, and make decisions. KR is a key component of AI systems, and is used in many areas, such as natural language processing, robotics, computer vision, and game playing.

KR techniques can be divided into two main categories: symbolic and non-symbolic. Symbolic techniques use symbols (such as variables, functions, and predicates) to represent knowledge, while non-symbolic techniques use numerical or graphical representations. 

Symbolic techniques include rule-based systems, logic programming, and ontologies. Non-symbolic techniques include neural networks, fuzzy logic, and Bayesian networks.

KR techniques can also be divided into two main types: declarative and procedural. Declarative techniques represent knowledge in a form that can be used to answer queries, while procedural techniques represent knowledge in a form that can be used to generate plans and make decisions. 

KR techniques are used in many AI applications, such as natural language processing, robotics, computer vision, and game playing. KR is also used to represent knowledge in databases, and to reason with that knowledge.
