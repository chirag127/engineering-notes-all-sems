### Alternative hypothesis

- An alternative hypothesis is a proposed statement or argument in a hypothesis test that indicates the existence of a statistical relationship between variables .
- It is often denoted as Ha or H1.
- It is the complement to the null hypothesis, which is the statement that there is no statistical relationship between variables.
- The alternative hypothesis usually aligns with the research hypothesis, which is the idea, phenomenon, or observation that the researcher wants to prove .
- The alternative hypothesis can be one-sided or two-sided, depending on whether it specifies the direction of the relationship or not.
- For example, if the research hypothesis is that girls take longer to get ready for school than boys, then the alternative hypothesis is Ha: girls time > boys time, and the null hypothesis is H0: girls time <= boys time.
- The alternative hypothesis is tested against the null hypothesis using a statistical test, such as a t-test, a z-test, or a chi-square test.
- The outcome of the test is either to reject the null hypothesis and accept the alternative hypothesis, or to fail to reject the null hypothesis and reject the alternative hypothesis.
- The decision is based on the significance level, which is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true, and the p-value, which is the probability of obtaining the observed data or more extreme data under the null hypothesis.
- The null hypothesis is rejected if the p-value is less than or equal to the significance level, and the alternative hypothesis is accepted.
- The null hypothesis is not rejected if the p-value is greater than the significance level, and the alternative hypothesis is rejected.
- The alternative hypothesis is a way of expressing the research question in a testable form and making a logical inference based on the data.
