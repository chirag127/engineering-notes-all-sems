### Skewness

- Skewness is a measure of the asymmetry of a probability distribution.
- A distribution is symmetric if it looks the same to the left and right of the center point.
- A distribution is positively skewed or right-skewed if the right tail is longer; the mass of the distribution is concentrated on the left of the figure. The mean is usually greater than the median, which is always greater than the mode.
- A distribution is negatively skewed or left-skewed if the left tail is longer; the mass of the distribution is concentrated on the right of the figure. The mean is usually less than the median, which is always less than the mode.
- A distribution is zero-skewed or symmetric if the mean, median and mode are equal.
- Skewness can be calculated using the formula:

$$
\text{Skewness} = \frac{3(\text{Mean} - \text{Median})}{\text{Standard Deviation}}
$$

- Alternatively, skewness can be calculated using the formula:

$$
\text{Skewness} = \frac{\text{E}[(X - \mu)^3]}{\sigma^3}
$$

where E is the expectation operator, X is a random variable, $\mu$ is the mean, and $\sigma$ is the standard deviation.

- Skewness can be used to test the normality of a distribution. A normal distribution has a skewness of zero. A positive or negative skewness indicates a deviation from normality.
- Skewness can also be used to describe the shape of a distribution and the location of the outliers. A high positive or negative skewness indicates a distribution with a long tail and extreme values. A low positive or negative skewness indicates a distribution with a short tail and less variation.
