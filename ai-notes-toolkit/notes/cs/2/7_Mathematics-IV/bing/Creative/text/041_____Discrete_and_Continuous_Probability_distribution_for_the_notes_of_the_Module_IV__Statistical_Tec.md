### Discrete and Continuous Probability Distribution

- A **probability distribution** is a function that describes all possible values of a random variable as well as the associated probabilities.
- A **discrete probability distribution** is a probability distribution of a **discrete** or **categorical** variable, which is a random variable that has **countable** values, such as a list of non-negative integers .
- A **continuous probability distribution** is a probability distribution of a **continuous** variable, which is a random variable that can assume one of an **infinite** number of different values within a specified range (which may be infinite) .
- Some examples of discrete probability distributions are:
  - **Binomial distribution**: The number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, each with a constant probability of success.
  - **Poisson distribution**: The number of events that occur in a fixed interval of time or space, given a constant average rate of occurrence.
  - **Geometric distribution**: The number of trials until the first success in a sequence of independent trials, each with a constant probability of success.
  - **Hypergeometric distribution**: The number of successes in a sample drawn without replacement from a finite population, given the population size, the sample size, and the number of successes in the population.
- Some examples of continuous probability distributions are:
  - **Uniform distribution**: The probability of any value within a specified range is equal.
  - **Normal distribution**: The probability of values near the mean is higher than the probability of values far from the mean, following a bell-shaped curve.
  - **Exponential distribution**: The probability of values decreases exponentially as they increase, given a constant rate of decay.
  - **Beta distribution**: The probability of values within a specified range is determined by two shape parameters, which can model various degrees of skewness and peakedness.
