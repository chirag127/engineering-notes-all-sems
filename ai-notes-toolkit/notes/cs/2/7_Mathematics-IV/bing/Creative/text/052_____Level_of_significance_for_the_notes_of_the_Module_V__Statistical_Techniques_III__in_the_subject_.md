### Level of Significance for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

- The level of significance is a concept used frequently in statistics to determine whether the null hypothesis must be accepted or rejected.
- The null hypothesis is a statement that assumes no difference or effect in a population parameter or a sample statistic.
- The level of significance refers to a constant probability of incorrect rejection of the null hypothesis when in fact, it is true.
- The level of significance is also known as the probability of type I error, which is the error of rejecting a true null hypothesis.
- The level of significance is usually denoted by the Greek letter alpha (α) and is preset by the researcher before the collection of data, together with the outcomes of error .
- The level of significance is often taken at 0.05 or 5%, which means that the researcher is willing to accept a 5% chance of making a type I error .
- The level of significance is compared with the p-value, which is the probability of obtaining the observed or more extreme results under the null hypothesis.
- The p-value is calculated from the test statistic, which is a measure of how far the observed data deviate from the null hypothesis.
- If the p-value is less than or equal to the level of significance, the null hypothesis is rejected and the alternative hypothesis is accepted .
- If the p-value is greater than the level of significance, the null hypothesis is not rejected and there is not enough evidence to support the alternative hypothesis .
- The level of significance can be lowered for a more conservative test, which means that an effect has to be larger to be considered statistically significant.
- The level of significance can also be raised for a more liberal test, which means that an effect can be smaller to be considered statistically significant.
- The level of significance is the evidentiary standard that the researcher sets to make the case that the effect exists in the population based on the sample data.
- The level of significance is related to the confidence level, which is the complement of the level of significance and represents the probability of not making a type I error.
- For example, if the level of significance is 0.05, the confidence level is 0.95 or 95%, which means that the researcher is 95% confident that the null hypothesis is correctly rejected or not rejected.
