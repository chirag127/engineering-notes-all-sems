# Exponential Curves

Exponential curves are mathematical functions that describe the growth or decay of a quantity over time. These curves are commonly used in the study of statistics, finance, and other fields where the rate of change of a quantity is important.

Some key points to remember about exponential curves are:

- The general form of an exponential function is `f(x) = ab^x`, where `a` is the initial value of the quantity, `b` is the growth factor, and `x` is the time variable.
- The growth factor `b` determines whether the quantity is increasing or decreasing over time. If `b > 1`, the quantity is increasing, and if `0 < b < 1`, the quantity is decreasing.
- The rate of change of an exponential function is proportional to the value of the function. This means that the larger the value of the function, the faster it is changing.
- Exponential functions have a horizontal asymptote, which is a line that the function approaches but never touches. For increasing exponential functions, the horizontal asymptote is the x-axis, and for decreasing exponential functions, it is a horizontal line above the x-axis.
- The inverse of an exponential function is a logarithmic function. Logarithmic functions are used to solve exponential equations and to model data that follows an exponential pattern.
