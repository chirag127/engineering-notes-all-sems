## Discrete Probability Distribution

A discrete probability distribution is a function that maps the possible outcomes of a discrete random variable to their corresponding probabilities. The probabilities must sum to 1.

Some common examples of discrete probability distributions include:

- **Binomial distribution:** Models the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, where each trial has the same probability of success.

- **Poisson distribution:** Models the number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space, where the events occur independently and at a constant rate.

- **Geometric distribution:** Models the number of trials needed to get the first success in a sequence of independent trials, where each trial has the same probability of success.

## Continuous Probability Distribution

A continuous probability distribution is a function that maps the possible outcomes of a continuous random variable to their corresponding probabilities. The probabilities are represented by the area under the curve of the probability density function.

Some common examples of continuous probability distributions include:

- **Normal distribution:** Also known as the Gaussian distribution, it is a symmetric bell-shaped curve that models many natural phenomena, such as heights, weights, and test scores.

- **Exponential distribution:** Models the time between two consecutive events in a Poisson process, where the events occur independently and at a constant rate.

- **Uniform distribution:** Models a random variable where all outcomes in a given interval are equally likely.
