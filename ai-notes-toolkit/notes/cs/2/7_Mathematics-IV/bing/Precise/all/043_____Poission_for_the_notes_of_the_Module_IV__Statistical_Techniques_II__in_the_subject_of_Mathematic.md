# Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution is a discrete probability distribution that expresses the probability of a given number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space if these events occur with a known constant rate and independently of the time since the last event.

Some key points to remember about the Poisson distribution are:

- The Poisson distribution is used to model the number of occurrences of an event in a fixed period of time or space.
- The mean and variance of a Poisson distribution are equal and are given by the parameter λ, which represents the average number of occurrences in the given interval.
- The probability mass function of a Poisson distribution is given by: P(x) = (λ^x * e^-λ) / x!
- The Poisson distribution is often used to model rare events, such as the number of phone calls received by a call center in an hour or the number of defects in a manufactured item.
- The Poisson distribution can be derived as a limiting case of the binomial distribution as the number of trials goes to infinity and the probability of success in each trial goes to zero, while the product of the two remains constant.
