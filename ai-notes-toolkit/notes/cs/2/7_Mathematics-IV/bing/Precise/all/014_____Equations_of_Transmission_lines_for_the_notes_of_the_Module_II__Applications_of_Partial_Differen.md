### Equations of Transmission lines

1. Transmission lines are used to transmit electrical energy from one point to another.
2. The equations that govern the behavior of transmission lines are derived from Maxwell's equations.
3. The Telegrapher's equations are a pair of linear partial differential equations that describe the voltage and current on an electric transmission line.
4. The Telegrapher's equations can be derived from the lumped element model of a transmission line, which represents the line as a series of infinitesimally small circuit elements.
5. The primary constants of a transmission line are the resistance, inductance, capacitance, and conductance per unit length.
6. The characteristic impedance of a transmission line is the ratio of the voltage to the current at any point on the line.
7. The propagation constant of a transmission line is a complex quantity that describes how the voltage and current waves decay and change phase as they travel along the line.
8. The reflection coefficient of a transmission line is the ratio of the reflected wave to the incident wave at a discontinuity in the line.
9. The input impedance of a transmission line is the impedance seen looking into the line from the input end.
10. The transmission line equations can be solved using a variety of techniques, including the method of characteristics and the Laplace transform.
