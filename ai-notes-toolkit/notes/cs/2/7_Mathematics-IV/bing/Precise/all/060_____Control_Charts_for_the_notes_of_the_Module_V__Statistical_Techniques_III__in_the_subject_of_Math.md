# Control Charts

Control charts are a type of statistical process control tool used to monitor and control the quality of a process. They are used to determine whether a process is in a state of statistical control or not. A process is said to be in statistical control when it is free from assignable causes of variation and only common causes of variation are present.

Control charts are constructed by plotting the process data over time and adding control limits to the chart. The control limits are calculated using statistical methods and represent the expected range of variation in the process. If the process data falls within the control limits, the process is considered to be in control. If the data falls outside the control limits, it is an indication that there may be an assignable cause of variation present and the process may need to be investigated.

There are several types of control charts, including:

- X-bar and R charts: used to monitor the mean and range of a continuous process variable.
- p-charts: used to monitor the proportion of nonconforming units in a sample.
- c-charts: used to monitor the number of nonconformities per unit.
- u-charts: used to monitor the number of nonconformities per unit, adjusted for the size of the unit.

Control charts are an important tool in quality control and process improvement. They can help identify when a process is out of control and may need to be adjusted, and can also be used to monitor the ongoing performance of a process to ensure that it remains in control.
