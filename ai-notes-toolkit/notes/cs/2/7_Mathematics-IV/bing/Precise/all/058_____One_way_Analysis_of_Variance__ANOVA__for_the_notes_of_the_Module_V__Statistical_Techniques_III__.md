# One way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

One way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) is a statistical technique used to test the hypothesis that the means of two or more populations are equal. It is used when the independent variable is categorical and the dependent variable is continuous.

The steps involved in performing a one way ANOVA are as follows:

1. State the null and alternative hypotheses.
2. Calculate the test statistic.
3. Determine the critical value and the p-value.
4. Make a decision based on the p-value and the level of significance.
5. Draw a conclusion.

The null hypothesis for a one way ANOVA is that the population means are equal, while the alternative hypothesis is that at least one population mean is different from the others.

The test statistic for a one way ANOVA is the F-statistic, which is calculated as the ratio of the between-group variance to the within-group variance.

The critical value and the p-value can be determined using an F-distribution table or by using a statistical software package.

If the p-value is less than the level of significance, the null hypothesis is rejected and it can be concluded that there is a significant difference between the population means. If the p-value is greater than the level of significance, the null hypothesis is not rejected and it cannot be concluded that there is a significant difference between the population means.

One way ANOVA is a useful statistical technique for comparing the means of two or more populations. It is important to carefully state the null and alternative hypotheses, calculate the test statistic, and make a decision based on the p-value and the level of significance.
