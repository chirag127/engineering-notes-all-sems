### Control Charts for Variables (p, np and c charts)

- Control charts are graphical tools that help monitor and improve the quality of a process or a product by detecting the presence of special causes of variation.
- Control charts can be classified into two types: variables control charts and attributes control charts.
- Variables control charts are used when the quality characteristic of interest is a continuous variable, such as length, weight, temperature, etc.
- Attributes control charts are used when the quality characteristic of interest is a discrete attribute, such as defective or non-defective, pass or fail, etc.
- There are four types of attributes control charts: p chart, np chart, c chart and u chart.
- p chart is used to monitor the proportion or fraction of defective items in a sample or a subgroup. It is suitable when the sample size is variable and the items can be classified into two categories: defective or non-defective.
- np chart is used to monitor the number of defective items in a sample or a subgroup. It is suitable when the sample size is constant and the items can be classified into two categories: defective or non-defective.
- c chart is used to monitor the number of defects in a unit or a subgroup. It is suitable when the unit size is constant and the defects can be counted, such as scratches, holes, cracks, etc.
- u chart is used to monitor the number of defects per unit or per subgroup. It is suitable when the unit size is variable and the defects can be counted, such as scratches, holes, cracks, etc.

- The general steps for constructing and using any control chart are:

  - Collect data and calculate the sample statistics, such as the proportion defective, the number of defects, etc.
  - Calculate the center line and the control limits for the control chart, using the appropriate formulas or tables.
  - Plot the sample statistics on the control chart and draw the center line and the control limits.
  - Analyze the control chart for any patterns or signals that indicate the process is out of control, such as points beyond the control limits, runs, trends, cycles, etc.
  - Investigate the causes of variation and take corrective actions if necessary.
  - Continue to monitor the process using the control chart and update the center line and the control limits as needed.
