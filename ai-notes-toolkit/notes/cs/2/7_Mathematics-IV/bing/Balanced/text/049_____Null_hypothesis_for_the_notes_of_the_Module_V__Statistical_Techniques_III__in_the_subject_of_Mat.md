### Null Hypothesis

- A null hypothesis is a theory based on insufficient evidence that requires further testing to prove whether the observed data is true or false .
- A null hypothesis is usually denoted by H0 and is often a statement of no effect or no relationship between variables .
- For example, a null hypothesis statement can be “the rate of plant growth is not affected by sunlight” or “there is no difference in the average height of male and female students”.
- A null hypothesis is contrasted with an alternative hypothesis, which is a statement that there is some effect or relationship between variables .
- An alternative hypothesis is usually denoted by H1 or Ha and is often the opposite of the null hypothesis .
- For example, an alternative hypothesis statement can be “the rate of plant growth is affected by sunlight” or “there is a difference in the average height of male and female students”.
- A null hypothesis and an alternative hypothesis are used in hypothesis testing, which is a method of statistical inference that evaluates the evidence for or against a null hypothesis .
- Hypothesis testing involves collecting data, calculating a test statistic, and comparing it to a critical value or a p-value to make a decision about the null hypothesis .
- If the test statistic is more extreme than the critical value or the p-value is less than the significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected and the alternative hypothesis is supported .
- If the test statistic is less extreme than the critical value or the p-value is greater than the significance level, the null hypothesis is not rejected and the alternative hypothesis is not supported .
- Rejecting the null hypothesis means that there is sufficient evidence to suggest that the null hypothesis is false .
- Not rejecting the null hypothesis means that there is insufficient evidence to suggest that the null hypothesis is false .
- However, not rejecting the null hypothesis does not mean that the null hypothesis is true, as there may be other factors that affect the outcome of the test, such as sample size, measurement error, or bias  .
- Therefore, a null hypothesis is never proven, but only rejected or not rejected based on the available data and the chosen significance level  .
