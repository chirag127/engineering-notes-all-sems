### Hypothesis

- A hypothesis is a tentative statement about the relationship between two or more variables.
- A hypothesis can be tested by collecting and analyzing data that are relevant to the statement.
- A hypothesis can be either null or alternative.
- A null hypothesis (H0) is a statement that there is no significant difference or relationship between the variables of interest.
- An alternative hypothesis (H1) is a statement that there is a significant difference or relationship between the variables of interest.
- A hypothesis can be either one-tailed or two-tailed.
- A one-tailed hypothesis specifies the direction of the difference or relationship between the variables (e.g., H1: x > y).
- A two-tailed hypothesis does not specify the direction of the difference or relationship between the variables (e.g., H1: x ≠ y).
- A hypothesis can be either simple or composite.
- A simple hypothesis involves only one parameter or statistic (e.g., H0: μ = 50).
- A composite hypothesis involves more than one parameter or statistic (e.g., H0: μ1 = μ2 = μ3).
- A hypothesis can be either point or interval.
- A point hypothesis specifies a single value for the parameter or statistic of interest (e.g., H0: μ = 50).
- An interval hypothesis specifies a range of values for the parameter or statistic of interest (e.g., H0: 40 ≤ μ ≤ 60).
