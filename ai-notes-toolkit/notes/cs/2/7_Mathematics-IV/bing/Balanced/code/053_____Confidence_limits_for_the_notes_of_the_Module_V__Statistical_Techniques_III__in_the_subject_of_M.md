### Confidence limits

- Confidence limits are a pair of numbers that specify the upper and lower bounds of a confidence interval .
- A confidence interval is a range of values that contains the true population parameter with a certain probability, usually 95% or 99% .
- Confidence limits are calculated from the sample statistic, the standard error, and the critical value for the chosen level of confidence .
- For example, if the sample mean is 7.4, the standard error is 1, and the critical value for 95% confidence is 1.96, then the confidence limits are 7.4 ± 1.96 × 1, or 5.4 and 9.4 .
- Confidence limits can be used to assess the precision and reliability of an estimate, and to compare different estimates or test hypotheses .
