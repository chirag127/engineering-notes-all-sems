### Conditional Probability

Conditional probability is the probability of one event occurring with some relationship to one or more other events. For example, the probability of a boy playing tennis in the evening may depend on whether it is a rainy day or not. Conditional probability can be calculated using the formula:

P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B)

where P(A|B) is the probability of event A given that event B has occurred, P(A and B) is the probability of both events A and B occurring, and P(B) is the probability of event B occurring.

Some examples of conditional probability are:

- The probability of a coin landing on heads given that it is biased towards heads is higher than the probability of a fair coin landing on heads.
- The probability of a student passing a test given that he or she has studied is higher than the probability of a student passing a test without studying.
- The probability of a person having a heart attack given that he or she is a smoker is higher than the probability of a person having a heart attack who is not a smoker.

Conditional probability can be used to analyze various situations involving dependent events, such as:

- The probability of drawing a red card from a deck of cards given that the first card drawn was a king.
- The probability of a person having a certain disease given that he or she has a positive test result.
- The probability of a team winning a game given that they have a lead at halftime.
