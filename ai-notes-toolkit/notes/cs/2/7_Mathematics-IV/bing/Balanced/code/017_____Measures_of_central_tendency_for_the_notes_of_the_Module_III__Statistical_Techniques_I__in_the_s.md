### Measures of central tendency

- Measures of central tendency are summary statistics that attempt to describe a whole set of data with a single value that represents the middle or centre of its distribution.
- Colloquially, measures of central tendency are often called averages.
- The most common measures of central tendency are the mean, the median, and the mode  .
- Mean (Average): Represents the sum of all values in a dataset divided by the total number of the values . For example, the mean of 2, 4, 6, and 8 is (2 + 4 + 6 + 8) / 4 = 5.
- Median: The middle value in a dataset that is arranged in ascending order (from the smallest value to the largest value) . For example, the median of 2, 4, 6, and 8 is (4 + 6) / 2 = 5. If the dataset has an odd number of values, the median is the value that is exactly in the middle. For example, the median of 2, 4, 5, 6, and 8 is 5.
- Mode: Defines the most frequently occurring value in a dataset . For example, the mode of 2, 4, 4, 6, and 8 is 4. A dataset can have more than one mode if there are multiple values that have the same frequency. For example, the mode of 2, 4, 4, 6, 6, and 8 is 4 and 6. A dataset can also have no mode if all values have different frequencies. For example, the mode of 2, 4, 5, 6, and 8 is none.
- Measures of central tendency are useful for describing the general characteristics of a dataset, but they do not provide information about the variability or spread of the data. For that, other measures such as range, variance, and standard deviation are needed.
