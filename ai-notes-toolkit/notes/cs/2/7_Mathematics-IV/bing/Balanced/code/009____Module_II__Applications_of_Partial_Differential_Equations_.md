## Module II: Applications of Partial Differential Equations:

Partial differential equations (PDEs) are equations that involve partial derivatives of functions of several variables. They are used to model various physical phenomena, such as heat, sound, fluid, elasticity, electromagnetism, etc. In this module, we will learn some of the common types and methods of solving PDEs, as well as some of their applications in different fields.

Some of the topics covered in this module are:

- First-order linear PDEs: These are PDEs of the form a(x,y)u_x + b(x,y)u_y = c(x,y)u + d(x,y), where u is the unknown function and a, b, c, d are given functions. They can be solved by the method of characteristics, which involves finding curves along which the PDE becomes an ordinary differential equation (ODE).
- Second-order linear PDEs: These are PDEs of the form a(x,y)u_xx + b(x,y)u_xy + c(x,y)u_yy + d(x,y)u_x + e(x,y)u_y + f(x,y)u = g(x,y), where u is the unknown function and a, b, c, d, e, f, g are given functions. They can be classified into three types: elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic, depending on the sign of b^2 - 4ac. They can be solved by various methods, such as separation of variables, Fourier series, Laplace transform, etc.
- Heat equation: This is a second-order parabolic PDE of the form u_t = k u_xx, where u is the temperature function, t is the time variable, x is the spatial variable, and k is the thermal conductivity constant. It models the diffusion of heat in a one-dimensional rod. It can be solved by separation of variables, Fourier series, or Laplace transform, depending on the boundary and initial conditions.
- Wave equation: This is a second-order hyperbolic PDE of the form u_tt = c^2 u_xx, where u is the displacement function, t is the time variable, x is the spatial variable, and c is the wave speed constant. It models the propagation of waves in a one-dimensional string. It can be solved by separation of variables, Fourier series, or d'Alembert's solution, depending on the boundary and initial conditions.
- Laplace equation: This is a second-order elliptic PDE of the form u_xx + u_yy = 0, where u is the potential function and x and y are the spatial variables. It models the steady-state distribution of heat, electric potential, fluid pressure, etc. in a two-dimensional region. It can be solved by separation of variables, Fourier series, or conformal mapping, depending on the shape and boundary conditions of the region.
- Applications of PDEs: PDEs are widely used in various fields of science and engineering, such as heat transfer, fluid dynamics, elasticity, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, finance, etc. Some examples of applications are:

  - The Black-Scholes equation is a second-order parabolic PDE that is used to model the price of a financial derivative, such as an option or a futures contract. It involves the variables of stock price, time, volatility, interest rate, and strike price.
  - The Navier-Stokes equations are a system of nonlinear PDEs that describe the motion of a viscous fluid, such as air or water. They involve the variables of velocity, pressure, density, and viscosity of the fluid.
  - The Schrödinger equation is a second-order linear PDE that governs the behavior of a quantum system, such as an electron or a photon. It involves the variables of wave function, time, energy, and potential of the system.
