

### Hypothesis for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

In the study of statistics, it is important to understand the concept of hypothesis testing. Hypothesis testing is a statistical method used to determine whether a hypothesis is true or false. In this module, we will be discussing the different types of hypotheses and the steps involved in hypothesis testing.

Here are the key points to remember:

1. Hypothesis: A hypothesis is an assumption or a claim about a population parameter.

2. Types of Hypotheses: There are two types of hypotheses - null hypothesis (H0) and alternative hypothesis (Ha).

3. Null Hypothesis: The null hypothesis (H0) is a statement that there is no significant difference between the observed and expected values.

4. Alternative Hypothesis: The alternative hypothesis (Ha) is a statement that there is a significant difference between the observed and expected values.

5. Steps in Hypothesis Testing: There are five steps involved in hypothesis testing - formulating the hypothesis, selecting the level of significance, selecting the test statistic, calculating the p-value, and making a decision.

6. Level of Significance: The level of significance (α) is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true. It is usually set at 0.05 or 0.01.

7. Test Statistic: The test statistic is a value calculated from the sample data that is used to test the hypothesis.

8. P-Value: The p-value is the probability of obtaining a test statistic as extreme as the one observed, assuming that the null hypothesis is true.

9. Decision: Based on the p-value, we either reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.

10. Errors in Hypothesis Testing: There are two types of errors in hypothesis testing - Type I error and Type II error.

In conclusion, hypothesis testing is an important tool in statistics that helps us make decisions based on sample data. Understanding the different types of hypotheses and the steps involved in hypothesis testing is essential to becoming proficient in statistics.
