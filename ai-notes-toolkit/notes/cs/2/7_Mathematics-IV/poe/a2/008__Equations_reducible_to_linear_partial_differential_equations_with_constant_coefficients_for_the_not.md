### Equations reducible to linear partial differential equations with constant coefficients

1. Homogeneous equations:
- Equation of the form $\frac{\partial^n y}{\partial x^n} + a_1\frac{\partial^{n-1} y}{\partial x^{n-1}} + ... + a_n y = 0$ where $a_i$ are constants can be reduced to a linear equation by the transformation $y = x^ku(x)$.
- Equation of the form $\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial y^2} + a_1\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} + a_2y = 0$ can be reduced to a linear equation by the transformation $y = xv(x)$.

2. Non-homogeneous equations:
- Equation of the form $\frac{\partial^n y}{\partial x^n} + a_1\frac{\partial^{n-1} y}{\partial x^{n-1}} + ... + a_n y = f(x)$ can be reduced to a linear equation by using the method of undetermined coefficients if $f(x)$ is a polynomial or trigonometric/exponential function. The auxiliary equation is solved to find the values of $n$ for which the method holds.
- Equation of the form $\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial x^2} + p(x)\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} + q(x)y = g(x)$ can be reduced to a linear equation by assuming a solution of the form $y = \exp(\int p(x)dx)\ocomplete{x}(x)$ and determining $c(x)$ such that it satisfies the equation. This method holds if $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are polynomials and $g(x)$ is a polynomial or an integral function.
