### T-test for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. A t-test is a statistical test that is used to compare the means of two groups. It is used to determine if there is a significant difference between the two means.
2. The t-test assumes that the data follows a normal distribution and has equal variance in the two groups.
3. The t-test can be either two-tailed or one-tailed. A two-tailed test determines if the means are different, without specifying the direction of the difference. A one-tailed test determines if one mean is greater than or less than the other.
4. The t-test calculates a t-statistic, which determines whether the difference between the means is statistically significant. If the p-value is less than the significance level, then the difference is significant. Common significance levels are 0.05 and 0.01.
5. The t-test requires that the samples are independent and that they have approximately equal variances. If the variances are not equal, a separate variance t-test can be used. The t-test is commonly used in comparing control and experimental groups.
