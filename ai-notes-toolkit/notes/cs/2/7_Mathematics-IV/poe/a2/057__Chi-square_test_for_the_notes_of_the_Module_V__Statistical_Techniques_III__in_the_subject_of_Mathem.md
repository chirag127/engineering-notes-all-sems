### Chi-square test for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. The chi-square test is a statistical test applied to sets of categorical data to evaluate how likely it is that any observed difference between the sets arose by chance.
2. It is used to determine whether there is a significant difference between the expected frequencies and the observed frequencies in one or more categories.
3. The chi-square test statistic is calculated as:
χ2 = ∑(O-E)2/E
Where O is the observed frequency and E is the expected frequency in each category.
4. A significant chi-square value indicates that there is a difference between the expected and observed frequencies. The calculated p-value is used to determine significance. If the p-value is less than the chosen alpha level, the null hypothesis is rejected.
5. Conditions for the chi-square test:
a) The data must be from categorical variables.
b) The data are independent.
c) Expected frequencies in each cell should be at least 5. This ensures validity of the chi-square distribution.
d) The data should represent a simple random sample.
6. The chi-square test can be used to test for independence between two categorical variables, test for goodness of fit, and to determine if a categorical data fits a specific distribution.
