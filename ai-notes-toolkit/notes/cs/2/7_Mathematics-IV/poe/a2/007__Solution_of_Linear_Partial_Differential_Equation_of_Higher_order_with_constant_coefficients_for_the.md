### Solution of Linear Partial Differential Equation of Higher order with constant coefficients for the notes of the Module I: Partial Differential Equations in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. A linear partial differential equation of order n with constant coefficients is of the form:

$$a_0(x,y,z,...)y^{(n)} + a_1(x,y,z,...)y^{(n-1)} + ... + a_(n-1)(x,y,z,...)y' + a_n(x,y,z,...)y = f(x,y,z,...)$$

where $a_0,a_1,...,a_n$ are constants and $f(x,y,z,...)$ is a given function.

2. The method of solution is to assume a solution of the form:

$$y = e^{rx} v(y)$$

where $r$ is a constant to be determined and $v(y)$ is an arbitrary function of $y$ alone.

3. Substitute this assumed solution in the given differential equation and equate coefficients of like powers of $e^{rx}$ to zero. This will lead to a system of $n$ equations in $n$ unknowns $r$ and the derivatives of $v(y)$.

4. Solving this system, we can determine $r$ and $v(y)$. The general solution is then obtained by substituting the values of $r$ and $v(y)$ and its derivatives in the assumed solution.

5. The order of the differential equation can be reduced by one if $r$ happens to be a root of the characteristic equation $|a_0r^n + a_1r^(n-1) + ... + a_(n-1)r + a_n| = 0$. In such a case, the solution can be obtained by the method of reduction of order.
