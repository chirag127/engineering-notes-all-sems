### Sampling Theory (Small and Large)

1. Small sample theory: When the sample size is small (less than 30), we use small sample theory. In small samples, the sample statistics like sample mean, median, etc. follow a probability distribution and are not consistent estimators of the population parameters. The distributions of sample statistics are studied and used to draw inferences about the population parameters.

2. Large sample theory: When the sample size is large (greater than 30), we use large sample theory. In large samples, the sample statistics are consistent estimators of the population parameters. The distributions of sample statistics under large sample theory can be approximated by normal distribution using central limit theorem. The inferences about the population parameters can be drawn using z-test or t-test.

The inferences based on large sample theory are more reliable compared to small sample theory as the sample statistics closely estimate the population parameters when sample size is large. However, in practice, even for large samples, the distribution of sample statistics may not follow the assumed distribution due to factors like non-normality of data or presence of outliers. Hence, suitable checks for assumptions and use of robust methods are recommended.
