### Normal distributions for the notes of the Module IV: Statistical Techniques II: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. A normal distribution is a very commonly occurring continuous probability distribution for a random variable. It is also known as Gaussian distribution.
2. It is defined by two parameters - mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ). The mean determines the central location of the distribution and the standard deviation determines the spread of the distribution.
3. The probability density function (pdf) of a normal distribution is:

f(x) = (1/√(2πσ2)) exp(-(x-μ)2/2σ2)

4. Some key properties of normal distribution:

- Symmetrical about the mean
- Bell shaped
- Mean, median and mode coincide
- 99.7% of the data lies within 3σ of the mean
- As σ increases, the curve becomes flatter
- Area under the curve is 1

5. Normal distribution is very useful because:

- Many natural phenomena follow normal distribution ( Heights, weights, IQ scores, etc.)
- Central Limit Theorem - Sum of independent random variables tend to follow normal distribution
- Easy to calculate probabilities and percentiles
- Foundation for many other statistical concepts and tests (Confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, etc.)

6. Applications of normal distribution:

- To model many natural phenomena and measurements
- In statistical analysis and inferences
- In machine learning and pattern recognition
