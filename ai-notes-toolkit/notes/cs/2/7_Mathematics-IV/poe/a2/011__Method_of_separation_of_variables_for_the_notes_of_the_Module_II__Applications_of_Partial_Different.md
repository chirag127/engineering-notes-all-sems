### Method of separation of variables for the notes of the Module II: Applications of Partial Differential Equations: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. Consider a partial differential equation of the form:
$$a(x,y) \\frac{\\partial u}{\\partial x} + b(x,y)\\frac{\\partial u}{\\partial y} = f(x,y)$$
where $a(x,y)$ and $b(x,y)$ are functions of $x$ and $y$ and $f(x,y)$ is a given function of $x$ and $y$.

2. Assume a solution of the form:
$$u(x,y) = X(x)Y(y)$$
where $X$ and $Y$ are functions of $x$ and $y$ respectively.

3. Substitute this assumed solution in the given partial differential equation. This will lead to
$$a(x,y)\\frac{\\partial X}{\\partial x} + b(x,y)\\frac{\\partial Y}{\\partial y} = f(x,y)$$

4. Equating the coefficients of $\\frac{\\partial X}{\\partial x}$ and $\\frac{\\partial Y}{\\partial y}$ to zero, we get
$$a(x,y)X^{\\prime} = 0 \\quad \\Rightarrow \\quad X = \\text{constant} = C_1$$
$$b(x,y)Y^{\\prime} = 0 \\quad \\Rightarrow \\quad Y = \\text{constant} = C_2$$

5. The required solution is
$$u(x,y) = C_1C_2$$
where $C_1$ and $C_2$ are arbitrary constants.
