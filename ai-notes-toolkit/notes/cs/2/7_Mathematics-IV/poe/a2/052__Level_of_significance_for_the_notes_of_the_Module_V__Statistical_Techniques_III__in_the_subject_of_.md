### Level of significance for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. Introduction
- Explain the level of significance and its importance in statistical analysis and hypothesis testing.
- Mention that level of significance (denoted by alpha or α) is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is actually true. It is also known as Type I error.

2. Selection of appropriate level of significance
- The most common levels of significance used are 1%, 5% and 10%.
- Select a level based on the consequences of decision made and acceptable error. Lower level of significance leads to more confident decisions but higher chance of type 1 error. Higher level leads to less confident decisions but lower chance of type 1 error.
- Explain that in exploratory studies, 10% level is used while in confirmatory studies, 5% or 1% level is used. For studies with serious consequences, 1% level is appropriate.

3. Impact of level of significance on hypothesis testing
- As level of significance decreases, the rejection region for null hypothesis decreases leading to less chance of rejecting the null hypothesis. Thus, a statistically significant result at 1% level will also be significant at 5% or 10% level but vice versa is not true.
- Decreasing the level of significance makes the test more rigorous and conservative as the chances of type 1 error decreases but chances of type 2 error increases.

4. Conclusion
- Briefly summarize the key points discussed.
- Highlight the importance of selecting an appropriate level of significance based on the context and implications of the study. An incorrect selection may lead to erroneous results and decisions.
