### F-test for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. F-test is a statistical test used to compare the variance of two populations. It is used to determine if two population variances are equal or not.
2. The F-test calculates the ratio of the two variances and compares it with a critical value to determine if they are equal or not. If the calculated F-value is greater than the critical value, then we reject the null hypothesis that the two population variances are equal.
3. The steps to perform an F-test are:
- State the null and alternative hypotheses. The null hypothesis is that the two population variances are equal. The alternative hypothesis is that the two population variances are not equal.
- Calculate the sample variances for the two populations.
- Calculate the F-value by dividing the larger variance by the smaller variance.
- Determine the critical value for the chosen significance level and degrees of freedom.
- Compare the calculated F-value with the critical value. If the F-value is greater than the critical value, reject the null hypothesis. Otherwise, fail to reject the null hypothesis.

4. The F-test is commonly used to test the equality of variance for two normal populations and to test the equality of two or more population variances when the samples are independent and represent random samples from each population.
