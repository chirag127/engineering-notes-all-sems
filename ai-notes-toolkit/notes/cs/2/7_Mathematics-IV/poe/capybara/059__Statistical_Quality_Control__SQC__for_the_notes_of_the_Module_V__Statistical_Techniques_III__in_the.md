### Statistical Quality Control (SQC)

Statistical Quality Control (SQC) is a set of statistical techniques used in quality control to monitor, control, and improve the quality of a product or process. SQC involves the use of statistical methods to analyze data and make decisions based on the results.

Here are some important points to understand about SQC:

- SQC is used to detect and control process variation. By analyzing data collected from a process over time, SQC helps identify the sources of variation and take corrective action to reduce it.

- SQC involves the use of control charts. Control charts are graphical representations of process data over time that help identify patterns and trends in the data. Control charts are used to monitor the process and detect when it is out of control.

- There are two types of control charts: variable and attribute control charts. Variable control charts are used to monitor continuous data, such as measurements of length or weight. Attribute control charts are used to monitor discrete data, such as the number of defects in a product.

- SQC also involves the use of sampling techniques. Sampling involves selecting a subset of data from a larger population and using it to make inferences about the population as a whole. Sampling is used in SQC to collect data on a process and analyze it to determine if the process is in control.

- SQC is used in many industries, including manufacturing, healthcare, and finance. It is used to improve product quality, reduce defects, and increase efficiency.

- SQC requires a thorough understanding of statistical methods and data analysis techniques. It is important to have trained professionals who can collect and analyze data, interpret the results, and make decisions based on the findings.

Overall, SQC is an important tool for quality control that helps ensure that products and processes meet the required standards. By monitoring and controlling process variation, SQC helps improve quality, reduce defects, and increase efficiency.
