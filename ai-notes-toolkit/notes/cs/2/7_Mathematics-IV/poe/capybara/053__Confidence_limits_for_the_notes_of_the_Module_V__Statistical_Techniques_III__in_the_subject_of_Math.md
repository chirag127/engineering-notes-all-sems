### Confidence limits for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

In Module V of Statistical Techniques III in Mathematics-IV KCS, you will learn about confidence limits. Confidence limits are an important concept in statistics, as they help us to determine how confident we can be in our statistical results. Below are some key points to keep in mind when studying confidence limits in this module:

- Confidence limits are a range of values that we can be reasonably sure contain the true value of a population parameter.
- The level of confidence is typically expressed as a percentage, such as 95% or 99%.
- As the level of confidence increases, the range of values that make up the confidence limits also increases.
- Confidence limits are calculated using a formula that takes into account the sample size, the standard deviation, and the level of confidence.
- The formula for confidence limits is different depending on whether we are calculating confidence limits for a population mean or a population proportion.
- In order to calculate confidence limits, we first need to know the sample mean or sample proportion, as well as the standard deviation or standard error.
- Confidence limits can be used to determine whether a particular sample is likely to have come from a population with a certain mean or proportion.
- Confidence limits can also be used to compare two different samples and determine whether they are significantly different from one another.

Overall, understanding confidence limits is crucial for anyone working with statistical data. By mastering this concept, you will be better equipped to analyze and interpret data, and to draw meaningful conclusions from your analyses.
