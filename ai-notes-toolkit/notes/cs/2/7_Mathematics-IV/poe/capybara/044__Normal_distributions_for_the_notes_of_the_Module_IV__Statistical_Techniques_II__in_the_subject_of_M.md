### Normal Distributions

In the field of statistics, normal distributions are an important concept. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- A normal distribution, also known as a Gaussian distribution, is a probability distribution that has a bell-shaped curve. This means that most of the data falls in the middle, with fewer data points on either extreme.

- The mean, median, and mode are all equal in a normal distribution. This is referred to as symmetry.

- The standard deviation is a measure of how spread out the data is. In a normal distribution, approximately 68% of the data falls within one standard deviation of the mean, and approximately 95% falls within two standard deviations.

- The area under the curve of a normal distribution is equal to 1. This means that the probability of any particular value occurring is 0, since the curve is continuous.

- Normal distributions can be used to model real-world phenomena, such as heights, weights, and test scores. This is because many natural processes tend to produce data that follows a normal distribution.

- The central limit theorem states that the sum of a large number of independent, identically distributed random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the underlying distribution of the individual variables.

- Z-scores can be used to standardize data and compare values from different normal distributions. A z-score represents the number of standard deviations a particular value is from the mean.

- The normal distribution is a key concept in hypothesis testing, where it is used to calculate p-values and determine statistical significance.

Understanding normal distributions is an important part of statistical analysis, and is essential for anyone studying mathematics or statistics.
