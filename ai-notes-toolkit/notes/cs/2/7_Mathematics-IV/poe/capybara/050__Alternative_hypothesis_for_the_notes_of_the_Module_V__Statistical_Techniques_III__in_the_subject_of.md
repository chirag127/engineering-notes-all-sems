### Alternative Hypothesis for the Notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the Subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

In the field of statistics, hypothesis testing is a crucial tool to analyze data and make decisions based on evidence. In this context, alternative hypothesis or research hypothesis is a statement that contradicts the null hypothesis and proposes a possible explanation for the observed data. Here are some key points about the alternative hypothesis that you should know for the notes of Module V: Statistical Techniques III in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS:

- The alternative hypothesis is denoted by H1, and it is a statement that asserts that there is a significant difference or relationship between the variables being studied. It is often framed in terms of a specific direction of effect or association, such as "the mean score of group A is greater than the mean score of group B" or "there is a positive correlation between X and Y".
- The alternative hypothesis is usually formulated after the null hypothesis, which is a statement that assumes no significant difference or relationship between the variables. The null hypothesis is denoted by H0, and it is tested against the alternative hypothesis using statistical methods such as t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square tests, etc.
- The alternative hypothesis can be one-sided or two-sided, depending on the research question and the available evidence. A one-sided alternative hypothesis only considers one direction of effect or association, while a two-sided alternative hypothesis considers both directions. For example, a one-sided alternative hypothesis for the mean difference between two groups may be "group A has a higher mean score than group B", while a two-sided alternative hypothesis may be "there is a significant difference in the mean score between group A and group B".
- The alternative hypothesis should be based on a clear and testable research question or hypothesis, and it should be supported by relevant literature or previous studies. It is important to avoid vague or unfounded claims that cannot be verified or falsified by empirical data.
- The alternative hypothesis should be stated in a concise and precise manner, using appropriate statistical language and notation. It should be clear to the reader what the alternative hypothesis is proposing and what evidence or data would support or reject it. It is also important to state any assumptions or limitations of the alternative hypothesis, and to acknowledge any potential sources of error or bias in the data or analysis.
