### Binomial

In probability theory, the binomial distribution is a discrete probability distribution that describes the outcomes of a fixed number of independent experiments, each of which can have two possible outcomes. 

Here are some key points to remember about the binomial distribution:

- The binomial distribution is used to model the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, where each trial has two possible outcomes (e.g., success or failure).

- The binomial distribution is characterized by two parameters: n, the number of trials, and p, the probability of success on each trial.

- The probability of obtaining k successes in n trials is given by the binomial probability mass function, which can be calculated using the formula P(X=k) = (n choose k) * p^k * (1-p)^(n-k), where (n choose k) is the binomial coefficient.

- The mean and variance of the binomial distribution are given by E(X) = np and Var(X) = np(1-p), respectively.

- The binomial distribution is often used in hypothesis testing, where the null hypothesis assumes a certain proportion of successes and the alternative hypothesis assumes a different proportion.

- The normal distribution can be used to approximate the binomial distribution when n is large and p is not too close to 0 or 1. This is known as the normal approximation to the binomial distribution.

In summary, the binomial distribution is a useful tool for modeling the outcomes of experiments with two possible outcomes. Understanding the properties of the binomial distribution can help you make informed decisions in a variety of contexts, from quality control to medical research.
