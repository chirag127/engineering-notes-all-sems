
### Church’s Thesis

Church’s Thesis is a statement proposed by the American mathematician Alonzo Church in 1936. It states that any function that can be computed by an algorithm can be computed by a Turing machine. This thesis is central to the study of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, as it implies that Turing Machines are the most general form of computation.

The following points summarize Church’s Thesis:

1. Any function that can be computed by an algorithm can be computed by a Turing machine.
2. Turing machines are the most general form of computation.
3. Turing machines are a powerful tool for describing recursive functions.
4. Turing machines can be used to solve problems that cannot be solved by algorithms.
5. Turing machines are capable of computing any computable function.
