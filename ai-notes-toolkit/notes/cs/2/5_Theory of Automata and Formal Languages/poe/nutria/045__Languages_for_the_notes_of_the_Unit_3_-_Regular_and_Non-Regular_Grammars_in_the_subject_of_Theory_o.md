
### Languages for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars in the Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

1. Regular Languages: Regular languages are a class of languages that can be described by a set of rules or regular expressions. These languages have a finite number of words, and they can be recognized by finite-state machines. Examples of regular languages include regular expressions, regular expressions over a given alphabet, and context-free languages. 

2. Non-Regular Languages: Non-regular languages are languages that cannot be described by regular expressions or finite-state machines. These languages have an infinite number of words, and they cannot be recognized by finite-state machines. Examples of non-regular languages include context-sensitive languages and recursive languages. 

3. Chomsky Hierarchy: The Chomsky Hierarchy is a classification of languages according to their complexity. It is divided into four classes: regular languages, context-free languages, context-sensitive languages, and recursive languages. Regular languages are the simplest, while recursive languages are the most complex. 

4. Finite-State Machines: Finite-state machines are machines that can recognize regular languages. They consist of a finite number of states, and they can only transition between states when given a certain input. Finite-state machines are used to recognize regular languages, and they can also be used to recognize context-free languages. 

5. Context-Free Grammars: Context-free grammars are a type of grammar that can be used to generate context-free languages. These grammars consist of a finite number of rules, and they can generate an infinite number of words. Context-free grammars are used to generate context-free languages, and they can also be used to generate context-sensitive languages. 

6. Turing Machines: Turing machines are machines that can recognize recursive languages. They consist of an infinite number of states, and they can transition between states when given a certain input. Turing machines are used to recognize recursive languages, and they can also be used to recognize any language.
