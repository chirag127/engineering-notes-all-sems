
### Chomsky Normal Form (CNF)

* Chomsky Normal Form (CNF) is a form of a grammar in which all production rules are of the form **A → BC** or **A → a**, where **A**, **B**, and **C** are non-terminal symbols, and **a** is a terminal symbol.
* In this form, all productions must contain at least one non-terminal symbol on the right-hand side.
* CNF is used to simplify the process of determining whether a given language is regular or not.
* CNF is also useful in proving that a language is non-regular.
* A grammar is said to be in CNF if it contains only productions of the form **A → BC** or **A → a**, and no other productions.
* A grammar is said to be in Greibach Normal Form (GNF) if all productions are of the form **A → aB** or **A → a**, where **A**, **B** are non-terminal symbols, and **a** is a terminal symbol.
