### Myhill-Nerode Theorem

- Myhill-Nerode theorem states that for any deterministic finite automaton (DFA), the number of distinct equivalence classes of states under the Myhill-Nerode equivalence relation is equal to the number of distinct subsets in the language accepted by the DFA.
- Myhill-Nerode equivalence relation is defined as: two states p and q of a DFA are equivalent if for every string w, p w-maps to an accepting state if and only if q w-maps to an accepting state.
- The Myhill-Nerode theorem provides a way to calculate the minimum number of states in a DFA that accepts a regular language. It does so by calculating the equivalence classes under the Myhill-Nerode equivalence relation.
- The importance of the Myhill-Nerode theorem is that it establishes a connection between the structure of a DFA and the language it accepts, enabling the minimization of DFAs. The minimum number of states in a DFA is equal to the number of equivalence classes of the Myhill-Nerode equivalence relation.

Then, we prove the other direction - if p and q are in the same subset, then they must be Myhill-Nerode equivalent. From these two implications, the theorem follows.
