### Definition for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars

1. Regular Grammar: A regular grammar is a formal grammar in which the production rules are of the form:

A → x	      (where A is a nonterminal symbol and x is a string of terminals and/or nonterminals)

or

A → a	      (where A is a nonterminal symbol and a is a terminal symbol)

Regular grammars generate regular languages. All regular grammars can be converted into finite state machines.

2. Non-Regular Grammar: A grammar is non-regular if it is not regular, i.e. if it contains production rules that are more complex than those allowed in a regular grammar. Non-regular grammars generate non-regular languages which are more expressive than regular languages. Context-free grammars are the simplest type of non-regular grammar.

3. Formal Language: A formal language is a set of strings defined by a formal grammar. It contains strings made up of symbols from an alphabet. The strings can be of any length, including zero.
