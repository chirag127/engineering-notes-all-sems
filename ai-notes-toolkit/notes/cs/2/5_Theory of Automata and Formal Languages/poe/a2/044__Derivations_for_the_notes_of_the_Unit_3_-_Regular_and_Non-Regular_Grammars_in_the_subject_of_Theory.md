### Derivations for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars

1. Regular Grammars:
- Regular grammars generate regular languages.
- They have rules of the form: A -> a|b|c where A is a non-terminal and a, b, c are terminals or A -> Bb where A and B are non-terminals.
- They have finite number of rules and finite number of symbols.
- Examples: Grammars generating binary strings of 0's and 1's.

2. Non-Regular Grammars:
- Non-regular grammars generate non-regular languages.
- They have rules of the forms: A -> aBb|bAc where A, B, C are non-terminals and a, b are terminals.
- They have either infinite number of rules or infinite number of symbols.
- Examples: Grammars generating context-free languages like palindromes, strings with equal number of a's and b's etc. which cannot be generated by regular grammars.

3. Properties:
- The languages generated by regular grammars are always regular languages whereas the languages generated by non-regular grammars are not always non-regular.
- Non-regular grammars have more generative power than regular grammars.
- Regular grammars can be finite state machines but non-regular grammars cannot.
