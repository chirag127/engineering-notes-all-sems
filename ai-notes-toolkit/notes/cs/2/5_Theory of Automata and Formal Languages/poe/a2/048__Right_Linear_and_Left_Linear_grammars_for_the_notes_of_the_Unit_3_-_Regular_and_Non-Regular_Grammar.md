### Right Linear and Left Linear grammars

Notes for Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars

- Right linear grammar: A grammar G = (V, Σ, P, S) is said to be right linear if every production in P is of the form A → aB or A → a where A, B ∈ V and a ∈ Σ.
- Left linear grammar: A grammar G = (V, Σ, P, S) is said to be left linear if every production in P is of the form A → Ba or A → a where A, B ∈ V and a ∈ Σ.
- Properties:
    - Every regular grammar is both right linear and left linear.
    - The languages generated by right linear grammars and left linear grammars are strict subsets of context-free languages.
    - The family of languages generated by right linear grammars and the family of languages generated by left linear grammars are incomparable.
- Examples:
    - Right linear grammar: S → aS | b
    - Left linear grammar: S → bS | a
- Applications: Right linear grammars and left linear grammars are useful in compiler design. They are used to specify the syntax of the programming languages in a concise manner.
