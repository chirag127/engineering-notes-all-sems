### Algebraic Method Using Arden’s Theorem

1. Arden's theorem states that for any regular expression E, there exists a finite state machine M such that L(M) = L(E) where L(M) and L(E) are languages accepted by M and denoted by E respectively.
2. The proof of Arden's theorem uses the algebraic laws of regular expressions to transform any regular expression into an equivalent regular expression in disjunctive normal form.
3. A regular expression is in disjunctive normal form if it is expressed as a disjunction (OR) of conjunctions (AND) of basic symbols/operators.
4. The transformation can be done in a finite number of steps due to the finite number of subexpressions in any regular expression. This yields a finite automaton with L(M) = L(E).
5. Hence, Arden's theorem provides an algebraic way to construct an equivalent finite state machine for any regular expression. This method is useful to analyze the language denoted by the expression and understand the working of the equivalent finite automaton.
