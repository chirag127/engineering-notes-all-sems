### Chomsky Normal Form(CNF)

- Every rule is of the form:

A -> BC or

A -> a

Where A, B and C are non-terminals and a is a terminal.
- There are no epsilon rules.
- The grammar has no useless symbols i.e. every non-terminal generates a string with at least one terminal.

Conversion to CNF:

1. Eliminate unit productions:

A -> B

Replace all occurrences of B with A.

2. Eliminate epsilon productions:

A -> Epsilon

Replace all occurrences of A with a new start symbol S.

Add:

S -> A

3. Eliminate indirect left recursion:

A -> Aalpha

Replace with:

A -> betaA'

A' -> alphaA' | Epsilon

4. Eliminate all other left recursions:

A -> Abeta

Replace with:

A -> betaA'

A' -> Epsilon

5. Distribute terminals over productions with two non-terminals on RHS:

A -> BC

Replace with:

A -> aB | aC

Where a is a terminal appearing in the strings generated by B and C.

The grammar is now in Chomsky Normal Form.

The key advantages of CNF are:

- It simplifies parsing.
- The machine can easily detect unreachability of symbols and useless symbols.
