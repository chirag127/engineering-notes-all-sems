### Languages for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

In the study of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, languages are an essential concept that plays a vital role in understanding different types of grammars. Languages are sets of strings formed from a given alphabet. In this unit, we will cover regular and non-regular grammars and their respective languages. Here are the languages that you should be familiar with:

#### Regular Languages

1. **Finite Languages**: A finite language consists of a finite number of strings formed from a finite alphabet.
2. **Regular Expressions**: A regular expression is a concise way of representing a set of strings that can be accepted by a deterministic finite automaton (DFA).
3. **Regular Sets**: A regular set is a set of strings that can be generated by a regular grammar.

#### Non-Regular Languages

1. **Context-Free Languages**: A context-free language is a language that can be generated by a context-free grammar. This type of language is not regular, and it cannot be recognized by a finite-state automaton.
2. **Context-Sensitive Languages**: A context-sensitive language is a language that can be generated by a context-sensitive grammar, which has rules of the form αAβ → αγβ, where A is a non-terminal symbol, and α and β are strings of terminals and non-terminals.
3. **Recursively Enumerable Languages**: A recursively enumerable language is a language that can be generated by a Turing machine. This type of language is not decidable, which means that it is impossible to determine whether a given string is a member of the language or not.

It is important to note that regular languages are a subset of context-free languages, which are a subset of context-sensitive languages, which are a subset of recursively enumerable languages. Therefore, regular languages are the most restrictive type of language, and recursively enumerable languages are the most general type of language.

In conclusion, understanding different types of languages is essential in the study of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages. Regular and non-regular languages have their respective grammars, which generate the languages. By mastering the different types of languages, you will be able to solve problems related to language recognition and generation efficiently.
