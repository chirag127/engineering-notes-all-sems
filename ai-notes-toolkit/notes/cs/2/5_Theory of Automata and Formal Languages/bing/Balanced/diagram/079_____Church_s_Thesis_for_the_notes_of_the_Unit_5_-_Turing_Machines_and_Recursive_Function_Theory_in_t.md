### Church's Thesis

- Church's thesis, also known as Church's theorem or the Church-Turing thesis, is a principle that states that the **recursive functions** are the only functions that can be **mechanically calculated**.
- A recursive function is a function that can be defined by a **finite set of rules** that involves calling the function itself.
- A function is mechanically calculable if it can be computed by a **finite procedure** that does not require any **ingenuity** or **insight**.
- The thesis is named after the American mathematician **Alonzo Church** and the British mathematician **Alan Turing**, who independently proposed the idea in the 1930s.
- The thesis is not a formal statement that can be proved or disproved, but rather a **hypothesis** that expresses a **natural** and **intuitive** notion of computability.
- The thesis has several **equivalent formulations** that use different models of computation, such as **lambda calculus**, **Turing machines**, **register machines**, **recursive equations**, etc.
- The thesis has important implications for the **foundations** of mathematics, logic, computer science, and philosophy, as it sets the **limits** of what can be computed and what can be **decided**.
- The thesis also has practical applications for the **design** and **analysis** of algorithms, programming languages, compilers, and software systems.
