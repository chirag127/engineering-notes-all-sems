# Greibach Normal Form (GNF)

Greibach Normal Form (GNF) is a form of context-free grammar (CFG) that is used to represent regular and non-regular grammars in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages. It is named after Sheila Greibach, who introduced it in 1965.

Here are some key points to remember about GNF:

1. In GNF, the right-hand side of each production rule starts with a terminal symbol, followed by zero or more non-terminal symbols.
2. GNF is used to represent non-regular grammars, which cannot be represented by regular expressions or finite automata.
3. GNF is useful for parsing algorithms, as it allows for efficient top-down parsing.
4. Every context-free grammar can be converted into an equivalent grammar in GNF.
5. The conversion process involves removing left recursion and left factoring from the original grammar.

In summary, GNF is a useful tool for representing and working with non-regular grammars in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages. It allows for efficient parsing and can be used to convert any context-free grammar into an equivalent GNF grammar.
