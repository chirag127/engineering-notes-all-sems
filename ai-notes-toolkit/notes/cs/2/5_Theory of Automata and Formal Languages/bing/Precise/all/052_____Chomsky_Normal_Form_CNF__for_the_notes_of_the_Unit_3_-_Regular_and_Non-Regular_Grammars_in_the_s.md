### Chomsky Normal Form (CNF)

Chomsky Normal Form (CNF) is a specific form of context-free grammar (CFG) that is used in the study of formal languages and automata theory. It is named after Noam Chomsky, who introduced it in 1956.

Here are some key points to remember about CNF:

1. A CFG is in CNF if all of its production rules are of the form `A -> BC` or `A -> a`, where `A`, `B`, and `C` are non-terminal symbols and `a` is a terminal symbol.
2. Any context-free grammar can be transformed into an equivalent grammar in CNF.
3. The transformation process involves introducing new non-terminal symbols and production rules to eliminate rules that do not conform to the CNF format.
4. CNF is useful for parsing algorithms, as it allows for efficient top-down or bottom-up parsing of strings in the language generated by the grammar.
5. CNF is also useful for proving theorems about context-free languages, as it provides a standard form for CFGs that can simplify proofs.
