# Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars

### Definition

- A **regular grammar** is a formal grammar that describes a regular language.
- A regular language is a language that can be expressed using a regular expression or a finite automaton.
- Regular grammars can be classified into two types: **right-linear** and **left-linear**.
- A **right-linear grammar** is a regular grammar in which all production rules are of the form `A → wB` or `A → w`, where `A` and `B` are non-terminal symbols and `w` is a string of terminal symbols.
- A **left-linear grammar** is a regular grammar in which all production rules are of the form `A → Bw` or `A → w`, where `A` and `B` are non-terminal symbols and `w` is a string of terminal symbols.
- A **non-regular grammar** is a formal grammar that describes a language that is not regular.
- Non-regular languages cannot be expressed using regular expressions or finite automata.
- Non-regular grammars can generate languages that are more complex than regular languages.
- Examples of non-regular languages include the language of palindromes and the language of correctly nested parentheses.
