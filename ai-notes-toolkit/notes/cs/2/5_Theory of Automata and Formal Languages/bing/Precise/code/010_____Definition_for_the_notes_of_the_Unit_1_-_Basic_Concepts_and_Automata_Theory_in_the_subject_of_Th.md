### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

1. **Automata Theory** is the study of abstract machines and their ability to solve computational problems.
2. **Formal Languages** are sets of strings of symbols that are constructed according to specific rules.
3. **Alphabets** are finite sets of symbols used to construct strings in formal languages.
4. **Strings** are finite sequences of symbols from an alphabet.
5. **Languages** are sets of strings over an alphabet.
6. **Regular Languages** are a subset of formal languages that can be recognized by finite automata.
7. **Finite Automata** are abstract machines that can recognize regular languages.
8. **Deterministic Finite Automata (DFA)** are finite automata where for each state and input symbol, there is exactly one transition to a next state.
9. **Nondeterministic Finite Automata (NFA)** are finite automata where for each state and input symbol, there can be multiple transitions to next states.
10. **Context-Free Languages** are a subset of formal languages that can be generated by context-free grammars.
11. **Context-Free Grammars** are formal grammars where the production rules have a single nonterminal symbol on the left side.
12. **Pushdown Automata** are abstract machines that can recognize context-free languages.
13. **Turing Machines** are abstract machines that can recognize recursively enumerable languages.
14. **Recursively Enumerable Languages** are a subset of formal languages that can be recognized by Turing machines.
