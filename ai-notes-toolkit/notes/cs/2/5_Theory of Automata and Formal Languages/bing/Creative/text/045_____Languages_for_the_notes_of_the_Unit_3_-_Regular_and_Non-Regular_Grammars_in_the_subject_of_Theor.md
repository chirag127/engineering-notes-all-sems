### Languages

- In automata theory, a formal language is a set of strings of symbols drawn from a finite alphabet .
- A formal language can be specified either by a set of rules (such as regular expressions or a context-free grammar) that generates the language, or by a formal machine that accepts (recognizes) the language .
- A word is a finite string of symbols from the alphabet.
- A language is a set of words, which may be finite or infinite.
- A formal language is a mathematical object that can be studied and reasoned about using formal methods.
- Formal languages are classified into different types based on their expressive power and the complexity of the machines or rules that define them.
- Regular languages are the simplest and most widely used type of formal languages .
- Regular languages can be defined by regular expressions, finite automata, or regular grammars .
- Regular languages have many applications in computer science, such as pattern matching, lexical analysis, text processing, and coding theory .
- Regular languages are closed under various operations, such as union, concatenation, intersection, complement, and star.
- Non-regular languages are formal languages that are not regular.
- Non-regular languages can be defined by more powerful machines or rules, such as pushdown automata, context-free grammars, Turing machines, or recursively enumerable grammars.
- Non-regular languages have applications in areas such as parsing, programming languages, artificial intelligence, and computability theory.
- Non-regular languages are not closed under some operations, such as intersection, complement, or star.
- The pumping lemma is a technique to prove that a language is non-regular by showing that it violates a property that all regular languages have.
