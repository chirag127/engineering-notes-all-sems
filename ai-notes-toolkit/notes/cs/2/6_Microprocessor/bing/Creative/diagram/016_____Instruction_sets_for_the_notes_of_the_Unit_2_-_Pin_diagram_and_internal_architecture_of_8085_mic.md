## Pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor

- The 8085 microprocessor is an 8-bit microprocessor that operates on 8 bits of data at a time.
- It has 40 pins and requires a +5V power supply.
- The pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor is shown below:

![Pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor](https://electronicsdesk.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Pin-Diagram-of-8085-Microprocessor.png)

- The pins can be classified into six groups:

  - Address and data bus: These are 16 lines (A0-A15) and 8 lines (D0-D7) that carry the address and data respectively. The address bus is unidirectional and the data bus is bidirectional. The lower 8 bits of the address bus are multiplexed with the data bus and are separated by using a latch.
  - Control and status signals: These are 6 lines (ALE, RD, WR, IO/M, S0, S1) that control the read, write, and input/output operations of the microprocessor. ALE is the address latch enable signal that indicates the presence of a valid address on the multiplexed bus. RD is the read signal that enables the microprocessor to read data from memory or I/O devices. WR is the write signal that enables the microprocessor to write data to memory or I/O devices. IO/M is the input/output or memory signal that distinguishes between memory and I/O operations. S0 and S1 are the status signals that indicate the type of operation being performed by the microprocessor.
  - Power supply and clock signals: These are 3 lines (Vcc, Vss, X1, X2) that provide the power supply and the clock frequency to the microprocessor. Vcc is the positive supply voltage (+5V) and Vss is the ground (0V). X1 and X2 are the crystal or clock inputs that determine the operating frequency of the microprocessor. The internal clock generator divides the input frequency by 2 to generate the system clock.
  - Interrupts: These are 5 lines (TRAP, RST 7.5, RST 6.5, RST 5.5, INTR) that allow the microprocessor to respond to external events or devices that require its attention. TRAP is the non-maskable interrupt that has the highest priority and cannot be disabled. RST 7.5, RST 6.5, and RST 5.5 are the maskable interrupts that can be enabled or disabled by using the EI and DI instructions. They have different priorities and can be vectored to specific locations in memory. INTR is the general-purpose interrupt that can be used by any device to request the microprocessor's service. It has the lowest priority and can be masked by using the SIM instruction. It requires an acknowledgment signal (INTA) from the microprocessor to complete the interrupt process.
  - Serial I/O ports: These are 2 lines (SID, SOD) that allow the microprocessor to communicate with other devices using serial data transfer. SID is the serial input data line that receives a serial bit stream from an external device. SOD is the serial output data line that sends a serial bit stream to an external device. The serial data transfer can be controlled by using the RIM and SIM instructions.
  - Other signals: These are 8 lines (RESET IN, RESET OUT, READY, HOLD, HLDA, CLK OUT, IO/M S0 S1) that perform various functions such as resetting, halting, synchronizing, and testing the microprocessor. RESET IN is the reset input signal that initializes the microprocessor and clears the registers and flags. RESET OUT is the reset output signal that indicates that the microprocessor is being reset and can be used to reset other devices. READY is the ready signal that indicates that the memory or I/O device is ready to exchange data with the microprocessor. If the signal is low, the microprocessor waits until it becomes high. HOLD is the hold signal that requests the microprocessor to relinquish the control of the buses.
