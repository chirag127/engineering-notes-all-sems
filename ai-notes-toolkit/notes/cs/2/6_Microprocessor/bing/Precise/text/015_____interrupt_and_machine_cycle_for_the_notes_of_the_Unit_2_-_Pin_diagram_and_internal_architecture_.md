### Interrupt and Machine Cycle

Interrupts are signals that temporarily halt the normal execution of the microprocessor and allow it to execute a special subroutine, called an interrupt service routine (ISR), to handle a specific event. After the ISR is completed, the microprocessor returns to its normal execution. Interrupts can be triggered by external devices or by internal events within the microprocessor.

The machine cycle is the basic operation performed by the microprocessor. It consists of fetching an instruction from memory, decoding it, and executing it. The machine cycle is divided into several smaller cycles, including the instruction fetch cycle, the memory read/write cycle, and the execute cycle.

In the context of the 8085 microprocessor, the interrupt and machine cycle are important concepts to understand in order to fully grasp the internal architecture and operation of the microprocessor. These concepts are also relevant when studying the instruction sets, addressing modes, instruction formats, and instruction classification of the 8085 microprocessor.
