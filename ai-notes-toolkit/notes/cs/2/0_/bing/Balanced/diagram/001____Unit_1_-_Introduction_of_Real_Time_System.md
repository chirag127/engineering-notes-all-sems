## Unit 1 - Introduction of Real Time System

- A real time system is a system that must respond to events or inputs within a specified time limit, otherwise it may fail to meet its objectives or cause undesirable consequences.
- Real time systems are often used in applications that require high reliability, safety, or performance, such as industrial control, avionics, robotics, or multimedia.
- Real time systems can be classified into two types: hard real time systems and soft real time systems.
- Hard real time systems are systems that must meet all their deadlines, otherwise they may cause catastrophic failures or unacceptable losses. Examples of hard real time systems are nuclear reactor control, air traffic control, or pacemaker.
- Soft real time systems are systems that can tolerate some deadline misses, but the quality of service or user satisfaction may degrade. Examples of soft real time systems are video streaming, online gaming, or voice recognition.
- Real time systems have several characteristics that distinguish them from other types of systems, such as concurrency, predictability, responsiveness, dependability, and adaptability.
- Concurrency means that a real time system can handle multiple events or tasks simultaneously, and coordinate their execution and communication.
- Predictability means that a real time system can guarantee the timing behavior of its tasks and events, and avoid any unexpected delays or interference.
- Responsiveness means that a real time system can react to events or inputs quickly and efficiently, and meet the specified deadlines.
- Dependability means that a real time system can provide correct and consistent results, and handle any faults or errors gracefully.
- Adaptability means that a real time system can adjust to changing conditions or requirements, and optimize its performance or resource utilization.
