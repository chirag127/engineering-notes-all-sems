### Common Approaches to Real Time Scheduling

- Real time scheduling is the process of assigning execution priorities and time slots to tasks that have timing constraints and deadlines.
- Real time scheduling aims to ensure that tasks meet their deadlines, avoid deadline misses, and maximize system utilization and performance.
- There are different approaches to real time scheduling, depending on the characteristics of the tasks, the system, and the environment.
- Some of the common approaches are:

  - **Clock-driven approach**: This approach is also known as time-driven or table-driven approach. It is mainly used for hard real time systems, where all the properties of the tasks (such as arrival times, execution times, deadlines, periods, etc.) are known at design time. In this approach, a static schedule is computed offline, based on the worst-case execution times of the tasks, and stored in a table. The table specifies the exact time instants when each task should start and finish execution. A timer interrupts the system at these time instants and triggers the execution of the corresponding task. The advantage of this approach is that it guarantees that all tasks will meet their deadlines, as long as the system is schedulable. The disadvantage is that it is inflexible and cannot handle dynamic changes in the system or the environment, such as task arrivals, variations in execution times, faults, etc.    

  - **Round-robin approach**: This approach is also known as cyclic executive or time-slicing approach. It is commonly used for soft real time systems, where tasks do not have strict deadlines and can tolerate some delays. In this approach, tasks are scheduled in a repetitive manner, based on a fixed time slice allocated to each task. The scheduler rotates through the ready queue of tasks, and assigns the processor to each task for one time slice. If the task finishes within the time slice, it is removed from the queue. If not, it is preempted and placed at the end of the queue. The advantage of this approach is that it is simple and fair, and provides a good average response time for tasks. The disadvantage is that it does not consider the priorities or deadlines of the tasks, and may cause some tasks to miss their deadlines or starve for the processor.  

  - **Weighted round-robin approach**: This approach is a variation of the round-robin approach, where tasks are assigned different weights based on their importance or urgency. The weight of a task determines the length of the time slice allocated to it. For example, a task with a weight of 2 will get twice as much time as a task with a weight of 1. The scheduler still rotates through the ready queue of tasks, but assigns the processor to each task for a time slice proportional to its weight. The advantage of this approach is that it can differentiate between tasks based on their weights, and provide a better service to more important or urgent tasks. The disadvantage is that it still does not consider the deadlines of the tasks, and may cause some tasks to miss their deadlines or starve for the processor. 

  - **Priority-driven approach**: This approach is also known as preemptive or dynamic approach. It is widely used for both hard and soft real time systems, where tasks have different priorities and deadlines, and the system or the environment may change dynamically. In this approach, each task is assigned a priority, which may be static or dynamic, based on some criteria such as deadline, period, criticality, etc. The scheduler always selects the highest priority task from the ready queue, and assigns the processor to it. If a higher priority task arrives or becomes ready, the current task is preempted and the higher priority task is executed. The advantage of this approach is that it can handle dynamic changes in the system or the environment, and provide a better service to higher priority or more urgent tasks. The disadvantage is that it may cause some tasks to miss their deadlines or starve for the processor, due to priority inversion, blocking, or overload.
