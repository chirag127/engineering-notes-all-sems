# Dynamic Versus Static Systems

In the context of real-time scheduling, dynamic and static systems refer to the way tasks are scheduled and managed.

## Dynamic Systems
- In a dynamic system, the scheduling decisions are made at runtime.
- The scheduler makes decisions based on the current state of the system, including the current workload and resource availability.
- Dynamic systems are more flexible and can adapt to changing conditions, but they require more computational overhead to make scheduling decisions.

## Static Systems
- In a static system, the scheduling decisions are made offline, before the system starts running.
- The scheduler uses a predetermined schedule that is calculated based on the known characteristics of the tasks and the system.
- Static systems are less flexible, but they have lower computational overhead since the scheduling decisions are made in advance.

In summary, dynamic systems are more flexible and can adapt to changing conditions, while static systems have lower computational overhead but are less flexible. The choice between a dynamic and static system depends on the specific requirements of the real-time system in question.
