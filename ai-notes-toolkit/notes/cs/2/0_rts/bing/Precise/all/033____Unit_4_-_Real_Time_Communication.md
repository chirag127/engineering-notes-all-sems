## Unit 4 - Real Time Communication

Real-time communication refers to the exchange of information between two or more parties with minimal delay. This type of communication can take place through various mediums, including voice, video, and text.

1. **Voice communication** - This is the most common form of real-time communication and involves the transmission of audio signals between two or more parties. Examples of voice communication include phone calls and voice over IP (VoIP) services such as Skype.

2. **Video communication** - This involves the transmission of video signals in addition to audio signals. Video communication allows parties to see each other while they communicate, which can enhance the overall communication experience. Examples of video communication include video conferencing and video calls.

3. **Text communication** - This involves the exchange of text-based messages in real-time. Text communication can take place through various mediums, including instant messaging, chat rooms, and SMS.

Real-time communication is essential in many fields, including business, education, and healthcare. It allows parties to communicate and collaborate in real-time, which can improve productivity, enhance learning, and facilitate the delivery of healthcare services.
