### Model of Real Time Communication

Real-time communication (RTC) is a type of communication in which information is transmitted and received instantaneously, allowing individuals or systems to communicate with one another in real-time. Real-time communication is used in a variety of applications, including teleconferencing, online gaming, and instant messaging.

The Model of Real-Time Communication includes the following components:

1. Sender: The sender is the person or system that initiates the communication. The sender encodes the message, which is then transmitted to the receiver.

2. Channel: The channel is the medium through which the message is transmitted. The channel can be wired or wireless, and can include various types of networks, such as LAN, WAN, and the internet.

3. Receiver: The receiver is the person or system that receives the message. The receiver decodes the message, which is then interpreted.

4. Feedback: Feedback is the response of the receiver to the message. Feedback can be immediate or delayed, and can include various types of data, such as acknowledgments, error messages, and status updates.

5. Noise: Noise is any interference that can disrupt the communication process. Noise can include physical noise, such as static, as well as semantic noise, such as language barriers.

6. Protocols: Protocols are the rules and standards that govern the communication process. Protocols can include various types of protocols, such as transport protocols, network protocols, and application protocols.

7. Quality of Service (QoS): QoS is a measure of the level of reliability and performance of the communication process. QoS can include various types of metrics, such as bandwidth, latency, and jitter.

8. Applications: Applications are the programs and systems that use real-time communication. Applications can include various types of applications, such as video conferencing, online gaming, and instant messaging.

In conclusion, the Model of Real-Time Communication is a comprehensive framework that includes various components, such as sender, channel, receiver, feedback, noise, protocols, QoS, and applications. Understanding the Model of Real-Time Communication is critical for developing and deploying real-time communication systems that are reliable, performant, and scalable.
