
## Unit 1 - Introduction of Real Time System

1. Real-time systems are computer systems that are designed to respond to external events within a specified time period.

2. Real-time systems are used in a variety of applications such as process control, robotics, navigation, and multimedia.

3. Real-time systems are characterized by their ability to respond to external events within a certain time frame.

4. In order to ensure that a real-time system meets its performance requirements, it must be designed to meet certain criteria such as timing constraints, resource constraints, and reliability.

5. Real-time systems are typically divided into two categories: hard real-time systems and soft real-time systems.

6. Hard real-time systems are systems which must guarantee that deadlines are met, and are typically used in safety-critical applications.

7. Soft real-time systems are systems which do not need to guarantee that deadlines are met, and are typically used in applications where a response is expected within a certain time frame, but where missing the deadline is not catastrophic.

8. Real-time systems must be designed carefully to ensure that they meet their timing requirements. This includes careful consideration of hardware, software, and system design.
