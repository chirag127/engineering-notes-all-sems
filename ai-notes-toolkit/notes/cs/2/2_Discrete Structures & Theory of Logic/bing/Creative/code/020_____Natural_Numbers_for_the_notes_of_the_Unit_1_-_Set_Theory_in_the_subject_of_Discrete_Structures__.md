### Natural Numbers

- Natural numbers are the numbers that are used for counting and ordering. They are also called positive integers or counting numbers.
- The set of natural numbers is denoted by N = {1, 2, 3, ...}.
- Natural numbers have some properties, such as:
  - Closure: For any two natural numbers a and b, a + b and a * b are also natural numbers.
  - Commutativity: For any two natural numbers a and b, a + b = b + a and a * b = b * a.
  - Associativity: For any three natural numbers a, b and c, (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) and (a * b) * c = a * (b * c).
  - Identity: For any natural number a, a + 0 = a and a * 1 = a, where 0 and 1 are natural numbers.
  - Distributivity: For any three natural numbers a, b and c, a * (b + c) = a * b + a * c.
  - Order: For any two natural numbers a and b, either a < b, a = b or a > b, where <, = and > are symbols for less than, equal to and greater than, respectively.
- Natural numbers can be represented in different ways, such as:
  - Decimal system: The most common way of writing natural numbers, using 10 digits from 0 to 9 and a place value system. For example, 123 = 1 * 10^2 + 2 * 10^1 + 3 * 10^0.
  - Binary system: A way of writing natural numbers, using only 2 digits, 0 and 1, and a place value system based on powers of 2. For example, 1011 = 1 * 2^3 + 0 * 2^2 + 1 * 2^1 + 1 * 2^0.
  - Octal system: A way of writing natural numbers, using 8 digits from 0 to 7, and a place value system based on powers of 8. For example, 173 = 1 * 8^2 + 7 * 8^1 + 3 * 8^0.
  - Hexadecimal system: A way of writing natural numbers, using 16 digits from 0 to 9 and A to F, where A represents 10, B represents 11, and so on, and a place value system based on powers of 16. For example, 2A3 = 2 * 16^2 + 10 * 16^1 + 3 * 16^0.
- Natural numbers can be divided into different subsets, such as:
  - Even numbers: The natural numbers that are divisible by 2. For example, 2, 4, 6, 8, ...
  - Odd numbers: The natural numbers that are not divisible by 2. For example, 1, 3, 5, 7, ...
  - Prime numbers: The natural numbers that have exactly two positive divisors, 1 and themselves. For example, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, ...
  - Composite numbers: The natural numbers that have more than two positive divisors. For example, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, ...
  - Perfect numbers: The natural numbers that are equal to the sum of their proper positive divisors, excluding themselves. For example, 6 = 1 + 2 + 3, 28 = 1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14, ...
  - Abundant numbers: The natural numbers that are greater than the sum of their proper positive divisors, excluding themselves. For example, 12 = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 6, 18 = 1 + 2 + 3 + 6 + 9, ...
  - Deficient numbers: The natural numbers that are less than the sum of their proper positive divisors, excluding themselves. For example, 8 = 1 + 2 + 4, 10 = 1 + 2 + 5, ...
