### Subgroups and order

- A **subgroup** of a group is a subset of the group that is itself a group under the same operation as the original group.
- A subgroup is **proper** if it does not contain the whole group.
- A subgroup is **normal** if it is invariant under conjugation by any element of the group, that is, if g<sup>-1</sup>Hg = H for all g in the group.
- The **order** of a group is the number of elements in the group. The order of a subgroup is the number of elements in the subgroup. The order of an element is the smallest positive integer n such that a<sup>n</sup> = e, where e is the identity element of the group.
- The order of a subgroup divides the order of the group, by Lagrange's theorem.
- The order of an element is the same as the order of the cyclic subgroup generated by that element.
- The subgroups of a group form a complete lattice under inclusion, called the lattice of subgroups.
