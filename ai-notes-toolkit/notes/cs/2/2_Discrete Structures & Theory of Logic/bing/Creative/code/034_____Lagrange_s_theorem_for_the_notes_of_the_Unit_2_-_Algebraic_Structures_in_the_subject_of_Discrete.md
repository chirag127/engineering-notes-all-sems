### Lagrange's theorem for the notes of the Unit 2 - Algebraic Structures in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

- Lagrange's theorem is one of the central theorems of abstract algebra  .
- It states that in group theory, for any finite group say G, the order of subgroup H of group G divides the order of G  .
- The order of the group represents the number of elements .
- Mathematically, Lagrange's theorem can be written as:

```math
|G| = n|H|
```

where |G| and |H| are the orders of G and H respectively, and n is a positive integer called the index of H in G .

- Lagrange's theorem can be proved using the concept of cosets of a subgroup .
- A coset of a subgroup H in G is a subset of G that is obtained by multiplying all the elements of H by a fixed element of G .
- There are two types of cosets: left cosets and right cosets .
- A left coset of H in G is denoted by gH, where g is any element of G, and it is defined as:

```math
gH = {gh : h ∈ H}
```

- A right coset of H in G is denoted by Hg, where g is any element of G, and it is defined as:

```math
Hg = {hg : h ∈ H}
```

- The proof of Lagrange's theorem is based on the following observations :

  - Every element of G belongs to some coset of H, since e ∈ H and g ∈ gH for any g ∈ G.
  - Every coset of H has the same number of elements as H, since the map h ↦ gh is a bijection from H to gH for any g ∈ G.
  - Two cosets of H are either disjoint or equal, since if gH and g'H have a common element gh = g'h', then gH = g'h'H = g'H for any h, h' ∈ H.

- Therefore, the cosets of H partition G into n disjoint subsets, each of size |H|, and hence |G| = n|H| .
- Lagrange's theorem has many important consequences and applications in group theory, such as:

  - The order of any element of a group divides the order of the group, since the order of an element is the same as the order of the cyclic subgroup generated by it.
  - A group of prime order is cyclic and simple, since the only subgroups of such a group are the trivial subgroup and the group itself.
  - The number of elements of a given order in a group is a multiple of the index of the subgroup generated by such an element.
  - The Euler's totient function φ(n) can be computed as the order of the group of units of the ring of integers modulo n.
  - The Fermat's little theorem and the Euler's theorem can be derived as special cases of Lagrange's theorem applied to the group of units of the ring of integers modulo n.
