### Algebra of proposition

- Algebra of proposition is the subbranch of mathematical logic that studies propositions and logical operators.
- Propositions are statements that can be either true or false, such as "It is raining" or "2 + 2 = 4".
- Logical operators are symbols that define new propositions from one or more given propositions, such as "and", "or", "not", "if...then", and "if and only if".
- The most common symbols for logical operators are:

| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ∧ | Conjunction | And |
| ∨ | Disjunction | Or |
| ¬ | Negation | Not |
| → | Implication | If...then |
| ↔ | Equivalence | If and only if |

- The most common symbols for propositions are p, q, r, ..., which can be replaced by any proposition.
- The truth value of a proposition is either T (true) or F (false), and it can be determined by using a truth table, which lists all possible combinations of truth values for the propositions and the logical operators involved.
- For example, the truth table for p ∧ q is:

| p | q | p ∧ q |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T | T | T |
| T | F | F |
| F | T | F |
| F | F | F |

- The truth table shows that p ∧ q is true only when both p and q are true, and false otherwise.
- Algebra of proposition also studies the properties and rules of logical operators, such as commutativity, associativity, distributivity, identity, complement, idempotence, De Morgan's laws, etc.
- For example, commutativity states that p ∧ q is equivalent to q ∧ p, and p ∨ q is equivalent to q ∨ p, meaning that they have the same truth value for any values of p and q.
- Two propositions are said to be equivalent if they have the same truth value for any values of their variables, and this can be denoted by p ≡ q.
- For example, p → q is equivalent to ¬p ∨ q, and p ↔ q is equivalent to (p → q) ∧ (q → p).
- Algebra of proposition also allows us to simplify complex propositions by using the rules and properties of logical operators, and to express any proposition in a standard form, such as the disjunctive normal form (DNF) or the conjunctive normal form (CNF).
- A DNF is a proposition that is an OR of AND-terms, where each AND-term is an AND of variables or negations of variables, such as (p ∧ q) ∨ (p ∧ ¬r) ∨ (¬p ∧ r).
- A CNF is a proposition that is an AND of OR-terms, where each OR-term is an OR of variables or negations of variables, such as (p ∨ q) ∧ (p ∨ ¬r) ∧ (¬p ∨ r).
- Any proposition can be converted to a DNF or a CNF by using the rules and properties of logical operators, such as distributivity and De Morgan's laws.
