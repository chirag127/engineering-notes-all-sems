### Contradiction for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

- A contradiction is an assertion of propositional logic that is false in all situations; that is, it is false for all possible values of its variables .
- A contradiction has a logical form that cannot possibly be true, no matter what truth values are assigned to the sentence letters.
- A contradiction can be used as a tool to detect disingenuous beliefs and bias, or to establish the truth or validity of a proposition by showing that assuming the proposition to be false leads to a contradiction.
- A contradiction can be expressed using the negation symbol (~) and the conjunction symbol (•). For example, the proposition A • ~A is a contradiction, because it is always false, regardless of the truth value of A.
- A contradiction can also be expressed using the biconditional symbol (≡) and the exclusive or symbol (⊕). For example, the proposition A ≡ (A ⊕ B) is a contradiction, because it is equivalent to A • ~A.
- A contradiction can be identified by using a truth table, a logical equivalence, or a proof by contradiction  .
