### Cyclic Groups

- A group (G, ∘) is called a cyclic group if there exists an element a∈G such that G is generated by a. In other words, every element of G can be written as a power of a (or a multiple of a if the operation is additive) .
- The element a is called a generator or a primitive element of G. A cyclic group may have more than one generator. For example, the group (Z, +) is cyclic and both 1 and -1 are generators of Z.
- The order of a cyclic group G is equal to the order of the generator a, denoted by |a|. The order of a is the smallest positive integer n such that a^n=e, where e is the identity element of G .
- A cyclic group can be finite or infinite. A finite cyclic group has a finite number of elements, and an infinite cyclic group has infinitely many elements. For example, the group (Z, +) is an infinite cyclic group, and the group (Z_n, +) is a finite cyclic group of order n .
- Some properties of cyclic groups are  :
  - Every cyclic group is abelian, meaning that the group operation is commutative: a∘b=b∘a for all a, b∈G.
  - Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic, meaning that if H is a subgroup of G, then there exists an element h∈H such that H is generated by h.
  - Every finite cyclic group of order n is isomorphic to the group (Z_n, +), meaning that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the elements of G and the elements of Z_n that preserves the group operation.
  - Every element of a finite cyclic group G has an order that divides the order of G, meaning that if a∈G and |a|=m, then m is a factor of |G|.
  - A finite cyclic group G has exactly one subgroup of order d for each divisor d of |G|, meaning that if d is a factor of |G|, then there is a unique subgroup H of G such that |H|=d.
  - A finite cyclic group G has exactly φ(d) generators, where φ is the Euler's totient function, meaning that the number of elements a∈G such that G is generated by a is equal to φ(|G|).
