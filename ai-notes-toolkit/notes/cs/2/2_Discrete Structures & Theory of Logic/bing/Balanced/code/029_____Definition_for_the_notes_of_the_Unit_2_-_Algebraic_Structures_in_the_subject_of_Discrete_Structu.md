### Definition for the notes of the Unit 2 - Algebraic Structures in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

- An **algebraic structure** is a mathematical object that consists of a set of elements and one or more operations that satisfy certain properties or axioms.
- An **algebraic system** is an algebraic structure with a finite number of operations.
- **Discrete mathematics** is the branch of mathematics that deals with finite or discrete structures, such as sets, logic, graphs, and combinatorics.
- **Discrete structures** are objects that can be enumerated or counted, such as integers, permutations, truth tables, etc.
- **Theory of logic** is the study of the principles and methods of reasoning, using formal systems of symbols and rules.

Some examples of algebraic structures are:

- **Groups**: A set with a single operation that is associative, has an identity element, and has an inverse for every element.
- **Rings**: A set with two operations, usually called addition and multiplication, that form an abelian group under addition and a monoid under multiplication, and satisfy the distributive law.
- **Fields**: A set with two operations, usually called addition and multiplication, that form an abelian group under both operations, and have a nonzero element that is a multiplicative identity.
