### Tautology

1. A tautology is a propositional formula that is always true, no matter what truth values are assigned to its propositional variables.
2. A tautology is a statement that is always true under any possible valuation of its variables.
3. The tautology of a formula does not depend upon the specific values assigned to propositional variables used in the formula. It is true for all value assignments.
4. The most common way to prove that a formula is a tautology is to show that it is a logical truth by either proving it is valid in terms of the possible combinations of truth values or by constructing a truth table for it.
5. Some examples of tautologies are:
    - (P or not P) - Law of Excluded Middle
    - (P implies (Q implies P)) - Repetition rule
    - ((P implies Q) implies ((R implies P) implies (R implies Q))) - Transitivity

6. Methods to prove tautologies:
    - Truth tables - exhaustive checking of all possibilities
    - Logical equivalences - reasoning using equivalences like De Morgan's laws, distribution, etc.
    - Direct proofs - direct logical reasoning to show the formula is true under all conditions
