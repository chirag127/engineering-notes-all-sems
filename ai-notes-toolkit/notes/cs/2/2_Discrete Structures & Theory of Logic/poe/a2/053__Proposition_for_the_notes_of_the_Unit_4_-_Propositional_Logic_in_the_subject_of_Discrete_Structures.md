### Proposition for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic

1. Introduction
- Definition of propositional logic. Propositional logic deals with propositions and ways of combining propositions.
- Propositions: Statements that are either true or false. Represented by p, q, r, etc.
- Connectives: Logical operators used to combine propositions. E.g. AND, OR, NOT, IMPLIES, EQUIVALENT, etc.

2. Truth Tables
- Truth tables show all possible combinations of truth values of propositions and the resulting truth value of the overall statement.
- Rules for constructing truth tables:
    - List all possible combinations of individual propositions being true or false.
    - Evaluate the expression by determining if the connective makes the overall statement true or false based on the truth values of the propositions.
- Examples of truth tables for basic connectives: AND, OR, NOT

3. Logical Equivalence
- Two propositions are logically equivalent if they have the same truth value for all combinations of truth values of variables.
- Used to simplify logical expressions.
