### Theory of Inference for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic

1. Inference: The process of deriving some information from given information is called inference. Inferences are made using inference rules.
2. Inference rules: The rules that are used to derive conclusions from given premises are called inference rules. The most common inference rules are:
- Modus ponens: If P → Q and P are both true, then Q must also be true.
- Modus tollens: If P → Q and Q is false, then P must also be false.
- Hypothetical syllogism: If P → Q and Q → R, then P → R.
- Disjunctive syllogism: If P ∨ Q is true and P is false, then Q must be true.
3. Validity and Soundness: An inference is valid if the conclusion follows from the premises. An inference is sound if it is valid and the premises are true. Sound inferences preserve truth.
4. Proof: A proof is a sequence of statements beginning with premises (propositions given as true) and ending with a conclusion (target proposition to show is true). Each statement in the proof either is a given premise or follows from previous statements by inference. A proof establishes the validity of an inference.
