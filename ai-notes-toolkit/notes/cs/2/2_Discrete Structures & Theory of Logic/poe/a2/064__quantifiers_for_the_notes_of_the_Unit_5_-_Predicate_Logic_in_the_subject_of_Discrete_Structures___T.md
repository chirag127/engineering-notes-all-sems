### Quantifiers

- Universal quantifier: For all. Denoted by ∀. Example: ∀x (x > 0) means for all x, x is greater than 0.
- Existential quantifier: There exists. Denoted by ∃. Example: ∃x (x > 0) means there exists an x such that x is greater than 0.
- Quantifiers are used to specify the scope of variables. They are placed before the variable.
- The scope of a quantifier is the part of the expression that follows it. Any restrictions on the variable are within the scope.
- The domain of discourse or simply the domain is the set of objects that the variable ranges over.
- Many mathematical statements can be precisely expressed using quantifiers. For example:

∀x ∈ N, x + 1 > x (For all natural numbers x, x + 1 is greater than x)
∃x ∈ R, x2 = 2 (There exists a real number x such that x squared is 2)

- Quantifiers can be nested to express more complex logical conditions. The innermost quantifier has the smallest scope.
- Universal quantifier takes precedence over existential quantifier. So an expression with both will be evaluated from left to right. For example: ∀x ∃y P(x, y) means for all x, there exists a y such that P(x, y) is true.
