### Definition and terminology for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic:

1. Graph: A graph is a mathematical structure that represents a relationship between entities. It consists of vertices (or nodes) and edges that connect the vertices.
2. Vertex (or node): A vertex (or node) refers to an entity in the graph. It is represented by a dot or a circle.
3. Edge: An edge refers to the relationship between two vertices. It is represented by a line connecting two vertices.
4. Degree of a vertex: The degree of a vertex refers to the number of edges connected to that vertex.
5. Directed graph: A directed graph is a graph where the edges have directions associated with them. The direction is indicated by an arrow.
6. Undirected graph: An undirected graph is a graph where the edges have no directions associated with them.
7. Weighted graph: A weighted graph is a graph where each edge is assigned a weight or cost. The weights are used to represent the cost or capacity of the edge.
8. Adjacency: Two vertices are said to be adjacent if there is an edge connecting them. Adjacent vertices share an edge.
9. Path: A path refers to a sequence of edges between two vertices. It represents a way to travel from one vertex to another.
10. Cycle: A cycle refers to a closed path in a graph where the starting and ending vertex is the same.
11. Connected graph: A connected graph is a graph where there is a path between every pair of vertices.
12. Disconnected graph: A disconnected graph is a graph where there is no path between at least one pair of vertices. It consists of two or more connected components.
