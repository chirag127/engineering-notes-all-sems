### Bipartite graphs for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A bipartite graph is a graph whose vertices can be divided into two disjoint and independent sets $U$ and $V$ such that every edge connects a vertex in $U$ to one in $V$.

- A bipartite graph has no odd-length cycles.
- The degree of all vertices in the same set must be equal.
- A graph is bipartite if and only if it has no odd-length cycles. This is a useful characterization of bipartite graphs.
- Bipartite graphs are also characterized by the property that the neighbors of every vertex can be properly two-colored (the two colorings correspond to the partition into $U$ and $V$).
- A maximum bipartite matching is a matching of maximum size in a bipartite graph. Bipartite matching has applications in job scheduling.
- The vertices of a bipartite graph can be arranged in two layers with each edge connecting a vertex in one layer to a vertex in the other layer. This makes bipartite graphs natural models for two-sided markets and other relations between two distinct types of entities.
- Checking whether a given graph is bipartite can be done in linear time using BFS or DFS.
