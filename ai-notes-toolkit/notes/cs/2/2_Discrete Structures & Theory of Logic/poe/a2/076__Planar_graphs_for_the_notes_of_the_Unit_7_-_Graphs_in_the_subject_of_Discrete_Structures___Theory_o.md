### Planar graphs

- A planar graph is a graph that can be drawn in a plane such that no edges intersect each other except at their endpoints.
- A graph is planar if it can be drawn on a flat surface with no edge intersections.
- Some key properties of planar graphs:
  - Maximum number of edges <= 3(n-2) where n is the number of vertices.
  - Maximum number of triangles = n/3.
  - Have at least one vertex of degree less than or equal to 5.
  - Every planar graph has a vertex of degree less than or equal to 3.
- Applications of planar graphs:
  - Modelling networks with non-intersecting connections like transportation systems, circuits, etc.
  - Testing connectivity and path finding in networks.
- Examples of planar graphs:
  - Cycle graph - All vertices have degree 2.
  - Complete graph - K4 is planar but K5 is not planar.
  - Grid graph - Formed by vertices on a grid with edges between adjacent vertices.
- Some related concepts:
  - Planar embeddings - Different ways of drawing a planar graph in a plane.
  - Kuratowski's theorem - A graph is planar if and only if it does not contain a subdivision of K5 or K3,3 as a minor.
  - Constrained planar graphs - Planar graphs with additional constraints like requiring certain edges, limiting maximum degree, etc.
