### Digital circuits and Boolean algebra

- Digital circuits are electronic circuits that operate on digital signals. They are designed to receive digital input and produce a digital output that is a logical function of the input.
- Boolean algebra is a system for representing and manipulating logical expressions by using the binary variables 0 and 1 along with logical operators like AND, OR and NOT. It forms the basis for the design of digital circuits.
- The basic logic gates are AND, OR and NOT gates. They perform the logical operations on input signals and produce output. By combining these gates, any logical function can be implemented.
- Truth tables are a convenient way to show the input and output combinations of a digital circuit. The output values can be determined for every possible combination of input values. They are useful in simplifying Boolean expressions.
- Karnaugh maps (K-maps) provide an alternative way of simplifying Boolean expressions. They are used to simplify expressions having up to 4-6 variables. The key steps in simplification using K-maps are:
    - Group 1s in the map to form the largest groups possible.
    - Form a prime implicant for each group.
    - The final simplified expression is the OR of all prime implicants.
- Boolean functions can also be represented and manipulated using binary numbers. Each binary digit represents an input variable and its value represents the function for that combination of inputs. This is known as the binary method.
