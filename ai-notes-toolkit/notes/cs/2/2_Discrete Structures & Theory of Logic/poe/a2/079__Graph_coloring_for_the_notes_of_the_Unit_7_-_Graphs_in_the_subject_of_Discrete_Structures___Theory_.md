### Graph coloring for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Graph coloring is a way of coloring the vertices of a graph such that no two adjacent vertices share the same color.
2. The minimum number of colors required to color a graph G is called the chromatic number of G and is denoted by χ(G).
3. A graph is k-colorable if it can be colored using at most k colors such that no two adjacent vertices have the same color.
4. Determining if a graph is 3-colorable is NP-complete. It implies that determining if a graph is k-colorable is NP-complete for any fixed k >= 3.
5. A proper coloring of a graph is a coloring where adjacent vertices have different colors. The proper chromatic number of a graph is the minimum number of colors in a proper coloring.
6. Applications of graph coloring include scheduling problems, frequency assignment problems, and register allocation in compilers.
