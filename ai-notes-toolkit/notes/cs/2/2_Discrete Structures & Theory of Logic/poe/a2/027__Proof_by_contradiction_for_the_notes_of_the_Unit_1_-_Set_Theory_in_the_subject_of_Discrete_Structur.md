### Proof by contradiction for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic:

1. Proof by contradiction is a method of proof where you assume the opposite of what you are trying to prove and show that it leads to a contradiction.
2. If P is the statement to be proved, you assume ~P (the negation of P).
3. Then you show that ~P leads to a contradiction.
4. Since a contradiction cannot be true, the only possibility is that P must be true.
5. For example, to prove that the square root of 2 is irrational:
 - Assume sqrt(2) is rational.
 - Then sqrt(2) = p/q where p and q are integers.
 - Squaring both sides gives 2 = p^2/q^2.
 - 2q^2 = p^2 (multiplying both sides by q^2).
 - 2 is even, so p^2 is even. This means p is even.
 - Now sqrt(2) = (2k)/q (since p is even, p = 2k for some integer k).
 - This is a contradiction since we cannot have sqrt(2) expressed as the ratio of two integers.
 - Therefore, the assumption that sqrt(2) is rational must be false. Hence, sqrt(2) is irrational.
