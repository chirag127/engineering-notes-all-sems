### Lagrange's theorem

* Lagrange's theorem states that if G is a finite group and H is a subgroup of G, then the order of H divides the order of G.
* In other words, if n is the order of G and h is the order of H, then h must divide n.
* Proof: Let g1, g2, ..., gn be a list of representatives of the left cosets of H in G. Then n = |G| and each gi is a member of G. Therefore, gih is also a member of G for each i. But gih = gjhk for some j, k and h. Since G is finite, there must be repeats in the list g1h, g2h, ..., gnh. Thus h divides n.
* Consequences: No subgroup of a finite cyclic group can have order greater than the group order. A nontrivial proper subgroup of a finite group cannot have the same order as the group. If G is a nontrivial finite group and p is a prime divisor of |G|, then G has an element of order p.
* The theorem does not hold for infinite groups - the integers under addition, for example, have a subgroup (the even integers) whose order does not divide the order of the group.
