
### Introduction to Unit 3 - Lattices in Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic 

1. A lattice is a partially ordered set in which any two elements have a unique supremum (also called a least upper bound) and a unique infimum (also called a greatest lower bound). 
2. A lattice can be represented graphically as a network of points, with lines connecting them. 
3. In the context of discrete structures, lattices are used to model the relationships between different elements. 
4. Lattices can be used to represent logical relationships between propositions, as well as to represent sets of objects with certain properties. 
5. A lattice is said to be complete if every pair of elements has a supremum and an infimum. 
6. A lattice is said to be distributive if the operations of meet and join are distributive over each other. 
7. A lattice is said to be modular if it satisfies the modular law. 
8. A lattice is said to be Boolean if it satisfies the laws of Boolean algebra. 
9. A lattice is said to be homogeneous if it is invariant under the action of a group of automorphisms. 
10. Lattices can be used to study the properties of algorithms, as well as to represent the structure of a problem space.
