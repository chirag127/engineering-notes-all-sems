
### Contradiction for the Notes of Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. A contradiction is a statement that is always false. It is a statement that is logically inconsistent.

2. In propositional logic, a contradiction is a statement that is logically equivalent to the negation of itself. For example, the statement "A and not A" is a contradiction.

3. A contradiction is an important concept in propositional logic because it can be used to prove the validity of an argument. If two statements are logically equivalent to each other, then the argument is valid.

4. Contradictions are also used to prove the invalidity of an argument. If two statements are logically equivalent to each other, then the argument is invalid.

5. Contradictions are also used to prove the truth of a statement. If a statement is logically equivalent to its negation, then the statement is true.
