
### Tautology for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in the Subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. A **tautology** is a statement that is always true, no matter what the values of its variables.
2. A tautology can be expressed in propositional logic using the logical connectives such as **AND**, **OR**, **NOT**, and **IF-THEN**.
3. A tautology can also be expressed using **quantifiers** such as **FOR ALL** and **THERE EXISTS**.
4. A tautology can be used to prove the validity of an argument.
5. Tautologies are useful in proving theorems in mathematics and logic.
6. Tautologies can also be used to simplify complex logical expressions.
7. Tautologies can be used to prove the consistency of a set of premises.
