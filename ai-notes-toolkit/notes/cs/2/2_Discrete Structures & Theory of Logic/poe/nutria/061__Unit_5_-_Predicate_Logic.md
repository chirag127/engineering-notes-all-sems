
## Unit 5 - Predicate Logic

* Predicate logic is a type of formal logic that extends propositional logic by allowing for the use of predicates, which are expressions that can be true or false of objects.
* Predicate logic is a more expressive language than propositional logic, as it allows for the expression of complex relationships between objects.
* Predicate logic is composed of two types of expressions: atomic sentences and compound sentences.
* Atomic sentences are statements that are either true or false, and do not contain any other expressions.
* Compound sentences are statements that are composed of atomic sentences and logical connectives.
* Logical connectives are symbols that can be used to form compound sentences, such as 'and', 'or', 'not', 'if-then', 'if-and-only-if', and 'for-all'.
* Predicate logic is used to express complex relationships between objects, and can be used to reason about the truth of statements.
