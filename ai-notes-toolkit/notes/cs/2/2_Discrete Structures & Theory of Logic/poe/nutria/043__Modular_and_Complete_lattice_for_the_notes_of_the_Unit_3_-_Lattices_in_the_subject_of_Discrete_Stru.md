
### Modular and Complete Lattice

* A lattice is a partially ordered set in which any two elements have a unique supremum (also called a least upper bound or join) and a unique infimum (also called a greatest lower bound or meet).
* A lattice is said to be **modular** if for all elements $a,b,c$ in the lattice, if $a \leq b$ then $(a \vee c) \wedge b = a \vee (c \wedge b)$.
* A lattice is said to be **complete** if every subset of the lattice has a supremum and an infimum.
* A lattice is said to be **modular and complete** if it is both modular and complete.
* Examples of modular and complete lattices include the powerset lattice and the lattice of subspaces of a vector space.
