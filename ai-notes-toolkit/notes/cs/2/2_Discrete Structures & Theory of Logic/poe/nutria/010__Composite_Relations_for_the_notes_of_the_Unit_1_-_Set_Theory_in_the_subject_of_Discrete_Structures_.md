
### Composite Relations for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the Subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. A **composite relation** is a relation that is composed of two or more component relations.
2. Composite relations are useful for representing complex relationships between two or more sets of data.
3. Examples of composite relations include:
    - Cartesian product: the set of all possible ordered pairs of elements from two sets
    - Union: the set of all elements that are in either of two sets
    - Intersection: the set of all elements that are in both of two sets
    - Complement: the set of all elements that are in one set but not in the other
4. Composite relations can be used to represent relationships between sets of data in a concise and efficient manner.
5. Composite relations can also be used to model real-world situations, such as the relationship between two sets of people, or the relationship between two sets of objects.
