### Discourse competence

Discourse competence refers to the ability to understand and produce connected text or speech, such as conversations, narratives, or expository texts. It is one of the four components of communicative competence, along with linguistic, sociolinguistic, and strategic competence.

Some key aspects of discourse competence include:
- Coherence: the ability to connect ideas and information in a logical and meaningful way.
- Cohesion: the use of linguistic devices, such as pronouns, conjunctions, and discourse markers, to link sentences and ideas together.
- Organization: the ability to structure text or speech in a clear and effective manner, using techniques such as outlining, paragraphing, and signposting.
- Genre awareness: understanding the conventions and expectations of different types of text or speech, such as academic writing, business letters, or casual conversation.

Developing discourse competence involves practicing skills such as summarizing, paraphrasing, and synthesizing information, as well as analyzing and producing different types of text or speech. It is an important aspect of effective communication in both personal and professional contexts.
