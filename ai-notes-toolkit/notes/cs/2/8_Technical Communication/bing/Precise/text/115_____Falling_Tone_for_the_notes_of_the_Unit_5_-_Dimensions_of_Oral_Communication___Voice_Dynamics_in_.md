### Falling Tone
- A falling tone is a type of intonation pattern in speech where the pitch of the voice falls at the end of a sentence or phrase.
- It is commonly used to indicate the end of a statement or to convey finality or certainty.
- In English, a falling tone is often used at the end of a declarative sentence, such as "I am going to the store."
- It can also be used to convey a sense of authority or command, such as in the sentence "Sit down."
- In contrast, a rising tone is often used to indicate a question or uncertainty.
- The use of falling and rising tones can vary between languages and dialects.
- In oral communication, the use of appropriate intonation patterns, including falling tones, can help convey meaning and emotion effectively.
- In the context of voice dynamics, the use of falling tones can add variety and interest to speech, and can help convey the intended message more effectively.
