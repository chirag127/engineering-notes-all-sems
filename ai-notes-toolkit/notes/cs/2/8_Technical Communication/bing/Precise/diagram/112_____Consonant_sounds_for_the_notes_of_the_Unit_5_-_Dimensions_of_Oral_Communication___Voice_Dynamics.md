### Consonant sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

1. Consonant sounds are produced when the airflow is obstructed in some way by the articulators (lips, teeth, tongue, etc.) in the vocal tract.
2. There are several ways to classify consonants, including by place of articulation, manner of articulation, and voicing.
3. Place of articulation refers to where in the vocal tract the obstruction occurs, and includes bilabial, labiodental, dental, alveolar, post-alveolar, retroflex, palatal, velar, and glottal.
4. Manner of articulation refers to how the obstruction is made, and includes stops, fricatives, affricates, nasals, liquids, and glides.
5. Voicing refers to whether the vocal cords vibrate during the production of the sound, and includes voiced and voiceless consonants.
6. Consonant sounds play an important role in the production of speech and in the perception of speech by listeners.
7. Proper production and perception of consonant sounds is essential for clear and effective communication.
