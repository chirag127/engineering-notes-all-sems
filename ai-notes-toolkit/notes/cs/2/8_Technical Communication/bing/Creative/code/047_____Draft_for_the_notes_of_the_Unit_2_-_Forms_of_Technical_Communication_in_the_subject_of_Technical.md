# Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

## Introduction

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as written, oral, visual, or digital.
- Technical communication can be classified into two main categories: academic and professional.

## Academic Technical Communication

- Academic technical communication is the type of communication that is used in academic settings, such as schools, colleges, and universities.
- Academic technical communication can have different purposes, such as informing, persuading, analyzing, or evaluating.
- Academic technical communication can have different formats, such as essays, reports, research papers, presentations, or posters.
- Academic technical communication requires following certain standards and conventions, such as citation, referencing, formatting, and style.

## Professional Technical Communication

- Professional technical communication is the type of communication that is used in professional settings, such as workplaces, industries, and organizations.
- Professional technical communication can have different purposes, such as instructing, explaining, proposing, or recommending.
- Professional technical communication can have different formats, such as manuals, proposals, memos, letters, emails, or websites.
- Professional technical communication requires following certain principles and guidelines, such as clarity, accuracy, conciseness, and ethics.

## Comparison and Contrast

- Academic and professional technical communication have some similarities and differences.
- Similarities:
  - Both types of communication aim to communicate effectively and efficiently to a specific audience and purpose.
  - Both types of communication use various forms and modes of communication, such as written, oral, visual, or digital.
  - Both types of communication require careful planning, drafting, revising, and editing.
- Differences:
  - Academic technical communication is more formal, structured, and analytical, while professional technical communication is more informal, flexible, and practical.
  - Academic technical communication is more focused on the content and the sources, while professional technical communication is more focused on the context and the outcomes.
  - Academic technical communication is more regulated by the academic institutions and disciplines, while professional technical communication is more influenced by the professional fields and situations.
