### Definition

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as manuals, reports, proposals, memos, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, etc.
- Technical communication can be written, spoken, visual, or multimodal, depending on the needs and preferences of the audience and the purpose.
- Technical communication is different from other types of communication because it requires:
  - Accuracy: The information and instructions must be correct, precise, and reliable.
  - Clarity: The information and instructions must be easy to understand, follow, and use.
  - Conciseness: The information and instructions must be brief, relevant, and to the point.
  - Coherence: The information and instructions must be organized, structured, and connected logically.
  - Consistency: The information and instructions must be uniform, compatible, and harmonious.
  - Completeness: The information and instructions must cover all the necessary aspects and details.
  - Courtesy: The information and instructions must be respectful, polite, and considerate of the audience.
  - Ethics: The information and instructions must be honest, fair, and responsible.
- Technical communication is also influenced by the context, such as the situation, the environment, the culture, the technology, and the expectations of the audience and the purpose.
