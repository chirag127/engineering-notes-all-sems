### Rising tone

- A rising tone is a change in the pitch of the voice that goes up at the end of an utterance.
- A rising tone can indicate a question, uncertainty, surprise, or interest.
- A rising tone can also be used to signal that the speaker is not finished and expects a response from the listener.
- A rising tone is common in some varieties of English, such as Australian English, where it is also known as high-rising terminal or Australian Question Intonation.
- A rising tone can affect the voice and tone of technical communication, which is the style and manner of conveying information in a clear, concise, and professional way .
- A rising tone can make the technical communication sound less confident, authoritative, or assertive, which may not be appropriate for the rhetorical situation, which is the audience, purpose, and context of the writing.
- A rising tone can also create confusion or misunderstanding, especially if the listener is not familiar with the speaker's accent or intonation patterns.
- Therefore, a rising tone should be used carefully and sparingly in technical communication, and only when it matches the intended meaning and effect of the message.
