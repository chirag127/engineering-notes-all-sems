### Completeness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, which are: clarity, conciseness, correctness, completeness, courtesy, consideration, and concreteness .
- Completeness means that the communication must convey all the facts and information required by the audience, and answer the questions of who, what, when, where, why, and how  .
- Completeness is important for technical communication because it helps to promote goodwill, avoid misunderstandings, and achieve the desired purpose of the message .
- To ensure completeness in technical communication, both the text and the visual components of the document should be examined and revised.
- Some strategies for achieving completeness in technical communication are :
  - Provide all the necessary details and background information for the audience to understand the message and take action.
  - Use clear and specific language and avoid vague or ambiguous terms.
  - Organize the information logically and use headings, lists, tables, graphs, and other visual aids to highlight the main points and support the text.
  - Anticipate and address the potential questions, concerns, or objections of the audience and provide evidence, examples, or references to back up the claims.
  - Include a clear and courteous call to action, such as a request, a recommendation, or an offer, and state the benefits or consequences of following or ignoring the message.
  - Check the accuracy, relevance, and completeness of the information and sources, and cite them properly.
  - Proofread and edit the document for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and format errors.
