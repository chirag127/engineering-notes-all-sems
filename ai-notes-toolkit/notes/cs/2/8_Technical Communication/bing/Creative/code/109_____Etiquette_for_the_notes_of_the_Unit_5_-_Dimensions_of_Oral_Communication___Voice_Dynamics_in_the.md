### Etiquette for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Etiquette is the set of rules and conventions that govern the behavior and conduct of individuals or groups in a given social or professional context.
- Etiquette is important for oral communication because it helps to create a positive impression, build rapport, avoid misunderstandings, and show respect and courtesy to the speaker and the audience.
- Some of the general etiquette rules for oral communication are:

  - Prepare and practice your speech or presentation beforehand, and know your purpose, audience, and main points.
  - Dress appropriately and professionally for the occasion, and avoid distracting accessories or gestures.
  - Arrive on time and check the equipment and the room layout before you start.
  - Greet the audience and introduce yourself and your topic clearly and briefly.
  - Maintain eye contact, posture, and facial expressions that convey confidence, interest, and enthusiasm.
  - Use a clear, loud, and varied voice that matches your message and tone, and avoid fillers, slang, jargon, or offensive language.
  - Adapt your speech or presentation to the audience's needs, expectations, and feedback, and use appropriate examples, evidence, and visual aids to support your points.
  - Organize your speech or presentation logically and coherently, and use transitions, signposts, and summaries to guide the audience.
  - Be concise and respectful of the time limit, and end with a strong conclusion and a call to action or a question.
  - Thank the audience and invite questions or comments, and answer them politely and honestly.
  - Follow up with the audience if necessary, and send thank-you notes or emails to the organizers or hosts.
