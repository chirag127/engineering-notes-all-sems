### Reading and Comprehension

- Reading is the process of decoding written symbols and extracting meaning from them.
- Comprehension is the ability to understand and interpret what is read.
- Reading and comprehension are essential skills for technical communication, as they enable you to access, analyze, and evaluate information from various sources and formats.
- Reading and comprehension involve several cognitive processes, such as:
  - **Attention**: focusing on the relevant information and ignoring distractions.
  - **Perception**: recognizing and identifying the symbols, words, and sentences.
  - **Memory**: storing and retrieving information from short-term and long-term memory.
  - **Reasoning**: making inferences, deductions, and judgments based on the information.
  - **Metacognition**: monitoring and regulating one's own reading and comprehension strategies and performance.
- Reading and comprehension can be improved by applying various strategies, such as:
  - **Previewing**: skimming the text to get an overview of the main idea, structure, and purpose.
  - **Predicting**: making guesses about what the text will say based on the title, headings, images, and prior knowledge.
  - **Questioning**: generating and answering questions before, during, and after reading to clarify and deepen one's understanding.
  - **Summarizing**: condensing the main points and supporting details of the text in one's own words.
  - **Paraphrasing**: restating the text in one's own words without changing the meaning or tone.
  - **Synthesizing**: integrating and organizing information from multiple sources and perspectives into a coherent whole.
  - **Evaluating**: assessing the credibility, accuracy, relevance, and usefulness of the information and the arguments presented in the text.
  - **Applying**: using the information and the skills learned from the text to solve problems, make decisions, or communicate effectively.
