### Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as written, oral, visual, or digital.
- Some common forms of technical communication are:

  - **Reports**: Documents that present facts, analysis, and recommendations on a topic. Reports can be formal or informal, internal or external, analytical or informational, etc. Examples of reports are feasibility studies, progress reports, lab reports, etc.
  - **Proposals**: Documents that persuade the reader to accept a plan, idea, or solution. Proposals can be solicited or unsolicited, internal or external, research or business, etc. Examples of proposals are grant proposals, project proposals, business proposals, etc.
  - **Instructions**: Documents that explain how to do something or how something works. Instructions can be procedural or descriptive, online or offline, user or technical, etc. Examples of instructions are manuals, guides, tutorials, etc.
  - **Correspondence**: Documents that communicate with a specific person or group. Correspondence can be formal or informal, internal or external, personal or professional, etc. Examples of correspondence are letters, memos, emails, etc.
  - **Presentations**: Oral or visual communication that delivers information or ideas to an audience. Presentations can be informative or persuasive, live or recorded, individual or group, etc. Examples of presentations are speeches, lectures, webinars, etc.
  - **Graphics**: Visual elements that support or enhance the message of a document. Graphics can be static or dynamic, simple or complex, quantitative or qualitative, etc. Examples of graphics are charts, tables, diagrams, images, etc.
  - **Websites**: Digital platforms that provide information or services to users. Websites can be static or dynamic, informational or interactive, personal or professional, etc. Examples of websites are blogs, wikis, portfolios, etc.

- Each form of technical communication has its own purpose, audience, structure, style, and design principles. Technical communicators should choose the appropriate form for their communication situation and follow the best practices for that form.
