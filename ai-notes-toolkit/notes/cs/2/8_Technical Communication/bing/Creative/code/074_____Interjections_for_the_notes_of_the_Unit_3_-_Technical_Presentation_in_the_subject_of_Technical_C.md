### Interjections

- Interjections are words or phrases that express emotions or feelings.
- Interjections are usually followed by an exclamation mark (!) or a comma (,).
- Interjections can be used to show surprise, joy, anger, disappointment, agreement, hesitation, etc.
- Interjections can also be used to attract attention, fill pauses, or introduce a topic.
- Interjections are not grammatically connected to the rest of the sentence. They can be placed at the beginning, middle, or end of a sentence.
- Some examples of interjections are:

  - Wow! That was an amazing presentation.
  - Ouch, that must have hurt.
  - Well, I don't know what to say.
  - Hmm, let me think about it.
  - Hey, can you please listen to me?
  - Oh, I see what you mean.
  - Ugh, I hate this assignment.
  - Yes, I agree with you.
  - Oops, I made a mistake.
  - Hooray, we passed the exam.

- Interjections can be useful in technical presentations to:

  - Express your enthusiasm and interest in the topic.
  - Engage the audience and make them feel involved.
  - Add variety and tone to your speech.
  - Emphasize a point or contrast a statement.
  - Show your reaction or feedback to a question or comment.

- However, interjections should be used sparingly and appropriately in technical presentations to:

  - Avoid sounding too informal or unprofessional.
  - Avoid interrupting the flow or clarity of your message.
  - Avoid distracting or annoying the audience.
  - Avoid overusing the same interjections or repeating yourself.

- Some tips for using interjections effectively in technical presentations are:

  - Choose interjections that suit your purpose, audience, and context.
  - Use interjections that match your tone and voice.
  - Use interjections that are relevant and meaningful to the content.
  - Use interjections that are natural and spontaneous, not forced or scripted.
  - Use interjections that are polite and respectful, not rude or offensive.
  - Use interjections that are clear and concise, not vague or ambiguous.
  - Use interjections that are appropriate and consistent, not excessive or contradictory.
