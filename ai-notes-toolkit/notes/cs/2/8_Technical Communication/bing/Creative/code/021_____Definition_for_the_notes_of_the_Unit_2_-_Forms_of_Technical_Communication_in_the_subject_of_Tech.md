# Definition for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as written, oral, visual, or digital.
- Some examples of technical communication are manuals, reports, proposals, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, videos, etc.
- Technical communication has some common characteristics, such as:
  - Accuracy: The information should be correct, precise, and verifiable.
  - Clarity: The information should be easy to understand and follow.
  - Conciseness: The information should be brief and to the point, avoiding unnecessary details or repetition.
  - Coherence: The information should be logically organized and connected, using appropriate transitions and signposts.
  - Completeness: The information should cover all the relevant aspects and answer the possible questions of the audience.
  - Consistency: The information should follow the same style, format, tone, and terminology throughout the document or presentation.
  - Courtesy: The information should respect the audience's needs, expectations, and preferences, and avoid any offensive or biased language.
  - Ethics: The information should be honest, fair, and respectful of the intellectual property and privacy of others.
  - Relevance: The information should be tailored to the specific purpose, context, and audience of the communication.
  - Usability: The information should be easy to access, navigate, and apply by the audience.
