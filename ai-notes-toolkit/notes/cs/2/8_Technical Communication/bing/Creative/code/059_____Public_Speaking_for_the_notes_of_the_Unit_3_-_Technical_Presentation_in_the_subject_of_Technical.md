# Public Speaking for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

Public speaking is the process of communicating information to an audience. It is usually done before a large group of people, like in a school, the workplace or even in a personal setting. Public speaking can have many purposes, such as informing, persuading, entertaining or inspiring the listeners.

Technical presentation is a type of public speaking that involves presenting complex or specialized information in a clear, concise and engaging way. Technical presentation can be challenging because it requires the speaker to balance the technical details with the audience's needs and interests. Technical presentation can be used for various purposes, such as teaching, demonstrating, proposing or reporting on a project.

The following are some tips for delivering a successful technical presentation:

- Know your subject matter. A great presentation is not about reading a bunch of slides - your attendees are capable of reading much faster on their own. You should be able to explain your topic in your own words, without relying on the slides. You should also be prepared to answer any questions that may arise from the audience. 
- Know your audience. Knowing your audience is crucial for any presentation, but it’s even more important for a technical one. You should tailor your presentation to the level of knowledge, interest and expectations of your audience. You should avoid using jargon, acronyms or technical terms that your audience may not understand. You should also use examples, analogies or stories that are relevant and relatable to your audience.  
- Configure your IDE (Integrated Development Environment). If you are going to show any code or live demos in your presentation, you should make sure that your IDE is set up properly and works smoothly. You should test your code and demos beforehand and have a backup plan in case something goes wrong. You should also adjust the font size, color and contrast of your IDE to make it easy for the audience to see and follow. 
- Minimize distractions. You should avoid anything that may distract you or your audience from your presentation. You should turn off your phone, email notifications or any other alerts that may interrupt you. You should also close any unnecessary tabs or windows on your computer and mute any sounds or music. You should also dress appropriately and professionally for your presentation and avoid any accessories or gestures that may draw attention away from your message.  
- Get the right equipment. You should make sure that you have the right equipment and tools for your presentation. You should check the compatibility and functionality of your laptop, projector, microphone, speakers, pointer, etc. You should also bring any adapters, cables, batteries or backup devices that you may need. You should arrive early and test your equipment before your presentation and have a backup plan in case something fails.  
- Rehearse in advance. You should practice your presentation several times before the actual day. You should rehearse your speech, your slides, your code and your demos. You should also time yourself and adjust your content and pace accordingly. You should seek feedback from others and improve your presentation based on their suggestions. You should also anticipate any potential questions or challenges from the audience and prepare your responses.  
- Finally, enjoy the experience. You should try to relax and have fun during your presentation. You should show enthusiasm and passion for your topic and convey it to your audience. You should also smile, make eye contact and use appropriate body language to engage your audience. You should also be confident and positive and avoid apologizing or making excuses for your presentation. You should also thank your audience for their attention and invite them to ask questions or share their opinions.
