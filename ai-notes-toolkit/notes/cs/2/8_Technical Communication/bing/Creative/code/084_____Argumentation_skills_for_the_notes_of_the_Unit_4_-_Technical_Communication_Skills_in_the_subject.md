# Argumentation skills for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills

- Argumentation skills are the abilities to develop and present arguments in a clear, logical and persuasive way.
- Arguments are claims supported by reasons and evidence that aim to convince others of a certain point of view or course of action.
- Argumentation skills are important for technical communication because they help to:
  - Analyze and evaluate complex issues and problems
  - Communicate and justify solutions and recommendations
  - Persuade and influence stakeholders and audiences
  - Demonstrate critical thinking and reasoning
- Some of the key aspects of argumentation skills are:
  - Style: The choice of words, tone, and structure that suit the purpose, context, and audience of the argument.
  - Devices: The rhetorical techniques that enhance the effectiveness and appeal of the argument, such as analogy, comparison, contrast, examples, etc.
  - Analysis: The process of breaking down the issue or problem into its components and identifying the main claims, reasons, and evidence.
  - Cohesion: The use of transitions, connectors, and signposts that link the parts of the argument and create a smooth and coherent flow.
  - Emphasis: The use of repetition, parallelism, and highlighting to stress the main points and key messages of the argument.
  - Critical thinking: The ability to question assumptions, identify biases, evaluate sources, and avoid fallacies in the argument.
  - Nuances: The recognition and acknowledgment of different perspectives, counterarguments, and limitations of the argument.
  - Exposition, narration, and description: The modes of writing that provide background information, context, and details to support the argument.
