### Strategic competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Strategic competence is the ability to use various verbal and nonverbal strategies to communicate effectively and overcome difficulties when communication breakdowns occur  .
- Strategic competence is an aspect of communicative competence, which is the overall ability to use language appropriately and effectively in different contexts and situations.
- Strategic competence performs assessment, planning, and execution functions in determining the most effective means of achieving a communicative or language learning goal. It connects declarative, procedural, and conditional knowledge .
- Strategic competence involves the following types of strategies :
  - Avoidance or reduction strategies: These are strategies to avoid or reduce the risk of communication problems, such as using simpler words, paraphrasing, or switching topics.
  - Achievement or compensatory strategies: These are strategies to overcome communication problems when they occur, such as using gestures, asking for clarification, or guessing the meaning of unknown words.
  - Stalling or time-gaining strategies: These are strategies to fill pauses or buy time to think, such as using fillers, repetitions, or hesitation devices.
  - Self-monitoring or self-repair strategies: These are strategies to check and correct one's own errors or misunderstandings, such as self-correction, reformulation, or summarization.
  - Interactional or co-operative strategies: These are strategies to involve the interlocutor or co-operate with them to facilitate communication, such as asking for feedback, confirmation, or help, or giving feedback, confirmation, or help.
- Strategic competence is important for technical communication because :
  - It helps to communicate complex or technical information in a clear and concise way, especially to non-expert audiences.
  - It helps to adapt to different communication situations and purposes, such as informing, instructing, persuading, or negotiating.
  - It helps to initiate innovation and bring strategic change by using one's knowledge to put new ideas into action.
  - It helps to cope with communication challenges and uncertainties, such as unfamiliar topics, cultural differences, or technical difficulties.
