# Discourse competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Discourse competence is the ability to understand and express oneself in a given language, combining grammatical forms and meaning to achieve a linked text (oral or written), in different communication situations .
- Discourse competence is one of the components of communicative competence, which also includes linguistic, sociolinguistic, pragmatic, and strategic competence .
- Discourse competence involves the following skills and knowledge:
  - Textual competence: the ability to read and comprehend different types of texts, such as narratives, descriptions, arguments, explanations, etc., and to identify their structure, purpose, and audience.
  - Rhetorical competence: the ability to use appropriate language and strategies to achieve a communicative goal, such as persuading, informing, entertaining, etc., and to adapt to the context, situation, and interlocutors .
  - Genre competence: the ability to recognize and produce different genres of communication, such as reports, letters, emails, presentations, etc., and to follow their conventions, formats, and styles .
  - Lexical competence: the ability to use a specific vocabulary and terminology related to a topic, field, or discourse community, and to avoid ambiguity, redundancy, or confusion .
  - Cohesion and coherence competence: the ability to organize and connect ideas, sentences, and paragraphs in a logical and meaningful way, using grammatical and lexical devices, such as pronouns, conjunctions, transitions, etc., and to maintain a consistent theme and focus throughout the text .
- Discourse competence is relevant for technical communication because it enables the communicator to:
  - Produce clear, concise, accurate, and effective texts that meet the needs and expectations of the audience and the purpose of the communication .
  - Use appropriate language and tone to establish rapport, credibility, and trust with the audience, and to avoid misunderstanding, misinterpretation, or offense .
  - Select and use the most suitable genre, format, and style for the communication situation, and to follow the norms and standards of the field or discipline .
  - Demonstrate knowledge and expertise in the topic, field, or discourse community, and to use the relevant vocabulary and terminology correctly and consistently .
  - Organize and structure the text in a logical and coherent way, and to guide the audience through the text using appropriate signals and transitions .
