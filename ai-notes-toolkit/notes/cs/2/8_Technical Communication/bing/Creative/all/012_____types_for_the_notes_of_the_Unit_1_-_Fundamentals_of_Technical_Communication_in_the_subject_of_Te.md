# Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

## What is Technical Communication?

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can be written, oral, visual, or a combination of these modes.
- Technical communication can be used for various purposes, such as informing, instructing, persuading, or entertaining.
- Technical communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context, audience, and purpose.

## What are the Characteristics of Technical Communication?

- Technical communication is audience-centered, which means it focuses on the needs, expectations, and preferences of the intended audience.
- Technical communication is purpose-oriented, which means it has a clear and specific goal or objective.
- Technical communication is accurate, which means it provides correct and reliable information and avoids errors and ambiguities.
- Technical communication is clear, which means it uses simple and precise language and avoids jargon and unnecessary details.
- Technical communication is concise, which means it uses the minimum words necessary to convey the message and avoids redundancy and repetition.
- Technical communication is coherent, which means it has a logical and consistent structure and flow and uses transitions and signposts to guide the reader.
- Technical communication is ethical, which means it respects the rights and interests of the audience and the source of information and avoids plagiarism and bias.

## What are the Types of Technical Communication?

- Technical communication can be classified into two broad categories: informative and persuasive.
- Informative technical communication provides factual information and instructions to the audience, such as reports, manuals, memos, emails, etc.
- Persuasive technical communication aims to influence the attitude or behavior of the audience, such as proposals, advertisements, brochures, etc.
- Technical communication can also be classified into different genres or forms, depending on the purpose, audience, and context, such as:

  - Technical description: describes the features, functions, and appearance of an object, process, or phenomenon.
  - Technical definition: explains the meaning, usage, and classification of a term, concept, or principle.
  - Technical classification: groups and categorizes objects, processes, or phenomena based on common characteristics or criteria.
  - Technical comparison: compares and contrasts two or more objects, processes, or phenomena based on similarities and differences.
  - Technical process: explains the steps, stages, or phases of a procedure, operation, or activity.
  - Technical instruction: provides directions, guidelines, or commands for performing a task, using a device, or achieving a goal.
  - Technical summary: provides a brief overview of the main points, findings, or conclusions of a longer document or presentation.
  - Technical abstract: provides a concise and comprehensive summary of the purpose, scope, methods, results, and implications of a research paper or project.
  - Technical proposal: presents a plan, idea, or solution for a problem, need, or opportunity and argues for its feasibility, benefits, and costs.
  - Technical report: presents the results, analysis, and recommendations of a research, investigation, or evaluation on a specific topic or issue.
  - Technical review: evaluates the quality, performance, or suitability of a product, service, or work based on criteria and standards.
  - Technical recommendation: provides advice, suggestions, or feedback on a product, service, or work based on personal experience or expertise.
  - Technical correspondence: communicates information, requests, responses, or opinions on a professional or technical matter, such as letters, emails, memos, etc.
  - Technical presentation: delivers information, instructions, or arguments on a technical topic or issue to an audience, using oral, visual, or multimedia modes.
