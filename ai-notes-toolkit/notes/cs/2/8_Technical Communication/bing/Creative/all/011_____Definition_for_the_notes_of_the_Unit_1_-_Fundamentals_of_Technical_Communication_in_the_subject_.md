# Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

### Definition

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can be written, oral, visual, or multimodal, depending on the needs and preferences of the audience and the context of the communication.
- Technical communication is characterized by the following features:

  - Accuracy: The information and instructions should be correct, precise, and verifiable.
  - Clarity: The information and instructions should be easy to understand, without ambiguity or confusion.
  - Conciseness: The information and instructions should be brief, without unnecessary details or repetition.
  - Coherence: The information and instructions should be logically organized and connected, with a clear purpose and flow.
  - Completeness: The information and instructions should cover all the relevant aspects and answer all the possible questions of the audience.
  - Consistency: The information and instructions should follow the same style, format, terminology, and tone throughout the communication.
  - Courtesy: The information and instructions should respect the audience's needs, expectations, and feelings, and avoid any offensive or biased language.
  - Ethics: The information and instructions should be honest, fair, and responsible, and avoid any plagiarism, fabrication, or misrepresentation.
  - Relevance: The information and instructions should be appropriate and useful for the audience and the situation.
  - Usability: The information and instructions should be easy to access, navigate, and apply by the audience.
