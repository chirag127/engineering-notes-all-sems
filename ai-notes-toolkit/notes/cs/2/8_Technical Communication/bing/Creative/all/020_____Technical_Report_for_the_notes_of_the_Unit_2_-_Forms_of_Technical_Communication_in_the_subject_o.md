# Technical Report for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

## Introduction

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as reports, manuals, proposals, memos, letters, emails, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, etc.
- Technical communication can be classified into two broad categories: academic and professional.
- Academic technical communication is aimed at students, researchers, and scholars who need to communicate their findings, methods, and arguments in a clear and rigorous manner.
- Professional technical communication is aimed at customers, clients, colleagues, managers, and stakeholders who need to understand, use, or evaluate a product, service, or process.
- Technical communication can also be classified into four main types: informative, instructive, persuasive, and expressive.
- Informative technical communication provides factual information about a topic, such as definitions, descriptions, classifications, comparisons, etc.
- Instructive technical communication provides directions or guidance on how to perform a task, such as procedures, instructions, manuals, etc.
- Persuasive technical communication provides arguments or reasons to convince the audience to adopt a certain point of view, action, or decision, such as proposals, recommendations, feasibility studies, etc.
- Expressive technical communication provides personal opinions, feelings, or experiences about a topic, such as reviews, reflections, blogs, etc.

## Characteristics of Technical Communication

- Technical communication has some common characteristics that distinguish it from other forms of communication, such as:
  - Purpose: Technical communication has a clear and specific purpose that guides the content, structure, and style of the document or presentation.
  - Audience: Technical communication has a defined and intended audience that influences the tone, level of detail, and format of the document or presentation.
  - Context: Technical communication has a specific context or situation that affects the expectations, needs, and preferences of the audience and the writer or speaker.
  - Accuracy: Technical communication has to be accurate and precise in providing factual information, data, and evidence that support the purpose and argument of the document or presentation.
  - Clarity: Technical communication has to be clear and concise in expressing the main ideas, concepts, and instructions that the audience needs to understand and follow.
  - Coherence: Technical communication has to be coherent and logical in organizing the information, arguments, and evidence in a consistent and meaningful way that helps the audience follow the flow and structure of the document or presentation.
  - Completeness: Technical communication has to be complete and comprehensive in covering all the relevant and necessary information, arguments, and evidence that the audience needs to know and consider.
  - Courtesy: Technical communication has to be courteous and respectful in addressing the audience, acknowledging their perspectives, and avoiding any offensive or biased language or tone.
  - Ethics: Technical communication has to be ethical and responsible in using and citing the sources of information, data, and evidence, as well as in respecting the intellectual property, privacy, and confidentiality of others.

## Process of Technical Communication

- Technical communication involves a systematic and iterative process that consists of four main stages: planning, drafting, revising, and delivering.
- Planning: This stage involves analyzing the purpose, audience, and context of the document or presentation, as well as gathering and evaluating the information, data, and evidence that will be used.
- Drafting: This stage involves creating the first version of the document or presentation, following the appropriate format, structure, and style for the type and genre of technical communication.
- Revising: This stage involves reviewing and improving the content, organization, and language of the document or presentation, as well as checking for any errors, inconsistencies, or gaps in the information, data, and evidence.
- Delivering: This stage involves finalizing and submitting the document or presentation, as well as presenting or distributing it to the intended audience, using the appropriate medium, mode, and channel of communication.
