## Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Technical communication can take various forms, depending on the context, purpose, audience, and medium of communication. Some of the common forms of technical communication are:

- **Technical reports**: These are documents that present the results of research, analysis, or experimentation. Technical reports usually have a formal structure, such as an abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, and references. Technical reports are often used to communicate scientific or engineering findings, recommendations, or proposals.
- **User manuals**: These are documents that provide instructions on how to use a product, system, or service. User manuals usually have a clear and concise language, a logical sequence of steps, and visual aids, such as diagrams, screenshots, or icons. User manuals are often used to guide the users of software, hardware, appliances, or devices.
- **Proposals**: These are documents that propose a solution to a problem, a plan for a project, or a request for funding. Proposals usually have a persuasive tone, a clear statement of the problem, a description of the proposed solution, a justification of the benefits, and a budget and timeline. Proposals are often used to seek approval, support, or collaboration from a client, a sponsor, or a stakeholder.
- **Presentations**: These are oral or visual forms of communication that deliver information, ideas, or arguments to an audience. Presentations usually have a clear purpose, a logical structure, a coherent flow, and an engaging delivery. Presentations are often used to inform, persuade, or instruct an audience, such as a class, a conference, or a meeting.
- **Emails**: These are electronic messages that communicate information, requests, or feedback to one or more recipients. Emails usually have a concise and courteous language, a clear subject line, a salutation and a signature, and a relevant attachment or link. Emails are often used to communicate with colleagues, clients, or customers in a professional or academic setting.
