# Considerations for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information in a clear, concise, and accurate manner to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Forms of technical communication include written, oral, visual, and digital communication, as well as non-verbal communication such as gestures, body language, and facial expressions.
- Written communication is the most common form of technical communication, and it involves creating documents such as reports, manuals, proposals, memos, letters, emails, etc. Written communication requires careful planning, drafting, revising, editing, and proofreading to ensure clarity, accuracy, and effectiveness.
- Oral communication is the second most common form of technical communication, and it involves delivering information verbally through presentations, speeches, lectures, interviews, meetings, etc. Oral communication requires careful preparation, practice, delivery, and feedback to ensure confidence, credibility, and engagement.
- Visual communication is the use of images, graphics, charts, diagrams, maps, icons, symbols, etc. to supplement or replace verbal or written communication. Visual communication can enhance understanding, attention, memory, and appeal of technical information, but it also requires careful design, selection, and integration to avoid confusion, distraction, or misinterpretation.
- Digital communication is the use of electronic media and devices to create, store, transmit, and receive technical information. Digital communication includes websites, blogs, podcasts, videos, social media, etc. Digital communication can increase accessibility, interactivity, and timeliness of technical information, but it also requires careful consideration of audience, purpose, context, and ethics to avoid misuse, abuse, or infringement.
- Non-verbal communication is the use of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, tone of voice, etc. to convey or reinforce technical information. Non-verbal communication can complement, contradict, or substitute verbal or written communication, and it can affect the perception, attitude, and behavior of the audience. Non-verbal communication requires careful awareness, control, and interpretation to avoid misunderstanding, miscommunication, or offense.
