# Methods for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

Technical communication is the method of producing and sharing ideas and information in organizations, especially about technical or specialized topics, using various modes and media, and for various purposes and audiences  .

Some of the common forms of technical communication are:

- **Letters and emails**: These are written messages that convey information or requests to a specific person or group. They are usually formal, concise, and clear, and follow a standard format and style. Letters and emails are used for various purposes, such as applying for a job, making an inquiry, giving feedback, or reporting a problem.
- **Instructions**: These are step-by-step directions that guide the reader to perform a specific task or procedure. They are usually written in the imperative mood, using numbered or bulleted lists, and accompanied by graphics or illustrations. Instructions are used for various purposes, such as installing a software, operating a device, or cooking a recipe .
- **Status reports**: These are written documents that summarize the progress, achievements, challenges, and plans of a project or a team. They are usually written in the past tense, using headings, tables, charts, and graphs. Status reports are used for various purposes, such as updating the stakeholders, evaluating the performance, or identifying the risks and opportunities.
- **Project proposals**: These are written documents that present a plan or a solution for a specific problem or need. They are usually written in the future tense, using persuasive language, and following a standard structure and format. Project proposals are used for various purposes, such as seeking approval, funding, or collaboration for a project.
- **Websites and blogs**: These are online platforms that provide information or opinions about a specific topic or domain. They are usually written in the present tense, using interactive and engaging language, and following a consistent design and layout. Websites and blogs are used for various purposes, such as educating, informing, entertaining, or influencing the audience .
- **Online help**: These are online resources that provide assistance or guidance to the users of a software, system, or product. They are usually written in the present tense, using simple and direct language, and following a hierarchical and searchable structure. Online help are used for various purposes, such as explaining the features, functions, or commands of a software, system, or product, or troubleshooting the common problems or errors .
- **User manuals**: These are printed or online documents that provide comprehensive information about a product or a system. They are usually written in the present tense, using technical and precise language, and following a logical and modular structure. User manuals are used for various purposes, such as describing the components, specifications, or operations of a product or a system, or providing safety, maintenance, or warranty information .
- **Technical manuals**: These are printed or online documents that provide detailed information about a technical process or procedure. They are usually written in the present tense, using specialized and accurate language, and following a systematic and sequential structure. Technical manuals are used for various purposes, such as explaining the principles, theories, or methods of a technical process or procedure, or providing instructions, examples, or exercises for learning or applying a technical process or procedure .
- **White papers**: These are written documents that provide authoritative and in-depth information or analysis about a specific topic or issue. They are usually written in the present tense, using formal and objective language, and following a problem-solution structure. White papers are used for various purposes, such as informing, educating, or persuading the audience, or establishing the credibility or expertise of the author or the organization.
- **Specifications**: These are written documents that define the requirements, standards, or criteria for a product, system, process, or service. They are usually written in the present tense, using precise and consistent language, and following a tabular or graphical format. Specifications are used for various purposes, such as ensuring the quality, functionality, or compatibility of a product, system, process, or service, or providing the basis for testing, evaluation, or verification .
