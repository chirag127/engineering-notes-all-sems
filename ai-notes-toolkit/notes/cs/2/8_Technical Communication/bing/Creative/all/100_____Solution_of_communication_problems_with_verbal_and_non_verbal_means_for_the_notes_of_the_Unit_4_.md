# Solution of communication problems with verbal and non verbal means

Communication problems are any obstacles or barriers that prevent effective exchange of information, ideas, or feedback. Communication problems can occur in any context, such as interpersonal, group, organizational, or intercultural. Communication problems can have negative impacts on productivity, performance, relationships, and satisfaction.

Some common communication problems are:

- Lack of clarity or specificity
- Misunderstanding or misinterpretation
- Ambiguity or vagueness
- Inconsistency or contradiction
- Irrelevance or off-topic
- Distortion or exaggeration
- Omission or suppression
- Noise or interference
- Emotions or biases
- Conflicts or disagreements

Some possible solutions to communication problems are:

- Verbal means: These are the use of words, either spoken or written, to convey messages. Verbal means can help to:

  - Clarify or specify the purpose, goal, or expectation of the communication
  - Confirm or verify the understanding or interpretation of the message
  - Provide or request feedback or feedforward
  - Use simple, precise, and concrete language
  - Avoid jargon, slang, or acronyms
  - Use examples, analogies, or illustrations
  - Repeat or rephrase the key points
  - Summarize or conclude the main idea
  - Ask open-ended or probing questions
  - Listen actively and empathetically
  - Acknowledge or validate the other's perspective or feelings
  - Express or manage one's own emotions
  - Resolve or negotiate conflicts or disagreements

- Non-verbal means: These are the use of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, tone of voice, and other cues to convey messages. Non-verbal means can help to:

  - Reinforce or complement the verbal message
  - Emphasize or highlight the important points
  - Show interest or attention
  - Establish or maintain rapport or trust
  - Demonstrate or convey emotions
  - Regulate or control the flow of the communication
  - Indicate or signal agreement or disagreement
  - Persuade or influence the other
  - Assert or defend one's position or rights
  - Respect or accommodate the other's culture or preferences

Communication problems can be avoided or minimized by using both verbal and non-verbal means effectively and appropriately. Communication skills can be improved by practicing, observing, learning, and seeking feedback. Communication is a dynamic and complex process that requires constant adaptation and improvement.
