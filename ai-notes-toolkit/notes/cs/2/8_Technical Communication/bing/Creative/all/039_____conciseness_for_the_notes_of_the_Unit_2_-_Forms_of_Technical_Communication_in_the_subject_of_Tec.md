# Conciseness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Conciseness is the quality of using only the words that are necessary to convey a message, without redundancy or verbosity.
- Conciseness is important for effective technical communication, as it helps the readers to understand the information quickly and clearly, and avoids wasting their time and attention.
- Some strategies to achieve conciseness in technical communication are:

  - Eliminate unnecessary words, such as filler words, modifiers, repetitions, and vague expressions.
  - Use active voice instead of passive voice, unless the passive voice is more appropriate for the context or purpose.
  - Use parallel structure to organize similar ideas or items in a list or a sentence.
  - Use specific and concrete words instead of abstract and general words, unless the abstraction or generalization is intended or required.
  - Use positive statements instead of negative statements, unless the negation is essential or emphatic.
  - Use shorter and simpler words, sentences, and paragraphs, as long as they do not compromise the accuracy, clarity, or tone of the message.
  - Use tables, graphs, charts, diagrams, or other visual aids to present numerical, statistical, or spatial data, instead of lengthy verbal descriptions.
  - Use headings, subheadings, bullets, numbers, or other formatting devices to highlight the main points and organize the information logically and visually.
