# Encoding process for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Encoding is the process of turning thoughts into communication.
- The encoder is the person who creates the message, adapts it to the receiver, and transmits it across a channel.
- The encoding process is based on the purpose of communication and the relation between the sender and the receiver.
- The encoder uses symbols to communicate their message, such as words, gestures, images, sounds, etc.
- The encoder has to consider the following factors while encoding the message:
  - The content of the message: what is the main idea or information to be conveyed?
  - The context of the message: what is the situation or setting of the communication?
  - The code of the message: what is the system of symbols or language to be used?
  - The channel of the message: what is the medium or tool to be used for communication?
  - The receiver of the message: who is the intended audience or listener of the message?
- The encoder has to ensure that the message is clear, accurate, concise, coherent, and appropriate for the receiver and the channel.
- The encoder has to avoid any barriers or noise that may interfere with the encoding process, such as distractions, emotions, biases, assumptions, etc.
- The encoding process is influenced by the encoder's communication skills, knowledge, attitude, culture, and personality.
- The encoding process is also affected by the dimensions of oral communication, such as verbal, vocal, and visual aspects.
- The encoder has to use the voice dynamics, such as pitch, volume, rate, tone, and pauses, to enhance the message and convey emotions and attitudes.
- The encoder has to use the nonverbal communication, such as eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, and posture, to support the message and establish rapport with the receiver.
- The encoding process is not a one-way activity, but a dynamic and interactive one, that requires feedback from the receiver and adaptation from the encoder.
