# Methods of Presentation

Methods of presentation are the ways of delivering information to an audience, such as speech, visuals, storytelling, humor, etc. Different methods of presentation have different advantages and disadvantages, depending on the purpose, context, and audience of the presentation. Some of the common methods of presentation are:

- **Speech**: Speech is the most common and basic method of presentation. It involves using verbal language to communicate the main points, arguments, and conclusions of the topic. Speech can be enhanced by using vocal variety, gestures, eye contact, and pauses. Speech can be effective for engaging the audience, persuading them, and conveying emotions. However, speech can also be limited by the speaker's clarity, confidence, and credibility. Speech can be difficult to follow if the speaker speaks too fast, too slow, too softly, or too loudly. Speech can also be boring if the speaker uses monotone, filler words, or jargon. Speech can be improved by rehearsing, using notes, and getting feedback.

- **Visuals**: Visuals are the method of presentation that involves using images, graphs, charts, diagrams, videos, or animations to support the speech. Visuals can be effective for illustrating complex, abstract, or numerical information, attracting the audience's attention, and enhancing the speaker's credibility. However, visuals can also be distracting, confusing, or misleading if they are not relevant, clear, or accurate. Visuals can also be dependent on the availability and quality of the technology and equipment. Visuals can be improved by using appropriate design principles, such as contrast, alignment, repetition, and proximity, and by following the 6x6 rule (no more than six words per line and six lines per slide).

- **Storytelling**: Storytelling is the method of presentation that involves using narratives, anecdotes, or examples to illustrate the topic. Storytelling can be effective for creating interest, empathy, and connection with the audience, as well as for making the topic memorable and relatable. However, storytelling can also be irrelevant, inappropriate, or exaggerated if it does not match the purpose, context, and audience of the presentation. Storytelling can also be time-consuming, distracting, or confusing if it is not well-structured, coherent, or concise. Storytelling can be improved by using a clear beginning, middle, and end, by using sensory details and emotions, and by using a moral or a lesson.

- **Humor**: Humor is the method of presentation that involves using jokes, puns, irony, sarcasm, or wit to lighten the mood, entertain, or persuade the audience. Humor can be effective for breaking the ice, reducing tension, and creating rapport with the audience, as well as for highlighting the absurdity or irony of the topic. However, humor can also be offensive, inappropriate, or insensitive if it does not respect the culture, values, or beliefs of the audience. Humor can also be risky, unpredictable, or backfire if it is not well-timed, well-delivered, or well-received. Humor can be improved by knowing the audience, using self-deprecation, and avoiding controversial topics.
