## Introduction

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as reports, manuals, proposals, memos, presentations, etc.
- Technical communication can be classified into two broad categories: oral and written.

## Oral Technical Communication

- Oral technical communication is the delivery of information and instructions through spoken words, such as lectures, speeches, interviews, meetings, etc.
- Oral technical communication has some advantages, such as:
  - It allows for immediate feedback and clarification from the audience.
  - It can convey emotions and tone more effectively than written communication.
  - It can be more persuasive and engaging than written communication.
- Oral technical communication also has some disadvantages, such as:
  - It can be affected by external factors, such as noise, distractions, interruptions, etc.
  - It can be difficult to remember and document the information and instructions.
  - It can be less precise and accurate than written communication.

## Written Technical Communication

- Written technical communication is the delivery of information and instructions through written words, such as documents, emails, websites, blogs, etc.
- Written technical communication has some advantages, such as:
  - It can provide a permanent and verifiable record of the information and instructions.
  - It can be more detailed and comprehensive than oral communication.
  - It can be accessed and reviewed at any time and place by the audience.
- Written technical communication also has some disadvantages, such as:
  - It can be time-consuming and costly to produce and distribute.
  - It can be misinterpreted or misunderstood by the audience due to lack of context and non-verbal cues.
  - It can be less interactive and dynamic than oral communication.

## Factors to Consider When Choosing a Form of Technical Communication

- The choice of a form of technical communication depends on various factors, such as:
  - The purpose and goal of the communication.
  - The audience and their needs, expectations, and preferences.
  - The content and complexity of the information and instructions.
  - The context and situation of the communication.
  - The resources and constraints of the communication.
- Some general guidelines for choosing a form of technical communication are:
  - Use oral communication when:
    - The information and instructions are simple, urgent, or confidential.
    - The audience is small, familiar, or cooperative.
    - The communication requires feedback, discussion, or persuasion.
  - Use written communication when:
    - The information and instructions are complex, detailed, or permanent.
    - The audience is large, diverse, or distant.
    - The communication requires accuracy, clarity, or reference.
