# Consonant sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Consonants are letters that represent certain speech sounds, specifically sounds that involve blocking the air before it leaves the mouth, such as with the tongue, lips, or throat.
- There are 24 consonant sounds in English. They are described in terms of their articulation, which is the way the speech organs (such as the tongue, lips, and teeth) are positioned and moved to produce the sound .
- The consonant sounds can be classified into different types based on the following criteria  :
  - The place of articulation: the location of the speech organs that are involved in the sound production, such as bilabial (both lips), dental (tongue and teeth), alveolar (tongue and alveolar ridge), etc.
  - The manner of articulation: the degree and type of closure or constriction of the vocal tract, such as plosive (complete closure and sudden release), fricative (narrow constriction and turbulent airflow), affricate (complete closure and slow release), etc.
  - The voicing: the presence or absence of vibration of the vocal cords, such as voiced (vocal cords vibrate) or voiceless (vocal cords do not vibrate).

- Consonant sounds are important for oral communication and voice dynamics, as they affect the clarity, accuracy, and expressiveness of speech. Some tips for improving consonant sounds are:
  - Practice the correct articulation of each consonant sound by using a mirror, a recorder, or a feedback from a native speaker.
  - Pay attention to the differences between voiced and voiceless consonants, and avoid adding or omitting voicing where it is not needed.
  - Be aware of the consonant clusters, which are two or more consonants that occur together in a word or a syllable, such as /st/, /sk/, /pl/, etc. Make sure to pronounce each consonant sound clearly and distinctly, without dropping or adding any sounds.
  - Use appropriate stress, intonation
