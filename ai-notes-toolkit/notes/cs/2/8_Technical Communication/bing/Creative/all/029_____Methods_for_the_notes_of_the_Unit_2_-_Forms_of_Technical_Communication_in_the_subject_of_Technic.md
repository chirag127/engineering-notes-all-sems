# Methods for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Technical communication can take various forms, such as manuals, reports, proposals, memos, emails, presentations, etc. In this unit, we will learn about some of the methods for taking notes of the different forms of technical communication.

## Methods for taking notes of manuals

Manuals are documents that provide instructions or guidance on how to use a product, service, or system. Manuals can be divided into sections, such as introduction, overview, features, installation, operation, maintenance, troubleshooting, etc. Some of the methods for taking notes of manuals are:

- Use headings and subheadings to organize your notes according to the structure of the manual.
- Use bullet points, numbers, or letters to list the main steps or procedures in each section.
- Use symbols, abbreviations, or acronyms to save space and time.
- Use diagrams, tables, charts, or graphs to illustrate or summarize the information in the manual.
- Use highlighters, colors, or symbols to mark the important or difficult parts of the manual.
- Use questions, comments, or summaries to review or clarify the information in the manual.

## Methods for taking notes of reports

Reports are documents that present the results or findings of a research, investigation, or analysis. Reports can have different formats and purposes, such as technical reports, feasibility reports, progress reports, etc. Some of the methods for taking notes of reports are:

- Use the title, abstract, introduction, and conclusion to get an overview of the report and its main points.
- Use the table of contents, headings, subheadings, and captions to identify the structure and organization of the report.
- Use the outline method to write down the main ideas and supporting details of each section or paragraph in the report.
- Use the Cornell method to divide your paper into two columns: one for the main points and one for the notes, questions, or summaries.
- Use the mind map method to draw a diagram that shows the relationships and connections between the main ideas and details of the report.
- Use the annotation method to write comments, questions, or summaries in the margins or on sticky notes.

## Methods for taking notes of proposals

Proposals are documents that propose a solution, plan, or action to a problem, need, or opportunity. Proposals can be divided into sections, such as introduction, background, objectives, methods, benefits, costs, schedule, etc. Some of the methods for taking notes of proposals are:

- Use the SQ3R method to survey, question, read, recite, and review the proposal.
- Use the survey method to scan the title, introduction, conclusion, and headings of the proposal to get an overview of its purpose and scope.
- Use the question method to generate questions based on the headings or subheadings of the proposal, such as what, why, how, when, where, who, etc.
- Use the read method to read the proposal carefully and look for the answers to your questions.
- Use the recite method to recite or write down the answers to your questions in your own words.
- Use the review method to review your notes and check for accuracy and completeness.

## Methods for taking notes of memos

Memos are short and informal documents that communicate information, requests, or instructions within an organization. Memos can have a simple format, such as date, to, from, subject, and message. Some of the methods for taking notes of memos are:

- Use the inverted pyramid method to write down the most important information first, followed by the less important information.
- Use the key word method to write down the key words or phrases that capture the main points or messages of the memo.
- Use the paraphrase method to write down the main points or messages of the memo in your own words.
- Use the summary method to write down a brief and concise summary of the memo.
- Use the response method to write down your reaction, opinion, or feedback to the memo.
