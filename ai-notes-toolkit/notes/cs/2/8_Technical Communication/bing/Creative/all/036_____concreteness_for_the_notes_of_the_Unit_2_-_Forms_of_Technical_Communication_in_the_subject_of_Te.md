# Concreteness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Concreteness is an aspect of communication that means being specific, definite, and vivid rather than vague and general .
- A concrete communication uses specific facts and figures  and supports them with evidence from credible sources.
- Concreteness is one of the six Cs of effective communication, along with clarity, correctness, completeness, conciseness, and courtesy .
- Concreteness helps the audience better understand the message and reduces the risk of misunderstanding .
- Concreteness also enhances the credibility and persuasiveness of the message by providing specific examples or explanations .
- To make a message more concrete, one should use concrete language, which refers to tangible things or actions, rather than abstract language, which refers to intangible ideas or qualities.
- One should also replace adjectives or adverbs with specific words, such as numbers, dates, names, or measurements.
- For example, instead of saying "The deadline has been moved forward", one should say "The deadline is now March 20th".
- Concreteness is especially important in technical communication, which deals with complex and precise information that requires accuracy and clarity.
