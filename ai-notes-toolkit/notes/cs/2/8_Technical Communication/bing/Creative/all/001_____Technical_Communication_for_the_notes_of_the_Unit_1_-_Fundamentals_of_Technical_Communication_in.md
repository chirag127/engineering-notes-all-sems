# Technical Communication

## Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

### Technical Communication Definition

- Technical communication is the process of conveying scientific, engineering, or other technical information to a specific audience for a specific purpose  .
- Technical communication can be used to define, instruct, inform, or persuade the audience about technical or specialized topics, such as computer applications, medical procedures, or environmental regulations.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as manuals, reports, proposals, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, videos, etc.
- Technical communication requires the use of clear, concise, accurate, and ethical language, as well as the appropriate design, format, and media for the intended audience and purpose.

### Technical Communication Characteristics

- Technical communication has some distinctive characteristics that distinguish it from other types of communication, such as:

  - Audience-centered: Technical communication focuses on the needs, expectations, and preferences of the audience, and adapts the content, tone, style, and format accordingly.
  - Purpose-driven: Technical communication has a clear and specific goal, such as to inform, instruct, persuade, or recommend, and uses the most effective strategies and techniques to achieve it.
  - Collaborative: Technical communication often involves working with other writers, subject matter experts, editors, reviewers, and stakeholders, and requires the use of communication skills, teamwork, and feedback.
  - Ethical: Technical communication follows the principles of honesty, integrity, respect, and responsibility, and avoids plagiarism, falsification, misrepresentation, or harm to the audience or the society.
  - Research-based: Technical communication relies on credible, relevant, and current sources of information, and uses appropriate methods of data collection, analysis, and citation.
  - Visual: Technical communication makes use of graphics, images, charts, tables, diagrams, and other visual elements to enhance the comprehension, retention, and appeal of the information.
  - Technology-mediated: Technical communication uses various tools and platforms, such as word processors, graphic editors, web authoring software, content management systems, etc., to create, edit, publish, and distribute the information.
