# Correctness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Correctness is one of the six qualities of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, completeness, coherence, and courtesy.
- Correctness means that the details of a message are accurate, the grammar and punctuation are correct, and the appearance and tone are appropriate for the context and audience.
- Correctness is important for technical communication because it enhances the credibility of the writer, avoids confusion and misunderstanding, and prevents errors and misinformation .
- Some aspects of correctness in technical communication are:

  - Passive voice: The passive voice is often used in technical writing to emphasize the action or result rather than the agent or performer. However, the passive voice can also make the writing denser, more complex, and less direct. Therefore, the passive voice should be used sparingly and only when necessary.
  - Commands and directions: Technical writing often contains a lot of commands and directions, such as instructions, procedures, or recommendations. These should be written in the imperative mood, using clear and specific verbs and avoiding vague or ambiguous terms.
  - Bullets and lists: Technical writing often relies heavily on bulleted or numbered lists, which can help organize and highlight the main points, steps, or features of a topic. However, lists should also follow some rules of correctness, such as using parallel structure, consistent punctuation, and logical order.
  - Precision and concision: Technical writing should be precise and concise, meaning that it should use the exact and appropriate words to convey the intended meaning, and avoid unnecessary or redundant words that can obscure or dilute the message. Precision and concision should be evident at all levels, from the overall document, to paragraphing, to sentence structure, to word choice, and even to punctuation.
