### Argumentation skills for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills

- Argumentation skills are the abilities to develop and present logical, coherent and persuasive arguments on a given topic or issue.
- Argumentation skills are important for technical communication because they help to:
  - Demonstrate your knowledge and understanding of the subject matter.
  - Support your claims and recommendations with relevant evidence and reasoning.
  - Address the needs and expectations of your audience and purpose.
  - Anticipate and respond to possible objections and counterarguments.
  - Establish your credibility and authority as a technical communicator.
- Some of the key elements of argumentation skills are:
  - Style: The way you express your arguments using language, tone, structure and format. Your style should be clear, concise, consistent and appropriate for your audience and purpose.
  - Devices: The techniques you use to make your arguments more effective and persuasive, such as definitions, examples, analogies, statistics, quotations, etc. You should use devices that are relevant, accurate and reliable.
  - Analysis: The process of breaking down your topic or issue into smaller parts and examining them critically. You should identify the main problem, question or claim, the supporting and opposing arguments, the assumptions and implications, and the strengths and weaknesses of each argument.
  - Cohesion and Emphasis: The ways you connect and organize your arguments to form a coherent and logical whole. You should use transitions, signposts, headings, summaries, etc. to guide your reader through your argument. You should also highlight the most important points and evidence to support your claim.
  - Critical Thinking: The ability to think rationally, objectively and independently about your topic or issue. You should evaluate the validity and soundness of your own and others' arguments, avoid logical fallacies and cognitive biases, and acknowledge the limitations and uncertainties of your argument.
  - Nuances: The subtle differences and variations in meaning, perspective and interpretation that affect your argument. You should consider the context, background, values and interests of your audience and stakeholders, and address the possible ambiguities, contradictions and exceptions in your argument.
  - Exposition, Narration and Description: The modes of writing that you use to present your argument. Exposition is the mode of explaining and informing your reader about your topic or issue. Narration is the mode of telling a story or an anecdote to illustrate your point. Description is the mode of creating a vivid picture or impression of your topic or issue. You should use the appropriate mode or a combination of modes to suit your audience and purpose.
