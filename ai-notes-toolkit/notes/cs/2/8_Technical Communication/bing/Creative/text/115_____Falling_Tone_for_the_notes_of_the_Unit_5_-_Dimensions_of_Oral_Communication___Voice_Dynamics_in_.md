### Falling Tone

- A falling tone is a type of intonation pattern in which the pitch of the voice falls from a high to a low level at the end of a sentence or a phrase.
- A falling tone is usually used to indicate the following functions or meanings in oral communication:
  - To show finality, completeness, or closure of a statement or a question.
  - To express certainty, confidence, or assertiveness of a speaker.
  - To convey information, facts, or opinions that are not open to discussion or debate.
  - To signal the end of a topic or a turn in a conversation.
- A falling tone can be marked by a downward arrow (↘) before the last stressed syllable of a sentence or a phrase.
- Examples of sentences or phrases that use a falling tone are:
  - "I'm leaving tomorrow." ↘
  - "What time is it?" ↘
  - "That's a fact." ↘
  - "Thank you for your attention." ↘
