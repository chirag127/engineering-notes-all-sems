### C.V./Resume writing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- A C.V. (curriculum vitae) or a resume is a document that summarizes one's education, skills, work experience, and other relevant information for a job application.
- A C.V. or a resume is usually the first impression that a potential employer gets of a job seeker, so it is important to make it clear, concise, and professional.
- A C.V. or a resume should be tailored to the specific job or industry that one is applying for, highlighting the most relevant qualifications and achievements.
- A C.V. or a resume should follow a consistent format and style, using appropriate headings, fonts, margins, and spacing.
- A C.V. or a resume should be proofread and edited for accuracy, grammar, spelling, and punctuation.
- A C.V. or a resume should be updated regularly to reflect any changes in one's education, skills, work experience, or achievements.

Some tips for writing a C.V. or a resume are:

- Use action verbs and keywords that match the job description and the employer's expectations.
- Quantify and qualify your accomplishments and skills with numbers, percentages, awards, or testimonials.
- Avoid using personal pronouns, such as I, me, or my, and use a third-person perspective instead.
- Avoid using vague or generic statements, such as "responsible for" or "worked on", and use specific and concrete examples instead.
- Avoid using negative or irrelevant information, such as reasons for leaving a previous job, salary expectations, or personal hobbies, unless they are directly related to the job or industry.
- Avoid using too much or too little information, and aim for a balance between brevity and completeness. A C.V. or a resume should typically be one or two pages long, depending on the level of experience and the type of job.
- Use bullet points, lists, and subheadings to organize and highlight your information.
- Use a reverse chronological order to present your education and work experience, starting with the most recent and working backwards.
- Use a functional or a combination format to emphasize your skills and achievements, rather than your work history, if you have gaps in your employment, are changing careers, or are a recent graduate.
- Use a professional and appropriate email address, phone number, and social media profiles, and make sure they are consistent across your C.V. or a resume and your online presence.
- Use a clear and simple file name, such as "FirstName_LastName_CV" or "FirstName_LastName_Resume", and save your document as a PDF or a Word file, depending on the employer's preference.
