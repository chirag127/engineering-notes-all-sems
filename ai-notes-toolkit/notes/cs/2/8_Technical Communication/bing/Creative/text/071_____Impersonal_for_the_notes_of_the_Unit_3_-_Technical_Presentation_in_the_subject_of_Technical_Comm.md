### Impersonal

- Impersonal communication is a style of communication that avoids using personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, we, etc.) and focuses on conveying data and important information.
- Impersonal communication is common in technical communication, where the purpose is to inform, instruct, or persuade the audience without expressing personal opinions or emotions.
- Impersonal communication can be achieved by using the passive voice, the third person point of view, objective language, and factual evidence.
- Impersonal communication can have some advantages, such as:
  - It can create a sense of objectivity and credibility, especially when dealing with scientific or technical topics.
  - It can avoid bias or prejudice, especially when addressing a diverse or sensitive audience.
  - It can maintain a professional and formal tone, especially when writing for academic or business purposes.
- Impersonal communication can also have some disadvantages, such as:
  - It can be dull or boring, especially when the topic is complex or abstract.
  - It can be vague or ambiguous, especially when the agent or the action is unclear or omitted.
  - It can be impersonal or distant, especially when the audience needs to be engaged or motivated.
