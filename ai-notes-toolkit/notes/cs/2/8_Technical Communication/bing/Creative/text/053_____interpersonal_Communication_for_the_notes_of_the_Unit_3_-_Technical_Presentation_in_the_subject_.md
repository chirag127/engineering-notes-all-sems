### Interpersonal Communication

- Interpersonal communication is the process by which people exchange information, feelings, and meaning through verbal and non-verbal messages .
- Interpersonal communication involves a conversation between at least two people who are interdependent and have some knowledge of each other .
- Interpersonal communication can be classified into different types, such as:
  - Dyadic communication: communication between two people, such as a dialogue, an interview, or a counseling session.
  - Group communication: communication among three or more people, such as a meeting, a discussion, or a presentation.
  - Public communication: communication from one person to a large audience, such as a speech, a lecture, or a performance.
- Interpersonal communication can also be categorized based on the level of intimacy, formality, and purpose, such as:
  - Casual communication: communication that is informal, spontaneous, and often based on common interests, such as chatting with friends, family, or co-workers.
  - Professional communication: communication that is formal, structured, and often based on specific goals, such as communicating with clients, customers, or superiors.
  - Personal communication: communication that is intimate, emotional, and often based on trust, such as communicating with a partner, a close friend, or a therapist.
- Interpersonal communication relies on both verbal and non-verbal cues, such as:
  - Verbal cues: the words, phrases, and sentences that are spoken or written, such as the content, tone, and style of the message.
  - Non-verbal cues: the gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, and other signals that are not spoken or written, such as the body language, facial expressions, and voice quality of the sender and receiver.
- Interpersonal communication can have various effects and outcomes, such as:
  - Informing: communication that provides or seeks information, such as asking or answering questions, giving or receiving instructions, or sharing or learning facts.
  - Persuading: communication that influences or changes the attitudes, beliefs, or behaviors of others, such as convincing, arguing, or negotiating.
  - Relating: communication that establishes or maintains relationships, such as expressing or receiving emotions, showing or receiving support, or building or sustaining trust.
  - Entertaining: communication that amuses or delights others, such as joking, storytelling, or performing.
