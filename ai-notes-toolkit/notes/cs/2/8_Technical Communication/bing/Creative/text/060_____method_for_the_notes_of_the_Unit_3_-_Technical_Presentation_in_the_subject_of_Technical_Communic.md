### Method for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience, such as experts, managers, customers, or the general public.
- The purpose of a technical presentation is to inform, persuade, or instruct the audience about a technical topic, such as a product, a process, a research finding, or a recommendation.
- The method for the notes of a technical presentation consists of the following steps:

  1. Analyze the audience and the context of the presentation. Consider the audience's background, needs, expectations, and preferences. Also, consider the time, place, and mode of the presentation, such as live, online, or recorded.
  2. Define the main goal and the specific objectives of the presentation. The main goal is the overall purpose of the presentation, such as to inform, persuade, or instruct. The specific objectives are the measurable outcomes that the presentation aims to achieve, such as to explain, demonstrate, compare, or recommend.
  3. Develop the main message and the key points of the presentation. The main message is the central idea that summarizes the presentation and conveys the main goal. The key points are the supporting arguments or evidence that back up the main message and address the specific objectives.
  4. Organize the content of the presentation into a logical structure. The structure consists of three main parts: introduction, body, and conclusion. The introduction should capture the audience's attention, establish the main message, and preview the key points. The body should develop the key points in a clear and coherent manner, using appropriate transitions, examples, and visual aids. The conclusion should summarize the main message and the key points, and provide a call to action or a recommendation.
  5. Prepare the notes for the presentation using an appropriate format and style. The notes can be either full sentences or bullet points, depending on the preference and comfort level of the presenter. The notes should be concise, accurate, and consistent, and should use simple and direct language. The notes should also include cues for delivery, such as pauses, emphasis, gestures, and eye contact.
  6. Practice and revise the presentation based on feedback and self-evaluation. The presenter should rehearse the presentation several times, preferably in front of a mirror, a recorder, or a mock audience. The presenter should also seek feedback from peers, instructors, or experts, and make necessary revisions to the content, structure, and delivery of the presentation. The presenter should also evaluate their own performance and identify their strengths and areas for improvement.
