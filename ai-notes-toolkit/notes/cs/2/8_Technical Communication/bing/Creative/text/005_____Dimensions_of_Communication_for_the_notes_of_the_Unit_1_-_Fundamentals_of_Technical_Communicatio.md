### Dimensions of Communication

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, feelings, emotions, and thoughts between two or more people. Communication can be verbal or nonverbal, oral or written, formal or informal, and intentional or unintentional. Communication can also be classified into different dimensions based on various criteria, such as the direction, the level, the context, and the effect of the communication. Some of the common dimensions of communication are:

- **Downward communication**: This is the communication that flows from a higher level to a lower level in an organization or a group. For example, a manager communicating with an employee, a teacher communicating with a student, or a parent communicating with a child. The purpose of downward communication is to provide instructions, feedback, information, guidance, or motivation to the subordinates or the receivers.
- **Upward communication**: This is the communication that flows from a lower level to a higher level in an organization or a group. For example, an employee communicating with a manager, a student communicating with a teacher, or a child communicating with a parent. The purpose of upward communication is to provide feedback, information, suggestions, complaints, or requests to the superiors or the senders.
- **Horizontal or lateral communication**: This is the communication that flows among people at the same level in an organization or a group. For example, a colleague communicating with another colleague, a friend communicating with another friend, or a sibling communicating with another sibling. The purpose of horizontal communication is to coordinate, cooperate, share, inform, or socialize with the peers or the partners.
- **Diagonal or cross-wise communication**: This is the communication that flows across different levels and functions in an organization or a group. For example, a salesperson communicating with an accountant, a student communicating with a librarian, or a neighbor communicating with a police officer. The purpose of diagonal communication is to facilitate, solve, clarify, or expedite the communication process among different parties or roles.
- **Verbal or nonverbal communication**: This is the communication that involves the use of words or symbols (verbal) or the use of gestures, expressions, or body language (nonverbal) to convey meaning. For example, a person communicating with words, signs, or writing (verbal) or a person communicating with eye contact, facial expressions, or posture (nonverbal). The purpose of verbal communication is to express, inform, persuade, or instruct, while the purpose of nonverbal communication is to complement, reinforce, regulate, or contradict the verbal communication.
- **Oral or written communication**: This is the communication that involves the use of speech or sound (oral) or the use of text or graphics (written) to convey meaning. For example, a person communicating with voice, phone, or video (oral) or a person communicating with email, letter, or report (written). The purpose of oral communication is to interact, communicate, or present, while the purpose of written communication is to document, record, or transmit.
- **Formal or informal communication**: This is the communication that follows or does not follow the established rules, norms, or protocols of an organization or a group (formal) or the communication that is spontaneous, casual, or personal (informal). For example, a person communicating with a boss, a client, or a stranger (formal) or a person communicating with a friend, a family member, or a colleague (informal). The purpose of formal communication is to maintain, respect, or comply with the authority, hierarchy, or professionalism, while the purpose of informal communication is to build, maintain, or enhance the relationship, trust, or rapport.
- **Intentional or unintentional communication**: This is the communication that is deliberate, planned, or conscious (intentional) or the communication that is accidental, unplanned, or subconscious (unintentional). For example, a person communicating with a purpose, a goal, or an agenda (intentional) or a person communicating with a slip, a mistake, or a habit (unintentional). The purpose of intentional communication is to achieve, influence, or persuade, while the purpose of unintentional communication is to reveal, expose, or disclose.
- **Content or relational communication**: This is the communication that focuses on the information, facts, or data (content) or the communication that focuses on the feelings, emotions, or attitudes (relational) of the communication.
