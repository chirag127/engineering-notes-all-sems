### Audience Analysis

- Audience analysis is the process of identifying and understanding the characteristics, needs, expectations, and preferences of the people who will receive your message.
- Audience analysis helps you to tailor your message to suit your audience's interests, level of knowledge, attitude, and context.
- Audience analysis involves three steps: identifying your audience, assessing your audience, and adapting your message.

#### Identifying your audience

- To identify your audience, you need to answer the following questions:
  - Who are they? Consider their demographics, such as age, gender, education, occupation, culture, etc.
  - What are their roles and responsibilities? Consider their positions, functions, authority, and influence in the organization or situation.
  - Why are they listening to you? Consider their goals, motivations, expectations, and needs from your message.
  - How will they receive your message? Consider the medium, format, time, and place of your communication.

#### Assessing your audience

- To assess your audience, you need to answer the following questions:
  - What do they know? Consider their prior knowledge, experience, and expertise on the topic of your message.
  - What do they need to know? Consider the essential information, facts, concepts, and skills that they need to understand and act on your message.
  - What do they want to know? Consider the additional information, details, examples, and evidence that they may be interested in or curious about.
  - What do they feel? Consider their attitude, opinion, and emotion toward the topic, the speaker, and the situation.

#### Adapting your message

- To adapt your message, you need to answer the following questions:
  - What is your purpose? Consider the main goal, objective, or outcome that you want to achieve with your message.
  - What is your key message? Consider the main idea, point, or argument that you want to convey to your audience.
  - How will you organize your message? Consider the structure, sequence, and logic of your message, such as introduction, body, and conclusion.
  - How will you support your message? Consider the types, sources, and quality of your evidence, such as facts, statistics, examples, testimonials, etc.
