### Encoding process for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Encoding is the process of turning thoughts into communication.
- The encoder is the person who creates the message, adapts it to the receiver, and transmits it across a channel.
- The encoding process is based on the purpose of communication and the relation between the sender and the receiver.
- The encoder uses symbols to communicate their message, such as words, gestures, images, sounds, etc.
- The encoder has to consider the following factors while encoding a message:
  - The content of the message: what is the main idea or information to be conveyed?
  - The context of the message: what is the situation or setting of the communication?
  - The code of the message: what is the system of symbols or signs to be used?
  - The channel of the message: what is the medium or tool to be used for sending the message?
  - The receiver of the message: who is the intended audience or target of the communication?
- The encoder has to choose the appropriate code and channel for the message, depending on the nature and needs of the receiver, the availability and accessibility of the medium, and the effectiveness and efficiency of the communication.
- The encoder has to encode the message in a clear, concise, coherent, and consistent manner, avoiding ambiguity, distortion, noise, or interference.
- The encoder has to encode the message in a way that appeals to the receiver's interest, attention, motivation, and emotions, using techniques such as repetition, emphasis, contrast, examples, etc.
- The encoder has to encode the message in a way that respects the receiver's background, culture, values, beliefs, and expectations, using appropriate language, tone, style, and etiquette.
- The encoder has to encode the message in a way that establishes rapport, trust, and credibility with the receiver, using feedback, empathy, and ethical principles.
- The encoding process is influenced by the encoder's skills, knowledge, attitude, personality, and perception.
- The encoding process is also influenced by the dimensions of oral communication and voice dynamics, such as pitch, volume, rate, pause, stress, intonation, articulation, pronunciation, etc.
- The encoding process is not a one-way or linear process, but a dynamic and interactive process that involves constant adaptation and adjustment to the changing communication situation.
