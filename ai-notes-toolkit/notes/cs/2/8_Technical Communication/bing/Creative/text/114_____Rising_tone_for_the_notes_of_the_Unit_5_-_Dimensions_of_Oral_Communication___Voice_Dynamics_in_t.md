### Rising tone

- A rising tone is an intonation pattern in which the pitch of the voice rises over time.
- A rising tone is mainly used for three purposes:
  - To ask yes/no questions, such as "Are you thirsty?"
  - To request clarification or indicate uncertainty, such as "So, is that the new doctor?"
  - To express high energy emotions, such as happiness, anger, or shock, such as "Wow!"
- A rising tone can also imply that the speaker is challenging, impatient, suspicious, or annoyed, depending on the context and tone of voice.
- A rising tone can be marked with a diagonal line (/) above the stressed syllable, such as "Are you /thirsty?"
- A rising tone can be contrasted with a falling tone, which is an intonation pattern in which the pitch of the voice falls over time. A falling tone is used for statements, commands, wh-questions, and finality.
