### Completeness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective technical communication, along with clarity, conciseness, correctness, coherence, courtesy, and credibility.
- Completeness means that the communication provides all the information that the audience needs to understand the message and take appropriate action.
- Completeness also means that the communication does not omit any important or relevant details, facts, or evidence that could affect the audience's decision or judgment.
- Completeness can be achieved by following these guidelines:
  - Identify the purpose and scope of the communication and the needs and expectations of the audience.
  - Provide all the essential information that answers the questions of who, what, when, where, why, and how.
  - Include any additional information that supports the main message, such as examples, illustrations, statistics, testimonials, references, etc.
  - Anticipate and address any potential questions, objections, or concerns that the audience might have.
  - Use headings, subheadings, lists, tables, graphs, etc. to organize and highlight the information.
  - Check for accuracy, completeness, and consistency of the information and avoid any gaps, contradictions, or ambiguities.
  - Provide clear and specific instructions, recommendations, or calls to action, if applicable.
  - End with a summary, conclusion, or feedback request, if appropriate.
