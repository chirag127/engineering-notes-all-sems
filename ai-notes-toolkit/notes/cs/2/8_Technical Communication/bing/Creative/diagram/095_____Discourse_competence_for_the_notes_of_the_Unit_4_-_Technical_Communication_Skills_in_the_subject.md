### Discourse competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Discourse competence is the ability to understand and express oneself in a given language, combining grammatical forms and meaning to achieve a linked text (oral or written), in different communication situations .
- Discourse competence is one of the components of communicative competence, which also includes linguistic, sociolinguistic, strategic, and intercultural competences .
- Discourse competence involves the following skills and knowledge:
  - Textual competence: the ability to read and comprehend different types of texts, such as narratives, descriptions, arguments, explanations, etc., and to produce coherent and cohesive texts of one's own.
  - Rhetorical competence: the ability to use appropriate linguistic devices and strategies to achieve one's communicative goals, such as persuading, informing, entertaining, etc., and to adapt one's style and tone to the audience, purpose, and context.
  - Genre competence: the ability to recognize and use different genres of communication, such as reports, proposals, emails, memos, etc., and to follow their conventions and expectations.
  - Lexical competence: the ability to use a specific vocabulary and terminology related to a particular field or domain, such as technical, academic, professional, etc., and to avoid ambiguity and confusion.
  - Pragmatic competence: the ability to use language appropriately and effectively in various social and cultural situations, such as making requests, giving feedback, expressing opinions, etc., and to understand the implicit meanings and intentions of others.
- Discourse competence is relevant for technical communication because it enables the communicator to:
  - Produce clear, concise, accurate, and coherent texts that convey the intended message and information to the target audience.
  - Use appropriate formats, structures, and styles for different types of technical documents, such as manuals, instructions, specifications, etc., and to follow the standards and guidelines of the field or organization.
  - Use suitable linguistic devices and strategies to achieve the communicative goals of technical communication, such as explaining, instructing, persuading, etc., and to address the needs and expectations of the audience.
  - Use a specific and precise vocabulary and terminology that reflects the technical knowledge and expertise of the field or domain, and to avoid jargon, slang, or vague expressions.
  - Use language respectfully and politely in various technical communication situations, such as collaborating, negotiating, presenting, etc., and to understand the cultural and social norms and values of the audience.
