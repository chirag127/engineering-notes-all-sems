### Falling Tone

- Falling tone is a type of intonation that involves a downward pitch movement at the end of a sentence or a phrase.
- Falling tone is used to signal the completion of a thought, a statement, or a question that requests information.
- Falling tone can also convey certainty, confidence, finality, or authority in oral communication.
- Some examples of falling tone are:

  - We're planning to go on a hike this weekend. (statement) 
  - How long have you been working here? (information question) 
  - Sorry, I'm not quite sure what you mean by 'valued possessions'. (statement) 
  - Please turn off your cell phones. (command) 

- Falling tone can be contrasted with rising tone, which involves an upward pitch movement at the end of a sentence or a phrase.
- Rising tone is used to signal uncertainty, doubt, surprise, interest, or a request for confirmation or feedback in oral communication.
- Some examples of rising tone are:

  - You're from Australia? (surprise) 
  - It's a nice day, isn't it? (tag question) 
  - Can you repeat that? (request) 
  - So you're saying that you don't like chocolate? (confirmation) 

- To produce a falling tone, the speaker should start with a high pitch and gradually lower it until the end of the sentence or the phrase.
- To practice falling tone, the speaker can use a hand gesture to trace the downward pitch movement, or use a musical instrument or an app to match the pitch level.
- To improve falling tone, the speaker should listen to native speakers of the target language and imitate their intonation patterns, or record their own speech and compare it with a model.
- To use falling tone effectively, the speaker should be aware of the context, the purpose, and the audience of their oral communication, and adjust their intonation accordingly.
