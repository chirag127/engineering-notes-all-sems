### Correctness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

Correctness is one of the key components of effective communication, especially in technical communication. Correctness means that the details of a message are accurate, the grammar and punctuation are correct, and the appearance and format are appropriate for the purpose and audience of the document.

Some of the aspects of correctness in technical communication are:

- **Passive voice**: The passive voice is often used in technical writing to emphasize the action or result rather than the agent or doer. However, the passive voice can also make the sentences longer, more complex, and less direct. Therefore, it is important to use the passive voice judiciously and avoid overusing it. A good rule of thumb is to use the passive voice when the agent is unknown, irrelevant, or obvious, and use the active voice when the agent is important, specific, or new.

- **Commands and directions**: Technical writing often contains a lot of commands and directions, such as instructions, procedures, or recommendations. These commands and directions should be clear, concise, and consistent. They should use the imperative mood, which is the verb form that expresses a command or request. For example, "Press the power button" or "Do not touch the wires". They should also use parallel structure, which means that the items in a list or series have the same grammatical form. For example, "To make a cake, you need to: measure the ingredients, mix them together, pour the batter into a pan, and bake it in the oven".

- **Bullets and lists**: Technical writing often relies heavily on bulleted or numbered lists, which help to organize and highlight the main points, steps, or features of a document. Bullets and lists should be used when the information is too long or complex to be presented in a paragraph, or when the information needs to be scanned or referenced quickly. Bullets and lists should follow some basic guidelines, such as: using a consistent style and punctuation for each item, using a lead-in sentence to introduce the list, using parallel structure for each item, and using numbers for sequential or ordered items and bullets for non-sequential or unordered items.

- **Precision and concision**: Technical writing should be precise and concise, which means that it should convey the exact and relevant information in the most efficient and clear way. Precision and concision can be achieved at all levels of the document, from the overall structure, to the paragraphing, to the sentence structure, to the word choice, and even to the punctuation. Some of the strategies for achieving precision and concision are: using headings and subheadings to organize the document, using topic sentences and transitions to guide the reader, using simple and direct sentences and avoiding unnecessary words or modifiers, using specific and concrete words and avoiding vague or abstract words, and using punctuation marks to clarify the meaning and tone of the sentences.

Correctness is essential for technical communication, as it helps to establish the credibility and professionalism of the writer, as well as to ensure the clarity and accuracy of the message. By following the guidelines and principles of correctness, technical writers can produce high-quality documents that meet the needs and expectations of their readers.
