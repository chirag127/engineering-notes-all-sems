### Emphasis

- Emphasis is the technique of highlighting the important or main points in a message, document, or presentation.
- Emphasis can be achieved by using various methods, such as:

  - Position: placing the key information at the beginning or end of a sentence, paragraph, or section, where it is more likely to catch the reader's or listener's attention.
  - Repetition: repeating the key information or idea several times throughout the message, document, or presentation, to reinforce its significance and memorability.
  - Contrast: using opposites, comparisons, or contrasts to show the difference between the key information and other information, or to create a sense of urgency or importance.
  - Typography: using different fonts, sizes, colors, styles, or formats to make the key information stand out from the rest of the text, such as bold, italic, underline, highlight, bullet points, headings, etc.
  - Visuals: using images, graphs, charts, diagrams, tables, or other visual aids to illustrate, support, or complement the key information, or to make it more appealing and engaging.

- Emphasis should be used strategically and sparingly, to avoid confusing or overwhelming the reader or listener, or diluting the impact of the message, document, or presentation.
- Emphasis should be consistent and coherent, to avoid creating contradictions or inconsistencies in the message, document, or presentation.
- Emphasis should be appropriate and ethical, to avoid misleading, exaggerating, or manipulating the reader or listener, or violating the norms and standards of the field, discipline, or profession.
