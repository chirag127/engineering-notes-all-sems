### Argumentation skills

- Argumentation skills are the abilities to develop and present arguments in a clear, logical, and persuasive way.
- Arguments are claims supported by reasons and evidence that aim to convince others of a certain point of view or course of action.
- Argumentation skills are important for technical communication because they help you to:
  - Analyze and evaluate complex issues and problems
  - Communicate your ideas and solutions effectively and convincingly
  - Support your claims with relevant and credible sources
  - Anticipate and address counterarguments and objections
  - Resolve conflicts and reach consensus with others

- Some of the key aspects of argumentation skills are:

  - Style: The way you express your arguments in terms of tone, language, and structure. You should use a style that is appropriate for your audience, purpose, and context. For example, you should use a formal and objective style for academic and professional writing, and a more informal and personal style for personal and creative writing.
  - Devices: The techniques you use to make your arguments more effective and appealing. Some of the common devices are:
    - Analysis: Breaking down a complex issue or problem into smaller and simpler parts to understand it better and identify the main points and subpoints.
    - Cohesion: Connecting your arguments and paragraphs with transitions, topic sentences, and signposts to create a clear and coherent flow of ideas.
    - Emphasis: Highlighting the most important or relevant points or evidence in your arguments with techniques such as repetition, contrast, comparison, examples, and illustrations.
    - Critical thinking: Applying logic, reasoning, and evidence to your arguments to ensure that they are valid, sound, and reliable. You should avoid logical fallacies, such as hasty generalizations, false dilemmas, ad hominem attacks, and circular reasoning, that can weaken your arguments and undermine your credibility.
    - Nuances: Acknowledging the complexity and diversity of perspectives on an issue or problem and avoiding oversimplification, bias, and stereotypes. You should consider the strengths and weaknesses of different arguments and evidence and show respect and empathy for others' views.
  - Exposition, narration, and description: The modes of writing that you use to present your arguments and evidence. Exposition is the mode of writing that explains, informs, or defines something. Narration is the mode of writing that tells a story or recounts an event. Description is the mode of writing that creates a vivid picture of something using sensory details. You can use different modes of writing to suit your purpose and audience and to make your arguments more engaging and convincing. For example, you can use exposition to provide background information, narration to provide an example or anecdote, and description to provide a visual image or metaphor.
