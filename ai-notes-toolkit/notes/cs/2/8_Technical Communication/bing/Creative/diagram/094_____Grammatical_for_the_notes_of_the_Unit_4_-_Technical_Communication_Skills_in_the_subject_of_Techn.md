### Grammatical Errors

Grammatical errors are instances of faulty, unconventional, or controversial usage of language that may hinder effective communication or cause confusion for the readers. Grammatical errors are different from factual errors, which occur when you use incorrect information in your writing, and typographical errors, which occur when you press the wrong key on your keyboard.

Some of the common grammatical errors in technical writing are:

- **Messy structure**: Many technical documents confuse readers and fail to achieve their aims because they were not planned properly to begin with. A clear and logical structure is essential for conveying complex information in a user-friendly way.
- **Too much jargon**: Jargon is the specialized vocabulary of a particular field or profession. While it may be useful for communicating with experts, it can also alienate or confuse non-experts or novices. Technical writers should avoid using too much jargon or explain it clearly when necessary.
- **Poor punctuation**: Punctuation marks are the symbols that help organize and clarify the meaning of sentences. They can also indicate the tone, mood, or emphasis of the writer. Poor punctuation can lead to ambiguity, misunderstanding, or loss of credibility. Technical writers should follow the rules and conventions of punctuation or use a digital tool to help check for errors  .
- **Inconsistency**: Inconsistency refers to the lack of uniformity or harmony in the use of language, such as spelling, capitalization, abbreviations, numbers, tense, voice, or format. Inconsistency can make the writing look unprofessional, sloppy, or confusing. Technical writers should maintain consistency throughout the document or follow a specific style guide .
- **Too much abstraction**: Abstraction is the process of generalizing or simplifying complex or concrete concepts or phenomena. While it may be useful for summarizing or explaining some ideas, it can also make the writing vague, unclear, or irrelevant. Technical writers should balance abstraction with concrete examples, details, or evidence to support their claims or arguments.
- **Unclear antecedents**: An antecedent is the word or phrase that a pronoun (such as he, she, it, they, etc.) refers to or replaces. An unclear antecedent is one that is ambiguous, missing, or too far from the pronoun. Unclear antecedents can cause confusion, misunderstanding, or grammatical errors. Technical writers should make sure that their pronouns have clear and consistent antecedents or avoid using pronouns when possible .
- **Dense presentation**: Dense presentation refers to the use of long, complex, or convoluted sentences or paragraphs that make the writing hard to read or understand. Dense presentation can also result from the lack of visual aids, such as headings, lists, tables, graphs, or diagrams, that can help organize and illustrate the information. Technical writers should use simple, clear, and concise language and employ visual aids to enhance the readability and usability of their documents .
