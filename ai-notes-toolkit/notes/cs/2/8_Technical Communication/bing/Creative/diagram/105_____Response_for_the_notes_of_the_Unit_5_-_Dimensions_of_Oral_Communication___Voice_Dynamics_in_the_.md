# Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

## Dimensions of Oral Communication

Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth. Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose of the message. Oral communication has several dimensions, such as:

- **Purpose**: The goal or objective of the oral communication, such as to inform, persuade, entertain, or instruct.
- **Audience**: The intended recipients of the oral communication, such as individuals, groups, or organizations. The audience can be homogeneous or heterogeneous, depending on their characteristics and needs.
- **Channel**: The medium or mode of delivery of the oral communication, such as face-to-face, telephone, video conference, podcast, or public speech. The channel can affect the quality and effectiveness of the oral communication.
- **Context**: The situation or environment in which the oral communication takes place, such as formal or informal, academic or professional, cultural or social. The context can influence the tone, style, and content of the oral communication.
- **Feedback**: The response or reaction of the audience to the oral communication, such as verbal or non-verbal, positive or negative, immediate or delayed. Feedback can help the speaker to evaluate and improve the oral communication.

## Voice Dynamics

Voice dynamics are the variations in the pitch, volume, rate, and quality of the voice that can enhance or detract from the oral communication. Voice dynamics can convey emotions, attitudes, and meanings that are not expressed by words alone. Voice dynamics can be controlled and modified by the speaker to suit the purpose, audience, channel, and context of the oral communication. Some examples of voice dynamics are:

- **Pitch**: The highness or lowness of the voice, measured in hertz (Hz). Pitch can indicate the level of interest, excitement, or confidence of the speaker. A high pitch can suggest enthusiasm, curiosity, or nervousness, while a low pitch can suggest authority, calmness, or boredom.
- **Volume**: The loudness or softness of the voice, measured in decibels (dB). Volume can indicate the level of energy, emotion, or emphasis of the speaker. A loud volume can suggest passion, anger, or urgency, while a soft volume can suggest intimacy, politeness, or hesitation.
- **Rate**: The speed or pace of the voice, measured in words per minute (wpm). Rate can indicate the level of clarity, fluency, or urgency of the speaker. A fast rate can suggest confidence, eagerness, or impatience, while a slow rate can suggest carefulness, thoughtfulness, or uncertainty.
- **Quality**: The tone or timbre of the voice, which depends on the shape and size of the vocal cords, the resonance of the vocal tract, and the articulation of the speech organs. Quality can indicate the level of personality, mood, or health of the speaker. A clear quality can suggest professionalism, sincerity, or wellness, while a hoarse quality can suggest casualness, sarcasm, or illness.
