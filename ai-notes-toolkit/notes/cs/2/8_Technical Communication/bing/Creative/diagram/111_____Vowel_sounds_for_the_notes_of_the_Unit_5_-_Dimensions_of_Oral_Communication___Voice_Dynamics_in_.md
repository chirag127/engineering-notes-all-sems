### Vowel sounds

- Vowel sounds are created when air flows smoothly, without interruption, through the throat and mouth.
- Vowels are open sounds because they involve no obstruction to the flow of air from the lungs as it passes up through the windpipe (trachea), through the voice box (larynx) and out of the mouth.
- Vowels are letters that represent speech sounds where air leaves the mouth without any blockage by the tongue, lips, or throat.
- The vowels in the English alphabet are a, e, i, o, u, and sometimes y.
- Vowels are contrasted with consonants, which represent sounds where air is obstructed or interrupted, such as the closing of the lips for b, m, and p .
- Vowels are further divided into two categories that are monophthongs (pure vowels) and diphthongs (vowel glides).
- A vowel is described taking into account the following criteria: (a) the height of the tongue, (b) the part of the tongue that is raised, (c) the shape of the lips, and (d) the length of the vowel.
- Different vowel sounds are produced as a speaker changes the shape and placement of articulators (parts of the throat and mouth).
- Vowel sounds are important for voice dynamics, as they affect the pitch, loudness, and quality of the voice.
- Vowel sounds also influence the meaning, clarity, and tone of the message, as they can create different effects such as emphasis, contrast, and emotion.
