### Interpersonal Skills

- Interpersonal skills are soft skills, which means they are skills that are often considered innate personality traits or habits  .
- Interpersonal skills are skills that you rely on when you interact and communicate with others. They cover a variety of scenarios where communication and cooperation are essential.
- Interpersonal skills are important for technical communication because they help you to build rapport, trust, and understanding with your audience, colleagues, and clients.
- Some examples of interpersonal skills are  :
  - Active listening: listening to others with the purpose of gathering information and engaging with the speaker.
  - Dependability: being reliable, responsible, and accountable for your actions and commitments.
  - Empathy: being able to understand and share the feelings and perspectives of others.
  - Emotional intelligence: being able to recognize and manage your own and others' emotions.
  - Conflict resolution: being able to handle and resolve disagreements and disputes in a constructive and respectful way.
  - Negotiation: being able to reach a mutually beneficial agreement or compromise with others.
  - Listening: being able to pay attention, comprehend, and respond appropriately to what others are saying.
  - Positive attitude: being optimistic, enthusiastic, and supportive of others.
  - Teamwork: being able to work effectively and cooperatively with others towards a common goal.
  - Collaboration: being able to share ideas, resources, and feedback with others.
  - Leadership: being able to inspire, motivate, and guide others.

- To improve your interpersonal skills, you can :
  - Practice self-awareness: reflect on your own strengths and weaknesses, and seek feedback from others.
  - Practice active listening: show interest, ask questions, and paraphrase what others are saying.
  - Practice empathy: try to put yourself in others' shoes and acknowledge their feelings and viewpoints.
  - Practice emotional intelligence: monitor and regulate your own and others' emotions, and avoid reacting impulsively or defensively.
  - Practice conflict resolution: focus on the problem, not the person, and use assertive and respectful communication.
  - Practice negotiation: prepare well, identify your interests and alternatives, and seek a win-win outcome.
  - Practice listening: pay attention, avoid distractions, and give feedback.
  - Practice positive attitude: express gratitude, appreciation, and encouragement to others.
  - Practice teamwork: communicate clearly, respect diversity, and support others.
  - Practice collaboration: share information, ideas, and resources, and solicit and provide feedback.
  - Practice leadership: set clear goals and expectations, delegate tasks, and recognize and reward others.
