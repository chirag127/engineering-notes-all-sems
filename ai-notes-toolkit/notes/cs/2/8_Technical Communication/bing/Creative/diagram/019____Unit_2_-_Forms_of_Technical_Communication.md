## Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

Technical communication is the process of conveying information to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Technical communication can take various forms, such as:

- **Written communication**: This includes documents such as reports, manuals, proposals, memos, emails, letters, etc. Written communication is used to provide detailed, precise, and permanent information to the audience. Written communication requires careful planning, organization, style, and revision to ensure clarity, accuracy, and completeness.
- **Oral communication**: This includes presentations, speeches, lectures, interviews, meetings, etc. Oral communication is used to deliver information verbally to the audience, often with the support of visual aids such as slides, charts, graphs, etc. Oral communication requires effective delivery, voice modulation, body language, and audience engagement to ensure interest, understanding, and feedback.
- **Visual communication**: This includes images, diagrams, graphs, charts, maps, icons, symbols, etc. Visual communication is used to supplement or replace verbal or written communication, especially when the information is complex, abstract, or quantitative. Visual communication requires appropriate design, layout, color, contrast, and alignment to ensure readability, attractiveness, and effectiveness.
- **Digital communication**: This includes websites, blogs, podcasts, videos, social media, etc. Digital communication is used to disseminate information online to a large and diverse audience, often with the possibility of interaction and feedback. Digital communication requires technical skills, multimedia tools, accessibility, and usability to ensure functionality, quality, and relevance.
