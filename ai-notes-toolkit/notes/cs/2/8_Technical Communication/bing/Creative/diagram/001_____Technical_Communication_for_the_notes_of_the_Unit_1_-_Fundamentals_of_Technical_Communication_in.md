# Technical Communication

## Definition

- Technical communication is used to convey scientific, engineering, or other technical information .
- Technical communication often involves communicating expert, technical, or highly complex information to a non-expert or more general audience.
- Technical communication often works to convince an audience to take some specific action.
- Technical communication might define, instruct, inform, or persuade.

## Characteristics

- Technical communication is a broad field and includes any form of communication that exhibits one or more of the following characteristics:
  - Communicating about technical or specialized topics, such as computer applications, medical procedures, or environmental regulations.
  - Communicating by using technology, such as web pages, help files, or social media sites.
  - Providing instructions about how to do something, regardless of how technical the task is or even if technology is used to create or distribute that communication.
- Technical communication is also characterized by the following features:
  - Audience-centered: Technical communication focuses on the needs, preferences, and goals of the audience, and adapts the content, tone, style, and format accordingly.
  - Purposeful: Technical communication has a clear and specific purpose, such as informing, instructing, persuading, or recommending.
  - Ethical: Technical communication follows the principles of honesty, accuracy, fairness, and respect for the audience, the subject matter, and the context.
  - Professional: Technical communication reflects the standards and expectations of the field, the organization, and the situation, and uses appropriate language, format, and design.
  - Collaborative: Technical communication often involves working with others, such as subject matter experts, peers, supervisors, clients, or users, and requires effective communication skills and teamwork.
  - Research-based: Technical communication relies on evidence, data, and sources to support the claims, arguments, and recommendations, and cites them properly.
  - Visual: Technical communication uses visual elements, such as images, graphs, tables, or diagrams, to enhance the understanding, appeal, and usability of the text.

## Process

- Technical communication follows a process that consists of the following stages:
  - Planning: This stage involves analyzing the audience, the purpose, and the context of the communication, and defining the scope, objectives, and outcomes of the project.
  - Researching: This stage involves gathering, evaluating, and synthesizing information from various sources, such as documents, interviews, observations, or experiments, to support the communication.
  - Drafting: This stage involves creating the initial version of the communication, using the appropriate language, format, and design, and following the conventions and guidelines of the field and the organization.
  - Revising: This stage involves reviewing, editing, and improving the communication, focusing on the content, organization, style, and mechanics, and ensuring the communication meets the audience's needs and expectations.
  - Testing: This stage involves testing the communication for usability, accessibility, and effectiveness, using various methods, such as feedback, reviews, or evaluations, and making the necessary changes based on the results.
  - Delivering: This stage involves distributing the communication to the intended audience, using the appropriate medium, channel, and format, and following the ethical and legal requirements of the situation.

## Types

- Technical communication can be classified into different types based on the purpose, the audience, the format, or the medium of the communication. Some common types of technical communication are:
  - User manuals: These are documents that provide instructions on how to use a product, a service, or a system, such as a software application, a device, or a website.
  - Proposals: These are documents that present a plan, a solution, or a recommendation for a problem, a need, or an opportunity, and persuade the audience to accept, approve, or fund the proposal.
  - Reports: These are documents that provide information, analysis, or evaluation on a topic, an issue, or a situation, and communicate the findings, results, or conclusions to the audience.
  - Memos: These are short documents that communicate information, updates, or requests within an organization, such as a company, a department, or a team.
  - Emails: These are electronic messages that communicate information, updates, or requests to one or more recipients, using an email application or a web-based service.
