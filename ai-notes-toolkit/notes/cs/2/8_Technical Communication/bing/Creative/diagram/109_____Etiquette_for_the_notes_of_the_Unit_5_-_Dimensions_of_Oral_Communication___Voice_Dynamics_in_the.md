### Etiquette for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Etiquette is the set of rules and norms that govern the appropriate and respectful behavior in a given social or professional context.
- Oral communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, opinions, or emotions through spoken words, gestures, or other non-verbal cues.
- Voice dynamics are the variations in pitch, volume, tone, speed, and clarity of one's voice that affect the delivery and impact of oral communication.
- Some of the dimensions of oral communication and voice dynamics are:

  - Purpose: The goal or objective of the communication, such as to inform, persuade, entertain, or instruct.
  - Audience: The intended or actual recipients of the communication, such as individuals, groups, or organizations.
  - Context: The situation or environment in which the communication takes place, such as formal or informal, public or private, face-to-face or mediated.
  - Content: The information, ideas, opinions, or emotions that are conveyed in the communication, such as facts, arguments, stories, or feelings.
  - Structure: The organization and arrangement of the content, such as introduction, body, and conclusion, or chronological, spatial, or topical order.
  - Style: The choice and use of language, such as vocabulary, grammar, syntax, and rhetoric, that reflect the purpose, audience, context, and content of the communication.
  - Delivery: The physical and vocal aspects of the communication, such as posture, gesture, eye contact, facial expression, pitch, volume, tone, speed, and clarity, that affect the impression and interpretation of the communication.

- Some of the etiquette rules and norms for oral communication and voice dynamics are:

  - Be prepared: Know your purpose, audience, context, content, structure, and style before you communicate. Practice and rehearse your communication if possible.
  - Be clear: Use simple, precise, and appropriate language. Avoid jargon, slang, and ambiguity. Pronounce your words correctly and articulate your sounds clearly.
  - Be concise: Use only the necessary and relevant information. Avoid repetition, filler words, and digression. Summarize your main points and conclusions.
  - Be courteous: Respect your audience and their opinions. Use polite and respectful language. Avoid interrupting, contradicting, or criticizing others. Acknowledge and address questions and feedback.
  - Be confident: Use a strong and steady voice. Adjust your pitch, volume, tone, and speed according to your purpose, audience, and context. Use vocal variety and emphasis to highlight your key points and emotions.
  - Be expressive: Use appropriate and natural gestures, eye contact, facial expressions, and body language to support and enhance your communication. Avoid distracting or conflicting non-verbal cues.
