### Seminar/Conferences Presentation Skills

Seminar and conference presentations are an important part of academic and professional communication. They allow you to share your research, ideas, and expertise with a wider audience, as well as to receive feedback and network with other experts in your field. However, presenting at a seminar or conference can also be challenging, especially if you are not confident or experienced in public speaking. Therefore, it is essential to develop and practice your presentation skills to deliver an effective and engaging presentation. Here are some tips and guidelines for seminar and conference presentation skills:

- **Don't touch that slide deck just yet.** Before you start creating your slides, you need to have a clear idea of what you want to say, why you want to say it, and how you want to say it. Think about your main message, your purpose, your audience, and your objectives. Write down your key points and structure your presentation logically and coherently. Use an outline or a storyboard to plan your presentation and avoid unnecessary or irrelevant information.
- **Build your presentation within time constraints.** One of the most common mistakes presenters make is to run out of time or to rush through their slides. To avoid this, you need to know how much time you have for your presentation and how to allocate it wisely. A general rule of thumb is to use one slide per minute, but this may vary depending on the content and the format of your presentation. Practice your presentation several times and time yourself to make sure you can cover all your points without going over or under the limit.
- **Use visuals to illuminate, not obscure.** Slides are a powerful tool to support your presentation, but they can also distract or confuse your audience if they are not well-designed. Use visuals that are relevant, clear, simple, and consistent. Avoid too much text, complex graphs, or flashy animations. Use colors, fonts, and layouts that are easy to read and understand. Use images, charts, diagrams, or videos that illustrate or enhance your message, not that compete or contradict it .
- **Aim for simplicity and consistency.** Your presentation should be easy to follow and remember for your audience. Use simple and clear language, avoid jargon or technical terms, and explain any acronyms or abbreviations. Use transitions, signposts, and summaries to guide your audience through your presentation and highlight your main points. Use repetition, examples, and stories to reinforce your message and make it memorable. Use a consistent tone, style, and format throughout your presentation .
- **Know your research audience.** Your presentation should be tailored to the knowledge, interests, and expectations of your audience. Research your audience before your presentation and find out who they are, what they know, what they want to know, and what they care about. Use appropriate language, examples, and references that suit your audience and their level of expertise. Anticipate and address any questions, objections, or concerns your audience may have. Engage your audience by asking questions, inviting feedback, or using humor or anecdotes .
- **Rehearse your presentation.** The best way to improve your presentation skills is to practice your presentation as much as possible. Rehearse your presentation in front of a mirror, a recorder, a friend, or a colleague. Get feedback on your content, delivery, and slides. Identify and correct any mistakes, weaknesses, or areas of improvement. Practice your presentation in a similar setting and with a similar equipment as the actual presentation. Practice your presentation until you feel confident and comfortable .
- **Prepare, prepare, prepare.** Besides rehearsing your presentation, you also need to prepare for any possible challenges or difficulties that may arise during your presentation. Prepare a backup plan in case of technical issues, such as a power outage, a projector failure, or a lost file. Prepare a handout or a summary of your presentation that you can distribute to your audience in case they want to review or refer to your information. Prepare for the question and answer session by anticipating and researching the most likely or the most difficult questions you may face .
- **Back up your backup.** Even if you have prepared well, you may still encounter unexpected problems or situations during your presentation.
