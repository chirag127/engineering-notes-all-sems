# Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

## Introduction
- Technical communication is the process of conveying information to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as reports, proposals, manuals, instructions, presentations, etc.
- Technical communication can be classified into two categories: internal and external.
- Internal technical communication is directed to the members of an organization, such as employees, managers, or stakeholders.
- External technical communication is directed to the customers, clients, or the public outside the organization.
- Technical communication can also be classified into two modes: written and oral.
- Written technical communication is the use of text, graphics, or symbols to convey information.
- Oral technical communication is the use of speech, gestures, or audio-visual aids to convey information.

## Forms of Internal Technical Communication
- Some common forms of internal technical communication are:
  - Memos: short, informal messages that communicate information within an organization.
  - Letters: formal, written messages that communicate information to a specific person or group outside the organization.
  - Emails: electronic messages that communicate information to one or more recipients.
  - Reports: documents that present facts, findings, or recommendations on a specific topic or problem.
  - Proposals: documents that offer a plan, solution, or idea for a specific problem or opportunity.
  - Manuals: documents that provide instructions, guidelines, or procedures for using a product, service, or system.
  - Newsletters: publications that provide updates, news, or information to a specific audience within an organization.

## Forms of External Technical Communication
- Some common forms of external technical communication are:
  - Brochures: printed or digital materials that provide information, features, or benefits of a product, service, or organization.
  - Flyers: single-page materials that advertise or promote a product, service, or event.
  - Websites: online platforms that provide information, interaction, or transaction for a product, service, or organization.
  - Blogs: online journals that provide personal opinions, insights, or experiences on a specific topic or interest.
  - Social media: online networks that allow users to create, share, or consume content on various topics or interests.
  - Presentations: oral or visual displays that communicate information, ideas, or arguments to an audience.
  - Podcasts: audio or video recordings that provide information, entertainment, or education on a specific topic or interest.
