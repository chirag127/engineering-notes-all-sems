### Dimensions of Communication

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, feelings, emotions, and thoughts between two or more people. Communication can be verbal or nonverbal, oral or written, formal or informal, and intentional or unintentional. Communication can also be classified into different dimensions based on the direction, level, and purpose of the communication. Some of the common dimensions of communication are:

- **Downward communication**: This is the communication that flows from a higher level to a lower level in an organization or a group. For example, a manager communicating with an employee, a teacher communicating with a student, or a parent communicating with a child. The purpose of downward communication is to provide instructions, feedback, information, or guidance to the subordinates or followers.
- **Upward communication**: This is the communication that flows from a lower level to a higher level in an organization or a group. For example, an employee communicating with a manager, a student communicating with a teacher, or a child communicating with a parent. The purpose of upward communication is to provide feedback, information, suggestions, or complaints to the superiors or leaders.
- **Horizontal or lateral communication**: This is the communication that occurs among people at the same level in an organization or a group. For example, a colleague communicating with another colleague, a friend communicating with another friend, or a sibling communicating with another sibling. The purpose of horizontal communication is to coordinate, cooperate, share, or socialize with the peers or equals.
- **Diagonal or cross-wise communication**: This is the communication that occurs among people at different levels and different functions in an organization or a group. For example, a salesperson communicating with an accountant, a student communicating with a librarian, or a neighbor communicating with a police officer. The purpose of diagonal communication is to facilitate, integrate, or solve problems across different departments or roles.
- **Verbal or nonverbal communication**: This is the communication that involves the use of words or symbols (verbal) or the use of gestures, expressions, or body language (nonverbal) to convey meaning. For example, a person communicating with words, writing, or sign language (verbal) or a person communicating with eye contact, facial expressions, or posture (nonverbal). The purpose of verbal communication is to express, inform, persuade, or instruct, while the purpose of nonverbal communication is to complement, reinforce, regulate, or contradict the verbal communication.
- **Content or relational communication**: This is the communication that focuses on the information or message (content) or the relationship or emotion (relational) of the communication. For example, a person communicating the facts, data, or logic of a topic (content) or a person communicating the feelings, attitudes, or values of a topic (relational). The purpose of content communication is to inform, explain, or persuade, while the purpose of relational communication is to establish, maintain, or change the relationship between the communicators.
