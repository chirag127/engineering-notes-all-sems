## Dimensions of Oral Communication

Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth. Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose. Oral communication can also be verbal or nonverbal, depending on the use of language and other cues.

Some of the dimensions of oral communication are:

- **Oral/Written Dimension**: This dimension refers to the difference between spoken communication and written communication. Spoken communication is more immediate, spontaneous, and interactive, while written communication is more deliberate, structured, and permanent. Spoken communication also relies more on vocal and visual cues, such as tone, pitch, volume, gestures, and facial expressions, while written communication relies more on words, punctuation, and grammar. 
- **Verbal/Nonverbal Dimension**: This dimension refers to the difference between the words used in communication and the other signals that accompany them. Verbal communication involves the words you say, and nonverbal communication involves how you say them—your tone of voice, your facial expression, body language, and so forth. Nonverbal communication can reinforce, contradict, or modify the verbal message, and can also convey emotions, attitudes, and relationships. 
- **Formal/Informal Dimension**: This dimension refers to the difference between the level of formality and the degree of familiarity in communication. Formal communication follows certain rules, conventions, and protocols, and is often used in professional, academic, or legal settings. Informal communication is more casual, relaxed, and personal, and is often used in social, friendly, or intimate settings. Formal communication tends to be more clear, precise, and objective, while informal communication tends to be more ambiguous, subjective, and expressive. 
- **Interaction Involvement Dimension**: This dimension refers to the difference between the level of engagement and the degree of attention in communication. Interaction involvement consists of three interrelated dimensions: responsiveness, perceptiveness, and attentiveness. Responsiveness is the degree to which you show interest, enthusiasm, and support for the speaker. Perceptiveness is the degree to which you understand, empathize, and interpret the speaker's message. Attentiveness is the degree to which you focus, listen, and avoid distractions during the communication. High interaction involvement indicates effective oral communication, while low interaction involvement indicates ineffective oral communication. 

## Voice Dynamics

Voice dynamics are the aspects of your voice that affect how you sound and how you communicate your message. Voice dynamics include:

- **Pitch**: Pitch is the highness or lowness of your voice. Pitch can vary depending on your mood, emotion, or intention. A high pitch can indicate excitement, surprise, or fear, while a low pitch can indicate calmness, confidence, or authority. Pitch can also be used to emphasize certain words or phrases, or to create contrast or interest in your speech. 
- **Volume**: Volume is the loudness or softness of your voice. Volume can vary depending on your audience, setting, or purpose. A loud volume can indicate confidence, enthusiasm, or anger, while a soft volume can indicate shyness, intimacy, or secrecy. Volume can also be used to attract attention, to show respect, or to create suspense or drama in your speech. 
- **Rate**: Rate is the speed at which you speak. Rate can vary depending on your mood, emotion, or intention. Rate can also be used to convey information, to create emphasis, or to show variation in your speech.
