### The flow of Communication

The flow of communication refers to the direction and channels of communication within an organization or between different parties. Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, opinions, or emotions with others. Communication can be verbal, nonverbal, written, or visual. Communication can also be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose.

The flow of communication can affect the efficiency, productivity, and quality of work in an organization. It can also influence the relationships, trust, and satisfaction among the employees, managers, customers, and other stakeholders. Therefore, it is important to understand the different types of communication flow and how to use them effectively.

There are five main types of communication flow within an organization:

- **Downward communication**: This is communication that flows from the higher levels of an organization to the lower levels, or down the chain of command. For example, a manager may communicate the goals, policies, procedures, or feedback to the employees. Downward communication can be used to inform, instruct, motivate, or evaluate the subordinates. Downward communication can also be used to establish authority and control. However, downward communication can also create problems such as distortion, misunderstanding, or resentment if it is not clear, timely, or respectful.

- **Upward communication**: This is communication that flows from the lower levels of an organization to the higher levels, or up the chain of command. For example, an employee may communicate the problems, suggestions, opinions, or achievements to the manager. Upward communication can be used to provide feedback, report progress, seek guidance, or express grievances. Upward communication can also be used to enhance participation and empowerment. However, upward communication can also face barriers such as filtering, distortion, or suppression if it is not encouraged, supported, or rewarded.

- **Lateral communication**: This is communication that flows among the same or similar levels of an organization, or across the chain of command. For example, a team member may communicate with another team member, or a department may communicate with another department. Lateral communication can be used to coordinate, collaborate, share information, or solve problems. Lateral communication can also be used to foster teamwork and innovation. However, lateral communication can also cause conflicts, confusion, or duplication if it is not aligned, consistent, or respectful.

- **Diagonal communication**: This is communication that flows across different levels and functions of an organization, or diagonally across the chain of command. For example, a salesperson may communicate with an engineer, or a customer service representative may communicate with a finance officer. Diagonal communication can be used to facilitate, integrate, or streamline the work processes. Diagonal communication can also be used to improve efficiency and quality. However, diagonal communication can also create challenges such as misunderstanding, miscommunication, or bypassing if it is not clear, accurate, or authorized.

- **External communication**: This is communication that flows between an organization and its external environment, such as customers, suppliers, competitors, regulators, media, or society. For example, a company may communicate its products, services, values, or reputation to the public. External communication can be used to promote, persuade, inform, or influence the external stakeholders. External communication can also be used to build trust and goodwill. However, external communication can also expose risks such as negative publicity, legal issues, or ethical dilemmas if it is not honest, ethical, or responsible.

Technical communication is a specific type of communication that involves communicating expert, technical, or highly complex information to a non-expert or more general audience. Technical communication often involves technology (though not always). Technical communication can be verbal, nonverbal, written, or visual. Technical communication can also be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose.

Technical communication responds to a specific rhetorical situation. Further, technical communication is dependent upon context. As technical communicators, it is important to consider your goals, your audience’s specific needs, and the context that creates the need for this text.

Technical communication can use any of the five types of communication flow described above, depending on the situation and purpose. For example, a technical writer may use downward communication to write a user manual for a software product, upward communication to write a progress report for a project manager, lateral communication to write a proposal for a potential client, diagonal communication to write a feasibility study for a new product idea, or external communication to write a press release for a new technology.
