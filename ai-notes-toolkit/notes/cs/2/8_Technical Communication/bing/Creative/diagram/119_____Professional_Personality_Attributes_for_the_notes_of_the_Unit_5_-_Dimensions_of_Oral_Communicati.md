### Professional Personality Attributes

Professional personality attributes are the habits, attitudes and physical traits of a person that influence how they perform in their professional roles. They are often related to the soft skills that are essential for effective communication, teamwork and problem-solving in the workplace. Some examples of professional personality attributes are:

- **Openness to experience**: This attribute refers to the depth and complexity of an individual's mental life and experiences. It involves being curious, creative, imaginative and willing to try new things. Openness to experience can help professionals to adapt to changing situations, learn from feedback and generate innovative ideas.
- **Conscientiousness**: This attribute refers to the tendency to control impulses and act in socially acceptable ways, behaviours that facilitate goal-directed behaviour. It involves being organized, responsible, diligent and self-disciplined. Conscientiousness can help professionals to meet deadlines, follow rules and standards, and deliver high-quality work.
- **Extroversion**: This attribute refers to the degree to which a person seeks stimulation and interaction from the external environment. It involves being outgoing, sociable, energetic and assertive. Extroversion can help professionals to network, collaborate, lead and persuade others.
- **Agreeableness**: This attribute refers to the degree to which a person is cooperative, friendly and compassionate towards others. It involves being polite, respectful, trusting and helpful. Agreeableness can help professionals to build rapport, resolve conflicts, support and motivate others.
- **Neuroticism**: This attribute refers to the degree to which a person experiences negative emotions such as anxiety, anger and sadness. It involves being sensitive, emotional, nervous and self-conscious. Neuroticism can hinder professional performance by impairing decision-making, reducing confidence and increasing stress. However, it can also motivate professionals to seek improvement, avoid mistakes and cope with challenges.
- **Capable**: This attribute refers to the ability to perform a task or job effectively and efficiently. It involves having the necessary skills, knowledge and experience to achieve the desired outcomes. Capable professionals are competent, proficient, skilled and knowledgeable.
- **Confident**: This attribute refers to the belief in one's own abilities and worth. It involves having a positive self-image, self-esteem and self-efficacy. Confident professionals are self-assured, assertive, optimistic and resilient.
- **Cooperative**: This attribute refers to the willingness to work with others towards a common goal. It involves being collaborative, supportive, flexible and respectful. Cooperative professionals are team players, communicators, listeners and contributors.
- **Willingness to learn**: This attribute refers to the desire to acquire new information and skills and to improve one's existing ones. It involves being curious, open-minded, receptive and proactive. Professionals who are willing to learn are learners, seekers, improvers and innovators.
- **Positive attitude**: This attribute refers to the tendency to have a favourable outlook on situations and events. It involves being optimistic, cheerful, enthusiastic and hopeful. Professionals who have a positive attitude are motivators, inspirers, encouragers and energizers.
- **Conflict resolution**: This attribute refers to the ability to handle and resolve disagreements and disputes in a constructive and respectful way. It involves being calm, rational, empathetic and diplomatic. Professionals who are good at conflict resolution are mediators, negotiators, peacemakers and problem-solvers.
- **Professional appearance**: This attribute refers to the way a person presents themselves physically in the workplace. It involves dressing appropriately, grooming well, maintaining hygiene and following the dress code. Professionals who have a professional appearance are respectful, credible, confident and competent.
- **Reliable**: This attribute refers to the consistency and dependability of a person's actions and behaviours. It involves being punctual, honest, trustworthy and loyal. Professionals who are reliable are dependable, accountable, responsible and faithful.
- **Ethical**: This attribute refers to the adherence to moral principles and values in the workplace. It involves being honest, fair, respectful and responsible. Professionals who are ethical are principled, honourable, righteous and virtuous.
- **Respectful**: This attribute refers to the recognition and appreciation of the dignity and worth of others. It involves being courteous, polite, considerate and tolerant. Professionals who are respectful are civil, gracious, humble and appreciative.
