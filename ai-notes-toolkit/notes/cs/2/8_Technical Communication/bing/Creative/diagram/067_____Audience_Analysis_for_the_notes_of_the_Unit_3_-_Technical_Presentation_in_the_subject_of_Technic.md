### Audience Analysis

- Audience analysis is the process of identifying and understanding the needs, interests, expectations, and characteristics of the people who will listen to or read your technical communication.
- Audience analysis helps you to tailor your message to suit your audience's needs and preferences, and to achieve your communication goals more effectively.
- Audience analysis involves three steps: identifying your audience, assessing your audience, and adapting to your audience.

#### Identifying your audience

- To identify your audience, you need to answer the following questions:
  - Who are they? What are their roles, backgrounds, education levels, and demographics?
  - What are they looking for? What are their needs, problems, goals, and motivations?
  - How will they use your communication? What are their expectations, preferences, and attitudes towards your topic and your medium?
  - How much do they know about your topic? What are their existing knowledge, skills, and experience?
  - How do they feel about your topic? What are their opinions, values, beliefs, and emotions?

#### Assessing your audience

- To assess your audience, you need to evaluate the following aspects of your audience:
  - Their level of interest: How much attention and engagement will they give to your communication?
  - Their level of understanding: How much information and explanation will they need to comprehend your communication?
  - Their level of agreement: How much persuasion and evidence will they need to accept your communication?
  - Their level of action: How much motivation and guidance will they need to act on your communication?

#### Adapting to your audience

- To adapt to your audience, you need to adjust the following elements of your communication:
  - Your purpose: What is the main goal of your communication? To inform, instruct, persuade, or entertain your audience?
  - Your content: What are the key points and supporting details of your communication? How do they relate to your audience's needs, interests, and expectations?
  - Your structure: How do you organize and sequence your communication? How do you use headings, subheadings, transitions, and summaries to guide your audience?
  - Your style: How do you use language, tone, voice, and visuals to communicate your message? How do you appeal to your audience's logic, emotions, and values?
