### Consonant sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Consonant sounds are speech sounds that are produced by partially or completely blocking the airflow through the vocal tract  .
- Consonants are represented by letters of the alphabet that correspond to those sounds, such as B, T, F, Z, etc. However, some letters can represent more than one consonant sound, such as C, which can be pronounced as /k/ or /s/.
- Consonant sounds can be classified according to different criteria, such as the place of articulation, the manner of articulation, and the voicing of the sound.
- The place of articulation refers to the location where the airflow is obstructed, such as the lips, the teeth, the alveolar ridge, the palate, etc. For example, the consonant sound /p/ is a bilabial sound, meaning that it is produced by bringing both lips together.
- The manner of articulation refers to the way the airflow is obstructed, such as by stopping it completely, by creating a narrow passage, by allowing air to escape through the nose, etc. For example, the consonant sound /t/ is a plosive sound, meaning that it is produced by a sudden release of air after a complete closure.
- The voicing of the sound refers to whether the vocal cords vibrate or not during the production of the sound. For example, the consonant sound /b/ is a voiced sound, meaning that the vocal cords vibrate, while the consonant sound /p/ is a voiceless sound, meaning that the vocal cords do not vibrate.
- Consonant sounds are important for oral communication, as they help to distinguish words and convey meaning. For example, the words "bat" and "pat" have different meanings because of the different consonant sounds at the beginning.
- Consonant sounds also affect the voice dynamics, such as the pitch, loudness, and quality of the voice. For example, voiced consonants tend to lower the pitch and loudness of the voice, while voiceless consonants tend to raise them.
- Consonant sounds can be practiced and improved by paying attention to the articulation, the airflow, and the voicing of the sound, and by using exercises such as tongue twisters, minimal pairs, and reading aloud.
