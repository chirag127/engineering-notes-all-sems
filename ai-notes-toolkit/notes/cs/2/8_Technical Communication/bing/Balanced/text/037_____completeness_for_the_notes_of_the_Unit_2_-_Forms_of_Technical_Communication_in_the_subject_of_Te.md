### Completeness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective technical communication, along with clarity, conciseness, correctness, coherence, courtesy, and credibility.
- Completeness means that the communication provides all the necessary and relevant information to the intended audience, without omitting any essential facts, figures, or details.
- Completeness also means that the communication is well-organized, logical, and easy to follow, with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion.
- Completeness is important for technical communication because it helps to achieve the following objectives:
  - To inform, instruct, persuade, or request action from the audience.
  - To avoid confusion, misunderstanding, or misinterpretation of the message.
  - To establish trust, credibility, and goodwill with the audience.
  - To comply with legal, ethical, or professional standards and requirements.
- Some of the strategies to ensure completeness in technical communication are:
  - Analyze the purpose, audience, and context of the communication.
  - Research and gather all the relevant and reliable information from credible sources.
  - Use the 5W1H method (who, what, when, where, why, and how) to cover all the important aspects of the topic.
  - Use headings, subheadings, lists, tables, charts, graphs, diagrams, or other visual aids to organize and present the information clearly and effectively.
  - Use transitions, connectors, or signposts to guide the audience through the communication and show the relationships between the ideas.
  - Provide definitions, explanations, examples, or illustrations to clarify any technical terms, concepts, or processes.
  - Provide references, citations, or acknowledgments to acknowledge the sources of information and avoid plagiarism.
  - Use feedback, revision, and editing to check and improve the quality and accuracy of the communication.
