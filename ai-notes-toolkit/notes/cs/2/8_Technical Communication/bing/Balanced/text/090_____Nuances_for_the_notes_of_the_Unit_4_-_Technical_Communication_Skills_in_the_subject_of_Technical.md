### Nuances for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Technical communication skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and efficiently in technical contexts, such as engineering, science, technology, and business.
- Technical communication skills include writing, speaking, listening, reading, and visualizing technical information, as well as using appropriate tools and technologies to facilitate communication.
- Technical communication skills are essential for professionals who need to convey technical information to various audiences, such as colleagues, clients, customers, regulators, and the general public.
- Technical communication skills can be improved by following some general principles, such as:
  - Know your purpose and audience: Identify the main goal and the intended readers of your communication, and tailor your message accordingly.
  - Be clear and concise: Use simple and precise language, avoid jargon and ambiguity, and eliminate unnecessary words and details.
  - Be accurate and credible: Provide accurate and reliable information, cite your sources, and acknowledge any limitations or uncertainties.
  - Be coherent and logical: Organize your information in a clear and logical structure, use transitions and signposts, and maintain a consistent tone and style.
  - Be ethical and respectful: Follow the relevant ethical standards and codes of conduct, respect the rights and opinions of others, and avoid plagiarism and dishonesty.
- Technical communication skills can be applied to various types of documents and presentations, such as:
  - Technical reports: Formal documents that present the results of a technical project, such as a research, design, or testing process.
  - Technical proposals: Documents that propose a technical solution to a problem or a technical approach to a project, such as a grant application, a contract bid, or a feasibility study.
  - Technical manuals: Documents that provide instructions or guidance on how to use, operate, or maintain a technical product, system, or service, such as a user manual, a troubleshooting guide, or a safety manual.
  - Technical articles: Documents that communicate technical information to a specific or general audience, such as a journal article, a magazine article, or a blog post.
  - Technical presentations: Oral or visual presentations that deliver technical information to a live audience, such as a conference presentation, a product demonstration, or a training session.
