## Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

Oral communication is the process of expressing ideas, feelings, and information through spoken words. Oral communication can be classified into two dimensions: verbal and non-verbal.

- Verbal communication refers to the use of language, words, and sounds to convey meaning. Verbal communication can be further divided into vocal and non-vocal elements. Vocal elements include pitch, volume, rate, tone, and accent. Non-vocal elements include silence, pauses, and filler words.
- Non-verbal communication refers to the use of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and physical appearance to convey meaning. Non-verbal communication can complement, contradict, or substitute verbal communication.

Voice dynamics are the variations in the vocal elements of verbal communication that affect the delivery and impact of a message. Voice dynamics can be used to emphasize key points, express emotions, create interest, and establish rapport with the audience. Voice dynamics include the following aspects:

- Pitch: the highness or lowness of a sound. Pitch can indicate the speaker's mood, attitude, and confidence. A high pitch can convey excitement, surprise, or nervousness. A low pitch can convey calmness, seriousness, or authority.
- Volume: the loudness or softness of a sound. Volume can indicate the speaker's intensity, enthusiasm, and assertiveness. A high volume can convey confidence, passion, or anger. A low volume can convey intimacy, secrecy, or boredom.
- Rate: the speed or pace of speaking. Rate can indicate the speaker's level of preparation, interest, and urgency. A fast rate can convey excitement, eagerness, or anxiety. A slow rate can convey calmness, thoughtfulness, or hesitation.
- Tone: the quality or character of a sound. Tone can indicate the speaker's attitude, emotion, and intention. A positive tone can convey warmth, friendliness, or sincerity. A negative tone can convey coldness, hostility, or sarcasm.
- Accent: the distinctive way of pronouncing words that reflects the speaker's regional, social, or cultural background. Accent can indicate the speaker's identity, origin, and education. A strong accent can convey authenticity, uniqueness, or pride. A weak accent can convey adaptability, assimilation, or insecurity.
