### Falling Tone for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Falling tone is a type of intonation that involves a downward pitch movement at the end of a sentence or a phrase.
- Falling tone is used to communicate different meanings and functions in oral communication, such as:
  - Completing a statement or an idea. For example, "I like this book."
  - Asking an information question that begins with who, what, where, when, why, how, how much, how many, how often, and so on. For example, "Where do you live?"  
  - Expressing certainty, confidence, or finality. For example, "That's the correct answer."
  - Indicating politeness, formality, or distance. For example, "Thank you for your cooperation."
- Falling tone is essential for clear communication in English, as it signals the end of a sentence or a question, and helps the listener understand the speaker's intention and attitude.  
- Falling tone can be contrasted with rising tone, which involves an upward pitch movement at the end of a sentence or a phrase, and is used to communicate different meanings and functions, such as:
  - Asking a yes-no question or a tag question. For example, "Do you like this book?"
  - Showing uncertainty, doubt, or hesitation. For example, "I think that's the correct answer?"
  - Expressing interest, curiosity, or surprise. For example, "You live in New York?"
  - Indicating friendliness, informality, or closeness. For example, "Thanks for your cooperation."
- To produce a falling tone, the speaker should start with a high pitch and then lower it gradually until the end of the sentence or the phrase. The speaker should also stress the last syllable or word of the sentence or the phrase, as it carries the most important information. 
- To practice falling tone, the speaker can use a visual aid, such as a line or a curve that goes down, and follow it with their voice. The speaker can also listen to and imitate native speakers who use falling tone in different contexts and situations.
