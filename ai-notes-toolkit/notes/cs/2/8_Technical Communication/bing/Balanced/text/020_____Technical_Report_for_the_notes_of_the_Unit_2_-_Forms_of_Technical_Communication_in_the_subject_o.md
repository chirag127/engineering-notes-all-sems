### Technical Report for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical report is a document that conveys the results of a scientific or technical research or project to a specific audience.
- A technical report usually has the following elements:
  - Title page: It contains the title of the report, the name and affiliation of the author(s), the date of submission, and the name of the organization or institution that commissioned the report.
  - Abstract: It is a concise summary of the main purpose, scope, methods, results, and conclusions of the report. It helps the reader to decide whether to read the full report or not.
  - Table of contents: It lists the main sections and subsections of the report, along with their page numbers. It helps the reader to navigate the report and find the information they need.
  - List of figures and tables: It lists the figures and tables that are included in the report, along with their captions and page numbers. It helps the reader to locate and understand the visual aids that support the text.
  - Introduction: It introduces the topic and the background of the report, states the problem or question that motivated the research or project, explains the objectives and scope of the report, and outlines the main points and structure of the report.
  - Literature review: It reviews the existing literature and research that are relevant to the topic and the problem of the report. It shows the reader what has been done and what gaps or limitations exist in the current knowledge or practice.
  - Methods: It describes the methods, procedures, techniques, tools, materials, and data that were used to conduct the research or project. It explains how the data were collected, analyzed, and interpreted, and how the results were obtained. It also discusses the validity, reliability, and limitations of the methods and data.
  - Results: It presents the findings and outcomes of the research or project, using tables, graphs, charts, diagrams, maps, photographs, or other visual aids to illustrate the data and highlight the main points. It also interprets and explains the results, and compares them with the objectives and expectations of the report.
  - Discussion: It discusses the implications, significance, and impact of the results, and relates them to the literature review and the problem of the report. It also identifies the strengths, weaknesses, and limitations of the research or project, and suggests possible improvements, extensions, or applications for future work.
  - Conclusion: It summarizes the main points and findings of the report, and answers the problem or question that motivated the research or project. It also states the main contributions and recommendations of the report, and indicates the direction for further research or action.
  - References: It lists the sources that were cited or consulted in the report, using a consistent and appropriate citation style. It helps the reader to verify the information and to locate the original sources for further reading or research.
  - Appendices: It contains the supplementary or supporting information that is not essential for the main text of the report, but may be useful or interesting for the reader. It may include raw data, calculations, code, samples, questionnaires, surveys, interviews, glossary, abbreviations, or other relevant documents.
