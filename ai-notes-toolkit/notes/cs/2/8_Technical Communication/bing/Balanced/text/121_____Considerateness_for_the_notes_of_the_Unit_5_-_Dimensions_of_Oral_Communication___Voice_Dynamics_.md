### Considerateness for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Considerateness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, completeness, correctness, concreteness, and courtesy .
- Considerateness means showing respect and empathy for the audience, understanding their needs and expectations, and avoiding words or expressions that might offend or hurt them  .
- Considerateness is important for oral communication because it helps to build rapport and trust with the listeners, stimulate a positive reaction, and avoid misunderstandings or conflicts  .
- Some features of considerate oral communication are  :
  - Emphasizing on "you" approach, which means focusing on the benefits or interests of the audience, rather than the speaker or the organization.
  - Empathizing with the audience and exhibiting interest in them, which means listening actively, acknowledging their feelings and opinions, and asking relevant questions.
  - Showing optimism and positivity towards the audience, which means emphasizing on "what is possible" rather than "what is impossible", and using positive words such as jovial, committed, thanks, warm, healthy, help, etc.
  - Adapting the message to the audience's level of knowledge, background, culture, and preferences, which means using appropriate language, tone, examples, and evidence.
  - Being polite and professional, which means avoiding rude, sarcastic, or aggressive expressions, and maintaining a respectful and courteous tone of voice.
- Considerateness can also be influenced by the conversational style of the speaker, which can be classified as high involvement or high considerateness. High involvement speakers tend to speak faster, interrupt more, and use more expressive features, such as gestures, intonation, and repetition. High considerateness speakers tend to speak slower, pause more, and use more supportive features, such as questions, feedback, and backchannels. Both styles have their advantages and disadvantages, depending on the context and the purpose of the communication.
- To achieve considerateness in oral communication, it is also important to create listenable messages, which are messages that are easy to understand and remember by the audience. Some characteristics of listenable messages are:
  - Simplicity, which means using familiar words, short sentences, and clear structure.
  - Organization, which means having a clear introduction, body, and conclusion, and using transitions and signposts to guide the audience.
  - Redundancy, which means repeating or rephrasing the main points or key information to reinforce the message and aid recall.
  - Personalization, which means using personal pronouns, stories, examples, and humor to connect with the audience and make the message more engaging and memorable.
  - Interaction, which means inviting the audience to participate, ask questions, give feedback, or share their opinions or experiences, to create a dialogue and a sense of involvement.
