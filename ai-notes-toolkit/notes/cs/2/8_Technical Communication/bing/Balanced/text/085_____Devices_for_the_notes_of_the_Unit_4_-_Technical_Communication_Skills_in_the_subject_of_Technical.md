### Devices for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is a broad field that includes any form of communication that is about technical or specialized topics, uses technology such as web pages or help files, or provides instruction about how to do something.
- Technical communication can take many forms, such as manuals, reports, proposals, memos, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, videos, etc.
- Technical communication has several characteristics that distinguish it from other types of communication, such as:
  - It is audience-centered, meaning it focuses on the needs, expectations, and preferences of the intended users of the information.
  - It is purpose-driven, meaning it has a clear and specific goal or objective that guides the content and structure of the document.
  - It is factual and accurate, meaning it provides reliable and verifiable information that is based on evidence and research.
  - It is clear and concise, meaning it uses simple and direct language, avoids unnecessary details, and organizes information logically and coherently.
  - It is ethical and honest, meaning it respects the rights and responsibilities of the communicators and the users, and does not mislead, deceive, or manipulate them.
  - It is accessible and inclusive, meaning it considers the diversity and accessibility of the users, and uses appropriate design elements, such as graphics, images, hyperlinks, video, audio, etc., to enhance the communication  .
- Devices for the notes of technical communication are tools or methods that help the technical communicators to create, manage, and deliver effective and efficient technical documents. Some examples of devices for the notes of technical communication are:
  - Quotations, paraphrases, and summaries, which are ways of using the words or ideas of other sources in the technical document. Quotations are exact copies of the source, paraphrases are restatements of the source in one's own words, and summaries are brief overviews of the main points of the source. Technical communicators typically distinguish these devices when taking notes, and cite the sources properly to avoid plagiarism and give credit to the original authors.
  - Outlines, which are plans or frameworks that show the main topics and subtopics of the technical document. Outlines help the technical communicators to organize their ideas, arrange their information logically, and identify the gaps or weaknesses in their content. Outlines can be formal or informal, depending on the level of detail and structure required.
  - Graphics, which are visual elements that supplement or complement the text in the technical document. Graphics can include tables, charts, graphs, diagrams, maps, photographs, icons, etc. Graphics help the technical communicators to illustrate complex or abstract concepts, highlight important or relevant data, compare or contrast different information, and attract or maintain the attention of the users. Graphics should be clear, accurate, consistent, and integrated with the text .
  - Hyperlinks, which are links that connect one part of the technical document to another part, or to an external source of information. Hyperlinks help the technical communicators to provide additional or related information, reference other sources, or direct the users to other resources. Hyperlinks should be descriptive, relevant, and functional .
  - Multimedia, which are forms of communication that use more than one medium, such as sound, video, animation, etc. Multimedia help the technical communicators to enhance the interactivity, engagement, and appeal of the technical document. Multimedia should be appropriate, compatible, and accessible for the users .
