### C.V./Resume writing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

A C.V. (curriculum vitae) or a resume is a document that summarizes your education, skills, work experience, and achievements. It is used to apply for jobs and showcase your qualifications to potential employers. A C.V. or a resume can also help you stand out from other candidates and increase your chances of getting an interview.

Here are some tips on how to write an effective C.V. or resume:

- **Tailor your C.V. or resume to every job you apply for.** Do not use a generic or one-size-fits-all C.V. or resume. Instead, research the job description and the company and customize your document to match the specific requirements and expectations of the employer. Highlight your relevant skills, achievements, and keywords that match the job description. 
- **Show your passion.** Employers want to hire candidates who are enthusiastic, motivated, and interested in the job and the company. Demonstrate your passion by mentioning your career goals, your values, and your reasons for applying. You can also include a personal statement or a professional summary that showcases your personality and your strengths.  
- **Be specific and show your impact.** Avoid vague or generic statements that do not convey your value or your contribution. Instead, use concrete examples and hard numbers to illustrate your accomplishments and your impact. For example, instead of saying "I improved sales", say "I increased sales by 25% in six months by implementing a new marketing strategy". Use action verbs and power words to describe your achievements and skills.   
- **Be confident.** Your C.V. or resume is your opportunity to showcase your best qualities and to impress the employer. Do not be shy or modest about your achievements and skills. Use positive and confident language and avoid words that undermine your credibility, such as "maybe", "hopefully", or "try". Emphasize your strengths and your potential and avoid mentioning your weaknesses or gaps in your experience.  
- **Proofread.** Your C.V. or resume should be free of spelling, grammar, punctuation, and formatting errors. These errors can make you look unprofessional and careless and can ruin your chances of getting an interview. Proofread your document several times and use tools such as spell checkers, grammar checkers, or online editors to help you catch and correct any mistakes. You can also ask someone else to review your C.V. or resume and give you feedback.   
- **Use a professional email address.** Your email address is one of the first things that the employer will see on your C.V. or resume. It should be professional and appropriate and reflect your name or your brand. Avoid using email addresses that are too casual, childish, or inappropriate, such as "cutiepie@gmail.com" or "badboy69@yahoo.com". Instead, use a simple and formal email address, such as "john.smith@gmail.com" or "jane.doe@outlook.com".  
- **Provide proof and use hard numbers.** Employers want to see evidence and data that support your claims and demonstrate your value. Whenever possible, use hard numbers, statistics, percentages, or metrics to quantify your achievements and skills. For example, instead of saying "I managed a team of 10 people", say "I led a team of 10 people and increased their productivity by 15%". Use charts, graphs, or tables to visualize your data and make it easier to understand.  
- **Use a clear and consistent format.** Your C.V. or resume should be easy to read and follow and have a clear and consistent layout and design. Use legible and standard fonts, such as Calibri, Georgia, Open Sans, or Cambria, and avoid fonts that are too fancy, decorative, or hard to read, such as Comic Sans, Papyrus, or Curlz. Use the same font size, color, and style throughout your document and avoid using too many different fonts or colors. Use headings, subheadings, bullet points, and white space to organize your information and make it more scannable.
