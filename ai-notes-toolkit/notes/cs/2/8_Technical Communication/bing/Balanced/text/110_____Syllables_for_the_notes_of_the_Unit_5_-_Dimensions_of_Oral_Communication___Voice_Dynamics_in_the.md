### Syllables for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- A syllable is a unit of pronunciation that consists of one vowel sound, with or without surrounding consonants, forming the whole or a part of a word     .
- Syllables can have more than one letter, but not more than one sound.
- Syllables can have more than one consonant and more than one vowel, as long as they have only one vowel sound.
- Syllables are important for oral communication because they affect the pace, rhythm, stress, and intonation of speech.
- Syllables can be classified into different types based on the number and position of consonants and vowels in them. Some common types are:
  - Open syllables: syllables that end with a vowel sound, such as `me`, `go`, `sky`, `see`.
  - Closed syllables: syllables that end with a consonant sound, such as `cat`, `dog`, `red`, `stop`.
  - Vowel-consonant-e syllables: syllables that have a vowel followed by a consonant and a silent e, such as `cake`, `fine`, `hope`, `use`.
  - Consonant-le syllables: syllables that have a consonant followed by the letters le, such as `table`, `little`, `cable`, `puzzle`.
  - R-controlled syllables: syllables that have a vowel followed by the letter r, which changes the vowel sound, such as `car`, `her`, `bird`, `fur`.
  - Double vowel syllables: syllables that have two vowels next to each other, which can make one sound or two sounds, such as `boat`, `rain`, `lion`, `idea`.
- Syllables can also be divided into stressed and unstressed syllables based on the emphasis or loudness given to them in speech. Stressed syllables are usually longer, louder, and higher in pitch than unstressed syllables.
- The stress pattern of a word can change its meaning, pronunciation, and part of speech. For example, `record` can be a noun or a verb depending on which syllable is stressed.
- The stress pattern of a sentence can also change its meaning and tone. For example, `I love you` can have different meanings depending on which word is stressed.
- Voice dynamics refers to the variation and control of the volume, pitch, tone, and quality of the voice in oral communication.
- Voice dynamics can be used to convey emotions, attitudes, intentions, and personalities of the speaker and to engage the listener's attention and interest.
- Voice dynamics can also be used to emphasize, clarify, and modify the meaning of the spoken words and to create contrast, rhythm, and harmony in speech.
- Voice dynamics can be improved by practicing vocal exercises, breathing techniques, articulation, and pronunciation, and by being aware of the purpose, audience, and context of the oral communication.
