### The flow of Communication

The flow of communication refers to the direction and channels of communication within an organization or between different parties. Communication can be classified into five main types: downward, upward, lateral, diagonal, and external.

- Downward communication is communication that flows from the higher levels of an organization to the lower levels, or down the chain of command. For example, a manager may communicate the goals and policies of the organization to the employees, or a teacher may communicate the syllabus and assignments to the students.
- Upward communication is communication that flows from the lower levels of an organization to the higher levels, or up the chain of command. For example, an employee may communicate feedback, suggestions, or complaints to the manager, or a student may communicate questions or concerns to the teacher.
- Lateral communication is communication that flows among the same level of an organization or between peers. For example, colleagues may communicate to coordinate tasks, share information, or solve problems, or classmates may communicate to work on a group project or study together.
- Diagonal communication is communication that flows across different levels and functions of an organization or between different parties. For example, a salesperson may communicate with a customer, or a technical writer may communicate with a subject matter expert.
- External communication is communication that flows between an organization and its external stakeholders, such as customers, suppliers, investors, media, or the public. For example, a company may communicate its products, services, or values to the potential or existing customers, or a nonprofit organization may communicate its mission, vision, or impact to the donors or beneficiaries.

The flow of communication is influenced by the context, purpose, and audience of the communication. Technical communication is a type of communication that involves communicating expert, technical, or highly complex information to a non-expert or more general audience . Technical communication responds to a specific rhetorical situation and is dependent upon the context that creates the need for the text. Technical communication also involves using technology, such as the web, multimedia, or software, to create and deliver information . Technical communicators must ensure accuracy, clarity, consistency, and usability when producing documents, presentations, videos, or other modes of communication . Technical communicators must also consider the ethical, legal, and social implications of their communication.
