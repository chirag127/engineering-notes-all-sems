### Solution of communication problems with verbal and non verbal means

Communication problems are any obstacles or barriers that prevent effective exchange of information, ideas, or feedback. Communication problems can occur at any level of an organization, between individuals or groups, and in any mode of communication, such as verbal, nonverbal, written, or visual. Communication problems can have negative impacts on productivity, performance, relationships, and morale. Therefore, it is important to identify and solve communication problems as soon as possible.

Some of the common communication problems and their solutions are:

- **Miscommunication**: This is when the sender and the receiver of a message interpret it differently, leading to confusion, misunderstanding, or conflict. Miscommunication can be caused by unclear or ambiguous language, cultural differences, assumptions, biases, or lack of feedback. The solution to miscommunication is to view the matter from the receiver's perspective and set the tone of the communication accordingly. Furthermore, the sender should use simple, precise, and respectful language, avoid jargon and slang, and provide examples or illustrations to clarify the message. The receiver should ask questions, paraphrase, or summarize the message to confirm understanding and provide feedback.
- **Noise**: This is any external or internal factor that interferes with the transmission or reception of a message, such as physical noise, distractions, emotions, stress, or fatigue. Noise can reduce the quality and effectiveness of communication, and cause errors, misunderstandings, or frustration. The solution to noise is to choose an appropriate communication channel and environment, such as face-to-face, phone, email, or chat, depending on the purpose, urgency, and complexity of the message. The sender and the receiver should also minimize distractions, such as background noise, notifications, or multitasking, and focus on the message. The sender and the receiver should also be aware of their own emotions and stress levels, and avoid communicating when they are angry, anxious, or tired.
- **Lack of feedback**: This is when the sender or the receiver of a message does not provide a response or a reaction, such as a nod, a smile, a comment, or a question. Lack of feedback can create uncertainty, doubt, or misunderstanding, and prevent the sender from knowing if the message was received, understood, or accepted. The solution to lack of feedback is to encourage and solicit feedback from the communication partner, and provide feedback in return. The sender and the receiver should use verbal and nonverbal cues, such as eye contact, gestures, facial expressions, tone of voice, and words, to show interest, attention, agreement, or disagreement. The sender and the receiver should also ask for clarification, confirmation, or evaluation of the message, and express appreciation or gratitude for the communication.
- **Lack of trust**: This is when the sender or the receiver of a message does not have confidence or faith in the communication partner, such as their honesty, credibility, competence, or reliability. Lack of trust can hinder communication, and create suspicion, resentment, or hostility. The solution to lack of trust is to build and maintain trust through consistent, respectful, and transparent communication. The sender and the receiver should be honest, open, and sincere in their communication, and avoid lying, hiding, or exaggerating information. The sender and the receiver should also acknowledge and respect each other's opinions, feelings, and needs, and avoid criticizing, blaming, or judging. The sender and the receiver should also follow through on their promises, commitments, and expectations, and apologize and forgive when necessary.
