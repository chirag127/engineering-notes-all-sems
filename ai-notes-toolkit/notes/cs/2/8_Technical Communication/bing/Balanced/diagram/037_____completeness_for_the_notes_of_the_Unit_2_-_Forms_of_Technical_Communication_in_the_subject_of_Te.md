### Completeness

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, correctness, coherence, courtesy, and credibility.
- Completeness means that a message contains all the information that the receiver needs to understand it and take appropriate action.
- Completeness also means that a message is accurate, factual, and specific, and does not omit any important details or mislead the receiver.
- Completeness is essential for achieving the purpose of communication, whether it is to inform, persuade, request, or instruct.
- Completeness can be achieved by following some guidelines, such as:

  - Answering the five W's and one H: who, what, when, where, why, and how.
  - Providing relevant examples, evidence, or data to support the main points.
  - Anticipating and addressing the potential questions, doubts, or objections of the receiver.
  - Using appropriate headings, subheadings, lists, tables, graphs, or diagrams to organize and present the information clearly and logically.
  - Checking the message for accuracy, completeness, and consistency before sending it.

- Completeness can be assessed by asking some questions, such as:

  - Does the message cover all the necessary information that the receiver needs to know?
  - Does the message provide enough details and examples to clarify the main points?
  - Does the message anticipate and answer the possible queries or concerns of the receiver?
  - Does the message avoid any ambiguity, vagueness, or distortion of facts?
