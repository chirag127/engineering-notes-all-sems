### Impersonal Technical Communication

- Impersonal technical communication is a style of writing or speaking that avoids using personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, we, etc.) and focuses on conveying data and important information.
- Impersonal technical communication is common in engineering, science, and business fields, where the goal is to present facts, results, and recommendations in a clear and objective way.
- Impersonal technical communication can use the passive voice, the third person, or the generic you to avoid referring to specific individuals or groups. For example:

  - Passive voice: "The wire was cut" instead of "I cut the wire".
  - Third person: "The team conducted the experiment" instead of "We conducted the experiment".
  - Generic you: "You can see the graph in Figure 1" instead of "I have shown the graph in Figure 1".

- Impersonal technical communication can have some advantages, such as:

  - It can make the message more concise and precise, by eliminating unnecessary words and opinions.
  - It can make the message more credible and authoritative, by emphasizing the evidence and logic rather than the personal views or feelings of the writer or speaker.
  - It can make the message more inclusive and respectful, by avoiding assumptions or biases about the audience or the subject matter.

- Impersonal technical communication can also have some disadvantages, such as:

  - It can make the message more impersonal and distant, by losing the connection and rapport with the audience or the stakeholders.
  - It can make the message more ambiguous and passive, by hiding the responsibility and accountability of the writer or speaker.
  - It can make the message more dull and boring, by lacking the creativity and personality of the writer or speaker.

- Impersonal technical communication is not always appropriate or effective, depending on the purpose, context, and audience of the message. Sometimes, using a personal or a mixed voice can be more suitable or persuasive, especially when:

  - The message is intended to motivate, inspire, or persuade the audience, such as in a proposal, a pitch, or a call to action.
  - The message is based on personal experience, observation, or reflection, such as in a case study, a report, or a testimonial.
  - The message is meant to establish trust, credibility, or rapport with the audience, such as in a letter, a memo, or a presentation.

- Impersonal technical communication is a skill that requires practice and awareness of the rhetorical situation and the expectations of the audience. It is important to choose the appropriate voice and tone for each message, and to balance the impersonal and the personal elements to achieve the desired effect.
