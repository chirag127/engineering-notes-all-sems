### Stimulus for Oral Communication

- A stimulus is an idea or a concept that is activated by the sender of a message .
- A stimulus can be internal or external, depending on the source of the idea or concept.
- Internal stimuli are generated from within the sender's mind, such as memories, feelings, thoughts, or opinions.
- External stimuli are triggered by the sender's environment, such as objects, events, people, or situations.
- A stimulus can be attention-getting in a productive or distracting way, depending on how relevant, novel, or intense it is.
- A productive stimulus can motivate the sender to communicate effectively and clearly, while a distracting stimulus can interfere with the sender's encoding or decoding process.
- A stimulus can also influence the receiver of a message, depending on how the receiver perceives, interprets, and responds to it.
- A stimulus can be verbal or nonverbal, depending on the mode of communication used by the sender .
- Verbal stimuli are expressed through words, either spoken or written .
- Nonverbal stimuli are expressed through gestures, facial expressions, body language, or other cues .
- A stimulus can be enhanced or diminished by the oral sensory system, which is the part of the nervous system that processes information from the mouth and throat.
- The oral sensory system can affect the sender's and the receiver's speech and language development, as well as their emotional regulation and social interaction.
- The oral sensory system can be stimulated or calmed by various activities, such as sucking, chewing, drinking, or talking .
- The oral sensory system can also be affected by various factors, such as oral hygiene, nutrition, allergies, or medical conditions.
