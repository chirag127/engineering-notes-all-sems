### Audience Analysis

- Audience analysis is the process of identifying and understanding the characteristics, needs, expectations, and preferences of the people who will receive your message.
- Audience analysis helps you to tailor your message to suit your audience's interests, level of knowledge, attitude, and context.
- Audience analysis involves three steps: identifying your audience, gathering information about your audience, and adapting your message to your audience.

#### Identifying your audience

- Your audience is the group of people who will read, listen to, or view your message.
- Your audience may be primary, secondary, or tertiary, depending on their role and relationship to your message.
- Primary audience: the people who have the most power and influence over your message, such as decision-makers, clients, or supervisors.
- Secondary audience: the people who have some interest or stake in your message, but are not directly affected by it, such as colleagues, peers, or competitors.
- Tertiary audience: the people who have little or no interest or stake in your message, but may encounter it by chance, such as the general public, media, or future readers.

#### Gathering information about your audience

- You can gather information about your audience by using various methods, such as observation, surveys, interviews, focus groups, or online research.
- You can gather information about your audience's demographics, such as age, gender, education, occupation, culture, or location.
- You can gather information about your audience's psychographics, such as values, beliefs, attitudes, opinions, motivations, or preferences.
- You can gather information about your audience's context, such as the purpose, occasion, setting, or expectations of your message.

#### Adapting your message to your audience

- You can adapt your message to your audience by using various strategies, such as selecting an appropriate tone, style, language, format, and medium.
- Tone: the attitude or emotion that you convey in your message, such as formal, informal, friendly, serious, or persuasive.
- Style: the way that you organize and present your message, such as logical, chronological, spatial, or problem-solution.
- Language: the words and expressions that you use in your message, such as technical, nontechnical, simple, complex, or jargon.
- Format: the layout and design of your message, such as headings, subheadings, lists, tables, graphs, or images.
- Medium: the channel or mode of delivery of your message, such as oral, written, visual, or digital.
