## Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can be written, oral, visual, or multimodal, depending on the context, audience, and purpose.
- Technical communication has the following characteristics:
  - Accuracy: The information and instructions are correct, precise, and reliable.
  - Clarity: The information and instructions are easy to understand and follow.
  - Conciseness: The information and instructions are brief and to the point, without unnecessary details or repetition.
  - Coherence: The information and instructions are logically organized and connected, with clear transitions and signposts.
  - Completeness: The information and instructions cover all the relevant aspects and answer all the possible questions of the audience.
  - Consistency: The information and instructions use the same format, style, tone, terminology, and conventions throughout.
  - Courtesy: The information and instructions respect the audience's needs, expectations, and preferences, and avoid offensive or biased language.
  - Ethics: The information and instructions are honest, fair, and respectful of the rights and responsibilities of the audience and the communicator.
  - Relevance: The information and instructions are appropriate and useful for the audience and the purpose.
  - Usability: The information and instructions are easy to access, navigate, and apply by the audience.

- Technical communication can have various purposes, such as:
  - Informing: Providing factual information or data to the audience, such as definitions, descriptions, explanations, or reports.
  - Instructing: Providing step-by-step directions or procedures to the audience, such as manuals, guides, or tutorials.
  - Persuading: Providing arguments or evidence to the audience, such as proposals, recommendations, or evaluations.
  - Collaborating: Providing feedback or input to the audience, such as reviews, comments, or suggestions.

- Technical communication can have various audiences, such as:
  - Experts: People who have a high level of knowledge or skill in a specific field or domain, such as engineers, scientists, or doctors.
  - Non-experts: People who have a low level of knowledge or skill in a specific field or domain, such as students, customers, or general public.
  - Mixed: People who have a varying level of knowledge or skill in a specific field or domain, such as managers, colleagues, or stakeholders.

- Technical communication can have various contexts, such as:
  - Academic: The setting or situation is related to education or research, such as a classroom, a laboratory, or a conference.
  - Professional: The setting or situation is related to work or career, such as a company, an industry, or a market.
  - Personal: The setting or situation is related to individual or social interests or goals, such as a hobby, a community, or a cause.

- Technical communication can have various modes, such as:
  - Written: The information and instructions are presented in text, such as a document, an email, or a website.
  - Oral: The information and instructions are presented in speech, such as a presentation, a podcast, or a video.
  - Visual: The information and instructions are presented in images, such as a graph, a diagram, or a photo.
  - Multimodal: The information and instructions are presented in a combination of text, speech, and images, such as a slideshow, a poster, or a brochure.
