### Thesis/Project Writing

- A thesis or a project is a form of technical communication that introduces, explains, and evaluates a research topic in a specific field of study.
- Technical communication is the art and science of making complex technical information accessible, usable, and relevant to a variety of people in a variety of settings.
- The purpose of a thesis or a project is to demonstrate the mastery of the skills and knowledge acquired in the master's program in technical communication .
- The steps involved in writing a thesis or a project are:

  - Choosing a topic that is relevant, original, and feasible for the chosen field of study.
  - Conducting a literature review to situate the topic within the context of the available research and identify the research gap or problem.
  - Developing a research question or a problem statement that guides the research process and the scope of the thesis or project.
  - Selecting a research method that is appropriate for the research question or problem and the type of data to be collected and analyzed.
  - Collecting and analyzing the data using the chosen research method and tools.
  - Writing and designing the thesis or project document that follows the conventions and expectations of the field and the audience.
  - Presenting and defending the thesis or project to a committee of experts and peers.

- The structure and format of a thesis or project document may vary depending on the field, the institution, and the style guide, but a typical thesis or project document consists of the following sections:

  - Title page: It contains the title of the thesis or project, the name of the author, the name of the institution, the date of submission, and other relevant information.
  - Abstract: It is a concise summary of the thesis or project that states the research question or problem, the research method, the main findings, and the implications or recommendations.
  - Table of contents: It lists the main sections and sub-sections of the thesis or project document along with their page numbers.
  - List of figures and tables: It lists the figures and tables used in the thesis or project document along with their captions and page numbers.
  - Introduction: It introduces the topic, the purpose, the scope, and the significance of the thesis or project. It also provides the background information, the research gap or problem, the research question or problem statement, and the overview of the thesis or project document.
  - Literature review: It reviews the relevant and current literature on the topic and identifies the theoretical framework, the key concepts, the research methods, and the findings of the previous studies. It also highlights the research gap or problem that the thesis or project aims to address.
  - Research method: It describes and justifies the research method chosen for the thesis or project. It explains the data collection and analysis procedures, the instruments and tools used, the sampling and selection criteria, the ethical considerations, and the limitations and challenges of the research method.
  - Results: It presents and summarizes the data collected and analyzed using the research method. It uses figures, tables, charts, graphs, and other visual aids to illustrate the results and make them clear and understandable.
  - Discussion: It interprets and evaluates the results in relation to the research question or problem, the literature review, and the theoretical framework. It discusses the implications, contributions, and limitations of the findings and suggests directions for future research.
  - Conclusion: It summarizes the main points and findings of the thesis or project and restates the research question or problem and the purpose and significance of the thesis or project. It also provides recommendations or suggestions for practice, policy, or further research based on the findings.
  - References: It lists the sources cited in the thesis or project document using the citation style and format required by the field, the institution, or the style guide.
  - Appendices: It includes any additional or supplementary material that supports the thesis or project document, such as data sets, questionnaires, interview transcripts, consent forms, etc.

- The writing and design of a thesis or project document should follow the principles of effective technical communication, such as:

  - Clarity: It uses clear and precise language, avoids jargon and ambiguity, and defines any technical terms or acronyms.
  - Accuracy: It uses reliable and valid sources, provides accurate and complete information, and acknowledges any errors or uncertainties.
  - Conciseness: It uses short and simple sentences and paragraphs, avoids unnecessary words or details, and focuses on the main points and findings.
