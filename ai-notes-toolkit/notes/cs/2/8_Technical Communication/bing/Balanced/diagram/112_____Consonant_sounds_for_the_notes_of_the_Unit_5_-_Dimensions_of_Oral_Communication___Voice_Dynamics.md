### Consonant sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Consonants are letters that represent certain speech sounds, specifically sounds that involve blocking the air before it leaves the mouth, such as with the tongue, lips, or throat.
- There are 24 consonant sounds in English. They are described in terms of their articulation, which is the way the speech organs (such as the tongue, lips, and teeth) are positioned and moved to produce the sound .
- Consonant sounds can be classified into different types based on their articulation, such as:
  - Plosives: Sounds with complete closure and sudden release of the air stream. Examples are /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /g/. E.g.: pin, bin, tin, din, kin, gun.
  - Affricates: Sounds with complete closure and slow release of the air stream, producing a friction noise. Examples are /tʃ/, /dʒ/. E.g.: chin, jam.
  - Fricatives: Sounds with partial closure and continuous release of the air stream, producing a hissing noise. Examples are /f/, /v/, /θ/, /ð/, /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /h/. E.g.: fan, van, thin, then, sin, zen, shin, vision, hat.
  - Nasals: Sounds with complete closure of the oral cavity and release of the air stream through the nose. Examples are /m/, /n/, /ŋ/. E.g.: man, nan, sing.
  - Liquids: Sounds with partial closure of the oral cavity and free flow of the air stream. Examples are /l/, /r/. E.g.: lap, rap.
  - Glides: Sounds with minimal closure of the oral cavity and smooth transition to a vowel. Examples are /w/, /j/. E.g.: wet, yet.
- Consonant sounds can also be classified into voiced and voiceless sounds, depending on whether the vocal cords vibrate or not during the articulation. Voiced sounds have a low-pitched hum, while voiceless sounds have no hum. Examples of voiced sounds are /b/, /d/, /g/, /v/, /z/, /ʒ/, /dʒ/, /m/, /n/, /ŋ/, /l/, /r/, /w/, /j/. Examples of voiceless sounds are /p/, /t/, /k/, /f/, /s/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/, /θ/, /h/.
- Consonant sounds are important for oral communication and voice dynamics, as they affect the clarity, accuracy, and expressiveness of speech. Some tips for improving consonant sounds are:
  - Pay attention to the position and movement of the tongue, lips, and teeth when producing consonant sounds.
  - Practice the contrast between voiced and voiceless sounds, especially in word-final position. E.g.: bad-pad, bed-pet, bag-pack.
  - Avoid dropping or weakening consonant sounds, especially in unstressed syllables. E.g.: interesting, chocolate, library.
  - Avoid adding or changing consonant sounds, especially in clusters. E.g.: ask-aks, strength-strenth, clothes-cloze.
