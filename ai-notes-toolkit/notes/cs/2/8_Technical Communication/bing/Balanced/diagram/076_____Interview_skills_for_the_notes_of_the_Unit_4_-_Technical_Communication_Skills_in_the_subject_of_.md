### Interview skills for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills

Interview skills are the abilities and techniques that help you communicate effectively and persuasively with potential employers during a job interview. Interview skills include both verbal and nonverbal communication skills, as well as other aspects such as preparation, research, and etiquette. Interview skills are important for technical communication because they help you showcase your knowledge, skills, and experience in a relevant and professional manner.

Some of the interview skills that are essential for technical communication are:

- **Active listening**: Active listening means paying close attention to who you’re communicating with by engaging with them, asking questions and rephrasing. Active listening helps you understand the interviewer's expectations, needs, and concerns, and respond accordingly. Active listening also shows your interest, respect, and attentiveness to the interviewer.
- **Verbal communication**: Verbal communication refers to the words and tone you use to convey your message. Verbal communication skills help you deliver clear, concise, and coherent answers that demonstrate your technical knowledge and abilities. Verbal communication skills also include the ability to ask relevant and insightful questions, use appropriate technical terminology and jargon, and avoid filler words and vague statements .
- **Nonverbal communication**: Nonverbal communication refers to the body language, facial expressions, eye contact, and gestures you use to supplement your verbal communication. Nonverbal communication skills help you convey confidence, enthusiasm, and professionalism during an interview. Nonverbal communication skills also include the ability to maintain a good posture, smile, nod, and use appropriate hand movements .
- **Friendliness**: Friendliness is the quality of being polite, courteous, and approachable during an interview. Friendliness helps you build rapport and trust with the interviewer, and create a positive impression. Friendliness also includes the ability to use appropriate greetings, salutations, and expressions of gratitude, as well as to show interest and appreciation for the interviewer's time and attention.
- **Brevity**: Brevity is the quality of being concise and to the point in your communication. Brevity helps you avoid unnecessary or irrelevant information, and focus on the key points and details that matter. Brevity also helps you respect the interviewer's time and attention span, and avoid rambling or repeating yourself.
- **Research**: Research is the process of gathering and analyzing information about the job, the company, and the industry before an interview. Research helps you prepare relevant and informed answers, and demonstrate your knowledge and interest in the position and the organization. Research also helps you identify the skills, qualifications, and expectations that the interviewer is looking for, and tailor your communication accordingly.
- **Preparation**: Preparation is the process of planning and practicing your communication before an interview. Preparation helps you anticipate and rehearse possible questions and scenarios, and develop confident and effective responses. Preparation also helps you identify and address your strengths and weaknesses, and improve your communication skills. Preparation also includes the ability to dress appropriately, arrive on time, and bring the necessary documents and materials for the interview.
- **Etiquette**: Etiquette is the set of rules and norms that govern appropriate and respectful behavior during an interview. Etiquette helps you create a professional and positive impression, and avoid any mistakes or misunderstandings that could harm your chances of getting hired. Etiquette includes the ability to follow the interviewer's lead, avoid interruptions or distractions, and thank the interviewer for the opportunity.
- **Transparency**: Transparency is the quality of being honest, open, and truthful in your communication. Transparency helps you establish credibility and trust with the interviewer, and show your integrity and accountability. Transparency also includes the ability to admit your mistakes, acknowledge your limitations, and provide constructive feedback.
- **Interviewing subject-matter experts (SMEs)**: Interviewing SMEs is the skill of asking relevant and effective questions to collect accurate and reliable information from experts in a specific field or domain. Interviewing SMEs is important for technical communication because it helps you gather the necessary data and insights to create clear, accurate, and useful technical documents and products. Interviewing SMEs requires the ability to establish rapport, use appropriate language and terminology, and verify and document the information.
