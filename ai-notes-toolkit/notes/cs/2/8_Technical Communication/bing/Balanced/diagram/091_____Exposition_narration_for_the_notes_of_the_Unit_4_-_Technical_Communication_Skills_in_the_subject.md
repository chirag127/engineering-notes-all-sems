### Exposition narration

- Exposition narration is a type of technical communication that explains or informs the reader about a topic, process, or phenomenon.
- Exposition narration can be used for various purposes, such as describing how something works, providing instructions, presenting facts and data, comparing and contrasting different concepts, or analyzing a problem and proposing a solution.
- Exposition narration can be organized in different ways, depending on the purpose and the audience of the communication. Some common methods of organization are:
  - Chronological: presenting the information in the order of time or sequence of events.
  - Spatial: presenting the information in the order of space or location.
  - Topical: presenting the information in the order of topics or categories.
  - Problem-solution: presenting the information in the order of a problem and its solution or solutions.
  - Cause-effect: presenting the information in the order of a cause and its effect or effects.
- Exposition narration should be clear, concise, accurate, and objective. It should use appropriate language, tone, and style for the intended audience and context. It should also use appropriate visual aids, such as graphs, tables, diagrams, or images, to support the verbal information.
- Exposition narration should follow the general structure of technical communication, which consists of:
  - Introduction: introducing the topic, purpose, scope, and main points of the communication.
  - Body: developing the main points with supporting details, examples, evidence, and explanations.
  - Conclusion: summarizing the main points, restating the purpose, and providing recommendations, implications, or suggestions for further action or research.
