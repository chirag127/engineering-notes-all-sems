### Correctness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

Correctness is one of the six qualities of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, completeness, coherence, and courtesy. Correctness means that the details of a message are accurate, the grammar and punctuation are correct, and the appearance and tone are appropriate for the context and audience. Correctness is important for technical communication because it enhances the credibility of the writer, avoids confusion and misunderstanding, and prevents errors and misinformation.

Some aspects of correctness in technical communication are:

- **Passive voice**: The passive voice is a verb structure that places the object of the action before the subject. For example, "The report was written by John." The passive voice is often used in technical communication to emphasize the result or process rather than the agent or actor. For example, "The experiment was conducted in a controlled environment." The passive voice can also be used to avoid assigning blame or responsibility. For example, "The deadline was missed due to unforeseen circumstances." However, the passive voice can also make the writing less direct, clear, and concise. Therefore, it should be used sparingly and only when appropriate for the purpose and audience of the document.

- **Commands and directions**: Technical communication often contains a lot of commands and directions, such as instructions, procedures, guidelines, and recommendations. Commands and directions are usually written in the imperative mood, which is a verb form that expresses a request, order, or advice. For example, "Turn on the device." "Do not touch the wires." "Follow the safety precautions." Commands and directions should be clear, specific, and consistent. They should also use polite and respectful language, especially when addressing the reader directly. For example, "Please enter your password." "You may want to backup your files."

- **Bullets and lists**: Technical communication often relies heavily on bulleted or numbered lists to organize and present information in a concise and readable way. Bullets and lists can be used to show a sequence of steps, a hierarchy of categories, a set of options, or a summary of points. Bullets and lists should be formatted consistently and follow parallel structure. Parallel structure means that each item in a list should have the same grammatical form and level of detail. For example, "To complete the project, you need to: - Conduct a literature review. - Design a survey. - Analyze the data. - Write a report." Bullets and lists should also be introduced by a clear and complete sentence that explains the purpose and scope of the list. For example, "Here are some tips for writing a good resume:"

- **Word choice**: Technical communication requires precise and accurate word choice to convey the intended meaning and avoid ambiguity or confusion. Technical communication should avoid jargon, slang, idioms, and euphemisms that may not be understood by the audience or may be inappropriate for the context. Technical communication should also avoid vague, abstract, or subjective words that may not convey the exact information or evidence. Technical communication should use specific, concrete, and objective words that are supported by facts, data, and examples. For example, instead of saying "The product is very good", say "The product has a high customer satisfaction rating of 95%".
