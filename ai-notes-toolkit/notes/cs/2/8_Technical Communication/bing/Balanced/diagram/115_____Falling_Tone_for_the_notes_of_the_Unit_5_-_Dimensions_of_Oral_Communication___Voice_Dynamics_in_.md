### Falling Tone

- Falling tone is a type of intonation in oral communication that involves a downward pitch movement at the end of a sentence or a phrase.
- Falling tone is used to signal the completion of a thought, a statement, or a question that requests information.
- Falling tone can also convey certainty, confidence, finality, or authority in oral communication.
- Examples of falling tone in oral communication are:

  - Statements: I'm going to the library. / She likes chocolate. / It's raining outside.
  - Information questions: Who are you? / What time is it? / How do you spell your name?
  - Commands: Sit down. / Stop talking. / Listen to me.

- To produce a falling tone, you need to start with a high or medium pitch and then lower your voice gradually until you reach the end of the sentence or the phrase.
- To practice falling tone, you can use a mirror, a recorder, or a partner and try to imitate the examples above or create your own sentences or questions with falling tone.
