# Synopsis of Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication: Definition, Types, and Principles

In this unit, we learned about the forms of technical communication, which include the definition and characteristics, the types and purposes, and the principles and strategies of technical communication.

Technical communication conveys specialized information to a specific audience for a specific purpose. It is accurate, clear, concise, and appropriate. Accuracy means that the information is correct, reliable, and verifiable. Clarity means that the information is easy to understand and follow. Conciseness means that the information is brief and to the point. Appropriateness means that the information is suitable and relevant for the audience and the purpose.

Technical communication can be classified into two main types: informative and persuasive. Informative technical communication aims to inform, explain, describe, or instruct the audience about a topic or a process. Persuasive technical communication aims to persuade, convince, or motivate the audience to take a certain action or adopt a certain attitude. Examples of informative technical communication are manuals, reports, proposals, and memos. Examples of persuasive technical communication are advertisements, brochures, flyers, and letters.

Technical communication follows four main principles: audience, purpose, context, and ethics. Audience refers to the intended readers or users of the technical communication. Purpose refers to the goal or objective of the technical communication. Context refers to the situation or environment in which the technical communication takes place. Ethics refers to the moral and professional standards that guide the technical communication. These principles help the technical communicator to design, develop, and deliver effective and appropriate technical communication.
