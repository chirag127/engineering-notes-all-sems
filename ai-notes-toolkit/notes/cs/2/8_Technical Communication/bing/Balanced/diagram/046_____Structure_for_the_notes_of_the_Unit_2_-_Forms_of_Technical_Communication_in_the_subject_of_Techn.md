# Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

## Introduction
- Technical communication is the process of conveying information to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as reports, proposals, manuals, instructions, presentations, etc.
- Technical communication can be classified into two categories: internal and external.
- Internal communication is directed to the members of an organization, such as employees, managers, or stakeholders.
- External communication is directed to the customers, clients, or the general public.

## Objectives
- To understand the purpose, characteristics, and types of technical communication.
- To identify the audience, context, and constraints of technical communication.
- To compare and contrast the different forms of technical communication.
- To apply the principles of effective technical communication to various forms of technical communication.

## Forms of Technical Communication
- Technical communication can take various forms, depending on the purpose, audience, context, and constraints of the communication situation.
- Some of the common forms of technical communication are:

### Reports
- Reports are documents that provide information, analysis, or recommendations on a specific topic or problem.
- Reports can be classified into two types: informational and analytical.
- Informational reports provide facts, data, or descriptions without interpretation or evaluation.
- Analytical reports provide interpretation, evaluation, or recommendations based on facts, data, or descriptions.
- Reports can also be classified based on their length, format, and structure, such as memos, letters, short reports, long reports, etc.

### Proposals
- Proposals are documents that propose a plan, a project, or a solution to a problem or a need.
- Proposals can be classified into two types: solicited and unsolicited.
- Solicited proposals are written in response to a request or a call for proposals from a potential client or sponsor.
- Unsolicited proposals are written without a specific request or a call for proposals from a potential client or sponsor.
- Proposals can also be classified based on their purpose, such as research proposals, grant proposals, business proposals, etc.

### Manuals
- Manuals are documents that provide instructions, guidelines, or procedures for using, operating, or maintaining a product, a system, or a service.
- Manuals can be classified into two types: user manuals and technical manuals.
- User manuals are written for the end-users of a product, a system, or a service, and they focus on how to use, operate, or maintain it.
- Technical manuals are written for the technicians, engineers, or specialists who design, develop, or repair a product, a system, or a service, and they focus on how it works, how it is constructed, or how it is tested.
- Manuals can also be classified based on their format, such as printed manuals, online manuals, video manuals, etc.

### Instructions
- Instructions are documents that provide step-by-step directions for performing a specific task or activity.
- Instructions can be classified into two types: procedural and process.
- Procedural instructions provide a sequence of actions or commands that must be followed to complete a task or achieve a goal.
- Process instructions provide a description of the stages or phases that occur in a natural or artificial phenomenon or operation.
- Instructions can also be classified based on their medium, such as written instructions, oral instructions, graphical instructions, etc.

### Presentations
- Presentations are oral or visual forms of communication that deliver information, ideas, or arguments to an audience.
- Presentations can be classified into two types: informative and persuasive.
- Informative presentations provide facts, data, or explanations about a topic or an issue.
- Persuasive presentations provide opinions, arguments, or recommendations about a topic or an issue.
- Presentations can also be classified based on their mode, such as live presentations, recorded presentations, online presentations, etc.

## Summary
- Technical communication is the process of conveying information to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as reports, proposals, manuals, instructions, presentations, etc.
- Technical communication can be classified into two categories: internal and external.
- Technical communication can be classified into different types based on the purpose, audience, context, and constraints of the communication situation.
- Technical communication should follow the principles of effective communication, such as clarity, accuracy, conciseness, coherence, completeness, etc.
