# Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

## Dimensions of Oral Communication

Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth. It involves the use of verbal and nonverbal elements to convey a message effectively and efficiently.

There are two main dimensions of oral communication: oral and written. These dimensions refer to the mode of delivery and the form of expression of the message.

### Oral Dimension

The oral dimension of oral communication involves the spoken communication of the message. It requires the use of voice, tone, pitch, volume, pace, accent, pronunciation, and articulation to create a clear and engaging speech. The oral dimension also involves the use of nonverbal cues, such as facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, posture, and proxemics, to enhance the meaning and impact of the message.

Some of the advantages of the oral dimension are:

- It allows for immediate feedback and clarification from the receiver.
- It can convey emotions and attitudes more effectively than written communication.
- It can create a sense of rapport and trust between the speaker and the listener.
- It can adapt to different situations and audiences more easily than written communication.

Some of the disadvantages of the oral dimension are:

- It can be affected by external noise and interference.
- It can be misunderstood or misinterpreted due to poor delivery or lack of nonverbal cues.
- It can be forgotten or distorted over time due to the lack of a permanent record.
- It can be influenced by the speaker's bias, prejudice, or personal agenda.

### Written Dimension

The written dimension of oral communication involves the transcription of thoughts and ideas into written words. It requires the use of language, grammar, syntax, punctuation, spelling, and style to create a coherent and accurate text. The written dimension also involves the use of visual aids, such as charts, graphs, tables, diagrams, and images, to support and illustrate the message.

Some of the advantages of the written dimension are:

- It can provide a clear and precise record of the message.
- It can reach a larger and more diverse audience than oral communication.
- It can allow for more time and careful planning before sending the message.
- It can be edited and revised before finalizing the message.

Some of the disadvantages of the written dimension are:

- It can lack the personal touch and emotional appeal of oral communication.
- It can be delayed or ignored by the receiver due to information overload or lack of interest.
- It can be misread or misinterpreted due to the absence of verbal and nonverbal cues.
- It can be affected by the reader's bias, prejudice, or personal agenda.

## Voice Dynamics

Voice dynamics are the variations in the quality and characteristics of the voice that affect the delivery and reception of oral communication. They include the following aspects:

- Volume: the loudness or softness of the voice.
- Pitch: the highness or lowness of the voice.
- Tone: the attitude or emotion conveyed by the voice.
- Pace: the speed or rate of the voice.
- Pause: the silence or break in the voice.
- Emphasis: the stress or accent on certain words or phrases.
- Modulation: the change or variation in the voice.

Voice dynamics can be used to enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of oral communication by:

- Capturing and maintaining the attention and interest of the listener.
- Expressing and conveying the message clearly and accurately.
- Emphasizing and highlighting the key points and ideas of the message.
- Creating and establishing a rapport and trust with the listener.
- Influencing and persuading the listener to accept or act on the message.

Voice dynamics can also be used to avoid or overcome the barriers and challenges of oral communication by:

- Reducing or eliminating the external noise and interference.
- Improving or correcting the verbal and nonverbal cues.
- Clarifying or confirming the understanding and interpretation of the message.
- Resolving or preventing the conflicts and misunderstandings that may arise from the message.
- Addressing or acknowledging the feedback and criticism that may follow the message.
