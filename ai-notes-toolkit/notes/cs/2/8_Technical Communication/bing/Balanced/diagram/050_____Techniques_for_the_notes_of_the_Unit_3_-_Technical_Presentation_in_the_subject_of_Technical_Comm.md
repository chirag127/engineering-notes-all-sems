### Techniques for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation

- Notes are brief and concise reminders of what you want to say or show in your presentation. They can help you stay on track, avoid forgetting important points, and maintain eye contact with your audience.
- Notes can be written on paper, cards, slides, or electronic devices. The format and style of your notes depend on your personal preference, the type of presentation, and the available equipment.
- Some general guidelines for preparing notes are:

  - Use keywords, phrases, or sentences that trigger your memory and summarize your main points. Avoid writing full sentences or paragraphs that you will read verbatim.
  - Use bullet points, numbers, symbols, or colors to organize and highlight your notes. Avoid cluttering your notes with too much information or details.
  - Use simple and clear language that is easy to read and understand. Avoid using jargon, acronyms, or abbreviations that may confuse you or your audience.
  - Use visual aids, such as diagrams, charts, graphs, or images, to supplement your notes and illustrate your points. Avoid relying solely on visual aids or using them as a substitute for your notes.
  - Practice using your notes before your presentation and make adjustments as needed. Avoid making changes to your notes at the last minute or during your presentation.

- Some examples of different types of notes are:

  - Outline notes: These are notes that follow the structure and sequence of your presentation. They include the main headings, subheadings, and key points of each section. They can help you keep track of your progress and transition smoothly between topics.
  - Cue cards: These are small cards that contain one or two points per card. They can help you focus on one idea at a time and avoid looking at your notes too often. They can also be easily shuffled or rearranged if needed.
  - Slide notes: These are notes that are displayed on the same slide as your visual aids. They can help you integrate your notes and visuals and avoid switching between different devices. They can also be seen by your audience, so they should be clear and concise.
  - Speaker notes: These are notes that are displayed on a separate screen or device that only you can see. They can help you add more details or explanations to your slides or cues. They can also include reminders, prompts, or questions for yourself or your audience.
