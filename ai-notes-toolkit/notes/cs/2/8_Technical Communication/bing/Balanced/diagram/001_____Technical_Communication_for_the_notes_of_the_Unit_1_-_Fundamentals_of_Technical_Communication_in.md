# Technical Communication

## Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

### Technical Communication Definition

- Technical communication is the process of **conveying scientific, engineering, or other technical information** to a specific audience for a specific purpose  .
- Technical communication can be used to **define, instruct, inform, or persuade** the audience about technical or specialized topics, such as products, systems, software, or services.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as **documents, manuals, reports, presentations, videos, websites, or podcasts**.
- Technical communication requires **clear, concise, accurate, and ethical** language that meets the needs and expectations of the audience.

### Technical Communication Characteristics

- Technical communication has some distinctive characteristics that distinguish it from other types of communication, such as:

  - **Audience-centered**: Technical communication focuses on the needs, goals, and preferences of the audience, and adapts the content, style, tone, and format accordingly.
  - **Purpose-driven**: Technical communication has a clear and specific purpose, such as explaining a process, providing instructions, or persuading the audience to take an action.
  - **Context-sensitive**: Technical communication considers the context in which the communication takes place, such as the situation, the environment, the culture, the expectations, and the constraints of the audience and the communicator.
  - **Visual-oriented**: Technical communication uses visual elements, such as images, graphs, tables, charts, diagrams, or icons, to enhance the understanding, retention, and appeal of the information.
  - **Ethical**: Technical communication follows the principles of honesty, integrity, respect, and responsibility, and avoids plagiarism, falsification, misrepresentation, or discrimination.

### Technical Communication Process

- Technical communication involves a series of steps that help the communicator plan, create, and deliver the information effectively and efficiently, such as:

  - **Analyze**: The communicator analyzes the audience, the purpose, and the context of the communication, and determines the scope, the goals, and the requirements of the information.
  - **Research**: The communicator researches the topic, gathers relevant and reliable information, and evaluates the sources and the data.
  - **Organize**: The communicator organizes the information in a logical and coherent way, and creates an outline or a structure for the communication.
  - **Design**: The communicator designs the layout, the format, the style, and the visual elements of the communication, and chooses the appropriate medium and channel for the delivery.
  - **Write**: The communicator writes the content, using clear, concise, accurate, and ethical language, and following the conventions and standards of the genre and the discipline.
  - **Revise**: The communicator revises the content, checking for errors, inconsistencies, gaps, or redundancies, and improving the clarity, coherence, and correctness of the communication.
  - **Edit**: The communicator edits the content, proofreading for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and syntax, and ensuring the compliance with the style guide and the citation format.
  - **Test**: The communicator tests the communication, evaluating its effectiveness, usability, and accessibility, and soliciting feedback from the audience or other reviewers.
  - **Deliver**: The communicator delivers the communication, using the appropriate medium and channel, and following the ethical and legal guidelines.
