### Devices for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is a broad field that includes any form of communication that is about technical or specialized topics, uses technology such as web pages or help files, or provides instruction about how to do something.
- Technical communication often relies not only on text, but also on graphics, images, hyperlinks, video, audio, etc. to convey information effectively and efficiently to the audience.
- Technical communication requires planning, drafting, and revising technical documents that are clear and concise, as well as following ethical and professional standards.
- Some examples of technical communication are manuals, reports, proposals, instructions, presentations, websites, blogs, wikis, podcasts, etc.  .
- Devices for the notes are tools or techniques that help the technical communicator to record, organize, and outline information from various sources, such as books, articles, websites, interviews, etc.
- Some common devices for the notes are:

  - Quotations: direct words or phrases from a source, enclosed in quotation marks and attributed to the original author. Quotations should be used sparingly and only when the exact wording is important or authoritative.
  - Paraphrases: restatements of the main ideas or information from a source, using one's own words and sentence structure. Paraphrases should be accurate and faithful to the original meaning, and also attributed to the original author.
  - Summaries: brief overviews of the main points or arguments from a source, using one's own words and omitting details or examples. Summaries should capture the essence and scope of the source, and also attributed to the original author.
  - Outlines: hierarchical structures that show the relationships and order of the main topics and subtopics in a document or a project. Outlines should be consistent and logical, using headings, subheadings, and bullet points.
  - Tables: visual representations of data or information in rows and columns, using labels, headings, and borders. Tables should be clear and concise, using appropriate units, formats, and alignments.
  - Charts: graphical representations of data or information, using symbols, shapes, colors, and labels. Charts should be relevant and accurate, using appropriate types, scales, and legends.
