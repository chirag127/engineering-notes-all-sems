### Courtesy

Courtesy is one of the key components of effective communication. It means showing respect, consideration and goodwill to the receiver of the message. Courtesy can help to establish rapport, build trust and avoid misunderstandings. Courtesy can also reflect the sender's professionalism, credibility and ethical standards.

Some of the ways to achieve courtesy in technical communication are:

- Use positive and polite words that convey the you-attitude, such as please, thank you, appreciate, glad, etc.
- Avoid negative and harsh words that convey the me-attitude, such as sorry, regret, unfortunately, etc.
- Address the receiver by name, title or appropriate salutation, such as Dear, Mr., Ms., Dr., etc.
- Avoid biased, sexist, racist or offensive language that may hurt the receiver's feelings or dignity, such as man, mankind, chairman, etc.
- Use inclusive and respectful terms that acknowledge the diversity and equality of the receiver, such as people, humanity, chairperson, etc.
- Provide clear and accurate information that meets the receiver's needs and expectations, and avoid vague, misleading or false statements that may cause confusion or distrust.
- Use a friendly and conversational tone that engages the receiver and shows interest and enthusiasm, and avoid a cold and formal tone that may sound distant and indifferent.
- Use appropriate humor, anecdotes or stories that relate to the topic and the receiver, and avoid irrelevant, inappropriate or offensive jokes that may distract or annoy the receiver.
- Use a positive and constructive feedback that praises the receiver's strengths and suggests areas for improvement, and avoid a negative and destructive criticism that attacks the receiver's weaknesses and faults.
