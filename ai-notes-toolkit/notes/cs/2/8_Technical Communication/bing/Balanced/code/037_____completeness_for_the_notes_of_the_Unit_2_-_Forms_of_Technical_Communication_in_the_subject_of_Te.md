### Completeness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, which are: clarity, conciseness, correctness, courtesy, completeness, concreteness, and coherence .
- Completeness means that the communication must convey all the facts and information required by the audience, and answer the questions of who, what, when, where, why, and how  .
- Completeness is important for technical communication because it helps to promote goodwill, avoid misunderstandings, and achieve the desired outcome of the message .
- Completeness can be achieved by examining both the text and the visual components of the document, and ensuring that they are clear, accurate, relevant, and sufficient.
- Completeness can also be achieved by following the principles of definition, instruction, information, and persuasion, which are the main purposes of technical communication.
- Completeness can be assessed by checking if the message covers all the necessary details, provides enough evidence and examples, anticipates the audience's needs and questions, and includes a clear call to action .
