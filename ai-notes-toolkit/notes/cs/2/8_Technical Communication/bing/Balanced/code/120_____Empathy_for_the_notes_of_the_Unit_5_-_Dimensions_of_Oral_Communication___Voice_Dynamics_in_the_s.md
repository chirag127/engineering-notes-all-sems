### Empathy

- Empathy is the ability to understand and share the feelings, thoughts, and experiences of another person.
- Empathy is an important skill for effective oral communication, as it helps to build rapport, trust, and mutual understanding with the audience or the interlocutor.
- Empathy can be expressed in various ways, such as:
  - Using appropriate verbal and non-verbal cues, such as tone of voice, facial expressions, gestures, eye contact, and body language, to convey interest, respect, and concern.
  - Listening actively and attentively, without interrupting, judging, or imposing one's own opinions or solutions.
  - Asking open-ended questions, paraphrasing, summarizing, and reflecting back what the speaker has said, to show comprehension and clarification.
  - Acknowledging and validating the speaker's emotions, feelings, and perspectives, without minimizing, dismissing, or contradicting them.
  - Showing empathy and compassion, without pitying, patronizing, or sympathizing with the speaker.
  - Offering support, encouragement, and feedback, without being intrusive, directive, or manipulative.
  - Sharing relevant personal experiences, stories, or examples, without dominating, diverting, or distracting from the speaker's message.
  - Expressing appreciation, gratitude, and recognition, without being insincere, excessive, or flattering.
