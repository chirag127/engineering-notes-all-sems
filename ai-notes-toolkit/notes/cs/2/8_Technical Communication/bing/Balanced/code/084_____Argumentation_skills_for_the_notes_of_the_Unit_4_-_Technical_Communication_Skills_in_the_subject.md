### Argumentation skills

- Argumentation skills are the ability to present and support a claim or a position using logical reasoning and evidence.
- Argumentation skills are important for technical communication because they help you to persuade your audience, justify your decisions, and resolve conflicts or disagreements.
- To develop argumentation skills, you need to follow these steps:

  - Identify your purpose and audience: What are you trying to achieve and who are you trying to convince?
  - Formulate your main claim or thesis: What is the main point or message you want to convey?
  - Gather and evaluate evidence: What facts, data, examples, or sources can you use to support your claim and counter possible objections?
  - Organize your argument: How will you structure your argument to make it clear, coherent, and convincing?
  - Write and revise your argument: How will you express your argument using appropriate language, tone, and style?
  - Present and defend your argument: How will you deliver your argument to your audience and respond to feedback or criticism?

- Some tips for effective argumentation are:

  - Use logic and reasoning: Avoid logical fallacies, such as hasty generalizations, false dilemmas, or ad hominem attacks, that can weaken your argument or undermine your credibility.
  - Use evidence and citation: Provide relevant and reliable evidence to support your claim and cite your sources properly to avoid plagiarism and acknowledge your sources.
  - Use counter-arguments and refutations: Anticipate and address the opposing views or potential objections to your claim and show why they are flawed or insufficient.
  - Use concession and qualification: Acknowledge the limitations or exceptions to your claim and show that you are aware of the complexity or uncertainty of the issue.
  - Use ethos, pathos, and logos: Appeal to the ethics, emotions, and logic of your audience to persuade them and establish your authority, credibility, and rapport.
