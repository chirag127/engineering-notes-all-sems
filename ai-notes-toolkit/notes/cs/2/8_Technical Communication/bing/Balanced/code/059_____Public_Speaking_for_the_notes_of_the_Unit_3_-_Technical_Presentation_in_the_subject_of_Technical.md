# Public Speaking for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

Public speaking is the skill of communicating verbally with an audience, usually with the purpose of informing, persuading, or entertaining them. Technical presentation is a type of public speaking that involves explaining complex or specialized topics to a non-expert audience, using appropriate language, visuals, and examples.

The following are some tips for delivering a successful technical presentation:

- Know your subject matter. You should be confident and knowledgeable about the topic you are presenting, and be able to answer questions from the audience. You should also be able to explain the main idea, the purpose, and the benefits of your topic in a clear and concise way.
- Know your audience. You should tailor your presentation to the level of understanding, interest, and expectations of your audience. You should avoid using jargon, acronyms, or technical terms that your audience may not be familiar with, or provide definitions or explanations when necessary. You should also use examples, analogies, or stories that relate to your audience's background, needs, or goals.
- Configure your IDE (Integrated Development Environment). If you are using a software tool or a code editor to demonstrate your topic, you should make sure that it is set up properly and works smoothly. You should also adjust the font size, color, and contrast of your screen to make it easy for the audience to see and follow your steps. You should also practice using your IDE before the presentation and have a backup plan in case of technical issues.
- Minimize distractions. You should avoid any unnecessary or irrelevant information, graphics, or animations that may distract the audience from your main message. You should also limit the number of slides, bullet points, and words on each slide, and use visuals, charts, or diagrams to illustrate your points instead of text. You should also speak clearly, loudly, and confidently, and maintain eye contact with the audience.
- Get the right equipment. You should check the availability and compatibility of the equipment you need for your presentation, such as a laptop, a projector, a microphone, a pointer, or a remote control. You should also test the equipment before the presentation and have a backup plan in case of technical issues.
- Rehearse in advance. You should practice your presentation several times, preferably in front of a mirror, a friend, or a colleague, and get feedback on your content, delivery, and timing. You should also time yourself and adjust your presentation to fit the allotted time. You should also anticipate possible questions from the audience and prepare answers for them.
- Finally, enjoy the experience. You should try to relax and have fun during your presentation, and show your enthusiasm and passion for your topic. You should also smile, use gestures, and vary your tone and pace to keep the audience engaged and interested. You should also thank the audience for their attention and invite them to ask questions or share their comments.
