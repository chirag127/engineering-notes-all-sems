### Interpersonal Communication

Interpersonal communication is the process by which people exchange information, feelings, and meaning through verbal and non-verbal messages. It usually involves verbal, face-to-face communication with two or more people, and relies on tone, gestures, facial expressions, and body language to convey the intended message. Interpersonal communication can also take place through other channels, such as phone, email, text, or social media, but these may have limitations in conveying non-verbal cues.

Some of the main characteristics of interpersonal communication are:

- It is dynamic, meaning it changes over time and according to the context and the relationship between the communicators.
- It is transactional, meaning it involves mutual influence and exchange of feedback between the communicators.
- It is contextual, meaning it depends on the physical, social, psychological, and cultural factors that shape the communication situation.
- It is relational, meaning it affects and is affected by the nature and quality of the relationship between the communicators.

Some of the main types of interpersonal communication are:

- Dyadic communication, which is communication between two people, such as a conversation, an interview, or a counseling session.
- Small group communication, which is communication among three or more people who share a common goal, such as a team, a committee, or a family.
- Public communication, which is communication from one person to a large audience, such as a speech, a lecture, or a presentation.

Some of the main purposes of interpersonal communication are:

- To establish and maintain relationships, such as making friends, expressing emotions, showing support, or resolving conflicts.
- To exchange information, such as sharing facts, opinions, ideas, or experiences.
- To influence others, such as persuading, motivating, instructing, or negotiating.
- To entertain, such as telling jokes, stories, or anecdotes.

Some of the main skills of interpersonal communication are:

- Listening, which is the ability to pay attention, understand, and respond to what others are saying.
- Verbal communication, which is the ability to use words, tone, and grammar to convey clear and appropriate messages.
- Non-verbal communication, which is the ability to use gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and body language to enhance or complement verbal messages.
- Emotional intelligence, which is the ability to recognize, manage, and express one's own and others' emotions in interpersonal situations.
- Interpersonal ethics, which is the ability to communicate with respect, honesty, and responsibility, and to avoid harming or offending others.
