### Modes of Presentation

- Modes of presentation are the ways of delivering information to an audience in a technical communication context.
- There are three main modes of presentation: oral, written, and visual.
- Each mode has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the purpose, audience, and context of the communication.
- Oral presentation involves speaking to an audience, either in person or through a mediated channel, such as a video conference or a podcast.
  - Oral presentation is suitable for conveying complex or abstract ideas, engaging the audience's attention and emotions, and getting immediate feedback.
  - Oral presentation requires careful planning, preparation, and practice, as well as effective delivery skills, such as voice, body language, and eye contact.
  - Oral presentation also has some limitations, such as the risk of forgetting or mispronouncing words, the difficulty of maintaining audience interest, and the lack of a permanent record of the information.
- Written presentation involves producing a text document, such as a report, a proposal, or a manual, that can be read by the audience, either in print or online.
  - Written presentation is suitable for providing detailed, accurate, and structured information, that can be accessed and reviewed at any time and place by the audience.
  - Written presentation requires clear, concise, and coherent writing skills, as well as appropriate formatting, style, and citation of sources.
  - Written presentation also has some limitations, such as the possibility of misunderstanding or misinterpretation by the audience, the lack of interaction and feedback, and the difficulty of updating or revising the information.
- Visual presentation involves creating and displaying images, such as graphs, charts, diagrams, or photographs, that can complement or substitute the oral or written information.
  - Visual presentation is suitable for illustrating or emphasizing key points, simplifying or clarifying complex or numerical data, and attracting or retaining the audience's attention.
  - Visual presentation requires design, layout, and color skills, as well as appropriate selection, integration, and interpretation of images.
  - Visual presentation also has some limitations, such as the risk of oversimplifying or distorting the information, the difficulty of conveying abstract or emotional concepts, and the dependence on the quality and availability of the equipment.
