### Etiquette for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Etiquette is the set of rules and norms that govern the appropriate and polite behavior in oral communication.
- Etiquette can vary depending on the context, culture, audience, purpose, and mode of oral communication.
- Some general principles of etiquette for oral communication are:

  - Be clear, concise, and coherent in your message. Avoid ambiguity, jargon, and filler words.
  - Be respectful, courteous, and considerate of your listeners. Avoid interrupting, dominating, or criticizing them.
  - Be attentive, responsive, and engaged in the communication. Show interest, ask questions, and provide feedback.
  - Be honest, sincere, and trustworthy in your communication. Avoid lying, exaggerating, or misleading your listeners.
  - Be confident, assertive, and professional in your communication. Avoid being timid, aggressive, or informal.
  - Be aware of your nonverbal cues, such as eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture, and tone of voice. Make sure they match your verbal message and the situation.
  - Be adaptable, flexible, and open-minded in your communication. Adjust your style, language, and content according to the needs and expectations of your listeners and the communication goal.

- Voice dynamics are the aspects of oral communication that relate to the quality, pitch, volume, rate, and modulation of the voice.
- Voice dynamics can affect the effectiveness, clarity, and impact of oral communication.
- Some general principles of voice dynamics for oral communication are:

  - Use a clear and audible voice that can be easily heard and understood by your listeners. Avoid mumbling, whispering, or shouting.
  - Use a pitch that is appropriate for your message, audience, and situation. Avoid monotony, high-pitched, or low-pitched voice.
  - Use a volume that is suitable for the size and acoustics of the room, the distance from your listeners, and the background noise. Avoid being too loud or too soft.
  - Use a rate that is neither too fast nor too slow for your message, audience, and situation. Avoid speaking too quickly, too slowly, or with too many pauses.
  - Use a modulation that is varied and expressive to convey your emotions, attitudes, and emphasis. Avoid being flat, dull, or boring.
