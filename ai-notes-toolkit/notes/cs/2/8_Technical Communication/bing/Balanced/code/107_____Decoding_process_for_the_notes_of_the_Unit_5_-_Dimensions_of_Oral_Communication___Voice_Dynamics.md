### Decoding process for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Decoding is the process of translating the message from its sign format into meaning.
- Decoding is the reverse of encoding, where the sender uses symbols to translate ideas or concepts into a coded message ready for communication.
- Decoding is performed by the person receiving a message.
- In oral communication, decoding includes listening and understanding.
- Decoding is often difficult to achieve because factors such as education, opinions, and emotional states shape how a receiver interprets a message.
- Factors that influence the decoding process include communication channel proficiency, shared mental models, communication noise, and discrepancies in topic knowledge or experience.
- Communication channel proficiency refers to the ability of the receiver to use the medium of communication effectively and efficiently.
- Shared mental models refer to the common understanding and expectations of the sender and the receiver about the message and its context.
- Communication noise refers to any interference or distortion that affects the quality and clarity of the message.
- Discrepancies in topic knowledge or experience refer to the differences in the level of familiarity and expertise of the sender and the receiver on the subject matter of the message.
- Decoding is essential for effective oral communication as it allows the receiver to make sense of the message and respond appropriately.
- Decoding also helps the receiver to provide feedback to the sender, which is crucial for maintaining the communication loop.
- Decoding can be improved by developing active listening skills, asking clarifying questions, avoiding assumptions and biases, and seeking confirmation of understanding.
