# Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

## Code and Content

- Code is the system of symbols and rules used to convey a message
- Content is the meaning or information conveyed by the message
- Code and content are interrelated and interdependent
- Code can be verbal (words, phrases, sentences) or non-verbal (gestures, facial expressions, body language, etc.)
- Content can be factual (based on facts, data, evidence) or emotional (based on feelings, opinions, attitudes, etc.)
- Code and content should be appropriate, clear, concise, coherent, and consistent for effective oral communication

## Stimulus and Response

- Stimulus is the factor that initiates or motivates oral communication
- Response is the reaction or feedback to the oral communication
- Stimulus and response are influenced by various factors, such as:

  - Purpose: The goal or objective of oral communication
  - Audience: The receiver or listener of oral communication
  - Context: The situation or environment of oral communication
  - Channel: The medium or mode of oral communication
  - Culture: The values, beliefs, norms, and customs of oral communication

- Stimulus and response should be relevant, appropriate, and respectful for effective oral communication

## Encoding and Decoding

- Encoding is the process of creating or producing oral messages
- Decoding is the process of interpreting or understanding oral messages
- Encoding and decoding involve the following steps:

  - Selecting: Choosing the code and content of oral messages
  - Organizing: Arranging the code and content of oral messages in a logical and coherent manner
  - Transmitting: Sending or delivering oral messages through a channel
  - Receiving: Listening or attending to oral messages through a channel
  - Interpreting: Assigning meaning or significance to oral messages
  - Evaluating: Assessing the validity or effectiveness of oral messages
  - Responding: Giving feedback or reaction to oral messages

- Encoding and decoding should be accurate, complete, and consistent for effective oral communication

## Pronunciation, Syllables, Vowel Sounds, and Consonant Sounds

- Pronunciation is the correct and standard way of articulating speech sounds
- Syllables are the units of sound that make up words
- Vowel sounds are the speech sounds produced by the vibration of the vocal cords and the free passage of air through the mouth
- Consonant sounds are the speech sounds produced by the partial or complete obstruction of air flow by the tongue, teeth, lips, or palate
- Pronunciation, syllables, vowel sounds, and consonant sounds are important for oral communication because they affect the clarity, intelligibility, and comprehensibility of speech
- Some tips for improving pronunciation, syllables, vowel sounds, and consonant sounds are:

  - Listen to native speakers and imitate their speech sounds
  - Use a dictionary or an online tool to check the pronunciation, syllables, and sounds of words
  - Practice speaking aloud and recording your voice
  - Identify and correct your common errors or mistakes
  - Seek feedback from others and learn from their corrections or suggestions
