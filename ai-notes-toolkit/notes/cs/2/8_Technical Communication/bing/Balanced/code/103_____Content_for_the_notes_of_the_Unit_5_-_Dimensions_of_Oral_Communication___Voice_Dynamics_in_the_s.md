## Dimensions of Oral Communication

- Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth.
- Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose.
- Oral communication can be classified into two dimensions: verbal and nonverbal.

### Verbal Dimension

- Verbal dimension refers to the use of language and the choice of words to convey a spoken message.
- Verbal dimension involves the following aspects:

  - Clarity: The message should be clear, concise, and coherent, avoiding ambiguity, jargon, and slang.
  - Accuracy: The message should be factual, precise, and correct, avoiding errors, exaggerations, and distortions.
  - Appropriateness: The message should be suitable, relevant, and respectful, considering the audience, the occasion, and the objective.
  - Fluency: The message should be delivered smoothly, confidently, and naturally, avoiding hesitations, repetitions, and fillers.

### Nonverbal Dimension

- Nonverbal dimension refers to the use of body language and vocal cues to enhance or modify a spoken message.
- Nonverbal dimension involves the following aspects:

  - Eye contact: The speaker should maintain eye contact with the audience, showing interest, attention, and confidence.
  - Facial expression: The speaker should use facial expressions that match the tone and emotion of the message, showing sincerity, enthusiasm, and empathy.
  - Gesture: The speaker should use gestures that complement and reinforce the message, showing emphasis, direction, and illustration.
  - Posture: The speaker should adopt a posture that reflects the attitude and intention of the message, showing openness, readiness, and professionalism.
  - Voice: The speaker should use voice qualities that suit the message and the audience, such as volume, pitch, rate, and tone.

## Voice Dynamics

- Voice dynamics refers to the modulation and variation of voice qualities to create interest, impact, and influence in oral communication.
- Voice dynamics can be used to:

  - Attract and sustain the attention of the audience, avoiding monotony and boredom.
  - Express and convey the meaning and emotion of the message, avoiding confusion and misunderstanding.
  - Persuade and motivate the audience to take action or change their attitude, avoiding resistance and indifference.

- Voice dynamics involves the following techniques:

  - Stress: The speaker should emphasize certain words or syllables to show importance, contrast, or surprise.
  - Pause: The speaker should insert brief silences between words or sentences to create suspense, transition, or reflection.
  - Intonation: The speaker should vary the pitch of the voice to show question, statement, or exclamation.
  - Inflection: The speaker should change the pitch of the voice within a word or sentence to show emotion, attitude, or emphasis.
  - Volume: The speaker should adjust the loudness of the voice to show confidence, authority, or excitement.
  - Rate: The speaker should alter the speed of the voice to show urgency, calmness, or complexity.
  - Tone: The speaker should modify the quality of the voice to show sincerity, friendliness, or sarcasm.
