## Unit 3 - Technical Presentation

A technical presentation is a type of oral communication that aims to inform, persuade, or instruct an audience on a specific topic, using clear and concise language, visual aids, and appropriate delivery techniques. Technical presentations are common in academic, professional, and scientific settings, where the presenter needs to convey complex or specialized information to a diverse or specialized audience.

Some of the objectives of a technical presentation are:

- To explain the background, purpose, methods, results, and implications of a research project, experiment, or innovation.
- To demonstrate the features, benefits, and applications of a product, service, or technology.
- To instruct the audience on how to perform a task, use a tool, or follow a procedure.
- To persuade the audience to adopt a certain viewpoint, opinion, or course of action.

Some of the characteristics of a technical presentation are:

- It is based on facts, data, and evidence, not opinions or emotions.
- It uses precise and accurate terminology, definitions, and units of measurement, avoiding ambiguity or confusion.
- It follows a clear and logical structure, with an introduction, a body, and a conclusion, and uses transitions and signposts to guide the audience.
- It supports the main points with visual aids, such as graphs, charts, diagrams, tables, images, or videos, that are relevant, clear, and easy to read and understand.
- It adapts to the audience's level of knowledge, interest, and expectations, and uses appropriate tone, style, and language.
- It engages the audience's attention, interest, and curiosity, and encourages interaction and feedback.
- It delivers the message with confidence, clarity, and enthusiasm, using effective verbal and nonverbal communication skills, such as voice, eye contact, gestures, and posture.
