# Rising tone

Rising tone is a type of intonation pattern in which the pitch of the voice rises over time. It is mainly used for:

- Questions with a yes/no answer, such as "Are you thirsty?" 
- Requests for clarification, which indicate uncertainty, such as "So, is that the new doctor?" 
- High energy emotions, such as happiness, anger, or shock, such as "Wow!"

Rising tone can also convey other meanings, such as:

- Challenging or impatient, when asking a question to which you expect an answer, such as "What do you mean?"
- Suspicious or annoyed, when asking a question to which you already know the answer, such as "Did you eat my cake?"
- Polite or tentative, when making a request or a suggestion, such as "Could you please pass me the salt?"

Rising tone can be marked with a diagonal line (/) at the end of the sentence, such as:

- You like chocolate, don't you? /
- How are you today? /
- That's amazing! /

Rising tone is different from falling tone, which is a type of intonation pattern in which the pitch of the voice falls over time. Falling tone is mainly used for:

- Statements, such as "I like chocolate."
- Commands, such as "Pass me the salt."
- Wh-questions, such as "What do you mean?"
- Low energy emotions, such as sadness, boredom, or relief, such as "Oh."

Falling tone can be marked with a diagonal line (\) at the end of the sentence, such as:

- I like chocolate. \
- Pass me the salt. \
- What do you mean? \

Rising tone and falling tone are important aspects of oral communication and voice dynamics, as they can affect the meaning, tone, and mood of the message. They can also help the speaker to engage the listener, express emotions, and create interest. Therefore, it is essential to use the appropriate intonation pattern according to the context and purpose of the communication.
