# Thesis/Project Writing for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the Subject of Technical Communication

- A thesis or a project is a form of technical communication that presents the results of a research or a design process in a clear, concise, and coherent manner.
- A thesis or a project typically consists of the following elements :
  - Introduction: This section introduces the purpose, scope, and significance of the chosen topic; situates the topic within the context of the available literature in the field of technical communication; explains the research method employed and the rationale for the research; and states the main research question or problem.
  - Literature Review: This section reviews the relevant and current literature in the field of technical communication that relates to the topic; identifies the gaps, controversies, or challenges in the existing knowledge; and establishes the theoretical or conceptual framework for the research.
  - Methodology: This section describes and justifies the research method or design used to collect and analyze the data; explains the procedures, instruments, participants, and ethical considerations involved in the research; and discusses the limitations and validity of the method or design.
  - Results: This section reports and summarizes the findings or outcomes of the data analysis; presents the data in tables, graphs, charts, or other visual forms; and highlights the key patterns, trends, or relationships in the data.
  - Discussion: This section interprets and evaluates the results in relation to the research question or problem; explains the implications, significance, or contributions of the results; compares and contrasts the results with the existing literature; and identifies the limitations, challenges, or recommendations for future research.
  - Conclusion: This section summarizes the main points and arguments of the thesis or project; restates the purpose, scope, and significance of the research; and provides a clear and concise answer to the research question or problem.
  - References: This section lists the sources cited or consulted in the thesis or project in a consistent and appropriate citation style.
  - Appendices: This section includes any additional or supplementary materials that support the thesis or project, such as data sets, questionnaires, interview transcripts, code, etc.

- A thesis or a project is a highly designed form of communication that requires practitioners to have a heightened awareness of the conventions and rhetorical situations in which they are communicating.
  - Conventions: These are the rules and expectations that govern the format, structure, style, tone, language, and citation of a thesis or project. Conventions may vary depending on the discipline, field, institution, or audience of the research. Practitioners should follow the guidelines and instructions provided by their supervisors, committees, or departments when writing a thesis or project.
  - Rhetorical situations: These are the factors that influence the purpose, audience, context, and message of a thesis or project. Rhetorical situations may change depending on the stage, scope, and mode of the research. Practitioners should consider the following aspects of the rhetorical situation when writing a thesis or project:
    - Purpose: This is the goal or objective of the research and the communication. The purpose of a thesis or project is to answer a research question or problem, to contribute to the existing knowledge or practice in the field of technical communication, and to demonstrate the skills and competencies of the researcher or designer.
    - Audience: This is the intended or potential readers or users of the thesis or project. The audience of a thesis or project may include the supervisor, the committee, the peers, the experts, the practitioners, the clients, the stakeholders, or the general public. Practitioners should consider the needs, expectations, background, and interests of their audience when writing a thesis or project.
    - Context: This is the situation or environment in which the thesis or project is produced and consumed. The context of a thesis or project may include the discipline, field, institution, culture, time, place, or medium of the research and the communication. Practitioners should consider the constraints, opportunities, norms, and values of their context when writing a thesis or project.
    - Message: This is the content or information that is communicated in the thesis or project. The message of a thesis or project should be clear, concise, coherent, accurate, relevant, and ethical.
