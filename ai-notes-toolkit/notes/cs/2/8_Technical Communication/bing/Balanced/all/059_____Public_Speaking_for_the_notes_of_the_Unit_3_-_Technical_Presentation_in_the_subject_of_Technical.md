# Public Speaking for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

Public speaking is the skill of communicating effectively with an audience, whether it is in person or online. Public speaking can be challenging, especially when the topic is technical and complex. However, with proper preparation and practice, anyone can deliver a successful technical presentation. Here are some tips to help you improve your public speaking skills for technical presentations:

- **Know your subject matter.** A great presentation is not about reading a bunch of slides - your attendees are capable of reading much faster on their own. You should be able to explain your topic in your own words, with confidence and clarity. You should also anticipate possible questions and objections from your audience, and prepare your answers in advance. If you are not sure about something, do not pretend to know it - admit your limitations and refer to other sources or experts if needed.

- **Know your audience.** Knowing your audience is crucial for any presentation, but it is even more important for a technical one. You should tailor your content, language, and tone to suit the level of knowledge, interest, and expectations of your audience. You should also consider the purpose and context of your presentation - are you informing, persuading, or teaching your audience? What are their needs and goals? How can you make your presentation relevant and valuable to them ?

- **Plan your presentation.** A well-structured presentation has a clear introduction, body, and conclusion. The introduction should capture the attention of your audience, establish your credibility, and state your main idea or thesis. The body should support your main idea with evidence, examples, and arguments. The conclusion should summarize your main points, restate your main idea, and end with a call to action or a memorable message. You should also use transitions to connect your ideas and guide your audience through your presentation .

- **Prepare engaging visuals.** Visual aids, such as slides, videos, and graphics, can enhance your presentation by making it more attractive, understandable, and memorable. However, you should not rely on your visuals to do the talking for you - they should complement, not replace, your verbal communication. You should use visuals that are relevant, simple, and clear, and avoid cluttering them with too much text, data, or colors. You should also practice using your visuals effectively, such as pointing to them, explaining them, and switching between them smoothly .

- **Rehearse in advance.** Practice makes perfect, especially when it comes to public speaking. You should rehearse your presentation several times before the actual event, preferably in front of a mirror, a recorder, or a friend. You should also time yourself and adjust your content and pace accordingly. Rehearsing will help you improve your delivery, confidence, and fluency, and also help you identify and correct any errors or weaknesses in your presentation .

- **Get the right equipment.** If you are giving a technical presentation online, you should make sure that you have the right equipment and software to deliver it smoothly and professionally. You should check your internet connection, microphone, camera, and speakers, and test them before the presentation. You should also familiarize yourself with the platform or tool you are using, such as Zoom, Skype, or PowerPoint, and learn how to use its features, such as screen sharing, chat, and polls. You should also have a backup plan in case of any technical issues or interruptions.

- **Enjoy the experience.** Public speaking can be stressful, but it can also be rewarding and fun. You should try to relax and enjoy the opportunity to share your knowledge and passion with your audience. You should also be positive and enthusiastic about your topic, and show your personality and humor. You should also interact with your audience, such as asking them questions, inviting them to comment, or acknowledging their feedback. By engaging your audience, you will make your presentation more lively and memorable, and also build rapport and trust with them .
