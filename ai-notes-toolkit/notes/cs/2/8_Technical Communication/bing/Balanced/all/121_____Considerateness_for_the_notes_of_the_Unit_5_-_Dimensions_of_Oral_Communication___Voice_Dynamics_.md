# Considerateness for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Considerateness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, completeness, correctness, concreteness, and courtesy .
- Considerateness means showing respect and empathy for the audience, their needs, interests, and feelings  .
- Considerateness also means using a positive and polite tone of voice, avoiding negative or offensive words, and emphasizing on what is possible rather than what is impossible .
- Considerateness is important for oral communication because it helps to build rapport, trust, and goodwill with the listeners, and to avoid misunderstandings, conflicts, and resentment .
- Considerateness can be achieved by using the following strategies in oral communication    :
  - Emphasize on "you" approach, which means focusing on the benefits and interests of the audience, rather than on the speaker's own agenda or ego  .
  - Empathize with the audience and exhibit interest in them, by using active listening skills, asking relevant questions, acknowledging their feedback, and expressing appreciation  .
  - Show optimism and enthusiasm towards the audience and the topic, by using positive words, gestures, and expressions, and by highlighting the opportunities and solutions rather than the problems and challenges  .
  - Use a listenable message, which means a message that is easy to understand and remember, by using simple and familiar words, short and clear sentences, and appropriate transitions and signposts.
  - Adapt the message to the audience's level of knowledge, expectations, and preferences, by using appropriate examples, analogies, and evidence, and by avoiding jargon, technical terms, or acronyms that may confuse or alienate the listeners.
  - Respect the audience's time and attention span, by being concise and relevant, and by avoiding unnecessary repetitions, digressions, or interruptions .
  - Be courteous and respectful, by using polite forms of address, titles, and pronouns, by avoiding sarcasm, criticism, or insults, and by apologizing or thanking when appropriate .
