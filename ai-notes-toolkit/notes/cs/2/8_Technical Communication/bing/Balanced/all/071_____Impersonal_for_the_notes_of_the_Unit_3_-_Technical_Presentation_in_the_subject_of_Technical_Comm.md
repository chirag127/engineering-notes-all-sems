# Impersonal Technical Communication

- Impersonal technical communication is a style of writing or speaking that avoids using personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, we, etc.) and focuses on conveying data and important information.
- Impersonal technical communication is often used in engineering, science, and business contexts, where the goal is to present facts, results, or instructions in a clear, objective, and concise manner.
- Impersonal technical communication can be achieved by using the passive voice, the third person, or the indefinite pronoun "one". For example:
  - Passive voice: "The wire was cut" instead of "I cut the wire".
  - Third person: "The researcher conducted the experiment" instead of "I conducted the experiment".
  - Indefinite pronoun: "One should follow the safety guidelines" instead of "You should follow the safety guidelines".
- Impersonal technical communication has some advantages and disadvantages. Some of the advantages are:
  - It can create a sense of professionalism, authority, and credibility.
  - It can avoid bias, subjectivity, and personal opinions.
  - It can reduce ambiguity and confusion by focusing on the facts and the actions.
  - It can appeal to a wider and more diverse audience by avoiding assumptions and stereotypes.
- Some of the disadvantages are:
  - It can create a sense of detachment, impersonality, and coldness.
  - It can obscure the human element and the context of the communication.
  - It can make the communication less engaging, interactive, and persuasive.
  - It can limit the expression of emotions, feelings, and attitudes.
