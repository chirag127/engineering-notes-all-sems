# Conciseness

- Conciseness is the quality of using only the words that are necessary to convey a message, without redundancy or repetition.
- Conciseness is important for effective technical communication, as it helps the reader to understand the information quickly and clearly, and avoids wasting their time and attention.
- Conciseness can be achieved by following some general principles, such as:

  - Using specific and precise words, rather than vague or general ones.
  - Avoiding unnecessary modifiers, such as adverbs, adjectives, or intensifiers, that do not add meaning or clarity to the sentence.
  - Eliminating wordy phrases, such as "due to the fact that", "in order to", or "in the event that", and replacing them with simpler words, such as "because", "to", or "if".
  - Deleting redundant words or phrases, such as "each and every", "first and foremost", or "free gift", that repeat the same idea or meaning.
  - Using active voice, rather than passive voice, whenever possible, as it makes the sentence more direct and concise.
  - Using parallel structure, which means using the same grammatical form for items in a list or series, as it makes the sentence more coherent and easy to follow.
  - Using lists, tables, or diagrams, when appropriate, to present information in a concise and organized way, rather than using long paragraphs or sentences.

- Conciseness does not mean sacrificing completeness or accuracy of the information, but rather choosing the most effective way to communicate it. Conciseness also does not mean using jargon, abbreviations, or acronyms, unless they are well-defined and understood by the audience. Conciseness should be balanced with other qualities of technical communication, such as clarity, correctness, and coherence.
