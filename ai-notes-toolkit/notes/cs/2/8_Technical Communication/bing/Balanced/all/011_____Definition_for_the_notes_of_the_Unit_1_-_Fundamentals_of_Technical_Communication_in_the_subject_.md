# Definition

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as manuals, reports, proposals, memos, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, etc.
- Technical communication can be written, spoken, visual, or multimodal, depending on the needs and preferences of the audience and the purpose of the communication.
- Technical communication is different from other types of communication because it requires:
  - Accuracy: The information and instructions must be correct, precise, and verifiable.
  - Clarity: The information and instructions must be easy to understand, follow, and use.
  - Conciseness: The information and instructions must be concise, avoiding unnecessary details or repetitions.
  - Coherence: The information and instructions must be logically organized and connected, using appropriate transitions and signposts.
  - Completeness: The information and instructions must cover all the relevant aspects and answer all the possible questions of the audience.
  - Consistency: The information and instructions must use consistent terminology, format, style, and tone throughout the document or presentation.
  - Courtesy: The information and instructions must respect the audience's needs, expectations, and feelings, using polite and positive language.
  - Ethics: The information and instructions must be honest, fair, and respectful of the rights and responsibilities of the communicator and the audience.
  - Relevance: The information and instructions must be relevant to the audience and the purpose of the communication, avoiding irrelevant or distracting information.
  - Usability: The information and instructions must be user-friendly, accessible, and adaptable to the audience's needs and preferences.
