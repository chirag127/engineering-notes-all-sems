# Unit 3 - Technical Presentation

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience, such as engineers, scientists, managers, or customers.
- The purpose of a technical presentation is to inform, persuade, or instruct the audience about a technical topic, such as a new product, a research project, a problem solution, or a design proposal.
- A technical presentation consists of three main parts: introduction, body, and conclusion. Each part has a specific function and structure that helps the presenter achieve the presentation objectives and engage the audience.
- The introduction of a technical presentation should:
  - Capture the audience's attention with a hook, such as a question, a story, a statistic, or a quote.
  - Establish the presenter's credibility and rapport with the audience by stating the presenter's name, affiliation, and qualifications.
  - Provide the background and context of the presentation topic by explaining why it is important, relevant, or interesting for the audience.
  - State the main purpose and objectives of the presentation by summarizing what the presenter will talk about and what the audience will learn or gain from it.
  - Preview the main points and the organization of the presentation by outlining the main topics and subtopics that will be covered in the body.
- The body of a technical presentation should:
  - Develop the main points and subpoints of the presentation in a logical and coherent order, such as chronological, spatial, topical, or problem-solution.
  - Support the main points and subpoints with evidence, such as facts, data, examples, diagrams, graphs, tables, or images.
  - Explain the evidence clearly and concisely by using appropriate language, terminology, definitions, and explanations.
  - Highlight the key points and transitions by using verbal and nonverbal cues, such as signposts, summaries, pauses, gestures, or eye contact.
  - Anticipate and address the potential questions, objections, or concerns of the audience by providing counterarguments, concessions, or refutations.
- The conclusion of a technical presentation should:
  - Signal the end of the presentation by using a closing statement, such as "In conclusion", "To summarize", or "Thank you for your attention".
  - Review the main points and objectives of the presentation by restating the main purpose and summarizing the main topics and subtopics covered in the body.
  - Emphasize the main message and implications of the presentation by highlighting the main benefits, outcomes, or recommendations of the presentation topic for the audience.
  - Provide a call to action or a follow-up by inviting the audience to take action, ask questions, give feedback, or contact the presenter for more information.
  - End the presentation with a memorable closing remark, such as a quote, a question, a challenge, or a recommendation.
