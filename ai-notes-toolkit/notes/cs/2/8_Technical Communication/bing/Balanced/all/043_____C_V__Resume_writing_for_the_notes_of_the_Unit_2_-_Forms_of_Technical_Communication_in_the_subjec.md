# C.V./Resume writing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- A C.V. (curriculum vitae) or a resume is a document that summarizes one's education, work experience, skills, achievements, and other relevant information for a potential employer or a recruiter.
- A C.V. or a resume is usually the first impression that one makes on a prospective employer or a recruiter, and therefore it is important to make it clear, concise, and professional.
- A C.V. or a resume can have different formats and styles depending on the purpose, the field, the country, and the personal preference of the writer. However, some common elements that are usually included in a C.V. or a resume are:

  - Personal details: name, contact information, address, etc.
  - Objective or summary: a brief statement of one's career goals or qualifications
  - Education: a list of one's academic degrees, certificates, diplomas, etc. with the name of the institution, the year of completion, and the grade or percentage obtained
  - Work experience: a list of one's previous or current jobs, internships, projects, etc. with the name of the organization, the duration, the position, and the responsibilities or achievements
  - Skills: a list of one's relevant skills, such as languages, computer programs, soft skills, etc. with the level of proficiency or expertise
  - Achievements: a list of one's awards, honors, publications, presentations, etc. that demonstrate one's accomplishments or recognition in the field
  - References: a list of one or more people who can vouch for one's work performance, character, or abilities, with their name, designation, contact information, and relationship to the writer

- Some tips for writing an effective C.V. or a resume are:

  - Tailor it to the specific job or organization that one is applying for, by highlighting the most relevant information and using keywords that match the job description or the industry
  - Use clear and concise language, avoiding jargon, slang, or unnecessary words
  - Use bullet points, headings, subheadings, and white space to organize the information and make it easy to read and scan
  - Use consistent formatting, font, and style throughout the document, and check for spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors
  - Use action verbs, numbers, and quantifiable results to describe one's work experience and achievements, and avoid using personal pronouns, such as I, me, or my
  - Keep it short and relevant, usually one or two pages, depending on the level of experience and the type of job
  - Update it regularly and keep a copy of it handy for future applications or opportunities
