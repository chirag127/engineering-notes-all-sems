# Essentials for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience, such as engineers, scientists, managers, or customers.
- A technical presentation has three main objectives: to inform, to persuade, or to instruct.
- A technical presentation should be well-organized, clear, concise, accurate, and engaging.
- A technical presentation should follow a basic structure that consists of an introduction, a body, and a conclusion.
- The introduction should capture the attention of the audience, establish the purpose and scope of the presentation, and preview the main points.
- The body should develop the main points with supporting evidence, such as facts, figures, examples, or visuals.
- The conclusion should summarize the main points, restate the purpose and significance of the presentation, and provide a call to action or a recommendation.
- A technical presentation should use appropriate language, tone, and style for the audience, the topic, and the occasion.
- A technical presentation should use effective verbal and nonverbal communication skills, such as voice, eye contact, gestures, posture, and facial expressions.
- A technical presentation should use appropriate visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, diagrams, or videos, to enhance the understanding and retention of the information.
- A technical presentation should be rehearsed and revised before the actual delivery, and should be evaluated and improved after the feedback from the audience.
