# Technical Report for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical report is a document that presents and analyzes data, findings, or results of a research, experiment, or project in a clear and objective manner.
- A technical report is intended for a specific audience, such as engineers, scientists, managers, or decision-makers, who need to understand the problem, methods, results, and implications of the work.
- A technical report usually follows a standard format and structure, which may vary depending on the purpose, scope, and context of the report.
- A technical report typically consists of the following sections:

  - Title page: It contains the title of the report, the name and affiliation of the author(s), the date of submission, and other relevant information.
  - Abstract: It is a concise summary of the report, highlighting the main purpose, methods, results, and conclusions of the work.
  - Table of contents: It lists the main sections and sub-sections of the report, along with their page numbers.
  - List of figures and tables: It enumerates the figures and tables used in the report, along with their captions and page numbers.
  - Introduction: It introduces the topic, background, motivation, objectives, and scope of the report, and provides an overview of the report structure.
  - Literature review: It reviews the existing literature and research related to the topic, and identifies the knowledge gaps and research questions that the report aims to address.
  - Methods: It describes the methods, procedures, techniques, tools, and materials used to conduct the research, experiment, or project, and explains the rationale and assumptions behind them.
  - Results: It presents and analyzes the data, findings, or outcomes of the research, experiment, or project, using tables, graphs, charts, diagrams, or other visual aids.
  - Discussion: It interprets and evaluates the results, and discusses their implications, limitations, and significance for the research problem, question, or hypothesis.
  - Conclusion: It summarizes the main points, results, and contributions of the report, and provides recommendations or suggestions for future work or applications.
  - References: It cites the sources of information, data, or ideas used in the report, following a consistent citation style, such as APA, MLA, IEEE, or Harvard.
  - Appendices: It includes any additional or supplementary information, data, or material that supports or complements the report, but is not essential for the main text, such as calculations, code, questionnaires, or raw data.
