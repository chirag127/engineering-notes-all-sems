# Speaking with a purpose

- Speaking with a purpose means having a clear and specific goal for your oral communication, such as informing, instructing, persuading, or entertaining your audience .
- Speaking with a purpose also means adapting your message to suit the needs, interests, and expectations of your audience, as well as the context and occasion of your speech .
- Speaking with a purpose requires you to plan, organize, and deliver your speech effectively, using appropriate language, tone, voice, and nonverbal cues .
- Speaking with a purpose can help you achieve various outcomes, such as:
  - Enhancing your credibility and professionalism as a technical communicator .
  - Building rapport and trust with your audience .
  - Increasing your audience's understanding and retention of your message .
  - Influencing your audience's attitudes, beliefs, or actions .
  - Engaging your audience's attention and interest .
- To speak with a purpose, you can use the following steps:
  - Identify your purpose: What do you want to achieve with your speech? What is the main idea or message you want to convey? 
  - Analyze your audience: Who are they? What do they know, need, or want to know about your topic? How will they react to your message? What are their expectations and preferences? 
  - Develop your content: What are the key points and supporting details you want to include in your speech? How will you organize them in a logical and coherent way? How will you use evidence, examples, or anecdotes to support your claims? 
  - Choose your delivery mode: How will you present your speech? Will you use notes, slides, or other visual aids? How will you use your voice, gestures, eye contact, and facial expressions to enhance your message?
