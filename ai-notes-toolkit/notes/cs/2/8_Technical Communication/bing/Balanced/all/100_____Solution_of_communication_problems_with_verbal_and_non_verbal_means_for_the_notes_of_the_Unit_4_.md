# Solution of communication problems with verbal and non verbal means

Communication problems can arise due to various factors, such as misunderstanding, misinterpretation, cultural differences, emotional barriers, or lack of feedback. To solve these problems, one needs to use both verbal and non verbal means of communication effectively. Here are some tips to improve communication skills using verbal and non verbal means:

- Verbal communication refers to the use of language to convey information. Verbal communication skills represent more than speaking abilities—they demonstrate how you deliver and receive messages in both speaking and written interactions. These skills focus on how you communicate rather than what you say.
  - To improve your verbal communication skills, you should:
    - Choose your words carefully and avoid jargon, slang, or ambiguous terms that may confuse or offend your audience.
    - Use clear and concise sentences and paragraphs that convey your main points and arguments.
    - Use appropriate tone, pitch, volume, and speed of speech to match the context and purpose of your communication.
    - Use active listening skills, such as paraphrasing, summarizing, asking questions, and giving feedback, to show your interest and understanding of the speaker's message.
    - Use positive and constructive language, such as praise, appreciation, encouragement, and suggestions, to build rapport and trust with your audience.
    - Adapt your communication style and content to suit the needs, preferences, and expectations of your audience.
- Non verbal communication refers to the use of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and other cues to convey information. Non verbal communication helps you effectively communicate and connect with others. When you understand the different types of non verbal cues, you can tailor your message to avoid miscommunication. Understanding the non verbal signals your team members are sending can help you identify if a coworker is uncomfortable or ill at ease.
  - To improve your non verbal communication skills, you should:
    - Use non verbal cues that complement your verbal message by adding to its meaning. For example, you can pat someone you offended at the back as you say sorry to him or her.
    - Use non verbal cues that regulate the flow of communication, such as nodding, raising your hand, or leaning forward, to indicate when you want to speak, listen, or interrupt.
    - Use non verbal cues that express your emotions, attitudes, and personality, such as smiling, frowning, or crossing your arms, to show how you feel about the topic or the person you are communicating with.
    - Use non verbal cues that reinforce your credibility, confidence, and professionalism, such as maintaining eye contact, using a firm handshake, or dressing appropriately, to create a positive impression on your audience.
    - Build your emotional intelligence skills, which are the ability to recognize, understand, and manage your own and others' emotions. This will help you to decode others’ nonverbal signals and respond appropriately.
    - Develop your cultural intelligence skills, which are the ability to adapt to different cultural contexts and norms. This will help you to avoid miscommunication and misunderstanding due to cultural differences in non verbal communication.
    - When in doubt, ask for clarification or feedback from your audience to ensure that your message is received and understood as intended.
