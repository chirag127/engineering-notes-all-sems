# Professional Personality Attributes

Professional personality attributes are the habits, attitudes and physical traits of a person that influence how they perform in their professional roles. They may vary from group to group and society to society, but some of them are universally valued and desired by employers and customers. Professional personality attributes can be developed and improved through self-awareness, learning and practice. Here are some of the most important professional personality attributes for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication:

- **Openness to experience**: This attribute refers to the depth and complexity of an individual's mental life and experiences. It involves being curious, creative, adventurous and willing to try new things. Openness to experience can help professionals to adapt to changing situations, generate innovative ideas and solutions, and appreciate different perspectives and cultures.
- **Conscientiousness**: This attribute refers to the tendency to control impulses and act in socially acceptable ways, behaviours that facilitate goal-directed behaviour. It involves being organized, responsible, diligent and self-disciplined. Conscientiousness can help professionals to plan and execute tasks efficiently, meet deadlines and quality standards, and follow rules and regulations.
- **Extroversion**: This attribute refers to the degree of sociability and assertiveness of an individual. It involves being outgoing, energetic, talkative and confident. Extroversion can help professionals to communicate effectively, build rapport and trust, and lead and motivate others.
- **Agreeableness**: This attribute refers to the degree of cooperativeness and compassion of an individual. It involves being friendly, polite, helpful and empathetic. Agreeableness can help professionals to work well in teams, resolve conflicts, and satisfy customers and clients.
- **Neuroticism**: This attribute refers to the degree of emotional stability and resilience of an individual. It involves being calm, composed, optimistic and self-assured. Neuroticism can help professionals to cope with stress, challenges and setbacks, and maintain a positive attitude and outlook.
- **Professional appearance**: This attribute refers to the way an individual dresses, grooms and presents themselves in a professional setting. It involves being neat, clean, appropriate and respectful. Professional appearance can help professionals to create a good first impression, convey credibility and competence, and show respect for themselves and others.
- **Reliability**: This attribute refers to the dependability and consistency of an individual. It involves doing what they say they will do and not making excuses. Reliability can help professionals to keep their commitments, deliver on their promises, and earn the trust and respect of others.
- **Ethical behaviour**: This attribute refers to the moral principles and values that guide an individual's actions and decisions. It involves being honest, fair, respectful and responsible. Ethical behaviour can help professionals to uphold their integrity, reputation and credibility, and avoid legal and ethical issues.
- **Communication skills**: This attribute refers to the ability to express and exchange information and ideas effectively and appropriately. It involves being clear, concise, coherent and courteous. Communication skills can help professionals to convey their messages, listen and understand others, and persuade and influence others.
- **Interpersonal skills**: This attribute refers to the ability to interact and relate with others in a positive and constructive way. It involves being respectful, empathetic, cooperative and supportive. Interpersonal skills can help professionals to build and maintain relationships, work in teams, and manage conflicts.
- **Problem-solving skills**: This attribute refers to the ability to identify, analyze and resolve issues and challenges. It involves being logical, creative, critical and resourceful. Problem-solving skills can help professionals to find effective and efficient solutions, overcome obstacles, and improve processes and outcomes.
- **Learning skills**: This attribute refers to the ability to acquire, retain and apply new knowledge and skills. It involves being curious, motivated, reflective and adaptable. Learning skills can help professionals to update and upgrade their competencies, cope with changes, and grow and develop.
