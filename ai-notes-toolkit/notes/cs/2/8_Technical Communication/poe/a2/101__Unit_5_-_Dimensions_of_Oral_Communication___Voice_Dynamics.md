## Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

1. Time: The time dimension of oral communication refers to how the pace or rate of speaking varies. For example:
- Speed: Talking fast vs slow. A fast pace can convey urgency while a slow pace can convey importance or careful consideration.
- Pauses: Including pauses or silence can add effect, such as creating suspense.

2. Language: The language dimension refers to the choices of words, level of formality, grammar used, etc. For example:
- Word choice: Simple vs complex vocabulary. Technical jargon vs layman's terms.
- Level of formality: Casual vs formal language. Contractions vs full words and proper grammar.
- Imagery: Use of descriptive words and metaphors to paint a vivid picture.

3. Volume: The volume dimension refers to the loudness or softness of speech. This can be used to emphasize important points or convey emotion. For example:
- Speaking loudly can convey excitement, importance or anger.
- Speaking softly can convey confidentiality or calmness.
- Whispering can convey secrecy or intimacy.

4. Pitch: The pitch dimension refers to the highness or lowness of voice. Like volume, pitch can be used to emphasize or convey emotion. For example:
- A higher pitch can convey energy or questioning.
- A lower pitch can convey seriousness or asserting authority.
- Varied pitch can keep an audience engaged and convey enthusiasm.
