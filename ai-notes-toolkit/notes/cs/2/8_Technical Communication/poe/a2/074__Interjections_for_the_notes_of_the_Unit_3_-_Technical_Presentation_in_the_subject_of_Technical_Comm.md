### Interjections for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication:

1. Start with a clear statement of the topic and goals of the presentation. For example, "In this presentation, I will discuss three ways to improve the performance of the ACME machine."

2. Use tone and body language appropriate for a technical presentation. Speak clearly and with confidence, use hand gestures appropriately to emphasize important points, and maintain eye contact with the audience.

3. Organize your content in a logical flow. For example, you might first explain the three methods to improve performance, then go into details on each method one by one. Use headings and transitions to indicate the structure to the audience.

4. Use visual aids like slides, props, or handouts to enhance your points. Visualizations of data or diagrams of processes are very helpful for technical presentations.

5. Keep your language concise and avoid unnecessary words. Explain technical terms when needed, but don't talk down to your audience or be too simplistic.

6. Highlight key terms and takeaways to help your audience follow along and remember the most important information. For example, you might say "To summarize, the three ways to improve ACME machine performance are X, Y, and Z."

7. Leave time for questions and be open to addressing concerns from your audience. Anticipate common questions and prepare answers ahead of time. If you don't know the answer to a question, offer to get back to the person with a response.

8. End strong by reemphasizing the key points and overall goals or conclusions. For example, "In conclusion, by implementing methods X, Y, and Z, we can increase ACME machine performance by 25%."
