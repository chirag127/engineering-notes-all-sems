### Interjections for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

Interjections are words or phrases used to express emotions or emphasize certain points during a technical presentation. Here are some common interjections that you can use to make your presentation more engaging and effective:

1. Wow - Use this interjection to express surprise or admiration for a particular aspect of your presentation. For example, "Wow, the results of this experiment are truly impressive!"

2. Oh - This is a versatile interjection that can convey a range of emotions, from surprise to disappointment. For instance, "Oh, I see what you mean now" or "Oh, that's too bad."

3. Ah - Use this interjection to indicate understanding or realization. For example, "Ah, I see how this algorithm works now."

4. Hmmm - This interjection conveys skepticism or uncertainty. For instance, "Hmmm, I'm not sure if this approach will work in practice."

5. Well - Use this interjection to introduce a new point or to transition to a new topic. For example, "Well, let's move on to the next section of the presentation."

6. Alright - This interjection is a way to signal agreement or approval. For instance, "Alright, I think we can move forward with this proposal."

7. Oops - This interjection is used to acknowledge a mistake or error. For example, "Oops, I forgot to mention an important detail earlier."

8. Yikes - Use this interjection to express alarm or concern. For instance, "Yikes, this bug could potentially cause a security breach!"

By using interjections appropriately, you can add personality and emotion to your technical presentation, making it more engaging and memorable for your audience. However, it's important to use interjections sparingly and in a way that is appropriate for the context and tone of your presentation.
