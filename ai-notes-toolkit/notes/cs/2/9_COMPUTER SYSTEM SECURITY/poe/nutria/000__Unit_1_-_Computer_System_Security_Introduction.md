
## Unit 1 - Computer System Security Introduction

1. Computer system security is a field of study that focuses on protecting data and information stored on computers from unauthorized access or manipulation. It involves the use of encryption, authentication, and other security measures to protect data from malicious actors.

2. Security threats can come from both inside and outside of an organization. Internal threats include employees or contractors who have access to sensitive information and may misuse it for personal gain. External threats include hackers, malware, and other malicious actors who may attempt to gain access to an organization's systems.

3. Security measures can include physical security, such as locks and access control systems, as well as software security, such as firewalls, antivirus programs, and encryption. Security measures should be tailored to the specific needs of an organization, and regularly updated to protect against new threats.

4. Security policies are important for ensuring that employees and contractors are aware of their responsibilities when it comes to protecting data. Policies should outline acceptable use of systems, as well as procedures for responding to security incidents.

5. Security audits are important for ensuring that security measures are effective and up-to-date. Audits can involve manual or automated testing of systems, as well as reviews of security policies and procedures.
