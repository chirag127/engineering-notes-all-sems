
### Access Control Concepts for the notes of the Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and leas in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

1. Access control is a security technique that regulates who has access to resources in a computing environment. It can be used to protect systems and data from unauthorized access, modification or destruction.

2. Access control systems are based on three core principles: authentication, authorization, and accountability. Authentication is the process of verifying that a user or device is who or what it claims to be. Authorization is the process of granting access to a user or device based on their identity. Accountability is the process of tracking user and device access to resources.

3. Access control can be implemented using a variety of techniques, including passwords, biometrics, tokens, and encryption.

4. Secure architecture principles, such as isolation and least privilege, are important concepts in access control. Isolation means that resources are kept apart from each other and access is restricted to authorized users. Least privilege means that users are only granted the minimum level of access necessary to perform their job.

5. Access control is an important component of computer system security and can help protect systems and data from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction.
