
## Unit 3 - Secure Architecture Principles: Isolation and Least Privilege

1. Isolation is the practice of ensuring that resources are not shared between different users or systems. It is a key security principle that helps to prevent unauthorized access to sensitive data.

2. Least privilege is the practice of granting the minimum amount of access necessary to perform a task. This helps to reduce the risk of unauthorized access.

3. Isolation can be achieved through physical separation, such as placing different users or systems on different networks, or through logical separation, such as using different accounts or access control lists.

4. Least privilege can be implemented through access control lists, which specify who has access to what resources and what level of access they have.

5. Isolation and least privilege are important security principles that help to protect data and systems from unauthorized access. They should be implemented whenever possible to ensure the security of sensitive data and systems.
