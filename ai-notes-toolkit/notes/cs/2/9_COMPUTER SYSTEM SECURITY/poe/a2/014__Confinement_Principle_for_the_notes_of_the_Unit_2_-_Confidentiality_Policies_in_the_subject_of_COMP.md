### Confinement Principle for the notes of the Unit 2 - Confidentiality Policies in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

1. The confinement principle states that a process should not be able to access resources or perform operations that would violate the security policy for that process.
2. A process should only be able to access the resources and perform the functions that it has been explicitly authorized to access or perform.
3. The operating system enforces the confinement principle via access control mechanisms that restrict processes to authorized resources and operations.
4. The confinement principle is a key concept in constructing secure systems because it limits the potential damage that could be caused by software flaws, programming errors, or malicious attacks.
5. Without effective confinement, a process could access or modify other processes, files, or resources in violation of the system's security policy.

The above points cover the key aspects of the Confinement Principle for maintaining confidentiality through access controls and restricting processes to authorized resources and functions only.
