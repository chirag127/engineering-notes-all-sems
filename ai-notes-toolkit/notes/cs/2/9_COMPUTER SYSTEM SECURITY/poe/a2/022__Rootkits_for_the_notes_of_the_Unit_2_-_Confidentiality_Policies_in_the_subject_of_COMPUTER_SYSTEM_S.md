### Rootkits for the notes of the Unit 2 - Confidentiality Policies in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY.

1. A rootkit is a collection of computer software, typically malicious, designed to enable access to a computer or areas of its software that are not otherwise allowed (for example, to an unauthorized user) while preventing detection of the intrusion.

2. Rootkits are often used for remote access and to obscure the fact that a system has been compromised, allowing for further exploitation. They may include replacements for system binaries so that detection tools cannot find the changes.

3. Rootkit installation can be automated, or an attacker can install it once they have obtained root or Administrator access. Obtaining this access is a result of direct attack on a system.

4. Once installed, it becomes possible to hide the intrusion as well as to maintain privileged access. The key is the ability to evade detection by system security measures.

5. Removal can be complicated or practically impossible, especially in cases where the rootkit resides in the system's kernel, or even firmware; reinstallation of the operating system may be the only safe decontamination measure.
