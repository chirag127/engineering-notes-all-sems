### Rootkits

Rootkits are a type of malware that can go unnoticed by traditional antivirus software. They are designed to hide their presence on a computer system, allowing an attacker to maintain access to the system and steal sensitive information.

Here are some key points to understand about rootkits:

- A rootkit is a collection of software tools used by an attacker to gain and maintain access to a computer system.
- Once installed, a rootkit can alter system files, processes, and data structures to hide its presence and evade detection by antivirus software.
- Rootkits can be used to steal sensitive information, such as login credentials, financial data, or intellectual property.
- Rootkits can be installed through a variety of methods, including email attachments, infected software downloads, or exploiting vulnerabilities in the operating system or other software.
- Detecting and removing rootkits can be difficult, as they are designed to be stealthy and evade detection by antivirus software.
- To prevent rootkits, it is important to keep software up-to-date with the latest security patches, use antivirus software, and avoid downloading and opening suspicious attachments or files.
- If a rootkit is suspected, it is recommended to seek the assistance of a qualified security professional to detect and remove the malware.
