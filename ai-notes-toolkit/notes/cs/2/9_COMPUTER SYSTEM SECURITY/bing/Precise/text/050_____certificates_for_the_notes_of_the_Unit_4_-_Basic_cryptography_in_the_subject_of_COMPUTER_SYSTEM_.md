### Certificates for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

1. A certificate is a digital document that contains information about the identity of an entity, such as a person, organization, or device.
2. Certificates are used to establish trust in digital communications by providing a way to verify the identity of the communicating parties.
3. Certificates are issued by a trusted third party, known as a Certificate Authority (CA), which verifies the identity of the certificate holder.
4. Certificates contain a public key, which is used in combination with a private key to encrypt and decrypt messages.
5. Certificates also contain information about the validity period of the certificate, the identity of the certificate holder, and the identity of the issuing CA.
6. Certificates can be used for a variety of purposes, including secure web browsing, email encryption, and digital signatures.
7. Certificates are an important part of public key infrastructure (PKI), which is a system for managing the creation, distribution, and revocation of certificates.
8. Certificates can be revoked if they are compromised or if the identity of the certificate holder changes.
9. Certificates can be stored in a variety of formats, including DER, PEM, and PFX.
10. Certificates can be managed using tools such as OpenSSL and the Microsoft Management Console (MMC).
