### Unit 4 - Basic Cryptography: Certificates

1. A certificate is a digital document that contains information about the identity of an entity, such as a person, organization, or device.
2. Certificates are used to verify the identity of the entity and to establish trust between parties in a communication.
3. Certificates are issued by a trusted third party, known as a Certificate Authority (CA).
4. The CA verifies the identity of the entity and issues a certificate that contains the entity's public key and other identifying information.
5. The certificate is signed by the CA using its private key, which allows others to verify the authenticity of the certificate.
6. Certificates are used in many applications, including secure web browsing, email encryption, and virtual private networks (VPNs).
7. Certificates can be revoked by the CA if the entity's private key is compromised or if the entity is no longer trusted.
8. Certificates have an expiration date, after which they are no longer valid.
9. Certificates can be renewed by the entity before they expire.
10. Certificates are an important part of public key infrastructure (PKI), which is a system for managing the creation, distribution, and revocation of certificates.
