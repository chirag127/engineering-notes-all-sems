## Unit 3 - Secure Architecture Principles: Isolation and Least Privilege

Isolation and least privilege are two fundamental principles of secure architecture. These principles are used to minimize the attack surface of a system and reduce the potential impact of a security breach.

1. **Isolation** refers to the separation of components, systems, or processes to prevent unauthorized access or interference. This can be achieved through various means, such as network segmentation, virtualization, and containerization.

2. **Least Privilege** is the principle of granting users and processes the minimum level of access required to perform their tasks. This reduces the potential damage that can be caused by a compromised account or process.

By implementing these principles, organizations can improve the security of their systems and reduce the risk of data breaches and other security incidents. It is important to regularly review and update security measures to ensure that they remain effective in the face of evolving threats.
