### Security Interface for Unit 3 - Secure Architecture Principles: Isolation and Least Privilege

#### Isolation
- Isolation refers to the separation of different components or systems to prevent unauthorized access or interference.
- This can be achieved through various methods such as network segmentation, virtualization, and sandboxing.
- Isolation helps to limit the potential damage that can be caused by a security breach by containing it within a specific area.

#### Least Privilege
- The principle of least privilege states that a user or system should only have the minimum level of access necessary to perform their tasks.
- This helps to prevent unauthorized access to sensitive information and reduces the potential damage that can be caused by a security breach.
- Implementing least privilege involves assigning appropriate permissions and access controls to users and systems.

These principles are important for designing secure architecture and ensuring the protection of sensitive information. By implementing isolation and least privilege, organizations can reduce the risk of security breaches and protect their systems and data.
