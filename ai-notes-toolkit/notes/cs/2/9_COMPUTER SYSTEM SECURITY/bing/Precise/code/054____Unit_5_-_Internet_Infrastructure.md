## Unit 5 - Internet Infrastructure

The internet infrastructure refers to the physical and logical components that make up the global system of interconnected computer networks known as the internet. These components include:

1. **Network Access Points (NAPs)**: These are physical locations where internet service providers (ISPs) connect their networks to exchange traffic.

2. **Internet Exchange Points (IXPs)**: These are physical locations where multiple ISPs exchange traffic with each other.

3. **Internet Backbone**: This refers to the high-speed data transmission lines that connect NAPs and IXPs and carry internet traffic across long distances.

4. **Internet Service Providers (ISPs)**: These are companies that provide internet access to customers by connecting them to the internet backbone.

5. **Domain Name System (DNS)**: This is a hierarchical and decentralized naming system for computers, services, or other resources connected to the internet. It translates human-readable domain names into the numerical IP addresses needed for the purpose of locating and identifying computer services and devices with the underlying network protocols.

6. **Routing**: This refers to the process of selecting a path for traffic in a network, or between or across multiple networks.

7. **Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP)**: This is the suite of communication protocols used to connect hosts on the internet. It is the foundation of the internet and is used to transmit data across the network.

8. **Internet of Things (IoT)**: This refers to the network of physical objects, such as devices, vehicles, and home appliances, that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet.

These components work together to provide the infrastructure that enables the internet to function and allows users to access and share information across the globe.
