### Confinement Principle

The confinement principle is a concept in computer system security that deals with preventing a server from disclosing information that the user of the service considers confidential. It is a mechanism for enforcing the principle of least privilege.

In the confinement principle, access control affects the server in two ways:

1. The goal of the service provider: The server must ensure that the resources that are being accessed on behalf of the client include only those resources that the client is authorized for.
2. The goal of the service user: The server must ensure the privacy of the service user.

The confinement mechanism must distinguish between the transmission of authorized data and the transmission of unauthorized data.

The confinement principle is an important concept in the study of confidentiality policies in computer system security. It helps to ensure that private information is not disclosed to unauthorized parties.
