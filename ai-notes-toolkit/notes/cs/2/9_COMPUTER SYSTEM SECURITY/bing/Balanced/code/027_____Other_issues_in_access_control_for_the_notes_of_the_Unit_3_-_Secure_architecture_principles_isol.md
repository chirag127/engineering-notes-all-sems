### Other issues in access control for computer system security

Access control is the process of granting or denying access to resources based on the identity and attributes of the requester. Access control systems are designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the resources, as well as to enforce the security policies and regulations of the organization.

However, access control systems are not perfect and may face some issues that can compromise their effectiveness and security. Some of the common issues are:

- **Not secure enough**: Access control systems may have vulnerabilities that can be exploited by attackers, such as weak encryption, default passwords, misconfiguration, outdated software, network exposure, etc. These vulnerabilities can allow unauthorized access, data leakage, denial of service, or even remote control of the system .
- **Keycards lack true authorization**: Keycards are a common method of authorization in access control systems, but they have some drawbacks. Keycards can be lost, stolen, duplicated, or shared, which can result in unauthorized access or identity fraud. Keycards also do not verify the identity of the holder, only the possession of the card.
- **Improper setup**: Access control systems require proper installation and configuration to function correctly and securely. Improper setup can lead to errors, malfunctions, or security breaches. For example, if the access control system is not connected to a backup power source, it may fail during a power outage and leave the resources unprotected .
- **Lack of management**: Access control systems need regular maintenance and monitoring to ensure their performance and security. Lack of management can lead to outdated software, unpatched vulnerabilities, inaccurate records, or unnoticed incidents. For example, if the access control system does not log the access events or alert the administrators of any anomalies, it may be difficult to detect and respond to any unauthorized access or attacks .

To address these issues, access control systems should follow some best practices, such as:

- **Use strong encryption and authentication**: Access control systems should use strong encryption algorithms and protocols to protect the communication and data transmission between the components. They should also use strong authentication methods, such as biometrics, tokens, or certificates, to verify the identity and attributes of the requester.
- **Change default passwords and settings**: Access control systems should change the default passwords and settings that come with the devices or software, as they may be easily guessed or found by attackers. They should also use complex and unique passwords and settings that are hard to crack or bypass .
- **Update software and firmware**: Access control systems should update the software and firmware of the devices and components regularly, as they may contain bugs or vulnerabilities that can be exploited by attackers. They should also use the latest versions of the software and firmware that are compatible and secure .
- **Limit network exposure**: Access control systems should limit the network exposure of the devices and components, as they may be targeted by attackers who can access the network. They should use firewalls, VPNs, or other methods to isolate the access control system from the public network or other untrusted networks .
- **Monitor and audit the system**: Access control systems should monitor and audit the system regularly, as they may detect and prevent any unauthorized access or attacks. They should use logs, alerts, reports, or other tools to record and analyze the access events and activities, and to notify the administrators of any anomalies or incidents .
