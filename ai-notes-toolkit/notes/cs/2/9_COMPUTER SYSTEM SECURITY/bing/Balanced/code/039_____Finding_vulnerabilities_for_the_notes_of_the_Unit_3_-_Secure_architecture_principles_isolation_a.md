### Finding vulnerabilities for the notes of the Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and least privilege in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Secure architecture principles are the guidelines and best practices for designing and implementing secure systems that can resist attacks and protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources.
- Isolation and least privilege are two of the most important secure architecture principles that aim to reduce the attack surface and limit the damage in case of a breach.
- Isolation means separating different components or layers of a system into independent and isolated segments that have minimal or no interaction with each other. This can prevent lateral movement of attackers and contain the impact of a compromise to a specific segment. Examples of isolation techniques include network segmentation, virtualization, containerization, sandboxing, and browser isolation.
- Least privilege means granting the minimum level of access or permissions to users, processes, or devices that are necessary to perform their tasks or functions. This can prevent unauthorized access, privilege escalation, and data leakage. Examples of least privilege techniques include role-based access control, mandatory access control, discretionary access control, and multi-factor authentication.
- Finding vulnerabilities for the notes of the Unit 3 involves identifying and analyzing the potential weaknesses or flaws in the application of isolation and least privilege principles in a given system or scenario. Some of the common steps for finding vulnerabilities are:

  - Define the scope and objectives of the vulnerability assessment.
  - Gather information about the system architecture, components, interfaces, users, roles, permissions, and policies.
  - Identify the potential threats and attack vectors that could exploit the isolation and least privilege principles.
  - Perform vulnerability scanning, testing, or analysis using tools, techniques, or methods such as penetration testing, static analysis, dynamic analysis, fuzzing, or code review.
  - Evaluate the severity and impact of the identified vulnerabilities based on the likelihood and consequences of exploitation.
  - Report and document the findings and recommendations for remediation or mitigation.
