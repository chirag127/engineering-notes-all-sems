## Unit 5 - Internet Infrastructure

- Internet infrastructure is the physical hardware, transmission media, and software used to interconnect computers and users on the Internet.
- Internet infrastructure is responsible for hosting, storing, processing, and serving the information that makes up websites, applications, and content.
- Internet infrastructure is a network of networks, composed of different types and levels of networks that connect in various ways.
- The main components of Internet infrastructure are:
  - End devices: These are the devices that users interact with, such as computers, smartphones, tablets, etc. They send and receive data over the Internet using protocols such as TCP/IP.
  - Local area networks (LANs): These are networks that connect end devices within a limited geographic area, such as a home, office, or school. They use devices such as switches, routers, and access points to transmit data over wired or wireless media .
  - Internet service providers (ISPs): These are companies that provide access to the Internet for end users and other networks. They use devices such as routers, edge platforms, and modems to connect LANs to the wider Internet .
  - Regional and backbone networks: These are networks that connect ISPs and other large networks across regions and continents. They use high-speed and high-capacity transmission media such as fiber-optic cables, satellites, and microwave links to carry large amounts of data.
  - Internet exchange points (IXPs): These are physical locations where different networks exchange traffic and data. They reduce the cost and latency of data transmission and improve the reliability and performance of the Internet.
  - Domain name system (DNS): This is a distributed database that maps domain names (such as www.example.com) to IP addresses (such as 192.168.1.1). It allows users to access websites and services using human-readable names instead of numerical addresses.
  - Web servers: These are computers that host and serve web pages and other content to end users. They use software such as Apache, Nginx, or IIS to process requests and send responses using protocols such as HTTP or HTTPS.
  - Web browsers: These are applications that end users use to access and view web pages and other content on the Internet. They use software such as Chrome, Firefox, or Safari to interpret and render HTML, CSS, and JavaScript code and display the web content to the user.
- The Internet is different from the Web. The Internet is an infrastructure, whereas the Web is a service built on top of the infrastructure. There are other services that use the Internet, such as email, chat, and online gaming.
- The Internet is constantly evolving and expanding. There are many challenges and opportunities for improving the Internet infrastructure, such as:
  - Increasing the availability and affordability of high-speed Internet access for all, especially in rural and underserved areas.
  - Enhancing the security and privacy of data and communications on the Internet, especially against cyberattacks, hacking, and surveillance.
  - Promoting the digital equity and inclusion of all people, especially those who face barriers to access and use the Internet, such as low-income, elderly, disabled, or marginalized groups.
  - Supporting the innovation and development of new technologies and applications that use the Internet, such as cloud computing, artificial intelligence, Internet of Things, and blockchain.
