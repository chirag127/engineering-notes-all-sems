### Sample Attacks

A sample attack is an example of a cyber attack, which is a set of actions performed by threat actors, who try to gain unauthorized access, steal data or cause damage to computers, computer networks, or other computing systems. A sample attack can be used to illustrate the types, methods, and impacts of cyber attacks, as well as the possible countermeasures and prevention strategies. Here are some sample attacks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Computer System Security Introduction in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY:

- **Identity theft, fraud, extortion**: These are cyber attacks that aim to obtain personal or financial information from the victims, such as names, passwords, credit card numbers, bank accounts, etc. The attackers may use this information to impersonate the victims, make fraudulent transactions, blackmail them, or sell their data to other criminals. For example, a phishing attack is a type of identity theft attack, where the attacker sends an email or a message that looks like it comes from a legitimate source, such as a bank, a company, or a government agency, and asks the victim to click on a link, download an attachment, or provide some sensitive information. The link or the attachment may contain malware, or lead to a fake website that collects the victim's data.
- **Malware, phishing, spamming, spoofing, spyware, trojans and viruses**: These are cyber attacks that involve malicious software or code that can infect, damage, or control the victim's computer or network. Malware can have various functions, such as stealing data, deleting files, encrypting data, displaying unwanted ads, spying on the user's activities, or creating backdoors for remote access. For example, a ransomware attack is a type of malware attack, where the attacker encrypts the victim's data and demands a ransom for the decryption key. The attacker may threaten to delete the data or expose it to the public if the ransom is not paid.
- **Stolen hardware, such as laptops or mobile devices**: These are cyber attacks that involve the physical theft or loss of devices that contain sensitive or valuable data. The attackers may access the data on the devices, or use them to launch other attacks, such as accessing the victim's accounts, networks, or contacts. For example, a laptop theft attack is a type of stolen hardware attack, where the attacker steals a laptop that belongs to an employee of a company or an organization, and tries to access the data on the laptop, such as confidential documents, passwords, or client information.
- **Denial-of-service and distributed denial-of-service attacks**: These are cyber attacks that aim to disrupt or degrade the availability or performance of a system or a service, such as a website, a server, or a network. The attackers may overload the system or the service with a large amount of requests or traffic, or exploit a vulnerability that causes the system or the service to crash or slow down. For example, a DDoS attack is a type of denial-of-service attack, where the attacker uses multiple compromised devices, such as computers, smartphones, or IoT devices, to launch a coordinated attack on a target system or service, making it inaccessible or unusable for the legitimate users.
- **Breach of access**: These are cyber attacks that involve the unauthorized or improper access to a system, a network, or a resource, such as a database, a file, or a device. The attackers may use various methods, such as guessing passwords, exploiting vulnerabilities, or bypassing security controls, to gain access to the target system, network, or resource. The attackers may then steal, modify, or destroy the data, or perform other malicious actions. For example, a SQL injection attack is a type of breach of access attack, where the attacker injects malicious SQL commands into a web application that interacts with a database, and executes them on the database server, resulting in data leakage, data corruption, or system compromise.
- **Password sniffing**: These are cyber attacks that involve the interception and capture of passwords or other authentication credentials that are transmitted over a network or a communication channel, such as a wireless network, an email, or a web browser. The attackers may use various tools, such as network analyzers, packet sniffers, or keyloggers, to monitor and record the passwords or the credentials.
