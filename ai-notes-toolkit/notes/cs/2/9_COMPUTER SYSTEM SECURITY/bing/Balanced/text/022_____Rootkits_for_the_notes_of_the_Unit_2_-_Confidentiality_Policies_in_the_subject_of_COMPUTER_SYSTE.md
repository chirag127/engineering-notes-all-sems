### Rootkits

- A rootkit is a type of malware that provides attackers with **root** or **administrator** access to a device by **evading detection** from the user and other software, including antivirus.
- A rootkit can be used to perform various malicious activities, such as stealing data, spying, installing backdoors, launching attacks, or hiding other malware.
- Rootkits can infect different levels of a device, such as the **kernel**, the **hardware**, the **firmware**, the **bootloader**, the **memory**, or the **hypervisor** .
- Rootkits are difficult to detect and remove, as they can **modify** or **bypass** the system's security mechanisms, such as logs, processes, files, or registry entries.
- To prevent rootkit infections, some best practices are:
  - **Do not overlook updates**. Updates can fix known vulnerabilities that rootkits can exploit.
  - **Detect rootkits with anti-malware software**. Anti-malware software can scan for rootkit signatures or anomalies and alert the user of any suspicious activity.
  - **Invest in anti-phishing protection**. Phishing emails can deliver rootkits as attachments or links that trick the user into opening or clicking them.
  - **Use strong passwords and encryption**. Strong passwords and encryption can prevent unauthorized access to the device or the data stored on it.
  - **Avoid using public or unsecured networks**. Public or unsecured networks can expose the device to network-based rootkits that can intercept or modify the traffic.
  - **Disable unnecessary services and ports**. Unnecessary services and ports can create entry points for rootkits to infiltrate the device.
  - **Enable secure boot and BIOS password**. Secure boot and BIOS password can prevent hardware or firmware rootkits from altering the boot process or the device's firmware.
  - **Monitor system performance and behavior**. System performance and behavior can indicate the presence of rootkits, such as slow speed, high CPU usage, crashes, or unusual network activity.
  - **Backup data regularly**. Backup data can help the user recover from a rootkit infection without losing important information.
  - **Use trusted sources and software**. Trusted sources and software can reduce the risk of downloading or installing rootkits disguised as legitimate applications or updates.
