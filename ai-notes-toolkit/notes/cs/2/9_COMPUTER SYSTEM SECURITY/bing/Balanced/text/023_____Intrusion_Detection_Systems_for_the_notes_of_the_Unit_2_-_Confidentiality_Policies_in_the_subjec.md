### Intrusion Detection Systems

- An intrusion detection system (IDS) is a device or software application that monitors a network or systems for malicious activity or policy violations  .
- An IDS can detect known threats and suspicious or malicious activity based on predefined rules or signatures, or unknown threats and anomalies based on statistical analysis or machine learning .
- An IDS can send alerts to IT and security teams when it detects any security risks and threats, and provide information such as source and destination IP addresses, protocols, ports, timestamps, and payloads .
- An IDS can be classified into different types based on the location, method, or behavior of the detection  :
  - Network-based IDS (NIDS): monitors network traffic and analyzes packets for signs of attacks or intrusions.
  - Host-based IDS (HIDS): monitors system activities and logs on a specific host for signs of unauthorized access or malicious software.
  - Wireless IDS (WIDS): monitors wireless network traffic and detects rogue access points, unauthorized devices, or attacks on wireless protocols.
  - Network behavior analysis (NBA): monitors network traffic and detects anomalies or deviations from normal behavior patterns.
  - Signature-based IDS: compares network or system activities with a database of known attack signatures or patterns.
  - Anomaly-based IDS: establishes a baseline of normal network or system activities and detects any deviations or anomalies from the baseline.
  - Hybrid IDS: combines two or more types of IDS to enhance the detection capabilities and reduce false positives or negatives.
- An IDS can be integrated with an intrusion prevention system (IPS) to form an intrusion detection and prevention system (IDPS), which can not only detect but also prevent or block malicious activities or policy violations   .
- An IDS can help improve the security posture of a network or system by providing visibility, detection, and response capabilities, but it also has some limitations and challenges, such as false positives or negatives, high resource consumption, evasion techniques, and scalability issues  .
