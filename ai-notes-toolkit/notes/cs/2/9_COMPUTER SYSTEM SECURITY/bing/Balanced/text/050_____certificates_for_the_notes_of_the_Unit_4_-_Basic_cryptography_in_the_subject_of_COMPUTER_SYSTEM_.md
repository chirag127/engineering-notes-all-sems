### Certificates

- A certificate is a digital document that contains information about the identity and public key of an entity, such as a person, a device, or a website.
- A certificate is issued and signed by a trusted authority, called a certificate authority (CA), that verifies the authenticity of the entity.
- A certificate can be used to establish secure communication, authenticate the identity of the entity, and prevent impersonation or tampering attacks.
- A certificate typically contains the following information:
  - The name and public key of the entity
  - The name and signature of the CA
  - The validity period of the certificate
  - The serial number of the certificate
  - The purpose and usage of the certificate
- A certificate can be encoded in different formats, such as X.509, PEM, DER, or PGP.
- A certificate can be stored in different locations, such as files, databases, or directories.
- A certificate can be revoked by the CA if it is compromised, expired, or no longer needed. A certificate revocation list (CRL) is a list of revoked certificates that is published by the CA. A certificate status protocol (CSP) is a service that allows clients to check the status of a certificate online.
