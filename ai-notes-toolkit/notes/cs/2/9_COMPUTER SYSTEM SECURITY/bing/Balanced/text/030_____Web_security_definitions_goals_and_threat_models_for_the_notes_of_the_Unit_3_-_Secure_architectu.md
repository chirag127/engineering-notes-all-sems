### Web security definitions goals and threat models

- Web security is the practice of protecting web applications and services from unauthorized access, manipulation, or damage by malicious actors or events.
- The goals of web security are to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of web resources and data, as well as to protect the privacy and identity of web users and clients.
- Threat models are systematic ways of identifying and analyzing the potential threats and vulnerabilities that may affect a web system, as well as the countermeasures and mitigations that can be implemented to reduce or eliminate the risks.
- There are different methods and frameworks for conducting threat modeling, such as STRIDE, DREAD, PASTA, OCTAVE, and OWASP. They typically involve the following steps:
  - Define the scope and boundaries of the web system, such as the components, interfaces, data flows, and trust boundaries.
  - Identify the assets and resources that need to be protected, such as sensitive data, credentials, functionality, and reputation.
  - Enumerate the potential threat sources and actors, such as hackers, insiders, competitors, or natural disasters, and their capabilities, motivations, and goals.
  - Analyze the possible attack vectors and scenarios, such as injection, cross-site scripting, denial-of-service, or phishing, and their likelihood and impact.
  - Evaluate the existing security controls and measures, such as encryption, authentication, authorization, or logging, and their effectiveness and limitations.
  - Prioritize the threats and vulnerabilities based on their severity and risk level, and recommend the appropriate countermeasures and mitigations, such as patching, hardening, monitoring, or training.
