# Public key distribution

Public key distribution is the process of sharing public keys among parties who want to communicate securely using public key cryptography. Public keys are used to encrypt messages or verify signatures, while private keys are kept secret by the owners. There are several methods of public key distribution, such as:

- **Public announcement**: A party broadcasts its public key to everyone who wants to communicate with it. This method is simple and convenient, but it is vulnerable to impersonation attacks, where an adversary can forge or modify the public key of a legitimate party.
- **Publicly available directory**: A party registers its public key with a trusted directory service, which maintains a database of public keys and their owners. Anyone who wants to communicate with a party can look up its public key from the directory. This method is more secure than public announcement, but it requires the directory service to be reliable and trustworthy.
- **Public-key authority**: A party obtains its public key from a trusted authority, which acts as a mediator between parties who want to communicate. The authority generates and distributes public keys to the parties, and also verifies their identities. This method is more secure than publicly available directory, but it requires the authority to be online and available at all times.
- **Public-key certificates**: A party obtains a certificate from a trusted certificate authority, which is a special type of public-key authority that issues digital certificates. A certificate is a document that binds a public key to the identity of its owner, and is signed by the certificate authority. Anyone who wants to communicate with a party can verify its certificate using the public key of the certificate authority. This method is the most secure and widely used method of public key distribution, but it requires the certificate authority to be trustworthy and secure.
