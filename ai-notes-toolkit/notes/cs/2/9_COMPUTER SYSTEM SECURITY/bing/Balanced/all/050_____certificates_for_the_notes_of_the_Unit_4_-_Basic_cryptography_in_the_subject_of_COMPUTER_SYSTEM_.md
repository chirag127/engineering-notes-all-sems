# Certificates for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- A certificate is an electronic document that proves the ownership or validity of a public key.  
- A certificate contains information about the key, the identity of its owner (called the subject), and the digital signature of an entity that has verified the certificate's contents (called the issuer).  
- A certificate can be used for encryption and authentication purposes. 
- A certificate can be issued by a trusted third party, such as a certificate authority (CA), or by a self-signed entity. 
- A certificate has a validity period, after which it expires and needs to be renewed or replaced. 
- A certificate can be revoked by the issuer or the subject if it is compromised or no longer needed. 
- A certificate follows a standard format, such as X.509 or PGP, that specifies the structure and content of the certificate. 
- A certificate can be part of a certificate chain, which is a sequence of certificates that link the subject's public key to a trusted root CA. 
- A certificate can be verified by checking the digital signature of the issuer, the validity period, the revocation status, and the certificate chain.
