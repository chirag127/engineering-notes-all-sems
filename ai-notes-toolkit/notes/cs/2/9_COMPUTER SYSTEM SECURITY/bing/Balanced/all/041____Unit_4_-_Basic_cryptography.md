# Unit 4 - Basic Cryptography

Cryptography is the art of securing information by turning plaintext into ciphertext. Plaintext is the original message that can be understood by anyone, while ciphertext is the encrypted message that can only be understood by someone who has the key to decrypt it. Cryptography has many applications in everyday life, such as digital signatures, time stamping, electronic money transactions, cryptocurrency, and a lot more.

Cryptography techniques include:

- Confidentiality: The ability to keep information secret from unauthorized parties.
- Integrity: The ability to ensure that information is not altered or corrupted during transmission or storage.
- Non-repudiation: The ability to prove that a message or transaction was sent or received by a specific party.
- Authentication: The ability to verify the identity of a party or the source of a message.

Cryptography can be classified into two main types:

- Symmetric cryptography: A type of cryptography that uses the same key for both encryption and decryption. The key must be shared securely between the sender and the receiver before communication. Symmetric cryptography is fast and efficient, but it has the problem of key distribution and management. Examples of symmetric cryptography algorithms are AES, DES, and RC4.
- Asymmetric cryptography: A type of cryptography that uses a pair of keys for encryption and decryption. The pair consists of a private key and a public key. The private key is kept secret by the owner, while the public key is shared with anyone who wants to communicate with the owner. Asymmetric cryptography solves the key distribution problem of symmetric cryptography, but it is slower and more complex. Examples of asymmetric cryptography algorithms are RSA, ECC, and DSA.

Cryptography also involves various mathematical concepts and algorithms, such as:

- Ciphers: Algorithms that perform encryption and decryption.
- Hash functions: Algorithms that map any input to a fixed-length output, called a hash or a digest. Hash functions are used for integrity and authentication purposes, as they are one-way and collision-resistant.
- Digital signatures: Algorithms that use asymmetric cryptography to generate and verify signatures that prove the authenticity and non-repudiation of a message.
- Cryptanalysis: The science of breaking cryptographic systems and finding weaknesses in them.
