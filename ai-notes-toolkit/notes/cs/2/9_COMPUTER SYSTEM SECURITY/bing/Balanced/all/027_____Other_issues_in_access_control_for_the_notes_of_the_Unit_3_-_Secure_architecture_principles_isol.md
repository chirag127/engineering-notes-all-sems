# Other issues in access control for computer system security

Access control is the process of granting or denying access to resources based on the identity and privileges of users or groups. Access control systems aim to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of resources from unauthorized or malicious actors. However, access control systems can also face some issues that affect their effectiveness and security. Some of these issues are:

- **Not secure enough**: Access control systems can have various vulnerabilities that can be exploited by attackers, such as weak passwords, default settings, misconfigurations, outdated software, network exposure, physical access, etc. These vulnerabilities can allow attackers to bypass, tamper with, or compromise the access control system and gain unauthorized access to resources . Therefore, access control systems should be properly set up, updated, and monitored to prevent or detect such attacks.
- **Keycards lack true authorization**: Keycards are a common method of authorization in access control systems, but they have some limitations and drawbacks. Keycards can be lost, stolen, duplicated, or shared, which can result in unauthorized access or identity fraud. Keycards also do not verify the identity of the user, but only the possession of the card, which can be insufficient for some scenarios. Therefore, keycards should be combined with other factors of authentication, such as passwords, biometrics, or tokens, to provide stronger and more reliable authorization.
- **Improper setup**: Access control systems can be improperly set up due to human errors, lack of knowledge, or negligence. Improper setup can include assigning incorrect or excessive permissions, granting access to wrong or unnecessary users or groups, failing to revoke access when needed, or leaving default or blank passwords . Improper setup can lead to security breaches, data leaks, or compliance violations. Therefore, access control systems should be carefully planned, implemented, and reviewed to ensure they meet the security and business requirements.
- **Lack of management**: Access control systems can be poorly managed due to insufficient resources, skills, or policies. Lack of management can include failing to update or patch the system, monitor or audit the access logs, enforce or review the access policies, or respond to incidents or alerts . Lack of management can result in security gaps, performance issues, or legal risks. Therefore, access control systems should be regularly managed, maintained, and evaluated to ensure they are effective and secure.
