### Email security

Email security is the practice of protecting email accounts and communications from unauthorized access, loss, or compromise. Email is often used to spread malware, spam, and phishing attacks, which can compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of email data and systems. Email security also involves establishing policies and using tools to prevent, detect, and respond to these threats.

Some of the topics covered in email security are:

- **Email encryption**: The process of transforming plain text email messages into unreadable ciphertext using cryptographic algorithms. Email encryption can be done at different levels, such as end-to-end, transport layer, or server-side. Email encryption can protect the content of email messages from eavesdropping, tampering, or interception by unauthorized parties.
- **Email authentication**: The process of verifying the identity and legitimacy of the sender and the recipient of an email message. Email authentication can be done using various protocols, such as Sender Policy Framework (SPF), DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM), and Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC). Email authentication can prevent spoofing, phishing, and impersonation attacks that aim to deceive or manipulate the recipients of email messages.
- **Email filtering**: The process of screening incoming and outgoing email messages based on predefined criteria, such as sender, recipient, subject, content, or attachments. Email filtering can be done using various techniques, such as spam detection, content analysis, or machine learning. Email filtering can block or quarantine unwanted, malicious, or suspicious email messages from reaching the inbox or the network of the recipients.
- **Email archiving**: The process of storing and preserving email messages and their metadata for future reference, compliance, or legal purposes. Email archiving can be done using various methods, such as cloud-based, on-premise, or hybrid solutions. Email archiving can ensure the availability, integrity, and accessibility of email data and records over time.

Some of the best practices for email security include:

- **Educate employees**: Employees should be trained on how to recognize and avoid email-based threats, such as malware, spam, and phishing. Employees should also be aware of the email security policies and procedures of the organization and follow them accordingly.
- **Create reliable passwords**: Passwords should be strong, unique, and changed regularly to protect email accounts from unauthorized access or compromise. Passwords should not be shared, reused, or written down. Password managers can be used to generate and store passwords securely.
- **Enable two-factor authentication**: Two-factor authentication (2FA) is a method of verifying the identity of the user by requiring two pieces of evidence, such as a password and a code sent to a phone or email. 2FA can add an extra layer of security to email accounts and prevent unauthorized access even if the password is compromised.
- **Download antivirus software**: Antivirus software is a program that can scan, detect, and remove malware from email messages and attachments. Antivirus software can also prevent malware from infecting the email system or the network of the recipients. Antivirus software should be updated regularly to keep up with the latest malware signatures and definitions.
- **Use email security tools**: Email security tools are applications or services that can enhance the security and protection of email communications and accounts. Email security tools can provide features such as email encryption, email authentication, email filtering, and email archiving. Email security tools should be compatible with the email system and the network of the organization and configured properly to meet the email security needs and goals of the organization.
