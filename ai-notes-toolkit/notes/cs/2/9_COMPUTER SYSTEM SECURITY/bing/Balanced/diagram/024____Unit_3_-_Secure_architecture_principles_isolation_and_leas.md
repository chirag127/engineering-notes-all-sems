## Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles: isolation and least privilege

- Isolation and least privilege are two fundamental principles of secure architecture design that aim to reduce the attack surface and limit the damage of a potential breach.
- Isolation refers to the separation of different components or layers of a system, such as data, processes, networks, or users, so that they cannot interfere with each other or access unauthorized resources.
- Least privilege refers to the restriction of the permissions or capabilities of each component or user to the minimum necessary to perform its function, so that it cannot abuse or compromise other resources or data.
- Some examples of applying isolation and least privilege in secure architecture are:

  - Using firewalls, virtual private networks (VPNs), or encryption to isolate network segments or data in transit from unauthorized access or interception.
  - Using containers, virtual machines, or microservices to isolate applications or processes from each other and from the underlying operating system or hardware.
  - Using role-based access control (RBAC), multi-factor authentication (MFA), or encryption keys to limit the access of users or components to the data or resources they need.
  - Using sandboxing, code signing, or antivirus software to isolate and verify the execution of untrusted or potentially malicious code or files.
  - Using audit logs, monitoring tools, or alerts to isolate and identify anomalous or suspicious activities or behaviors in the system.
