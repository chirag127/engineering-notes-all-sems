## Unit 5 - Internet Infrastructure

- Internet infrastructure is the physical hardware, transmission media, and software used to interconnect computers and users on the Internet.
- Internet infrastructure is responsible for hosting, storing, processing, and serving the information that makes up websites, applications, and content.
- Internet infrastructure is a network of networks, composed of different types and levels of networks that connect in various ways.
- The main components of Internet infrastructure are:
  - End devices: These are the devices that users interact with, such as computers, smartphones, tablets, etc. End devices can send and receive data over the Internet using various protocols and applications.
  - Local area networks (LANs): These are networks that connect end devices within a limited geographic area, such as a home, office, or school. LANs can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth.
  - Routers: These are devices that forward data packets between different networks. Routers can connect LANs to other LANs, or to wide area networks (WANs), which span larger geographic areas.
  - Internet service providers (ISPs): These are organizations that provide access to the Internet for end users and other networks. ISPs can use various technologies, such as cable, DSL, fiber, satellite, or cellular, to connect to the Internet backbone.
  - Internet backbone: This is the core network of high-speed and high-capacity routers and links that connects different ISPs and regions of the world. The Internet backbone is composed of multiple networks owned by different entities, such as governments, universities, or corporations.
  - Internet exchange points (IXPs): These are physical locations where different ISPs and backbone networks can exchange traffic and data. IXPs help reduce the cost and latency of data transmission and improve the reliability and performance of the Internet.
  - Domain name system (DNS): This is a distributed database that maps domain names, such as www.example.com, to IP addresses, such as 192.168.1.1, which are used to identify and locate devices on the Internet. DNS servers are responsible for resolving domain names to IP addresses and vice versa.
  - Web servers: These are devices that store and deliver web pages and other web content to end users. Web servers use the hypertext transfer protocol (HTTP) to communicate with web browsers and other web clients.
  - Other servers and services: These are devices and software that provide various functions and features on the Internet, such as email, file transfer, streaming, social media, e-commerce, etc. These servers and services use different protocols and applications to interact with end users and other servers.
