### Real world protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Cryptography is the science of securing information by transforming it into a form that only the intended recipients can understand and use.
- Cryptography is used in many real world applications, such as web APIs, user authentication, digital signatures, encryption, hashing, and blockchain.
- Some of the cryptographic techniques that are commonly used in real world protocols are:

  - Symmetric encryption: A method of encryption that uses the same key to encrypt and decrypt the data. Symmetric encryption is fast and efficient, but requires a secure way of sharing the key between the parties. Examples of symmetric encryption algorithms are AES, DES, and RC4.
  - Asymmetric encryption: A method of encryption that uses a pair of keys: a public key and a private key. The public key can be used to encrypt the data, but only the private key can decrypt it. The private key can also be used to sign the data, and the public key can verify the signature. Asymmetric encryption is more secure and flexible than symmetric encryption, but also more computationally intensive. Examples of asymmetric encryption algorithms are RSA, ECC, and ElGamal.
  - Hashing: A method of transforming any data into a fixed-length string, called a hash or a digest, that uniquely identifies the data. Hashing is useful for verifying the integrity and authenticity of the data, as any change in the data will result in a different hash. Hashing is also used for generating keys, passwords, and digital fingerprints. Examples of hashing algorithms are SHA, MD5, and BLAKE.
  - Digital signatures: A method of proving the origin and integrity of a message by using asymmetric encryption. The sender uses their private key to sign the message, and the receiver uses the sender's public key to verify the signature. Digital signatures are used for authentication, authorization, and non-repudiation. Examples of digital signature schemes are DSA, ECDSA, and EdDSA.
  - Encryption modes: A method of applying an encryption algorithm to a message, depending on the size and structure of the message. Encryption modes can affect the security, performance, and error propagation of the encryption. Examples of encryption modes are ECB, CBC, CTR, and GCM.
  - Key exchange: A method of securely establishing a shared secret key between two or more parties, without revealing the key to anyone else. Key exchange is necessary for using symmetric encryption in a network. Examples of key exchange protocols are Diffie-Hellman, ECDH, and RSA-KEM.
  - Authentication: A method of verifying the identity of a party or a message, by using cryptographic techniques such as passwords, certificates, tokens, or signatures. Authentication is used for access control, accountability, and trust. Examples of authentication protocols are Kerberos, OAuth, and SSL/TLS.
  - Blockchain: A method of storing and transferring data in a distributed ledger, that is maintained by a network of nodes, without a central authority. Blockchain uses cryptography to ensure the validity, consistency, and immutability of the data. Blockchain is used for applications such as cryptocurrencies, smart contracts, and digital identity. Examples of blockchain platforms are Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Hyperledger.
