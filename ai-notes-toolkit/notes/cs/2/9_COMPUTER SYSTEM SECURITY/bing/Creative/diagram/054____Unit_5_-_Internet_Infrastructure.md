## Unit 5 - Internet Infrastructure

- Internet infrastructure is the physical hardware, transmission media, and software used to interconnect computers and users on the internet.
- Internet infrastructure is responsible for hosting, storing, processing, and serving the information that makes up websites, applications, and content.
- Internet infrastructure is composed of different layers and components, such as:
  - Network access layer: This layer connects end-user devices, such as computers, smartphones, and routers, to the internet through various access technologies, such as cable, DSL, fiber, wireless, satellite, or cellular.
  - Internet backbone layer: This layer consists of high-speed, high-capacity networks that carry data across long distances and between different regions and countries. These networks are owned and operated by internet service providers (ISPs), telecommunications companies, governments, or academic institutions.
  - Internet exchange layer: This layer facilitates the interconnection and exchange of data between different internet backbone networks. Internet exchange points (IXPs) are physical locations where multiple ISPs can connect and exchange traffic, reducing costs and improving performance.
  - Content delivery layer: This layer optimizes the delivery of content and services to end-users by caching and distributing data across multiple servers and locations. Content delivery networks (CDNs) are networks of servers that store copies of popular or frequently accessed content, such as videos, images, or web pages, and deliver them to users based on their geographic proximity.
- Internet infrastructure is constantly evolving and expanding to meet the growing demand and diversity of internet users and applications. Some of the current trends and challenges in internet infrastructure include:
  - Increasing bandwidth and speed: As more users and devices access the internet, and as more applications require higher quality and resolution of data, such as video streaming, gaming, or virtual reality, the internet infrastructure needs to provide more bandwidth and speed to accommodate the traffic and reduce latency.
  - Enhancing security and privacy: As more data and information are transmitted and stored on the internet, the internet infrastructure needs to protect them from unauthorized access, interception, modification, or theft. This involves implementing encryption, authentication, firewalls, and other security measures at different layers and components of the internet infrastructure.
  - Promoting digital equity and inclusion: As the internet becomes more essential for education, work, health, and social activities, the internet infrastructure needs to ensure that everyone can access and benefit from it, regardless of their location, income, or background. This involves expanding the coverage and affordability of internet access, especially in rural and remote areas, and addressing the digital divide and the digital skills gap among different groups of users.
- Internet infrastructure is not the same as the web. The web is a service built on top of the internet infrastructure that allows users to access and share information through web pages and web browsers. The web is one of the many services that use the internet infrastructure, along with email, instant messaging, voice over IP, and others.
