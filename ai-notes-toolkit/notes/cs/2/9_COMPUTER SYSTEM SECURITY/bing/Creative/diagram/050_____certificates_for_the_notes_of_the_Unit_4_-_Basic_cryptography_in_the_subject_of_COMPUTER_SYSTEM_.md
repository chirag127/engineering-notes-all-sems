### Certificates

- A certificate is an electronic document that proves the ownership or validity of a public key .
- A certificate contains information about the key, the identity of its owner (called the subject), and the digital signature of an entity that has verified the certificate's contents (called the issuer) .
- A certificate can be used for encryption and authentication purposes.
- A certificate can be issued by a trusted authority (such as a certificate authority or CA) or self-signed by the owner of the key.
- A certificate has a validity period, after which it expires and needs to be renewed or revoked.
- A certificate can be formatted according to different standards, such as X.509, PGP, or S/MIME.
- A certificate can be stored in different formats, such as PEM, DER, or PKCS.
- A certificate can be verified by checking the digital signature of the issuer and the validity period of the certificate.
- A certificate can be revoked by the issuer or the owner if it is compromised or no longer needed.
- A certificate can be part of a certificate chain, which is a sequence of certificates that link the subject's public key to a trusted root certificate.
- A certificate can be used for different applications, such as web browsing, email, VPN, digital signatures, or code signing.
