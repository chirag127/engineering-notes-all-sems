## Computer System Security

- Computer system security is the protection of computer systems and networks from unauthorized access, modification, disruption, or destruction.
- Computer system security aims to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources in a computer system.
- Computer system security involves the use of various techniques, tools, and policies to prevent, detect, and respond to security threats and incidents.

## Security Goals

- The main security goals of computer system security are:
  - Confidentiality: the prevention of unauthorized disclosure of information.
  - Integrity: the prevention of unauthorized modification or deletion of information.
  - Availability: the prevention of unauthorized denial of access to information or services.
- Other security goals that may be relevant for computer system security are:
  - Authentication: the verification of the identity of a user or a system.
  - Authorization: the granting of access rights and privileges to a user or a system.
  - Accountability: the ability to trace the actions and activities of a user or a system.
  - Non-repudiation: the prevention of denial or dispute of the validity of an action or a transaction.

## Security Threats

- A security threat is any potential event or action that can compromise the security goals of a computer system.
- Security threats can be classified into two categories:
  - Passive threats: the threats that only involve eavesdropping or monitoring of information without modifying or disrupting it. Examples of passive threats are traffic analysis, wiretapping, and sniffing.
  - Active threats: the threats that involve modification, disruption, or destruction of information or services. Examples of active threats are masquerading, replay, modification, denial of service, and malware.

## Security Attacks

- A security attack is any actual attempt to exploit a security vulnerability or weakness in a computer system.
- Security attacks can be classified into two categories:
  - Insider attacks: the attacks that are launched by authorized users or systems within the computer system. Examples of insider attacks are disgruntled employees, malicious insiders, or compromised accounts.
  - Outsider attacks: the attacks that are launched by unauthorized users or systems outside the computer system. Examples of outsider attacks are hackers, crackers, or cybercriminals.

## Security Mechanisms

- A security mechanism is any method or tool that is used to implement or enforce a security policy or goal in a computer system.
- Security mechanisms can be classified into two categories:
  - Preventive mechanisms: the mechanisms that are used to prevent or deter security attacks from occurring. Examples of preventive mechanisms are encryption, authentication, access control, firewalls, and antivirus software.
  - Reactive mechanisms: the mechanisms that are used to detect or respond to security attacks that have occurred. Examples of reactive mechanisms are intrusion detection, intrusion prevention, auditing, logging, and backup.
