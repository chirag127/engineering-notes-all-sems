### Finding vulnerabilities for the notes of the Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and leas in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Secure architecture principles are guidelines for designing a system that is resilient to threats and risks, and that ensures confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources .
- Isolation and leas are two of the secure architecture principles that aim to reduce the attack surface and limit the impact of a breach .
- Isolation means separating different components or layers of a system, such as data, processes, networks, or users, so that they can only communicate through well-defined interfaces and protocols .
- Leas means following the principle of least privilege, which grants the minimum level of access or permissions required for a component or user to perform its function .
- Finding vulnerabilities for the notes of the Unit 3 involves identifying and analyzing the potential weaknesses or flaws in the implementation or design of the system that could compromise the isolation or leas principles.
- Some of the common methods or tools for finding vulnerabilities are:
  - Static analysis: examining the source code or configuration files of the system for errors, bugs, or misconfigurations that could lead to security issues.
  - Dynamic analysis: testing the system at runtime for unexpected or malicious inputs, outputs, or behaviors that could exploit or bypass the security mechanisms.
  - Penetration testing: simulating real-world attacks on the system to discover and exploit the vulnerabilities and assess the impact and severity of the breach.
  - Vulnerability scanning: using automated tools or databases to scan the system for known or common vulnerabilities and generate reports or recommendations for remediation.
  - Threat modeling: analyzing the system from the perspective of an attacker to identify the assets, threats, vulnerabilities, and countermeasures of the system.
