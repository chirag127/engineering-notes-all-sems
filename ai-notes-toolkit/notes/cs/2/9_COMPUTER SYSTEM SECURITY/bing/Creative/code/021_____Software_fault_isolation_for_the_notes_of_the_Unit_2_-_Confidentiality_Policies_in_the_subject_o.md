### Software fault isolation

- Software fault isolation (SFI) is a technique to prevent untrusted or faulty code from compromising the security or reliability of a system.
- SFI works by enforcing a set of rules on the untrusted code, such as restricting its memory access, limiting its control flow, and checking its inputs and outputs.
- SFI can be implemented at different levels, such as the hardware, the operating system, the compiler, or the runtime environment.
- SFI can provide various benefits, such as:
  - Isolating the effects of bugs or attacks in the untrusted code, preventing them from affecting the rest of the system or other applications.
  - Reducing the attack surface of the system, by limiting the privileges and capabilities of the untrusted code.
  - Enabling the reuse of legacy or third-party code, without compromising the security or performance of the system.
  - Facilitating the development and deployment of modular and extensible systems, by allowing the integration of different components with different trust levels.
- SFI can also pose some challenges, such as:
  - Introducing overhead and complexity in the system, due to the additional checks and mechanisms required to enforce the rules on the untrusted code.
  - Requiring modifications or adaptations of the untrusted code, to make it compliant with the rules and compatible with the system.
  - Depending on the assumptions and guarantees of the underlying platform, which may vary across different architectures and environments.
  - Balancing the trade-offs between security, performance, and usability, depending on the goals and requirements of the system and the untrusted code.
