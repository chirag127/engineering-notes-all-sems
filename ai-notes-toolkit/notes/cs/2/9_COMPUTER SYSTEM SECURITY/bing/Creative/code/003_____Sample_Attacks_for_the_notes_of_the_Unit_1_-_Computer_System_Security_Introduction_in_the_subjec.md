### Sample Attacks

A cyber attack is any attempt to expose, alter, disable, destroy, steal or gain unauthorized access to or make unauthorized use of an asset by hacking into a susceptible system. Cyber attacks can target any computer system, network, device, or data.

Some examples of common cyber attacks are:

- **Identity theft, fraud, extortion**: These are attacks that aim to steal personal or financial information from individuals or organizations, and use it for illegal purposes or blackmail. For example, phishing is a technique that uses fraudulent emails or websites to trick users into revealing their passwords, credit card numbers, or other sensitive data.
- **Malware, phishing, spamming, spoofing, spyware, trojans and viruses**: These are malicious software or web scripts that are designed to infect, damage, or monitor a system or network, or to perform unauthorized actions. For example, ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts the victim's files and demands a ransom for their decryption.
- **Stolen hardware, such as laptops or mobile devices**: These are attacks that involve physically stealing or accessing a device that contains sensitive or confidential data, such as personal or business information, or login credentials. For example, a thief may steal a laptop from a hotel room and access the owner's email account or online banking.
- **Denial-of-service and distributed denial-of-service attacks**: These are attacks that aim to disrupt or prevent the normal functioning of a system or network by overwhelming it with traffic or requests. For example, a botnet is a network of compromised computers that can be used to launch a coordinated DDoS attack against a target website, making it inaccessible to legitimate users .
- **Breach of access**: These are attacks that exploit a vulnerability or weakness in a system or network to gain unauthorized access to its resources or data. For example, a hacker may exploit a SQL injection flaw in a web application to execute malicious commands on the database server and retrieve sensitive information.
- **Password sniffing**: These are attacks that intercept and capture the passwords or other authentication data of users or systems as they are transmitted over a network. For example, a hacker may use a packet sniffer to capture the login credentials of a user who is accessing a website over an unsecured Wi-Fi network.
- **System infiltration**: These are attacks that involve gaining persistent or stealthy access to a system or network, and installing backdoors, rootkits, or other malware that allow the attacker to maintain control or monitor the system's activities. For example, a hacker may use a trojan horse to infect a system and create a remote shell that allows the hacker to execute commands on the system.
- **Website defacement**: These are attacks that involve altering or replacing the content or appearance of a website, usually with a political or malicious message. For example, a hacker may deface a government website with a propaganda or a hate speech.

To prevent or mitigate cyber attacks, some of the best practices are:

- Use strong and unique passwords for different accounts and devices, and change them regularly.
- Use encryption, VPN, or HTTPS to protect the data in transit and at rest.
- Use antivirus, firewall, and other security software to protect the system or network from malware and intrusions.
- Update the system or network regularly with the latest security patches and updates.
- Avoid opening or clicking on suspicious links, attachments, or pop-ups in emails or websites.
- Educate the users or employees about the common cyber threats and how to avoid them.
- Backup the data regularly and store it in a secure location.
- Implement a disaster recovery plan and a business continuity plan in case of a cyber attack.
