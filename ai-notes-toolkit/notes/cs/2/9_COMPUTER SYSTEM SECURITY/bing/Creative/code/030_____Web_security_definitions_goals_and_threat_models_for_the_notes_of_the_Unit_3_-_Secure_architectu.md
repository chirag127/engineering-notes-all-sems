# Web security definitions, goals and threat models

Web security is the practice of protecting web applications and services from unauthorized access, misuse, modification, or destruction. Web security aims to achieve the following goals:

- Confidentiality: ensuring that only authorized parties can access the data or resources on the web.
- Integrity: ensuring that the data or resources on the web are not altered or corrupted by unauthorized parties.
- Availability: ensuring that the data or resources on the web are accessible and functional for authorized parties.
- Accountability: ensuring that the actions and events on the web are traceable and auditable by authorized parties.

Threat modeling is a method of analyzing the web security risks and designing countermeasures to prevent or mitigate them. Threat modeling involves the following steps:

- Identify the assets: determine what data or resources on the web are valuable and need protection.
- Identify the threats: determine what types of attacks or incidents can compromise the assets on the web.
- Identify the vulnerabilities: determine what weaknesses or flaws in the web application or service can be exploited by the threats.
- Identify the controls: determine what measures or techniques can be implemented to prevent or reduce the impact of the threats on the assets.
- Prioritize the risks: determine the likelihood and severity of the threats and the effectiveness and cost of the controls, and rank the risks accordingly.

There are different approaches and frameworks for threat modeling, such as:

- STRIDE: a mnemonic that categorizes threats into six types: Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, and Elevation of privilege.
- DREAD: a mnemonic that quantifies threats based on five factors: Damage potential, Reproducibility, Exploitability, Affected users, and Discoverability.
- PASTA: a process-oriented framework that consists of seven stages: Process for Attack Simulation and Threat Analysis.
- OCTAVE: a self-directed framework that focuses on organizational and operational aspects of security: Operationally Critical Threat, Asset, and Vulnerability Evaluation.
- Trike: a risk-based framework that uses a formal model to represent the relationships between assets, threats, and controls.
