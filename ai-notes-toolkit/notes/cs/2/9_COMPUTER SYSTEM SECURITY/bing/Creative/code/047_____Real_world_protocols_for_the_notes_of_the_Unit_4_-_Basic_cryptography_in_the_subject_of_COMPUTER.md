# Real world protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Cryptography is the science of securing information by transforming it into a form that only the intended recipients can understand and use.
- Cryptography is used in many real world applications, such as web APIs, user authentication, digital signatures, encryption, hashing, and blockchain.
- Some of the cryptographic techniques that are commonly used in real world protocols are:

  - Symmetric encryption: This is a method of encrypting and decrypting data using a single secret key that is shared by both parties. Symmetric encryption is fast and efficient, but requires a secure way of distributing the key. Examples of symmetric encryption algorithms are AES, DES, and RC4.
  - Asymmetric encryption: This is a method of encrypting and decrypting data using a pair of keys: a public key and a private key. The public key can be shared with anyone, while the private key is kept secret by the owner. Asymmetric encryption is slower and less efficient than symmetric encryption, but does not require a secure way of distributing the key. Examples of asymmetric encryption algorithms are RSA, ECC, and ElGamal.
  - Hashing: This is a method of transforming any data into a fixed-length string of bits, called a hash or a digest, that uniquely identifies the data. Hashing is irreversible, meaning that it is impossible to recover the original data from the hash. Hashing is used to verify the integrity and authenticity of data, as well as to index and store data efficiently. Examples of hashing algorithms are SHA-2, SHA-3, and BLAKE2.
  - Digital signatures: This is a method of proving the identity and authenticity of a message or a document by using asymmetric encryption and hashing. The sender of the message or the document generates a hash of the data, and then encrypts the hash with their private key. The receiver of the message or the document decrypts the hash with the sender's public key, and then compares it with the hash of the data. If the hashes match, the message or the document is authentic and has not been tampered with. Examples of digital signature schemes are RSA, DSA, and ECDSA.
  - Encryption modes: This is a method of applying symmetric encryption to data in different ways, depending on the size and structure of the data. Encryption modes affect the security and performance of the encryption process, as well as the resistance to various attacks. Examples of encryption modes are ECB, CBC, CTR, and GCM.
  - Key exchange: This is a method of establishing a shared secret key between two parties over an insecure channel, using asymmetric encryption and hashing. Key exchange is used to enable symmetric encryption for secure communication. Examples of key exchange protocols are Diffie-Hellman, ECDH, and RSA-KEM.
  - Authentication: This is a method of verifying the identity of a user or a device, using cryptographic techniques such as hashing, digital signatures, and encryption. Authentication is used to prevent unauthorized access to resources and services. Examples of authentication protocols are OAuth, OpenID Connect, and Kerberos.
  - Blockchain: This is a method of creating and maintaining a distributed ledger of transactions, using cryptographic techniques such as hashing, digital signatures, and encryption. Blockchain is used to enable trustless and decentralized systems for various applications, such as cryptocurrencies, smart contracts, and supply chains. Examples of blockchain platforms are Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Hyperledger.
