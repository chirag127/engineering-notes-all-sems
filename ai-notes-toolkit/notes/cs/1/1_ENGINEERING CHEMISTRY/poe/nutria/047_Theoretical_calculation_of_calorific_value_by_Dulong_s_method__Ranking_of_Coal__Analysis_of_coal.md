
# Theoretical Calculation of Calorific Value by Dulong's Method
Calorific value is the amount of energy released when a unit mass of fuel is burned. Dulong's method is a simple theoretical method used to calculate the calorific value of a fuel from its elemental composition. This method is based on the assumption that the calorific value of a fuel is the sum of the calorific values of its constituent elements. The formula for calculating the calorific value using Dulong's method is as follows:

Calorific value (in kcal/kg) = 3.7 x Carbon content + 8.8 x Hydrogen content + 25.2 x Oxygen content + 11.3 x Sulphur content

# Ranking of Coal
Coal is ranked based on its calorific value, volatile matter content, ash content and moisture content. The higher the calorific value, the higher the ranking of the coal. The calorific value is determined by Dulong's method, as described above.

# Analysis of Coal
The analysis of coal involves the determination of its calorific value, volatile matter content, ash content and moisture content. The calorific value is determined by Dulong's method, as described above. The volatile matter content is determined by heating the coal sample in an oven at a temperature of 950˚C. The ash content is determined by heating the coal sample in an oven at a temperature of 950˚C and then measuring the mass of the ash residue. The moisture content is determined by heating the coal sample in an oven at a temperature of 105˚C and then measuring the mass of the water evaporated.
