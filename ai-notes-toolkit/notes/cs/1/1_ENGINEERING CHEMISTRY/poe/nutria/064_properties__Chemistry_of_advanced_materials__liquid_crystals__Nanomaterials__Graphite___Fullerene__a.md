
# Properties, Chemistry of Advanced Materials (Liquid Crystals, Nanomaterials, Graphite & Fullerene)

1. Liquid Crystals: Liquid crystals are materials that exhibit an ordered arrangement of molecules in the liquid state. They are composed of organic molecules that are arranged in a specific way, allowing them to have properties of both liquids and solids. Examples of liquid crystals include the liquid crystal display (LCD) used in digital watches and televisions.

2. Nanomaterials: Nanomaterials are materials that are composed of particles that are less than 100 nanometers in size. These materials have unique properties due to their small size, such as increased surface area, increased reactivity, and increased strength. Examples of nanomaterials include carbon nanotubes, quantum dots, and graphene.

3. Graphite: Graphite is a form of carbon with a layered structure. It is a soft, dark gray solid with a metallic luster. It is used in many applications, such as lubricants, batteries, and pencils.

4. Fullerenes: Fullerenes are molecules composed of carbon atoms in the shape of a hollow sphere, ellipsoid, or tube. They are used in a variety of applications, such as in medicine, electronics, and nanotechnology.
