
# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical Problems

* Ultraviolet (UV) spectroscopy is a technique used to study the structure and composition of molecules. It involves the use of light in the ultraviolet region of the electromagnetic spectrum, which is absorbed by molecules in the sample, causing them to vibrate. The vibrations are then detected and used to identify the molecules present in the sample.

* Infrared (IR) spectroscopy is a technique used to study the structure and composition of molecules. It involves the use of light in the infrared region of the electromagnetic spectrum, which is absorbed by molecules in the sample, causing them to vibrate. The vibrations are then detected and used to identify the molecules present in the sample.

* Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy is a technique used to study the structure and composition of molecules. It involves the use of a magnetic field to align the nuclei of atoms in a sample, and then detecting the energy released when the nuclei return to their original alignment. The energy released is used to identify the molecules present in the sample.

* Numerical problems are mathematical problems that require numerical solutions. These problems can involve equations, inequalities, systems of equations, and other mathematical concepts. Solutions to these problems involve finding the numerical values of unknown variables, or the numerical values of certain parameters.
