
## Determination of Calorific Values and Analysis of Coal

1. Calorific value is a measure of the energy content of a fuel, expressed as the amount of heat released when a given weight of fuel is completely burned.
2. The calorific value of coal is generally determined by bomb calorimetry, which is a process in which the heat released by the combustion of a sample of coal is measured in a bomb calorimeter.
3. The bomb calorimeter consists of a steel container, known as a bomb, which is surrounded by an insulated jacket. The sample of coal is placed in the bomb and ignited. As the coal burns, the heat released is measured by the calorimeter.
4. The calorific value of coal is usually expressed in units of kilojoules per kilogram (kJ/kg) or British thermal units per pound (BTU/lb).
5. In addition to determining the calorific value of coal, it is also important to analyze the coal for other properties such as moisture content, ash content, volatile matter, and fixed carbon.
6. Moisture content is a measure of the amount of water present in the coal, and is usually expressed in units of percent by weight.
7. Ash content is a measure of the amount of inorganic material present in the coal, and is usually expressed in units of percent by weight.
8. Volatile matter is a measure of the amount of hydrocarbons present in the coal that are released when the coal is heated, and is usually expressed in units of percent by weight.
9. Fixed carbon is a measure of the amount of carbon in the coal that remains after the volatile matter has been burned off, and is usually expressed in units of percent by weight.
