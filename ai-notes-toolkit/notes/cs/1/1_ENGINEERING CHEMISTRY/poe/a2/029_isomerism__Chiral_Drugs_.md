# Isomerism, Chiral Drugs

## Isomerism
- Isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.
- Structural isomers have the same atoms but different arrangements e.g. butane and methylpropane.
- Stereoisomers have the same atom connectivity but differ in the arrangement of atoms in space.
- Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images that are chiral e.g. left and right hands.
- Diastereomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images e.g. cis-trans isomers.

## Chiral Drugs
- Many drugs have centres of chirality so can exist as enantiomers.
- Biological molecules are chiral so can interact differently with the two enantiomers of a chiral drug.
- One enantiomer may have the desired therapeutic effect while the other may be less active or have adverse effects.
- Chiral drugs are often administered as racemic mixtures of both enantiomers. Separation of enantiomers can allow administration of the therapeutically active enantiomer only but this can be more expensive to produce.
- Examples of chiral drugs include ibuprofen, naproxen and thalidomide. Thalidomide caused birth defects when administered as a racemate due to the other enantiomer's teratogenic effects. The (S)-enantiomer is now used therapeutically.
