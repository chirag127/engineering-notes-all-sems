# Proximate and Ultimate Analysis Method

1. Proximate analysis: It is a method to determine the amount of fixed carbon, volatile matter and ash content in a fuel.
- Fixed carbon: It is the non-volatile carbonaceous solid left after volatile materials are driven off from a fuel during combustion. It is measured by strongly heating the fuel in the absence of air and collecting the remaining solid residue.
- Volatile matter: It refers to the gaseous combustion products evolved from a fuel when it is heated strongly in the absence of air. It is generally hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen and sulfur compounds which are driven off as gases by heating.
- Ash: It refers to the inorganic residue remaining after complete combustion of a fuel. It consists of metallic oxides present in the fuel.

2. Ultimate analysis: It determines the elemental composition of a fuel i.e. percentage by mass of carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen and sulfur. It provides a deeper insight into the fuel composition. The elemental composition is generally determined by chemical methods.

# Chemistry of Biogas

- Biogas is produced by the anaerobic digestion of biomass such as animal dung, sewage, municipal waste, agricultural residues, etc.
- The main constituents of biogas are:
-- Methane: 55-65%
-- Carbon dioxide: 35-45%
-- Hydrogen: 0-1%
-- Nitrogen: 0-1%
-- Oxygen: 0-0.5%
-- H2S and other trace gases: 0-3%
- The high methane content makes biogas a good renewable source of energy which can be used for heating, lighting, running vehicles, electricity generation, etc.
- The nutrient-rich digested biomass left behind can be used as an excellent organic fertilizer.
