### Liquid Crystals; Introduction, Types and Applications of liquid crystals, Industrially

- Liquid crystals are organic materials that exhibit properties intermediate between liquids and crystals. They have molecular order but flow like liquids.
- The two types of liquid crystals are thermotropic and lyotropic. Thermotropic liquid crystals change properties with temperature whereas lyotropic liquid crystals change properties with concentration.
- Nematic, smectic and cholesteric are the three forms of thermotropic liquid crystals. Nematic liquid crystals have molecules aligned in the same direction. Smectic liquid crystals have molecules arranged in layers. Cholesteric liquid crystals have molecules arranged in a helical structure.
- Liquid crystals have applications in LCD displays, thermometers, watches, calculators, etc. They are used for their optical and electrical properties which can be controlled by external stimuli like electric fields or temperature.
- In LCD displays, liquid crystals are used for their ability to rotate polarized light. The alignment of liquid crystal molecules is controlled using electric fields which changes their optical properties and produces images on the display.
- The unique ordered structure and responsive nature of liquid crystals enable their use in various applications as sensitive detection systems and fast-acting switches. Their intermediary properties open up possibilities for new soft materials with tunable functionality.
