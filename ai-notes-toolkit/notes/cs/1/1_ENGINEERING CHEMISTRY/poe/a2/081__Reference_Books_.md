## Reference Books:

1. Introduction to Algorithms by Thomas H. Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest, and Clifford Stein.
- This is a comprehensive book covering fundamental algorithms and data structures. It is suitable for both undergraduate and graduate level.
- The algorithms and data structures are explained in a clean, elegant pseudocode, making them easy to understand.
- Complex topics are explained with great clarity and depth. It includes such topics as sorting, searching, graph algorithms, and string processing.

2. Operating System Concepts by Abraham Silberschatz, Peter B. Galvin, and Greg Gagne.
- This is a highly comprehensive textbook covering all essential topics in operating systems.
- It explains concepts with a balance of theory and practical applications. The explanations are clear and thorough.
- It covers processes, threads, concurrency, deadlocks, CPU scheduling, memory management, file systems, and more.
- The book includes illustrations, examples, and end-of-chapter exercises. It is suitable for both undergraduate and graduate level.

3. Introduction to the Theory of Computation by Michael Sipser.
- This is a rigorous but accessible introduction to computability theory and formal languages.
- It covers topics like finite automata, Turing machines, decidability, NP-completeness, and other key topics.
- The book takes a theoretical approach but explains topics and concepts clearly with helpful examples.
- It is suitable as a textbook for undergraduate or graduate level courses on theory of computation.
