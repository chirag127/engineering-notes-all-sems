# Stereochemistry

Stereochemistry is the study of the three-dimensional arrangement of atoms in molecules and their manipulation. It is useful for identifying compounds and elements.

## Identifying Enantiomers
Enantiomers are stereoisomers that are mirror images of each other and non-superimposable. They have centers of chirality and rotate polarized light in opposite directions. To identify enantiomers:

1. Locate the chiral center(s): Carbons bonded to four different atoms/groups.
2. Draw mirror image arrangements: Switch positions of groups around chiral center.
3. Check for superimposability: If mirror images cannot be overlapped, the molecules are enantiomers.

## Identifying Diastereomers
Diastereomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images and have centers of chirality or double bonds. To identify diastereomers:

1. Locate multiple chiral centers and/or double bonds.
2. Arrange groups in 3D space in different ways.
3. Check if arrangements are not mirror images and cannot be superimposed. If so, they are diastereomers.

## Identifying Elements Using Stereochemistry
The stereochemistry of a molecule can be used to identify elements present. For example:

- Tetrahedral carbons: C
- Flat trigonal planar: sp2 hybridized C, N, O
- Octahedral: sp3d2 hybridized C, Si, N, P, S
- Square planar: d8 configuration transition metals like Pt, Pd
