# Polymers

1. Polymers are long chain molecules composed of repeating structural units. These structural units are monomers that are joined together by covalent bonds to form a polymer.
2. The properties of polymers depend on their molecular structure. The molecular structure is determined by the monomers used, the order in which they are arranged, and the bonding between them.
3. There are two main types of polymers:

- Thermoplastics: Polymers that soften on heating and harden on cooling. They can be remelted and recast several times. Examples include polyethylene, polyvinyl chloride or PVC, polystyrene, etc.
- Thermosets: Polymers that harden irreversibly after heating. They cannot be remelted and recast. Examples include epoxy resins, polyurethanes, etc.

4. Polymers have a very wide range of applications due to their useful properties:

- Plastics: Used to make storage containers, pipes, insulation for wires, etc. due to properties like durability, lightweight, and low cost.
- Adhesives: Glues are polymers that bond surfaces together.
- Coatings: Polymers are used as paints and varnishes to protect surfaces.
- Fibers: Polymer fibers are used to make fabrics, ropes, etc. Nylon and polyester are common examples.
- Medical Applications: Contact lenses, heart valves, etc. are made from biocompatible polymers.

5. The widespread use of polymers has environmental concerns due to their non-biodegradable nature. Bio-based and biodegradable polymers are being researched to address these issues.
