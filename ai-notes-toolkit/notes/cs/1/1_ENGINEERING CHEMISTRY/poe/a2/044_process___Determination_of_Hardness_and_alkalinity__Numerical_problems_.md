# Process, Determination of Hardness and Alkalinity, Numerical Problems

1. Process
- Water is passed through ion exchange resins to remove calcium and magnesium ions responsible for hardness
- Soap solution is added to raw water sample and lather formation is observed to determine hardness categorically
- Titrimetric analysis involving EDTA is used to determine total hardness in terms of calcium carbonate

2. Determination of Hardness
- Temporary hardness is determined by boiling the water sample and observing the drop in hardness. The original hardness minus hardness after boiling gives temporary hardness
- Permanent hardness is determined by passing water through ion exchange resins and observing the residual hardness. This gives permanent hardness
- Total hardness is sum of temporary and permanent hardness

3. Determination of Alkalinity
- Methyl orange or phenolphthalein is added to water as pH indicator
- Water sample is titrated against HCl till the first colour change of indicator
- Volume of HCl used gives alkalinity in terms of CaCO3 which is usually in the range of 30-300 ppm for potable water

4. Numerical Problems
- A water sample has total hardness of 300 ppm. After boiling, the hardness reduces to 100 ppm. Determine temporary and permanent hardness.
Temporary hardness = Original hardness - Hardness after boiling
       = 300 ppm - 100 ppm = 200 ppm
Permanent hardness = 100 ppm
- A water sample requires 25 mL of 0.1 N HCl for alkalinity titration. Determine alkalinity in ppm.
1 mL of 0.1 N HCl neutralizes 4.6 mg of CaCO3.
25 mL will neutralize 25 x 4.6 = 115 mg of CaCO3
Alkalinity = 115 ppm
