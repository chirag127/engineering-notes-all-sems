# Stereochemistry

1. Chirality: A molecule is chiral if it cannot be superimposed on its mirror image. Chiral molecules have an 'handedness' - either left-handed or right-handed. Examples of chiral molecules are sugars like glucose and amino acids.
2. Enantiomers: Chiral molecules that are mirror images of each other are called enantiomers. They have the same chemical and physical properties except for the way they rotate plane-polarized light.
3. Diastereomers: Stereoisomers that are not enantiomers are called diastereomers. They have different chemical and physical properties. Cis-trans isomers and conformational isomers are types of diastereomers.
4. Cis-trans isomers: Molecules with the same structural formula but differ in the arrangement of atoms in space are called cis-trans isomers. They arise from restricted rotation about double bonds or rings. Cis isomers have functional groups on the same side and trans have them on the opposite sides.
5. Conformation: The 3D arrangement of atoms of a molecule is called its conformation. It can change by rotation about single bonds resulting in different conformers with varying stability and energies. The most stable conformer is the one with minimum energy and is the predominant form.
