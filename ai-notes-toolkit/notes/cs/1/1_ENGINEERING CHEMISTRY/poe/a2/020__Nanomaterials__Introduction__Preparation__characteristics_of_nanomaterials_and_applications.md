### Nanomaterials; Introduction, Preparation, characteristics of nanomaterials and applications

**Introduction**
- Nanomaterials refer to materials that have particles or constituents of nanoscale dimensions, typically 1 to 100 nanometers.
- At the nanoscale, the properties of materials differ from the properties of individual atoms or molecules or bulk matter. These unique properties make nanomaterials widely useful.

**Preparation of Nanomaterials**
- Top-down approach: Milling and grinding of bulk materials to produce nanoparticles. E.g. ball milling, attrition milling.
- Bottom-up approach: Building nanoparticles from atoms or molecules. E.g. chemical synthesis, precipitation reactions, sol-gel process.

**Characteristics of Nanomaterials**
- Large surface area to volume ratio: As size decreases, surface area increases dramatically leading to more reactivity.
- Quantum effects: Nanoparticles exhibit quantum effects like quantum confinement leading to changes in optical, electrical and magnetic properties.
- Interfacial phenomena: Interfaces play an important role in determining properties of nanocomposites leading to more interactions between components.

**Applications of Nanomaterials**
- Nanomedicine: Drug delivery, biosensors, tissue engineering.
- Electronics: Chips, displays, storage devices.
- Catalysis: Nanoparticles have high catalytic efficiency due to high surface area.
- Coatings and films: Light-weight, durable, scratch-resistant.
- Composite materials: Lightweight but strong materials with enhanced properties.
- Environmental remediation: Removal of contaminants from water using nanoparticles.
