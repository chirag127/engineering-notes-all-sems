### Chemistry of Advanced Materials:

1. Carbon Nanotubes: Carbon nanotubes are allotropes of carbon with a cylindrical nanostructure. They have exceptional properties such as high strength, flexibility, thermal and electrical conductivity. They are widely used in electronics, optics, materials science, biology, etc.

2. Graphene: Graphene is a two-dimensional form of carbon consisting of a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice. It is the thinnest and lightest material and also the strongest material ever discovered. It has excellent optical transmittance and high electrical conductivity making it suitable for applications in electronics, computing, and other areas.

3. Metamaterials: Metamaterials are artificial materials engineered to exhibit properties that may not be found in nature. They are designed by structuring the material at an atomic or molecular level. Metamaterials can manipulate electromagnetic waves, sound waves, and other physical phenomena in unprecedented ways. They enable technologies like invisibility cloaking, superlenses, and other applications.

4. Self-healing Materials: Self-healing materials have the ability to automatically repair damage caused by wear or external stress. They contain healing agents that are activated when damage occurs, repairing the material. Self-healing provides enhanced material longevity, reliability, and safety. Various self-healing materials such as polymers, ceramics, metals, and concrete are being developed for applications where damage tolerance is critical.

5. Biomaterials: Biomaterials are synthetic materials used to interact with biological systems. They are widely used in medical applications such as implants, prosthetics, drug delivery, tissue engineering, etc. Biomaterials should be non-toxic, non-carcinogenic, and non-inflammatory. They are often designed to degrade over time and be replaced with natural tissue. Common biomaterials include metals, ceramics, polymers, and composites.
