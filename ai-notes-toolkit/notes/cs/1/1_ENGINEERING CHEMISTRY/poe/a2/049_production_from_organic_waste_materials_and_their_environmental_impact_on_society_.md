 Here is a formal write-up on the topic of organic waste material production and its environmental impact on society:

I. Introduction
- Organic waste materials such as food scraps, yard waste, and sewage sludge can be converted into useful products through various production processes.
- These conversion processes have environmental impacts, both positive and negative, that must be evaluated.

II. Production processes
A. Composting
- Organic waste is decomposed by microorganisms into a nutrient-rich material that can be used as a soil amendment.
- Positive impacts: Reduces amount of waste sent to landfills; produces a useful product.
- Negative impacts: Can produce odor and greenhouse gases if not properly maintained; heavy metal contaminants may remain in the compost.

B. Anaerobic digestion
- Organic waste is broken down by microorganisms in an oxygen-free environment to produce biogas that can be used for energy and a nutrient-rich digestate.
- Positive impacts: Generates renewable energy; digestate can be used as a fertilizer.
- Negative impacts: Requires significant resources to operate; can produce odor if not properly maintained.

III. Environmental impacts on society
- Production of organic waste materials reduces burden on landfills and incineration, conserving resources.
- Useful products such as compost, digestate, and biofuels are created, which can improve soil health, provide energy, and cycle nutrients.
- However, there are costs to implementing and operating organic waste processing facilities, and improper operation can negatively impact the environment with odors, pests, and other issues. Regulations help minimize negative impacts.

IV. Conclusion
- While the production of resources from organic waste shows environmental benefit, proper system design, implementation, and operation are critical to maximize benefits and minimize harms to society.  With continued improvements, these resource recovery methods can play an important role in sustainable waste management.
