### Corrosion: Introduction to corrosion, Types of corrosion, Cause of corrosion, Corrosion

1. Introduction to Corrosion: Corrosion is the gradual destruction of materials by chemical reaction with their environment. Almost all metals can suffer from corrosion. Corrosion causes significant material damage or loss to materials. As metals corrode, the mechanical properties are degraded until the structure fails.

2. Types of Corrosion: The most common types of corrosion are:
- Uniform corrosion: Corrosion occurs uniformly across the surface of the metal. Rusting of iron is a common example of uniform corrosion.
- Galvanic corrosion: Two dissimilar metals in contact with each other undergo corrosion. The less noble metal acts as the anode and corrodes faster.
- Pitting corrosion: Corrosion occurs in the form of pits or holes in the metal surface. Pitting can penetrate deep into the metal and cause failure.
- Crevice corrosion: Corrosion occurs in confined spaces or crevices where the electrolyte is stagnant. Crevices create localized corrosion cells leading to rapid metal loss.
- Stress corrosion cracking: Corrosion combined with tensile stress leads to cracking of metals. Certain metals are susceptible to SCC in specific environments.

3. Cause of Corrosion: The three conditions required for corrosion are:
1. Presence of an anode (metal that corrodes): The anode in a corrosion cell is the metal that corrodes.
2. Presence of an electrolyte: An electrolyte is required to conduct electric current between the anode and cathode. Electrolytes can be acids, bases or salts in aqueous solutions or humid environments.
3. Presence of a cathode (metal that does not corrode): The cathode is the other metal surface that completes the circuit. It does not corrode and can be the same or a different metal as the anode.

4. Prevention of Corrosion: Corrosion can be prevented or mitigated using methods like:
- Material selection: Choose corrosion resistant metals or alloys.
- Coatings: Use protective coatings such as paints or other barrier layers.
- Cathodic protection: Supply external electric current to make the structure a cathode and reduce corrosion.
- Chemical addition: Use corrosion inhibitors or antioxidants that reduce the corrosion rate.
- Control environment: Control moisture, oxygen, acidity, and other factors that accelerate corrosion.
