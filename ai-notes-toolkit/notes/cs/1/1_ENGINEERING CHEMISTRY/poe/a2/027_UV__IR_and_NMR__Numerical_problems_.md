UV spectroscopy:

- UV radiation has wavelengths in the range of 100-400 nm.
- Absorption of UV radiation leads to excitation of electrons from the ground state to a higher energy state.
- The amount of absorption depends on the presence of chromophores in the molecule that can absorb UV radiation.
- Common chromophores include double bonds (C=C) and triple bonds (C≡C).
- The position and intensity of absorption peaks can be used to determine structural features of the molecule.

IR spectroscopy:

- IR radiation has wavelengths in the range of 700 nm to 1 mm.
- Absorption of IR radiation leads to vibrational excitation of bonds in the molecule.
- The amount of absorption at a particular IR wavelength depends on the types of bonds in the molecule and their vibrational modes.
- Common vibrations that absorb IR include stretching and bending vibrations of bonds between atoms like C-H, O-H, N-H, etc.
- The position and intensity of absorption peaks can be used to identify functional groups in the molecule.

NMR spectroscopy:

- NMR spectroscopy uses radiofrequency radiation to excite nuclear spin states of atoms, usually hydrogen (1H) or carbon (13C).
- The chemical environment of the atoms affects the resonance frequency of their nuclear spins.
- By detecting the resonance frequency, NMR can probe the local chemical environment of hydrogen or carbon atoms in a molecule.
- NMR spectra show peaks at different positions corresponding to nonequivalent atoms in the molecule, allowing identification of molecular structure.
