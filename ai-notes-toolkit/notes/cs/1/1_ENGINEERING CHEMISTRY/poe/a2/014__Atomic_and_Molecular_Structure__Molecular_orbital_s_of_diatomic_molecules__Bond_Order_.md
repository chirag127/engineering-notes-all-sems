### Atomic and Molecular Structure: Molecular orbital’s of diatomic molecules, Bond Order,

1. Molecular orbitals are formed by the combination of atomic orbitals of the constituent atoms in a molecule. For homonuclear diatomic molecules like H2, N2, etc. having equal energy atomic orbitals, the molecular orbitals are formed by the linear combination of atomic orbitals.

2. The molecular orbitals so formed are filled with electrons in the increasing order of their energy. The bonding molecular orbitals have lower energy and are more stable than the anti-bonding molecular orbitals which have higher energy.

3. The bond order is defined as the difference between the number of bonding electrons and the number of anti-bonding electrons. Bond order is directly proportional to the stability and strength of the bond. A bond order of one corresponds to a single bond, two to a double bond and three to a triple bond.

4. In hydrogen molecule, the 1s atomic orbitals of the two atoms combine to form two molecular orbitals - bonding (lower energy) and antibonding (higher energy). The bonding molecular orbital is filled with two electrons resulting in a bond order of 1 and a stable H2 molecule.

5. In nitrogen molecule, each nitrogen atom contributes five electrons. The molecular orbitals formed are filled as: bonding1s2, bonding2s2, antibonding2s2. This results in a bond order of 3 and a triple bond between the two nitrogen atoms.
