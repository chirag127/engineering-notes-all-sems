### Chemistry of Engineering Materials:

1. Metals: Metals are crystalline solids with metallic bonding between atoms. The bonding is delocalized and electrons are free to move throughout the metal. This allows for electrical and thermal conductivity. Alloying different metals allows for tuning of material properties. Common engineering metals include steel, aluminum, titanium, copper, etc.

2. Polymers: Polymers are long chain macromolecules composed of repeating subunits. They can be classified as thermoplastics or thermosets based on their processing abilities. Thermoplastics soften upon heating and harden upon cooling, allowing for recycling and remolding. Common polymers include polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene, polyvinyl chloride, etc. Polymer properties are highly dependent on molecular weight, crystallinity, and chemical structure.

3. Ceramics: Ceramics are inorganic and nonmetallic solids made of metallic or covalent bonded molecules. They exhibit high hardness, strength, and heat resistance. Common ceramics include cement, glass, silicon carbide, alumina, silicon nitride, etc. Properties are dependent on composition, grain size, and defect concentration. Ceramics can withstand high temperatures but are brittle.

4. Composites: Composites are made of two or more constituent materials with different properties. They allow for combining properties and optimizing materials for applications. Fiber-reinforced composites contain fibers such as carbon or glass in a polymer or ceramic matrix. Particulate composites contain particles to modify properties. Composites can exhibit properties of multiple constituents and are lightweight but expensive.
