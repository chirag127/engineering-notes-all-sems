### Stereochemistry: Optical isomerism in compounds without chiral carbon, Geometrical

1. Optical isomerism can exist even without chiral carbon. This is possible due to constrained geometries or presence of double bonds.
2. In cycloalkanes, if the ring contains 5 or more carbons, they can have rotational isomers. One particular arrangement may be more stable due to overlap of orbitals leading to restricted rotation. Such isomers are optical isomers and are nonsuperimposable.
3. In alkenes with double bonds, if the groups on either side of double bond are different, the double bond cannot rotate and can lead to cis-trans isomers which are geometrical isomers. They are nonsuperimposable mirror images and hence optically active.
4. For a carbon-carbon double bond, the cis isomer has both similar groups on the same side of the double bond while in trans, similar groups lie on opposite sides of the double bond.
5. Like enantiomers, cis-trans isomers also have identical physical properties except for their interaction with polarized light. They also have different chemical reactivities.
6. Other examples include allene and cumulene which can also exhibit optical isomerism due to their rigid structures where free rotation is not possible.
