### Atomic and Molecular Structure: Molecular orbitals of diatomic molecules, Bond Order

Molecular orbitals are formed when atomic orbitals combine. In diatomic molecules, the atomic orbitals of two atoms combine to form molecular orbitals. These molecular orbitals can be bonding, non-bonding, or anti-bonding.

- Bonding molecular orbitals are lower in energy than the atomic orbitals from which they are formed. Electrons in bonding orbitals stabilize the molecule.
- Non-bonding molecular orbitals have the same energy as the atomic orbitals from which they are formed. Electrons in non-bonding orbitals do not affect the stability of the molecule.
- Anti-bonding molecular orbitals are higher in energy than the atomic orbitals from which they are formed. Electrons in anti-bonding orbitals destabilize the molecule.

The bond order is a measure of the strength of a chemical bond. It is calculated as the number of bonding electrons minus the number of anti-bonding electrons, divided by two. A higher bond order indicates a stronger bond.

For example, in the case of a diatomic molecule such as O2, the bond order is 2, indicating a double bond between the two oxygen atoms. In the case of N2, the bond order is 3, indicating a triple bond between the two nitrogen atoms.
