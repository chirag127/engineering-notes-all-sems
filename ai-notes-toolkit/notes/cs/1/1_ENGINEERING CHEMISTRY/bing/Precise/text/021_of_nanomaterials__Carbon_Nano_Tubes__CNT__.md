# Nanomaterials: Carbon Nano Tubes (CNT)

- Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) are cylindrical molecules made of carbon atoms, with diameters typically measuring in the nanometers.
- CNTs exhibit extraordinary strength and unique electrical properties, and are efficient conductors of heat.
- They have potential applications in nanotechnology, electronics, optics, and other fields of materials science.
- CNTs can be single-walled (SWCNTs) or multi-walled (MWCNTs).
- The structure of a CNT can be described by a vector (n,m) which represents the number of unit vectors along two directions in the honeycomb crystal lattice of graphene.
- CNTs can be synthesized using various methods, including chemical vapor deposition (CVD), arc discharge, and laser ablation.
- CNTs have potential applications in a wide range of fields, including electronics, energy storage, drug delivery, and structural materials.
- However, there are still challenges to be addressed in the large-scale production and application of CNTs, including the control of their structure and properties, and the potential environmental and health risks associated with their use.
