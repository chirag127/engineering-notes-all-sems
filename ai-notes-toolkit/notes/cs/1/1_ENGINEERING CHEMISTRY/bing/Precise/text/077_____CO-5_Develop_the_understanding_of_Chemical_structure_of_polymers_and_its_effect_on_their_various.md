### CO-5 Develop the understanding of Chemical structure of polymers and its effect on their various properties

1. Polymers are large molecules made up of repeating units called monomers.
2. The chemical structure of a polymer refers to the arrangement of its monomers and the type of bonds that hold them together.
3. The chemical structure of a polymer affects its physical and mechanical properties, such as its strength, elasticity, and melting point.
4. The type of bonds between monomers can affect the polymer's properties. For example, polymers with covalent bonds between monomers are generally stronger and more rigid than those with weaker bonds, such as hydrogen bonds or van der Waals forces.
5. The arrangement of monomers can also affect the polymer's properties. For example, linear polymers, where monomers are arranged in a straight chain, are generally stronger and more rigid than branched polymers, where monomers are arranged in a branched structure.
6. The presence of functional groups, such as carboxyl or amino groups, can also affect the polymer's properties by introducing additional bonding or interactions between monomers.
7. The degree of polymerization, or the number of monomers in a polymer chain, can also affect its properties. Generally, as the degree of polymerization increases, the polymer becomes stronger and more rigid.
8. In summary, the chemical structure of a polymer, including the type of bonds between monomers, the arrangement of monomers, the presence of functional groups, and the degree of polymerization, can all affect its physical and mechanical properties. Understanding these relationships is important for the design and development of new polymers with desired properties.
