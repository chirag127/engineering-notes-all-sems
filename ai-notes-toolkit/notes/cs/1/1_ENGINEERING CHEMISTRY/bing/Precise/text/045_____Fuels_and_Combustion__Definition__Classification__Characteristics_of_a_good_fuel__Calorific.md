### Fuels and Combustion

- **Definition**: A fuel is any material that can be burned to release energy in the form of heat. Combustion is the process of burning a fuel in the presence of oxygen to produce heat and light.

- **Classification**: Fuels can be classified into three main categories: solid, liquid, and gaseous. Solid fuels include coal, wood, and charcoal. Liquid fuels include petroleum, diesel, and kerosene. Gaseous fuels include natural gas, propane, and hydrogen.

- **Characteristics of a good fuel**: A good fuel should have a high calorific value, which means it should release a large amount of heat when burned. It should also be easy to ignite, burn cleanly without producing harmful emissions, and be readily available at a reasonable cost.

- **Calorific value**: The calorific value of a fuel is the amount of heat energy released when a unit quantity of the fuel is burned completely. It is usually measured in units of energy per unit mass, such as joules per gram or calories per gram. The higher the calorific value of a fuel, the more heat it will release when burned.
