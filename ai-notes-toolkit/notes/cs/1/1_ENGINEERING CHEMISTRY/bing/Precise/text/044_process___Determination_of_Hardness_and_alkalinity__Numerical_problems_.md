# Process of Determination of Hardness and Alkalinity

Hardness and alkalinity are important parameters in water quality analysis. Hardness refers to the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium ions in water, while alkalinity refers to the water's ability to neutralize acids.

## Determination of Hardness

1. One of the most common methods for determining the hardness of water is by using a titration method with ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) as the titrant.
2. A buffer solution is added to the water sample to adjust the pH to around 10.
3. A few drops of an indicator, such as Eriochrome Black T, are added to the sample. The indicator will change color from red to blue when all the calcium and magnesium ions have been complexed by the EDTA.
4. The EDTA solution is then added to the sample until the color change occurs, indicating the end point of the titration.
5. The volume of EDTA used is then used to calculate the hardness of the water sample.

## Determination of Alkalinity

1. Alkalinity is determined by titrating a water sample with a strong acid, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, to a pH of around 4.5.
2. An indicator, such as phenolphthalein or bromcresol green, is used to indicate the end point of the titration.
3. The volume of acid used is then used to calculate the alkalinity of the water sample.
