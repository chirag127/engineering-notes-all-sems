# Proximate and Ultimate Analysis Method

Proximate analysis is a method used to determine the composition of coal, biomass, and other solid fuels. It measures the following parameters:
- Moisture content
- Volatile matter
- Fixed carbon
- Ash content

Ultimate analysis, on the other hand, determines the elemental composition of the fuel, including:
- Carbon
- Hydrogen
- Nitrogen
- Sulfur
- Oxygen

# Numerical Problems

Numerical problems related to proximate and ultimate analysis involve calculating the composition of the fuel based on the given data. For example, if the moisture content, volatile matter, fixed carbon, and ash content of a coal sample are given, the heating value of the coal can be calculated.

# Chemistry of Biogas

Biogas is a renewable energy source produced by the anaerobic digestion of organic matter. The main components of biogas are methane (CH4) and carbon dioxide (CO2). The anaerobic digestion process involves the breakdown of organic matter by microorganisms in the absence of oxygen. The process can be divided into four stages:
1. Hydrolysis
2. Acidogenesis
3. Acetogenesis
4. Methanogenesis

During hydrolysis, complex organic compounds are broken down into simpler compounds. In acidogenesis, these simple compounds are further broken down into volatile fatty acids and other byproducts. During acetogenesis, the volatile fatty acids are converted into acetic acid, hydrogen, and carbon dioxide. Finally, during methanogenesis, methane is produced from acetic acid, hydrogen, and carbon dioxide.
