### CO-3 Utilize the theory of construction of electrodes, batteries and fuel cells in redesigning new engineering

1. **Electrodes**: Electrodes are conductive materials that allow the flow of electric current in and out of a cell or battery. They are typically made of metal or carbon and are designed to have a high surface area to facilitate the transfer of electrons.
2. **Batteries**: Batteries are devices that store electrical energy in the form of chemical energy. They consist of one or more electrochemical cells, each of which contains two electrodes separated by an electrolyte. The chemical reactions that occur within the cell generate a flow of electrons from one electrode to the other, producing an electric current.
3. **Fuel Cells**: Fuel cells are electrochemical devices that convert the chemical energy of a fuel directly into electricity. Like batteries, they consist of an anode, a cathode, and an electrolyte. However, unlike batteries, fuel cells require a continuous supply of fuel and oxygen to generate electricity.
4. **Redesigning**: The theory of construction of electrodes, batteries, and fuel cells can be utilized in redesigning new engineering solutions. This can involve improving the efficiency and performance of existing technologies, or developing entirely new systems that take advantage of the latest advances in materials science and electrochemistry.
