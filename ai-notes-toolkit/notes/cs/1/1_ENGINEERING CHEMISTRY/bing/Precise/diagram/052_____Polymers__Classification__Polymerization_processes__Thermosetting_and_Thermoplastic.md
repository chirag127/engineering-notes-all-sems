### Polymers; Classification, Polymerization processes, Thermosetting and Thermoplastic

Polymers are large molecules made up of repeating subunits called monomers. They can be classified in several ways, including by their chemical structure, physical properties, and synthesis methods.

#### Classification
When classified by chemical structure, there are two generally recognized classes of plastic materials: Thermosets, having cross-linked molecular chains, and Thermoplastics, which are made up of linear molecular chains .

#### Polymerization processes
Thermoset polymers require a two-stage polymerization process. In the first stage, the liquid resin is mixed with a hardener to create a thermosetting plastic. In the second stage, the plastic is heated to cure and harden it .

#### Thermosetting and Thermoplastic
Thermosetting polymers are made up of cross-linked molecular chains, while thermoplastic polymers are made up of linear molecular chains. Thermoplastics soften when heated and harden when cooled, while thermosets do not soften when heated .

Thermoplastics are reversible and reproducible in that they soften when heated and stiffen when cooled. Most linear polymers and branched structure polymers with flexible chains are thermoplastics .

Thermosetting polymers, on the other hand, do not soften when heated. Instead, they undergo a chemical change that makes them hard and rigid. This process is irreversible, meaning that once a thermoset has been cured, it cannot be melted or reshaped .
