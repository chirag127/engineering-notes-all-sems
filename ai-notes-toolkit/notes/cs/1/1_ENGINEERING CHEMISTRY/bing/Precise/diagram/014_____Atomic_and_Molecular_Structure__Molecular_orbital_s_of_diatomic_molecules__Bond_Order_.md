### Atomic and Molecular Structure: Molecular orbitals of diatomic molecules, Bond Order

Molecular orbitals are formed by the combination of atomic orbitals of two or more atoms in a molecule. In diatomic molecules, the atomic orbitals of the two atoms combine to form molecular orbitals that are either bonding or antibonding.

- Bonding molecular orbitals are lower in energy than the atomic orbitals from which they are formed and are more stable. Electrons in bonding orbitals hold the atoms together in a chemical bond.

- Antibonding molecular orbitals are higher in energy than the atomic orbitals from which they are formed and are less stable. Electrons in antibonding orbitals weaken the chemical bond between the atoms.

The bond order is a measure of the strength of a chemical bond. It is calculated as the number of electrons in bonding orbitals minus the number of electrons in antibonding orbitals, divided by two.

For example, in a diatomic molecule such as O2, the bond order is 2, indicating a double bond between the two oxygen atoms. In N2, the bond order is 3, indicating a triple bond between the two nitrogen atoms.

Bond order can also be used to predict the stability of a molecule. Molecules with higher bond orders are generally more stable than those with lower bond orders. For example, N2 with a bond order of 3 is more stable than O2 with a bond order of 2.
