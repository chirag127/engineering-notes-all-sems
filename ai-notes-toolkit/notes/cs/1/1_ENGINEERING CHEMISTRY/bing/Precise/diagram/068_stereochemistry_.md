# Stereochemistry

Stereochemistry is a subdiscipline of chemistry that involves the study of the relative spatial arrangement of atoms that form the structure of molecules and their manipulation. It is also known as 3D chemistry, where the prefix "stereo" means "three-dimensionality"  .

The study of stereochemistry focuses on the relationships between stereoisomers, which by definition have the same molecular formula and sequence of bonded atoms .

Some important concepts in stereochemistry include:

- **Chirality**: This property refers to the handedness of molecules. Just like how your left foot doesn't quite fit your right shoe, molecules can also have properties that depend on their handedness .
- **Stereoisomers**: Stereoisomers are isomers that have the same molecular formula and sequence of bonded atoms, but differ in the three-dimensional orientations of their atoms in space .
- **Enantiomers**: Enantiomers are a type of stereoisomer that are mirror images of each other, but are not superimposable .
- **Diastereomers**: Diastereomers are a type of stereoisomer that are not mirror images of each other .
- **R,S system**: This is a system used to assign configurations to chiral molecules .
- **Optical activity**: This refers to the ability of a chiral molecule to rotate the plane of polarized light .
- **Fischer projections**: Fischer projections are a way to represent the three-dimensional structure of a molecule in two dimensions .
