### Water Technology

#### Sources of Water
Water is a vital resource for life and is obtained from various sources. Some of the common sources of water include:
1. Surface water: This includes water from rivers, lakes, and reservoirs.
2. Groundwater: This is water that is found underground in aquifers and is obtained through wells and boreholes.
3. Rainwater: This is water that is collected from precipitation and can be harvested and stored for use.
4. Desalination: This is the process of removing salt and other minerals from seawater to make it suitable for use.

#### Impurities in Water
Water from natural sources is not pure and contains various impurities. These impurities can be classified into two categories:
1. Dissolved impurities: These are impurities that are dissolved in water and include salts, minerals, and organic matter.
2. Suspended impurities: These are impurities that are not dissolved in water and include sand, silt, and clay particles.

#### Hardness of Water
Hardness of water refers to the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts in water. These salts can cause problems in industrial and domestic settings by forming scale in boilers and pipes, reducing the effectiveness of soap and detergents, and causing skin irritation. Water hardness is classified into two types:
1. Temporary hardness: This is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium and can be removed by boiling the water.
2. Permanent hardness: This is caused by the presence of sulfates and chlorides of calcium and magnesium and cannot be removed by boiling. It can be removed by using ion exchange resins or by adding chemicals such as sodium carbonate.

#### Boiler Troubles
Boiler troubles refer to problems that can occur in boilers due to the presence of impurities in water. Some common boiler troubles include:
1. Scale formation: This is caused by the deposition of calcium and magnesium salts on the inner surfaces of boilers and can reduce heat transfer efficiency and increase fuel consumption.
2. Corrosion: This is caused by the reaction of water with the metal surfaces of boilers and can lead to the weakening and failure of boiler components.
3. Priming and foaming: This is caused by the presence of dissolved and suspended impurities in water and can lead to the carryover of water into the steam, reducing the quality of the steam and causing damage to downstream equipment.
