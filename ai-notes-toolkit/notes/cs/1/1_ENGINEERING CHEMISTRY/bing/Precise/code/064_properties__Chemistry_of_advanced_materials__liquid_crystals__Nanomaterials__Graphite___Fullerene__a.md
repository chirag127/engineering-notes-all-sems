# Properties, Chemistry of Advanced Materials

## Liquid Crystals
- Liquid crystals are a state of matter that has properties between those of a conventional liquid and those of a solid crystal.
- They can flow like a liquid, but have the long-range order of a solid.
- Liquid crystals are used in displays, such as LCD screens, because they can be manipulated to control the passage of light.
- There are several types of liquid crystals, including nematic, smectic, and cholesteric.

## Nanomaterials
- Nanomaterials are materials with at least one dimension in the nanometer range (1-100 nm).
- They have unique properties due to their small size, such as increased surface area and quantum effects.
- Nanomaterials are used in a variety of applications, including electronics, medicine, and energy production.
- Some common types of nanomaterials include nanoparticles, nanotubes, and nanowires.

## Graphite
- Graphite is a form of carbon that consists of layers of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice.
- It is a good conductor of heat and electricity and has a high melting point.
- Graphite is used in a variety of applications, including batteries, lubricants, and pencils.
- Graphite can be transformed into other forms of carbon, such as diamond and graphene, under certain conditions.

## Fullerene
- Fullerenes are a class of carbon molecules that consist of carbon atoms arranged in a spherical or ellipsoidal shape.
- The most well-known fullerene is the buckminsterfullerene, or "buckyball," which consists of 60 carbon atoms arranged in a soccer ball-like shape.
- Fullerenes have unique properties, such as high stability and the ability to encapsulate other molecules.
- They have potential applications in medicine, electronics, and materials science.
