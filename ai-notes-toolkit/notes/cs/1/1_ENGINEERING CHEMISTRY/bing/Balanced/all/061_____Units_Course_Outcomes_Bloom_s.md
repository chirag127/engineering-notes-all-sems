# Units Course Outcomes Bloom's

- Units course outcomes are statements that describe what students are expected to know and be able to do by the end of a unit of study.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a framework that classifies learning objectives into six levels of cognitive complexity: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Units course outcomes should be aligned with Bloom's taxonomy to ensure that students achieve different levels of learning and demonstrate higher-order thinking skills.
- Some examples of units course outcomes and their corresponding Bloom's levels are:

  - By the end of this unit, students will be able to **remember** the definitions and formulas of basic concepts in physics. (Remember)
  - By the end of this unit, students will be able to **understand** the main principles and laws of thermodynamics and how they relate to real-world phenomena. (Understand)
  - By the end of this unit, students will be able to **apply** the concepts and methods of calculus to solve problems involving rates of change, optimization, and integration. (Apply)
  - By the end of this unit, students will be able to **analyze** the structure and function of different types of cells and tissues in the human body. (Analyze)
  - By the end of this unit, students will be able to **evaluate** the strengths and weaknesses of different ethical theories and arguments. (Evaluate)
  - By the end of this unit, students will be able to **create** their own research questions and hypotheses based on the literature review and data collection methods. (Create)

- Units course outcomes should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) to ensure that they are clear, realistic, and assessable.
- Units course outcomes should be communicated to students at the beginning of the unit and reviewed throughout the unit to monitor their progress and provide feedback.
