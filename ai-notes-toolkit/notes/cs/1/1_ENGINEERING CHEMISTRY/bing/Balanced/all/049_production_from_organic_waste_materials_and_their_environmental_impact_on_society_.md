# Production from organic waste materials and their environmental impact on society

- Organic waste materials are any biodegradable substances that come from plants or animals, such as food scraps, yard trimmings, paper, wood, leather, etc.
- Organic waste materials can be converted into useful products, such as compost, biogas, biofuels, bioplastics, etc., through various processes, such as composting, anaerobic digestion, fermentation, pyrolysis, etc.
- Composting is the natural decomposition of organic waste materials by microorganisms under aerobic conditions, resulting in a nutrient-rich soil amendment that can improve soil quality and fertility.
- Anaerobic digestion is the breakdown of organic waste materials by microorganisms under anaerobic conditions, producing biogas (a mixture of methane and carbon dioxide) and digestate (a solid or liquid residue that can be used as fertilizer or soil conditioner).
- Fermentation is the conversion of organic waste materials into ethanol or other organic acids by microorganisms under anaerobic or low-oxygen conditions, which can be used as biofuels or chemical feedstocks.
- Pyrolysis is the thermal decomposition of organic waste materials at high temperatures in the absence of oxygen, producing biochar (a carbon-rich solid that can enhance soil properties and sequester carbon), bio-oil (a liquid that can be refined into biofuels or chemicals), and syngas (a gas that can be used for heat or power generation).
- The production of useful products from organic waste materials can have various environmental and social benefits, such as:
  - Reducing the amount of waste that goes to landfills or dumpsites, which can emit greenhouse gases, leachate, and odors, and pose health and safety risks   .
  - Saving natural resources and energy that would otherwise be used to produce synthetic fertilizers, fuels, plastics, etc., and reducing the associated environmental impacts, such as greenhouse gas emissions, water pollution, soil degradation, etc.  .
  - Enhancing soil health and productivity by adding organic matter and nutrients, improving water retention and infiltration, and suppressing pests and diseases .
  - Providing renewable and clean sources of energy that can reduce dependence on fossil fuels and contribute to climate change mitigation and adaptation .
  - Creating economic and social opportunities for waste management, product development, and value addition, especially for rural and marginalized communities  .
