# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical problems

UV, IR and NMR are spectroscopic techniques that can be used to identify organic compounds by analyzing their molecular structure and functional groups.

## UV spectroscopy

UV spectroscopy measures the absorption of ultraviolet light by a molecule. It can be used to determine the presence and extent of conjugation in a molecule, as well as the number and position of double bonds.

- The wavelength of maximum absorption (λmax) is inversely proportional to the extent of conjugation. More conjugated molecules absorb at longer wavelengths (lower energy).
- The intensity of absorption (ε) is proportional to the number of double bonds in a molecule. More double bonds result in higher absorption coefficients.

### Numerical problem

Example: A compound with molecular formula C6H6O has a UV spectrum with λmax = 280 nm and ε = 13000. What is the structure of the compound?

Solution: The molecular formula indicates that the compound has 4 degrees of unsaturation, which means it has either 4 double bonds, 2 triple bonds, or 1 ring and 2 double bonds. The UV spectrum suggests that the compound has a high degree of conjugation, as the λmax is relatively long and the ε is relatively high. Therefore, the most likely structure is a benzene ring with a hydroxyl group attached, as shown below.

## IR spectroscopy

IR spectroscopy measures the absorption of infrared light by a molecule. It can be used to determine the presence and type of functional groups in a molecule, as well as the bond strength and polarity.

- The frequency of absorption (ν) is proportional to the bond strength and inversely proportional to the bond length. Stronger and shorter bonds absorb at higher frequencies (higher energy).
- The intensity of absorption (I) is proportional to the bond polarity and the number of bonds. More polar and more numerous bonds result in higher absorption intensities.

### Numerical problem

Example: A compound with molecular formula C4H8O has an IR spectrum with a strong absorption at 1720 cm-1 and a weak absorption at 3300 cm-1. What is the structure of the compound?

Solution: The molecular formula indicates that the compound has 1 degree of unsaturation, which means it has either 1 double bond or 1 ring. The IR spectrum suggests that the compound has a carbonyl group, as the strong absorption at 1720 cm-1 corresponds to the C=O stretch. The weak absorption at 3300 cm-1 could be due to an alcohol group, as the O-H stretch is usually weak and broad. Therefore, the most likely structure is a cyclic ketone with a hydroxyl group attached, as shown below.

## NMR spectroscopy

NMR spectroscopy measures the absorption of radio waves by a molecule in a magnetic field. It can be used to determine the number and type of hydrogen atoms in a molecule, as well as their relative positions and environments.

- The chemical shift (δ) is the measure of how far a signal is from a reference signal, usually tetramethylsilane (TMS). It depends on the electronegativity and hybridization of the atoms attached to the hydrogen. More electronegative and more sp-hybridized atoms cause a higher chemical shift (more downfield).
- The integration (I) is the measure of the area under a signal. It is proportional to the number of hydrogen atoms that give rise to the signal. More hydrogen atoms result in a larger integration value.
- The multiplicity (M) is the measure of the splitting pattern of a signal. It depends on the number of neighboring hydrogen atoms that are not equivalent to the signal. The multiplicity follows the n+1 rule, where n is the number of neighboring hydrogen atoms. More neighboring hydrogen atoms result in a more complex multiplicity.
