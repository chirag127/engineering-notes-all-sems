# Understanding of Chemistry of Engineering Materials (Cement)

Cement is a widely used engineering material that binds aggregates such as sand and gravel to form concrete. Cement is composed of various chemical compounds that undergo hydration reactions when mixed with water, resulting in the hardening and strength development of the cement paste.

Some of the main chemical compounds in cement are:

- **Calcium silicates**: These are the most abundant compounds in cement, accounting for about 70% of the mass. They are responsible for most of the strength and durability of cement. There are two types of calcium silicates: tricalcium silicate (C3S) and dicalcium silicate (C2S). C3S hydrates faster and contributes more to the early strength of cement, while C2S hydrates slower and contributes more to the long-term strength of cement.

- **Calcium aluminates**: These are the second most abundant compounds in cement, accounting for about 10% of the mass. They are responsible for the setting and initial hardening of cement. There are two types of calcium aluminates: tricalcium aluminate (C3A) and tetracalcium aluminoferrite (C4AF). C3A hydrates rapidly and generates heat, which can cause cracking and shrinkage of cement. C4AF hydrates moderately and contributes to the color and resistance to sulfates of cement.

- **Calcium sulfate**: This is a compound that is added to cement as a set regulator, accounting for about 5% of the mass. It controls the hydration rate of C3A and prevents flash setting of cement. The most common form of calcium sulfate is gypsum (CaSO4.2H2O), which dehydrates partially to form hemihydrate (CaSO4.0.5H2O) or anhydrite (CaSO4) during the grinding of cement clinker.

- **Other compounds**: These are minor compounds that are present in cement, accounting for less than 5% of the mass. They include magnesium oxide (MgO), sodium oxide (Na2O), potassium oxide (K2O), iron oxide (Fe2O3), and others. They affect the properties of cement such as color, fineness, soundness, and resistance to alkali-silica reaction.
