# Polymer Composites

- A polymer composite is a multi-phase material in which reinforcing fillers are integrated with a polymer matrix, resulting in synergistic mechanical properties that cannot be achieved from either component alone.
- A polymer is a natural or synthetic substance composed of very large molecules, called macromolecules, that are multiples of simpler chemical units called monomers.
- A polymer composite can consist of a continuous phase or matrix, which is typically the resin or polymer phase, and a dispersed phase or reinforcement, which can be continuous or noncontinuous, and can be made of various materials such as fibers, particles, or flakes .
- Polymer composites are increasingly being used in various engineering fields, such as aerospace, automotive, biomedical, construction, and energy, because of their advantages over conventional materials, such as high strength-to-weight ratio, corrosion resistance, design flexibility, and low cost .
- Polymer composites can be classified into different types based on the nature of the matrix, the reinforcement, and the interface between them. Some common types of polymer composites are:
  - Thermoset composites: These composites have a matrix that is a thermosetting polymer, which undergoes an irreversible chemical reaction during curing and cannot be remelted or reshaped. Examples of thermoset composites are epoxy composites, polyester composites, and vinyl ester composites.
  - Thermoplastic composites: These composites have a matrix that is a thermoplastic polymer, which can be melted and reshaped by heating and cooling. Examples of thermoplastic composites are polypropylene composites, nylon composites, and polycarbonate composites.
  - Fiber-reinforced composites: These composites have a matrix that is reinforced by fibers, which can be continuous or discontinuous, and can be made of various materials such as glass, carbon, aramid, or natural fibers. Examples of fiber-reinforced composites are glass fiber-reinforced polymer (GFRP), carbon fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP), and natural fiber-reinforced polymer (NFRP).
  - Particle-reinforced composites: These composites have a matrix that is reinforced by particles, which can be spherical, cubic, or irregular in shape, and can be made of various materials such as ceramics, metals, or polymers. Examples of particle-reinforced composites are polymer nanocomposites, metal matrix composites, and ceramic matrix composites.
  - Hybrid composites: These composites have a matrix that is reinforced by two or more types of reinforcements, which can have different properties and functions. Examples of hybrid composites are carbon-glass hybrid composites, metal-polymer hybrid composites, and ceramic-polymer hybrid composites.
