# CO-1 Get an understanding of the theoretical principles of chemistry of molecular structure, bonding and

- Molecular structure refers to the arrangement of atoms in a molecule and the chemical bonds that hold them together.
- Bonding is the process of forming chemical bonds between atoms to create molecules or compounds.
- Theoretical principles of chemistry of molecular structure and bonding include:

  - The octet rule: atoms tend to form bonds that give them eight electrons in their valence shell, or outermost energy level.
  - The Lewis structure: a representation of the valence electrons of atoms using dots and lines to show bonds and lone pairs.
  - The VSEPR theory: the shape of a molecule is determined by the repulsion between the electron pairs around the central atom.
  - The hybridization theory: the orbitals of the central atom mix to form hybrid orbitals that can accommodate the bonding and non-bonding electron pairs.
  - The molecular orbital theory: the orbitals of the atoms in a molecule combine to form molecular orbitals that can be occupied by electrons and determine the bond order, bond length, and bond energy.
  - The valence bond theory: the bonds between atoms are formed by the overlap of atomic orbitals that contain unpaired electrons.
  - The electronegativity: the ability of an atom to attract electrons in a bond. It affects the polarity, dipole moment, and intermolecular forces of a molecule.
  - The resonance: the phenomenon of having more than one valid Lewis structure for a molecule that differs only in the distribution of electrons. It affects the stability, bond length, and bond energy of a molecule.
