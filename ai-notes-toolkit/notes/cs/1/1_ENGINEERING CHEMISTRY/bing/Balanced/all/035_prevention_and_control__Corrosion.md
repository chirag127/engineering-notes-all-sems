# Prevention and Control of Corrosion

Corrosion is the deterioration of a metal or its properties due to a reaction with its environment. Corrosion can cause loss of material, strength, functionality, and aesthetics of metal structures and devices. Corrosion can also pose safety and environmental hazards if not properly controlled.

Some of the methods to prevent and control corrosion are:

- **Material selection**: Choosing a metal or alloy that is resistant to the specific corrosive environment or has a protective oxide layer. For example, stainless steel, aluminum, and copper are often used for their corrosion resistance.
- **Coating**: Applying a layer of paint, plastic, metal, or ceramic on the metal surface to isolate it from the corrosive environment. For example, steel pipes are often coated with zinc or epoxy to prevent rusting.
- **Cathodic protection**: Connecting the metal to a more easily corroded metal (called a sacrificial anode) or an external power source (called an impressed current) to make the metal a cathode and reduce its corrosion rate. For example, underground pipelines and storage tanks are often protected by cathodic protection systems.
- **Corrosion inhibitors**: Adding chemicals to the corrosive environment to reduce the corrosion rate of the metal. For example, corrosion inhibitors are often added to cooling water systems, oil and gas pipelines, and industrial processes.
- **Design and maintenance**: Designing the metal structure or device to avoid crevices, stress concentrations, galvanic couples, and other factors that can accelerate corrosion. Also, performing regular inspection, cleaning, and repair of the metal to remove corrosion products and prevent further damage. For example, bridges, buildings, and vehicles are often designed and maintained to prevent corrosion.
