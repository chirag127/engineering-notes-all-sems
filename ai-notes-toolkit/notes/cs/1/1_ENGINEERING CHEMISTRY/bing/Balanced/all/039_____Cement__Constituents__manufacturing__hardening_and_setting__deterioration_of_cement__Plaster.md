# Cement; Constituents, manufacturing, hardening and setting, deterioration of cement, Plaster

- Cement is a binder material that sets and hardens by chemical reactions with water and other ingredients.
- The main constituents of cement are calcium silicates, aluminates, and sulfates, which are derived from limestone, clay, and gypsum.
- The manufacturing of cement involves four main stages: raw material preparation, clinker production, clinker grinding, and cement packing.
  - Raw material preparation involves crushing, grinding, and blending of limestone, clay, and other additives to obtain a homogeneous mixture.
  - Clinker production involves heating the raw material mixture in a rotary kiln at high temperatures (1400-1500°C) to form nodules of clinker, which are mainly calcium silicates and aluminates.
  - Clinker grinding involves cooling and grinding the clinker with gypsum and other additives to obtain the final product of cement.
  - Cement packing involves filling and sealing the cement in bags or containers for storage and transportation.
- Hardening and setting of cement are two different processes that occur after the cement is mixed with water and other ingredients.
  - Setting is the initial stiffening of the cement paste, which occurs within a few hours after mixing. Setting is influenced by the type and amount of cement, water, and additives, as well as the temperature and humidity of the environment.
  - Hardening is the gradual increase in strength and durability of the cement paste, which occurs over days, weeks, or months after setting. Hardening is influenced by the hydration and crystallization of the cement compounds, as well as the curing conditions and external factors such as moisture, temperature, and load.
- Deterioration of cement is the loss of quality and performance of the cement paste or concrete due to various factors, such as:
  - Chemical attack: the reaction of the cement paste or concrete with aggressive substances, such as acids, sulfates, chlorides, carbon dioxide, etc., which can cause corrosion, leaching, expansion, cracking, or weakening of the material.
  - Physical damage: the exposure of the cement paste or concrete to mechanical stress, such as abrasion, erosion, impact, fatigue, etc., which can cause wear, tear, or fracture of the material.
  - Thermal effects: the exposure of the cement paste or concrete to extreme or fluctuating temperatures, such as fire, frost, or thermal shock, which can cause expansion, contraction, spalling, or cracking of the material.
- Plaster is a mixture of cement, sand, water, and sometimes other additives, such as lime, fibers, or pigments, that is applied to a surface to form a smooth or decorative coating.
  - Plaster can be used for various purposes, such as protection, insulation, decoration, or repair of walls, ceilings, floors, or other structures.
  - Plaster can be classified into different types, such as gypsum plaster, lime plaster, cement plaster, etc., depending on the type of binder and additives used.
  - Plaster can be applied in different ways, such as by hand, by machine, or by spray, depending on the consistency and properties of the mixture and the desired finish.
