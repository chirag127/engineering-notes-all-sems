# K4

- K4 is a **symmetric-key algorithm** that is used to encrypt and decrypt data.
- K4 was developed by **David Chaum** and **Jan Camenisch** in 1990 as a variant of the **Blum-Goldwasser cryptosystem**.
- K4 uses a **one-time pad** to generate a random key stream that is XORed with the plaintext to produce the ciphertext.
- K4 also uses a **public-key algorithm** to encrypt and decrypt the one-time pad, which is sent along with the ciphertext.
- K4 is **provably secure** under the assumption that the **decisional Diffie-Hellman problem** is hard and that the **one-time pad** is truly random and secret.
- K4 has some advantages over other symmetric-key algorithms, such as:
  - It is **efficient** and **fast**, since it only requires XOR operations and modular exponentiations.
  - It is **malleable**, meaning that the ciphertext can be modified without affecting the decryption.
  - It is **non-interactive**, meaning that the sender and the receiver do not need to communicate before or during the encryption and decryption process.
  - It is **deniable**, meaning that the sender and the receiver can deny having participated in the communication, since the one-time pad can be chosen arbitrarily.
- K4 has some disadvantages over other symmetric-key algorithms, such as:
  - It is **not authenticated**, meaning that the ciphertext can be tampered with or forged by an adversary without detection.
  - It is **not robust**, meaning that the decryption can fail if the one-time pad is corrupted or lost.
  - It is **not practical**, meaning that the one-time pad requires a large amount of storage and transmission, and that the public-key algorithm introduces additional complexity and overhead.
