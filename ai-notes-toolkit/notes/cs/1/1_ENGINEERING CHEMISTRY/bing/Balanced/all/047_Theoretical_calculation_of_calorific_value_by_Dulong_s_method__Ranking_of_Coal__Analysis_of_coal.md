## Theoretical calculation of calorific value by Dulong’s method

- Calorific value (CV) is the amount of heat released by the complete combustion of a unit mass of a fuel.
- Dulong's method is a formula that estimates the CV of a fuel from its ultimate analysis, which is the percentage of carbon (C), hydrogen (H), oxygen (O), nitrogen (N) and sulfur (S) in the fuel.
- Dulong's method is based on the assumption that the heat of combustion of an organic compound is nearly equal to the heats of combustion of the elements in it, multiplied by their percentage content in the compound.
- Dulong's formula for CV is:

  CV = 337C + 1442(H - O/8) + 93S

  where C, H, O and S are the percentage of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and sulfur in the fuel, respectively, and CV is in kJ/kg.

- Dulong's formula has some limitations, such as:

  - It does not account for the moisture and ash content of the fuel, which affect the CV.
  - It does not account for the variation in the heats of combustion of different forms of carbon, such as graphite, coal and coke.
  - It does not account for the variation in the heats of combustion of different forms of hydrogen, such as gas, liquid and solid.
  - It does not account for the variation in the heats of combustion of different forms of sulfur, such as pyrite, sulfate and sulfide.

- Therefore, Dulong's formula may not be accurate for all types of fuels, especially those with high moisture, ash, nitrogen or oxygen content.
- Some modifications of Dulong's formula have been proposed to improve its accuracy, such as:

  - Adding a correction factor for the moisture content of the fuel, such as:

    CV = 337C + 1442(H - O/8) + 93S - 18.6M

    where M is the percentage of moisture in the fuel.

  - Adding a correction factor for the ash content of the fuel, such as:

    CV = 337C + 1442(H - O/8) + 93S - 18.6M - 0.212A

    where A is the percentage of ash in the fuel.

  - Adding a correction factor for the nitrogen content of the fuel, such as:

    CV = 337C + 1442(H - O/8) + 93S - 18.6M - 0.212A - 23.2N

    where N is the percentage of nitrogen in the fuel.

  - Using different coefficients for different forms of carbon, hydrogen and sulfur, such as:

    CV = 337Cg + 326Cc + 314Ck + 1442(H - O/8) + 93Ss + 71Sp + 25So

    where Cg, Cc and Ck are the percentage of graphite, coal and coke in the fuel, respectively, and Ss, Sp and So are the percentage of sulfide, sulfate and sulfur in the fuel, respectively.

## Ranking of Coal

- Coal is a combustible sedimentary rock that is formed from the accumulation and compression of plant matter over millions of years.
- Coal is classified into different ranks based on its degree of metamorphism, which is the process of physical and chemical changes that occur due to heat and pressure.
- The degree of metamorphism affects the properties of coal, such as its carbon content, volatile matter, moisture, ash, calorific value, hardness, coking ability and combustion characteristics.
