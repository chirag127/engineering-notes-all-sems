# Stereochemistry

Stereochemistry is the branch of chemistry that deals with the three-dimensional (3D) structure of molecules and their effect on physical and chemical properties  . Stereochemistry is important for understanding the biological activity, synthesis, and analysis of organic compounds.

## Types of Stereoisomers

Stereoisomers are molecules that have the same molecular formula and the same connectivity of atoms, but differ in the spatial arrangement of atoms or groups in 3D space . Stereoisomers can be classified into two main types: enantiomers and diastereomers.

### Enantiomers

Enantiomers are stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other . Enantiomers have the same physical and chemical properties, except for their interaction with plane-polarized light and other chiral molecules. Enantiomers rotate plane-polarized light in opposite directions, and are said to have opposite optical activities. Enantiomers are also called optical isomers or chiral isomers.

### Diastereomers

Diastereomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images of each other . Diastereomers have different physical and chemical properties, and may have different optical activities. Diastereomers can be further subdivided into several types, such as cis-trans isomers, conformational isomers, and atropisomers.

#### Cis-trans isomers

Cis-trans isomers are diastereomers that differ in the orientation of atoms or groups around a double bond or a ring . Cis isomers have the same atoms or groups on the same side of the double bond or the ring, while trans isomers have them on opposite sides . Cis-trans isomers are also called geometric isomers or E-Z isomers.

#### Conformational isomers

Conformational isomers are diastereomers that differ in the rotation of atoms or groups around a single bond . Conformational isomers can interconvert by bond rotation, and are usually in equilibrium with each other. Conformational isomers are also called rotamers or conformers.

#### Atropisomers

Atropisomers are diastereomers that differ in the rotation of atoms or groups around a bond that is hindered by steric strain or resonance . Atropisomers cannot interconvert by bond rotation, and are usually separated by physical methods. Atropisomers are also called axial chiral isomers or helical isomers.
