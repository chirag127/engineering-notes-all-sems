# Values, Gross & Net calorific value, Determination of calorific value by Bomb Calorimeter

- Values are the principles or standards of behavior that guide one's actions and judgments. They are often based on one's beliefs, preferences, culture, or morality. Values can vary from person to person, group to group, or society to society. Some examples of values are honesty, loyalty, freedom, equality, justice, etc.
- Calorific value is the amount of heat or energy produced by a fuel during complete combustion. It is a variable that represents the amount of heat or energy released and is measured in Gross Calorific Value (GCV) or Net Calorific Value (NCV) .
- Gross calorific value (GCV) is the total amount of heat or energy released when a unit mass or volume of fuel is burned in the presence of oxygen. It includes the latent heat of vaporization of water vapors produced during combustion. GCV is also known as higher heating value (HHV) or higher calorific value (HCV) .
- Net calorific value (NCV) is the amount of heat or energy released when a unit mass or volume of fuel is burned in the presence of oxygen, minus the latent heat of vaporization of water vapors produced during combustion. NCV is also known as lower heating value (LHV) or lower calorific value (LCV) .
- The formula for calorific value is: GCV - latent heat of water vapors = NCV .
- A bomb calorimeter is an instrument used to determine the calorific value of a fuel. It consists of a strong metal container called a bomb, which is filled with oxygen and a sample of fuel. The bomb is immersed in a known mass of water in an insulated vessel. The fuel is ignited by an electric spark and burns completely in the oxygen. The heat released by the combustion is transferred to the water and the temperature rise of the water is measured. The calorific value of the fuel can be calculated by using the following formula: Calorific value = (heat capacity of water x mass of water x temperature rise of water) / mass of fuel .
