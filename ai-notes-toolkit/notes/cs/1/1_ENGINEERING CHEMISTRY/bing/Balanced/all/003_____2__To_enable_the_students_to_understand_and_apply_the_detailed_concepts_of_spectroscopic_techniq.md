# 2. To enable the students to understand and apply the detailed concepts of spectroscopic techniques and

- Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter.
- Spectroscopic techniques are methods that use different types of radiation to obtain information about the structure, composition, and properties of matter.
- Some of the common spectroscopic techniques are:

  - Ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectroscopy: This technique uses ultraviolet and visible light to measure the absorption or emission of photons by molecules. It can be used to determine the concentration, purity, and identity of substances, as well as to study the electronic transitions and molecular orbitals of molecules.
  - Infrared (IR) spectroscopy: This technique uses infrared radiation to measure the vibration and rotation of molecular bonds. It can be used to identify functional groups, molecular structures, and intermolecular interactions of organic and inorganic compounds.
  - Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy: This technique uses radio waves to measure the magnetic properties of atomic nuclei. It can be used to determine the number, type, and location of atoms, as well as the connectivity and conformation of molecules.
  - Mass spectrometry (MS): This technique uses electric and magnetic fields to separate and measure the mass-to-charge ratio of ions. It can be used to determine the molecular weight, formula, and structure of compounds, as well as to identify and quantify mixtures of substances.
  - X-ray spectroscopy: This technique uses X-rays to measure the scattering or absorption of photons by atoms. It can be used to determine the crystal structure, phase, and composition of materials, as well as to study the electronic and chemical properties of atoms.

- To understand and apply the detailed concepts of spectroscopic techniques, the students need to:

  - Learn the basic principles, instrumentation, and applications of each technique.
  - Know how to interpret and analyze the spectroscopic data and spectra of different compounds and materials.
  - Be able to compare and contrast the advantages and limitations of different techniques for various purposes and situations.
  - Be able to perform and report the spectroscopic experiments in a safe and accurate manner.
