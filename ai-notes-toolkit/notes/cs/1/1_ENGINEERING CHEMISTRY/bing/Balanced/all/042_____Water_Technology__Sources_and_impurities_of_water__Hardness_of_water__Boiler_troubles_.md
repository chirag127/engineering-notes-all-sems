# Water Technology: Sources and impurities of water, Hardness of water, Boiler troubles

## Sources and impurities of water

- Water is a vital resource for human and industrial activities. It is obtained from various sources, such as surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), groundwater (wells, springs, etc.), rainwater, seawater, etc.
- Water from different sources may contain various impurities, such as dissolved solids (salts, minerals, metals, etc.), dissolved gases (oxygen, carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, etc.), suspended solids (sand, clay, silt, etc.), colloidal particles (organic matter, microorganisms, etc.), and dissolved organic compounds (pesticides, herbicides, etc.).
- The impurities in water may affect its quality, suitability, and safety for various purposes, such as drinking, domestic use, industrial use, agricultural use, etc. Therefore, water quality analysis and treatment are essential to ensure the desired water quality standards.

## Hardness of water

- Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts in water. These salts react with soap to form insoluble precipitates, called scum or curd, which reduce the lathering and cleaning ability of soap. Hard water also causes scaling or deposition of calcium and magnesium salts on the surfaces of pipes, boilers, heat exchangers, etc., which reduces their efficiency and lifespan.
- Hardness of water is classified into two types: temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, which can be removed by boiling the water or adding lime. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, which cannot be removed by boiling or lime treatment, but require other methods, such as ion exchange, reverse osmosis, etc.
- Hardness of water is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3) concentration in milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm). The degree of hardness of water is generally categorized as follows:

| Degree of hardness | Hardness range (mg/L or ppm) |
|--------------------|------------------------------|
| Soft               | 0 - 75                       |
| Moderately hard    | 75 - 150                     |
| Hard               | 150 - 300                    |
| Very hard          | > 300                        |

## Boiler troubles

- Boiler troubles are the problems or defects that occur in boilers due to the presence of impurities in the feed water or the improper operation or maintenance of the boiler system. Some of the common boiler troubles are:

  - Scale formation: Scale is the hard and adherent coating of calcium and magnesium salts, such as calcium sulfate, magnesium hydroxide, and calcium bicarbonate, that forms on the inner walls of the boiler tubes and vessels. Scale reduces the heat transfer efficiency and increases the fuel consumption and operating cost of the boiler. Scale also increases the risk of overheating and bursting of the boiler tubes. Scale can be prevented or removed by using soft water, adding scale inhibitors, or mechanical cleaning.

  - Sludge formation: Sludge is the soft and loose deposit of insoluble impurities, such as silica, iron oxide, and organic matter, that settles at the bottom or low-lying areas of the boiler. Sludge reduces the water circulation and heat transfer efficiency of the boiler. Sludge also causes corrosion and pitting of the boiler metal. Sludge can be prevented or removed by using clarified water, adding sludge conditioners, or blowdown operation.

  - Corrosion: Corrosion is the deterioration or damage of the boiler metal due to the chemical or electrochemical reaction with the water or the dissolved gases, such as oxygen and carbon dioxide, in the water. Corrosion reduces the strength and durability of the boiler metal and causes leaks and failures of the boiler. Corrosion can be prevented or controlled by using deaerated water, adding corrosion inhibitors, or cathodic protection.

  - Priming and foaming: Priming is the phenomenon of carrying over of water droplets along with the steam from the boiler. Foaming is the formation of stable bubbles on the surface of the water in the boiler. Both priming and foaming are caused by the presence of high concentration of dissolved solids, suspended solids, or organic matter in the water.
