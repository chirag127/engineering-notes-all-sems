# Thiokol, Nylon, Buna-N and Buna-S and their environmental impact on society, Speciality

- Thiokol is a synthetic rubber made from ethylene dichloride and sodium polysulfide. It is used as a sealant, adhesive, and binder for solid rocket propellants. It has high resistance to heat, cold, and chemicals, but it is also flammable and toxic. 
- Nylon is a synthetic polymer made from coal, water, and air. It is used as a fiber for clothing, carpets, parachutes, and other products. It has high strength, elasticity, and durability, but it is also non-biodegradable and requires a lot of energy and water to produce. 
- Buna-N is a synthetic rubber made from acrylonitrile and butadiene. It is also known as nitrile rubber or NBR. It is used as a material for hoses, gloves, seals, and gaskets. It has high resistance to oil, fuel, and other chemicals, but it is also non-renewable and can release harmful substances when burned. 
- Buna-S is a synthetic rubber made from styrene and butadiene. It is also known as styrene butadiene rubber or SBR. It is used as a material for tires, footwear, and rubber bands. It has good abrasion and weather resistance, but it is also non-renewable and can cause environmental pollution when disposed of. 

- The environmental impact of these synthetic polymers on society is complex and depends on various factors, such as their production, use, and disposal methods, their alternatives, and their benefits and drawbacks. Some of the general impacts are:

  - They reduce the dependence on natural resources, such as rubber, cotton, and silk, which may have limited availability, high cost, or ethical issues. 
  - They provide new and improved properties, such as strength, durability, flexibility, and resistance, which may enhance the performance, safety, and comfort of various products and applications. 
  - They consume a lot of energy, water, and raw materials, which may deplete the natural resources, increase the greenhouse gas emissions, and contribute to climate change. 
  - They generate a lot of waste, which may accumulate in landfills, oceans, and soil, and harm the wildlife, ecosystems, and human health. 
  - They may release toxic substances, such as dioxins, furans, and heavy metals, during their production, use, or disposal, which may contaminate the air, water, and food, and cause cancer, hormonal disruption, and other diseases. 

- The speciality of these synthetic polymers is that they are man-made materials that can be designed and modified to suit specific needs and purposes. They can also be combined with other materials, such as fillers, additives, and reinforcements, to create composites with enhanced properties and functions.
