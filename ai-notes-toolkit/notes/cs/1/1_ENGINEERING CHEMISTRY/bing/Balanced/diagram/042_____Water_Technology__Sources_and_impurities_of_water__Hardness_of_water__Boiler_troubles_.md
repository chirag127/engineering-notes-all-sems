### Water Technology: Sources and impurities of water, Hardness of water, Boiler troubles

- Water is a vital resource for various domestic, industrial and agricultural purposes. It is obtained from different sources, such as surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), underground water (wells, springs, etc.), rainwater, seawater, etc.
- Water contains various impurities, such as dissolved solids (salts, minerals, metals, etc.), dissolved gases (oxygen, carbon dioxide, etc.), suspended solids (clay, silt, sand, etc.), colloidal particles (organic matter, bacteria, etc.), and dissolved organic compounds (pesticides, herbicides, etc.).
- The impurities in water affect its quality, suitability and usage. For example, water with high dissolved solids may cause scaling, corrosion, or fouling of pipes and equipment. Water with high dissolved gases may cause cavitation, pitting, or embrittlement of metal surfaces. Water with high suspended solids may cause turbidity, sedimentation, or clogging of filters and membranes. Water with high colloidal particles may cause color, odor, or taste problems. Water with high dissolved organic compounds may cause toxicity, carcinogenicity, or bioaccumulation issues.
- The hardness of water is a measure of its ability to form insoluble precipitates with soap or other metal ions. It is caused by the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts, such as bicarbonates, sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates. Hardness of water is classified into two types: temporary hardness and permanent hardness.
- Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium. It can be removed by boiling the water, which converts the bicarbonates into insoluble carbonates that can be filtered out. Alternatively, it can be removed by adding lime (calcium hydroxide), which reacts with the bicarbonates to form insoluble calcium carbonate and soluble magnesium hydroxide.
- Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium. It cannot be removed by boiling or lime treatment. It can be removed by ion exchange, which involves passing the water through a resin that exchanges the calcium and magnesium ions with sodium or hydrogen ions. Alternatively, it can be removed by reverse osmosis, which involves forcing the water through a semi-permeable membrane that rejects the dissolved salts.
- The hardness of water is harmful to the boilers, as the deposition of salts occurs, which reduces the efficiency of the boiler. Hard water also forms curds with soap, interferes with dyeing, and strains the skin. The hardness of water is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3) concentration, which is the amount of CaCO3 that would produce the same effect as the actual hardness-causing salts. The units of hardness are milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm) of CaCO3.
- Boiler troubles are the problems that arise due to the use of hard water in boilers. They include scale formation, sludge formation, priming and foaming, caustic embrittlement, and boiler corrosion.
- Scale formation is the formation of hard and adherent coating on the inner walls of the boiler by the precipitation of salts, such as calcium sulfate, magnesium hydroxide, and calcium carbonate. Scale reduces the heat transfer efficiency, increases the fuel consumption, and causes overheating and bursting of boiler tubes. Scale can be prevented by using soft water, adding scale inhibitors, or using external or internal descaling methods.
- Sludge formation is the formation of soft and loose deposits on the bottom of the boiler by the precipitation of salts, such as calcium carbonate, magnesium carbonate, and iron oxide. Sludge reduces the water circulation, increases the steam contamination, and causes erosion and corrosion of boiler parts. Sludge can be prevented by using soft water, adding sludge conditioners, or using blowdown or mechanical cleaning methods.
- Priming and foaming are the phenomena of carryover of water droplets along with the steam due to the presence of impurities, such as oil, grease, or organic matter. Priming and foaming reduce the steam quality, cause water hammering, and damage the steam equipment. Priming and foaming can be prevented by using soft water, adding anti-foaming agents, or using steam separators or baffles.
