### CO-4 Develop understanding of the sources, impurities and hardness of water, apply the concepts of

- Sources of water: Water is obtained from natural sources such as rain, rivers, lakes, springs, wells, glaciers, and oceans. Water can also be obtained from artificial sources such as dams, reservoirs, canals, pipelines, and desalination plants.
- Impurities in water: Water contains various impurities such as dissolved gases, minerals, organic matter, microorganisms, and suspended solids. Some of these impurities are harmless or beneficial, while others are harmful or undesirable. Impurities can affect the physical, chemical, and biological properties of water, such as its color, odor, taste, pH, hardness, conductivity, turbidity, and dissolved oxygen.
- Hardness of water: Hardness of water is a measure of the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts in water. Hard water forms scales or deposits on pipes, boilers, heaters, and other appliances, and reduces the effectiveness of soap and detergents. Hardness of water can be classified into temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, and can be removed by boiling or adding lime. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of chlorides, sulfates, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, and can be removed by ion exchange or reverse osmosis.
- Concepts of water treatment: Water treatment is the process of removing or reducing the impurities in water to make it suitable for various purposes, such as drinking, irrigation, industrial use, or environmental protection. Water treatment involves various methods, such as coagulation, sedimentation, filtration, disinfection, aeration, softening, and demineralization. Water treatment can be done at different levels, such as household, community, or municipal. Water treatment can also be classified into conventional and advanced methods. Conventional methods use physical and chemical processes, while advanced methods use biological, membrane, or electrochemical processes.
