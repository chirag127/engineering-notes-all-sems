### CO-1 Get an understanding of the theoretical principles of chemistry of molecular structure, bonding and reactions

- Molecular structure refers to the arrangement of atoms in a molecule and the bond lengths and angles between them.
- Bonding refers to the forces that hold atoms together in a molecule and the type and number of electrons involved in forming the bonds.
- Reactions refer to the processes that change the molecular structure and bonding of substances by breaking and forming new bonds.

Some of the theoretical principles of chemistry of molecular structure, bonding and reactions are:

- The **valence shell electron pair repulsion (VSEPR) theory** predicts the shape of a molecule based on the repulsion between the electron pairs in the valence shell of the central atom.
- The **valence bond (VB) theory** describes the formation of a covalent bond by the overlap of atomic orbitals of the bonding atoms and the hybridization of the orbitals to match the molecular geometry.
- The **molecular orbital (MO) theory** describes the formation of a covalent bond by the combination of atomic orbitals of the bonding atoms to form molecular orbitals that extend over the entire molecule and have different energy levels and shapes.
- The **Lewis structure** is a representation of a molecule that shows the valence electrons as dots or lines around the symbols of the atoms and the bonds as lines between the atoms.
- The **octet rule** states that atoms tend to form bonds in such a way that they achieve a stable configuration of eight valence electrons, except for hydrogen and helium, which achieve two valence electrons.
- The **electronegativity** is a measure of the tendency of an atom to attract electrons in a bond. The difference in electronegativity between two atoms determines the polarity of the bond and the dipole moment of the molecule.
- The **bond order** is a measure of the number of bonds between two atoms. The higher the bond order, the stronger and shorter the bond.
- The **bond energy** is the amount of energy required to break a bond between two atoms. The higher the bond energy, the more stable the bond.
- The **bond length** is the distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms. The bond length depends on the bond order, the size of the atoms, and the electronegativity difference.
- The **bond angle** is the angle between two bonds that share a common atom. The bond angle depends on the molecular geometry, the hybridization of the central atom, and the lone pairs of electrons.
- The **chemical reaction** is a process that involves the rearrangement of atoms and bonds to form new substances. The reactants are the substances that undergo the change and the products are the substances that are formed.
- The **chemical equation** is a representation of a chemical reaction that shows the symbols and formulas of the reactants and products and the coefficients that indicate the relative amounts of each substance.
- The **law of conservation of mass** states that the total mass of the reactants is equal to the total mass of the products in a chemical reaction.
- The **law of conservation of energy** states that the total energy of the reactants is equal to the total energy of the products in a chemical reaction.
- The **reaction rate** is the measure of how fast a chemical reaction occurs. The reaction rate depends on the concentration, temperature, pressure, and catalyst of the reactants and products.
- The **equilibrium** is the state of a chemical reaction in which the forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate and the concentrations of the reactants and products remain constant.
- The **equilibrium constant (K)** is the ratio of the concentrations of the products to the concentrations of the reactants at equilibrium, raised to the power of their coefficients in the balanced chemical equation.
- The **Le Chatelier's principle** states that if a stress is applied to a system at equilibrium, the system will shift in the direction that minimizes the effect of the stress.
