### Liquid Crystals; Introduction, Types and Applications of liquid crystals, Industrially

- Liquid crystals (LCs) are substances that exhibit a phase of matter that is between a solid and a liquid, with some degree of molecular order and mobility .
- Liquid crystals can be classified into two main categories: **thermotropic** and **lyotropic** .
  - Thermotropic LCs are formed by heating a crystalline solid and depend on temperature and pressure for their phase transitions .
  - Lyotropic LCs are formed by dissolving a solute in a solvent and depend on concentration and temperature for their phase transitions .
- Liquid crystals can also be classified by their molecular structure and orientation, such as **nematic**, **smectic**, **cholesteric**, **discotic**, and **polymeric** .
  - Nematic LCs have molecules that are aligned along a common direction, called the director, but have no positional order .
  - Smectic LCs have molecules that are arranged in layers, with some degree of positional order within each layer .
  - Cholesteric LCs have molecules that are arranged in layers, with the director varying from layer to layer, forming a helical structure .
  - Discotic LCs have molecules that are shaped like discs and are stacked in columns, with the director perpendicular to the column axis .
  - Polymeric LCs have molecules that are composed of repeating units, such as polymers, that can form LC phases .
- Liquid crystals have many applications in various fields, such as display technology, optics, sensors, lasers, and medicine  .
  - Display technology: Liquid crystals are widely used in flat panel displays, such as LCDs, because they can modulate light by applying electric fields .
  - Optics: Liquid crystals can be used to create optical devices, such as lenses, filters, polarizers, and switches, that can change their optical properties by applying external stimuli .
  - Sensors: Liquid crystals can be used to sense various physical and chemical parameters, such as temperature, pressure, pH, humidity, and electric fields, by changing their optical or electrical properties .
  - Lasers: Liquid crystals can be used to create tunable lasers, that can change their wavelength by adjusting the LC phase or orientation .
  - Medicine: Liquid crystals can be used to deliver drugs, enhance imaging, and monitor biological processes, by exploiting their self-assembly, biocompatibility, and responsiveness .
