# Polymers
- Polymers are large molecules composed of repeating units called monomers, which are linked by covalent bonds.
- Polymers can be classified into two main categories: synthetic and natural. Synthetic polymers are artificially made from petroleum or other sources, while natural polymers are derived from living organisms or plants.
- Polymers can also be classified based on their structure, properties, and applications. Some common types of polymers are thermoplastics, thermosets, elastomers, fibers, and biopolymers.

# Polymer Blends and Composites
- Polymer blends are mixtures of two or more polymers that are physically mixed but not chemically bonded. Polymer blends can improve the properties and performance of the individual polymers, such as mechanical strength, thermal stability, and compatibility.
- Polymer composites are materials that consist of a polymer matrix and a reinforcing filler, such as fibers, particles, or nanomaterials. Polymer composites can enhance the properties and functionality of the polymer matrix, such as stiffness, toughness, electrical conductivity, and biodegradability.

# Conducting and Biodegradable Polymers
- Conducting polymers are polymers that can conduct electricity due to the presence of conjugated double bonds or delocalized electrons along the polymer chain. Conducting polymers have applications in biosensors, tissue engineering, drug delivery, and antibacterial activity.
- Biodegradable polymers are polymers that can be degraded by microorganisms or hydrolytic enzymes in the environment. Biodegradable polymers have applications in biomedical devices, packaging, agriculture, and waste management.
- Some polymers can be both conducting and biodegradable, such as polypyrrole, polyaniline, and polythiophene. These polymers can combine the advantages of electrical conductivity and environmental friendliness.
