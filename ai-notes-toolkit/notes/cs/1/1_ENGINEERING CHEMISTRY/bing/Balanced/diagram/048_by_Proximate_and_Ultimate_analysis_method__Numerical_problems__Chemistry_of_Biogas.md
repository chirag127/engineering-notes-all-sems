# Proximate and Ultimate Analysis of Biomass

## Proximate Analysis

- Proximate analysis is the determination by prescribed methods of moisture, volatile matter, fixed carbon, and ash. All components should sum to 100% of the biomass.
- Moisture is the water content of the biomass, which affects its heating value and combustion efficiency. Moisture is determined by heating the sample in an oven at 105°C until constant weight.
- Volatile matter is the fraction of the biomass that is released as gases and vapors when heated in the absence of air. Volatile matter is determined by heating the sample in a crucible at 950°C for 7 minutes.
- Fixed carbon is the solid residue that remains after the volatile matter is driven off. Fixed carbon is calculated by subtracting the moisture, volatile matter, and ash from 100%.
- Ash is the inorganic residue that remains after the biomass is completely burned. Ash is determined by heating the sample in a muffle furnace at 750°C until constant weight.
- Proximate analysis is useful for estimating the heating value, combustion behavior, and storage stability of biomass.

## Ultimate Analysis

- Ultimate analysis is the determination by prescribed methods of the mass percentages of chemical elements (carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur) in the biomass.
- Carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen are determined by combusting the sample in a high-temperature furnace with oxygen and measuring the resulting gases with a thermal conductivity detector.
- Oxygen is calculated by subtracting the carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, sulfur, and ash from 100%.
- Sulfur is determined by oxidizing the sample with perchloric acid and measuring the sulfate ion with a turbidimetric method.
- Ultimate analysis is useful for calculating the stoichiometry, emissions, and environmental impact of biomass combustion.

## Numerical Problems

- Example 1: A biomass sample has the following proximate analysis: moisture 10%, volatile matter 65%, fixed carbon 20%, and ash 5%. Calculate the dry basis and ash-free basis proximate analysis of the sample.
- Solution: The dry basis proximate analysis is obtained by dividing each component by the dry mass fraction (1 - moisture). The ash-free basis proximate analysis is obtained by dividing each component by the combustible mass fraction (1 - moisture - ash).

| Component | Wet Basis (%) | Dry Basis (%) | Ash-Free Basis (%) |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|--------------------|
| Moisture  | 10            | -             | -                  |
| Volatile Matter | 65     | 72.22         | 76.05              |
| Fixed Carbon    | 20     | 22.22         | 23.42              |
| Ash             | 5      | 5.56          | -                  |

- Example 2: A biomass sample has the following ultimate analysis: carbon 50%, hydrogen 6%, nitrogen 1%, oxygen 40%, sulfur 0.5%, and ash 2.5%. Calculate the higher heating value (HHV) and lower heating value (LHV) of the sample using the Dulong formula and the Channiwala formula.
- Solution: The Dulong formula is HHV = 33.8C + 144.2(H - O/8) + 9.5S kJ/kg, where C, H, O, and S are the mass fractions of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and sulfur in the biomass. The Channiwala formula is HHV = 0.3491C + 1.1783H + 0.1005S - 0.1034O - 0.0151A kJ/g, where A is the mass fraction of ash in the biomass. The LHV is obtained by subtracting the latent heat of vaporization of water from the HHV. The latent heat of vaporization of water is 2442 kJ/kg at 25°C.

|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Dulong  | 18676.5     | 16234.5     |
| Channiwala | 18674.8  | 16232.8     |
