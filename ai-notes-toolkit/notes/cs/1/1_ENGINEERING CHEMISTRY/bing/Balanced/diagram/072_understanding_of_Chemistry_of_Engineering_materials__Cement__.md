# Chemistry of Engineering Materials (Cement)

Cement is a binding material that is widely used in construction and civil engineering. It is made by heating a mixture of raw materials, mainly limestone and clay, in a kiln at high temperatures. The resulting product, called clinker, is then ground with a small amount of gypsum to form a fine powder called cement.

## Composition and Properties of Cement

- Cement is mainly composed of four compounds: tricalcium silicate (3CaO · SiO2), dicalcium silicate (2CaO · SiO2), tricalcium aluminate (3CaO · Al2O3), and tetracalcium aluminoferrite (4CaO · Al2O3Fe2O3) .
- These compounds have different properties and influence the strength, setting time, heat of hydration, and durability of cement.
- Tricalcium silicate is the most abundant and reactive compound in cement. It is responsible for the initial setting and early strength development of cement. It also generates a lot of heat during hydration, which can cause thermal cracking in concrete.
- Dicalcium silicate is less reactive and contributes to the long-term strength and resistance to chemical attack of cement. It hydrates slowly and produces less heat than tricalcium silicate.
- Tricalcium aluminate is the most soluble and reactive compound in cement. It reacts rapidly with water and forms ettringite, a needle-like crystal that can cause expansion and cracking in concrete. It also reacts with sulfate ions in water and forms gypsum, which can cause delayed ettringite formation and sulfate attack.
- Tetracalcium aluminoferrite is the least reactive compound in cement. It has little effect on the strength and setting time of cement, but it can improve the resistance to corrosion and alkali-silica reaction of cement.

## Types and Uses of Cement

- There are different types of cement that are classified according to their chemical composition, physical properties, and applications.
- Some of the common types of cement are:

  - Portland cement: The most widely used type of cement, which is suitable for general purpose concrete and mortar. It has a moderate strength and setting time, and a moderate heat of hydration. It can be further classified into different grades, such as Type I, II, III, IV, and V, depending on the amount and ratio of the four compounds in the cement.
  - Blended cement: A type of cement that contains a mixture of Portland cement and other materials, such as fly ash, slag, silica fume, or natural pozzolans. These materials can enhance the properties and performance of cement, such as reducing the heat of hydration, improving the workability, and increasing the durability of concrete.
  - White cement: A type of cement that has a low iron content and a high whiteness. It is used for decorative and architectural purposes, such as tiles, terrazzo, and precast concrete products.
  - Masonry cement: A type of cement that is specially formulated for masonry construction, such as bricklaying and plastering. It has a high workability and water retention, and a low compressive strength. It is usually mixed with sand and water to form a mortar.
  - Oil well cement: A type of cement that is used for sealing and stabilizing oil and gas wells. It has a high resistance to high temperature and pressure, and a low permeability and shrinkage. It is usually mixed with water and additives to form a slurry that is pumped into the well.

## Hydration and Setting of Cement

- Hydration is the chemical reaction between cement and water that produces a hard and solid mass called concrete.
- Hydration involves the formation of various products, such as calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H), calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2), ettringite, and gypsum, that fill the space between the cement particles and the aggregates.
- Hydration is influenced by several factors, such as the water-cement ratio, the temperature, the curing conditions, and the presence of admixtures and additives.
- Setting is the process of stiffening and hardening of cement paste due to the hydration of cement. Setting is divided into two stages: initial setting and final setting.
- Initial setting is the time when the cement paste starts to lose its plasticity and becomes difficult to work with. Final setting is the time when the cement paste becomes rigid and can resist external forces.
