### CO-5 Develop the understanding of Chemical structure of polymers and its effect on their various properties

- Polymers are large molecules made up of repeating units called monomers, which are linked together by covalent bonds.
- The chemical structure of polymers determines their physical and chemical properties, such as molecular weight, degree of polymerization, crystallinity, solubility, melting point, glass transition temperature, elasticity, strength, etc.
- Some of the factors that affect the chemical structure of polymers are:

  - The type and arrangement of monomers: Different monomers have different functional groups, polarity, size, and shape, which affect the way they interact with each other and form bonds. The arrangement of monomers can be linear, branched, or cross-linked, which affects the flexibility, density, and rigidity of the polymer chain.
  - The type and degree of polymerization: Polymerization is the process of linking monomers together to form polymers. There are two main types of polymerization: addition and condensation. Addition polymerization involves the addition of monomers without the elimination of any by-product, while condensation polymerization involves the elimination of a small molecule, such as water, as a by-product. The degree of polymerization is the number of monomers in a polymer chain, which affects the molecular weight and the chain length of the polymer.
  - The type and degree of crystallization: Crystallization is the process of arranging polymer chains in a regular and ordered manner, forming regions of high density and low mobility called crystallites. The degree of crystallization is the fraction of the polymer that is crystalline, which affects the transparency, stiffness, and melting point of the polymer. The type of crystallization depends on the cooling rate, the molecular weight, and the chain structure of the polymer. There are two main types of crystallization: spherulitic and lamellar. Spherulitic crystallization involves the formation of spherical aggregates of crystallites, while lamellar crystallization involves the formation of thin layers of crystallites.
  - The type and degree of cross-linking: Cross-linking is the process of forming covalent bonds between different polymer chains, creating a network structure. The degree of cross-linking is the number of cross-links per unit volume of the polymer, which affects the elasticity, toughness, and solubility of the polymer. The type of cross-linking depends on the functional groups, the catalysts, and the reaction conditions of the polymerization. There are two main types of cross-linking: homogeneous and heterogeneous. Homogeneous cross-linking involves the formation of cross-links between similar polymer chains, while heterogeneous cross-linking involves the formation of cross-links between different polymer chains or other molecules.
