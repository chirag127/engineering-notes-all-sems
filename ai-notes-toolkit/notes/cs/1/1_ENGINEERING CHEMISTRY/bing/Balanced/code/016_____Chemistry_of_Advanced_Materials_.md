### Chemistry of Advanced Materials:

- Chemistry of advanced materials is the branch of chemistry that deals with the design, synthesis, characterization and application of materials that have novel properties and functions.
- Advanced materials in chemistry include nanomaterials, biomaterials, energy materials, smart materials, functional materials and composite materials that are useful for the developing world.
- The study of advanced materials in chemistry has helped to develop synthetic chemicals, the interplay between the structure of materials, the processing methods to manipulate them and the performance of the final products.
- Some of the principles and concepts that are involved in the chemistry of advanced materials are:

  - Molecular design: The ability to design and synthesize molecules with specific structures and functions that can be used as building blocks for advanced materials.
  - Self-assembly: The spontaneous organization of molecules or nanoparticles into ordered structures or patterns without external intervention.
  - Supramolecular chemistry: The study of the interactions and associations of molecules that are held together by non-covalent bonds, such as hydrogen bonding, electrostatic forces, van der Waals forces and π-π stacking.
  - Nanotechnology: The manipulation of matter at the nanoscale (1-100 nm) to create new materials and devices with novel properties and functions.
  - Biomimetics: The imitation of natural systems or processes to create new materials or devices that have similar or improved functions.
  - Green chemistry: The design of chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate the use or generation of hazardous substances and minimize the environmental impact of chemistry.

- Some of the applications of advanced materials in chemistry are:

  - Nanomaterials: Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the nanoscale. They can exhibit unique physical, chemical, optical, electrical and magnetic properties that are different from their bulk counterparts. Nanomaterials can be used for various purposes, such as drug delivery, catalysis, sensors, electronics, energy conversion and storage, biomedicine, nanomedicine and nanotoxicology.
  - Biomaterials: Biomaterials are materials that are compatible with biological systems and can be used for medical or biotechnological purposes. Biomaterials can be natural or synthetic, and can have different functions, such as scaffolds, implants, prosthetics, drug carriers, biosensors, bioactuators and bioelectronics.
  - Energy materials: Energy materials are materials that can convert, store or transport energy in different forms, such as heat, light, electricity, chemical or mechanical. Energy materials can be used for various applications, such as solar cells, fuel cells, batteries, supercapacitors, thermoelectrics, piezoelectrics and hydrogen storage.
  - Smart materials: Smart materials are materials that can respond to external stimuli, such as temperature, pressure, electric field, magnetic field, light, pH or chemical agents, and change their properties or functions accordingly. Smart materials can be used for various purposes, such as actuators, sensors, switches, memory devices, displays and coatings.
  - Functional materials: Functional materials are materials that have specific or multiple functions that are determined by their structure, composition or processing. Functional materials can be used for various purposes, such as catalysts, adsorbents, separations, optics, electronics, magnetics and superconductors.
  - Composite materials: Composite materials are materials that are composed of two or more different materials that have different properties and are combined to create a new material that has improved or synergistic properties. Composite materials can be used for various purposes, such as structural, mechanical, thermal, electrical, optical and acoustic applications.
