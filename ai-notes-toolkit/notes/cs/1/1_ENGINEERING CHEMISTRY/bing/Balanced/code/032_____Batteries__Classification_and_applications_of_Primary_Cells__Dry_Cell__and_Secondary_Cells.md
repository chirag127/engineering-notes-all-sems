### Batteries; Classification and applications of Primary Cells (Dry Cell) and Secondary Cells

- A **battery** is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy by using a spontaneous redox reaction that occurs within the device.
- A battery consists of one or more **cells**, which are the basic units of electrochemical power.
- Cells can be classified into two types: **primary cells** and **secondary cells**.
- **Primary cells** are cells that are designed to be used once and discarded, and not recharged with electricity and reused like secondary cells.
  - Primary cells have an irreversible electrochemical reaction occurring in the cell, which means that the reactants are consumed and cannot be regenerated.
  - Primary cells are also called **dry cells** because they do not contain a liquid electrolyte, but a paste or a solid.
  - Examples of primary cells are zinc-carbon cells, alkaline cells, lithium cells, etc.
  - Applications of primary cells include flashlights, toys, remote controls, clocks, etc.
- **Secondary cells** are cells that are designed to be used multiple times by recharging them with electricity.
  - Secondary cells have a reversible electrochemical reaction occurring in the cell, which means that the reactants can be regenerated by applying an external voltage.
  - Secondary cells are also called **rechargeable batteries** because they can be reused after recharging.
  - Examples of secondary cells are lead-acid batteries, nickel-cadmium batteries, lithium-ion batteries, etc.
  - Applications of secondary cells include automobiles, laptops, mobile phones, electric vehicles, etc.
