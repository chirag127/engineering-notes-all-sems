# Water Technology: Sources and impurities of water, Hardness of water, Boiler troubles

## Sources of water
- Water is essential for life and various industrial applications.
- Water can be obtained from natural or artificial sources, such as rivers, lakes, wells, springs, rain, snow, glaciers, etc.
- The quality and quantity of water depend on the source and the location of the water.
- The sources of water can be classified into two types: stationary and flowing.
- Stationary sources include ponds, lakes, reservoirs, etc. They are usually contaminated by surface runoff, organic matter, and microorganisms.
- Flowing sources include rivers, streams, canals, etc. They are usually less contaminated than stationary sources, but may contain suspended solids, dissolved salts, and pollutants from industrial and agricultural activities.

## Impurities of water
- Water is never pure in nature. It always contains various types of impurities, which can affect its physical, chemical, and biological properties.
- Impurities in water can be classified into three types: physical, chemical, and biological.
- Physical impurities include suspended solids, turbidity, color, odor, taste, and temperature. They can affect the appearance, smell, and taste of water, and may cause clogging of pipes and filters.
- Chemical impurities include dissolved solids, hardness, alkalinity, acidity, dissolved gases, and various organic and inorganic compounds. They can affect the chemical reactions, corrosion, scaling, and fouling of water, and may cause health problems for humans and animals.
- Biological impurities include microorganisms, such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, algae, protozoa, etc. They can cause diseases, spoilage, and decomposition of water, and may interfere with disinfection and purification processes.

## Hardness of water
- Hardness of water is the property of water that prevents the formation of lather or foam with soap.
- Hardness of water is mainly caused by the presence of calcium and magnesium salts, such as carbonates, bicarbonates, sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates.
- Hardness of water can be classified into two types: temporary and permanent.
- Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium. It can be removed by boiling the water, which causes the bicarbonates to decompose into carbonates, which precipitate as scale.
- Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium. It cannot be removed by boiling the water, but can be removed by chemical methods, such as ion exchange, lime-soda process, etc.
- Hardness of water can affect the quality and efficiency of water for various purposes, such as domestic, industrial, and agricultural uses.

## Boiler troubles
- Boiler troubles are the problems that occur in boilers due to the use of impure water as feed water.
- Boiler troubles can affect the performance, safety, and lifespan of boilers, and may cause damage to the boiler and its accessories.
- Boiler troubles can be classified into three types: scale and sludge formation, corrosion, and priming and foaming.
- Scale and sludge formation is the deposition of insoluble salts and impurities on the inner surfaces of the boiler. It can reduce the heat transfer, increase the fuel consumption, and cause overheating and bursting of the boiler tubes.
- Corrosion is the deterioration of the metal parts of the boiler due to the action of oxygen, carbon dioxide, acids, and salts in the water. It can weaken the strength, reduce the efficiency, and cause leakage and failure of the boiler.
- Priming and foaming is the carryover of water droplets and bubbles along with the steam from the boiler. It can reduce the quality and quantity of steam, cause water hammer and erosion in the pipes and turbines, and affect the operation and control of the boiler.
