### Spectroscopic Techniques and Applications: Elementary idea and simple applications of

Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter. It can be used to identify, characterize, and quantify different substances based on their absorption, emission, or scattering of radiation. Spectroscopy can also provide information about the structure, dynamics, and environment of molecules and atoms.

There are different types of spectroscopy based on the nature of the radiation, the region of the electromagnetic spectrum, the type of interaction, and the method of measurement. Some of the common types of spectroscopy are:

- **Absorption spectroscopy**: This technique measures the amount of radiation that is absorbed by a sample when it is exposed to a range of wavelengths. The absorption spectrum of a sample can reveal its chemical composition, molecular structure, and concentration. Examples of absorption spectroscopy are ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis), infrared (IR), and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy.
- **Emission spectroscopy**: This technique measures the amount of radiation that is emitted by a sample when it is excited by an external source of energy, such as heat, electricity, or light. The emission spectrum of a sample can reveal its chemical composition, electronic structure, and temperature. Examples of emission spectroscopy are atomic emission (AE), flame emission (FE), and fluorescence spectroscopy.
- **Scattering spectroscopy**: This technique measures the amount of radiation that is scattered by a sample when it is illuminated by a beam of radiation. The scattering spectrum of a sample can reveal its size, shape, and orientation. Examples of scattering spectroscopy are Raman, Rayleigh, and Mie scattering spectroscopy.

Spectroscopy has a variety of applications in different fields of science and technology, such as:

- **Medicine**: Spectroscopy can be used for diagnosing diseases, monitoring treatment, and imaging tissues and organs. For example, NMR spectroscopy can be used to measure the metabolic changes in cells and tissues, IR spectroscopy can be used to detect the presence of biomolecules, and fluorescence spectroscopy can be used to label and track specific molecules in living systems.
- **Physics**: Spectroscopy can be used for studying the properties and interactions of atoms, molecules, and nuclei. For example, AE spectroscopy can be used to determine the elemental composition of a sample, NMR spectroscopy can be used to measure the magnetic properties of nuclei, and Raman spectroscopy can be used to probe the vibrational modes of molecules.
- **Chemistry**: Spectroscopy can be used for identifying and characterizing chemical compounds, reactions, and mechanisms. For example, UV-Vis spectroscopy can be used to measure the absorption and transmission of light by a sample, IR spectroscopy can be used to identify the functional groups and bonds in a molecule, and fluorescence spectroscopy can be used to measure the fluorescence and quenching of a molecule.
- **Astronomy**: Spectroscopy can be used for studying the composition, temperature, and motion of celestial objects, such as stars, planets, and galaxies. For example, AE spectroscopy can be used to measure the emission lines of elements in the spectra of stars, IR spectroscopy can be used to detect the presence of molecules and dust in the interstellar medium, and Raman spectroscopy can be used to analyze the surface and atmosphere of planets.
