## Unit-2: 8

- This unit covers the topic of **linear equations** and their applications in various contexts.
- A linear equation is an equation of the form `ax + b = c`, where `a`, `b`, and `c` are constants and `x` is a variable.
- To solve a linear equation, we need to isolate `x` on one side of the equation by using inverse operations, such as adding, subtracting, multiplying, or dividing both sides by the same number.
- A linear equation can represent a **relation** between two quantities that vary linearly, such as the distance traveled by a car at a constant speed, the cost of a product based on its quantity, or the amount of interest earned on an investment over time.
- To graph a linear equation, we can use the **slope-intercept form** of the equation, which is `y = mx + b`, where `m` is the slope and `b` is the y-intercept. The slope tells us how steep the line is and the y-intercept tells us where the line crosses the y-axis.
- To find the slope of a line, we can use the **slope formula**, which is `m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)`, where `(x1, y1)` and `(x2, y2)` are any two points on the line. The slope can also be interpreted as the **rate of change** of `y` with respect to `x`.
- To find the y-intercept of a line, we can substitute `x = 0` into the equation and solve for `y`. Alternatively, we can find the y-intercept by plugging in the slope and any point on the line into the slope-intercept form and solving for `b`.
- To write the equation of a line given the slope and a point, we can use the **point-slope form** of the equation, which is `y - y1 = m(x - x1)`, where `m` is the slope and `(x1, y1)` is the point. To write the equation of a line given two points, we can first find the slope using the slope formula and then use the point-slope form.
- To find the **x-intercept** of a line, we can substitute `y = 0` into the equation and solve for `x`. The x-intercept is the point where the line crosses the x-axis.
- To find the **solution** of a system of two linear equations, we can use one of the following methods: **substitution**, **elimination**, or **graphing**. The solution is the point where the two lines intersect, or the ordered pair that satisfies both equations. If the two lines are parallel, there is no solution. If the two lines are the same, there are infinitely many solutions.
