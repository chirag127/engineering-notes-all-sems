## Theoretical calculation of calorific value by Dulong’s method

- Calorific value (CV) is the amount of heat released by the complete combustion of a unit mass of a fuel.
- Dulong's method is a formula that estimates the CV of a fuel from its ultimate analysis, which is the percentage of carbon (C), hydrogen (H), oxygen (O), nitrogen (N) and sulfur (S) in the fuel.
- Dulong's formula is based on the assumption that the heat of combustion of an organic compound is nearly equal to the heats of combustion of the elements in it, multiplied by their percentage content in the compound .
- Dulong's formula for CV is:

CV = 337C + 1442(H - O/8) + 93S kJ/kg

where C, H, O and S are the percentage of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and sulfur in the fuel, respectively.

- Dulong's formula has some limitations, such as:
  - It does not account for the variation of CV with moisture content, ash content and volatile matter content of the fuel.
  - It does not account for the latent heat of vaporization of water formed during combustion.
  - It does not account for the heat losses due to incomplete combustion, radiation and convection.
  - It does not account for the differences in the chemical structure and bonding of the fuel molecules.
- Therefore, Dulong's formula is only an approximation and may not give accurate results for some fuels, especially those with high oxygen or nitrogen content, such as biomass and peat.
- Some modifications of Dulong's formula have been proposed to improve its accuracy, such as:
  - Adding a correction factor for the moisture content of the fuel.
  - Adding a correction factor for the nitrogen content of the fuel.
  - Using different coefficients for different types of fuels, such as coal, oil and gas .

## Ranking of Coal

- Coal is a fossil fuel that is formed from the decomposition and compaction of plant matter over millions of years.
- Coal is classified into different ranks based on its degree of metamorphism, which is the change in the physical and chemical properties of the coal due to heat and pressure.
- The rank of coal indicates its quality, such as its heating value, carbon content, moisture content, volatile matter content, ash content and hardness.
- The main ranks of coal are:
  - Peat: The lowest rank of coal, which is not technically a coal, but a partially decomposed plant matter with high moisture and low heating value. It is used as a soil conditioner and a fuel for domestic heating.
  - Lignite: The lowest rank of true coal, which is brownish-black and crumbly, with high moisture and low heating value. It is used for power generation and gasification.
  - Sub-bituminous: A low rank of coal, which is black and dull, with low moisture and moderate heating value. It is used for power generation and gasification.
  - Bituminous: A medium to high rank of coal, which is black and shiny, with low to medium moisture and high heating value. It is used for power generation, metallurgy and coking.
  - Anthracite: The highest rank of coal, which is hard and glossy, with very low moisture and very high heating value. It is used for domestic heating, metallurgy and coking.

## Analysis of coal

- Analysis of coal is the process of determining the composition and properties of coal, such as its moisture, ash, volatile matter, fixed carbon, sulfur, calorific value, etc.
- Analysis of coal is important for its utilization, classification, quality control and environmental impact assessment.
- Analysis of coal can be done by various methods, such as:
  - Proximate analysis: A method that determines the moisture, ash, volatile matter and fixed carbon content of coal by heating it in a furnace and measuring the weight loss or gain. It gives an indication of the heating value, combustion characteristics and coking properties of coal.
