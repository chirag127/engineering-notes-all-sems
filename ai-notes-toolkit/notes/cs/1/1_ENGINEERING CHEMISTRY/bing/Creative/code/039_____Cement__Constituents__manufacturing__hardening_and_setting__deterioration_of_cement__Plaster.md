### Cement; Constituents, manufacturing, hardening and setting, deterioration of cement, Plaster

- Cement is a binding material that sets, hardens, and adheres to other materials to bind them together.
- The main constituents of cement are calcium, silicon, aluminum, iron and other ingredients that are combined in different proportions depending on the type and grade of cement.
- The manufacturing process of cement involves crushing, grinding, blending, heating, cooling, and storing of raw materials, clinker, and cement.
- The hardening and setting of cement are due to the formation of interlocking crystals reinforced by rigid gels formed by the hydration and hydrolysis of the constitutional compounds.
- The initial and final setting times of cement are of practical importance, as they determine the length of time in which cement mixes remain plastic and workable, and the length of time required for the cement to attain sufficient strength.
- The setting and hardening of cement are influenced by various factors, such as water-cement ratio, temperature, admixtures, curing, and environmental conditions.
- The deterioration of cement can occur due to various causes, such as corrosion of embedded metals, chemical attack, physical damage, thermal effects, and biological agents.
- The deterioration of cement can result in loss of strength, durability, and serviceability of concrete structures.
- Plaster is a mixture of cement, sand, water, and sometimes lime or gypsum that is applied to walls, ceilings, or other surfaces to form a smooth or decorative finish.
- Plaster can be used for various purposes, such as protection, insulation, fire resistance, sound absorption, and aesthetic enhancement.
