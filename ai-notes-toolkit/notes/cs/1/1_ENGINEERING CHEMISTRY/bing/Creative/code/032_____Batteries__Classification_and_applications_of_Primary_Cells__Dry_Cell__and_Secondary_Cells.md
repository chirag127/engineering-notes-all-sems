### Batteries; Classification and applications of Primary Cells (Dry Cell) and Secondary Cells

- A battery is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy by means of a spontaneous redox reaction.
- Batteries can be broadly divided into two major types: primary cells and secondary cells  .
- Primary cells are batteries that are designed to be used once and discarded, and not recharged with electricity and reused like secondary cells. The electrochemical reaction occurring in the cell is not reversible, rendering the cell unrechargeable.
- Examples of primary cells are dry cells, alkaline cells, mercury cells, lithium cells, etc  .
- Applications of primary cells include flashlights, toys, remote controls, clocks, radios, etc .
- Secondary cells are batteries that can be recharged after use by passing current through the electrodes in the opposite direction, i.e. from the negative terminal to the positive terminal. The electrochemical reaction occurring in the cell is reversible, rendering the cell rechargeable.
- Examples of secondary cells are lead-acid cells, nickel-cadmium cells, lithium-ion cells, etc    .
- Applications of secondary cells include automobiles, laptops, mobile phones, cameras, etc   .
- A dry cell is a type of primary cell that has a paste-like electrolyte and a zinc anode that also serves as the container . The cathode is a carbon rod surrounded by a mixture of manganese dioxide and carbon powder .
- The chemical reaction in a dry cell is: Zn(s) + 2MnO2(s) + 2NH4+(aq) → Zn(NH3)2 2+(aq) + Mn2O3(s) + H2O(l) + 2e− .
- The voltage of a dry cell is about 1.5 V .
- A dry cell is commonly used in devices that require low current and low voltage, such as flashlights, toys, remote controls, etc .
