### CO-4 Develop understanding of the sources, impurities and hardness of water, apply the concepts of

- Sources of water: Water is a natural resource that is essential for life. Water can be obtained from various sources, such as surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), groundwater (wells, springs, etc.), rainwater, seawater, and ice and snow. Different sources of water have different characteristics and quality, depending on the factors that affect them, such as climate, geology, pollution, and human activities  .
- Impurities in water: Water is rarely pure in nature. It contains various dissolved and suspended substances that may be organic or inorganic, natural or anthropogenic, beneficial or harmful. Some of the common impurities in water are minerals, salts, metals, gases, microorganisms, pesticides, industrial wastes, and sediments. Impurities in water can affect its physical, chemical, and biological properties, such as color, odor, taste, pH, hardness, conductivity, turbidity, and dissolved oxygen .
- Hardness of water: Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. These ions form insoluble compounds with soap, causing it to precipitate and form scum. Hard water also causes scaling or deposition of minerals on pipes, boilers, heaters, and other equipment, reducing their efficiency and lifespan. Hardness of water can be classified as temporary or carbonate hardness, and permanent or non-carbonate hardness, depending on the type of salts that cause it. Temporary hardness is caused by bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, and can be removed by boiling or adding lime. Permanent hardness is caused by sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, and can be removed by ion exchange or chemical precipitation  .
