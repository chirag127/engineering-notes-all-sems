- Atomic structure refers to the arrangement of protons, neutrons and electrons in an atom. Protons and neutrons form the nucleus, which is the central core of the atom, while electrons orbit around the nucleus in shells or orbitals. The number of protons in an atom determines its atomic number and its identity as an element, while the number of neutrons determines its mass number and its isotopes. The number of electrons in an atom determines its charge and its chemical properties.
- Molecular structure refers to the arrangement of atoms in a molecule, which is a group of two or more atoms held together by chemical bonds. The type and number of bonds between atoms determine the shape and polarity of the molecule, which affect its physical and chemical properties. The most common types of bonds are covalent bonds, where atoms share electrons, and ionic bonds, where atoms transfer electrons and form oppositely charged ions. Other types of bonds include metallic bonds, where electrons are delocalized among metal atoms, and hydrogen bonds, where a hydrogen atom is attracted to a highly electronegative atom such as oxygen or nitrogen.
- Chemistry of atomic and molecular structure is the study of how the structure of atoms and molecules affects their behavior and interactions. It involves concepts such as atomic orbitals, electron configuration, valence shell electron pair repulsion (VSEPR) theory, molecular orbital theory, hybridization, resonance, bond order, bond length, bond angle, bond energy, electronegativity, dipole moment, intermolecular forces, etc. It also involves methods such as spectroscopy, crystallography, mass spectrometry, etc. to determine the structure of atoms and molecules.
