# Nanomaterials, Carbon Nanotubes (CNT)

- Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the nanometer range (1-100 nm).
- Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) are a type of nanomaterial that are composed of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice and rolled into a cylindrical shape.
- CNTs can have different structures depending on how the hexagons are oriented along the tube axis. These structures are called chiralities and are denoted by a pair of integers (n,m) that indicate how many times the hexagonal lattice is shifted along the tube circumference and length, respectively.
- CNTs can be classified into two main types: single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs). SWCNTs consist of a single layer of carbon atoms, while MWCNTs consist of multiple concentric layers of carbon atoms.
- CNTs have remarkable physical and chemical properties, such as high tensile strength, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, optical activity, and chemical stability. These properties depend on the chirality, diameter, length, and functionalization of the CNTs.
- CNTs have potential applications in various fields, such as nanoelectronics, nanomedicine, nanocomposites, energy storage, and sensors. Some examples of CNT-based devices are field-effect transistors, solar cells, drug delivery systems, biosensors, and supercapacitors.
