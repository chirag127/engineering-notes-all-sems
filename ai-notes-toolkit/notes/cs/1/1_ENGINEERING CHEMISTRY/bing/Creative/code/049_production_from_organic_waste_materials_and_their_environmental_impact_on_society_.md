# Production from organic waste materials and their environmental impact on society

Organic waste materials are any biodegradable substances that originate from plants or animals, such as food scraps, yard trimmings, paper, wood, leather, etc. Organic waste materials can be used as a source of energy, nutrients, and materials for various purposes, such as composting, biogas production, biofuels, bioplastics, etc. However, organic waste materials also have environmental impacts on society, depending on how they are managed and utilized.

Some of the environmental impacts of organic waste materials are:

- **Greenhouse gas emissions**: Organic waste materials that are disposed of in landfills or dumps undergo anaerobic decomposition, which produces methane, a potent greenhouse gas that contributes to global warming and climate change . Methane emissions from landfills and dumps are one of the major sources of human-generated methane in the world. On the other hand, organic waste materials that are used for biogas production or biofuels can reduce greenhouse gas emissions by displacing fossil fuels and capturing methane.
- **Soil and water pollution**: Organic waste materials that are not properly treated or disposed of can contaminate soil and water resources with pathogens, toxins, nutrients, and organic matter. This can cause health problems for humans and animals, as well as eutrophication, algal blooms, and oxygen depletion in aquatic ecosystems . Composting organic waste materials can reduce soil and water pollution by stabilizing organic matter, reducing pathogens, and improving soil quality and fertility.
- **Resource depletion**: Organic waste materials that are not recovered or recycled can represent a loss of valuable resources that could be used for other purposes, such as energy, materials, or nutrients. This can lead to increased demand for natural resources, such as land, water, fossil fuels, minerals, etc., which can have negative environmental and social impacts, such as deforestation, desertification, biodiversity loss, conflict, poverty, etc . Valorizing organic waste materials can reduce resource depletion by creating new products and services from waste, such as bioplastics, fertilizers, animal feed, etc.

Therefore, organic waste materials have both positive and negative environmental impacts on society, depending on how they are managed and utilized. The best practices for organic waste management are to reduce, reuse, recycle, and recover organic waste materials, and to avoid or minimize their disposal in landfills or dumps. This can help to mitigate greenhouse gas emissions, soil and water pollution, and resource depletion, and to create a circular and sustainable economy from waste .
