# Chemicals, Synthesis of typical organic compounds by conventional and Green route (Adipic acid)

- Adipic acid is a dicarboxylic acid with the formula HOOC(CH2)4COOH. It is mainly used as a precursor for the production of nylon 6,6.
- The conventional route of synthesis of adipic acid involves the oxidation of benzene or cyclohexane with nitric acid or air, followed by hydrolysis of the resulting cyclohexanone oxime. This process generates large amounts of nitrous oxide, a greenhouse gas, and nitric acid, a corrosive and toxic substance.
- The green route of synthesis of adipic acid aims to use renewable and biodegradable feedstocks, such as glucose, and to minimize the environmental impact of the process. Some examples of green routes are:
  - The oxidation of glucose with nitric acid to produce glucaric acid, which is then hydrogenated to adipic acid. This route requires less temperature and pressure than the conventional route, and avoids the formation of nitrous oxide. However, it still uses nitric acid as an oxidant, which is not environmentally friendly.
  - The oxidation of glucose with hydrogen peroxide and a metal catalyst, such as tungsten or molybdenum, to produce glucaric acid, which is then hydrogenated to adipic acid. This route uses a green oxidant and a recyclable catalyst, and operates under mild conditions. However, it suffers from low yield and selectivity of glucaric acid.
  - The oxidation of glucose with oxygen and a metal catalyst, such as gold or palladium, to produce gluconic acid, which is then dehydrated to 2-keto-gluconic acid, and finally decarboxylated to adipic acid. This route uses a green oxidant and a recyclable catalyst, and operates under mild conditions. It also has a high yield and selectivity of adipic acid.
