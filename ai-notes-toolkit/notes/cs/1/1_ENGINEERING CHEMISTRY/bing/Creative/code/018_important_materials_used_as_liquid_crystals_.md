# Important Materials Used as Liquid Crystals

Liquid crystals are substances that have properties of both liquids and solids. They can flow like liquids, but their molecules are arranged in an ordered way like solids. Liquid crystals are widely used in displays, sensors, thermometers, and other devices.

Some of the important materials used as liquid crystals are:

- **Nematic liquid crystals**: These are the most common type of liquid crystals. They have no positional order, but their molecules are aligned along a common direction called the director. Nematic liquid crystals can change their orientation when an electric field is applied, which makes them useful for displays. Some examples of nematic liquid crystals are 4-cyano-4'-pentylbiphenyl (5CB), 4-cyano-4'-octylbiphenyl (8CB), and 4-cyano-4'-heptyl-terphenyl (7HT).
- **Smectic liquid crystals**: These are liquid crystals that have a layered structure, where the molecules are arranged in parallel planes. Within each layer, the molecules can have different degrees of order and orientation. Smectic liquid crystals can exhibit various phases, such as smectic A, smectic C, smectic F, and smectic H. Some examples of smectic liquid crystals are 4-octyl-4'-cyanobiphenyl (OCB), 4-(trans-4'-n-hexylcyclohexyl)isothiocyanatobenzene (HAT6), and 4-(trans-4'-n-pentylcyclohexyl)isothiocyanatobenzene (PCH5).
- **Cholesteric liquid crystals**: These are liquid crystals that have a helical structure, where the director rotates along the axis of the helix. The pitch of the helix determines the color of the liquid crystal, which can change with temperature, pressure, or electric field. Cholesteric liquid crystals are also known as chiral nematic liquid crystals, because they are derived from nematic liquid crystals with chiral molecules. Some examples of cholesteric liquid crystals are cholesteryl chloride, cholesteryl oleyl carbonate, and cholesteryl nonanoate.
- **Ferroelectric liquid crystals**: These are liquid crystals that have a permanent electric dipole moment, which can be switched by an electric field. Ferroelectric liquid crystals are usually smectic C liquid crystals with chiral molecules, which form a tilted structure. Ferroelectric liquid crystals can have fast response times and high contrast ratios, which make them suitable for displays and optical devices. Some examples of ferroelectric liquid crystals are (S)-4-(2-methylbutyl)phenyl-4'-octyloxybiphenyl-4-carboxylate (DOBAMBC), (S)-4-(2-methylbutyl)phenyl-4'-heptyloxybiphenyl-4-carboxylate (HOBAMBC), and (S)-4-(2-methylbutyl)phenyl-4'-hexyloxybiphenyl-4-carboxylate (HOBAMBC).
- **Discotic liquid crystals**: These are liquid crystals that have disc-shaped molecules, which can form columns or stacks. Discotic liquid crystals can have different types of order, such as columnar, nematic, or hexagonal. Discotic liquid crystals can have high charge carrier mobility and self-assembly properties, which make them attractive for organic electronics and nanotechnology. Some examples of discotic liquid crystals are hexa-peri-hexabenzocoronene (HBC), hexakis(hexyloxy)triphenylene (HAT6), and hexakis(alkoxy)benzene (HAB).
