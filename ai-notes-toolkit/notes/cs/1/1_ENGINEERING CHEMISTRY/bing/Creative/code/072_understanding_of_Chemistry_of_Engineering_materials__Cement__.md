# Chemistry of Engineering Materials (Cement)

Cement is a binding material that is widely used in construction and civil engineering. It is made from raw materials that are mainly composed of calcium carbonate, alumina, silica, iron oxide, and other minor elements. Cement is produced by heating the raw materials in a kiln at high temperatures, forming a clinker that is then ground into a fine powder. Cement can be classified into different types based on its composition, properties, and applications.

## Composition of Cement

The main chemical compounds that make up cement are:

- Tricalcium silicate (3CaO · SiO2), also known as alite, which is responsible for the initial setting and early strength development of cement.
- Dicalcium silicate (2CaO · SiO2), also known as belite, which contributes to the long-term strength and durability of cement.
- Tricalcium aluminate (3CaO · Al2O3), also known as aluminate, which reacts rapidly with water and causes flash setting and heat generation of cement.
- Tetracalcium aluminoferrite (4CaO · Al2O3Fe2O3), also known as ferrite, which influences the color and resistance to sulfates of cement.

Other minor compounds that are present in cement are:

- Calcium sulfate (CaSO4), which is added to control the setting time and hydration rate of cement.
- Magnesium oxide (MgO), which affects the volume stability and soundness of cement.
- Sodium oxide (Na2O) and potassium oxide (K2O), which are alkalis that can cause alkali-aggregate reaction and deterioration of concrete.
- Titanium oxide (TiO2), which imparts a white color to cement.

## Properties of Cement

The properties of cement that are important for engineering applications are:

- Fineness, which is the measure of the particle size distribution of cement. Fineness affects the rate of hydration, water demand, workability, and strength of cement.
- Setting time, which is the time required for cement to harden and lose its plasticity. Setting time depends on the type and amount of cement, water, admixtures, and temperature. Setting time can be divided into initial setting time, which is the time when cement starts to stiffen, and final setting time, which is the time when cement reaches its maximum hardness.
- Hydration, which is the chemical reaction between cement and water that produces heat and forms a solid mass of hydrated products. Hydration is influenced by the composition, fineness, water-cement ratio, curing conditions, and admixtures of cement. Hydration results in the development of strength, durability, and shrinkage of cement.
- Strength, which is the ability of cement to resist external forces. Strength is determined by the type and amount of cement, water-cement ratio, curing conditions, and admixtures of cement. Strength can be measured by compressive strength, which is the resistance to crushing, and tensile strength, which is the resistance to pulling.
- Durability, which is the ability of cement to withstand environmental conditions and chemical attacks. Durability is affected by the type and amount of cement, water-cement ratio, curing conditions, and admixtures of cement. Durability can be evaluated by the resistance to sulfate attack, chloride attack, alkali-aggregate reaction, carbonation, freezing and thawing, abrasion, and corrosion.

## Types of Cement

There are different types of cement that are used for various purposes and applications. Some of the common types of cement are:

- Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC), which is the most widely used type of cement. It has a moderate strength and durability and can be used for general construction and concrete works.
- Rapid Hardening Cement (RHC), which is a type of OPC that has a higher fineness and a lower water-cement ratio. It has a higher strength and a shorter setting time and can be used for fast-track construction and repair works.
- Low Heat Cement (LHC), which is a type of OPC that has a lower content of tricalcium aluminate and a higher content of dicalcium silicate. It has a lower heat of hydration and a slower strength development and can be used for mass concrete works and dams.
- Sulphate Resisting Cement (SRC), which is a type of OPC that has a lower content of tricalcium aluminate and a higher content of tetracalcium aluminoferrite. It has a higher resistance to sulfate attack and can be used for marine structures
