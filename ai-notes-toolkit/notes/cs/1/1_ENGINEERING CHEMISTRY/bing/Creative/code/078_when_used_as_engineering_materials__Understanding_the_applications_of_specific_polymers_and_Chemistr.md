# Polymers and their applications as engineering materials

Polymers are large molecules that are composed of repeating units called monomers. Polymers can have different properties depending on the type, arrangement, and bonding of the monomers. Polymers can be classified into two main categories: thermoplastics and thermosets.

## Thermoplastics

Thermoplastics are polymers that can be melted and reshaped by heating and cooling. They are usually linear or branched chains of monomers that are held together by weak intermolecular forces, such as van der Waals forces or hydrogen bonds. Thermoplastics can be recycled and reused, but they may degrade or lose their properties over time. Some examples of thermoplastics are:

- Polyethylene (PE): A simple and widely used polymer that has good chemical resistance, low density, and high flexibility. It is used for packaging, bottles, pipes, films, and insulation.
- Polypropylene (PP): A polymer that has high strength, stiffness, and resistance to heat and chemicals. It is used for automotive parts, containers, carpets, and textiles.
- Polyvinyl chloride (PVC): A polymer that has good electrical and thermal insulation, fire resistance, and durability. It is used for pipes, cables, flooring, and window frames.
- Polystyrene (PS): A polymer that has high clarity, rigidity, and impact resistance. It is used for food packaging, disposable cups, and foam insulation.
- Polyethylene terephthalate (PET): A polymer that has high strength, transparency, and resistance to moisture and gases. It is used for bottles, fabrics, and films.

## Thermosets

Thermosets are polymers that cannot be melted and reshaped by heating and cooling. They are usually cross-linked networks of monomers that are held together by strong covalent bonds. Thermosets are cured or hardened by heat, pressure, or chemical reactions. They are not recyclable and can only be decomposed by burning. Some examples of thermosets are:

- Epoxy: A polymer that has high strength, adhesion, and resistance to chemicals and corrosion. It is used for coatings, adhesives, and composite materials.
- Polyurethane (PU): A polymer that has high elasticity, toughness, and resistance to abrasion and impact. It is used for foams, elastomers, and coatings.
- Bakelite: A polymer that has high electrical and thermal insulation, hardness, and stability. It is used for electrical switches, handles, and knobs.
- Melamine: A polymer that has high resistance to heat, fire, and stains. It is used for laminates, tableware, and countertops.

## Chemistry of polymers

The chemistry of polymers involves the synthesis, structure, and properties of the monomers and the polymers. The synthesis of polymers can be done by two main methods: addition polymerization and condensation polymerization.

### Addition polymerization

Addition polymerization is a process in which monomers are added together to form a polymer without the loss of any atoms or molecules. The monomers must have a double or triple bond that can be broken and joined with other monomers. The polymerization can be initiated by heat, light, or a catalyst. The polymerization can be classified into three types: chain-growth, step-growth, and ring-opening.

- Chain-growth polymerization: A process in which the polymer chain grows by adding one monomer at a time to the active end of the chain. The chain can have a radical, cationic, or anionic center that reacts with the monomer. The polymerization can be controlled by the concentration, temperature, and type of the initiator and the monomer. Examples of chain-growth polymers are PE, PP, PVC, and PS.
- Step-growth polymerization: A process in which the polymer chain grows by adding two or more monomers at a time to the chain ends. The monomers must have two or more functional groups that can react with each other. The polymerization can be controlled by the stoichiometry, temperature, and type of the monomers and the catalyst. Examples of step-growth polymers are PET, PU, and epoxy.
