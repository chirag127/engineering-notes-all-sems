### 4. To enable the students to understand and apply detailed concepts of water source, water impurities, hardness

- Water source is the origin of water that is used for various purposes, such as drinking, irrigation, industry, etc. Water sources can be classified into surface water and groundwater, depending on whether they are found above or below the ground level.
- Surface water sources include rivers, lakes, ponds, streams, reservoirs, etc. They are generally more accessible and abundant, but also more prone to contamination and pollution from human and natural activities. Surface water sources may also vary in quantity and quality depending on the season, climate, and geography.
- Groundwater sources include wells, springs, aquifers, etc. They are generally more reliable and consistent, but also more difficult and expensive to access and manage. Groundwater sources may also be affected by overexploitation, salinization, and contamination from human and natural activities.
- Water impurities are any substances or organisms that are present in water and affect its quality and suitability for various uses. Water impurities can be classified into physical, chemical, and biological, depending on their nature and origin.
- Physical impurities are those that alter the appearance, taste, odor, or temperature of water, such as turbidity, color, suspended solids, dissolved gases, etc. They are usually caused by soil erosion, sedimentation, weathering, etc. Physical impurities can be removed by filtration, sedimentation, aeration, etc.
- Chemical impurities are those that change the composition, pH, hardness, or conductivity of water, such as salts, metals, acids, bases, pesticides, etc. They are usually caused by natural processes, such as dissolution, precipitation, oxidation, etc., or human activities, such as agriculture, industry, mining, etc. Chemical impurities can be removed by coagulation, precipitation, ion exchange, adsorption, etc.
- Biological impurities are those that introduce living organisms or their products into water, such as bacteria, viruses, protozoa, algae, fungi, etc. They are usually caused by organic matter decomposition, animal and human wastes, sewage, etc. Biological impurities can cause diseases, such as cholera, typhoid, dysentery, etc. Biological impurities can be removed by disinfection, chlorination, ozonation, ultraviolet radiation, etc.
- Hardness is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. Hardness affects the properties and uses of water, such as its ability to form lather with soap, its corrosiveness, its scaling tendency, etc. Hardness can be classified into temporary and permanent, depending on whether it can be removed by boiling or not.
- Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium in water. It can be removed by boiling, which converts the bicarbonates into insoluble carbonates, which precipitate out of the water. Temporary hardness can also be removed by adding lime, which reacts with the bicarbonates and forms insoluble hydroxides, which precipitate out of the water.
- Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of chlorides, sulfates, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium in water. It cannot be removed by boiling, as these salts are soluble in water. Permanent hardness can be removed by adding soda ash, which reacts with the salts and forms insoluble carbonates, which precipitate out of the water. Permanent hardness can also be removed by ion exchange, which replaces the calcium and magnesium ions with sodium ions, which do not cause hardness.
