# Theoretical calculation of calorific value by Dulong’s method, Ranking of Coal, Analysis of coal

- Calorific value of coal is the amount of heat released by the complete combustion of a unit mass of coal. It is an important parameter for evaluating the quality and efficiency of coal.
- Dulong's method is a formula that estimates the calorific value of coal from its ultimate analysis, which is the percentage of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen and sulfur in coal.
- Dulong's formula is given by:

$$CV = 337C + 1442(H - O/8) + 93S$$

where CV is the calorific value in kJ/kg, and C, H, O, S are the percentages of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and sulfur in coal, respectively.

- Dulong's formula is based on the assumption that the heat of combustion of an organic compound is nearly equal to the heats of combustion of the elements in it, multiplied by their percentage content in the compound .
- Dulong's formula has some limitations, such as:
  - It does not account for the moisture and ash content of coal, which affect the calorific value.
  - It does not account for the variation of the heats of combustion of the elements with temperature and pressure.
  - It does not account for the formation of complex compounds during coal combustion, such as CO, CO2, H2O, SO2, etc.
  - It does not account for the variation of the calorific value of coal with its rank and type.
- Therefore, Dulong's formula is only an approximation and should be used with caution. Some modifications and corrections have been proposed to improve the accuracy of Dulong's formula, such as the modified Dulong formula and the Vandralek formula .

- Ranking of coal is the classification of coal according to its degree of metamorphism, or the change in its physical and chemical properties due to the action of heat and pressure over time. The rank of coal reflects its maturity, quality and potential uses.
- The main ranks of coal are, from lowest to highest:
  - Peat: The lowest rank of coal, formed from partially decomposed plant matter in wetlands. It has a high moisture content, low calorific value, and high volatile matter. It is not a true coal, but a precursor of coal.
  - Lignite: The lowest rank of true coal, formed from peat under moderate pressure and temperature. It has a brown color, a high moisture content, a low calorific value, and a high volatile matter. It is also called brown coal.
  - Sub-bituminous: A rank of coal intermediate between lignite and bituminous, formed from lignite under higher pressure and temperature. It has a black color, a lower moisture content, a higher calorific value, and a lower volatile matter than lignite. It is also called black lignite.
  - Bituminous: A high rank of coal, formed from sub-bituminous coal under higher pressure and temperature. It has a black color, a low moisture content, a high calorific value, and a moderate volatile matter. It is the most abundant and widely used rank of coal. It is also called soft coal.
  - Anthracite: The highest rank of coal, formed from bituminous coal under very high pressure and temperature. It has a shiny black color, a very low moisture content, a very high calorific value, and a very low volatile matter. It is the hardest and cleanest rank of coal. It is also called hard coal or stone coal.
- The rank of coal can be determined by various parameters, such as:
  - Fixed carbon: The percentage of carbon in coal that remains after the volatile matter is driven off by heating. It increases with the rank of coal.
  - Volatile matter: The percentage of coal that is converted into gases and vapors by heating. It decreases with the rank of coal.
  - Moisture: The percentage of water in coal. It decreases with the rank of coal.
  - Ash: The percentage of inorganic matter in coal that remains after combustion. It varies with the rank of coal and the source of coal.
  - Calorific value: The amount of heat released by the combustion of a unit mass of coal. It increases with the rank of coal.
  - Carbon-hydrogen ratio: The ratio of the percentage of carbon to the percentage of hydrogen in coal. It increases with the rank of coal.
