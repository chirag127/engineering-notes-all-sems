# Proximate and Ultimate Analysis Method

- Proximate analysis is the technique used to analyze the compounds in a mixture, such as coal, biomass, or biogas.
- Ultimate analysis is the technique used to analyze the elements present in a compound, such as carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, sulfur, and oxygen.
- Proximate analysis consists of determination of ash content, moisture content, volatile matter, and fixed carbon on an as-received basis.
- Ultimate analysis provides a convenient method for reporting the major organic elemental composition of coal or other fuels.
- Proximate and ultimate analysis can be used to estimate the heating value, combustion characteristics, and environmental impacts of solid fuels and biogas .

# Numerical Problems

- Example 1: A coal sample has the following proximate analysis (wt%): moisture 10, volatile matter 35, fixed carbon 45, ash 10. Calculate the mass of air required for complete combustion of 1 kg of coal, assuming that the coal contains only carbon and the air is dry. Assume that the excess air is 20%.
- Solution 1: The mass of carbon in 1 kg of coal is 0.45 kg. The stoichiometric air required for complete combustion of carbon is 2.67 kg of air per kg of carbon. The actual air required is 1.2 times the stoichiometric air, which is 3.2 kg of air per kg of carbon. Therefore, the mass of air required for complete combustion of 1 kg of coal is 3.2 x 0.45 = 1.44 kg.
- Example 2: A biogas sample has the following ultimate analysis (wt%): carbon 50, hydrogen 10, nitrogen 5, oxygen 35. Calculate the higher heating value (HHV) of the biogas in kJ/kg, assuming that the biogas contains only methane and carbon dioxide. Use the following values for the HHV of methane and carbon dioxide: 55.5 MJ/kg and -393.5 kJ/mol, respectively.
- Solution 2: The mass of methane in 1 kg of biogas is 0.5 kg. The mass of carbon dioxide in 1 kg of biogas is 0.5 kg. The HHV of biogas is the sum of the HHV of methane and carbon dioxide, multiplied by their respective masses. Therefore, the HHV of biogas is (55.5 x 0.5) + (-393.5 x 0.5 x 44/12) = 24.8 MJ/kg.

# Chemistry of Biogas

- Biogas is a mixture of gases produced by the anaerobic digestion of organic matter, such as animal manure, sewage, or crop residues.
- The main components of biogas are methane (CH4) and carbon dioxide (CO2), which account for about 50-70% and 30-50% of the biogas volume, respectively.
- Biogas may also contain small amounts of other gases, such as hydrogen sulfide (H2S), ammonia (NH3), hydrogen (H2), nitrogen (N2), and oxygen (O2), depending on the feedstock and the digestion conditions.
- Biogas can be used as a renewable source of energy for heating, cooking, electricity generation, or vehicle fuel.
- Biogas can also reduce greenhouse gas emissions by capturing methane that would otherwise be released to the atmosphere from organic waste decomposition.
