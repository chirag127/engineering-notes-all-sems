# Polymer Composites

- Polymer composites are **mixtures of two materials** that result in **synergistic properties**.
- Most polymer composites consist of:
  - **Continuous phase or matrix** which is typically the resin or polymer phase.
  - **Discontinuous phase or reinforcement** which is typically a fiber, particle, or flake that provides strength, stiffness, or other desirable properties.
- Polymer composites are classified based on the **type of matrix** and the **type of reinforcement**.
  - **Thermoset matrix composites** are made of polymers that undergo irreversible chemical reactions during curing, such as epoxy, polyester, or phenolic resins.
  - **Thermoplastic matrix composites** are made of polymers that can be melted and reshaped, such as polyethylene, polypropylene, or nylon.
  - **Fiber-reinforced composites** are made of continuous or discontinuous fibers embedded in a matrix, such as carbon fiber, glass fiber, or natural fiber composites.
  - **Particle-reinforced composites** are made of fine particles dispersed in a matrix, such as ceramic, metal, or polymer composites.
  - **Flake-reinforced composites** are made of thin platelets aligned in a matrix, such as graphite, mica, or metal composites.
- Polymer composites are increasingly being used in various engineering fields, such as aerospace, automotive, biomedical, construction, and energy, because of their **high strength-to-weight ratio, corrosion resistance, fatigue resistance, thermal insulation, and design flexibility** .
