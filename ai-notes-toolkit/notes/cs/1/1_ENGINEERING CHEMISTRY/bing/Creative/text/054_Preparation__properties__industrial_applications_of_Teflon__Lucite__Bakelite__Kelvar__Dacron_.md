# Preparation, properties, industrial applications of Teflon, Lucite, Bakelite, Kelvar, Dacron

## Teflon

- Teflon is a synthetic polymer of tetrafluoroethylene (C2F4) with the formula (C2F4)n.
- It is prepared by free radical polymerization of tetrafluoroethylene in a pressurized vessel at high temperature.
- It has the following properties:
  - It is a white solid compound at room temperature with a density of about 2.2 g/cm3.
  - It has a high melting point of about 600 K and a low glass transition temperature of about 130 K.
  - It is chemically inert and resistant to most acids, bases, solvents, and oxidizing agents. The only chemicals that can affect it are alkali metals and some fluorinating agents.
  - It has a low coefficient of friction and a high non-stick property, making it suitable for coating surfaces that require lubrication or easy cleaning.
  - It has a low water absorption capacity and a high dielectric strength, making it useful for electrical insulation and waterproofing.
- It has the following industrial applications:
  - It is widely used as a coating material for cookware, bakeware, irons, hair dryers, and other appliances that require non-stick and heat-resistant surfaces.
  - It is also used as a coating material for pipes, valves, seals, gaskets, bearings, and other components that require corrosion resistance and low friction.
  - It is also used as a fabric protector for clothing, carpets, upholstery, and other textiles that require stain resistance and durability.
  - It is also used as a raw material for producing other fluoropolymers, such as PTFE, FEP, PFA, and ETFE, which have various applications in aerospace, chemical, medical, and electrical industries.

## Lucite

- Lucite is a trade name for polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA), a transparent thermoplastic polymer of methyl methacrylate (MMA) with the formula (C5O2H8)n.
- It is prepared by free radical polymerization of methyl methacrylate in the presence of an initiator, such as benzoyl peroxide, and a chain transfer agent, such as dodecyl mercaptan.
- It has the following properties:
  - It is a clear, colorless, and brittle solid at room temperature with a density of about 1.18 g/cm3.
  - It has a high glass transition temperature of about 378 K and a low melting point of about 463 K.
  - It is resistant to weathering, UV radiation, and most chemicals, except for strong acids, bases, and organic solvents.
  - It has a high refractive index of about 1.49 and a low birefringence, making it suitable for optical applications.
  - It has a high tensile strength, modulus of elasticity, and hardness, but a low impact strength and elongation at break, making it prone to cracking and shattering.
- It has the following industrial applications:
  - It is widely used as a substitute for glass in windows, lenses, screens, signs, displays, and other transparent or translucent objects that require clarity, light transmission, and shatter resistance.
  - It is also used as a molding material for making various objects, such as jewelry, toys, furniture, art, and dental prosthetics, that require aesthetic appeal, durability, and biocompatibility.
  - It is also used as a coating material for enhancing the gloss, scratch resistance, and UV protection of other substrates, such as wood, metal, and paper.
