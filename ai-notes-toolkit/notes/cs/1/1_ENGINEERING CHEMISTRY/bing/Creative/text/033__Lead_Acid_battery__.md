# Lead Acid Battery

- A lead acid battery is a type of **rechargeable battery** that uses **lead** and **lead oxide** as electrodes and **sulfuric acid** as electrolyte.
- It was the **first** type of rechargeable battery ever invented by **Gaston Planté** in **1859**.
- It has a relatively **low energy density** compared to modern batteries, but it is **cheap**, **reliable**, and **easy to manufacture** .
- It is widely used in **automotive**, **industrial**, and **backup power** applications .
- It operates on the principle of **reversible chemical reactions** between the electrodes and the electrolyte during **charging** and **discharging** .
- The main reactions are:

  - Charging: Pb + PbO2 + 2H2SO4 -> 2PbSO4 + 2H2O
  - Discharging: 2PbSO4 + 2H2O -> Pb + PbO2 + 2H2SO4

- The **voltage** of a single lead acid cell is about **2 V**, and the **capacity** depends on the **size** and **design** of the battery.
- The **efficiency** of a lead acid battery is about **75-85%**, and the **lifetime** is about **3-5 years** or **500-1000 cycles** .
- The main **advantages** of lead acid batteries are:

  - Low cost
  - High power output
  - Good low-temperature performance
  - Simple and safe charging
  - Recyclable

- The main **disadvantages** of lead acid batteries are:

  - Low energy density
  - Heavy and bulky
  - Sulfation and corrosion
  - Water loss and maintenance
  - Environmental hazards
