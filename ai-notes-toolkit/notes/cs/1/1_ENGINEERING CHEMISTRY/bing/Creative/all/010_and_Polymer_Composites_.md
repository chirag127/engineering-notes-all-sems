# Polymer Composites

- A polymer composite is a multi-phase material in which reinforcing fillers are integrated with a polymer matrix, resulting in synergistic mechanical properties that cannot be achieved from either component alone.
- A polymer is a natural or synthetic substance composed of very large molecules, called macromolecules, that are multiples of simpler chemical units called monomers.
- Most polymer composites consist of a continuous phase or matrix, which is typically the resin or polymer phase, and a dispersed phase or reinforcement, which is typically a fiber, particle, or flake.
- Polymer composites are increasingly being used in various engineering fields, such as aerospace, automotive, biomedical, construction, and energy, due to their advantages over conventional materials, such as high strength-to-weight ratio, corrosion resistance, design flexibility, and low cost .
- Some examples of polymer composites are:
  - Carbon fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP), which is used for aircraft structures, wind turbine blades, and sports equipment.
  - Glass fiber reinforced polymer (GFRP), which is used for boat hulls, pipes, and tanks.
  - Wood plastic composite (WPC), which is used for decking, fencing, and furniture.
  - Metal matrix composite (MMC), which is used for engine components, brake discs, and armor.
  - Ceramic matrix composite (CMC), which is used for high-temperature applications, such as rocket nozzles, turbine blades, and heat exchangers.
