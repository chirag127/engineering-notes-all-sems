## Unit-4: 8

- This unit covers the topic of **linear equations** and their applications in various contexts.
- A linear equation is an equation that can be written in the form **ax + b = c**, where **a**, **b**, and **c** are constants and **x** is a variable.
- The solution of a linear equation is the value of **x** that makes the equation true. To find the solution, we can use different methods, such as **isolating x**, **substitution**, or **elimination**.
- A linear equation can represent a **relationship** between two quantities that are proportional or have a constant difference. For example, the equation **y = 2x + 5** represents a line with a slope of 2 and a y-intercept of 5.
- A linear equation can also model a **situation** that involves a constant rate of change or a fixed amount. For example, the equation **y = 50x + 100** models the cost of renting a car for **x** days, where the daily rate is 50 dollars and the initial fee is 100 dollars.
- A system of linear equations is a set of two or more linear equations that have the same variables. The solution of a system of linear equations is the ordered pair **(x, y)** that satisfies all the equations in the system. To find the solution, we can use different methods, such as **graphing**, **substitution**, or **elimination**.
- A system of linear equations can represent a **comparison** between two relationships or situations that involve linear equations. For example, the system **y = 2x + 5** and **y = 3x - 2** compares the lines with slopes 2 and 3 and y-intercepts 5 and -2, respectively.
- A system of linear equations can also model a **problem** that involves finding the values of two unknown quantities that are related by linear equations. For example, the system **2x + y = 10** and **3x - y = 8** models a problem where **x** and **y** are the prices of two items that have a total cost of 10 dollars and a difference of 8 dollars, respectively.
