# 4. To enable the students to understand and apply detailed concepts of water source, water impurities, hardness

- Water source: The origin or place from where water is obtained for various purposes such as drinking, irrigation, industry, etc. Examples of water sources are rivers, lakes, wells, springs, glaciers, rainwater, etc.
- Water impurities: Any substance or material that is present in water and affects its quality, appearance, taste, odor, or suitability for a specific use. Water impurities can be classified into two types: dissolved and suspended.
  - Dissolved impurities: These are the substances that are dissolved in water and cannot be seen by the naked eye. They include salts, minerals, metals, gases, organic compounds, etc. Dissolved impurities can affect the hardness, acidity, alkalinity, conductivity, and corrosiveness of water.
  - Suspended impurities: These are the substances that are suspended in water and can be seen by the naked eye or by using a microscope. They include sand, clay, silt, algae, bacteria, fungi, etc. Suspended impurities can affect the turbidity, color, odor, and biological quality of water.
- Hardness: The property of water that causes it to form scales or deposits when heated or to interfere with the action of soap or detergent. Hardness is mainly caused by the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium ions in water. Hardness can be classified into two types: temporary and permanent.
  - Temporary hardness: This is the hardness that can be removed by boiling water or by adding lime (calcium hydroxide). It is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium in water. When water is boiled, the bicarbonates decompose into carbonates, which precipitate as insoluble salts. The reaction is as follows:

    `Ca(HCO3)2 + heat -> CaCO3 + H2O + CO2`

    `Mg(HCO3)2 + heat -> MgCO3 + H2O + CO2`

  - Permanent hardness: This is the hardness that cannot be removed by boiling water or by adding lime. It is caused by the presence of chlorides, sulfates, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium in water. These salts do not decompose or precipitate when water is heated. The only way to remove permanent hardness is by using ion exchange methods or reverse osmosis. The reaction is as follows:

    `CaCl2 + 2NaOH -> Ca(OH)2 + 2NaCl`

    `MgSO4 + 2NaOH -> Mg(OH)2 + Na2SO4`
