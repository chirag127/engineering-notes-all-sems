# K4

- K4 is a **symmetric-key algorithm** that was used by the German Navy during World War II for encrypting messages.
- K4 is also known as **Tirpitz cipher** or **Offizier** (officer) cipher, as it was mainly used by high-ranking naval officers.
- K4 is a **four-rotor Enigma machine** with a **plugboard** and a **reflector**. It is similar to the three-rotor Enigma used by the German Army and Air Force, but with an additional rotor that increases the cryptographic strength.
- K4 has a **total of 26^4 = 456,976 possible rotor settings** and **150,738,274,937,250 possible plugboard settings**. However, some of these settings are equivalent or weak, and can be eliminated by cryptanalysis.
- K4 was **introduced in February 1942** and was used until the end of the war. It was **never broken** by the Allied codebreakers, despite their efforts and successes with other Enigma variants.
- K4 was **considered very secure** by the Germans, and was used for transmitting important strategic and operational information, such as the locations and movements of U-boats, battleships, and convoys.
- K4 was **also used for deception** by sending false or misleading messages to confuse the enemy. For example, in 1943, the Germans used K4 to send fake reports of a large naval force heading to Norway, in order to divert Allied attention from the real invasion of Sicily.
