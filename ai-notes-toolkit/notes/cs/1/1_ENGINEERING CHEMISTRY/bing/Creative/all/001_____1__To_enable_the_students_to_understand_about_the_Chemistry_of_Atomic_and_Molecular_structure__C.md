# Chemistry of Atomic and Molecular Structure

## 1.1 Atomic Structure

- An atom is the smallest unit of matter that retains the identity of an element.
- An atom consists of a dense nucleus surrounded by a cloud of electrons.
- The nucleus contains protons and neutrons, which are collectively called nucleons.
- Protons have a positive charge, neutrons have no charge, and electrons have a negative charge.
- The number of protons in an atom is called the atomic number (Z) and determines the identity of the element.
- The number of protons and neutrons in an atom is called the mass number (A) and determines the mass of the atom.
- Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons are called isotopes.
- The average mass of the isotopes of an element weighted by their natural abundance is called the atomic mass or atomic weight.
- The electrons in an atom occupy different energy levels or shells, which are further divided into subshells and orbitals.
- The arrangement of electrons in an atom is called the electronic configuration and follows the Aufbau principle, the Pauli exclusion principle, and the Hund's rule.
- The outermost electrons in an atom are called the valence electrons and determine the chemical properties and reactivity of the element.

## 1.2 Molecular Structure

- A molecule is a group of two or more atoms held together by chemical bonds.
- A chemical bond is a force of attraction between atoms that results from the sharing or transfer of electrons.
- There are three main types of chemical bonds: covalent, ionic, and metallic.
- A covalent bond is formed when two atoms share one or more pairs of electrons. Covalent bonds can be single, double, or triple, depending on the number of electron pairs shared.
- A covalent bond can be polar or nonpolar, depending on the difference in electronegativity between the bonded atoms. Electronegativity is a measure of the tendency of an atom to attract electrons in a bond.
- A polar covalent bond has an unequal distribution of electron density, resulting in partial positive and negative charges on the bonded atoms. A nonpolar covalent bond has an equal distribution of electron density, resulting in no net charge on the bonded atoms.
- An ionic bond is formed when one atom transfers one or more electrons to another atom, resulting in the formation of ions. Ions are atoms or groups of atoms that have a net positive or negative charge.
- A cation is a positively charged ion, and an anion is a negatively charged ion. Ionic bonds are usually formed between metals and nonmetals, or between polyatomic ions.
- A metallic bond is formed when metal atoms share a pool of delocalized electrons, resulting in a sea of electrons. Metallic bonds are usually formed between metal atoms of the same or different elements.
- The shape and geometry of a molecule are determined by the arrangement of the atoms and the type and number of bonds around the central atom.
- The shape and geometry of a molecule can be predicted using the valence shell electron pair repulsion (VSEPR) theory, which states that the electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and try to minimize the repulsion by adopting the most optimal shape.
- The shape and geometry of a molecule can also be influenced by the presence of lone pairs, which are nonbonding electron pairs around a central atom. Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding pairs and tend to distort the ideal geometry of a molecule.
- The shape and geometry of a molecule affect its physical and chemical properties, such as polarity, intermolecular forces, boiling point, melting point, solubility, and reactivity.
