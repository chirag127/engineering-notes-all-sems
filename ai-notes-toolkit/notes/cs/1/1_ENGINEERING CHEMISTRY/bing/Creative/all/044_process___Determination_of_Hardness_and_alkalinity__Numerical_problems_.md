# Process of Determination of Hardness and Alkalinity

## Alkalinity
- Alkalinity is the measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids.
- Alkalinity is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium, and other metals in water.
- Alkalinity is expressed as milligrams per liter of equivalent calcium carbonate (mg/L CaCO3).
- Alkalinity can be measured by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid (H2SO4), using a pH meter or an indicator to detect the endpoint.
- The endpoint of the titration depends on the type and amount of alkaline substances in water.
- The most common endpoints are pH 4.5, pH 8.3, and pH 10.0, corresponding to total, carbonate, and bicarbonate alkalinity, respectively.
- The volume of acid used to reach the endpoint is proportional to the alkalinity of the water sample.
- The alkalinity can be calculated using the following formula:

    Alkalinity (mg/L CaCO3) = (Volume of acid (mL) x Normality of acid (eq/L) x 50,000) / Volume of water sample (mL)

## Hardness
- Hardness is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- Hardness is mainly caused by the dissolution of limestone, dolomite, gypsum, and other minerals in water.
- Hardness is expressed as milligrams per liter of equivalent calcium carbonate (mg/L CaCO3).
- Hardness can be measured by titrating a water sample with a standard complexing agent, such as ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), using a buffer solution and an indicator to detect the endpoint.
- The endpoint of the titration is when all the calcium and magnesium ions are complexed by EDTA, and the indicator changes color from red to blue.
- The volume of EDTA used to reach the endpoint is proportional to the hardness of the water sample.
- The hardness can be calculated using the following formula:

    Hardness (mg/L CaCO3) = (Volume of EDTA (mL) x Normality of EDTA (eq/L) x 50,000) / Volume of water sample (mL)

## Numerical Problems
- Example 1: A water sample has a volume of 100 mL and requires 12.5 mL of 0.02 N H2SO4 to reach the endpoint of pH 4.5. What is the total alkalinity of the water sample?

    Solution: Total alkalinity (mg/L CaCO3) = (12.5 mL x 0.02 eq/L x 50,000) / 100 mL = 125 mg/L CaCO3

- Example 2: A water sample has a volume of 100 mL and requires 10 mL of 0.01 N EDTA to reach the endpoint of the titration with Eriochrome Black T indicator. What is the total hardness of the water sample?

    Solution: Total hardness (mg/L CaCO3) = (10 mL x 0.01 eq/L x 50,000) / 100 mL = 50 mg/L CaCO3
