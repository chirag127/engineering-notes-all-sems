# Polymers; Classification, Polymerization processes, Thermosetting and Thermoplastic

- Polymers are large molecules composed of repeating units called monomers, which are linked by covalent bonds.
- Polymers can be classified based on various factors, such as their chemical structure, their thermal behavior, their mode of synthesis, and their molecular forces .
- Based on their chemical structure, polymers can be divided into two main groups: thermosets and thermoplastics .
  - Thermosets are polymers that have cross-linked molecular chains, which give them high strength and rigidity. They cannot be melted or reshaped once they are cured by heat or chemical agents  . Examples of thermosets are epoxy, phenolic, and silicone resins.
  - Thermoplastics are polymers that have linear or slightly branched molecular chains, which allow them to be softened by heating and solidified by cooling. They can be repeatedly molded and recycled without losing their properties  . Examples of thermoplastics are polyethylene, polypropylene, and polystyrene.
- Based on their thermal behavior, polymers can be classified into two groups: crystalline and amorphous.
  - Crystalline polymers are polymers that have ordered and regular arrangements of molecular chains, which result in sharp melting points and high densities. They are usually opaque and have high tensile strength and stiffness. Examples of crystalline polymers are polyethylene, polypropylene, and nylon.
  - Amorphous polymers are polymers that have random and irregular arrangements of molecular chains, which result in broad melting ranges and low densities. They are usually transparent and have low tensile strength and stiffness. Examples of amorphous polymers are polystyrene, polyvinyl chloride, and polycarbonate.
- Based on their mode of synthesis, polymers can be classified into two types: addition and condensation .
  - Addition polymers are polymers that are formed by the addition of monomers without the elimination of any by-product. They are usually made by chain polymerization, which involves the initiation, propagation, and termination of a growing polymer chain . Examples of addition polymers are polyethylene, polypropylene, and polystyrene .
  - Condensation polymers are polymers that are formed by the condensation of monomers with the elimination of a small molecule, such as water or alcohol. They are usually made by step polymerization, which involves the formation of dimers, trimers, and larger oligomers until a high molecular weight polymer is obtained . Examples of condensation polymers are nylon, polyester, and polyurethane .
- Based on their molecular forces, polymers can be classified into four categories: elastomers, fibers, thermoplastics, and thermosets.
  - Elastomers are polymers that have weak intermolecular forces and high flexibility. They can be stretched to several times their original length and return to their original shape when the stress is removed. Examples of elastomers are natural rubber, synthetic rubber, and silicone.
  - Fibers are polymers that have strong intermolecular forces and high orientation. They have high tensile strength and stiffness, and can be spun into thin filaments. Examples of fibers are cotton, wool, silk, nylon, and polyester.
  - Thermoplastics are polymers that have moderate intermolecular forces and low orientation. They have moderate tensile strength and stiffness, and can be molded into various shapes. Examples of thermoplastics are polyethylene, polypropylene, and polystyrene.
  - Thermosets are polymers that have strong intermolecular forces and high cross-linking. They have high tensile strength and rigidity, and cannot be melted or reshaped. Examples of thermosets are epoxy, phenolic, and silicone resins.
