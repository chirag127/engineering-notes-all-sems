# Prevention and Control of Corrosion

Corrosion is the deterioration of a metal or its properties due to a reaction with its environment. Corrosion can cause loss of material, strength, functionality, and aesthetics of metal products. Corrosion can also pose safety and environmental hazards. Therefore, it is important to prevent and control corrosion in various applications.

Some of the methods to prevent and control corrosion are:

- **Material selection**: Choosing a metal or alloy that is resistant to the corrosive environment or compatible with the other metals in contact. For example, stainless steel is resistant to many acids and bases, while copper is compatible with brass and bronze.
- **Coating**: Applying a protective layer of paint, plastic, metal, or ceramic on the metal surface to isolate it from the corrosive environment. For example, galvanizing is a process of coating steel with zinc to prevent rusting.
- **Cathodic protection**: Applying an external electric current to the metal to make it the cathode of an electrochemical cell, thus preventing it from corroding. For example, underground pipelines and storage tanks are often cathodically protected by connecting them to a sacrificial anode, such as magnesium, that corrodes instead of the metal.
- **Corrosion inhibitors**: Adding chemicals to the corrosive environment to reduce the rate of corrosion by forming a protective film on the metal surface or interfering with the corrosion reaction. For example, sodium nitrite is a corrosion inhibitor for steel in cooling water systems.
- **Design modification**: Changing the shape, size, or arrangement of the metal parts to reduce the exposure to the corrosive environment or to avoid crevices, stress concentration, or galvanic coupling. For example, using smooth and rounded edges, avoiding sharp corners and cracks, and providing drainage and ventilation for metal structures.
