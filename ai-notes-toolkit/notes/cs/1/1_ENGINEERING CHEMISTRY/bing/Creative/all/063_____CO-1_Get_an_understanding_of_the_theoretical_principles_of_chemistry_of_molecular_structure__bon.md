# CO-1 Get an understanding of the theoretical principles of chemistry of molecular structure, bonding and reactions

- Molecular structure
  - Explain the concept of atoms, molecules, ions, and their symbols and formulas.
  - Describe the periodic table and its trends in atomic properties, such as atomic size, ionization energy, and electronegativity.
  - Apply the octet rule and Lewis structures to represent the valence electrons and bonding patterns of simple molecules and ions.
  - Predict the molecular geometry and polarity of molecules and ions using the VSEPR theory and dipole moments.
  - Compare and contrast the types of intermolecular forces, such as London dispersion, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding, and ion-dipole, and their effects on physical properties, such as boiling point, solubility, and vapor pressure.
- Bonding
  - Define the concepts of bond length, bond energy, bond order, and bond polarity.
  - Distinguish between ionic, covalent, and metallic bonds, and their properties, such as conductivity, hardness, and melting point.
  - Explain the hybridization theory and how it accounts for the formation of different types of covalent bonds, such as sigma and pi bonds, and the shapes of molecules, such as linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, etc.
  - Describe the molecular orbital theory and how it explains the electronic structure, bond order, and magnetic properties of diatomic molecules, such as H2, O2, N2, etc.
  - Apply the concept of resonance to represent the delocalization of electrons in molecules and ions, such as benzene, carbonate, nitrate, etc.
- Reactions
  - Classify the types of chemical reactions, such as synthesis, decomposition, single displacement, double displacement, combustion, oxidation-reduction, acid-base, and precipitation reactions, and their general characteristics and examples.
  - Write and balance chemical equations to represent chemical reactions, using symbols, formulas, coefficients, and phases.
  - Apply the law of conservation of mass and the mole concept to calculate the stoichiometric relationships between reactants and products in chemical reactions, such as mass, moles, volume, and concentration.
  - Identify the limiting reactant and the excess reactant in a chemical reaction, and calculate the theoretical yield, the actual yield, and the percent yield of a product.
  - Use the concept of equilibrium and the equilibrium constant to describe the dynamic state of reversible reactions, and how they are affected by changes in concentration, temperature, and pressure, according to Le Chatelier's principle.
