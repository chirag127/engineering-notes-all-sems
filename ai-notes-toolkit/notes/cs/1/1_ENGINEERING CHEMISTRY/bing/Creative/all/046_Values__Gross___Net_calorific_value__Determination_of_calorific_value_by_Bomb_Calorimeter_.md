# Values, Gross & Net calorific value, Determination of calorific value by Bomb Calorimeter

- Values are the numerical quantities that represent the worth or importance of something. They can be measured in different units depending on the context and the purpose.
- Calorific value is a value that measures the amount of heat or energy produced by a fuel during complete combustion. It is expressed in units of energy per unit mass or volume of the fuel, such as joules per kilogram (J/kg) or kilocalories per gram (kcal/g).
- Gross calorific value (GCV) is the total amount of heat or energy released when a fuel is burned with oxygen in a closed system, such as a bomb calorimeter. It includes the latent heat of vaporization of the water vapour produced from the combustion of hydrogen in the fuel and the water present as moisture in the fuel.
- Net calorific value (NCV) is the amount of heat or energy available from a fuel after subtracting the latent heat of vaporization of the water vapour produced from the combustion of hydrogen in the fuel and the water present as moisture in the fuel. It is the actual amount of heat or energy that can be utilized for practical purposes, such as heating or power generation.
- The formula for calculating the net calorific value from the gross calorific value is:

  NCV = GCV - 586 (water as moisture + water formed from H2) cal/g

  where 586 is the latent heat of vaporization of water in cal/g at 15.5°C, the standard reference temperature for calorific value measurements.

- A bomb calorimeter is an instrument used to determine the calorific value of a fuel by measuring the heat released from its combustion in a closed and constant-volume system. It consists of a strong metal container called a bomb, which holds the fuel sample and oxygen under high pressure, a water jacket that surrounds the bomb and absorbs the heat from the combustion, a stirrer that ensures uniform temperature distribution in the water, a thermometer that measures the temperature change in the water, and an ignition device that ignites the fuel sample inside the bomb.
- The working principle of a bomb calorimeter is based on the first law of thermodynamics, which states that the energy of a closed system is conserved. Therefore, the heat released from the combustion of the fuel sample in the bomb is equal to the heat absorbed by the water jacket and the bomb itself. The calorific value of the fuel can be calculated by using the following formula:

  GCV = (Qw + Qb) / m

  where Qw is the heat absorbed by the water, Qb is the heat absorbed by the bomb, and m is the mass of the fuel sample.

- The heat absorbed by the water can be calculated by multiplying the mass of the water, the specific heat capacity of the water, and the temperature change in the water:

  Qw = mw x cw x (Tf - Ti)

  where mw is the mass of the water, cw is the specific heat capacity of the water, Tf is the final temperature of the water, and Ti is the initial temperature of the water.

- The heat absorbed by the bomb can be calculated by multiplying the heat capacity of the bomb and the temperature change in the water:

  Qb = Cb x (Tf - Ti)

  where Cb is the heat capacity of the bomb, which can be determined by calibrating the bomb calorimeter with a standard fuel of known calorific value.

- The net calorific value of the fuel can be obtained by subtracting the latent heat of vaporization of the water vapour produced from the combustion of hydrogen in the fuel and the water present as moisture in the fuel from the gross calorific value. The amount of water vapour produced can be calculated by using the following formula:

  water vapour = 9 x H / 100

  where H is the percentage of hydrogen in the fuel by mass.

- The amount of water present as moisture in the fuel can be determined by drying the fuel sample in an oven and measuring the mass loss. The percentage of moisture in the fuel by mass can be calculated by using the following formula:

  moisture = (md - mw) / md x 100

  where md is the mass of the dry fuel sample and mw is the mass of the wet fuel sample.
