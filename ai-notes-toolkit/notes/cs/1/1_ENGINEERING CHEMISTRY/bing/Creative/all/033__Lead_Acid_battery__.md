# Lead Acid Battery

A lead acid battery is a type of rechargeable battery that was first invented in 1859 by French physicist Gaston Planté. It is the oldest type of rechargeable battery and is widely used in various applications, such as automotive, grid, and backup power.

## Structure and Operation

A lead acid battery consists of two electrodes submerged in an electrolyte of sulfuric acid. The positive electrode is made of grains of metallic lead oxide, while the negative electrode is attached to a grid of metallic lead. The electrodes are separated by a porous separator that prevents short circuits.

When the battery is charged, an electric current is applied to the electrodes, causing the lead oxide to be reduced to lead and the lead to be oxidized to lead sulfate. The sulfuric acid is also consumed and water is produced. The chemical reactions are as follows:

Positive electrode: PbO2 + 4H+ + SO42- + 2e- → PbSO4 + 2H2O

Negative electrode: Pb + SO42- → PbSO4 + 2e-

Overall: PbO2 + Pb + 2H2SO4 → 2PbSO4 + 2H2O

When the battery is discharged, the reverse reactions occur, producing electricity and restoring the original materials. The chemical reactions are as follows:

Positive electrode: PbSO4 + 2H2O → PbO2 + 4H+ + SO42- + 2e-

Negative electrode: PbSO4 + 2e- → Pb + SO42-

Overall: 2PbSO4 + 2H2O → PbO2 + Pb + 2H2SO4

## Types and Applications

Lead acid batteries are classified into two types: flooded and valve-regulated. Flooded batteries have excess electrolyte that covers the electrodes and allows gas to escape during charging. Valve-regulated batteries have a sealed case that prevents gas leakage and maintains internal pressure. They also have a mechanism to recombine the gas into water, reducing water loss.

Flooded batteries are cheaper and more durable, but they require regular maintenance, such as topping up the electrolyte, cleaning the terminals, and equalizing the charge. They also have a higher risk of corrosion, spillage, and explosion. Valve-regulated batteries are more expensive and less tolerant to overcharging, but they are maintenance-free, safer, and more compact. They also have a lower self-discharge rate and can be mounted in any orientation.

Lead acid batteries are used in various applications, such as:

- Automotive: Lead acid batteries are the standard type of battery for starting, lighting, and ignition (SLI) systems in vehicles. They provide a high current for a short duration to start the engine and power the lights and other accessories. They are also used as an auxiliary battery in hybrid and electric vehicles to support the high-voltage battery and critical systems .
- Grid: Lead acid batteries are used for grid energy storage, such as peak shaving, frequency regulation, and load leveling. They can store excess energy from renewable sources, such as solar and wind, and release it when needed. They can also provide backup power in case of grid failure or outage.
- Backup: Lead acid batteries are used for uninterruptible power supply (UPS) systems, such as in data centers, hospitals, and telecommunication networks. They can provide reliable and continuous power in case of power interruption or fluctuation. They can also protect sensitive equipment from voltage spikes and surges.

## Advantages and Disadvantages

Lead acid batteries have some advantages and disadvantages compared to other types of batteries, such as:

- Advantages:
  - Low cost: Lead acid batteries are the cheapest type of rechargeable battery, making them affordable and widely available.
  - High power: Lead acid batteries can deliver a high current for a short duration, making them suitable for SLI and UPS applications.
  - Long lifespan: Lead acid batteries can last for several years if properly maintained and operated.
  - Recyclable: Lead acid batteries are highly recyclable, reducing environmental impact and waste disposal costs.
