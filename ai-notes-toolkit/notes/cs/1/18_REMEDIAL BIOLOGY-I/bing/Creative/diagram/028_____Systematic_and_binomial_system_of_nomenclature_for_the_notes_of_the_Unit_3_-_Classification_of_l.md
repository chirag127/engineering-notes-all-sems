### Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature

- Systematic is the branch of biology that deals with the identification, naming and classification of living organisms based on their similarities and differences.
- Classification is the process of grouping organisms into categories based on their characteristics, such as morphology, anatomy, physiology, genetics, etc.
- Nomenclature is the system of assigning names to organisms according to a set of rules and conventions.
- Binomial nomenclature is the most widely used system of nomenclature in biology, which was developed by Carl Linnaeus in the 18th century .
- Binomial nomenclature assigns a unique and universal name to each species, consisting of two words: the genus name and the specific epithet .
- The genus name is the first word, which is capitalized and italicized. It indicates the group of closely related species to which the organism belongs .
- The specific epithet is the second word, which is lowercase and italicized. It indicates the particular species within the genus .
- For example, the scientific name of human is Homo sapiens, where Homo is the genus name and sapiens is the specific epithet.
- The advantages of binomial nomenclature are that it avoids confusion, ambiguity and duplication of names, and facilitates communication and comparison among scientists  .
- The rules and conventions of binomial nomenclature are governed by the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN) for animals and the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICNafp) for plants.
- Some of the basic rules of binomial nomenclature are :
  - The name of a species must be unique and not identical to any other species name.
  - The name of a species must be based on a type specimen, which is a preserved specimen that serves as the reference for the name.
  - The name of a species must be published in a scientific journal or book with a description and illustration of the organism.
  - The name of a species must be in Latin or Latinized form, and follow the grammatical rules of Latin.
  - The name of a species must be written in italics or underlined when handwritten.
  - The name of a species must be cited with the author and year of publication, or with an abbreviated authority name, when referring to the original source of the name.
