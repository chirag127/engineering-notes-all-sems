# Morphology

Morphology is the study of the physical form and external structure of plants. It is different from plant anatomy, which is the study of the internal structure of plants, especially at the microscopic level. Plant morphology is useful in the visual identification of plants and in understanding their evolution and development  .

Plant morphology includes both the vegetative and reproductive structures of plants. Vegetative structures are those that are not directly involved in reproduction, such as roots, stems, and leaves. Reproductive structures are those that are involved in producing seeds, spores, or gametes, such as flowers, fruits, and cones.

There are four major areas of investigation in plant morphology:

- Growth: how plants develop from a single cell to a complex organism
- Morphological variation: how plants differ in their form and structure due to genetic and environmental factors
- Evolution of plant form: how plants have changed over time and adapted to different habitats and conditions
- Classification of plant forms: how plants are grouped and named based on their similarities and differences

Some of the common terms and concepts used in plant morphology are  :

- Meristem: a region of undifferentiated cells that can divide and differentiate into various types of plant tissues
- Apical meristem: a meristem at the tip of a root or shoot that is responsible for primary growth (lengthening)
- Lateral meristem: a meristem along the sides of a root or shoot that is responsible for secondary growth (thickening)
- Node: a point on a stem where one or more leaves are attached
- Internode: the portion of a stem between two nodes
- Axil: the angle between a leaf and the stem
- Bud: an undeveloped shoot that can grow into a branch, flower, or leaf
- Leaf: a flattened structure that is usually green and photosynthetic
- Petiole: the stalk that connects a leaf to the stem
- Blade: the broad and flat part of a leaf
- Vein: a vascular bundle that transports water and nutrients in a leaf
- Stipule: a small appendage at the base of a petiole
- Simple leaf: a leaf that has a single blade
- Compound leaf: a leaf that has two or more blades (leaflets) attached to a common petiole
- Root: a structure that anchors a plant in the soil and absorbs water and minerals
- Taproot: a main root that grows vertically downward and gives rise to lateral roots
- Fibrous root: a root system that consists of many thin and branching roots of similar size
- Adventitious root: a root that arises from a stem or leaf rather than from the root system
- Stem: a structure that supports the leaves and flowers and transports water and nutrients
- Herbaceous stem: a soft and green stem that usually dies back at the end of the growing season
- Woody stem: a hard and brown stem that persists for many years and has secondary growth
- Bark: the outer layer of a woody stem that protects it from injury and water loss
- Wood: the inner layer of a woody stem that consists of xylem (water-conducting tissue)
- Pith: the central core of a stem that consists of parenchyma (storage tissue)
- Flower: a reproductive structure that produces pollen and seeds
- Sepal: a modified leaf that encloses and protects the flower bud
- Petal: a modified leaf that is usually colorful and attracts pollinators
- Stamen: the male reproductive organ of a flower that consists of an anther (pollen-producing structure) and a filament (stalk)
- Carpel: the female reproductive organ of a flower that consists of a stigma (pollen-receiving structure), a style (neck), and an ovary (seed-producing structure)
- Pistil: a single carpel or a group of fused carpels
- Inflorescence: a group of flowers arranged on a common axis
- Fruit: a mature ovary that contains seeds and may have other structures attached to it
- Seed: a structure that contains an embryo (young plant) and a food supply (endosperm or cotyledon) and is enclosed by a seed coat
- Germination: the process of a seed sprouting and developing into a seedling
