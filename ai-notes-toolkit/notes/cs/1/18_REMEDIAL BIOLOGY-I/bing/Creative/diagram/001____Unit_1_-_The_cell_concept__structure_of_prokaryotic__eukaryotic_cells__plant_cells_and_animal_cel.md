## Unit 1 - The cell concept, structure of prokaryotic, eukaryotic cells, plant cells and animal cells, Structure and function of cell membrane, cell organelles and their function, Basics of cell signaling, Structure and use of compound microscope, Basic chemical constituents of living body

- The cell concept: The cell is the basic unit of life. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells. Cells arise from pre-existing cells by division. Cells contain genetic material that determines their structure and function. Cells are capable of metabolism, growth, response, and reproduction.
- Structure of prokaryotic cells: Prokaryotic cells are cells that lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They are usually unicellular and have a simple structure. Prokaryotic cells have a cell wall, a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and a circular DNA molecule called a nucleoid. Some prokaryotic cells also have flagella, pili, or capsules for locomotion, attachment, or protection.
- Structure of eukaryotic cells: Eukaryotic cells are cells that have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They are usually multicellular and have a complex structure. Eukaryotic cells have a cell wall (in plants, fungi, and some protists), a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and a linear DNA molecule organized into chromosomes in the nucleus. Eukaryotic cells also have various organelles, such as mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, peroxisomes, vacuoles, and cytoskeleton, that perform specific functions.
- Structure of plant cells and animal cells: Plant cells and animal cells are both eukaryotic cells, but they have some differences in their structure. Plant cells have a cell wall made of cellulose, a large central vacuole that stores water and other substances, and chloroplasts that perform photosynthesis. Animal cells do not have a cell wall, a central vacuole, or chloroplasts. Animal cells have centrioles that help in cell division, and lysosomes that digest macromolecules and foreign particles.
- Structure and function of cell membrane: The cell membrane is a thin, flexible layer that surrounds the cell and separates it from the external environment. The cell membrane is composed of a phospholipid bilayer, with hydrophilic heads facing the aqueous environment and hydrophobic tails facing the interior. The cell membrane also contains proteins, carbohydrates, and cholesterol that perform various functions. The cell membrane is selectively permeable, meaning that it allows some substances to pass through, but not others. The cell membrane regulates the transport of materials, maintains the cell's shape, communicates with other cells, and participates in cell signaling.
- Cell organelles and their function: Cell organelles are specialized structures within the cell that perform specific tasks. Some of the major cell organelles are:

  - Nucleus: The nucleus is the control center of the cell. It contains the genetic material (DNA) that directs the synthesis of proteins and other molecules. The nucleus is surrounded by a nuclear envelope, which has pores that allow the passage of molecules. The nucleus also contains a nucleolus, which produces ribosomes.
  - Mitochondria: Mitochondria are the powerhouses of the cell. They convert the energy stored in organic molecules into ATP, the universal energy currency of the cell. Mitochondria have a double membrane, with an inner membrane folded into cristae that increase the surface area for cellular respiration. Mitochondria also have their own DNA and ribosomes, and can divide independently of the cell.
  - Chloroplasts: Chloroplasts are the sites of photosynthesis in plant cells. They capture light energy and use it to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen. Chloroplasts have a double membrane, with an inner membrane forming stacks of flattened sacs called thylakoids, which contain the pigment chlorophyll. Chloroplasts also have their own DNA and ribosomes, and can divide independently of the cell.
