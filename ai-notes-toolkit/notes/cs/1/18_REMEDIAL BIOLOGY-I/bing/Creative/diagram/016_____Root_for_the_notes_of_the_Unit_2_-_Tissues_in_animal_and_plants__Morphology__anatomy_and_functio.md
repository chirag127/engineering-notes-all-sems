### Root

- The root is usually an underground part of the plant that is primarily responsible for the fixation and absorption of water and minerals from the soil.
- The root with its branches is known as the root system.
- There are four main functions of the root system:
  - Absorption of water and minerals from the soil
  - Providing a proper anchorage to the plant parts
  - Storing reserve food material
  - Synthesis of plant growth regulators
- There are two types of root systems:
  - Taproot system: It consists of a main root (primary root) that grows vertically downward and gives rise to lateral roots (secondary and tertiary roots). It is found in dicotyledonous plants, such as carrot, radish, and mango.
  - Fibrous root system: It consists of many fine roots of similar size and shape that arise from the base of the stem. It is found in monocotyledonous plants, such as grass, wheat, and rice.
- The root has three main regions:
  - Root cap: It is a thimble-like structure that covers the tip of the root and protects the meristematic tissue from injury and abrasion. It also secretes mucilage that lubricates the passage of the root through the soil.
  - Root meristem: It is a region of actively dividing cells that are responsible for the growth and elongation of the root. It gives rise to three primary tissues: epidermis, cortex, and stele.
  - Root hair zone: It is a region where the epidermal cells produce thin outgrowths called root hairs that increase the surface area for absorption of water and minerals.
- The root has a radial, or concentric, arrangement of tissues within the root that determines patterns and rates of nutrient transport from the soil to the vascular tissue.
- The root has four main types of tissues:
  - Epidermis: It is the outermost layer of cells that covers the root and forms the root hairs. It acts as a protective barrier and a selective permeable membrane for absorption of water and minerals.
  - Cortex: It is the region between the epidermis and the stele that consists of parenchyma cells. It acts as a storage site for starch and other organic substances. It also contains air spaces that allow the diffusion of gases and water.
  - Stele: It is the central core of the root that consists of vascular tissues (xylem and phloem) and pericycle. It acts as a transport system for water and minerals (xylem) and organic substances (phloem). It also produces lateral roots (pericycle).
  - Endodermis: It is the innermost layer of the cortex that surrounds the stele. It acts as a selective barrier that regulates the movement of water and minerals into the stele. It has a suberized band called the Casparian strip that blocks the apoplastic pathway of water and minerals.
