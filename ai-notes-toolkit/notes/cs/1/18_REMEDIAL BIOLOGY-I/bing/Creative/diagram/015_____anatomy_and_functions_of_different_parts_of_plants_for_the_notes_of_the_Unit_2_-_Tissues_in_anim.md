### Anatomy and functions of different parts of plants

- Plants are multicellular eukaryotes that belong to the kingdom Plantae. They have a variety of structures and functions that enable them to survive and reproduce in different environments.
- Plants have two main organ systems: the shoot system and the root system. The shoot system consists of the stem, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds. The root system consists of the roots and their associated structures.
- The shoot system and the root system are connected by vascular tissues, which transport water, minerals and organic substances throughout the plant. The vascular tissues are composed of xylem and phloem. Xylem conducts water and minerals from the roots to the shoots. Phloem conducts organic substances, such as sugars and hormones, from the sources (e.g. leaves) to the sinks (e.g. fruits).
- The stem is the main axis of the shoot system. It supports the leaves, flowers and fruits, and provides a pathway for the vascular tissues. The stem can be herbaceous (soft and green) or woody (hard and brown). The stem can also have different modifications, such as tendrils, thorns, bulbs, tubers and corms, to adapt to different functions and environments.
- The leaves are the main photosynthetic organs of the plant. They capture light energy and convert it into chemical energy (sugars) by the process of photosynthesis. The leaves also regulate gas exchange, transpiration and temperature by opening and closing their stomata. The stomata are pores on the leaf surface that allow the entry and exit of carbon dioxide, oxygen and water vapour. The leaves can have different shapes, sizes, arrangements and margins to suit different functions and environments.
- The flowers are the reproductive organs of the plant. They produce gametes (pollen and ovules) and facilitate pollination and fertilization. The flowers consist of four main whorls: the calyx, the corolla, the androecium and the gynoecium. The calyx is the outermost whorl, composed of sepals, which protect the flower bud. The corolla is the next whorl, composed of petals, which attract pollinators. The androecium is the male whorl, composed of stamens, which produce pollen. The gynoecium is the female whorl, composed of carpels, which produce ovules. The flowers can have different symmetries, numbers, positions and types to suit different functions and environments.
- The fruits are the mature ovaries of the plant. They contain seeds, which are the products of fertilization. The fruits protect and disperse the seeds, and provide nourishment for the developing and germinating seeds. The fruits can be classified into different types, such as simple, aggregate, multiple and accessory, based on the number and origin of the carpels and other floral parts. The fruits can also have different shapes, sizes, colours and textures to suit different functions and environments.
- The seeds are the dormant embryos of the plant. They consist of a seed coat, an embryo and a food reserve. The seed coat protects the embryo from physical and biological damage. The embryo consists of a radicle (embryonic root), a plumule (embryonic shoot) and one or two cotyledons (seed leaves). The food reserve consists of starch, proteins and oils, which provide energy and nutrients for the germination and growth of the seedling. The seeds can have different adaptations, such as dormancy, dispersal and germination, to suit different functions and environments.
- The roots are the main organs of the root system. They anchor the plant to the soil, absorb water and minerals, and store food. The roots can be classified into different types, such as taproot and fibrous root, based on the pattern of branching. The roots can also have different modifications, such as adventitious roots, prop roots, aerial roots and root nodules, to adapt to different functions and environments.
