### Fruit

- A fruit is the **soft, pulpy part of a flowering plant that contains seeds** .
- It is formed from the **ovaries of angiosperms** (plants that produce flowers and seeds enclosed in a carpel) and is exclusive only to this group of plants .
- Fruits are a characteristic of flowering plants. As soon as **pollen and fertilization occur**, the ovary of a plant becomes a fruit and the ovules become a seed.
- Fruits are classified into four types namely:
  - **Simple fruits**: These are fruits that develop from a single ovary of a single flower. Examples are apple, mango, peach, cherry, etc.
  - **Aggregate fruits**: These are fruits that develop from many ovaries of a single flower. Examples are raspberry, blackberry, strawberry, etc.
  - **Multiple fruits**: These are fruits that develop from many ovaries of many flowers that are fused together. Examples are pineapple, fig, mulberry, etc.
  - **Accessory fruits**: These are fruits that develop from other parts of the flower besides the ovary, such as the receptacle, calyx, or bracts. Examples are apple, pear, pomegranate, etc.
- Fruits have different functions in the life cycle of plants, such as:
  - **Protection**: Fruits protect the seeds from predators, parasites, and diseases by providing a physical barrier or a chemical defense.
  - **Dispersal**: Fruits help in the dispersal of seeds by various agents, such as wind, water, animals, or gravity. Some fruits have adaptations for dispersal, such as wings, hooks, spines, or fleshy pulp that attracts animals.
  - **Germination**: Fruits provide nutrients and favorable conditions for the germination of seeds. Some fruits have dormancy mechanisms that prevent germination until the environmental factors are suitable.
  - **Reproduction**: Fruits attract pollinators and seed dispersers by their color, shape, odor, or taste. Some fruits have symbiotic relationships with animals that help in their pollination or seed dispersal.
