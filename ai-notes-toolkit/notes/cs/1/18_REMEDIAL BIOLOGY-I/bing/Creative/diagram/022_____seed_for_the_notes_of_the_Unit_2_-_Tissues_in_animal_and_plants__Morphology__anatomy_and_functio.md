## Tissues in animal and plants

- A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.
- Tissues are found in both animals and plants, but they have some differences.
- Animal tissues are made of living cells that do not have cell walls. Plant tissues have cell walls that provide support and protection.
- Animal tissues are classified into four main types: connective, nervous, muscle, and epithelial tissues. Plant tissues are classified into three main types: epidermis, ground tissue, and vascular tissue.

### Connective tissue

- Connective tissue is a type of animal tissue that provides support, structure, and protection to the body.
- Connective tissue is composed of cells and extracellular matrix, which is a material that fills the space between the cells and contains fibers and ground substance.
- Examples of connective tissue are blood, cartilage, bone, adipose tissue, and tendons.

### Nervous tissue

- Nervous tissue is a type of animal tissue that allows rapid communication between different parts of the body.
- Nervous tissue is composed of neurons, which are specialized cells that transmit electrical signals, and glial cells, which support and protect the neurons.
- Examples of nervous tissue are the brain, spinal cord, and nerves.

### Muscle tissue

- Muscle tissue is a type of animal tissue that can contract to allow movement of body parts or substances within the body.
- Muscle tissue is composed of muscle fibers, which are long and thin cells that contain contractile proteins called actin and myosin.
- Examples of muscle tissue are skeletal muscle, cardiac muscle, and smooth muscle.

### Epithelial tissue

- Epithelial tissue is a type of animal tissue that covers the surfaces of the body and forms the lining of internal organs and cavities.
- Epithelial tissue is composed of tightly packed cells that form one or more layers and have little or no extracellular matrix.
- Examples of epithelial tissue are the skin, the lining of the digestive tract, and the lining of the respiratory tract.

### Epidermis

- Epidermis is a type of plant tissue that forms the outermost layer of the plant body and protects it from water loss, infection, and damage.
- Epidermis is composed of a single layer of cells that may have specialized structures such as hairs, stomata, and cuticle.
- Examples of epidermis are the leaf epidermis, the root epidermis, and the stem epidermis.

### Ground tissue

- Ground tissue is a type of plant tissue that fills the space between the epidermis and the vascular tissue and performs various functions such as photosynthesis, storage, and support.
- Ground tissue is composed of three types of cells: parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma.
- Examples of ground tissue are the mesophyll of the leaf, the cortex of the stem, and the pith of the stem.

### Vascular tissue

- Vascular tissue is a type of plant tissue that transports water and minerals from the roots to the leaves and organic substances from the leaves to the rest of the plant.
- Vascular tissue is composed of two types of conducting cells: xylem and phloem.
- Examples of vascular tissue are the vascular bundles of the stem, the veins of the leaf, and the root hairs of the root.
