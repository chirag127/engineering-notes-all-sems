### Concepts of alleles and genes
- Genes are the units of heredity that determine the traits of an organism. They are made of DNA and located on chromosomes.
- Alleles are the different versions of a gene that can exist. For example, the gene for eye color can have alleles for blue, brown, green, etc.
- Each organism inherits two alleles for each gene, one from each parent. The combination of alleles determines the phenotype (observable trait) and the genotype (genetic makeup) of the organism.
- Some alleles are dominant, meaning they mask the effect of the other allele, and some are recessive, meaning they are only expressed when both alleles are the same. For example, brown eyes are dominant over blue eyes, so a person with one brown and one blue allele will have brown eyes.
- Some alleles are codominant, meaning they are both expressed equally, and some are incomplete dominant, meaning they produce a blended phenotype. For example, red and white flowers can produce pink flowers when crossed.

### Mendelian Experiments
- Mendel was a monk who experimented with pea plants and discovered the basic principles of genetics.
- He crossed plants with different traits and observed the patterns of inheritance in the offspring.
- He formulated the law of segregation, which states that each organism has two alleles for each gene, and they separate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one allele.
- He also formulated the law of independent assortment, which states that the alleles of different genes are inherited independently of each other, as long as they are on different chromosomes.
- He used the terms dominant and recessive to describe the relationship between alleles, and used Punnett squares to predict the genotypic and phenotypic ratios of the offspring.

### Cell cycle (Elementary Idea)
- The cell cycle is the series of events that a cell undergoes from its formation to its division.
- The cell cycle consists of two main phases: interphase and mitotic phase.
- Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle, where the cell grows, performs its normal functions, and replicates its DNA.
- Interphase can be divided into three subphases: G1, S, and G2. G1 is the gap phase where the cell grows and prepares for DNA synthesis. S is the synthesis phase where the DNA is replicated. G2 is the second gap phase where the cell prepares for mitosis.
- Mitotic phase is the shortest phase of the cell cycle, where the cell divides into two identical daughter cells.
- Mitotic phase can be divided into two subphases: mitosis and cytokinesis. Mitosis is the process of nuclear division, where the chromosomes are separated and distributed to the daughter nuclei. Cytokinesis is the process of cytoplasmic division, where the cell membrane pinches in and splits the cell into two.

### Mitosis and meiosis
- Mitosis and meiosis are two types of cell division that produce different types of daughter cells.
- Mitosis is the type of cell division that occurs in somatic cells (body cells) and produces two diploid (2n) daughter cells that are genetically identical to the parent cell.
- Mitosis consists of four stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase. Prophase is the stage where the chromosomes condense and the nuclear envelope breaks down. Metaphase is the stage where the chromosomes align at the equator of the cell. Anaphase is the stage where the sister chromatids separate and move to the opposite poles of the cell. Telophase is the stage where the chromosomes decondense and the nuclear envelope reforms.
- Meiosis is the type of cell division that occurs in germ cells (sex cells) and produces four haploid (n) daughter cells that are genetically different from the parent cell.
- Meiosis consists of two rounds of division: meiosis I and meiosis II. Meiosis I is the reductional division, where the homologous chromosomes pair up and exchange segments in a process called crossing over, and then separate into two haploid cells. Meiosis II is the equational division, where the sister chromatids separate and produce four haploid cells.
- Meiosis is important for sexual reproduction, as it generates genetic diversity
