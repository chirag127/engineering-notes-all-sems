### Tissues in animal and plants

- Tissues are groups of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.
- There are four main types of animal tissues: epithelial, connective, muscle, and nervous.
- There are three main types of plant tissues: epidermis, ground tissue, and vascular tissue.

#### Epithelial tissue

- Epithelial tissue covers the body surface and lines the body cavities, organs, and glands.
- Epithelial tissue protects the body from infection, dehydration, and mechanical damage.
- Epithelial tissue also secretes substances such as mucus, hormones, and enzymes, and absorbs nutrients and gases.
- Epithelial tissue can be classified by the shape and arrangement of the cells: squamous (flat), cuboidal (cube-shaped), columnar (tall and narrow), and transitional (variable).
- Epithelial tissue can also be classified by the number of cell layers: simple (one layer), stratified (more than one layer), and pseudostratified (one layer that appears to be more than one).

#### Connective tissue

- Connective tissue supports, binds, and protects other tissues and organs.
- Connective tissue consists of cells, fibers, and ground substance (matrix).
- Connective tissue can be classified by the type and amount of fibers and ground substance: loose (few fibers, more ground substance), dense (more fibers, less ground substance), cartilage (firm but flexible matrix), bone (hard and rigid matrix), and blood (liquid matrix).

#### Muscle tissue

- Muscle tissue can contract and relax to produce movement.
- Muscle tissue can be classified by the structure and function of the cells: skeletal (striated, voluntary, attached to bones), cardiac (striated, involuntary, found in the heart), and smooth (non-striated, involuntary, found in the walls of hollow organs).

#### Nervous tissue

- Nervous tissue consists of neurons (nerve cells) and neuroglia (supporting cells).
- Nervous tissue transmits and processes electrical signals throughout the body.
- Nervous tissue can be classified by the location and function of the neurons: central nervous system (brain and spinal cord), peripheral nervous system (nerves and ganglia), sensory neurons (receive stimuli), motor neurons (control muscles and glands), and interneurons (connect sensory and motor neurons).

#### Epidermis

- Epidermis is the outermost layer of plant tissue that covers the leaves, stems, roots, flowers, fruits, and seeds.
- Epidermis protects the plant from water loss, infection, and physical damage.
- Epidermis also allows gas exchange, light absorption, and secretion of substances such as wax and resin.
- Epidermis can be modified to form structures such as hairs, glands, stomata, and cuticle.

#### Ground tissue

- Ground tissue is the most abundant and diverse type of plant tissue that fills the space between the epidermis and the vascular tissue.
- Ground tissue performs various functions such as photosynthesis, storage, support, and wound healing.
- Ground tissue can be classified by the type and shape of the cells: parenchyma (thin-walled, spherical, versatile), collenchyma (thick-walled, elongated, flexible), and sclerenchyma (thick-walled, rigid, dead).

#### Vascular tissue

- Vascular tissue is the specialized plant tissue that transports water, minerals, sugars, and hormones throughout the plant.
- Vascular tissue consists of two types of conducting cells: xylem (transports water and minerals from the roots to the leaves) and phloem (transports sugars and hormones from the leaves to the roots and other parts).
- Vascular tissue can be arranged in different patterns depending on the type of plant: vascular bundles (in stems and leaves), vascular cylinder (in roots), and vascular cambium (in woody plants).
