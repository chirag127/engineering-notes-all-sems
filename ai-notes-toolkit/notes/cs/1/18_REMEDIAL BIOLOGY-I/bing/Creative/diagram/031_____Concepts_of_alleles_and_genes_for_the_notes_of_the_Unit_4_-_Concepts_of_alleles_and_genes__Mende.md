# Concepts of alleles and genes

- Genes are the basic units of heredity that determine the traits of an organism. They are made of DNA and are located on chromosomes.
- Alleles are different versions of the same gene that may produce different phenotypes (observable characteristics) or genotypes (genetic makeup) in an organism.
- For example, the gene for eye color has two alleles: brown and blue. An individual can have two brown alleles, two blue alleles, or one of each. The combination of alleles determines the eye color of the individual.
- Each organism inherits two alleles for each gene, one from each parent. The two alleles may be the same (homozygous) or different (heterozygous).
- The expression of alleles depends on their dominance and recessiveness. A dominant allele is one that masks the effect of another allele, while a recessive allele is one that is masked by another allele.
- For example, the brown allele for eye color is dominant over the blue allele. An individual with one brown and one blue allele will have brown eyes, because the brown allele is expressed and the blue allele is hidden. An individual with two blue alleles will have blue eyes, because there is no dominant allele to mask the blue allele.
- The inheritance of alleles follows the laws of Mendelian genetics, which are based on the experiments of Gregor Mendel, the father of genetics. He studied the inheritance of traits in pea plants and discovered the principles of segregation, independent assortment, and dominance.
