### Root

The root is the descending portion of the plant axis that is usually underground. It has four main functions:

- Absorption of water and minerals from the soil
- Providing a proper anchorage to the plant parts
- Storing reserve food material
- Synthesis of plant growth regulators

There are two types of root systems:

- Taproot system: A single main root that grows vertically downward and gives off lateral branches. It is found in dicotyledonous plants, such as carrot, radish, and bean.
- Fibrous root system: A cluster of many thin roots that spread out in all directions. It is found in monocotyledonous plants, such as grass, wheat, and rice.

The root has three main regions:

- Root cap: A protective layer of cells that covers the tip of the root and secretes mucilage to lubricate the soil.
- Root meristem: A region of actively dividing cells that produces new root tissues and increases the length of the root.
- Root hair zone: A region of elongated epidermal cells that form root hairs, which increase the surface area for absorption.

The root has a radial, or concentric, arrangement of tissues within the root. The main tissues are:

- Epidermis: The outermost layer of cells that protects the root and absorbs water and minerals.
- Cortex: A thick layer of parenchyma cells that store starch and other substances.
- Endodermis: A single layer of cells that surrounds the vascular cylinder and regulates the movement of water and minerals into the xylem and phloem.
- Pericycle: A layer of meristematic cells that gives rise to lateral roots and vascular cambium.
- Vascular cylinder: The central core of the root that contains the xylem and phloem, which transport water and organic nutrients, respectively.

The root anatomy and morphology are critical components of nutrient acquisition and transport. The root also communicates with other plants through chemical signals and symbiotic associations with fungi and bacteria.
