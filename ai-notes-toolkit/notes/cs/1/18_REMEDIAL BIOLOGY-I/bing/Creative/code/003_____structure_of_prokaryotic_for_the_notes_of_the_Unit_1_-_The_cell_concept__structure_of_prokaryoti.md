# Structure of Prokaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic cells are unicellular organisms that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Prokaryotic cells have a simple structure, consisting of the following components:
  - **Plasma membrane**: an outer covering that separates the cell's interior from its surrounding environment. It regulates the passage of substances into and out of the cell.
  - **Cytoplasm**: a gel-like substance that fills the cell and contains the genetic material and other cellular structures.
  - **DNA**: the genetic material of the cell, usually in the form of a single circular chromosome that is located in a region called the nucleoid. Some prokaryotes also have extra-chromosomal DNA called plasmids that can confer additional traits to the cell.
  - **Ribosomes**: the sites of protein synthesis in the cell. They are composed of RNA and protein and are smaller than eukaryotic ribosomes.
  - **Cell wall**: a rigid layer that surrounds the plasma membrane and provides shape, protection, and support to the cell. Most prokaryotes have a cell wall made of peptidoglycan, a polymer of sugars and amino acids.
- Some prokaryotic cells also have additional structures that are not present in all prokaryotes, such as:
  - **Capsule**: a sticky layer of polysaccharides or proteins that covers the cell wall and helps the cell adhere to surfaces and evade the immune system of the host.
  - **Flagella**: long, whip-like appendages that extend from the cell and enable the cell to move by rotating.
  - **Pili**: short, hair-like projections that allow the cell to attach to other cells or surfaces and exchange genetic material.
  - **Endospores**: dormant, resistant structures that form inside some bacterial cells when the environmental conditions are unfavorable. They can survive extreme heat, cold, dehydration, and radiation and germinate when the conditions improve.
