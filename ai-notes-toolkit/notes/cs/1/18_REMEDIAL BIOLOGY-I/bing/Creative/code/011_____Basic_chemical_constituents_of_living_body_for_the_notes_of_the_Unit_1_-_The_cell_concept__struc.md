### Basic chemical constituents of living body

The living body is composed of various chemical elements and compounds that are essential for its structure and function. Some of the main chemical constituents of living body are:

- **Water**: Water is the most abundant and universal solvent in living organisms. It makes up about 60% of the human body mass and about 70-90% of the mass of most cells. Water is involved in many biochemical reactions, such as hydrolysis, dehydration synthesis, and osmosis. Water also helps maintain the body temperature, pH, and fluid balance.
- **Organic compounds**: Organic compounds are molecules that contain carbon and hydrogen, and usually other elements such as oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus. The four major classes of organic compounds in living organisms are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. These compounds are the building blocks of the cell structure and the source of energy and information for the cell function.
  - **Carbohydrates**: Carbohydrates are molecules that consist of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms, usually in the ratio of 1:2:1. Carbohydrates are classified into monosaccharides (simple sugars), disaccharides (double sugars), and polysaccharides (complex sugars). Carbohydrates serve as the main source of energy for the cell, as well as structural components of the cell wall, cell membrane, and extracellular matrix.
  - **Lipids**: Lipids are molecules that consist of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms, but with a lower proportion of oxygen than carbohydrates. Lipids are classified into fatty acids, triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids, and waxes. Lipids serve as the main source of energy storage for the cell, as well as structural components of the cell membrane and hormones.
  - **Proteins**: Proteins are molecules that consist of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and sometimes sulfur atoms. Proteins are composed of amino acids, which are linked by peptide bonds to form polypeptides. Proteins serve as the main source of structural and functional diversity for the cell, as they perform various roles such as enzymes, hormones, antibodies, receptors, transporters, and structural proteins.
  - **Nucleic acids**: Nucleic acids are molecules that consist of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and phosphorus atoms. Nucleic acids are composed of nucleotides, which are linked by phosphodiester bonds to form polynucleotides. Nucleic acids serve as the main source of genetic information and regulation for the cell, as they store, transmit, and express the genetic code in the form of DNA and RNA.
- **Inorganic compounds**: Inorganic compounds are molecules that do not contain carbon and hydrogen, or contain carbon but not in the form of organic compounds. Some of the main inorganic compounds in living organisms are minerals, gases, and electrolytes. These compounds are involved in various physiological processes, such as oxygen transport, acid-base balance, nerve conduction, and muscle contraction.
  - **Minerals**: Minerals are elements or compounds that are found in nature in their pure form or as salts. Minerals are essential for the formation and maintenance of bones, teeth, and blood cells, as well as the function of enzymes, hormones, and cofactors. Some of the important minerals in living organisms are calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, iron, zinc, copper, iodine, and selenium.
  - **Gases**: Gases are elements or compounds that exist in the gaseous state at normal temperature and pressure. Gases are essential for the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the cells and the environment, as well as the regulation of pH and blood pressure. Some of the important gases in living organisms are oxygen, carbon dioxide, nitrogen, and hydrogen.
  - **Electrolytes**: Electrolytes are elements or compounds that dissociate into ions when dissolved in water. Electrolytes are essential for the maintenance of fluid balance, osmotic pressure, and electrical potential across the cell membrane, as well as the transmission of nerve impulses and muscle contractions. Some of the important electrolytes in living organisms are sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, and phosphate.
