### Flower

A flower is a modified stem with specialized leaves that serve as the reproductive organs of a plant. Flowers are the site of pollination and fertilization, which lead to the formation of fruits and seeds. Flowers can have different shapes, colors, sizes and arrangements of their parts, depending on the type and function of the flower.

The main parts of a flower are:

- **Sepals**: These are the outermost whorl of modified leaves that enclose and protect the flower bud. They are usually green and leaf-like, but sometimes they can be colorful and petal-like.
- **Petals**: These are the next whorl of modified leaves that are usually bright and colorful to attract pollinators. They may also have nectar glands or other structures that aid in pollination.
- **Stamens**: These are the male reproductive organs of a flower, consisting of a filament (stalk) and an anther (sac) that produces pollen grains. Pollen grains contain the male gametes (sperm cells) that fertilize the female gametes (egg cells) in the ovules.
- **Pistil**: This is the female reproductive organ of a flower, consisting of an ovary (chamber) that contains one or more ovules, a style (tube) that connects the ovary to the stigma (tip), and a stigma that receives and germinates the pollen grains. The ovules contain the female gametes (egg cells) that are fertilized by the male gametes (sperm cells) in the pollen grains. The fertilized ovules develop into seeds, and the ovary develops into a fruit.

The arrangement of the floral parts on the floral axis is called the **floral formula**. The floral formula can be represented by symbols and numbers that indicate the number and type of floral parts in each whorl. For example, the floral formula of a lily is:

$$\begin{array}{c}
\star\\
\overline{P_3+3}\\
\overline{A_3+3}\\
\overline{G_{(3)}}\\
\end{array}$$

This means that the flower has radial symmetry ($\star$), three sepals and three petals in two whorls ($\overline{P_3+3}$), six stamens in two whorls ($\overline{A_3+3}$), and a pistil with three fused carpels ($\overline{G_{(3)}}$).

The function of a flower is to facilitate sexual reproduction in plants. The flower allows the transfer of pollen from the anthers to the stigma, either by self-pollination (within the same flower or plant) or cross-pollination (between different flowers or plants) with the help of pollinators such as insects, birds, bats, wind, or water. The flower also allows the fusion of the male and female gametes in the ovules, resulting in the formation of zygotes that develop into embryos and endosperms. The flower then transforms into a fruit that protects and disperses the seeds, which can germinate and grow into new plants.
