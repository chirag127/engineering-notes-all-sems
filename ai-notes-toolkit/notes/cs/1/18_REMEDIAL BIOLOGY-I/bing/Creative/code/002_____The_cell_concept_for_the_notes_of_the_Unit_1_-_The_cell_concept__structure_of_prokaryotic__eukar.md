### The cell concept

- The cell concept is the idea that all living things are composed of one or more cells, which are the basic units of life.
- The cell concept was first proposed by Robert Hooke in 1665, who observed cork cells under a microscope and coined the term "cell".
- The cell concept was further developed by Anton van Leeuwenhoek, who discovered microorganisms and animalcules, and by Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann, who stated that all plants and animals are made of cells.
- The cell concept is one of the fundamental principles of biology, along with the gene concept, the evolution concept, and the homeostasis concept.

### Structure of prokaryotic, eukaryotic, plant, and animal cells

- Prokaryotic cells are cells that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually unicellular and have a simple structure. Examples of prokaryotes are bacteria and archaea.
- Eukaryotic cells are cells that have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually multicellular and have a complex structure. Examples of eukaryotes are protists, fungi, plants, and animals.
- Plant cells are eukaryotic cells that have a cell wall, a large central vacuole, and plastids, such as chloroplasts, which are involved in photosynthesis. Plant cells also have a cytoskeleton, a plasma membrane, a nucleus, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, lysosomes, and peroxisomes.
- Animal cells are eukaryotic cells that lack a cell wall, a large central vacuole, and plastids. Animal cells also have a cytoskeleton, a plasma membrane, a nucleus, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, lysosomes, peroxisomes, and sometimes centrioles and cilia or flagella.

### Structure and function of cell membrane

- The cell membrane, also known as the plasma membrane, is a thin, flexible, and selectively permeable layer that surrounds the cytoplasm of a cell. It regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell and maintains the cell's shape and integrity.
- The cell membrane is composed of a phospholipid bilayer, which has a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and a hydrophobic (water-fearing) tail. The phospholipid bilayer forms a fluid mosaic, in which various proteins, carbohydrates, and cholesterol molecules are embedded or attached.
- The cell membrane has different functions, such as:
  - Transport: The cell membrane allows the passage of some molecules, such as oxygen, carbon dioxide, water, and small polar molecules, by simple diffusion or facilitated diffusion. It also uses active transport, such as pumps, carriers, and vesicles, to move other molecules, such as ions, glucose, and amino acids, against their concentration gradients.
  - Communication: The cell membrane has receptors, which are proteins that bind to specific molecules, such as hormones, neurotransmitters, and growth factors, and trigger a response in the cell. It also has adhesion molecules, which are proteins that help the cell attach to other cells or the extracellular matrix.
  - Recognition: The cell membrane has glycoproteins and glycolipids, which are carbohydrates attached to proteins or lipids, and serve as markers or antigens that identify the cell as self or non-self. They are involved in immune system responses and tissue compatibility.
  - Enzymatic activity: The cell membrane has enzymes, which are proteins that catalyze chemical reactions, such as metabolism, signal transduction, and digestion.

### Cell organelles and their functions

- Cell organelles are specialized structures within a cell that perform specific functions. Some of the major cell organelles are:

  - Nucleus: The nucleus is the control center of the cell. It contains the genetic material (DNA) and the nucleolus, which produces ribosomes. The nucleus is surrounded by a double membrane called the nuclear envelope, which has pores that allow the exchange of materials with the cytoplasm.
  - Ribosomes: Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis. They are composed of two subunits, one large and one small, that are made of ribosomal RNA and proteins
