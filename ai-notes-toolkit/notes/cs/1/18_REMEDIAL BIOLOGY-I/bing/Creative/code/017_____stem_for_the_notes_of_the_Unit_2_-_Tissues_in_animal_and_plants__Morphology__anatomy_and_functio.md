## Tissues in animal and plants

- A tissue is a level of organization in the body of a multicellular organism. It is usually formed by the merging of cells having the same structure and function.
- Tissues are found in both animals and plants, but they have some differences and similarities.
- The four main types of animal tissues are connective, nervous, muscle, and epithelial tissues.
  - Connective tissue: It provides support, protection, and binding for other tissues. It consists of cells and extracellular matrix. Examples are blood, bone, cartilage, and adipose tissue.
  - Nervous tissue: It transmits and processes information in the form of electrical signals. It consists of neurons and glial cells. Examples are brain, spinal cord, and nerves.
  - Muscle tissue: It contracts and relaxes to produce movement. It consists of muscle fibers and myofibrils. Examples are skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscles.
  - Epithelial tissue: It covers the external and internal surfaces of the body and forms glands. It consists of tightly packed cells that form layers or sheets. Examples are skin, lining of digestive tract, and sweat glands.
- The three main tissue systems in plants are the epidermis, ground tissue, and vascular tissue.
  - Epidermis: It is the outermost layer of cells that covers and protects the plant body. It may have specialized structures such as stomata, trichomes, and cuticle.
  - Ground tissue: It is the bulk of the plant body that fills the space between the epidermis and the vascular tissue. It performs various functions such as photosynthesis, storage, and support. It consists of three types of cells: parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma.
  - Vascular tissue: It is the tissue that transports water, minerals, and organic substances throughout the plant. It consists of two types of conducting cells: xylem and phloem.
- Some of the differences between plant and animal tissues are:
  - In plants, there is an abundance of dead cells, such as sclerenchyma and xylem, that provide mechanical strength and support. In animals, the living cells are more predominant and the dead cells are limited to structures such as hair and nails.
  - Plant tissue requires less energy for maintenance and growth as compared to animal tissue. Animal tissue requires more energy for movement, respiration, and other metabolic activities.
  - In plant tissue, differentiation between cells is observed during development, resulting in permanent and meristematic tissues. Permanent tissues are mature and specialized, while meristematic tissues are immature and capable of division. Animal tissues, on the other hand, are derived from three germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm, and do not have meristematic tissues.
