### Inflorescence

- An inflorescence is the arrangement of a cluster of flowers on a floral axis or peduncle   .
- The inflorescence is of two main types, they are: **Racemose** and **Cymose**  .
- In racemose inflorescence, the main axis or peduncle is indeterminate and continues to grow, producing flowers in acropetal succession (from base to apex)  .
- In cymose inflorescence, the main axis or peduncle is determinate and stops growing after producing a terminal flower, producing flowers in basipetal succession (from apex to base)  .
- Racemose inflorescence can be further divided into the following types:

| Types of Inflorescence | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raceme | The peduncle of the branch bearing flowers is elongated and unbranched, and the flowers are stalked or pedicellate | Mustard, Raddish |
| Panicle | When the peduncle is branched, and each branch bears stalked flowers, the inflorescence is called a panicle | Delonix regia (Gulmohar), Azadirachta indica (Neem) |
| Spike | In the spike inflorescence, the peduncle is elongated and unbranched, and the flowers are sessile or without stalks | Achyranthes, Amaranthus |
| Spikelet | When the peduncle is branched, and each branch bears sessile flowers, the inflorescence is called a spikelet | Members of Poaceae (Gramineae or grass family) |
| Catkin | The spike inflorescence is pendulous or hanging, and the flowers are unisexual | Mulberry, Acalypha |
| Spadix | The spike inflorescence is fleshy and covered by a large, often brightly colored bract called a spathe | Colocasia, Alocasia |
| Corymb | The peduncle is branched, and the branches are of different lengths, such that the flowers are at the same level | Candytuft, Jasmine |
| Umbel | The peduncle is unbranched, and the flowers are stalked and arise from the same point | Onion, Allium |
| Capitulum or Head | The peduncle is flattened and bears sessile flowers on its surface | Sunflower, Marigold |

- Cymose inflorescence can be further divided into the following types :

| Types of Inflorescence | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple Cyme | The main axis terminates in a flower, and one or more lateral branches arise from the axils of the lower bracts, each ending in a flower | Jasmine, Ixora |
| Compound Cyme | The lateral branches of the simple cyme also end in flowers, forming a more complex inflorescence | Calotropis, Vinca |
| Helicoid Cyme | The lateral branches of the simple cyme are produced only on one side of the main axis, forming a scorpioid or helicoid curve | Heliotropium, Begonia |
| Dichasium | The main axis terminates in a flower, and two lateral branches arise from the axils of the lower bracts, each ending in a flower, forming a Y-shaped pattern | Silene, Caryophyllaceae |
| Monochasium | The main axis terminates in a flower, and only one lateral branch arises from the axil of the lower bract, ending in a flower | Euphorbia, Pedilanthus |

- Some special types of inflorescence are :

| Types of Inflorescence | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cyathium | The inflorescence consists of a cup-shaped involucre enclosing a single female flower and several male flowers | Euphorbia |
| Hypanthodium | The inflorescence consists of a hollow receptacle enclosing numerous unisexual flowers | Ficus |
