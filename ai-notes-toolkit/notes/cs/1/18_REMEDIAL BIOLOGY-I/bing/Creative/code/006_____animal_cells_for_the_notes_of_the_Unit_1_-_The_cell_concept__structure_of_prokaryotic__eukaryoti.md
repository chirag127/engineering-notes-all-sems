# Animal Cells

- Animal cells are the basic structural and functional units of animal tissues and organs.
- Animal cells are eukaryotic cells, meaning they have membrane-bound organelles suspended in the cytoplasm enveloped by a plasma membrane.
- Animal cells have several structural organelles that enable them to function properly and elicit mechanisms that benefit the host (animal) .
- The working together of all animal cells gives an animal its ability to move, to reproduce, to respond to stimuli, to digest and absorb food, etc. .
- Some of the common types of animal cells are skin cells, muscle cells, blood cells, nerve cells, and fat cells.
- Some of the major organelles and structures of animal cells are:

  - **Plasma membrane**: The outermost layer of the cell that regulates the entry and exit of substances and protects the cell from the external environment  .
  - **Nucleus**: The largest organelle that contains the genetic material (DNA) and controls the cellular activities  .
  - **Nucleolus**: A dense region inside the nucleus that produces ribosomes .
  - **Cytoplasm**: The fluid-filled matrix that contains the organelles and provides a medium for biochemical reactions  .
  - **Ribosomes**: The small structures that synthesize proteins from amino acids  .
  - **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)**: A network of membranous tubules that transport and modify proteins and lipids  . There are two types of ER: rough ER (with ribosomes attached) and smooth ER (without ribosomes).
  - **Golgi apparatus**: A stack of flattened sacs that sort, package, and deliver proteins and lipids to various destinations  .
  - **Mitochondria**: The organelles that produce energy (ATP) by cellular respiration  .
  - **Lysosomes**: The organelles that contain digestive enzymes and break down unwanted materials and waste products   .
  - **Peroxisomes**: The organelles that contain oxidative enzymes and break down toxic substances and fatty acids .
  - **Centrosomes**: The structures that organize the microtubules and play a role in cell division  .
  - **Cytoskeleton**: The network of protein filaments that provide shape, support, and movement to the cell  . The main components of the cytoskeleton are microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments.
  - **Vesicles**: The small membrane-bound sacs that store and transport substances within or outside the cell  .
  - **Vacuoles**: The large membrane-bound sacs that store water, salts, sugars, and other substances . Animal cells have smaller and more numerous vacuoles than plant cells.
