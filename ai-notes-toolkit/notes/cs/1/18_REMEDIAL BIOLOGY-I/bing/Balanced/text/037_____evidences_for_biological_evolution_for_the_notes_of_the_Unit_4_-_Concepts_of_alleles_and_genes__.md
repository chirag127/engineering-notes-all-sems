### Evidences for biological evolution

Biological evolution is the change in the inherited characteristics of populations of organisms over time. There are many lines of evidence that support the theory of evolution, such as:

- **Fossils**: Fossils are the preserved remains or traces of organisms that lived in the past. Fossils can show how organisms have changed over time, or how they are related to each other. For example, fossils of dinosaurs, whales, and horses show how these animals have evolved from their ancestors  .
- **Comparative anatomy**: Comparative anatomy is the study of the similarities and differences in the structures of different organisms. Comparative anatomy can reveal how organisms are related by descent from a common ancestor, or how they have adapted to different environments. For example, the forelimbs of humans, bats, whales, and cats have the same basic bones, but different shapes and functions. These are called homologous structures, and they indicate a common ancestry . On the other hand, the wings of birds, bats, and insects have different structures, but similar functions. These are called analogous structures, and they indicate convergent evolution .
- **Embryology**: Embryology is the study of the development of embryos, or the early stages of life. Embryology can show how organisms share common features or stages in their development, or how they differ from each other. For example, the embryos of vertebrates, such as fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals, have similar features, such as a tail, gill slits, and a notochord. These features indicate a common ancestry  . On the other hand, the embryos of different plants, such as mosses, ferns, and flowering plants, have different features, such as spores, seeds, and flowers. These features indicate divergent evolution.
- **Molecular biology**: Molecular biology is the study of the molecules that make up the cells and genes of living organisms. Molecular biology can show how organisms share similar DNA sequences, proteins, or biochemical pathways, or how they differ from each other. For example, the DNA sequences of humans and chimpanzees are about 98% identical, indicating a close evolutionary relationship . On the other hand, the DNA sequences of humans and bacteria are very different, indicating a distant evolutionary relationship .
- **Biogeography**: Biogeography is the study of the distribution of organisms across the Earth. Biogeography can show how organisms have evolved in response to geographic isolation, climate change, or continental drift. For example, the marsupials, such as kangaroos, koalas, and wombats, are mostly found in Australia, indicating that they evolved from a common ancestor that was isolated from other continents . On the other hand, the cacti, such as saguaro, prickly pear, and barrel cactus, are found in different continents, such as North America, South America, and Africa, indicating that they evolved independently from different ancestors .
- **Direct observation**: Direct observation is the witnessing of evolutionary changes in living organisms over a short period of time. Direct observation can show how organisms have evolved in response to natural or artificial selection, genetic drift, or gene flow. For example, the bacteria, such as E. coli and Staphylococcus, have evolved resistance to antibiotics, indicating that they have adapted to a new environment . On the other hand, the finches, such as Darwin's finches, have evolved different beak shapes and sizes, indicating that they have diversified into different niches .
