### Structure of Prokaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic cells are unicellular organisms that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Prokaryotic cells have a nucleoid region, which is an irregularly-shaped region that contains the circular, double-stranded DNA .
- Prokaryotic cells have a plasma membrane, which is an outer covering that separates the cell's interior from its surrounding environment.
- Prokaryotic cells have a cell wall, which is an outer layer that maintains the cell's shape, protects the cell interior, and prevents the cell from bursting when it takes up water . The cell wall of most bacteria contains peptidoglycan, a polymer of linked sugars and polypeptides.
- Some prokaryotic cells have a capsule, which is an additional outer covering that protects the cell when it is engulfed by other organisms, helps the cell adhere to surfaces, and prevents dehydration .
- Some prokaryotic cells have flagella, which are long, whip-like structures that help the cell move .
- Some prokaryotic cells have pili, which are short, hair-like structures that help the cell attach to other cells or surfaces, and exchange genetic material .
- Prokaryotic cells have ribosomes, which are small structures that synthesize proteins . Prokaryotic ribosomes are smaller and simpler than eukaryotic ribosomes.
- Some prokaryotic cells have plasmids, which are small, circular pieces of DNA that can replicate independently of the main chromosome and carry genes for special functions, such as antibiotic resistance .
- Some prokaryotic cells have inclusions, which are granules or vesicles that store nutrients, waste products, or other substances .
- Some prokaryotic cells have endospores, which are dormant, resistant structures that can survive harsh conditions and germinate into new cells when conditions improve .
