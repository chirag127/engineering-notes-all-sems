### Major Groups for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Classification of Living Organisms

- Classification of living organisms is the process of grouping them based on their similarities and differences in various aspects such as morphology, anatomy, physiology, genetics, etc.
- Classification helps to organize the diversity of life, to understand the evolutionary relationships among different groups of organisms, and to provide a universal system of nomenclature for naming and identifying them.
- One of the most widely accepted systems of classification is the five kingdom classification, proposed by Robert H. Whittaker in 1969  .
- The five kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. They are based on the following criteria:
  - Cell structure: whether the cells are prokaryotic (lacking a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles) or eukaryotic (having a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles).
  - Mode of nutrition: whether the organisms are autotrophic (capable of making their own food by photosynthesis or chemosynthesis) or heterotrophic (dependent on other sources of organic matter for food).
  - Thallus organization: whether the body of the organism is unicellular (consisting of a single cell), colonial (consisting of a loose aggregation of similar cells), filamentous (consisting of a chain of similar cells), or multicellular (consisting of many cells of different types and functions).
  - Phylogenetic relationships: whether the organisms share a common ancestry and evolutionary history with other groups of organisms.
  - Reproduction: whether the organisms reproduce asexually (by binary fission, budding, fragmentation, spore formation, etc.) or sexually (by fusion of gametes, conjugation, etc.).

- The following table summarizes the main characteristics and examples of each kingdom:

| Kingdom | Cell structure | Mode of nutrition | Thallus organization | Phylogenetic relationships | Reproduction | Examples |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------------------|--------------|----------|
| Monera | Prokaryotic | Autotrophic or heterotrophic | Unicellular, colonial, or filamentous | Bacteria and archaea | Asexual or sexual | E. coli, cyanobacteria, methanogens, etc. |
| Protista | Eukaryotic | Autotrophic or heterotrophic | Unicellular, colonial, or multicellular | Algae, protozoa, and slime molds | Asexual or sexual | Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena, diatoms, etc. |
| Fungi | Eukaryotic | Heterotrophic | Unicellular, colonial, or multicellular | Saprophytic or parasitic fungi | Asexual or sexual | Yeast, mushroom, bread mold, ringworm, etc. |
| Plantae | Eukaryotic | Autotrophic | Multicellular | Photosynthetic plants | Asexual or sexual | Moss, fern, pine, rose, mango, etc. |
| Animalia | Eukaryotic | Heterotrophic | Multicellular | Animals | Asexual or sexual | Sponge, jellyfish, earthworm, cockroach, frog, lion, etc. |

- The five kingdom classification is not the only system of classification, and it has some limitations and drawbacks. For example, it does not account for the molecular and genetic differences among the organisms, it does not reflect the true evolutionary relationships among some groups of organisms, and it does not include the viruses, which are not considered as living organisms.
- Therefore, some alternative systems of classification have been proposed, such as the six kingdom system, the three domain system, and the phylogenetic system. However, the five kingdom system is still widely used and taught in biology courses.

- Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature are two methods of naming and identifying the organisms based on their scientific names.
- Systematic is the branch of biology that deals with the classification and nomenclature of living organisms. It aims to provide a logical and consistent system of naming and grouping the organisms based on their natural relationships and characteristics.
- Binomial system of nomenclature is the method of giving a scientific name to each organism, consisting of two parts: the genus name and the species name. For example, the scientific name of human is Homo sapiens, where Homo is the genus name and sapiens is the species name.
