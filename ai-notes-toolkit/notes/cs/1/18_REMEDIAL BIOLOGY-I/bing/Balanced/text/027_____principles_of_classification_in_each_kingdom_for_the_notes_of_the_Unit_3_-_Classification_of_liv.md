### Principles of classification in each kingdom

- Classification is the process of grouping living organisms into categories based on their similarities and differences.
- Classification helps to organize the diversity of life and to understand the evolutionary relationships among different groups of organisms.
- The most widely used system of classification is the five-kingdom classification proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969.
- The five kingdoms are: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
- Each kingdom has its own principles of classification based on certain characteristics, such as mode of nutrition, cell structure, body organization, reproduction and phylogeny.

#### Monera
- Monera is the kingdom of prokaryotic organisms, which lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- Monera includes bacteria, cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) and archaebacteria (extremophiles).
- Monera is classified into two major groups: Eubacteria and Archaebacteria.
- Eubacteria are the common bacteria that inhabit various environments and have diverse metabolic pathways. They are further classified based on their shape, cell wall structure, staining properties, oxygen requirement, mode of nutrition and genetic characteristics.
- Archaebacteria are the ancient bacteria that live in extreme conditions, such as high temperature, high salinity, high acidity or high methane concentration. They have unique cell wall composition, membrane lipids and ribosomal RNA sequences. They are further classified based on their habitat, such as methanogens, halophiles, thermophiles and acidophiles.

#### Protista
- Protista is the kingdom of eukaryotic organisms, which have a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, but are not multicellular or have a simple level of multicellularity.
- Protista includes protozoa (animal-like protists), algae (plant-like protists) and slime molds (fungus-like protists).
- Protista is classified into three major groups: Protozoa, Algae and Slime molds.
- Protozoa are the heterotrophic protists that move by flagella, cilia or pseudopodia. They are further classified based on their locomotion, such as flagellates, ciliates, amoeboids and sporozoans.
- Algae are the autotrophic protists that have chlorophyll and other pigments for photosynthesis. They are further classified based on their pigmentation, cell wall composition, storage products and life cycle, such as green algae, red algae, brown algae and diatoms.
- Slime molds are the saprophytic protists that feed on decaying organic matter. They have a complex life cycle that involves a plasmodial stage (a mass of multinucleate cytoplasm) and a sporangial stage (a structure that produces spores). They are further classified based on their plasmodial or cellular nature, such as plasmodial slime molds and cellular slime molds.

#### Fungi
- Fungi is the kingdom of eukaryotic organisms that are heterotrophic and feed by absorption of organic matter.
- Fungi include molds, yeasts, mushrooms and lichens.
- Fungi is classified into four major groups: Zygomycota, Ascomycota, Basidiomycota and Deuteromycota.
- Zygomycota are the fungi that produce thick-walled sexual spores called zygospores. They have a coenocytic (non-septate) hyphae (filaments) and asexual spores called sporangiospores. They include bread molds and dung fungi.
- Ascomycota are the fungi that produce sexual spores called ascospores inside a sac-like structure called ascus. They have a septate hyphae and asexual spores called conidia. They include yeasts, penicillium, morels and truffles.
- Basidiomycota are the fungi that produce sexual spores called basidiospores on a club-shaped structure called basidium. They have a septate hyphae and asexual spores called basidiospores. They include mushrooms, puffballs, rusts and smuts.
- Deuteromycota are the fungi that have no known sexual stage and are classified based on their asexual spores and hyphal structure. They include ringworm, athlete's foot and cheese molds.
