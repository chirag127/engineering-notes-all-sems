### Flower

A flower is a modified shoot that bears the reproductive organs of a plant. It consists of four types of modified leaves: sepals, petals, stamens and carpels. These are arranged in a specific pattern on a floral axis. The function of a flower is to produce gametes, facilitate pollination and form seeds and fruits.

- Sepals are the outermost whorl of the flower. They are usually green and protect the flower bud before it opens. They may also help in photosynthesis. The collective term for all the sepals is calyx.
- Petals are the next whorl of the flower. They are usually colorful and attract pollinators. They may also produce nectar or fragrance. The collective term for all the petals is corolla.
- Stamens are the male reproductive organs of the flower. They consist of a filament and an anther. The anther produces pollen grains, which contain the male gametes. The collective term for all the stamens is androecium.
- Carpels are the female reproductive organs of the flower. They consist of an ovary, a style and a stigma. The ovary contains one or more ovules, which contain the female gametes. The style is a slender stalk that connects the ovary to the stigma. The stigma is the receptive surface where pollen grains land and germinate. The collective term for all the carpels is gynoecium.

The arrangement and number of floral parts may vary among different plants. Some flowers may have only one or two types of floral parts, while others may have all four. Some flowers may have multiple whorls of the same type of floral part, while others may have only one. Some flowers may have symmetrical or regular shapes, while others may have asymmetrical or irregular shapes. Some flowers may have separate or distinct floral parts, while others may have fused or united floral parts.

The study of flower structure and function is called flower morphology and anatomy. It helps in understanding the diversity and evolution of plants, as well as their pollination and reproduction strategies. It also helps in identifying and classifying plants based on their floral characteristics.
