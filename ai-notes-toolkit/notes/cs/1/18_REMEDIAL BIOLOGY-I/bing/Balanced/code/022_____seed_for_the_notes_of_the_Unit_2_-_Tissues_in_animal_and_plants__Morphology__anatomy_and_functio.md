### Tissues in animal and plants

- A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function .
- Tissues are found in animals and plants, but they have different structures and functions .
- The four main types of animal tissues are:
  - Connective tissue: It supports and binds other tissues, such as blood, cartilage, bone, and fat  .
  - Nervous tissue: It allows rapid communication between different parts of the body, such as the brain, spinal cord, and nerves  .
  - Muscle tissue: It can contract to allow movement of body parts, such as the heart, skeletal muscles, and smooth muscles  .
  - Epithelial tissue: It covers and protects the body surfaces, such as the skin, lining of the digestive tract, and glands  .
- The three main tissue systems in plants are:
  - Epidermis: It is the outermost layer of cells that covers and protects the plant body, such as the leaves, stems, and roots .
  - Ground tissue: It is the bulk of the plant body that performs various functions, such as photosynthesis, storage, and support, such as the parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma .
  - Vascular tissue: It is the tissue that carries water and minerals from the roots to the leaves, and food from the leaves to the rest of the plant, such as the xylem and phloem  .

### Morphology, anatomy and functions of different parts of plants: Root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower, fruit and seed

- Root: It is the part of the plant that grows underground and anchors the plant to the soil. It also absorbs water and minerals from the soil and stores food.
  - Morphology: The root has a primary root that grows from the seed, and secondary roots that branch from the primary root. The root has a root cap that protects the growing tip, a root hair zone that increases the surface area for absorption, and a root zone that contains the vascular tissue.
  - Anatomy: The root has three layers of tissues: the epidermis, the cortex, and the stele. The epidermis is the outer layer that has root hairs. The cortex is the middle layer that stores food. The stele is the inner layer that contains the xylem and phloem.
  - Functions: The root anchors the plant to the soil, absorbs water and minerals from the soil, and stores food for the plant.
- Stem: It is the part of the plant that grows above the ground and supports the leaves, flowers, and fruits. It also transports water and minerals from the roots to the leaves, and food from the leaves to the rest of the plant.
  - Morphology: The stem has nodes and internodes. The nodes are the points where leaves and buds are attached. The internodes are the segments between the nodes. The stem has a terminal bud at the tip that produces new growth, and lateral buds along the sides that produce branches.
  - Anatomy: The stem has three layers of tissues: the epidermis, the cortex, and the stele. The epidermis is the outer layer that protects the stem. The cortex is the middle layer that provides support and storage. The stele is the inner layer that contains the xylem and phloem.
  - Functions: The stem supports the leaves, flowers, and fruits, and transports water and minerals from the roots to the leaves, and food from the leaves to the rest of the plant.
