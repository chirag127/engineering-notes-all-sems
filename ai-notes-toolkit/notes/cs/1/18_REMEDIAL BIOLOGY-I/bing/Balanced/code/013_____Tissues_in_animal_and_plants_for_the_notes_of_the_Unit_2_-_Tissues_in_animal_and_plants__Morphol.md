### Tissues in animal and plants

- Tissues are groups of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.
- Tissues are found in both animals and plants, but the types and functions of tissues are different in each group.
- Animal tissues can be classified into four main types: epithelial, connective, muscle, and nervous.
  - Epithelial tissue covers the body surface and lines the internal organs and cavities. It provides protection, secretion, absorption, and sensation.
  - Connective tissue supports and binds other tissues and organs. It consists of cells and extracellular matrix. Examples of connective tissue are blood, bone, cartilage, adipose, and ligaments.
  - Muscle tissue can contract and relax to produce movement. It is composed of muscle fibers that contain actin and myosin filaments. There are three types of muscle tissue: skeletal, cardiac, and smooth.
  - Nervous tissue consists of neurons and glial cells. It transmits and processes information in the form of electrical and chemical signals. It forms the brain, spinal cord, and nerves.
- Plant tissues can be classified into three main types: epidermis, ground tissue, and vascular tissue.
  - Epidermis is the outermost layer of the plant body. It protects the plant from water loss, infection, and injury. It may have specialized structures such as stomata, trichomes, and cuticle.
  - Ground tissue fills the space between the epidermis and the vascular tissue. It performs various functions such as photosynthesis, storage, and support. It is composed of three types of cells: parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma.
  - Vascular tissue transports water and nutrients throughout the plant. It consists of two types of conducting elements: xylem and phloem. Xylem carries water and minerals from the roots to the leaves. Phloem carries organic substances from the leaves to the rest of the plant.
