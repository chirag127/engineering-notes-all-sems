- Concepts of alleles and genes
  - Genes are chunks of DNA that contribute to particular traits or functions by coding for proteins that influence physiology .
  - Alleles are different versions of a gene, which vary according to the nucleotide base present at a particular genome location .
  - An individual's combination of alleles is known as their genotype .
  - In sexually reproducing organisms, each parent gives an allele for each gene, giving the offspring two alleles per gene.
  - Alleles can be dominant or recessive, meaning that they can mask or be masked by another allele in the expression of a trait.
- Mendelian Experiments
  - Mendelian experiments were conducted by Gregor Mendel, a 19th century monk and scientist, who studied the inheritance of traits in pea plants.
  - Mendel observed that some traits, such as seed shape and flower color, were inherited in discrete units, which he called "heritable factors" or genes.
  - Mendel formulated the laws of segregation and independent assortment, which state that each parent contributes one allele for each gene to the offspring, and that the alleles for different genes are inherited independently of each other.
  - Mendel also used the concepts of dominant and recessive alleles, and devised the Punnett square method to predict the genotypes and phenotypes of the offspring from a cross.
- Cell cycle (Elementary Idea)
  - The cell cycle is the series of events that take place in a cell leading to its division and duplication.
  - The cell cycle consists of four main phases: G1, S, G2, and M.
  - In G1, the cell grows and prepares for DNA replication.
  - In S, the cell replicates its DNA, resulting in two copies of each chromosome.
  - In G2, the cell grows and prepares for mitosis.
  - In M, the cell divides into two daughter cells, each with the same number and type of chromosomes as the parent cell.
- Mitosis and meiosis
  - Mitosis and meiosis are two types of cell division that occur in eukaryotic cells.
  - Mitosis is the process of dividing a somatic cell (non-reproductive cell) into two identical daughter cells, each with the same number and type of chromosomes as the parent cell.
  - Mitosis consists of four stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase.
  - In prophase, the chromosomes condense and the nuclear envelope breaks down.
  - In metaphase, the chromosomes align at the equator of the cell.
  - In anaphase, the sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles of the cell.
  - In telophase, the chromosomes decondense and the nuclear envelope reforms.
  - Mitosis is followed by cytokinesis, which is the division of the cytoplasm and the formation of two daughter cells.
  - Meiosis is the process of dividing a germ cell (reproductive cell) into four haploid daughter cells, each with half the number of chromosomes as the parent cell.
  - Meiosis consists of two rounds of division: meiosis I and meiosis II.
  - In meiosis I, the homologous chromosomes pair up and exchange segments in a process called crossing over, then separate and move to opposite poles of the cell.
  - In meiosis II, the sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles of the cell.
  - Meiosis is followed by cytokinesis, which is the division of the cytoplasm and the formation of four daughter cells.
