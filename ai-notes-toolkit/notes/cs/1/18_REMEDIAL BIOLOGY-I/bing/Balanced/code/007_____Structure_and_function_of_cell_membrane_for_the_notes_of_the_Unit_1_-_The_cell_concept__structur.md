### Structure and function of cell membrane

- The cell membrane, also known as the plasma membrane, is a thin semi-permeable membrane that surrounds the cytoplasm of a cell .
- The cell membrane is composed primarily of fatty-acid-based lipids and proteins, and some carbohydrate groups that are attached to some of the lipids and proteins .
- The most common type of lipid in the cell membrane is the phospholipid, which has a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and two hydrophobic (water-fearing) tails. The phospholipids form a bilayer, with the heads facing the aqueous environment and the tails facing each other.
- The cell membrane also contains cholesterol, which helps to maintain the fluidity and stability of the membrane.
- The proteins in the cell membrane can be classified into two types: integral and peripheral. Integral proteins span the entire membrane and are embedded in the phospholipid bilayer, while peripheral proteins are attached to the surface of the membrane or to the integral proteins.
- The proteins in the cell membrane have various functions, such as transport, enzymatic activity, signal transduction, cell recognition, intercellular joining, and attachment to the cytoskeleton .
- The carbohydrate groups in the cell membrane are usually attached to the lipids or proteins, forming glycolipids or glycoproteins, respectively. The carbohydrate groups act as recognition sites for other cells or molecules, and play a role in cell-cell communication and adhesion.

- The main functions of the cell membrane include :
  - Protecting the integrity of the interior of the cell by keeping the constituents of the cell in and unwanted substances out.
  - Providing support and maintaining the shape of the cell.
  - Regulating cell growth through the balance of endocytosis and exocytosis.
  - Acting as a selectively permeable membrane by allowing the entry of only selected substances into the cell and the exit of waste products from the cell.
  - Playing an important role in cell signalling and communication by receiving and transmitting signals from hormones, neurotransmitters, and other signalling molecules.
