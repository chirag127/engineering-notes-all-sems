# Systematic and Binomial System of Nomenclature

- Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature is a formal and universal method of naming living organisms by using two Latin words that indicate the genus and the species of the organism  .
- The system was developed by Carl Linnaeus, a Swedish botanist, in his work Systema Naturae (The System of Nature) in the 18th century .
- The advantages of using binomial nomenclature are:
  - It avoids confusion and ambiguity that may arise from using common names that vary across languages, regions and cultures  .
  - It reflects the evolutionary relationships and biological characteristics of the organisms by grouping them into hierarchical categories based on their similarities and differences   .
  - It facilitates communication and exchange of information among scientists and researchers worldwide  .
- The rules of binomial nomenclature are:
  - The first word is the genus name and the second word is the species name    .
  - The genus name is always capitalized and the species name is always lowercase    .
  - Both words are written in italics or underlined when handwritten    .
  - The genus name can be abbreviated with the first letter followed by a period, but the species name must be written in full    .
  - The name of the author who first described the organism can be added after the binomial name in parentheses or as a superscript    .
- Some examples of binomial names are:
  - Homo sapiens (modern humans) 
  - Escherichia coli (a common bacterium) 
  - Canis lupus (gray wolf) 
  - Rosa rubiginosa (sweet briar rose)
