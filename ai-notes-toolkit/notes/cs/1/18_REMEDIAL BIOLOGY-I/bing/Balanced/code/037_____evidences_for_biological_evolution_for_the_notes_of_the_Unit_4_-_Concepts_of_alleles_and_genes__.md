# Evidences for Biological Evolution

Biological evolution is the change in the inherited characteristics of populations of organisms over time. Evolution explains the diversity and adaptation of life on Earth. There are many lines of evidence that support the theory of evolution, such as:

- **Fossils**: Fossils are the preserved remains or traces of organisms that lived in the past. Fossils show the sequence of appearance and disappearance of different groups of organisms over geological time. Fossils also reveal the transitional forms that link the ancestors and descendants of living species. For example, the fossil record of whales shows how they evolved from land mammals to aquatic mammals over millions of years  .
- **Comparative Anatomy**: Comparative anatomy is the study of the similarities and differences in the structures of different organisms. Comparative anatomy can show the homologous structures and analogous structures of different species. Homologous structures are structures that have a common origin but may have different functions, such as the forelimbs of humans, whales, bats, and birds. Analogous structures are structures that have a similar function but different origin, such as the wings of insects, birds, and bats. Homologous structures indicate a common ancestry, while analogous structures indicate a common selective pressure .
- **Embryo Development**: Embryo development is the process of growth and differentiation of a fertilized egg into a multicellular organism. Embryo development can show the similarities and differences in the early stages of different organisms. Embryo development can reveal the vestigial structures and atavisms of different species. Vestigial structures are structures that have no apparent function in the present organism but were functional in the ancestors, such as the tailbone in humans. Atavisms are structures that reappear in the present organism but were absent in the recent ancestors, such as the hind limbs in some whales. Vestigial structures and atavisms indicate the evolutionary history of different species .
- **Molecular Biology**: Molecular biology is the study of the structure and function of the molecules of life, such as DNA, RNA, and proteins. Molecular biology can show the similarities and differences in the genetic and biochemical composition of different organisms. Molecular biology can reveal the molecular homologies and molecular clocks of different species. Molecular homologies are similarities in the DNA sequences or protein structures of different species that indicate a common ancestry, such as the cytochrome c protein that is involved in cellular respiration. Molecular clocks are methods of estimating the time of divergence of different species based on the rate of mutation of certain genes or proteins, such as the mitochondrial DNA that is inherited from the mother .
- **Biogeography**: Biogeography is the study of the distribution and diversity of organisms across different regions and continents. Biogeography can show the patterns and processes of evolution in different geographical areas. Biogeography can reveal the endemic species and adaptive radiations of different regions. Endemic species are species that are found only in a specific area and nowhere else, such as the lemurs of Madagascar. Adaptive radiations are events of rapid diversification of a single ancestral species into many different species that occupy different ecological niches, such as the finches of the Galapagos Islands .
- **Direct Observation**: Direct observation is the empirical evidence of evolution that can be seen in the present time. Direct observation can show the changes and adaptations of organisms in response to environmental factors, such as natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow, and mutation. Direct observation can reveal the artificial selection and experimental evolution of different populations. Artificial selection is the human intervention in the breeding of organisms to produce desired traits, such as the domestication of dogs from wolves. Experimental evolution is the manipulation of the conditions and variables of evolution in a laboratory setting, such as the evolution of antibiotic resistance in bacteria .
