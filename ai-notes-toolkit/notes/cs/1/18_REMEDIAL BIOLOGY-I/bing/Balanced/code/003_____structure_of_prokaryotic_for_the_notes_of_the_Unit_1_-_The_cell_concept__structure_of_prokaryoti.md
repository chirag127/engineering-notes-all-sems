### Structure of Prokaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic cells are unicellular organisms that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Prokaryotic cells have a nucleoid region, which is an irregularly-shaped region that contains the cell’s DNA and is not surrounded by a nuclear envelope .
- Prokaryotic cells have a plasma membrane, which is an outer covering that separates the cell’s interior from its surrounding environment.
- Prokaryotic cells have a cell wall, which is an outer covering that protects the cell and gives it shape. The cell wall of most bacteria contains peptidoglycan, a polymer of linked sugars and polypeptides .
- Some prokaryotic cells have a capsule, which is an additional outer covering that protects the cell when it is engulfed by other organisms or helps the cell adhere to surfaces .
- Prokaryotic cells have cytoplasm, which is a gel-like substance that fills the cell and contains the cellular structures and molecules.
- Prokaryotic cells have ribosomes, which are small structures that synthesize proteins from amino acids and genetic information.
- Some prokaryotic cells have plasmids, which are small circular pieces of DNA that can replicate independently of the main chromosome and carry genes for special functions, such as antibiotic resistance.
- Some prokaryotic cells have flagella, which are long whip-like structures that help the cell move through liquid environments.
- Some prokaryotic cells have pili, which are short hair-like structures that help the cell attach to other cells or surfaces, or exchange genetic material.
