### Mitosis and Meiosis

Mitosis and meiosis are two types of cell division that occur in eukaryotic organisms. They have different purposes and outcomes, and they involve different numbers and arrangements of chromosomes.

#### Mitosis
- Mitosis is the process by which most cells in the body divide, such as skin cells, muscle cells, and nerve cells.
- Mitosis involves a single round of cell division, and produces two identical, diploid daughter cells. Diploid means that each cell has two sets of chromosomes, one from each parent.
- Mitosis consists of four main phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase. During these phases, the chromosomes condense, align at the equator of the cell, separate, and move to opposite poles of the cell. Cytokinesis, the division of the cytoplasm, occurs in telophase.
- The main function of mitosis is to enable growth, repair, and maintenance of the body.

#### Meiosis
- Meiosis is the process by which gametes are produced, such as sperm and egg cells.
- Meiosis involves two rounds of cell division, and produces four different, haploid daughter cells. Haploid means that each cell has one set of chromosomes, half the number of the diploid cells.
- Meiosis consists of two stages: meiosis I and meiosis II. Each stage has four phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase. During these phases, the chromosomes pair up, exchange segments, align at the equator of the cell, separate, and move to opposite poles of the cell. Cytokinesis occurs in telophase I and telophase II.
- The main function of meiosis is to generate genetic diversity and enable sexual reproduction.
