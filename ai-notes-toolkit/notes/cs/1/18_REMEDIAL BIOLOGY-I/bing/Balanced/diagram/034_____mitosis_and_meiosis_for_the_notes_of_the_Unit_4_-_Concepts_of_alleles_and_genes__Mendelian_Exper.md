### Mitosis and Meiosis

- Mitosis and meiosis are two types of cell division that occur during the cell cycle.
- Mitosis is the process by which most cells in the body divide, involves a single round of cell division, and produces two identical, diploid daughter cells .
- Meiosis is the process by which gametes (sex cells) are produced, involves two rounds of cell division, and produces four different, haploid daughter cells .
- The main difference between mitosis and meiosis is that mitosis maintains the same number of chromosomes in the daughter cells as in the parent cell, while meiosis reduces the number of chromosomes by half in the daughter cells .
- Mitosis and meiosis are both important for the growth, development, and reproduction of organisms.
- Mitosis and meiosis have different phases that involve the separation of chromosomes and the division of the cytoplasm.
- The phases of mitosis are: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase.
- The phases of meiosis are: prophase I, metaphase I, anaphase I, telophase I, prophase II, metaphase II, anaphase II, and telophase II.
- Mitosis and meiosis are regulated by the cell cycle, which consists of four phases: G1, S, G2, and M.
- The cell cycle is controlled by various checkpoints and proteins that ensure the accuracy and timing of cell division.
- Mitosis and meiosis are influenced by various factors, such as genetic variation, environmental conditions, and errors in DNA replication or chromosome segregation .
- Mitosis and meiosis have different roles and consequences in the evolution of life, such as maintaining genetic stability, generating genetic diversity, and enabling adaptation and speciation  .
