## Concepts of alleles and genes
- Genes are segments of DNA that code for proteins and influence traits or functions .
- Alleles are different versions of a gene that have different nucleotide sequences .
- An individual's combination of alleles is called their genotype.
- The expression of alleles in the phenotype (observable traits) is influenced by the mode of inheritance (dominant, recessive, codominant, etc.).

## Mendelian Experiments
- Mendel was a monk who studied inheritance patterns in pea plants (Pisum sativum) in the 19th century .
- He crossed plants with different traits (such as flower color, seed shape, etc.) and observed the ratios of the offspring in different generations .
- He discovered that traits are inherited in discrete units (genes) that do not blend together, and that each parent contributes one allele for each gene .
- He formulated three laws of inheritance: the law of segregation, the law of independent assortment, and the law of dominance .

## Cell cycle (Elementary Idea)
- The cell cycle is the process by which a cell grows, duplicates its DNA, and divides into two daughter cells .
- The cell cycle consists of four main stages: G1, S, G2, and M .
- G1, S, and G2 are collectively called interphase, which is the period of growth and preparation for division .
- M is the mitotic phase, which includes mitosis (nuclear division) and cytokinesis (cytoplasmic division) .
- The cell cycle is regulated by checkpoints that ensure the cell is ready to proceed to the next stage.
