### Mechanism of evolution

- Evolution is the process by which modern organisms have descended from ancient ancestors.
- Evolution is responsible for both the remarkable similarities and the amazing diversity of life on Earth.
- Evolution occurs when the frequency of genes or traits in a population changes over generations.
- There are different mechanisms of evolution that affect the gene pool of a population   :
  - **Mutation**: a random change in the DNA sequence of an organism that can create new genetic variation or alter existing genes   .
  - **Recombination**: the process of shuffling and exchanging genetic material during sexual reproduction that can create new combinations of genes  .
  - **Natural selection**: the differential survival and reproduction of individuals with certain traits that are better adapted to their environment   .
  - **Sexual selection**: the preference for certain traits in mates that can affect the reproductive success of individuals   .
  - **Artificial selection**: the intentional breeding of individuals with certain traits by humans that can change the characteristics of a population  .
  - **Genetic drift**: the random change in the frequency of genes or traits due to chance events that can affect small populations more strongly   .
  - **Gene flow**: the movement of genes or traits between populations due to migration, dispersal, or interbreeding that can increase or decrease genetic variation   .
- These mechanisms of evolution can act together or separately to shape the diversity and complexity of life on Earth .
- These mechanisms of evolution can also lead to the formation of new species or speciation, which is the splitting of a single evolutionary lineage into two or more genetically independent ones .
- Evolution can be studied at different levels, such as microevolution (evolution within a population) and macroevolution (evolution above the species level)  .
- Evolution can be supported by various types of evidence, such as fossils, comparative anatomy, molecular biology, biogeography, and embryology .
