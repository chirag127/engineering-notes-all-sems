### Fruit

- A fruit is the part of a flowering plant that contains the seeds.
- It is the structure of a plant formed from ovaries after flowering.
- It is the edible part of the plant, not all fruits are edible.
- It maintains the symbiotic relationship between a plant and with animal or human.
- Fruits, whether false or true, may be classified into three groups: simple, aggregate, and multiple or composite.

#### Simple Fruits
- When the ovary of a single flower with or without accessory parts matures into one fruit, it is called simple fruit.
- Simple fruits may be dry or fleshy.
- The dry fruit again may be dehiscent, indehiscent, or schizocarpic.
- Dehiscent fruits split open at maturity to release the seeds.
- Indehiscent fruits do not split open at maturity and retain the seeds.
- Schizocarpic fruits break up into one-seeded segments called mericarps.
- Fleshy fruits have a soft and juicy pericarp.
- Simple fruit examples: grapes, tomatoes, bananas, pepo, hesperidium, blueberry, etc.

#### Aggregate Fruits
- When the ovaries of a single flower with many pistils mature into a single fruit, it is called aggregate fruit.
- Aggregate fruits are formed from a single flower with many pistils, each of which develops into a small fruit called a fruitlet.
- The fruitlets are arranged on a common receptacle.
- Aggregate fruit examples: strawberry, raspberry, blackberry, etc.

#### Multiple or Composite Fruits
- When the ovaries of many flowers of an inflorescence mature into a single fruit, it is called multiple or composite fruit.
- Multiple or composite fruits are formed from a cluster of flowers called an inflorescence.
- Each flower in the inflorescence produces a fruit, but these join together as they mature to form a single mass.
- Multiple or composite fruit examples: pineapple, jackfruit, fig, mulberry, etc.
