## Unit 4 - Concepts of alleles and genes, Mendelian Experiments, Cell cycle (Elementary Idea), mitosis and meiosis, techniques to study mitosis and meiosis. Origin of life, evidences for biological evolution, Mechanism of evolution −Variation (Mutation and Recombination), Natural Selection with examples, Types of natural selection

1. **Alleles and genes**: Alleles are different versions of the same gene that determine distinct traits that can be passed on from parents to offspring. Genes are segments of DNA that carry the instructions for making proteins, which in turn help determine an organism's traits.
2. **Mendelian Experiments**: Gregor Mendel, an Austrian monk, conducted experiments on pea plants to understand the inheritance of traits. He discovered that traits are inherited in predictable patterns, now known as the laws of Mendelian inheritance.
3. **Cell cycle**: The cell cycle is the series of events that take place in a cell leading to its division and duplication. It consists of two main stages: interphase, during which the cell grows and replicates its DNA, and the mitotic phase, during which the cell divides.
4. **Mitosis and meiosis**: Mitosis is the process by which a cell divides its nucleus and its genetic material into two identical daughter cells. Meiosis, on the other hand, is the process by which the number of chromosomes in a cell is halved to produce gametes for sexual reproduction.
5. **Techniques to study mitosis and meiosis**: There are several techniques used to study mitosis and meiosis, including microscopy, flow cytometry, and chromosome spreads.
6. **Origin of life**: The origin of life on Earth is still a mystery, but there are several theories about how it may have happened. One theory is that life originated from simple organic molecules in a "primordial soup" of chemicals.
7. **Evidences for biological evolution**: There are many evidences for biological evolution, including the fossil record, comparative anatomy, and molecular biology.
8. **Mechanism of evolution**: Evolution occurs through several mechanisms, including mutation, recombination, and natural selection.
9. **Variation**: Variation refers to the differences in traits among individuals of a species. It can arise through mutation, which is a change in the DNA sequence, or through recombination, which is the reshuffling of genetic material during sexual reproduction.
10. **Natural Selection**: Natural selection is the process by which certain traits become more or less common in a population over time due to their effect on the survival and reproduction of the individuals that possess them. There are several types of natural selection, including directional, stabilizing, and disruptive selection.
