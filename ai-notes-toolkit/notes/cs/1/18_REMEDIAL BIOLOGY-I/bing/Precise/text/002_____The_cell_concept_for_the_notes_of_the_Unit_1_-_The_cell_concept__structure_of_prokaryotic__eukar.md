### The Cell Concept

1. The cell is the basic unit of life and is the smallest unit that can perform all the functions necessary for life.
2. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
3. Cells arise from pre-existing cells through the process of cell division.
4. The cell concept includes the structure of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, as well as plant and animal cells.
5. Prokaryotic cells are simple in structure and lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are typically found in bacteria and archaea.
6. Eukaryotic cells are more complex in structure and have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are typically found in plants, animals, fungi, and protists.
7. Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole, while animal cells do not.
8. The cell membrane is a selectively permeable barrier that separates the cell from its environment and controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
9. Cell organelles are specialized structures within the cell that perform specific functions. These include the nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, and peroxisomes.
10. Cell signaling is the process by which cells communicate with each other and respond to their environment.
11. The compound microscope is a tool used to observe cells and their structures. It uses multiple lenses to magnify the image of the specimen.
12. The basic chemical constituents of living bodies include water, carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.
