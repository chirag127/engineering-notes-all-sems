### Structure and function of cell membrane

The cell membrane, also known as the plasma membrane, is a thin layer of lipid and protein molecules that encloses the cell, separating its internal environment from the external environment. The cell membrane has several important functions, including:

1. **Selective permeability:** The cell membrane is selectively permeable, meaning that it allows certain molecules to pass through while preventing others from crossing the membrane. This is achieved through the presence of transport proteins that facilitate the movement of specific molecules across the membrane.

2. **Protection:** The cell membrane provides a physical barrier that protects the cell from its external environment.

3. **Cell signaling:** The cell membrane contains various receptor proteins that can bind to specific signaling molecules, such as hormones, and initiate a cellular response.

4. **Cell adhesion:** The cell membrane also contains adhesion proteins that allow cells to stick to one another and to the extracellular matrix, which is important for the formation of tissues and organs.

The cell membrane is composed of a lipid bilayer, which consists of two layers of phospholipid molecules. The hydrophobic tails of the phospholipids face inward, while the hydrophilic heads face outward. This arrangement creates a hydrophobic interior that prevents the passage of water-soluble molecules. The cell membrane also contains various proteins, including transport proteins, receptor proteins, and adhesion proteins, that are embedded in the lipid bilayer or attached to its surface.

In summary, the cell membrane is a vital component of the cell that plays a crucial role in maintaining the integrity of the cell and regulating the exchange of materials between the cell and its environment. Its structure and composition allow it to perform these functions effectively.
