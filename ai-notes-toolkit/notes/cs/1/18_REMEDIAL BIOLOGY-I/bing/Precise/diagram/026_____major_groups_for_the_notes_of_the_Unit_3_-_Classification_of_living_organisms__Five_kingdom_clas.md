### Major Groups for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Classification of Living Organisms

1. **Five Kingdom Classification**: This system of classification was proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969 and is based on the following criteria: 
    - Cellular organization (prokaryotic or eukaryotic)
    - Mode of nutrition (autotrophic or heterotrophic)
    - Body organization (unicellular or multicellular)
    - Phylogenetic relationships
    The five kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia.

2. **Major Groups and Principles of Classification in Each Kingdom**:
    - **Monera**: This kingdom includes all prokaryotic organisms, including bacteria and cyanobacteria. The classification is based on shape, cell wall composition, mode of nutrition, and genetic characteristics.
    - **Protista**: This kingdom includes all unicellular eukaryotic organisms, including protozoa, algae, and slime molds. The classification is based on mode of nutrition, motility, and cell structure.
    - **Fungi**: This kingdom includes all multicellular eukaryotic organisms that obtain their food by absorbing nutrients from other organisms. The classification is based on the structure of their fruiting bodies and spores, as well as their mode of reproduction.
    - **Plantae**: This kingdom includes all multicellular eukaryotic organisms that are autotrophic, meaning they produce their own food through photosynthesis. The classification is based on the presence or absence of vascular tissue, seeds, and flowers.
    - **Animalia**: This kingdom includes all multicellular eukaryotic organisms that are heterotrophic, meaning they obtain their food by consuming other organisms. The classification is based on body symmetry, presence or absence of a backbone, and the type of body covering.

3. **Systematic and Binomial System of Nomenclature**: Systematics is the study of the diversity of organisms and their relationships. The binomial system of nomenclature is a standardized system for naming species, where each species is given a two-part name consisting of the genus and species name.

4. **Concept of Animal and Plant Classification**: The classification of animals and plants is based on their shared characteristics and evolutionary relationships. This is done through the use of taxonomic ranks, such as kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species.
