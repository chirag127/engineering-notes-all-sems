### Variation

Variation refers to the differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species. Variation can be caused by genetic and environmental factors.

1. **Genetic Variation:** Genetic variation is the result of differences in the genetic makeup of individuals within a population. This can be caused by mutation, recombination, and gene flow.
    - **Mutation:** A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence of an organism. Mutations can occur spontaneously or be induced by external factors such as radiation or chemicals. Some mutations can be beneficial, while others can be harmful or neutral.
    - **Recombination:** Recombination is the process by which genetic material is exchanged between chromosomes during the formation of gametes. This results in new combinations of alleles, increasing genetic variation within a population.
    - **Gene Flow:** Gene flow is the movement of alleles between populations. This can occur through migration or interbreeding, and can introduce new alleles into a population, increasing genetic variation.

2. **Environmental Variation:** Environmental variation is the result of differences in the environment that individuals are exposed to. This can include factors such as diet, climate, and exposure to toxins. Environmental variation can affect the expression of an individual's genotype, resulting in differences in phenotype.

Variation is an important concept in the study of evolution, as it provides the raw material for natural selection to act upon. Natural selection can only occur if there is variation in the population, and the individuals with the most advantageous traits are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing on their advantageous traits to their offspring.
