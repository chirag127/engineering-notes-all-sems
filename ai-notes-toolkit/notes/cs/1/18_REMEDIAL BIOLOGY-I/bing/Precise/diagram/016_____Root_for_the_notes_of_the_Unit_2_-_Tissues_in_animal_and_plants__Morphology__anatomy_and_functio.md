### Root

Root is the part of the plant that is typically found below the surface of the soil. It is the first part of the plant to emerge from the seed during germination. The primary functions of the root are to anchor the plant in the soil, absorb water and minerals from the soil, and store food.

#### Tissues in Roots

Roots have different types of tissues that perform various functions. The outermost layer of the root is the epidermis, which protects the root and absorbs water and minerals from the soil. The cortex is the layer beneath the epidermis and is made up of parenchyma cells that store food. The endodermis is the innermost layer of the cortex and regulates the movement of water and minerals into the root. The vascular tissue, which includes the xylem and phloem, is responsible for transporting water, minerals, and food throughout the plant.

#### Morphology of Roots

Roots can have different shapes and sizes, depending on the plant species and the environment in which the plant is growing. Some common root morphologies include taproots, fibrous roots, and adventitious roots. Taproots are long and thick, with smaller lateral roots branching off from the main root. Fibrous roots are thin and spread out, with many small roots growing from the base of the stem. Adventitious roots grow from parts of the plant other than the base of the stem, such as from the stem or leaves.

#### Anatomy of Roots

The anatomy of the root refers to the internal structure of the root. The root cap is a protective layer of cells at the tip of the root that helps the root grow through the soil. The root apical meristem is the region of actively dividing cells behind the root cap. The zone of elongation is where the cells of the root elongate and push the root cap forward. The zone of maturation is where the cells of the root differentiate into the various types of root tissues.

#### Functions of Roots

Roots perform several important functions for the plant. They anchor the plant in the soil, providing stability and support. They absorb water and minerals from the soil, which are essential for the plant's growth and survival. They also store food, which the plant can use during times of stress or when it is not actively growing. Additionally, roots can play a role in vegetative reproduction, with some plants producing new shoots from their roots.
