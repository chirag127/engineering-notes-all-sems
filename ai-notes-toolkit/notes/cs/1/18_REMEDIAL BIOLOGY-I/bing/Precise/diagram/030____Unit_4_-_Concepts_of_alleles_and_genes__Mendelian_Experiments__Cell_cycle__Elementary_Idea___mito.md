## Unit 4 - Concepts of alleles and genes, Mendelian Experiments, Cell cycle (Elementary Idea), mitosis and meiosis, techniques to study mitosis and meiosis. Origin of life, evidences for biological evolution, Mechanism of evolution −Variation (Mutation and Recombination), Natural Selection with examples, Types of natural selection

1. **Alleles and genes**: Alleles are different versions of the same gene that determine distinct traits. Genes are segments of DNA that carry the genetic information responsible for the inheritance of traits.
2. **Mendelian Experiments**: Gregor Mendel conducted experiments on pea plants to study the inheritance of traits. He discovered the principles of segregation and independent assortment, which form the basis of modern genetics.
3. **Cell cycle**: The cell cycle is the series of events that take place in a cell leading to its division and duplication. It consists of two main stages: interphase and the mitotic phase.
4. **Mitosis and meiosis**: Mitosis is the process of cell division that results in two identical daughter cells. Meiosis is the process of cell division that results in four genetically diverse daughter cells.
5. **Techniques to study mitosis and meiosis**: Some techniques used to study mitosis and meiosis include microscopy, flow cytometry, and chromosome staining.
6. **Origin of life**: The origin of life on Earth is still a mystery, but several theories have been proposed, including the primordial soup theory and the panspermia theory.
7. **Evidences for biological evolution**: Evidence for biological evolution includes the fossil record, comparative anatomy, and molecular biology.
8. **Mechanism of evolution**: The main mechanisms of evolution are mutation, gene flow, genetic drift, and natural selection.
9. **Variation (Mutation and Recombination)**: Variation is the raw material for evolution. It arises from mutation, which introduces new genetic information, and recombination, which shuffles existing genetic information.
10. **Natural Selection with examples**: Natural selection is the process by which certain traits become more or less common in a population due to their effect on survival and reproduction. Examples include the peppered moth and antibiotic resistance in bacteria.
11. **Types of natural selection**: There are several types of natural selection, including directional selection, stabilizing selection, and disruptive selection.
