### Anatomy and Functions of Different Parts of Plants

#### Unit 2 - Tissues in Animal and Plants, Morphology, Anatomy and Functions of Different Parts of Plants: Root, Stem, Leaf, Inflorescence, Flower, Fruit and Seed

##### Root
- The root anchors the plant in the soil and absorbs water and minerals from the soil.
- The root also stores food and nutrients for the plant.
- The root system can be divided into two types: taproot and fibrous root.
- The taproot is a single, large root that grows deep into the soil, while fibrous roots are a network of smaller roots that spread out near the surface of the soil.

##### Stem
- The stem supports the plant and transports water, minerals, and food between the roots and the leaves.
- The stem also stores food and nutrients for the plant.
- The stem can be divided into two types: herbaceous and woody.
- Herbaceous stems are soft and green, while woody stems are hard and brown.

##### Leaf
- The leaf is the main site of photosynthesis in the plant, where sunlight is converted into energy for the plant.
- The leaf also helps regulate water loss through small openings called stomata.
- Leaves can be simple or compound, and their shape and arrangement on the stem can vary widely.

##### Inflorescence
- The inflorescence is the arrangement of flowers on the stem of the plant.
- The inflorescence can take many different forms, including spikes, racemes, umbels, and heads.
- The type of inflorescence can affect the way the plant is pollinated and how the seeds are dispersed.

##### Flower
- The flower is the reproductive organ of the plant, where the male and female reproductive cells are produced and fertilization takes place.
- The flower is made up of several parts, including the petals, sepals, stamens, and pistil.
- The petals and sepals are often brightly colored to attract pollinators, while the stamens and pistil are involved in the production and transfer of pollen.

##### Fruit
- The fruit is the mature ovary of the flower, which contains the seeds of the plant.
- The fruit can take many different forms, including berries, drupes, capsules, and nuts.
- The type of fruit can affect how the seeds are dispersed and how the plant is able to reproduce.

##### Seed
- The seed is the mature ovule of the plant, which contains the embryo of the new plant.
- The seed is surrounded by a protective coat and often contains a food supply for the developing embryo.
- The seed can remain dormant for a period of time before germinating and growing into a new plant.
