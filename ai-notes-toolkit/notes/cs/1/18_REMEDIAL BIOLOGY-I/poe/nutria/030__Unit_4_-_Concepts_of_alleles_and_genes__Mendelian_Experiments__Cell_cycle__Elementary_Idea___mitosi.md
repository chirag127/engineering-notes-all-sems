
## Unit 4 - Concepts of alleles and genes

* Alleles are alternative forms of a gene that can produce different traits in an organism. 
* Mendelian experiments were conducted by Gregor Mendel to understand the inheritance of traits.
* Cell cycle is a sequence of events that cells go through to replicate and divide.
* Mitosis is the process of cell division that produces two identical daughter cells.
* Meiosis is the process of cell division that produces four daughter cells with half the number of chromosomes as the parent cell.
* Techniques to study mitosis and meiosis include fluorescent microscopy, immunofluorescence, and electron microscopy.

## Origin of life and evidences for biological evolution

* The origin of life is unknown, but many theories exist to explain the emergence of life from non-living matter.
* Evidence for biological evolution includes the fossil record, genetic evidence, and homologous structures.

## Mechanism of evolution −Variation (Mutation and Recombination)

* Variation is a key component of evolution, and can be caused by mutation and recombination.
* Mutation is a change in the DNA sequence of an organism, and can be caused by environmental factors or errors in replication.
* Recombination is the process of exchanging genetic material between two chromosomes, and can lead to the formation of new combinations of genes.

## Natural Selection with examples

* Natural selection is the process by which certain traits become more common in a population over time.
* Examples of natural selection include the development of antibiotic resistance in bacteria, the evolution of camouflage in animals, and the development of drought-resistant crops.

## Types of natural selection

* There are three main types of natural selection: directional selection, stabilizing selection, and disruptive selection.
* Directional selection occurs when an extreme trait is favored over other traits.
* Stabilizing selection occurs when the average trait is favored over other traits.
* Disruptive selection occurs when both extreme traits are favored over the average trait.
