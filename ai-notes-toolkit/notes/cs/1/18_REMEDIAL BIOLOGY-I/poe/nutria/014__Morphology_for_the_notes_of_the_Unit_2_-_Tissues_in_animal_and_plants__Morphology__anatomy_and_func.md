
### Morphology for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Tissues in Animals and Plants, Morphology, Anatomy and Functions of Different Parts of Plants: Root, Stem, Leaf, Inflorescence, Flower, Fruit and Seed

1. Tissues in Animals and Plants: Tissues are groups of cells that are organized to perform specific functions. In animals, there are four main types of tissue: epithelial, connective, muscle, and nervous. In plants, there are three main types of tissue: dermal, vascular, and ground. 

2. Morphology: Morphology is the study of the structure and form of organisms. It focuses on the external features of organisms, such as their size, shape, and color. Morphology also looks at the internal structure of organisms, such as their organs and tissues.

3. Anatomy: Anatomy is the study of the internal structure of organisms. It focuses on the organs and tissues of organisms, such as their size, shape, and location. Anatomy also looks at the external features of organisms, such as their size, shape, and color.

4. Functions of Different Parts of Plants: 

- Root: The root system provides anchorage to the plant and absorbs water and minerals from the soil. 
- Stem: The stem provides support to the plant and transports water and minerals from the roots to the other parts of the plant. 
- Leaf: The leaves are the main photosynthetic organs of the plant. They absorb light energy from the Sun and use it to produce food for the plant. 
- Inflorescence: The inflorescence is the flowering part of the plant. It contains the reproductive organs of the plant. 
- Flower: The flower is the reproductive organ of the plant. It contains the male and female reproductive organs (stamens and pistils). 
- Fruit: The fruit is the mature ovary of the plant. It contains the seeds of the plant. 
- Seed: The seed is the reproductive unit of the plant. It contains the embryo of the plant, which will grow into a new plant.
