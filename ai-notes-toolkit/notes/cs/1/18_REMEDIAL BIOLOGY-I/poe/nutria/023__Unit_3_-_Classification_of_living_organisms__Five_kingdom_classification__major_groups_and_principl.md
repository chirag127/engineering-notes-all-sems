

## Unit 3 - Classification of living Organisms

* Five kingdom classification: This classification system was first proposed by the biologist Carolus Linnaeus in the 18th century and is still used today. It categorizes living organisms into five distinct groups, namely Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. 
* Major groups and principles of classification in each kingdom: Each kingdom is further divided into smaller groups based on certain characteristics. For example, the Plantae kingdom is divided into flowering plants, conifers, and ferns. The Animalia kingdom is divided into invertebrates and vertebrates. 
* Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature: The systematic and binomial system of nomenclature is a system of naming organisms that uses two Latin names, the genus and the species. This system was first proposed by the Swedish botanist Carl Linnaeus in the 18th century and is still used today. 
* Concept of animal and plant classification: Animal and plant classification is the process of grouping living organisms into categories based on their shared characteristics. This is done in order to better understand the diversity of life on Earth.
