
## Unit 2 - Tissues in Animal and Plants, Morphology, Anatomy and Functions of Different Parts of Plants: Root, Stem, Leaf, Inflorescence, Flower, Fruit and Seed

1. Tissues in Animal and Plants: 
    - Animal tissues are composed of cells, proteins and other molecules that perform specific functions. They are divided into four main types: epithelial, connective, muscle and nerve tissues. 
    - Plant tissues are composed of cells, proteins, and other molecules that perform specific functions. They are divided into three main types: dermal, ground and vascular tissues. 

2. Morphology: 
    - Morphology is the study of the form and structure of organisms and their parts. It is used to describe and classify organisms based on their physical characteristics. 

3. Anatomy: 
    - Anatomy is the study of the structure of organisms and their parts. It is used to understand how organisms are put together and how they work. 

4. Functions of Different Parts of Plants: 
    - Root: The root is the part of the plant that anchors it to the soil and absorbs water and minerals from the soil. 
    - Stem: The stem is the part of the plant that supports the leaves and flowers and transports water and nutrients from the roots to the other parts of the plant. 
    - Leaf: The leaf is the part of the plant that produces food for the plant through photosynthesis. 
    - Inflorescence: The inflorescence is the part of the plant that produces the flowers. 
    - Flower: The flower is the reproductive part of the plant. It produces the pollen and nectar that attract pollinators. 
    - Fruit: The fruit is the part of the plant that contains the seeds of the plant. 
    - Seed: The seed is the part of the plant that contains the genetic information of the plant.
