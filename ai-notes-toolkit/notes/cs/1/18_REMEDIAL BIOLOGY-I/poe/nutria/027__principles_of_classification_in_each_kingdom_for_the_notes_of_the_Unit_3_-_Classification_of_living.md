
### Principles of Classification in Each Kingdom

1. Five Kingdom Classification: This classification system was proposed by Robert Whittaker in 1969 and divides all living organisms into five kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia.

2. Major Groups: The Five Kingdom Classification system is further divided into major groups, such as prokaryotes, eukaryotes, fungi, plants, and animals.

3. Principles of Classification in Each Kingdom: Each kingdom has its own set of principles for classification. For example, the Monera kingdom is divided into two major groups: prokaryotes and eukaryotes. The Protista kingdom is divided into four major groups: protozoa, algae, slime molds, and water molds. The Fungi kingdom is divided into two major groups: fungi and lichens. The Plantae kingdom is divided into two major groups: vascular plants and non-vascular plants. The Animalia kingdom is divided into two major groups: invertebrates and vertebrates.

4. Systematic and Binomial System of Nomenclature: The systematic and binomial system of nomenclature is used to classify organisms. This system assigns each organism a two-part scientific name consisting of a genus and species name.

5. Concept of Animal and Plant Classification: Animals and plants are classified based on their physical characteristics, such as size, shape, color, and behavior. This classification system is known as taxonomy.
