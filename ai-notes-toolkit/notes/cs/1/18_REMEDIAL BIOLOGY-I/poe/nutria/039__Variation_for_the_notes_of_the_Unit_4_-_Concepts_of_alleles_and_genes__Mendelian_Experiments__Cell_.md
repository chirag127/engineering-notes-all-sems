
### Variation for the Notes of the Unit 4 - REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-I KCS

1. Alleles and Genes: Alleles are variants of a gene which can be dominant or recessive and are responsible for the various traits of an organism. Genes are the basic units of heredity which carry the instructions for the development of an organism. 

2. Mendelian Experiments: Mendelian experiments are experiments conducted by Gregor Mendel to study the inheritance of traits in pea plants. He concluded that traits are inherited in a predictable manner and are passed on from parents to offspring in the form of discrete units.

3. Cell Cycle (Elementary Idea): The cell cycle is a series of events that occur in a cell in order to produce two identical daughter cells. It consists of four main stages: interphase, prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase.

4. Mitosis and Meiosis: Mitosis is the process of cell division which involves the replication of the chromosomes and their equal distribution into two daughter cells. Meiosis is the process of cell division which involves the production of four daughter cells with half the number of chromosomes as the parent cell.

5. Techniques to Study Mitosis and Meiosis: There are various techniques used to study mitosis and meiosis such as cell staining, light microscopy, electron microscopy, and immunofluorescence.

6. Origin of Life: The origin of life is the emergence of life from non-living matter. It is believed that life originated from a chemical reaction which occurred in a primordial soup.

7. Evidences for Biological Evolution: There is a vast amount of evidence to support the theory of biological evolution such as fossil records, comparative anatomy, comparative embryology, molecular biology, and biogeography.

8. Mechanism of Evolution - Variation (Mutation and Recombination): Variation is the process by which different forms of a gene are produced. It can occur through mutation or recombination. Mutations are random changes in the DNA sequence which can result in a new allele. Recombination is the process by which different combinations of alleles are created.

9. Natural Selection with Examples: Natural selection is the process by which organisms with certain traits are more likely to survive and reproduce than those without those traits. For example, in a population of birds, those with longer beaks are more likely to survive and reproduce as they can reach food more easily.

10. Types of Natural Selection: There are three main types of natural selection: directional selection, stabilizing selection, and disruptive selection. Directional selection favors one extreme of a trait, stabilizing selection favors the average of a trait, and disruptive selection favors both extremes of a trait.
