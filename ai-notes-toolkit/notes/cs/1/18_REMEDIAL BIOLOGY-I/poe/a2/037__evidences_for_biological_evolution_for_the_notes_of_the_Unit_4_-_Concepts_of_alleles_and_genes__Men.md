### Evidences for Biological Evolution

- Fossil Records: Fossils provide evidence for evolution by showing change in organisms over time. The fossil record shows transition from ancient to modern-day organisms. For example, fossils of ancient human ancestors depict the gradual change from apes to modern humans.
- Comparative Anatomy: The anatomical similarities and differences between living organisms provide evidence for evolution. Organisms that share a common ancestor have more similar anatomies compared to those that do not. For example, humans and apes share similar bone structures since they share a common ancestor.
- Comparative Embryology: The embryonic development of different organisms provides evidence for evolution. Organisms that share a common ancestor show similar embryonic stages. For example, human and fish embryos show similar early stages of development with gill slits and tails, indicating their common ancestry.
- Biogeography: The distribution of plants and animals on Earth matches the evolutionary relationships between species. Species that share a common ancestor are found in geographical areas that were once joined. For example, marsupials are mostly found in Australia and South America, indicating that these regions were once connected.
- Molecular Similarities: The DNA and proteins of organisms provide evidence for evolution. Species that share a common ancestor show more molecular similarities compared to those that do not. For example, humans share 98% of their DNA with chimpanzees, indicating that they share a common ancestor.
