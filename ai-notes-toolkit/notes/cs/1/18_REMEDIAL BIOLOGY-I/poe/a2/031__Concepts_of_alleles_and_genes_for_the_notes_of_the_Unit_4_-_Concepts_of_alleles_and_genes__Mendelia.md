### Concepts of alleles and genes

- Alleles: Alternative forms of a gene occupying the same locus on a chromosome.
- Gene: A segment of DNA that contains the instructions for making a protein. It is the basic physical and functional unit of heredity.
- Homozygous: Having two identical alleles of a given gene.
- Heterozygous: Having two different alleles of a given gene.

Mendelian Experiments: Gregor Mendel conducted experiments on pea plants and proposed laws of inheritance which formed the foundation of genetics.

- Law of Dominance: When two different alleles are present for a trait, one allele will be dominant and the other recessive. The dominant allele will be expressed in the phenotype.
- Law of Segregation: During gamete formation, the two alleles for a trait separate or segregate from each other such that each gamete receives only one allele.

Cell cycle (Elementary Idea): The cell cycle is a series of stages that lead to cell growth and division into two daughter cells. The key stages are:

- G1 phase: Cell growth
- S phase: DNA replication
- G2 phase: Preparation for mitosis
- M phase: Mitosis and cytokinesis

Mitosis and Meiosis: Mitosis results in two identical daughter cells, while meiosis results in four sex cells with half the genetic material. Meiosis involves two divisions: Meiosis I and Meiosis II. Meiosis I involves crossing over and segregation of homologous chromosomes. Meiosis II is similar to mitosis.
