### Concept of animal and plant classification for the notes of the Unit 3 - Classification of living organisms (Five kingdom classification, major groups and principles of classification in each kingdom), Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature, Concept of animal and plant classification in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-I KCS.

- Living organisms are classified into 5 kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.
- Kingdom Monera: Prokaryotic unicellular organisms (Bacteria). Classification based on nutrition mode, shape, motility.
- Kingdom Protista: Eukaryotic unicellular organisms (Protozoa, Algae). Classification based on mode of nutrition, locomotion, complexity of cell structure.
- Kingdom Fungi: Eukaryotic heterotrophic organisms lacking chlorophyll (Mushrooms, Molds). Classification based on type of reproduction, structure of fruiting body.
- Kingdom Plantae: Eukaryotic autotrophic organisms with chlorophyll (Algae, Plants). Classification based on presence/absence of vascular tissues, seeds, flowers, etc.
- Kingdom Animalia: Eukaryotic heterotrophic organisms lacking cell walls (Sponges, Worms, Insects, Amphibians, Reptiles, Birds, Mammals). Classification based on levels of organization, symmetry, body cavity, etc.

- Binomial nomenclature: Genus (capitalized) + Species (lowercase). Uniquely identifies each organism using a two-word Latin name.
- Taxonomic hierarchy: Domain > Kingdom > Phylum > Class > Order > Family > Genus > Species.
- Similar characteristics are used to classify organisms into the same group. Broad to specific classification. Closely-related groups have a common ancestor.
