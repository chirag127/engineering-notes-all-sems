### Successive Differentiation (nth order derivatives)

- Successive differentiation is the process of differentiating a given function repeatedly until n times, where n is a positive integer.
- The result of successive differentiation is called the nth derivative or the nth order derivative of the function.
- The nth derivative of a function f(x) is denoted by f^(n)(x) or d^n f/dx^n, where the superscript n or the exponent n indicates the number of times the function is differentiated.
- The nth derivative of a function f(x) can be obtained by applying the chain rule, the product rule, the quotient rule, or the power rule of differentiation, depending on the form of the function.
- The nth derivative of a function f(x) can also be expressed as a combination of the lower order derivatives of f(x) and its products with other functions, using the Leibnitz theorem or the generalized product rule.
- The Leibnitz theorem states that the nth derivative of the product of two functions u(x) and v(x) is given by:

f^(n)(x) = (u(x)v(x))^(n) = sum_{k=0}^n (n choose k) u^(n-k)(x) v^(k)(x)

where (n choose k) is the binomial coefficient.

- The nth derivative of a function f(x) can be used to study the properties and behavior of the function, such as its concavity, inflection points, maxima, minima, and Taylor series expansion.
