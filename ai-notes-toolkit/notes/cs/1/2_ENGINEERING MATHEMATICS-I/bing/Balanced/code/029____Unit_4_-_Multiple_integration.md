## Unit 4 - Multiple integration

- Multiple integration is the extension of single-variable integration to functions of two or more variables, such as f(x,y) or f(x,y,z).
- Multiple integration can be used to calculate areas, volumes, masses, centroids, moments of inertia, and other geometric or physical quantities that depend on more than one variable.
- The basic idea of multiple integration is to divide the region of integration into small subregions, approximate the function by a constant on each subregion, and add up the contributions from each subregion.
- There are different types of multiple integrals, depending on the number and nature of the variables and the region of integration. The most common ones are:

  - Double integrals: These are integrals of functions of two variables over a region in the xy-plane. They can be written as $$\iint_R f(x,y) dA$$ where R is the region of integration and dA is an infinitesimal area element. Double integrals can be evaluated by iterated integration, where one variable is integrated first and then the other, or by changing to polar coordinates, where the region is expressed in terms of the radius and angle of a point.

  - Triple integrals: These are integrals of functions of three variables over a region in the xyz-space. They can be written as $$\iiint_V f(x,y,z) dV$$ where V is the region of integration and dV is an infinitesimal volume element. Triple integrals can be evaluated by iterated integration, where one variable is integrated first, then the second, and then the third, or by changing to cylindrical or spherical coordinates, where the region is expressed in terms of the radius, angle, and height or distance of a point.

  - Line integrals: These are integrals of functions of two or three variables along a curve in the plane or space. They can be written as $$\int_C f(x,y) ds$$ or $$\int_C f(x,y,z) ds$$ where C is the curve of integration and ds is an infinitesimal arc length element. Line integrals can be evaluated by parametrizing the curve, substituting the parametric equations into the integrand, and integrating with respect to the parameter.

  - Surface integrals: These are integrals of functions of three variables over a surface in the space. They can be written as $$\iint_S f(x,y,z) dS$$ where S is the surface of integration and dS is an infinitesimal surface area element. Surface integrals can be evaluated by parametrizing the surface, substituting the parametric equations into the integrand, and integrating with respect to the parameters.

- Multiple integration has many applications in physics, engineering, and mathematics, such as calculating the work done by a force along a path, the flux of a vector field across a surface, the area of a surface, the volume of a solid, the mass of a body, the center of mass of a system, the moment of inertia of a rigid body, and the probability of a random event.
