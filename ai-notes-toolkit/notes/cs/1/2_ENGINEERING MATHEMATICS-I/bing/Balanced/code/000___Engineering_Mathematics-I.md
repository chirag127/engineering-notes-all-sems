# Engineering Mathematics-I

Engineering Mathematics-I is a course that covers the basic concepts and techniques of calculus and its applications in engineering problems. The course aims to provide the students with the mathematical tools and skills that are essential for engineering analysis and modelling. The course may have different syllabi depending on the university or institution, but some of the common topics are:

- Differential calculus: This topic includes the study of functions, limits, continuity, derivatives, rules of differentiation, higher order derivatives, applications of derivatives, maxima and minima, curve sketching, mean value theorems, Taylor and Maclaurin series, indeterminate forms, and L'Hospital's rule.
- Integral calculus: This topic includes the study of indefinite and definite integrals, methods of integration, integration by parts, integration by substitution, integration by partial fractions, improper integrals, applications of integrals, area, volume, arc length, surface area, work, and average value.
- Differential equations: This topic includes the study of ordinary differential equations and their solutions, first order and first degree equations, separable equations, homogeneous equations, exact equations, linear equations, Bernoulli equations, applications of differential equations, modelling, growth and decay, mixing problems, orthogonal trajectories, and Newton's law of cooling.
- Linear algebra: This topic includes the study of matrices, determinants, systems of linear equations, Gaussian elimination, Cramer's rule, inverse of a matrix, rank of a matrix, linear independence, vector spaces, basis, dimension, linear transformations, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, diagonalization, and applications of linear algebra, such as linear models, Markov chains, and cryptography.
- Complex numbers: This topic includes the study of complex numbers, their algebraic and geometric representations, polar form, De Moivre's theorem, roots of complex numbers, complex functions, complex differentiation, complex integration, Cauchy-Riemann equations, Cauchy's integral theorem, Cauchy's integral formula, and applications of complex numbers, such as Fourier series, Laplace transforms, and electrical circuits.
