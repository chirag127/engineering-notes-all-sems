### Curve tracing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Differential Calculus- I in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Curve tracing is the process of sketching the graph of a curve given its equation in cartesian, polar or parametric form. It involves finding the important features of the curve, such as its domain, range, intercepts, asymptotes, symmetry, extrema, inflection points, and concavity. Curve tracing helps to visualize the behavior of the curve and to analyze its properties.

The general steps for curve tracing are:

- Identify the type of the curve (cartesian, polar or parametric) and its degree (linear, quadratic, cubic, etc.).
- Find the domain and range of the curve by considering the possible values of the variables and any restrictions imposed by the equation.
- Find the x- and y-intercepts of the curve by setting one variable to zero and solving for the other.
- Find the vertical and horizontal asymptotes of the curve by finding the limits of the function as x or y approaches infinity or a finite value.
- Find the symmetry of the curve by checking if the equation remains unchanged or changes sign when x or y is replaced by -x or -y.
- Find the extrema of the curve by finding the critical points where the first derivative is zero or undefined and applying the first or second derivative test to determine if they are local maxima, minima or saddle points.
- Find the inflection points of the curve by finding the points where the second derivative is zero or undefined and checking the sign of the second derivative on either side of the points to determine if the concavity changes.
- Find the concavity of the curve by finding the intervals where the second derivative is positive (concave up) or negative (concave down).
- Sketch the curve by plotting the important points and using the information from the previous steps to guide the shape of the curve.

  - The domain and range are both all real numbers.
  - The x-intercepts are x = -2, x = 1 and x = 2, obtained by factoring the equation as y = (x + 2)(x - 1)(x - 2).
  - The y-intercept is y = 2, obtained by setting x = 0.
  - There are no vertical or horizontal asymptotes, as the function is continuous and does not approach infinity or a finite value as x or y increases or decreases.
  - The curve is symmetric about the origin, as y = -(-x)^3 + 3(-x) + 2 = -y.
  - The extrema are x = -1 and x = 1, obtained by finding the zeros of the first derivative y' = 3x^2 - 3. Applying the second derivative test, y'' = 6x, we see that x = -1 is a local maximum and x = 1 is a local minimum, as y'' < 0 and y'' > 0 respectively at these points.
  - The inflection point is x = 0, obtained by finding the zero of the second derivative y'' = 6x. The concavity changes from concave down to concave up at this point, as y'' < 0 for x < 0 and y'' > 0 for x > 0.
  - The concavity is concave down for x < 0 and concave up for x > 0, as determined by the sign of the second derivative y'' = 6x.
