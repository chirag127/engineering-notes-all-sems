### Skew-Hermitian matrices

- A square matrix of complex numbers is said to be a skew-Hermitian matrix if it is equal to the negative of its conjugate transpose, also called tranjugate .
- Mathematically, a skew-Hermitian matrix A satisfies the equation A* = -A, where A* is the conjugate transpose of A .
- The conjugate transpose of a matrix is obtained by taking the transpose of the matrix and then replacing each element by its complex conjugate .
- The complex conjugate of a complex number a + bi is a - bi, where a and b are real numbers and i is the imaginary unit .
- For example, if A = [ i 1 + i 2 − 3 i − 1 + i 2 i 1 − 2 − 3 i − 1 0], then A* = [ − i 1 − i − 2 + 3 i 1 − i − 2 i − 1 + 2 + 3 i 1 0] and A* = -A.
- For a skew-Hermitian matrix, the diagonal elements are always equal to zero or purely imaginary numbers .
- The eigenvalues of a skew-Hermitian matrix are also either zero or purely imaginary numbers .
- A skew-Hermitian matrix is an example of a normal matrix, which means that it commutes with its conjugate transpose, i.e., AA* = A*A .
- A normal matrix is diagonalizable, which means that it can be written as A = PDP*, where P is a unitary matrix and D is a diagonal matrix .
- The eigenvectors of a skew-Hermitian matrix for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal, which means that they form a basis for the vector space .
- Skew-Hermitian matrices are important in quantum mechanics, where they are used to represent observables, such as angular momentum and spin.
