### Green's theorem and Stoke's theorem (without proof) and their applications

- Green's theorem is a special case of the Kelvin–Stokes theorem, when applied to a region in the xy-plane.
- Green's theorem states that the line integral of a vector field F around a simple closed curve C in a plane is equal to the double integral of the curl of F over the region R enclosed by C.
- Mathematically, Green's theorem can be written as:

$$\oint_C F \cdot ds = \iint_R \nabla \times F \cdot dA$$

- Green's theorem is primarily utilised for the integration of lines and grounds. This Green’s theorem exhibits the connection between line integrals and area integrals.
- Some applications of Green's theorem are:
  - Finding the area of a plane region by using a line integral around its boundary.
  - Finding the work done by a force field along a closed curve by using a double integral over the region enclosed by the curve.
  - Finding the circulation and flux of a vector field along a closed curve by using a double integral over the region enclosed by the curve.

- Stoke's theorem is a generalization of Green's theorem to three dimensions. It states that the surface integral of the curl of a vector field F over a smooth oriented surface S in space is equal to the line integral of F around the boundary curve C of S.
- Mathematically, Stoke's theorem can be written as:

$$\iint_S \nabla \times F \cdot dS = \oint_C F \cdot ds$$

- Stoke's theorem relates a vector surface integral over surface S in space to a line integral around the boundary of S.
- Some applications of Stoke's theorem are:
  - Finding the work done by a force field along a curve in space by using a surface integral over a surface bounded by the curve.
  - Finding the circulation and flux of a vector field along a curve in space by using a surface integral over a surface bounded by the curve.
  - Finding the curl of a vector field in space by using a surface integral over a small surface patch around a point.
