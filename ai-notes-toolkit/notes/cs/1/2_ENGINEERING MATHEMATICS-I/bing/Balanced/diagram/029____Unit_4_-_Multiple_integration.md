## Unit 4 - Multiple integration

- Multiple integration is the extension of single-variable integration to functions of two or more variables, such as f(x,y) or f(x,y,z).
- Multiple integration can be used to calculate areas, volumes, masses, centroids, moments of inertia, and other geometric and physical quantities of regions and solids in the plane or in space.
- The main types of multiple integrals are:
  - Double integrals: integrals of functions of two variables over a region in the xy-plane.
  - Triple integrals: integrals of functions of three variables over a region in the xyz-space.
  - Line integrals: integrals of functions along a curve in the plane or in space.
  - Surface integrals: integrals of functions over a surface in space.
- The methods and techniques of multiple integration include:
  - Iterated integrals: evaluating multiple integrals by performing successive single-variable integrations.
  - Fubini's theorem: a result that allows changing the order of integration in iterated integrals under certain conditions.
  - Change of variables: transforming multiple integrals into simpler ones by using substitutions of variables, such as polar, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates.
  - Green's theorem: a result that relates a line integral of a vector field around a simple closed curve in the plane to a double integral of the curl of the vector field over the region enclosed by the curve.
  - Divergence theorem: a result that relates a surface integral of a vector field over a closed surface in space to a triple integral of the divergence of the vector field over the solid enclosed by the surface.
  - Stokes' theorem: a result that relates a surface integral of the curl of a vector field over an oriented surface in space to a line integral of the vector field along the boundary curve of the surface.
