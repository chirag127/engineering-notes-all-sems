### Approximation of errors for the notes of the Unit 3 - Differential Calculus-II in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

- Differentials are useful tools for approximating the values and errors of functions that depend on one or more variables.
- A differential of a function is the product of its derivative and the differential of the independent variable, i.e., `dy = f'(x)dx` or `df(x) = f'(x)dx`.
- A differential can be used to estimate the change in the value of a function when the independent variable changes by a small amount, i.e., `Δy ≈ dy`.
- The difference between the actual change in the value of the function and the differential approximation is called the error, i.e., `error = Δy - dy`.
- The error can be expressed as a fraction of the actual change in the value of the function, i.e., `relative error = error / Δy`.
- The relative error can be multiplied by 100 to obtain the percentage error, i.e., `percentage error = relative error * 100`.
- The error, relative error, and percentage error can be used to measure the accuracy and precision of the differential approximation.
- The smaller the error, the more accurate the approximation is. The smaller the relative error, the more precise the approximation is.
- The error, relative error, and percentage error depend on the choice of the independent variable and the point of approximation.
- The error, relative error, and percentage error can be minimized by choosing the independent variable and the point of approximation that make the derivative of the function as small as possible.
