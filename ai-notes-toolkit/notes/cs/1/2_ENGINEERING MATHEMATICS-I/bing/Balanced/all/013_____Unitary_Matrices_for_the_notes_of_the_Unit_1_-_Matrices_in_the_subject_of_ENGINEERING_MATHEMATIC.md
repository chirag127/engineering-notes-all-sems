# Unitary Matrices

A unitary matrix is a complex square matrix that satisfies the following equation:

U^H U = U U^H = I

where U^H is the conjugate transpose of U and I is the identity matrix. This means that the inverse of a unitary matrix is its conjugate transpose.

Some properties of unitary matrices are:

- The unitary matrix is a non-singular matrix, meaning that its determinant is not zero.
- The unitary matrix is an invertible matrix, meaning that it has a unique inverse.
- The product of two unitary matrices is a unitary matrix, meaning that unitary matrices form a group under matrix multiplication.
- The inverse of a unitary matrix is another unitary matrix, meaning that unitary matrices are closed under inversion.
- A matrix is unitary if and only if its transpose is unitary, meaning that unitary matrices are symmetric with respect to conjugation and transposition.
- A matrix is unitary if its rows are orthonormal and its columns are orthonormal, meaning that the dot product of any two distinct rows or columns is zero and the norm of any row or column is one.

Some examples of unitary matrices are:

- The identity matrix I is a unitary matrix, since I^H I = I I^H = I.
- The matrix A = [2^(-1/2) 2^(-1/2) 0; -2^(-1/2)i 2^(-1/2)i 0; 0 0 i] is a unitary matrix, since A^H A = A A^H = I and its rows and columns are orthonormal.
- The matrix B = [1/sqrt(2) 1/sqrt(2); -1/sqrt(2) 1/sqrt(2)] is a unitary matrix, since B^H B = B B^H = I and its rows and columns are orthonormal. This matrix is also an example of a real unitary matrix, which is the same as an orthogonal matrix.
