# Curve tracing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Differential Calculus- I in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Curve tracing is the process of sketching the graph of a curve given its equation in cartesian, polar or parametric form. It involves finding the domain, range, symmetry, intercepts, asymptotes, intervals of increase and decrease, local extrema, points of inflection, concavity and curvature of the curve.

Some general steps for curve tracing are:

- Identify the type of curve (cartesian, polar or parametric) and its degree (linear, quadratic, cubic, etc.).
- Find the domain and range of the curve by solving for the values of x and y that make the equation valid.
- Find the symmetry of the curve by checking if the equation is unchanged when x or y is replaced by -x or -y, or when x and y are interchanged.
- Find the x-intercepts and y-intercepts of the curve by setting y or x equal to zero and solving for the other variable.
- Find the vertical and horizontal asymptotes of the curve by finding the limits of y as x approaches infinity or negative infinity, or vice versa.
- Find the intervals of increase and decrease of the curve by finding the first derivative of y with respect to x and checking its sign.
- Find the local maxima and minima of the curve by finding the critical points where the first derivative is zero or undefined, and applying the first or second derivative test.
- Find the points of inflection of the curve by finding the second derivative of y with respect to x and checking where it changes sign.
- Find the concavity and curvature of the curve by finding the sign of the second derivative and the value of the radius of curvature.
- Sketch the curve using the above information and adding any other relevant features such as cusps, loops, branches, etc.
