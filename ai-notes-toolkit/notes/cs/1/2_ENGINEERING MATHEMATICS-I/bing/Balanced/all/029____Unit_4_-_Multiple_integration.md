## Unit 4 - Multiple integration

- Multiple integration is the extension of single-variable integration to functions of two or more variables, such as f(x,y) or f(x,y,z).
- Multiple integration can be used to calculate areas, volumes, masses, centroids, moments of inertia, and other geometric and physical quantities of regions and solids in the plane or in space.
- Multiple integration can also be used to evaluate integrals that cannot be solved by single-variable methods, such as integrals involving trigonometric functions, exponential functions, or logarithmic functions of two or more variables.

- The main types of multiple integrals are:

  - Double integrals: These are integrals of functions of two variables over a region in the xy-plane. They can be computed by iterated integration, where the inner integral is with respect to one variable and the outer integral is with respect to the other variable. The order of integration can be changed if the region and the function allow it. Double integrals can also be computed by changing to polar coordinates, which can simplify the region and the function in some cases.
  - Triple integrals: These are integrals of functions of three variables over a region in the xyz-space. They can be computed by iterated integration, where the innermost integral is with respect to one variable, the middle integral is with respect to another variable, and the outermost integral is with respect to the third variable. The order of integration can be changed if the region and the function allow it. Triple integrals can also be computed by changing to cylindrical or spherical coordinates, which can simplify the region and the function in some cases.
  - Line integrals: These are integrals of functions of two or three variables along a curve in the plane or in space. They can be computed by parametrizing the curve with a vector function, and then integrating the function times the magnitude of the derivative of the vector function. Line integrals can be used to calculate the work done by a force field along a curve, the circulation of a vector field along a curve, or the flux of a vector field across a curve.
  - Surface integrals: These are integrals of functions of three variables over a surface in space. They can be computed by parametrizing the surface with a vector function of two parameters, and then integrating the function times the magnitude of the cross product of the partial derivatives of the vector function. Surface integrals can be used to calculate the area of a surface, the mass of a surface, the flux of a vector field across a surface, or the divergence of a vector field over a surface.
