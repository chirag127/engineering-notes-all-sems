## Unit 2 - Differential Calculus- I

Differential calculus is the branch of mathematics that studies the rates of change of functions and their properties. It is one of the two main branches of calculus, along with integral calculus.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- **Limits and continuity**: A limit is the value that a function approaches as its input gets closer to a certain point. Continuity is the property of a function that means it has no breaks or gaps in its graph. A function is continuous at a point if its limit at that point equals its value at that point.
- **Derivatives and rules of differentiation**: A derivative is the rate of change of a function at a given point. It measures how sensitive the function is to small changes in its input. The rules of differentiation are formulas that allow us to find the derivatives of various types of functions, such as polynomials, trigonometric, exponential, logarithmic, etc.
- **Applications of derivatives**: Derivatives have many applications in various fields of science, engineering, economics, etc. Some of the common applications are finding the slope and equation of a tangent line, finding the maximum and minimum values of a function, finding the rate of change of a quantity, finding the elasticity of demand, etc.
- **Higher-order derivatives**: Higher-order derivatives are the derivatives of the derivatives of a function. They measure how the rate of change of a function changes as the input changes. For example, the second derivative of a function is the rate of change of its first derivative, and the third derivative is the rate of change of its second derivative, and so on. Higher-order derivatives can be used to study the concavity, inflection points, and curvature of a function.
