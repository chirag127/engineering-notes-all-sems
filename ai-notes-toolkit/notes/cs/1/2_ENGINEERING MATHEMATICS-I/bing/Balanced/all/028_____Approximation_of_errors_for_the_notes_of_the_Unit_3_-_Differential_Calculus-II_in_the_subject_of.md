# Approximation of errors

- In differential calculus, approximation of errors is a method of estimating the possible errors or uncertainties in the values of functions or measurements using linear approximations and differentials.
- A linear approximation of a function f at a point x = a is an equation of the form y = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a), where f'(a) is the derivative of f at a. This equation represents the tangent line to the graph of f at a, and it can be used to approximate the values of f near a.
- A differential of a function f at a point x = a is an expression of the form dy = f'(a)dx, where dx is a small change in x and dy is the corresponding change in y. This expression can be used to approximate the change in f when x changes by a small amount dx.
- For example, consider the function f(x) = 1/x at a = 2. The linear approximation of f at a is y = f(2) + f'(2)(x - 2) = 1/2 - 1/4(x - 2). The differential of f at a is dy = f'(2)dx = -1/4 dx. If x increases from 2 to 2.01, then dx = 0.01 and dy = -0.0025. The linear approximation gives y = 0.4975 and the differential gives dy = -0.0025. The actual value of f(2.01) is 0.497512, which is close to the linear approximation and the differential.
- The approximation of errors can be applied to functions of several variables as well. For example, suppose we want to estimate the error in the volume of a cuboid with edge lengths a, b, and c, which are measured with absolute errors Δa, Δb, and Δc. The volume of the cuboid is V = abc, and the absolute error in V is ΔV = V - V0, where V0 is the true volume. Using differentials, we can write dV = V'(a, b, c)da + V'(a, b, c)db + V'(a, b, c)dc, where V' is the partial derivative of V with respect to each variable. If we assume that the errors are small, we can approximate ΔV by dV and use the measured values of a, b, and c. This gives ΔV ≈ bcdΔa + acdΔb + abcΔc. The relative error in V is ΔV/V, which can be approximated by ΔV/(abc). This gives ΔV/V ≈ Δa/a + Δb/b + Δc/c. This formula shows how the relative error in the volume depends on the relative errors in the edge lengths.
