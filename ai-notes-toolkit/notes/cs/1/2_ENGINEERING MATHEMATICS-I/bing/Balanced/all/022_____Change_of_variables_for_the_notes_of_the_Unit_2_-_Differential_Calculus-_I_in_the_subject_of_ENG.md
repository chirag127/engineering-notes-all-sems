# Change of variables

- Change of variables is a technique that allows us to transform a given function or equation into a simpler or more convenient form by introducing new variables that are related to the original ones.
- Change of variables is useful in differential calculus for solving different types of problems, such as:
  - Finding derivatives of composite functions using the chain rule.
  - Reducing nonlinear differential equations to linear ones by applying suitable substitutions.
  - Evaluating integrals by reversing the chain rule, also known as integration by substitution.
- Change of variables can be applied to functions of one or more variables, depending on the context and the problem.
- Change of variables requires us to find the relationship between the new and the old variables, and to express the function or equation in terms of the new variables. This may involve using algebraic manipulation, implicit differentiation, or integration by parts.
- Change of variables also requires us to adjust the limits of integration, if any, according to the new variables. This may involve using inverse functions, graphs, or geometric reasoning.
- Change of variables can simplify the computation and the interpretation of the results, but it also introduces some challenges, such as:
  - Choosing an appropriate substitution that makes the problem easier to solve.
  - Keeping track of the variables and their domains, ranges, and units.
  - Applying the correct formulas and rules for derivatives, integrals, and differential equations.
  - Checking the validity and the accuracy of the solution.
