# Skew-Hermitian Matrix

- A square matrix of complex numbers is said to be a **skew-Hermitian matrix** if that matrix is equal to the negative of its conjugate transpose, also called tranjugate    .
- Mathematically, the definition of the skew-Hermitian matrix is as follows: A square matrix A = [a ij] n × n is such that A* = −A, that is, for every a ij ∈ A, a ij = −a ji*.
- The conjugate transpose of a matrix A, denoted by A*, is obtained by taking the transpose of A and then replacing each element by its complex conjugate    .
- For example, if A = [ i 1 + i 2 − 3 i − 1 + i 2 i 1 − 2 − 3 i − 1 0], then A* = [ −i − 1 − i − 2 + 3 i 1 − i − 2 − i − 1 + 2 + 3 i 1 0] and −A = [ −i − 1 − i − 2 + 3 i 1 − i − 2 − i − 1 + 2 + 3 i 1 0], so A is a skew-Hermitian matrix.

## Properties of the Skew-Hermitian Matrix

- The characteristics of the skew-Hermitian matrix are as follows    :
  - Every skew-Hermitian matrix is an example of a normal matrix. Although not all normal matrices are skew-Hermitian matrices.
  - Any skew-Hermitian matrix is diagonalizable. Its eigenvalues are either purely imaginary or zeros.
  - The diagonal elements of a skew-Hermitian matrix are always equal to zero.
  - The sum of two skew-Hermitian matrices is also a skew-Hermitian matrix.
  - The product of two skew-Hermitian matrices is a skew-Hermitian matrix if and only if they commute, that is, AB = BA.
  - The inverse of a skew-Hermitian matrix is also a skew-Hermitian matrix, if it exists.
  - The conjugate of a skew-Hermitian matrix is also a skew-Hermitian matrix.
  - The determinant of a skew-Hermitian matrix is a real number.
