# Engineering Mathematics-I

Engineering Mathematics-I is a course that covers the basic concepts and techniques of calculus and its applications in engineering problems. The course aims to develop the students' ability to model, analyze, and solve engineering problems using mathematical tools. The course also introduces the students to the use of computer algebra systems for performing calculations and visualizing results.

The syllabus of Engineering Mathematics-I may vary depending on the university and the branch of engineering, but some of the common topics are:

- **Functions and Limits**: Definition and properties of functions, types of functions, limit and continuity of functions, L'Hospital's rule, indeterminate forms, asymptotes, and graphical representation of functions.
- **Differentiation and Applications**: Derivative of a function, rules of differentiation, chain rule, implicit differentiation, higher order derivatives, Leibniz's theorem, Rolle's theorem, mean value theorem, Taylor's theorem, maxima and minima of functions, curve sketching, and optimization problems.
- **Integration and Applications**: Indefinite and definite integrals, fundamental theorem of calculus, methods of integration, integration by parts, integration by substitution, integration by partial fractions, improper integrals, area under a curve, arc length, surface area, volume, and work.
- **Differential Equations and Applications**: Definition and classification of differential equations, order and degree of differential equations, solution of first order differential equations, separable equations, homogeneous equations, exact equations, linear equations, Bernoulli's equations, applications of differential equations, modelling of physical phenomena, population dynamics, radioactive decay, Newton's law of cooling, and orthogonal trajectories.
- **Vector Algebra and Applications**: Vectors and scalars, addition and subtraction of vectors, dot product and cross product of vectors, scalar and vector triple products, applications of vector algebra, forces, moments, work, and torque.

Some of the learning outcomes of Engineering Mathematics-I are:

- To understand the concepts and principles of calculus and its relevance in engineering.
- To apply the techniques of differentiation and integration to solve engineering problems.
- To use differential equations to model and analyze engineering systems and phenomena.
- To perform vector operations and use them to describe physical quantities and situations.
