# Characteristic equation

- The characteristic equation is the equation that is used to find the eigenvalues of a square matrix.
- The eigenvalues are the scalars that satisfy the equation **A**v = λv, where **A** is the matrix and v is a nonzero vector called an eigenvector.
- The characteristic equation is obtained by subtracting λ from the diagonal entries of **A** and then finding the determinant of the resulting matrix. This determinant is called the characteristic polynomial of **A**.
- The characteristic equation can be written as det(**A** - λ**I**) = 0, where **I** is the identity matrix of the same size as **A**.
- The characteristic equation is a polynomial equation in λ of degree n, where n is the order of **A**. The roots of this equation are the eigenvalues of **A**.
- The characteristic equation is invariant under matrix similarity, meaning that if **A** and **B** are similar matrices, then they have the same characteristic equation and the same eigenvalues.
- The characteristic equation can be used to find the properties of a matrix, such as its rank, trace, determinant, and inverse. It can also be used to solve systems of linear differential equations, linear recurrence relations, and matrix differential equations.
