# Partial derivatives

- A partial derivative is a derivative where we hold some variables constant and find the rate of change of a function with respect to one variable .
- For example, if f(x,y) is a function of two variables, then the partial derivative of f with respect to x is denoted by f_x or ∂f/∂x and is obtained by treating y as a constant and differentiating f with respect to x  .
- Similarly, the partial derivative of f with respect to y is denoted by f_y or ∂f/∂y and is obtained by treating x as a constant and differentiating f with respect to y  .
- The partial derivatives of a function can be used to find the slope of a tangent line to a surface, the direction and rate of change of a function, and the optimization of a function .
- The partial derivatives of a function can be calculated using the same rules and formulas as ordinary derivatives, such as the power rule, the product rule, the quotient rule, and the chain rule  .

## Examples

- Find the partial derivatives of f(x,y) = xy + x^2y
  - f_x = y + 2xy (by treating y as a constant and applying the power rule and the product rule)
  - f_y = x + x^2 (by treating x as a constant and applying the power rule and the product rule)
- Find the partial derivatives of g(x,y) = sin(x)cos(y)
  - g_x = cos(x)cos(y) (by treating y as a constant and applying the chain rule)
  - g_y = -sin(x)sin(y) (by treating x as a constant and applying the chain rule)
- Find the partial derivatives of h(x,y) = e^(x+y)/(x-y)
  - h_x = (e^(x+y)(x-y) + e^(x+y))/(x-y)^2 (by treating y as a constant and applying the quotient rule and the chain rule)
  - h_y = (e^(x+y)(x-y) - e^(x+y))/(x-y)^2 (by treating x as a constant and applying the quotient rule and the chain rule)
