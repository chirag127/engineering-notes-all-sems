# Green’s theorem and Stoke’s theorem (without proof) and their applications

## Green’s theorem

- Green’s theorem is a formula that relates a line integral around a simple closed curve C to a double integral over the plane region D bounded by C.
- Green’s theorem can be written as:

$$\oint_C P dx + Q dy = \iint_D \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right) dA$$

- where P and Q are functions of x and y that have continuous first partial derivatives on an open region that contains D.
- Green’s theorem is a special case of the Kelvin–Stokes theorem, when applied to a region in the xy-plane.
- Green’s theorem can be used to evaluate line integrals without parametrizing the curve C, by finding a related double integral over the region D.
- Green’s theorem can also be used to find the area of a plane region D, by choosing P and Q such that their partial derivatives are equal to 1 and -1, respectively.
- Green’s theorem can also be used to find the circulation and curl of a vector field along a curve C, by choosing P and Q to be the components of the vector field.

## Stokes’ theorem

- Stokes’ theorem is a formula that relates a surface integral of the curl of a vector field over a smooth surface S to a line integral of the vector field along the boundary curve C of S.
- Stokes’ theorem can be written as:

$$\iint_S \nabla \times \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$$

- where F is a vector field that has continuous partial derivatives on an open region that contains S.
- Stokes’ theorem is a generalization of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, the Fundamental Theorem for Line Integrals, and Green’s theorem to higher dimensions.
- Stokes’ theorem can be used to evaluate surface integrals without parametrizing the surface S, by finding a related line integral over the curve C.
- Stokes’ theorem can also be used to find the flux and divergence of a vector field across a surface S, by choosing F to be the curl of another vector field.
- Stokes’ theorem can also be used to verify the curl identities and the vector identities involving the curl operator.

## Applications

- Green’s theorem and Stokes’ theorem can be applied to various problems in physics, engineering, and geometry, such as calculating work, circulation, flux, curl, divergence, potential, and area.
- Some examples of applications are:

  - Finding the work done by a force field along a closed curve.
  - Finding the circulation of a fluid around a closed curve.
  - Finding the flux of a magnetic field across a surface.
  - Finding the curl of a vector field at a point on a surface.
  - Finding the divergence of a vector field at a point in space.
  - Finding the electric potential of a charge distribution on a surface.
  - Finding the area of a surface or a plane region .
  - Finding the centroid of a plane region.
  - Finding the moment of inertia of a plane region.
