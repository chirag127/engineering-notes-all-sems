# Volume integral

- A volume integral is a type of multiple integral that extends the concept of area integral to three-dimensional regions.
- A volume integral can be used to find the volume of a solid, the mass of a solid with varying density, the electric charge of a region with varying charge density, and other physical quantities that involve integration over a volume.
- A volume integral can be expressed in Cartesian, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates, depending on the shape and symmetry of the region of integration.
- A volume integral can be evaluated by using the following formula:

$$\iiint_V f(x,y,z) \, dV = \iiint_V f(x,y,z) \, dx \, dy \, dz$$

where $f(x,y,z)$ is the integrand function, $V$ is the region of integration, and $dV$ is the differential volume element.

- A volume integral can also be computed by using the divergence theorem or the Stokes' theorem, which relate the volume integral of a vector field to the surface integral of its divergence or curl, respectively. These theorems are useful for simplifying the calculation of volume integrals in some cases.
