### Change of variables

- Change of variables is a technique for transforming a function of one or more variables into a function of different variables, usually to simplify the calculation or the interpretation of the function.
- Change of variables can be applied to different types of functions, such as algebraic, trigonometric, exponential, logarithmic, etc.
- Change of variables can also be applied to different types of operations, such as differentiation, integration, limits, etc.
- Change of variables can be done in different ways, depending on the context and the goal of the transformation. Some common methods are:

  - Substitution: replacing one variable with another expression involving a new variable, such as $x = u^2$ or $y = \sin v$.
  - Coordinate transformation: changing the coordinate system of the function, such as from Cartesian to polar, cylindrical, spherical, etc.
  - Function composition: applying a function to the original function, such as $f(x) = g(h(x))$ or $f(x,y) = g(h(x,y))$.

- Change of variables can have different effects on the function, such as:

  - Changing the domain and range of the function, such as from $[0, \infty)$ to $(-\infty, \infty)$ or from $[0, 2\pi)$ to $[0, 1)$.
  - Changing the shape and orientation of the graph of the function, such as from a parabola to a hyperbola or from a circle to an ellipse.
  - Changing the rate of change and the extrema of the function, such as from increasing to decreasing or from concave up to concave down.
  - Changing the integrability and the value of the integral of the function, such as from non-integrable to integrable or from infinite to finite.

- Change of variables can be useful for:

  - Simplifying the function or the operation, such as by reducing the number of variables, eliminating radicals or trigonometric functions, or making the function linear or separable.
  - Solving equations or inequalities involving the function, such as by isolating the variable, factoring the expression, or applying the inverse function.
  - Finding the properties or the behavior of the function, such as by finding the domain, range, zeros, asymptotes, extrema, or periodicity of the function.
  - Evaluating the function or the operation, such as by finding the value, the limit, the derivative, or the integral of the function.
