### Inverse of a matrix

- A square matrix A is said to have an inverse if there exists another square matrix B such that AB = BA = I, where I is the identity matrix of the same order as A and B  .
- The matrix B is called the inverse of A and is denoted by A^-1. Thus, AA^-1 = A^-1A = I .
- A matrix that has an inverse is called invertible or nonsingular, and a matrix that does not have an inverse is called noninvertible or singular .
- A square matrix is singular if and only if its determinant is zero .
- The inverse of a matrix can be found by various methods, such as the adjoint method, the Gauss-Jordan method, or the row reduction method .
- Some properties of inverse matrices are:
  - The inverse of inverse matrix is equal to the original matrix. That is, (A^-1)^-1 = A.
  - If A and B are invertible matrices, then AB is also invertible. Thus, (AB)^-1 = B^-1A^-1.
  - If A is nonsingular then (A^T)^-1 = (A^-1)^T, where A^T is the transpose of A.
  - The product of a matrix and its inverse and vice versa is always equal to the identity matrix. That is, AA^-1 = A^-1A = I.
