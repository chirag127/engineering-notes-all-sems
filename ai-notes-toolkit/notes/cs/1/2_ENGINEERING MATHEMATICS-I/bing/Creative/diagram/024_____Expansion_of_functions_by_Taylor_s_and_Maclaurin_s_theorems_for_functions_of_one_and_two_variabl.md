### Expansion of functions by Taylor’s and Maclaurin’s theorems for functions of one and two variables

- A Taylor series is a series expansion of a function about a point.
- A Maclaurin series is a special case of a Taylor series where the point is zero.
- The general formula for the Taylor series of a function of one variable is:

$$
f(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + \frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2 + \frac{f'''(a)}{3!}(x-a)^3 + \cdots
$$

- The general formula for the Taylor series of a function of several variables is:

$$
T(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_m) = f(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_m) + \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{\partial f(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_m)}{\partial x_j}(x_j - a_j) + \frac{1}{2!} \sum_{j=1}^m \sum_{k=1}^m \frac{\partial^2 f(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_m)}{\partial x_j \partial x_k}(x_j - a_j)(x_k - a_k) + \cdots
$$

- The Taylor series for a function will converge to that function if the function is infinitely differentiable and the remainder term goes to zero as the degree of the polynomial increases.
- The Taylor series can be used to approximate functions and calculate their values, derivatives, and integrals.
- The Taylor series can also be used to model physical phenomena, such as harmonic motion, sound waves, and heat transfer.
