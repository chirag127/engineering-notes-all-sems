### Characteristic equation

- The characteristic equation of a square matrix A is the equation that is solved to find the eigenvalues of A, also called the roots of the characteristic polynomial.
- The characteristic polynomial of A is a polynomial function of a variable x, denoted by p(x) or p_A(x), that is obtained by subtracting x times the identity matrix I from A and taking the determinant  .
- Mathematically, the characteristic equation and the characteristic polynomial are related as follows:

  p(x) = det(A - xI) = 0

  where det denotes the determinant, and I is the identity matrix of the same size as A   .

- The characteristic polynomial is a monic polynomial of degree n, where n is the size of the matrix A. It has n roots, which may be real or complex, distinct or repeated .
- The characteristic equation and the characteristic polynomial are invariant under matrix similarity, meaning that if A and B are similar matrices, then they have the same characteristic equation and the same characteristic polynomial  .
- The characteristic equation and the characteristic polynomial can be used to find various properties of a matrix, such as its trace, determinant, rank, inverse, and diagonalization  .
