### Principle of operation of alternator and synchronous motor

#### Alternator
- An alternator is an electrical machine that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy in the form of alternating current (AC).
- The basic principle of operation of an alternator is electromagnetic induction, which states that an electromotive force (EMF) is induced in a conductor when it is subjected to a changing magnetic field.
- In an alternator, the mechanical energy is used to rotate a magnetic field (rotor) inside a set of stationary windings (stator), which induces an alternating EMF in the stator windings.
- The frequency of the generated AC voltage is determined by the speed of rotation of the rotor and the number of magnetic poles on the rotor.

#### Synchronous Motor
- A synchronous motor is an AC motor that operates at a constant speed, determined by the frequency of the AC power supply and the number of magnetic poles on the rotor.
- The basic principle of operation of a synchronous motor is similar to that of an alternator, with the roles of the rotor and stator reversed.
- In a synchronous motor, the stator windings are energized with an AC voltage, which creates a rotating magnetic field. The rotor, which is magnetized, is attracted to the rotating magnetic field and starts to rotate at the same speed as the magnetic field.
- The rotor of a synchronous motor can be magnetized either by a direct current (DC) supplied through slip rings or by permanent magnets.
