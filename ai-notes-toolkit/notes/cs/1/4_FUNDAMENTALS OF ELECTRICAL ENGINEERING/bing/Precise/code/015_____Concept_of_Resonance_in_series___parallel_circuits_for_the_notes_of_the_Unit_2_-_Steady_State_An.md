### Concept of Resonance in Series & Parallel Circuits

Resonance is a phenomenon that occurs in both series and parallel AC circuits. It is characterized by a large increase in current or voltage at a particular frequency, known as the resonant frequency.

#### Series Resonance

In a series resonant circuit, the resonant frequency is determined by the values of the inductance (L) and capacitance (C) in the circuit. At the resonant frequency, the inductive reactance (XL) and capacitive reactance (XC) are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign, resulting in a net reactance of zero. This means that the circuit behaves as a purely resistive circuit at the resonant frequency, allowing maximum current to flow.

The resonant frequency of a series resonant circuit can be calculated using the formula:

f = 1 / (2π * √(LC))

where f is the resonant frequency, L is the inductance, and C is the capacitance.

#### Parallel Resonance

In a parallel resonant circuit, the resonant frequency is also determined by the values of the inductance and capacitance in the circuit. At the resonant frequency, the inductive susceptance (BL) and capacitive susceptance (BC) are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign, resulting in a net susceptance of zero. This means that the circuit behaves as an open circuit at the resonant frequency, allowing minimum current to flow.

The resonant frequency of a parallel resonant circuit can be calculated using the same formula as for a series resonant circuit:

f = 1 / (2π * √(LC))

where f is the resonant frequency, L is the inductance, and C is the capacitance.

#### Applications of Resonance

Resonance has many practical applications in electrical engineering. For example, it is used in the design of filters, oscillators, and tuned circuits. In a filter circuit, resonance can be used to selectively pass or block certain frequencies. In an oscillator circuit, resonance can be used to generate a stable frequency. In a tuned circuit, resonance can be used to maximize the transfer of energy between two circuits.
