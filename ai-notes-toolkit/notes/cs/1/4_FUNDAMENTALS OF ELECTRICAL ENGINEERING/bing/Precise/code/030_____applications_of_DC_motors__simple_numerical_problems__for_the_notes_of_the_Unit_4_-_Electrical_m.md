### Unit 4 - Electrical Machines: Applications of DC Motors (Simple Numerical Problems)

DC motors are widely used in various applications due to their versatility and efficiency. Some of the common applications of DC motors include:

1. **Electric vehicles:** DC motors are used in electric vehicles to convert electrical energy into mechanical energy to drive the wheels. The high starting torque and speed control of DC motors make them suitable for this application.

2. **Industrial machinery:** DC motors are used in various industrial machinery such as lathes, drills, and milling machines. The speed control and high starting torque of DC motors make them suitable for these applications.

3. **Household appliances:** DC motors are used in various household appliances such as vacuum cleaners, washing machines, and fans. The efficiency and speed control of DC motors make them suitable for these applications.

4. **Robotics:** DC motors are used in robotics to provide motion to the robot. The speed control and high starting torque of DC motors make them suitable for this application.

Simple numerical problems involving DC motors can be solved using the basic equations governing the operation of DC motors. These equations include the torque equation, the speed equation, and the power equation.

Example problem:

A DC motor has an armature resistance of 0.5 ohms and is connected to a 240V supply. The motor draws a current of 50A when running at a speed of 1500 rpm. Calculate the torque developed by the motor.

Solution:

The torque developed by the motor can be calculated using the torque equation: T = k * Ia * phi

Where T is the torque, k is a constant, Ia is the armature current, and phi is the flux.

The value of k can be calculated using the speed equation: N = (V - Ia * Ra) / (k * phi)

Where N is the speed, V is the supply voltage, Ia is the armature current, Ra is the armature resistance, and phi is the flux.

Substituting the given values into the speed equation, we get:

1500 = (240 - 50 * 0.5) / (k * phi)

Solving for k, we get k = 0.24 / phi

Substituting this value of k into the torque equation, we get:

T = (0.24 / phi) * 50 * phi

Solving for T, we get T = 12 Nm

Therefore, the torque developed by the motor is 12 Nm.
