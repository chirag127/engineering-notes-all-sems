### Ideal and Practical Transformer

A transformer is a static device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through electromagnetic induction. It is used to either increase or decrease the voltage of an alternating current (AC) supply.

#### Ideal Transformer

An ideal transformer is an imaginary transformer that has no losses, meaning that the power input is equal to the power output. In an ideal transformer, the following conditions are assumed to be true:

1. The windings have zero resistance.
2. The core has infinite permeability, so all the magnetic flux is confined to the core.
3. There are no leakage fluxes, meaning that all the magnetic flux links both the primary and secondary windings.
4. There are no iron losses (hysteresis and eddy current losses) in the core.
5. There is no magnetizing current, meaning that the magnetizing branch of the equivalent circuit can be neglected.

#### Practical Transformer

In reality, no transformer is ideal. A practical transformer has losses, meaning that the power input is greater than the power output. In a practical transformer, the following conditions are true:

1. The windings have some resistance, which causes copper losses (I^2R losses).
2. The core does not have infinite permeability, so some of the magnetic flux leaks out of the core.
3. There are leakage fluxes, meaning that not all the magnetic flux links both the primary and secondary windings.
4. There are iron losses (hysteresis and eddy current losses) in the core.
5. There is a magnetizing current, meaning that the magnetizing branch of the equivalent circuit cannot be neglected.

In summary, an ideal transformer is an imaginary transformer that has no losses, while a practical transformer has losses. The losses in a practical transformer include copper losses, leakage fluxes, iron losses, and magnetizing current. These losses reduce the efficiency of the transformer and must be taken into account when designing and operating a transformer.
