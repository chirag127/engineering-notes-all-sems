### Power Factor

Power factor is a measure of how effectively electrical power is being used. It is defined as the ratio of real power to apparent power. In an AC circuit, power factor is the cosine of the phase angle between the current and voltage waveforms. A power factor of 1 indicates that the current and voltage are in phase and all the power is being used effectively. A power factor less than 1 indicates that some of the power is being wasted.

In a purely resistive circuit, the phase angle is 0, and the power factor is unity (cos φ= 1), which means that the current is in phase with the input (supply) voltage. Power factor is an important concept in the steady-state analysis of single-phase AC circuits. It is used to calculate the apparent, active, and reactive powers in a circuit. The concept of power factor improvement is also important, as it can help to reduce power losses and improve the efficiency of the circuit.
