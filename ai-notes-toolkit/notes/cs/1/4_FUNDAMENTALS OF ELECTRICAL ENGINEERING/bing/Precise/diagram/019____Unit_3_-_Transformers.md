## Unit 3 - Transformers

Transformers are electrical devices that transfer electrical energy between two or more circuits through electromagnetic induction. A varying current in any one coil of the transformer produces a varying magnetic flux, which, in turn, induces a varying electromotive force across any other coils wound around the same core. Electrical energy can be transferred between the two coils, without a metallic connection between the two circuits.

- **Principle of operation:** Transformers operate on the principle of electromagnetic induction, which states that a changing magnetic field induces an electromotive force in a conductor.
- **Construction:** A transformer consists of two or more coils of insulated wire wound around a common iron core. The coil that is connected to the source of electrical energy is called the primary coil, and the coil that is connected to the load is called the secondary coil.
- **Types of transformers:** There are several types of transformers, including step-up transformers, step-down transformers, isolation transformers, and autotransformers.
- **Applications:** Transformers are used in a wide range of applications, including power generation, transmission, and distribution, as well as in electronic devices such as radios, televisions, and computers.
