# Applications (Numerical problems related to slip only)

- Slip is the difference between the synchronous speed and the actual speed of an induction motor, expressed as a fraction or percentage of the synchronous speed.
- Slip can be calculated by the formula: `s = (Ns - N)/Ns`, where `s` is the slip, `Ns` is the synchronous speed, and `N` is the actual speed of the motor.
- Slip is an important parameter that affects the performance and efficiency of an induction motor. It determines the torque, power, current, and power factor of the motor.
- Here are some numerical problems related to slip only:

## Problem 1

- A three-phase induction motor has a synchronous speed of 1200 rpm and runs at 1140 rpm. Calculate the slip of the motor.

### Solution

- The slip of the motor can be calculated by the formula: `s = (Ns - N)/Ns`
- Substituting the given values, we get: `s = (1200 - 1140)/1200`
- Simplifying, we get: `s = 0.05`
- Therefore, the slip of the motor is 0.05 or 5%.

## Problem 2

- A four-pole, 50 Hz induction motor has a slip of 4%. Calculate the actual speed of the motor.

### Solution

- The actual speed of the motor can be calculated by rearranging the formula: `N = Ns - sNs`
- The synchronous speed of the motor can be calculated by the formula: `Ns = 120f/p`, where `f` is the frequency and `p` is the number of poles.
- Substituting the given values, we get: `Ns = 120*50/4`
- Simplifying, we get: `Ns = 1500 rpm`
- Substituting the value of `Ns` and `s`, we get: `N = 1500 - 0.04*1500`
- Simplifying, we get: `N = 1440 rpm`
- Therefore, the actual speed of the motor is 1440 rpm.
