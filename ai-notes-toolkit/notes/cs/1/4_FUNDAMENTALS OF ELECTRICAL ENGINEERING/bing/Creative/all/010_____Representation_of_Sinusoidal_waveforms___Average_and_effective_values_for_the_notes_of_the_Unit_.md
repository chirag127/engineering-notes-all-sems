# Representation of Sinusoidal waveforms – Average and effective values

- A sinusoidal waveform is a periodic function that oscillates between positive and negative peak values with a constant frequency.
- The peak value (Vp) of a sinusoidal waveform is the maximum or minimum amplitude of the waveform.
- The average value (Vav) of a sinusoidal waveform is the arithmetic mean of the waveform over one complete cycle.
- The average value of a sinusoidal waveform is zero, as the positive and negative areas cancel each other out.
- The effective value (Vrms) of a sinusoidal waveform is the equivalent DC value that would produce the same heating effect in a resistor as the AC waveform.
- The effective value of a sinusoidal waveform is also known as the root mean square (RMS) value, as it is calculated by taking the square root of the mean of the squares of the instantaneous values over one cycle.
- The RMS value of a sinusoidal waveform is equal to the peak value multiplied by 0.707, or divided by 1.414.
- The form factor of a sinusoidal waveform is the ratio of the RMS value to the average value. For a sinusoidal waveform, the form factor is 1.11.
- The peak factor of a sinusoidal waveform is the ratio of the peak value to the RMS value. For a sinusoidal waveform, the peak factor is 1.414.
- A phasor is a vector representation of a sinusoidal waveform, with the length of the vector equal to the peak value and the angle of the vector equal to the phase angle.
- A resistor is a passive component that opposes the flow of current and dissipates energy as heat.
- A resistor in an AC circuit behaves the same as in a DC circuit, as the resistance is independent of the frequency and phase of the voltage and current.
- The voltage and current across a resistor are in phase, meaning they have the same frequency and phase angle.
- The power dissipated by a resistor in an AC circuit is equal to the product of the RMS voltage and current, or the square of the RMS current multiplied by the resistance.
