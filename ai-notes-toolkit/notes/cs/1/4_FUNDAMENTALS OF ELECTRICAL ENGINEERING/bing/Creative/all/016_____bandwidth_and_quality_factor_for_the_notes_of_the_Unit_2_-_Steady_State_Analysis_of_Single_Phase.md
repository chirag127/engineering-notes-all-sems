# Bandwidth and Quality Factor for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the Subject of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering

- Bandwidth of a resonant circuit is the range of frequencies over which the current or voltage amplitude is equal to or greater than 70.7% of its maximum value at the resonant frequency  .
- Quality factor of a resonant circuit is a dimensionless quantity that measures how underdamped the circuit is, or equivalently, how narrow the bandwidth of the circuit is    .
- For a series resonant circuit, the quality factor is given by Q = X_L / R, where X_L is the inductive reactance, and R is the resistance .
- For a parallel resonant circuit, the quality factor is given by Q = R / X_L, where R is the resistance, and X_L is the inductive reactance.
- The resonant frequency of a circuit is the frequency at which the inductive reactance and the capacitive reactance are equal in magnitude, and the circuit behaves as a pure resistor   .
- The resonant frequency of a circuit is given by f_r = 1 / (2π√(LC)), where L is the inductance, and C is the capacitance   .
- The bandwidth of a resonant circuit is related to the quality factor and the resonant frequency by the formula BW = f_r / Q, where BW is the bandwidth, f_r is the resonant frequency, and Q is the quality factor    .
- The average power dissipated by a series resonant circuit is given by P = V_rms^2 / R, where V_rms is the root mean square voltage, and R is the resistance .
- The average power dissipated by a parallel resonant circuit is given by P = I_rms^2 R, where I_rms is the root mean square current, and R is the resistance.
- A high quality factor indicates a low rate of energy loss relative to the stored energy of the circuit, and a narrow bandwidth    .
- A low quality factor indicates a high rate of energy loss relative to the stored energy of the circuit, and a wide bandwidth    .
- Resonant circuits are used for tuning, filtering, and amplifying signals in various applications, such as radio, television, and telecommunications    .
