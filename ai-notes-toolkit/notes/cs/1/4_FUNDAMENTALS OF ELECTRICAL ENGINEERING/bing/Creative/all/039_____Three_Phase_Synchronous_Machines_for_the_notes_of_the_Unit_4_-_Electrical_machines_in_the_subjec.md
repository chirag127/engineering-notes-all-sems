# Three Phase Synchronous Machines

- A three phase synchronous machine is a type of electric machine that can operate as either a generator or a motor.
- A synchronous generator converts mechanical energy into three phase electrical energy, while a synchronous motor converts three phase electrical energy into mechanical energy.
- A synchronous machine consists of two main parts: a stator and a rotor.
- The stator is the stationary part of the machine that contains a three phase winding, which is connected to the external AC supply or load.
- The rotor is the rotating part of the machine that contains a direct current (DC) winding, which is excited by a DC source or an exciter.
- The DC winding on the rotor produces a magnetic field that rotates at the same speed as the rotor, called the synchronous speed.
- The synchronous speed is determined by the frequency of the AC supply and the number of poles on the rotor, according to the formula: Ns = 120f/P, where Ns is the synchronous speed in revolutions per minute (rpm), f is the frequency in hertz (Hz), and P is the number of poles.
- The rotor can be either cylindrical or salient-pole, depending on the shape and distribution of the magnetic poles.
- A cylindrical rotor has a smooth surface and uniform air gap, and is used for high-speed machines, such as turbo-generators.
- A salient-pole rotor has protruding poles and non-uniform air gap, and is used for low-speed machines, such as hydro-generators and single-phase motors.
- The interaction between the rotating magnetic field of the rotor and the rotating magnetic field of the stator produces a torque that causes the rotor to rotate at the synchronous speed, regardless of the load or input power.
- A synchronous machine is said to be in synchronism when the rotor and the stator fields are rotating at the same speed and direction.
- A synchronous machine can operate at different power factors, depending on the magnitude and direction of the rotor field with respect to the stator field.
- A synchronous machine can operate at unity power factor, leading power factor, or lagging power factor, by adjusting the DC excitation current on the rotor.
- A synchronous machine can also operate as a synchronous condenser, which is a device that can supply or absorb reactive power to or from the power system, by overexciting or underexciting the rotor field.
