# Unit 3 - Transformers

- A transformer is a device that converts alternating current (AC) from one state to another. It can be designed to “step up” or “step down” the voltage and current in the process, which is very useful for various applications  .
- A transformer works on the principle of magnetic induction, which means that a changing current in one coil of wire produces a changing magnetic field in the transformer's core, which induces a changing voltage across any other coils wound around the same core .
- A transformer consists of two or more coils of wire, called the primary and secondary windings, which are wrapped around a common core, usually made of iron or steel. The primary winding is connected to the AC source, and the secondary winding is connected to the load. The ratio of the number of turns in the primary and secondary windings determines the voltage and current transformation.
- A transformer can be classified into different types based on its construction, function, and application. Some common types of transformers are:
  - Step-up transformer: A transformer that increases the voltage and decreases the current in the secondary winding compared to the primary winding. It is used to transmit electrical power over long distances with less power loss .
  - Step-down transformer: A transformer that decreases the voltage and increases the current in the secondary winding compared to the primary winding. It is used to operate low-voltage devices, such as doorbells and toy electric trains .
  - Isolation transformer: A transformer that has the same number of turns in the primary and secondary windings, and thus does not change the voltage or current. It is used to isolate the load from the source for safety or noise reduction purposes.
  - Auto-transformer: A transformer that has only one winding, which acts as both the primary and secondary winding. It has a tap that can be adjusted to vary the output voltage. It is used to regulate the voltage or to provide a small voltage boost or reduction.
  - Current transformer: A transformer that has a large number of turns in the secondary winding and a small number of turns in the primary winding, and thus produces a low voltage and high current in the secondary winding. It is used to measure the current in a circuit or to provide protection against overcurrent.
  - Potential transformer: A transformer that has a small number of turns in the secondary winding and a large number of turns in the primary winding, and thus produces a high voltage and low current in the secondary winding. It is used to measure the voltage in a circuit or to provide insulation against high voltage.
