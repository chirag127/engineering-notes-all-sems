# Types of Batteries

A battery is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy. It consists of one or more cells, each containing a positive and a negative electrode, and an electrolyte that allows the flow of electric charge between the electrodes.

There are four main types of batteries:

- **Primary batteries**: These are non-rechargeable batteries that can only be used once. They have a limited shelf life and capacity. Examples of primary batteries are alkaline, zinc-carbon, and lithium batteries.
- **Secondary batteries**: These are rechargeable batteries that can be used multiple times by restoring their chemical energy through an external power source. They have a longer shelf life and capacity, but they also suffer from self-discharge and memory effect. Examples of secondary batteries are lead-acid, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, and lithium-ion batteries.
- **Reserve batteries**: These are batteries that are activated only when needed, such as in emergency situations. They have a long storage life and high power density, but they are also expensive and hazardous. Examples of reserve batteries are thermal batteries, magnesium batteries, and silver-zinc batteries.
- **Fuel cells**: These are batteries that generate electricity by continuously converting the chemical energy of a fuel and an oxidant. They have a high efficiency and environmental friendliness, but they also require a constant supply of fuel and oxygen, and a complex system to manage them. Examples of fuel cells are hydrogen-oxygen, methanol, and ethanol fuel cells.
