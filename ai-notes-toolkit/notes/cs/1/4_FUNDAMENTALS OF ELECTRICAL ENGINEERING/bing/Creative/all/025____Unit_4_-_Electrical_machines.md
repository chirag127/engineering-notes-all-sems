# Unit 4 - Electrical machines

- Electrical machines are devices that use electromagnetic forces to convert electrical energy to mechanical energy or vice versa.
- Electrical machines can be classified into three main types: transformers, generators, and motors.
- Transformers are electrical machines that transfer electrical power from one circuit to another without changing the frequency, but with a different voltage and current.
- Generators are electrical machines that convert mechanical power to electrical power by inducing an electromotive force in a coil of wire that rotates in a magnetic field.
- Motors are electrical machines that convert electrical power to mechanical power by creating a torque on a shaft that rotates in a magnetic field.
- Electrical machines can also be classified based on the type of current they use: direct current (DC) or alternating current (AC).
- DC machines are electrical machines that operate on DC power sources, such as batteries or rectifiers. DC machines can be further divided into DC motors and DC generators.
- AC machines are electrical machines that operate on AC power sources, such as the grid or inverters. AC machines can be further divided into synchronous machines and induction machines.
- Synchronous machines are AC machines that operate at a constant speed that is proportional to the frequency of the AC power source. Synchronous machines can be either synchronous motors or synchronous generators.
- Induction machines are AC machines that operate at a variable speed that depends on the load and the slip between the rotor and the stator. Induction machines can be either induction motors or induction generators.
- Electrical machines are important for many applications, such as power generation, transmission, distribution, conversion, and utilization. Electrical machines are also used in various industries, such as transportation, manufacturing, mining, and agriculture.
- Electrical machines are subject to various losses, such as copper losses, iron losses, friction losses, and windage losses. These losses reduce the efficiency and performance of electrical machines.
- Electrical machines are designed and analyzed using various principles and methods, such as circuit theory, magnetic circuits, electromechanical energy conversion, equivalent circuits, phasor diagrams, power factor, torque-speed characteristics, and per-unit system. These principles and methods help to understand the behavior and characteristics of electrical machines.
