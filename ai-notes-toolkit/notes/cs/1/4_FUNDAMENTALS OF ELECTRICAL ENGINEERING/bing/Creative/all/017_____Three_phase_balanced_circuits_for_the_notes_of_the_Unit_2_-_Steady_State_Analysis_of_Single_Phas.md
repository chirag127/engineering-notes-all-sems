# Three phase balanced circuits

- A three phase balanced circuit is a type of alternating current (AC) power system that consists of three voltage sources and three loads connected in either star (Y) or delta (Δ) configuration.
- The three voltage sources have equal magnitudes and frequencies, but are phase shifted by 120° with respect to each other. The three loads have equal impedances and power factors, and are connected in the same configuration as the sources.
- A three phase balanced circuit has several advantages over a single phase or a two phase circuit, such as higher power transfer, lower line currents, higher efficiency, and simpler analysis.
- The analysis of a three phase balanced circuit can be simplified by using the following steps:
  - Choose a reference phase, usually phase a, and assign the positive direction of the current and voltage in that phase.
  - Apply Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL) and Kirchhoff's current law (KCL) to the reference phase and obtain the voltage and current expressions for that phase.
  - Use the phase shift property of the three phase system to obtain the voltage and current expressions for the other two phases, phase b and phase c, by shifting the reference phase expressions by 120° and -120° respectively.
  - Calculate the line and phase voltages and currents by using the star-delta transformation and the phasor diagram.
  - Calculate the power and power factor of each phase and the total power of the system by using the complex power formula and the power triangle.
