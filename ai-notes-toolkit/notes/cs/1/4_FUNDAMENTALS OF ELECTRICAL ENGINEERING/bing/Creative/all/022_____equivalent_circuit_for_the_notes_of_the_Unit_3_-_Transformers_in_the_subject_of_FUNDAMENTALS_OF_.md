# Equivalent Circuit of a Transformer

- An equivalent circuit of a transformer is a graphical representation of a transformer circuit in which the resistance and leakage reactance are imagined to be external to the winding .
- The equivalent circuit of a transformer shows all the electrical parameters such as winding resistance, reactance, admittance, susceptance, primary and secondary voltages, currents, etc.
- The equivalent circuit of a transformer can be referred to as the primary or secondary side, depending on which side is considered as the input and which side is considered as the output .
- The equivalent circuit of a transformer can be derived from the open-circuit and short-circuit tests of a practical transformer, which measure the no-load and full-load losses respectively .
- The equivalent circuit of a transformer can be simplified by using the transformation ratio K = E2/E1, where E1 and E2 are the induced emfs in the primary and secondary windings respectively.
- The equivalent circuit of a transformer can be used to analyze the performance and efficiency of a transformer under various load conditions.

## No-Load Equivalent Circuit of a Transformer

- The no-load equivalent circuit of a transformer is shown in the figure below:

![No-Load Equivalent Circuit of a Transformer](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/equivalent-circuit-of-a-transformer-explained-with-examples/images/no_load_equivalent_circuit.jpg)

- The no-load equivalent circuit of a transformer consists of the primary applied voltage V1, the induced emf E1, the no-load current I0, the core loss resistance Rc, and the magnetizing reactance Xm.
- The no-load current I0 is the current that flows in the primary winding when the secondary winding is open-circuited. It is very small (about 2-5% of the full-load current) and has a lagging power factor (about 0.2-0.3) due to the inductive nature of the transformer.
- The no-load current I0 can be resolved into two components: the active component Iw and the reactive component Im. The active component Iw represents the core loss (hysteresis and eddy current losses) in the transformer, and the reactive component Im represents the magnetizing current that establishes the flux in the core.
- The core loss resistance Rc is the equivalent resistance that accounts for the core loss in the transformer. It is given by Rc = E1/Iw, where E1 is the induced emf in the primary winding and Iw is the active component of the no-load current.
- The magnetizing reactance Xm is the equivalent reactance that accounts for the magnetizing current in the transformer. It is given by Xm = E1/Im, where E1 is the induced emf in the primary winding and Im is the reactive component of the no-load current.

## Exact Equivalent Circuit of a Transformer

- The exact equivalent circuit of a transformer is shown in the figure below:

![Exact Equivalent Circuit of a Transformer](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/equivalent-circuit-of-a-transformer-explained-with-examples/images/exact_equivalent_circuit.jpg)

- The exact equivalent circuit of a transformer consists of the primary applied voltage V1, the induced emf E1, the no-load current I0, the core loss resistance Rc, the magnetizing reactance Xm, the primary winding resistance R1, the primary leakage reactance X1, the secondary winding resistance R2, the secondary leakage reactance X2, the secondary induced emf E2, and the secondary load current I2.
- The primary winding resistance R1 and the primary leakage reactance X1 are the resistance and reactance of the primary winding, which cause a voltage drop in the primary circuit. They are measured by the short-circuit test of the transformer, in which the secondary winding is short-circuited and a reduced voltage is applied to the primary winding to produce the full-load current.
- The secondary winding resistance R2 and the secondary leakage reactance X2 are the resistance and reactance of the secondary winding, which cause a voltage drop in the secondary circuit. They are also measured by the short-circuit test of the transformer, in which the secondary winding is short-circuited and a reduced voltage is applied to the primary winding to produce the full-load current
