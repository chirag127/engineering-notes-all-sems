# ACB

- ACB stands for Air Circuit Breaker, which is an electrical device used to provide overcurrent and short-circuit protection for electric circuits over 800 Amps to 10kA   .
- ACB operates at atmospheric pressure in air, where air-blast as an arc quenching medium .
- ACB is used in the switching mechanism and protection of the electrical system, especially in low voltage applications below 450V .
- ACB has three main components: the contacts, the arc chute, and the operating mechanism .
- The contacts are the conductive parts that allow the current to flow through the circuit breaker. They are made of copper or silver alloy and are mounted on a movable and a fixed contact carrier .
- The arc chute is the device that extinguishes the arc that forms when the contacts separate. It consists of a series of metal plates that create a low resistance path for the arc and split it into smaller arcs that are easier to quench .
- The operating mechanism is the system that controls the opening and closing of the contacts. It can be manual, spring-loaded, or electromagnetic .
- ACB can be classified into two types: plain air circuit breaker and air blast circuit breaker .
- Plain air circuit breaker is the simplest type of ACB, where the arc is quenched by the natural cooling of air. It has a low breaking capacity and a long arcing time .
- Air blast circuit breaker is the type of ACB where the arc is quenched by a blast of compressed air. It has a high breaking capacity and a short arcing time .
- ACB has several advantages, such as high speed of operation, low maintenance, high reliability, and easy replacement of parts  .
- ACB also has some disadvantages, such as high cost, high noise level, high power consumption, and sensitivity to environmental conditions  .
- ACB is widely used in industrial and commercial applications, such as distribution panels, power plants, substations, and switchboards  .
