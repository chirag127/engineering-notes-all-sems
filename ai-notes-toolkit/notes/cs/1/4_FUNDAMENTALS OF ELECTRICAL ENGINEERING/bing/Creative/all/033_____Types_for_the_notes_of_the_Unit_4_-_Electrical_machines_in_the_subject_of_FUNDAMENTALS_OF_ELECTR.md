# Types of Electrical Machines

Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. They can be classified into two main categories: static and rotating machines  .

## Static Machines

Static machines are stationary devices that do not have any moving parts. They transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another without changing the frequency. The most common example of a static machine is a transformer  .

### Transformers

Transformers are devices that can step up or step down the voltage and current of an alternating current (AC) supply. They consist of two or more coils of wire that are wound around a common magnetic core. When an AC voltage is applied to one coil, called the primary, it induces an AC voltage in the other coil, called the secondary, according to the ratio of the number of turns in each coil. Transformers are used for various purposes, such as power transmission, distribution, isolation, impedance matching, and voltage regulation  .

## Rotating Machines

Rotating machines are devices that have a rotating part, called the rotor, that interacts with a stationary part, called the stator, to produce or consume mechanical power. They can be classified into three main types: direct current (DC) machines, synchronous machines, and induction machines .

### DC Machines

DC machines are machines that operate on direct current (DC) supply. They can be further classified into two types: DC generators and DC motors .

#### DC Generators

DC generators are machines that convert mechanical energy into DC electrical energy. They consist of a coil of wire that rotates in a magnetic field, which induces a DC voltage across the coil terminals. The direction of the induced voltage depends on the direction of rotation and the polarity of the magnetic field. DC generators are used for applications that require a constant or variable DC supply, such as batteries, welding, and electroplating .

#### DC Motors

DC motors are machines that convert DC electrical energy into mechanical energy. They consist of a coil of wire that is placed in a magnetic field, which exerts a torque on the coil, causing it to rotate. The direction of rotation depends on the direction of the current and the polarity of the magnetic field. DC motors are used for applications that require a variable speed and torque, such as electric vehicles, cranes, and robots .

### Synchronous Machines

Synchronous machines are machines that operate on alternating current (AC) supply and rotate at a constant speed that is proportional to the frequency of the supply. They can be further classified into two types: synchronous generators and synchronous motors .

#### Synchronous Generators

Synchronous generators are machines that convert mechanical energy into AC electrical energy. They consist of a rotor that has permanent magnets or electromagnets that create a magnetic field, and a stator that has coils of wire that are connected to the AC supply. When the rotor rotates, it induces an AC voltage in the stator coils, which is in phase with the supply voltage. Synchronous generators are used for applications that require a constant frequency and voltage, such as power plants, grid synchronization, and frequency regulation .

#### Synchronous Motors

Synchronous motors are machines that convert AC electrical energy into mechanical energy. They consist of a rotor that has permanent magnets or electromagnets that create a magnetic field, and a stator that has coils of wire that are connected to the AC supply. When the stator coils are energized, they create a rotating magnetic field, which attracts the rotor and makes it rotate at the same speed as the stator field. Synchronous motors are used for applications that require a constant speed and power factor, such as fans, pumps, and compressors .
