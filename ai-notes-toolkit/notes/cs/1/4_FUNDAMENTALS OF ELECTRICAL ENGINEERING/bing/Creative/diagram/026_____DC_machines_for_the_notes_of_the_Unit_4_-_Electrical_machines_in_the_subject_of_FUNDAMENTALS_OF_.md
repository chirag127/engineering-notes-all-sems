### DC machines

- A DC machine is an electromechanical device that is used to convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa.
- The working principle of a DC machine is based on an effect when a current carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, and then the magnetic force generates a torque that rotates the DC machine .
- The main components of a DC machine are:
  - Yoke: The outer frame that supports and protects the machine. It is usually made of cast iron or steel. It also provides a low reluctance path for the magnetic flux.
  - Poles and pole shoes: The projections on the inner side of the yoke that carry the field windings. The pole shoes are the enlarged ends of the poles that spread the flux over a larger area of the armature.
  - Field windings: The coils of wire that are wound on the poles and produce the magnetic field when energized by a DC source. The field windings can be connected in different ways to achieve different types of DC machines .
  - Armature core: The cylindrical rotor that rotates inside the magnetic field. It is made of laminated iron sheets to reduce eddy current losses. It has slots on its surface that carry the armature windings.
  - Armature windings: The coils of wire that are connected to the commutator segments and carry the induced current in the armature. The armature windings can be either lap wound or wave wound depending on the number of poles and parallel paths.
  - Commutator: The mechanical device that converts the alternating current in the armature into direct current at the terminals. It consists of copper segments that are insulated from each other and connected to the armature windings.
  - Brushes: The sliding contacts that press against the commutator and collect or supply the current to the external circuit. They are usually made of carbon or graphite.
  - Bearings: The devices that support the armature shaft and reduce friction and wear.
- The types of DC machines are:
  - Separately excited DC machine: A DC machine in which the field winding is supplied by a separate DC source.
  - Shunt-wound DC machine: A DC machine in which the field winding is connected in parallel with the armature winding.
  - Series-wound DC machine: A DC machine in which the field winding is connected in series with the armature winding.
  - Compound-wound DC machine: A DC machine in which the field winding is composed of two parts, one connected in series and one connected in parallel with the armature winding.
- The applications of DC machines are:
  - DC generators: DC machines that convert mechanical energy into electrical energy. They are used for supplying power to DC loads, such as batteries, electroplating, welding, etc.
  - DC motors: DC machines that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy. They are used for driving various machines, such as fans, pumps, cranes, electric vehicles, etc.
