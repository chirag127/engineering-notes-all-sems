### Fundamentals of earthing and lightning protection

- Earthing and lightning protection are important for everyone that uses electrical equipment and that includes the large majority of the world.
- Earthing, also known as grounding, is the process of connecting the electrical system or equipment to the earth or a conductive body that serves as earth.
- Earthing provides a low-resistance path for fault currents and leakage currents to flow to the ground, thus preventing electric shock, fire, and damage to the equipment.
- Earthing also helps to stabilize the voltage level of the system and reduce electromagnetic interference.
- Lightning protection is the process of intercepting or diverting lightning and providing a certain path for conducting the surges safely to the ground by adequate down conductors to grounding electrodes.
- Lightning protection helps prevent disastrous events like fires, injuries, and deaths caused by direct or indirect lightning strikes.
- Lightning protection also helps to limit dangerous system overvoltages to safe values by using overhead ground wires and surge arresters, connected to ground.
- Surge arresters are devices that protect electrical equipment from overvoltage transients caused by external or internal events, such as lightning, switching, or short circuits.
- Surge arresters act as a switch that conducts the excess voltage to the ground when the voltage exceeds a certain level, and restores to a non-conducting state when the voltage returns to normal.
- Common methods for earthing and lightning protection include:
  - Rod earthing: using one or more metal rods driven into the ground and connected to the electrical system or equipment by conductors.
  - Plate earthing: using one or more metal plates buried in the ground and connected to the electrical system or equipment by conductors.
  - Strip earthing: using one or more metal strips laid horizontally in the ground and connected to the electrical system or equipment by conductors.
  - Mesh earthing: using a network of interconnected conductors laid on or under the ground and connected to the electrical system or equipment by conductors.
  - Ring earthing: using a closed loop of conductors encircling the structure or equipment to be protected and connected to the ground electrodes by vertical conductors.
  - Counterpoise earthing: using one or more conductors laid on or above the ground and connected to the electrical system or equipment by conductors.
  - Franklin rod: using a pointed metal rod mounted on the top of the structure to be protected and connected to the ground electrode by a down conductor.
  - Faraday cage: using a mesh of conductors covering the entire structure or equipment to be protected and connected to the ground electrode by multiple down conductors.
  - Early streamer emission (ESE) device: using a device that emits a streamer of ions towards the approaching lightning and attracts it to the device, which is connected to the ground electrode by a down conductor.
- The basic principles of earthing and lightning protection are:
  - Providing a low-resistance path for the fault currents and lightning currents to flow to the ground.
  - Minimizing the potential difference between the earth and the electrical system or equipment.
  - Avoiding loops or parallel paths in the earthing and lightning protection system.
  - Ensuring adequate separation and bonding between the earthing and lightning protection system and other metallic systems near it.
  - Following the latest industry standards and procedures for earthing and lightning protection design, installation, testing, and maintenance.
