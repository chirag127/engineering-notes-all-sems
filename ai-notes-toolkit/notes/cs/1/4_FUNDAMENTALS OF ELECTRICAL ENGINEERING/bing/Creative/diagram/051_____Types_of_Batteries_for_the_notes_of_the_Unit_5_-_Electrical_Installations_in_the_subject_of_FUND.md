### Types of Batteries

Batteries are devices that store electrical energy in the form of chemical energy and convert it into electrical current when needed. Batteries are widely used in electrical engineering for various applications, such as powering portable devices, electric vehicles, backup systems, etc.

There are many types of batteries, but they can be broadly classified into four categories:

- **Primary batteries**: These are non-rechargeable batteries that can only be used once. They have a fixed amount of chemical energy that is consumed during discharge. Examples of primary batteries are alkaline, zinc-carbon, lithium, etc.
- **Secondary batteries**: These are rechargeable batteries that can be used multiple times. They have a reversible chemical reaction that allows them to regain their chemical energy when connected to an external power source. Examples of secondary batteries are lead-acid, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, lithium-ion, etc.
- **Reserve batteries**: These are batteries that are activated only when needed. They have a separate electrolyte that is added to the battery cell when required. They have a long shelf life and high energy density. Examples of reserve batteries are thermal batteries, magnesium batteries, etc.
- **Fuel cells**: These are batteries that generate electrical energy from the continuous supply of fuel and oxidant. They do not store chemical energy, but convert it directly into electrical energy. They have high efficiency and low emissions. Examples of fuel cells are hydrogen-oxygen, methanol, etc.

Each type of battery has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the application, cost, performance, safety, environmental impact, etc. Some of the factors that are used to compare different types of batteries are   :

- **Voltage**: The potential difference between the terminals of the battery. It determines the amount of electrical energy that can be delivered by the battery. It depends on the chemistry of the battery and the number of cells connected in series.
- **Capacity**: The amount of electrical charge that can be delivered by the battery. It is measured in ampere-hours (Ah) or milliampere-hours (mAh). It depends on the size, chemistry, and discharge rate of the battery.
- **Energy density**: The amount of energy that can be stored by the battery per unit volume or mass. It is measured in watt-hours per liter (Wh/L) or watt-hours per kilogram (Wh/kg). It determines the size and weight of the battery for a given application.
- **Power density**: The amount of power that can be delivered by the battery per unit volume or mass. It is measured in watts per liter (W/L) or watts per kilogram (W/kg). It determines the performance and speed of the battery for a given application.
- **Cycle life**: The number of times that the battery can be charged and discharged before its capacity drops below a certain level. It depends on the chemistry, design, and usage of the battery.
- **Self-discharge**: The loss of charge that occurs in the battery when it is not in use. It is measured in percentage per month or year. It depends on the chemistry, temperature, and age of the battery.
- **Cost**: The price of the battery per unit of energy or power. It depends on the materials, manufacturing, and market factors of the battery.
