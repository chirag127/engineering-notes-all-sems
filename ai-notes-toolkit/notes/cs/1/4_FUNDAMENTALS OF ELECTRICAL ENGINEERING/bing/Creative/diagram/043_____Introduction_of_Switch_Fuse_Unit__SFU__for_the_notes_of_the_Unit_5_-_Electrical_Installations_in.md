### Introduction of Switch Fuse Unit (SFU)

- A switch fuse unit (SFU) is a device that combines the functions of a switch and a fuse in one unit.
- A switch is a device that can make or break an electrical circuit by opening or closing the contacts.
- A fuse is a device that protects an electrical circuit from overcurrent by melting a metal wire or strip when the current exceeds a certain value.
- A switch fuse unit (SFU) can be used to isolate and protect an electrical circuit or a load from overcurrent, short circuit, or overload.
- A switch fuse unit (SFU) consists of three main parts: a switch, a fuse carrier, and a fuse link.
- The switch is the part that can open or close the circuit by moving a handle or a lever.
- The fuse carrier is the part that holds the fuse link and connects it to the switch contacts.
- The fuse link is the part that contains the metal wire or strip that melts when the current exceeds the rated value.
- A switch fuse unit (SFU) can be classified into two types: open type and enclosed type.
- An open type SFU is a type that has the switch, the fuse carrier, and the fuse link exposed to the air and mounted on a base plate or a panel.
- An enclosed type SFU is a type that has the switch, the fuse carrier, and the fuse link enclosed in a metal or plastic box or enclosure.
- An enclosed type SFU provides better protection from dust, moisture, and accidental contact than an open type SFU.
- A switch fuse unit (SFU) can be further classified into different types based on the number of poles, the current rating, the voltage rating, and the breaking capacity.
- The number of poles refers to the number of switch contacts and fuse links in a SFU. A SFU can have one, two, three, or four poles depending on the number of phases and the neutral in the circuit.
- The current rating refers to the maximum current that a SFU can carry without melting the fuse link. A SFU can have different current ratings such as 16 A, 32 A, 63 A, 100 A, etc.
- The voltage rating refers to the maximum voltage that a SFU can withstand without breaking down. A SFU can have different voltage ratings such as 240 V, 415 V, 600 V, etc.
- The breaking capacity refers to the maximum current that a SFU can interrupt without damaging the switch contacts and the fuse link. A SFU can have different breaking capacities such as 10 kA, 20 kA, 50 kA, etc.
