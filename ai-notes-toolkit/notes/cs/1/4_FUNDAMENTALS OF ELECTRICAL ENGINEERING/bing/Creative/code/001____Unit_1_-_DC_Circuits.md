## Unit 1 - DC Circuits

DC circuits are electrical circuits that operate with direct current (DC) sources, such as batteries, solar cells, or DC generators. DC circuits are characterized by constant voltage, current, and power.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- Basic concepts of voltage, current, resistance, and power.
- Ohm's law, which relates voltage, current, and resistance in a simple linear equation.
- Kirchhoff's laws, which are two rules that govern the conservation of charge and energy in a circuit.
- Series and parallel circuits, which are two common ways of connecting circuit elements.
- Voltage and current dividers, which are simple formulas that allow us to find the voltage or current across a part of a circuit.
- Equivalent resistance, which is a way of simplifying a complex circuit into a single resistor.
- Nodal and mesh analysis, which are two systematic methods of solving circuits using Kirchhoff's laws and linear algebra.
- Superposition principle, which is a technique that allows us to find the contribution of each source in a circuit with multiple sources.
- Thevenin's and Norton's theorems, which are two powerful theorems that allow us to replace a part of a circuit with an equivalent source and a resistor.
- Maximum power transfer theorem, which is a theorem that tells us how to optimize the power delivered from a source to a load.
- Delta-wye transformation, which is a way of converting a circuit with a triangular arrangement of resistors into an equivalent circuit with a star-shaped arrangement of resistors, or vice versa.
