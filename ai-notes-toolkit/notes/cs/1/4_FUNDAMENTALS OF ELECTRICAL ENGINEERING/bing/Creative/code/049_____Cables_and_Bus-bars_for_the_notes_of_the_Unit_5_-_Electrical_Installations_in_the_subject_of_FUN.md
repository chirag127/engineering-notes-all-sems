### Cables and Bus-bars

Cables and bus-bars are two methods of transporting electrical energy in commercial and industrial distribution systems. They have different advantages and disadvantages depending on the application and design requirements.

#### Cables

- Cables are insulated conductors that can be run in trays or conduit.
- Cables can be flexible and can follow complex routes.
- Cables can be easily terminated and connected to various equipment and devices.
- Cables can provide protection from electric shock, short circuit, and fire hazards.
- Cables can be affected by environmental factors such as temperature, moisture, corrosion, and electromagnetic interference.
- Cables can have high voltage drop and power loss due to their resistance and reactance.
- Cables can be expensive and difficult to change once installed.

#### Bus-bars

- Bus-bars are metal bars, usually made of copper or aluminum, that carry large amounts of current.
- Bus-bars are used in electrical panels, switchboards, and bus ducts.
- Bus-bars can be compact and can save space compared to cables.
- Bus-bars can have low voltage drop and power loss due to their low resistance and reactance.
- Bus-bars can provide high reliability and efficiency of power distribution.
- Bus-bars can be exposed to physical damage, accidental contact, and arc flash hazards.
- Bus-bars can require special tools and skills for installation and modification.
