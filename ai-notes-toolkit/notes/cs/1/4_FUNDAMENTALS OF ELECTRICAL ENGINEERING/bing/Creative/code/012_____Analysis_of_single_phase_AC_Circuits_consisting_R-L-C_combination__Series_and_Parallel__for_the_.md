### Analysis of single phase AC Circuits consisting R-L-C combination (Series and Parallel)

- A single phase AC circuit is a circuit that is powered by a single alternating voltage or current source.
- A R-L-C circuit is a circuit that contains a resistor (R), an inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) connected in series or parallel.
- The behavior of a R-L-C circuit depends on the frequency of the AC source and the values of the components.
- The impedance (Z) of a R-L-C circuit is the total opposition to the AC current flow. It is a complex quantity that has a magnitude and a phase angle.
- The admittance (Y) of a R-L-C circuit is the reciprocal of the impedance. It is also a complex quantity that has a magnitude and a phase angle.
- The impedance and admittance of a R-L-C circuit can be represented by phasor diagrams or complex numbers.

#### Series R-L-C Circuit

- In a series R-L-C circuit, the components are connected end-to-end in a single loop.
- The voltage across each component is different, but the current through each component is the same.
- The total voltage across the circuit is the phasor sum of the voltages across each component.
- The total impedance of the circuit is the phasor sum of the impedances of each component.
- The total impedance of the circuit can be calculated by the formula:

$$Z = R + j(X_L - X_C)$$

where $R$ is the resistance, $X_L$ is the inductive reactance, $X_C$ is the capacitive reactance, and $j$ is the imaginary unit.

- The total current of the circuit can be calculated by Ohm's law:

$$I = \frac{V}{Z}$$

where $V$ is the source voltage and $Z$ is the total impedance.

- The power factor of the circuit is the cosine of the phase angle between the voltage and the current. It indicates how efficiently the circuit converts the AC power to the useful power.
- The power factor of the circuit can be calculated by the formula:

$$pf = \frac{R}{|Z|}$$

where $R$ is the resistance and $|Z|$ is the magnitude of the impedance.

- The power factor can also be expressed as leading or lagging, depending on whether the current leads or lags the voltage.
- The power factor is leading when the capacitive reactance is greater than the inductive reactance, and lagging when the inductive reactance is greater than the capacitive reactance.
- The power factor is unity when the inductive reactance and the capacitive reactance are equal, and the circuit is purely resistive.
