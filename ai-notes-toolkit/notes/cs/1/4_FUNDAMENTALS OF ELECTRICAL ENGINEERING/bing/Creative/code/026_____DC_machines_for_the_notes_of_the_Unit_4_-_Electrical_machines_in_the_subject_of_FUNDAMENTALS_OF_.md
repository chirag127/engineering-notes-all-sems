# DC Machines

## Introduction

- A DC machine is an electro-mechanical device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa .
- A DC machine can operate as a generator or a motor depending on the direction of power flow and the polarity of the terminals.
- A DC machine consists of two main parts: a stator and a rotor .
- The stator is the stationary part that contains the field windings and the magnetic poles .
- The rotor is the rotating part that contains the armature windings and the commutator .
- The armature windings are the conductors that carry the current induced by the magnetic field or the current that produces the torque .
- The commutator is a mechanical device that reverses the direction of the current in the armature windings every half cycle to maintain a unidirectional torque .
- The brushes are the contacts that connect the external circuit to the commutator segments .

## Classification of DC Machines

- DC machines can be classified into three types based on the connection of the field windings to the armature windings:
  - Series DC machines: The field windings are connected in series with the armature windings. The field current is equal to the armature current. The field flux varies with the load current. These machines have high starting torque and low speed regulation. They are suitable for applications such as traction, hoists, cranes, etc.
  - Shunt DC machines: The field windings are connected in parallel with the armature windings. The field current is independent of the armature current. The field flux is constant and independent of the load current. These machines have low starting torque and high speed regulation. They are suitable for applications such as fans, blowers, pumps, etc.
  - Compound DC machines: The field windings are a combination of series and shunt windings. The field flux is partly constant and partly variable with the load current. These machines have moderate starting torque and speed regulation. They are suitable for applications such as generators, compressors, lathes, etc.

## Working Principle of DC Machines

- The working principle of a DC machine is based on the phenomenon of electromagnetic induction and the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field .
- When a DC machine operates as a generator, the mechanical energy supplied to the rotor is converted into electrical energy in the armature windings. The induced emf in the armature windings is given by the equation:

  - E = PΦZN/60A

  - Where E is the induced emf, P is the number of poles, Φ is the flux per pole, Z is the total number of armature conductors, N is the speed of rotation in rpm, and A is the number of parallel paths in the armature.

- When a DC machine operates as a motor, the electrical energy supplied to the armature windings is converted into mechanical energy in the rotor. The torque produced by the armature windings is given by the equation:

  - T = PΦZI/2πA

  - Where T is the torque, P is the number of poles, Φ is the flux per pole, Z is the total number of armature conductors, I is the armature current, and A is the number of parallel paths in the armature.

- The direction of the induced emf or the torque can be determined by applying Fleming's right-hand rule or Fleming's left-hand rule respectively .
