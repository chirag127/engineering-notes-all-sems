# Unit 3 - Transformers

- A transformer is a device that converts alternating current (AC) from one state to another. It can be designed to “step up” or “step down” the voltage and current in the process, which is very useful .
- A transformer works on the principle of magnetic induction. A varying current in any coil of the transformer produces a varying magnetic flux in the transformer's core, which induces a varying electromotive force (EMF) across any other coils wound around the same core.
- Transformers are employed for widely varying purposes; e.g., to reduce the voltage of conventional power circuits to operate low-voltage devices, such as doorbells and toy electric trains, and to raise the voltage from electric generators so that electric power can be transmitted over long distances.
- Transformers are electrical devices consisting of two or more coils of wire, called windings, that are linked by a magnetic core. The primary winding is the coil that receives the input power, and the secondary winding is the coil that delivers the output power. The ratio of the number of turns in the primary and secondary windings determines the voltage transformation ratio.
- Transformers can be classified into different types based on their construction, function, and application. Some common types are:
  - Step-up transformer: A transformer that increases the output voltage and decreases the output current compared to the input.
  - Step-down transformer: A transformer that decreases the output voltage and increases the output current compared to the input.
  - Isolation transformer: A transformer that has the same input and output voltage, but provides electrical isolation between the circuits.
  - Auto-transformer: A transformer that has only one winding, which acts as both the primary and the secondary, with a tap to adjust the output voltage.
  - Current transformer: A transformer that measures the current in a circuit by producing a proportional current in its secondary winding.
  - Potential transformer: A transformer that measures the voltage in a circuit by producing a proportional voltage in its secondary winding.
  - Instrument transformer: A transformer that is used to isolate and scale down the voltage or current of a circuit for measurement or protection purposes.
  - Power transformer: A transformer that is used to transfer power between different voltage levels in a power system.
  - Distribution transformer: A transformer that is used to supply power to the end users at a lower voltage level.
  - Audio transformer: A transformer that is used to match the impedance of audio devices and to isolate them from ground loops.
  - Pulse transformer: A transformer that is used to transmit pulses of voltage or current in digital circuits.
  - RF transformer: A transformer that is used to transfer radio frequency signals between circuits with different impedances.
