# Types of Electrical Machines

Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy to mechanical energy or vice versa, or change the form of electrical energy. They are broadly classified into two types: static and dynamic.

## Static Electrical Machines

Static electrical machines are stationary devices that do not have any moving parts. They transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another by electromagnetic induction. The most common example of a static electrical machine is a transformer, which can step up or step down the voltage and current of an alternating current (AC) supply. Transformers are used for various purposes, such as power transmission, distribution, isolation, impedance matching, and voltage regulation.

## Dynamic Electrical Machines

Dynamic electrical machines are rotating devices that have a rotor and a stator. The rotor is the part that rotates and the stator is the part that remains stationary. The rotor and the stator are connected by a magnetic field, which enables the transfer of energy between them. Dynamic electrical machines can be classified into three main types: direct current (DC) machines, synchronous machines, and induction machines.

### DC Machines

DC machines are machines that operate on direct current (DC) supply. They can be used as generators or motors. A DC generator converts mechanical energy to DC electrical energy by using a commutator, which is a device that reverses the direction of current in the rotor coils. A DC motor converts DC electrical energy to mechanical energy by using a commutator, which is a device that maintains the direction of current in the rotor coils. DC machines have two main types: brushed and brushless. Brushed machines have brushes that make contact with the commutator and supply current to the rotor. Brushless machines have permanent magnets or electronic switches that supply current to the rotor without brushes.

### Synchronous Machines

Synchronous machines are machines that operate on alternating current (AC) supply and have a constant speed of rotation. They can be used as generators or motors. A synchronous generator converts mechanical energy to AC electrical energy by using a field winding, which is a coil that produces a magnetic field in the rotor. A synchronous motor converts AC electrical energy to mechanical energy by using a field winding, which is a coil that receives a magnetic field from the stator. Synchronous machines have two main types: wound-field and permanent magnet. Wound-field machines have field windings that can be excited by an external DC source or by self-excitation. Permanent magnet machines have permanent magnets that produce a constant magnetic field in the rotor.

### Induction Machines

Induction machines are machines that operate on alternating current (AC) supply and have a variable speed of rotation. They can be used as generators or motors. An induction generator converts mechanical energy to AC electrical energy by using a squirrel-cage rotor, which is a rotor that has short-circuited bars that induce currents in the stator. An induction motor converts AC electrical energy to mechanical energy by using a squirrel-cage rotor, which is a rotor that receives currents from the stator. Induction machines have two main types: single-phase and three-phase. Single-phase machines have one set of stator windings that produce a pulsating magnetic field. Three-phase machines have three sets of stator windings that produce a rotating magnetic field.
