# MCCB

MCCB stands for Moulded Case Circuit Breaker. It is a type of electrical protection device that is used to protect the electrical circuit from overload, short circuit, instantaneous over current and earth fault. It is an advanced version of MCB (Miniature Circuit Breaker)  .

Some of the features of MCCB are:

- It has a current rating of up to 2500A  .
- It can be used for a wide range of voltages and frequencies with adjustable trip settings  .
- It can protect against ground faults or residual currents with specially designed MCCBs .
- It has a moulded case that encloses the contacts, arc extinguishing mechanism and trip unit  .
- It can be manually or electrically operated  .
- It can be reset after a fault clearance  .

Some of the advantages of MCCB are:

- It provides reliable and accurate protection for the circuit  .
- It can be customized to suit different applications and requirements  .
- It can be easily installed and maintained  .
- It has a long service life and high breaking capacity  .

Some of the disadvantages of MCCB are:

- It is more expensive than MCB  .
- It is larger and heavier than MCB  .
- It may require external accessories for remote control and indication  .

Some of the types of MCCB are:

- Thermal magnetic MCCB: It uses a bimetallic strip and an electromagnet to sense the overload and short circuit currents respectively  .
- Electronic MCCB: It uses an electronic circuit to measure the current and trigger the trip unit  .
- Hybrid MCCB: It combines the thermal magnetic and electronic features to provide more accurate and flexible protection  .

Some of the applications of MCCB are:

- Industrial and commercial installations  .
- Power distribution and transmission systems  .
- Motor control and protection  .
- Renewable energy systems  .
