# Types of Electrical Machines

Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy to mechanical energy or vice versa. They can be classified into two main categories: static and dynamic.

## Static Electrical Machines

Static electrical machines are stationary devices that do not have any moving parts. They are used to transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another without changing its frequency. The most common example of a static electrical machine is a transformer, which can step up or step down the voltage and current of an alternating current (AC) source. Transformers can be single-phase or three-phase, depending on the number of input and output terminals .

## Dynamic Electrical Machines

Dynamic electrical machines are rotating devices that have a rotor and a stator. They are used to convert mechanical energy to electrical energy or vice versa. The rotor is the part that rotates and the stator is the part that remains stationary. The rotor and the stator are connected by a magnetic field, which can be generated by permanent magnets, electromagnets, or induction. The most common examples of dynamic electrical machines are generators and motors .

### Generators

Generators are dynamic electrical machines that convert mechanical energy to electrical energy. They are driven by a prime mover, such as a turbine, an engine, or a water wheel. The mechanical energy causes the rotor to rotate and cut the magnetic field lines of the stator, inducing an electromotive force (EMF) in the stator coils. The EMF produces an AC output voltage and current, which can be delivered to a load or a grid. Generators can be classified into two types: direct current (DC) generators and alternating current (AC) generators.

#### DC Generators

DC generators are generators that produce a DC output voltage and current. They have a commutator, which is a device that reverses the direction of the current in the rotor coils every half cycle, so that the output polarity remains constant. DC generators can be further classified into two types: separately excited DC generators and self-excited DC generators. Separately excited DC generators have an external source of DC voltage that supplies the field current to the rotor coils. Self-excited DC generators have no external source of DC voltage and rely on the residual magnetism of the rotor to initiate the field current .

#### AC Generators

AC generators are generators that produce an AC output voltage and current. They have no commutator, and the output polarity changes every half cycle, following a sinusoidal waveform. AC generators can be further classified into two types: synchronous generators and asynchronous generators. Synchronous generators have a constant rotor speed and a constant output frequency, which is equal to the product of the rotor speed and the number of poles. Asynchronous generators have a variable rotor speed and a variable output frequency, which depends on the load and the slip. Slip is the difference between the synchronous speed and the actual speed of the rotor .

### Motors

Motors are dynamic electrical machines that convert electrical energy to mechanical energy. They are driven by a source of electrical voltage and current, such as a battery, a generator, or a grid. The electrical energy causes the stator to produce a magnetic field, which interacts with the rotor and creates a torque that makes the rotor rotate. The mechanical energy can be used to drive a load, such as a fan, a pump, or a machine. Motors can be classified into two types: direct current (DC) motors and alternating current (AC) motors.

#### DC Motors

DC motors are motors that operate on a DC source of voltage and current. They have a commutator, which is a device that reverses the direction of the current in the rotor coils every half cycle, so that the torque remains constant. DC motors can be further classified into two types: permanent magnet DC motors and wound field DC motors. Permanent magnet DC motors have permanent magnets in the rotor, which set up a constant magnetic field. Wound field DC motors have electromagnets in the rotor, which can be controlled by varying the field current .
