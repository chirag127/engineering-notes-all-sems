# ACB

- ACB stands for Air Circuit Breaker, which is an electrical device used to provide overcurrent and short-circuit protection for electric circuits over 800 Amps to 10K Amps   .
- ACB operates at atmospheric pressure in air, where air-blast as an arc quenching medium .
- ACB is usually used in low voltage applications below 450V, such as distribution panels .
- ACB has three main components: the contacts, the arc chute and the operating mechanism .
- The contacts are the parts that carry the current and make or break the circuit. They consist of a fixed contact and a moving contact .
- The arc chute is the part that extinguishes the arc that forms when the contacts separate. It consists of a series of metal plates that create a low resistance path for the arc and split it into smaller arcs that are easier to quench .
- The operating mechanism is the part that controls the opening and closing of the contacts. It can be manual, spring-loaded, pneumatic or electromagnetic .
- ACB can be classified into two types: plain air circuit breaker and air blast circuit breaker .
- Plain air circuit breaker uses the natural properties of air to quench the arc. It has a simple design and low maintenance cost, but it has a slow speed of operation and a large size .
- Air blast circuit breaker uses a blast of compressed air to quench the arc. It has a high speed of operation and a small size, but it has a complex design and a high maintenance cost .
- ACB has several advantages, such as high breaking capacity, high reliability, low noise, easy installation and low environmental impact  .
- ACB also has some disadvantages, such as high cost, high power consumption, high air pressure requirement and sensitivity to dust and moisture  .
- ACB is used in the switching mechanism and protection of the electrical system, such as power plants, substations, industrial plants, commercial buildings and residential buildings  .
