# Single Phase Induction Motor

A single phase induction motor is a type of electric motor that operates on single phase alternating current. It is similar to a three phase induction motor in construction, except that it has only one stator winding instead of three. The stator winding produces a pulsating magnetic field that induces currents in the rotor, which is a squirrel cage made of copper or aluminum bars. The rotor currents create a magnetic field that interacts with the stator field and produces torque, causing the rotor to rotate.

However, a single phase induction motor has no starting torque, because the stator field does not rotate and the rotor is stationary. To make the motor self-starting, various methods are used to create a rotating magnetic field at least at the start. Some of the common methods are:

- **Permanent-split capacitor motor**: This motor has a capacitor connected in series with a second stator winding, which is placed at an angle to the main winding. The capacitor creates a phase shift between the two windings, resulting in a rotating magnetic field. The capacitor remains in the circuit during the operation of the motor, and provides a high power factor and efficiency. This motor is suitable for applications that require constant speed and low starting torque, such as fans, blowers, pumps, etc.

- **Capacitor-start induction motor**: This motor has a capacitor connected in series with a second stator winding, which is placed at an angle to the main winding. The capacitor creates a phase shift between the two windings, resulting in a rotating magnetic field. The capacitor is disconnected from the circuit by a centrifugal switch once the motor reaches a certain speed, and the motor runs on the main winding only. This motor is suitable for applications that require high starting torque and moderate speed, such as compressors, refrigerators, air conditioners, etc.

- **Shaded-pole motor**: This motor has a salient-pole stator with a small copper ring or loop around a part of each pole, called the shaded pole. The shaded pole creates a phase shift in the magnetic flux, resulting in a rotating magnetic field. The shaded pole remains in the circuit during the operation of the motor, and provides a low power factor and efficiency. This motor is suitable for applications that require low starting torque and low speed, such as fans, clocks, toys, etc.

Some of the advantages of single phase induction motors are:

- They are simple, cheap, and easy to maintain.
- They can operate on single phase power supply, which is widely available.
- They have a smooth and quiet operation.

Some of the disadvantages of single phase induction motors are:

- They have no starting torque, and require special methods to start.
- They have a low power factor and efficiency, and consume more current than three phase motors of the same power rating.
- They have a lower speed and torque than three phase motors of the same size.
