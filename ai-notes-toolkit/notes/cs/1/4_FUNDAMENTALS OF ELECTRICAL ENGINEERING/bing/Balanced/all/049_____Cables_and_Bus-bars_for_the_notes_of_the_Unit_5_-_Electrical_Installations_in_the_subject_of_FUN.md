# Cables and Bus-bars

Cables and bus-bars are two methods of transporting electrical energy in distribution systems. They have different advantages and disadvantages depending on the application and design requirements.

## Cables

- Cables are insulated conductors that are usually made of copper or aluminum wires.
- Cables can be flexible or rigid, and can be run in trays, conduits, ducts, or overhead lines.
- Cables can carry low, medium, or high voltage currents, depending on their insulation and rating.
- Cables can be easily routed and terminated, and can accommodate different load configurations and locations.
- Cables have some drawbacks, such as:
  - They have higher resistance and losses than bus-bars, which reduces their efficiency and increases their heating.
  - They have larger bend radii and cross-sectional areas than bus-bars, which requires more space and supports.
  - They are more susceptible to mechanical damage, corrosion, and insulation degradation, which affects their reliability and safety.
  - They are more difficult to change or modify once installed, which reduces their flexibility and adaptability.

## Bus-bars

- Bus-bars are metal bars that are usually made of copper or aluminum, and are enclosed in a metal casing or housing.
- Bus-bars can carry large amounts of current, typically in the range of hundreds or thousands of amperes.
- Bus-bars are used for short-distance power distribution, such as in switchboards, panel boards, bus ducts, or busway systems.
- Bus-bars have some advantages, such as:
  - They have lower resistance and losses than cables, which increases their efficiency and reduces their heating.
  - They have smaller bend radii and cross-sectional areas than cables, which saves space and supports.
  - They have higher mechanical strength and durability than cables, which enhances their reliability and safety.
  - They are easier to change or modify, and can accommodate different plug-in devices, which increases their flexibility and adaptability.
- Bus-bars have some drawbacks, such as:
  - They have higher initial cost than cables, which may affect their economic feasibility.
  - They have lower insulation and isolation than cables, which may increase the risk of short circuits and faults.
  - They have higher electromagnetic interference than cables, which may affect the performance of sensitive equipment and devices.
