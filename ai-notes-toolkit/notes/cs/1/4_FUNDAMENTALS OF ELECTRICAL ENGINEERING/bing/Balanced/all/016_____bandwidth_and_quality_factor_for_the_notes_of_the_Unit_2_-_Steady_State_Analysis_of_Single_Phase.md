# Bandwidth and Quality Factor for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the Subject of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering

- Bandwidth of a resonant circuit is the range of frequencies over which the current or voltage amplitude is equal to or greater than 70.7% of its maximum value  .
- Quality factor of a resonant circuit is a dimensionless quantity that measures how underdamped an oscillator or resonator is, or equivalently, how narrow the bandwidth of the resonator is   .
- The quality factor of an AC circuit is related to bandwidth as inversely proportional, that is, Q = f<sub>r</sub>/BW, where f<sub>r</sub> is the resonant frequency and BW is the bandwidth   .
- A high Q resonant circuit has a narrow bandwidth and a low Q resonant circuit has a wide bandwidth  .
- The quality factor and the bandwidth of a resonant circuit depend on whether the circuit is series or parallel .
- For a series resonant circuit, the quality factor is given by Q = X<sub>L</sub>/R = 1/(RωC), where X<sub>L</sub> is the inductive reactance, R is the resistance, ω is the angular frequency, and C is the capacitance  .
- For a parallel resonant circuit, the quality factor is given by Q = R/X<sub>L</sub> = RCω, where R is the resistance, X<sub>L</sub> is the inductive reactance, C is the capacitance, and ω is the angular frequency .
- The bandwidth of a resonant circuit can be calculated from the quality factor and the resonant frequency using the formula BW = f<sub>r</sub>/Q, where f<sub>r</sub> is the resonant frequency and Q is the quality factor   .
- The resonant frequency of a series or parallel resonant circuit can be calculated from the inductance and the capacitance using the formula f<sub>r</sub> = 1/(2π√LC), where L is the inductance and C is the capacitance  .
- The average power of a resonant circuit can be calculated from the rms voltage and the resistance using the formula P<sub>avg</sub> = V<sub>rms</sub><sup>2</sup>/R, where V<sub>rms</sub> is the root mean square voltage and R is the resistance .
