# Concept of Resonance in series & parallel circuits

- Resonance is a phenomenon that occurs in AC circuits when the frequency of the applied voltage is such that the impedance of the circuit is either minimum or maximum.
- Resonance can be classified into two types: series resonance and parallel resonance.
- Series resonance occurs when the circuit consists of a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor connected in series. At resonance, the inductive reactance and the capacitive reactance cancel each other, making the circuit purely resistive. The impedance of the circuit is minimum and the current is maximum. The resonant frequency is given by:

$$f_r = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$$

- Parallel resonance occurs when the circuit consists of a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor connected in parallel. At resonance, the admittance of the circuit is minimum and the impedance is maximum. The current in the circuit is minimum and the voltage across the parallel combination is maximum. The resonant frequency is given by:

$$f_r = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$$

- The quality factor (Q) of a resonant circuit is a measure of how sharp the resonance is. It is defined as the ratio of the resonant frequency to the bandwidth of the circuit. The bandwidth is the range of frequencies for which the current or voltage is at least half of its maximum value. The quality factor is given by:

$$Q = \frac{f_r}{B}$$

- For a series resonant circuit, the quality factor is also equal to the ratio of the inductive reactance to the resistance at resonance. For a parallel resonant circuit, the quality factor is also equal to the ratio of the resistance to the inductive reactance at resonance. The quality factor is given by:

$$Q = \frac{X_L}{R} = \frac{R}{X_L}$$

- The higher the quality factor, the narrower the bandwidth and the sharper the resonance. A high quality factor indicates a low energy loss in the circuit. A low quality factor indicates a high energy loss in the circuit.
