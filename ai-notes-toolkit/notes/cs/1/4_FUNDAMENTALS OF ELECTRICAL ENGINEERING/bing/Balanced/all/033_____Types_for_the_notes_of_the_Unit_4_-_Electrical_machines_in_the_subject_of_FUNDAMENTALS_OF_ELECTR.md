# Types of Electrical Machines

Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. They can be classified into two main categories: static and rotating.

## Static Electrical Machines

Static electrical machines are stationary devices that do not have any moving parts. They are used to transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another without changing its frequency or waveform. The most common example of a static electrical machine is a transformer, which can step up or step down the voltage and current of an alternating current (AC) supply. Transformers can be single-phase or three-phase, depending on the number of input and output terminals.

## Rotating Electrical Machines

Rotating electrical machines are devices that have a rotating part, called a rotor, that interacts with a stationary part, called a stator, to produce or consume electrical or mechanical power. They can be classified into three main types: direct current (DC) machines, synchronous machines, and induction machines.

### DC Machines

DC machines are rotating electrical machines that operate on direct current (DC) supply. They can be used as generators or motors, depending on the direction of power flow. DC machines have a commutator and brushes that switch the polarity of the current in the rotor coils as they rotate, to maintain a constant torque. DC machines can be further classified into permanent magnet DC machines, which have permanent magnets in the rotor, and wound-field DC machines, which have electromagnets in the rotor.

### Synchronous Machines

Synchronous machines are rotating electrical machines that operate on alternating current (AC) supply. They can be used as generators or motors, depending on the direction of power flow. Synchronous machines have a constant speed that is proportional to the frequency of the supply. The rotor of a synchronous machine can be excited by a DC source or by permanent magnets, creating a magnetic field that synchronizes with the rotating magnetic field of the stator. Synchronous machines can be further classified into cylindrical-rotor machines, which have a smooth cylindrical rotor, and salient-pole machines, which have a rotor with projecting poles.

### Induction Machines

Induction machines are rotating electrical machines that operate on alternating current (AC) supply. They can be used as motors only, as they cannot generate electricity. Induction machines have a rotor that is not electrically connected to the stator, but rather induced by the rotating magnetic field of the stator. The rotor can be either a squirrel-cage rotor, which has a cage-like structure of conductors, or a wound-rotor, which has coils that can be connected to external resistors or capacitors. Induction machines are also called asynchronous machines, as their speed is not equal to the synchronous speed of the stator.
