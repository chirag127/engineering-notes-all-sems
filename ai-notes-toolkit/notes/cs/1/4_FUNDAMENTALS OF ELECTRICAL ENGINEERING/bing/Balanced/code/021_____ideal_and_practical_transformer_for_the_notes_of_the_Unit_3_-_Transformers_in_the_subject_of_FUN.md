### Ideal and Practical Transformer

- A transformer is a device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through mutual induction.
- An ideal transformer is a theoretical model that assumes no losses and perfect coupling between the primary and secondary windings.
- A practical transformer is a real device that has some losses and imperfect coupling between the primary and secondary windings.

#### Ideal Transformer

- An ideal transformer has the following characteristics:
  - It has no winding resistance, core loss, leakage flux, or magnetizing current.
  - It has infinite permeability of the core, so that all the flux produced by the primary winding links with the secondary winding.
  - It has no voltage drop across the windings, so that the primary and secondary voltages are equal to the induced emfs.
  - It has 100% efficiency, meaning that the power input to the primary winding is equal to the power output from the secondary winding.

#### Practical Transformer

- A practical transformer has the following characteristics:
  - It has some winding resistance, core loss, leakage flux, and magnetizing current.
  - It has finite permeability of the core, so that some of the flux produced by the primary winding does not link with the secondary winding.
  - It has some voltage drop across the windings, so that the primary and secondary voltages are not equal to the induced emfs.
  - It has less than 100% efficiency, meaning that the power input to the primary winding is greater than the power output from the secondary winding.
