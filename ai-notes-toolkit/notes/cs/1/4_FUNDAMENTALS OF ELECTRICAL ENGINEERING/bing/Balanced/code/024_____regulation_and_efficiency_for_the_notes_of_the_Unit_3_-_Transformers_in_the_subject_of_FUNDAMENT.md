### Regulation and Efficiency of Transformers

- Regulation of a transformer is the measure of how well the output voltage of the transformer remains constant under varying load conditions. It is defined as the percentage change in the secondary voltage from no-load to full-load at a given power factor and frequency. A lower regulation indicates a better transformer performance.
- Efficiency of a transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power. It is expressed as a percentage and depends on the losses in the transformer. The losses in a transformer are mainly of two types: core losses and copper losses. Core losses are due to the hysteresis and eddy currents in the magnetic core of the transformer and are constant for a given voltage and frequency. Copper losses are due to the resistance of the windings and vary with the load current. The efficiency of a transformer can be improved by reducing the losses or increasing the output power.
- The Department of Energy (DOE) has played a role in regulating the efficiency of transformers in the United States since 2007. The most recent of these standards are the DOE 2016 efficiency requirements, which were deployed with efficiency and consumer benefits in mind. The 2016 regulations increase the minimum efficiency levels for three categories of distribution transformers: low-voltage dry-type, liquid-immersed, and medium-voltage dry-type. The 2016 regulations aim for 98.70 to 99.55% transformer efficiency ratings, compared to the 2010 regulations that aimed for 98.36 to 99.49%.
