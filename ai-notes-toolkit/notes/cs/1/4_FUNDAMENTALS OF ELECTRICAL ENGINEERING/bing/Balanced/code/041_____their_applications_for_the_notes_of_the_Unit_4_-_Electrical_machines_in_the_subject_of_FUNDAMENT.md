# Applications of Electrical Machines

Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. They are classified into two main categories: rotating and linear machines. Rotating machines include motors and generators, while linear machines include actuators and transformers.

Some of the applications of electrical machines are:

- **DC machines**: These are machines that operate on direct current (DC) and have commutators and brushes to switch the direction of current in the armature. They are mainly used for supplying excitation of small and medium-range alternators, in electrolytic processes, welding processes and variable speed motor drives .
- **AC machines**: These are machines that operate on alternating current (AC) and have either wound or permanent magnet rotors. They are further divided into synchronous and induction machines.
  - **Synchronous machines**: These are machines that operate at a constant speed and have a fixed relationship between the frequency of the AC supply and the number of poles. They are used as alternators to generate AC power, as synchronous motors to drive constant speed loads, and as synchronous condensers to improve power factor.
  - **Induction machines**: These are machines that operate at a variable speed and have a slip between the rotor speed and the synchronous speed. They are the most widely used type of AC machines, as they are simple, rugged, and cheap. They are used as induction motors to drive variable speed loads, as induction generators to convert mechanical power into AC power, and as induction regulators to control voltage and power flow.
- **Universal machines**: These are machines that can operate on both AC and DC supply. They are usually series-wound motors that have high starting torque and variable speed. They are used in applications such as industrial fans, blowers, pumps, machine tools, household appliances, power tools, and disk drives.
