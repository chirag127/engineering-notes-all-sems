### Cables and Bus-bars

Cables and bus-bars are two methods of transporting electrical energy in distribution systems. They have different advantages and disadvantages depending on the application and design requirements.

#### Cables

- Cables are insulated conductors that can be run in trays or conduit.
- Cables can be flexible and easy to install in different locations and configurations.
- Cables can provide protection from electric shock, short circuit, and fire hazards.
- Cables can also reduce electromagnetic interference and noise in sensitive circuits.
- Cables have some drawbacks, such as:
  - Higher cost per ampere carried compared to bus-bars.
  - Larger space requirement due to bend radii and insulation thickness.
  - Higher voltage drop and power loss due to higher resistance and reactance.
  - Higher maintenance and replacement costs due to aging and deterioration of insulation.

#### Bus-bars

- Bus-bars are metal bars, usually made of copper or aluminum, that are used to carry large amounts of current.
- Bus-bars are often enclosed in a metal casing or duct to provide mechanical support and protection.
- Bus-bars have some advantages, such as:
  - Lower cost per ampere carried compared to cables.
  - Smaller space requirement due to compact design and no insulation.
  - Lower voltage drop and power loss due to lower resistance and reactance.
  - Higher reliability and durability due to less components and connections.
- Bus-bars have some drawbacks, such as:
  - Lower flexibility and adaptability to different locations and configurations.
  - Higher risk of electric shock, short circuit, and fire hazards due to exposed conductors.
  - Higher electromagnetic interference and noise in nearby circuits.
  - Higher corrosion and oxidation due to environmental factors.
