### Concept of Linearity

- Linearity is a property of an element or a system that describes a linear relationship between cause and effect .
- A linear element or system obeys the principles of **homogeneity** and **additivity** .
- Homogeneity means that if the input is scaled by a factor, the output is scaled by the same factor .
- Additivity means that if the input is the sum of two or more signals, the output is the sum of the corresponding outputs .
- A linear element or system can be represented by a straight line on a graph of input versus output .
- A nonlinear element or system does not follow the principles of homogeneity and additivity, and has a curved or irregular shape on a graph of input versus output .
- Linearity is important for circuit analysis because it allows us to use **superposition** to simplify complex circuits with multiple sources .
- Superposition states that the response of a linear system to multiple inputs is equal to the sum of the responses to each input individually.
- To apply superposition, we can turn off all but one source at a time, and find the partial response due to that source. Then we can add up all the partial responses to get the total response.
