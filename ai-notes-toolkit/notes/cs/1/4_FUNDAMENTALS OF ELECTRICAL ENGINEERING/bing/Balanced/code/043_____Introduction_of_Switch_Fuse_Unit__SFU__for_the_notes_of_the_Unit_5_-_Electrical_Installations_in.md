### Introduction of Switch Fuse Unit (SFU) for the notes of the Unit 5 - Electrical Installations in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- A switch fuse unit (SFU) is a type of low voltage switchgear that combines a switch and a fuse in a single unit.
- A switch is a device that can make or break an electrical circuit by opening or closing the contacts. A fuse is a device that can interrupt an electrical circuit by melting when the current exceeds a certain value.
- The purpose of a switch fuse unit is to provide protection and control for electrical circuits and equipment. The switch can be used to manually switch on or off the circuit, while the fuse can provide overcurrent protection in case of short circuit or overload.
- A switch fuse unit is also known as an ironclad switch, because it is usually made of iron or steel to withstand high currents and temperatures. It can be mounted on a panel board or a wall, and it has a handle for operating the switch and a window for inspecting the fuse.
- A switch fuse unit can be classified according to the number of poles and the type of fuse. It can be double pole for controlling single phase two-wire circuits, triple pole for controlling three-phase three-wire circuits, or triple pole with neutral link for controlling three-phase four-wire circuits. The respective switches are known as double pole ironclad (DPIC), triple pole ironclad (TPIC), and triple pole with neutral link ironclad (TPNIC) switches .
- The type of fuse used in a switch fuse unit can be high rupturing capacity (HRC) fuse, cartridge fuse, or rewirable fuse. The fuse rating should be less than the switch rating, and it should be selected based on the connected load. For example, a 32 A fuse can be used for a 63 A rating SFU unit .
- A switch fuse unit has several advantages, such as simplicity, reliability, low cost, and easy maintenance. However, it also has some disadvantages, such as large size, slow operation, and limited breaking capacity. Therefore, it is suitable for low voltage and low power applications, such as lighting, heating, and small motors.
