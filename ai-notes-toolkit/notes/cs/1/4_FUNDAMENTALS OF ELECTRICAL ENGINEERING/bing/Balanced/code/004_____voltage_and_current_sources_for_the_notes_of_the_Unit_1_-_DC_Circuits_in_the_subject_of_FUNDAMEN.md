### Voltage and Current Sources

- A voltage source is a device that generates a potential difference (voltage) across its two terminals, regardless of the current flowing through it .
- A current source is a device that establishes a specified amount of electric current in a portion of a circuit, regardless of the voltage across it .
- Examples of voltage sources are batteries, generators, solar cells, etc. Examples of current sources are photodiodes, transistors, etc.
- Voltage and current sources can be either independent or dependent. Independent sources have fixed values of voltage or current, while dependent sources have values that depend on some other circuit element .
- There are four possible dependent sources: the voltage-controlled voltage source (VCVS), the current-controlled voltage source (CCVS), the voltage-controlled current source (VCCS), and the current-controlled current source (CCCS).
- The sign convention for passive components and sources is to assign positive polarity to the terminal where current enters and negative polarity to the terminal where current leaves.
- The power delivered by a source is given by the product of voltage and current. A source delivers positive power when it supplies energy to the circuit, and negative power when it absorbs energy from the circuit .
