### Representation of Sinusoidal waveforms – Average and effective values

- A sinusoidal waveform is a periodic function that oscillates between positive and negative peak values with a constant frequency and amplitude.
- The general equation of a sinusoidal waveform is `v(t) = Vm sin(ωt + θ)`, where `v(t)` is the instantaneous value, `Vm` is the peak value, `ω` is the angular frequency, `t` is the time, and `θ` is the phase angle.
- The average value of a sinusoidal waveform is the arithmetic mean of the positive half-cycle or the negative half-cycle. It is calculated by dividing the area under the curve by the base length.
- The average value of a sinusoidal waveform is given by `Vav = 0.637 Vm` for a full cycle, and `Vav = 0.9 Vm` for a half cycle.
- The effective value or the root mean square (RMS) value of a sinusoidal waveform is the equivalent DC value that would produce the same heating effect in a resistor as the AC waveform.
- The RMS value of a sinusoidal waveform is given by `Vrms = 0.707 Vm` for a full cycle, and `Vrms = Vm` for a half cycle.
- The form factor of a sinusoidal waveform is the ratio of the RMS value to the average value. It is given by `FF = Vrms / Vav`.
- The form factor of a sinusoidal waveform is 1.11 for a full cycle, and 1.414 for a half cycle.
- The peak factor of a sinusoidal waveform is the ratio of the peak value to the RMS value. It is given by `PF = Vm / Vrms`.
- The peak factor of a sinusoidal waveform is 1.414 for a full cycle, and 1 for a half cycle.
- A sinusoidal waveform can be represented by a phasor, which is a rotating vector with a magnitude equal to the peak value and an angle equal to the phase angle.
- A phasor can be used to simplify the analysis of AC circuits by using complex numbers and trigonometric identities.
- A resistor in an AC circuit behaves the same as in a DC circuit. The voltage and current are in phase and the resistance is constant.
- The power dissipated by a resistor in an AC circuit is given by `P = Vrms Irms cos(φ)`, where `φ` is the power factor angle.
- The power factor of a resistor is 1, which means that the voltage and current are in phase and the power is maximum.
