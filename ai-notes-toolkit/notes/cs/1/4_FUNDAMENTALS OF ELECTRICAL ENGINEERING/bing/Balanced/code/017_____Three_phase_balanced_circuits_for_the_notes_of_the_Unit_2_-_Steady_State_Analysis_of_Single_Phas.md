### Three phase balanced circuits

- A three phase balanced circuit is a type of alternating current (AC) power system that consists of three voltage sources and three loads connected in either star (Y) or delta (Δ) configuration.
- In a balanced system, each of the three instantaneous voltages have equal amplitudes but are separated from the other voltages by a phase angle of 120°. The three voltages (or phases) are typically labeled a, b and c .
- The advantages of using a three phase balanced circuit over a single phase circuit are: higher power transmission, higher efficiency, smoother torque in motors, and simpler transformer design.
- The power delivered by a three phase balanced circuit can be calculated by the formula: P = √3 V L I L cos θ, where V L is the line voltage, I L is the line current, and θ is the power factor angle .
- The power factor of a three phase balanced circuit is the ratio of the real power (P) to the apparent power (S), where S = √3 V L I L. The power factor can also be expressed as the cosine of the angle between the voltage and current phasors.
- The three phase balanced circuit can be analyzed by using the per-phase equivalent circuit, which is a single phase circuit corresponding to the reference phase. The per-phase equivalent circuit simplifies the calculations by using the phase voltage (V ph ), the phase current (I ph ), and the phase impedance (Z ph ) .
- The relationship between the line and phase quantities depends on the type of connection. For a star connection, V L = √3 V ph and I L = I ph. For a delta connection, V L = V ph and I L = √3 I ph .
