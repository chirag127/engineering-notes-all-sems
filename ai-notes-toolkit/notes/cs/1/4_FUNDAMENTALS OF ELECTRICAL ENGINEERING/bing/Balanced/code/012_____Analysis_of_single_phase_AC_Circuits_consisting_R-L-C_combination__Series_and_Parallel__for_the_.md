### Analysis of single phase AC Circuits consisting R-L-C combination (Series and Parallel)

- A single phase AC circuit is a circuit that is powered by a single alternating voltage or current source.
- A R-L-C circuit is a circuit that contains a resistor (R), an inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) as its elements. These elements can be connected in series or parallel across the AC source.
- The behavior of a R-L-C circuit depends on the frequency of the AC source and the values of R, L and C. The circuit can act as a filter, an oscillator, a resonator or a damper depending on these factors.

#### Series R-L-C Circuit

- In a series R-L-C circuit, the resistor, the inductor and the capacitor are connected in series across the AC source. The same current flows through all the elements and the voltage drops across each element are different.
- The total voltage across the series R-L-C circuit is equal to the phasor sum of the individual voltage drops across R, L and C. The total impedance of the series R-L-C circuit is equal to the phasor sum of the individual impedances of R, L and C.
- The impedance triangle of a series R-L-C circuit is a right-angled triangle with the resistance R as the base, the reactance X as the perpendicular and the impedance Z as the hypotenuse. The angle between Z and R is called the phase angle and it indicates the phase difference between the voltage and the current in the circuit.
- The power factor of a series R-L-C circuit is the cosine of the phase angle and it measures the ratio of the true power to the apparent power in the circuit. The power factor can be improved by adding or subtracting a suitable reactance to the circuit.
- The series R-L-C circuit can exhibit resonance when the frequency of the AC source is such that the inductive reactance and the capacitive reactance are equal and opposite. At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is minimum and equal to the resistance R. The current in the circuit is maximum and in phase with the voltage. The power factor is unity and the power dissipation is maximum.

#### Parallel R-L-C Circuit

- In a parallel R-L-C circuit, the resistor, the inductor and the capacitor are connected in parallel across the AC source. The same voltage is applied across all the elements and the currents through each element are different.
- The total current through the parallel R-L-C circuit is equal to the phasor sum of the individual currents through R, L and C. The total admittance of the parallel R-L-C circuit is equal to the phasor sum of the individual admittances of R, L and C.
- The admittance triangle of a parallel R-L-C circuit is a right-angled triangle with the conductance G as the base, the susceptance B as the perpendicular and the admittance Y as the hypotenuse. The angle between Y and G is called the phase angle and it indicates the phase difference between the current and the voltage in the circuit.
- The power factor of a parallel R-L-C circuit is the cosine of the phase angle and it measures the ratio of the true power to the apparent power in the circuit. The power factor can be improved by adding or subtracting a suitable susceptance to the circuit.
- The parallel R-L-C circuit can also exhibit resonance when the frequency of the AC source is such that the inductive susceptance and the capacitive susceptance are equal and opposite. At resonance, the admittance of the circuit is maximum and equal to the conductance G. The voltage across the circuit is maximum and in phase with the current. The power factor is unity and the power dissipation is maximum.
