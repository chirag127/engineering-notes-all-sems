### Analysis of single phase AC Circuits consisting R-L-C combination (Series and Parallel)

- A single phase AC circuit is a circuit that is powered by a single alternating voltage or current source.
- A R-L-C circuit is a circuit that contains a resistor (R), an inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) as its elements.
- A R-L-C circuit can be connected in series or parallel, depending on how the elements are arranged with respect to the voltage or current source.
- In a series R-L-C circuit, the elements are connected end-to-end, so that the same current flows through all of them. The total impedance (Z) of the circuit is the sum of the individual impedances of the elements.
- In a parallel R-L-C circuit, the elements are connected across the voltage source, so that the same voltage is applied to all of them. The total admittance (Y) of the circuit is the sum of the individual admittances of the elements.
- The impedance (Z) and the admittance (Y) are complex quantities that have both magnitude and phase angle. They depend on the frequency (f) of the AC source, as well as the values of R, L and C.
- The impedance (Z) and the admittance (Y) can be represented by phasor diagrams or complex numbers in rectangular or polar forms. They can also be related by the equation Y = 1/Z.
- The impedance (Z) and the admittance (Y) can be decomposed into two components: resistance (R) or conductance (G), which are in phase with the voltage or current; and reactance (X) or susceptance (B), which are 90 degrees out of phase with the voltage or current.
- The reactance (X) and the susceptance (B) can be further divided into two types: inductive (XL or BL), which are positive and lag the voltage or current by 90 degrees; and capacitive (XC or BC), which are negative and lead the voltage or current by 90 degrees.
- The impedance (Z) and the admittance (Y) can be represented by triangles, where the hypotenuse is Z or Y, the horizontal side is R or G, and the vertical side is X or B. The angle between Z or Y and R or G is called the phase angle (φ), which indicates the phase difference between the voltage and the current in the circuit.

The power factor is a measure of how efficiently the circuit uses the power from the source. It ranges from 0 to 1, where 1 means that the circuit is purely resistive and all the power is dissipated as heat, and 0 means that the circuit is purely reactive and no power is transferred to the circuit.
