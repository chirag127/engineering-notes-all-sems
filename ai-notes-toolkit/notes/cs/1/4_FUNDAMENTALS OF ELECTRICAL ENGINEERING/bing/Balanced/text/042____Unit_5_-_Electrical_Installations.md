## Unit 5 - Electrical Installations

This unit covers the following topics:

- Electrical symbols and diagrams
- Electrical circuits and components
- Electrical safety and regulations
- Electrical measurements and calculations
- Electrical installation and testing

### Electrical symbols and diagrams

- Electrical symbols are used to represent different electrical devices and components in a circuit diagram.
- Electrical diagrams are graphical representations of electrical circuits that show the connections and arrangements of the components.
- There are different types of electrical diagrams, such as schematic diagrams, wiring diagrams, and layout diagrams.
- Schematic diagrams show the logic and function of a circuit using standard symbols and conventions.
- Wiring diagrams show the physical connections and layout of the wires and devices in a circuit.
- Layout diagrams show the location and orientation of the components and devices in a circuit.

### Electrical circuits and components

- An electrical circuit is a closed path that allows electric current to flow from a source of voltage to a load.
- A source of voltage is a device that provides electrical energy to a circuit, such as a battery, a generator, or a solar panel.
- A load is a device that consumes electrical energy in a circuit, such as a lamp, a motor, or a heater.
- A switch is a device that can open or close a circuit, allowing or stopping the current flow.
- A resistor is a device that limits the current flow in a circuit, producing heat and voltage drop.
- A capacitor is a device that stores electrical energy in a circuit, producing voltage and current changes.
- An inductor is a device that opposes the change of current in a circuit, producing magnetic fields and voltage spikes.
- A diode is a device that allows current to flow in one direction only, blocking the reverse current.
- A transistor is a device that can amplify or switch electrical signals in a circuit, acting as a current-controlled switch.
- A LED is a device that emits light when current flows through it, acting as a light source and an indicator.

### Electrical safety and regulations

- Electrical safety is the prevention of electric shock, fire, or explosion caused by electrical hazards in a circuit or an installation.
- Electrical hazards are the potential sources of danger or harm from electricity, such as exposed wires, faulty insulation, overloaded circuits, or wet conditions.
- Electrical regulations are the rules and standards that govern the design, installation, operation, and maintenance of electrical systems and equipment, such as the National Electrical Code (NEC) in the US, or the British Standard (BS) in the UK.
- Electrical regulations ensure the safety, efficiency, and reliability of electrical systems and equipment, as well as the protection of people, property, and environment from electrical hazards.
- Electrical regulations specify the requirements for electrical wiring, grounding, protection, testing, labeling, and documentation of electrical systems and equipment.

### Electrical measurements and calculations

- Electrical measurements are the methods and instruments used to measure the electrical quantities in a circuit, such as voltage, current, resistance, power, energy, and frequency.
- Electrical calculations are the mathematical operations and formulas used to determine the electrical quantities in a circuit, such as Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws, power formula, energy formula, and frequency formula.
- Ohm's law states that the voltage across a resistor is equal to the product of the current through it and its resistance, or V = IR.
- Kirchhoff's current law (KCL) states that the sum of the currents entering a node in a circuit is equal to the sum of the currents leaving that node, or Iin = Iout.
- Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL) states that the sum of the voltages around a closed loop in a circuit is equal to zero, or Vloop = 0.
- Power formula states that the power consumed or delivered by a device in a circuit is equal to the product of the voltage across it and the current through it, or P = VI.
- Energy formula states that the energy consumed or delivered by a device in a circuit is equal to the product of the power and the time, or E = Pt.
- Frequency formula states that the frequency of an alternating current (AC) or a periodic signal is equal to the inverse of the time period, or f = 1/T.

### Electrical installation and testing

- Electrical installation is the process of planning, designing, installing, and connecting the electrical systems and equipment in a building or a facility, such as lighting, power, communication, and control systems.
- Electrical testing is the process of inspecting, measuring, and verifying the performance, functionality, and safety of the electrical systems and equipment in a building or a facility, using various tools and instruments, such as multimeters, oscilloscopes, and testers.
