### Principle of operation and introduction to methods of starting for the notes of the Unit 4 - Electrical machines in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. They can be classified into two main categories: direct current (DC) machines and alternating current (AC) machines.
- DC machines operate on the principle of electromagnetic induction, which states that when a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force that tends to rotate it. This force is called torque and it is proportional to the current, the magnetic field and the angle between them. DC machines can be further divided into DC generators and DC motors, depending on whether they produce or consume electrical energy.
- DC generators convert mechanical energy into electrical energy by rotating a coil of wire in a magnetic field. The induced voltage in the coil depends on the number of turns, the magnetic flux and the speed of rotation. DC generators can be classified into three types: separately excited, self-excited and permanent magnet. Separately excited generators have an independent source of field current, while self-excited generators use part of the output voltage to supply the field current. Permanent magnet generators have permanent magnets instead of electromagnets to provide the magnetic field.
- DC motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy by applying a voltage to a coil of wire that rotates in a magnetic field. The direction of rotation depends on the polarity of the voltage and the magnetic field. DC motors can be classified into three types: shunt, series and compound. Shunt motors have a constant field current, while series motors have a variable field current that depends on the load. Compound motors have a combination of shunt and series field windings.
- AC machines operate on the principle of rotating magnetic field, which states that a set of coils carrying alternating currents can produce a magnetic field that rotates at a constant speed. The speed of rotation depends on the frequency of the currents and the number of poles in the coils. AC machines can be further divided into synchronous machines and induction machines, depending on whether they have a fixed or a variable speed of rotation.
- Synchronous machines have a rotor that rotates at the same speed as the rotating magnetic field. The rotor can be either a permanent magnet or an electromagnet. Synchronous machines can be used as generators or motors, depending on whether they produce or consume electrical energy. Synchronous generators are commonly used in power plants to generate electricity, while synchronous motors are used in applications that require precise speed control, such as clocks and robots.
- Induction machines have a rotor that rotates at a slightly lower speed than the rotating magnetic field. The rotor can be either a squirrel cage or a wound rotor. Induction machines can only be used as motors, as they cannot generate electricity. Induction motors are the most widely used type of electric motors, as they are simple, cheap, reliable and efficient. They are used in various applications, such as fans, pumps, compressors, conveyors and elevators.
- Starting methods for electrical machines are the techniques used to initiate the operation of the machines. They are necessary because the machines may not be able to start under normal conditions, due to high starting currents, low starting torques, mechanical stresses or other factors. Starting methods can be classified into two types: full voltage and reduced voltage methods.
- Full voltage methods apply the rated voltage to the machine terminals at the start. They are simple and cheap, but they may cause high starting currents, low power factor, voltage dips and mechanical shocks. Full voltage methods are suitable for small and lightly loaded machines, such as shunt and compound DC motors, permanent magnet DC motors, permanent magnet synchronous motors and squirrel cage induction motors.
- Reduced voltage methods apply a lower voltage than the rated voltage to the machine terminals at the start. They are more complex and expensive, but they can reduce the starting currents, improve the power factor, limit the voltage dips and minimize the mechanical stresses. Reduced voltage methods are suitable for large and heavily loaded machines, such as series DC motors, wound rotor DC motors, wound rotor synchronous motors and wound rotor induction motors.
- Some examples of reduced voltage methods are: star-delta starter, autotransformer starter, resistor starter, reactor starter, soft starter and variable frequency drive. Each of these methods has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the type and size of the machine, the load characteristics, the supply conditions and the desired performance.
