### Cables and Bus-bars

- Cables and bus-bars are two methods of transporting electrical energy in distribution systems.
- Cables are insulated conductors that are usually made of copper or aluminum and can be run in trays or conduit. Cables can carry low, medium or high voltage currents and are flexible and easy to install. Cables can also be shielded to reduce electromagnetic interference.
- Bus-bars are metal bars that are usually made of copper or aluminum and are used to carry large amounts of current. Bus-bars are not insulated and are enclosed in a metal casing or duct. Bus-bars can carry low or medium voltage currents and are rigid and compact. Bus-bars can also be segmented to isolate faults and prevent short circuits.
- Some advantages of cables over bus-bars are:
  - Cables can be routed in any direction and can bend around obstacles.
  - Cables can be easily terminated and connected to different types of equipment.
  - Cables can be easily replaced or repaired if damaged.
- Some advantages of bus-bars over cables are:
  - Bus-bars can carry more current per unit cross-section than cables.
  - Bus-bars have lower resistance and impedance than cables, which reduces power losses and voltage drops.
  - Bus-bars have higher mechanical strength and thermal stability than cables, which reduces sagging and overheating.
  - Bus-bars have lower installation and maintenance costs than cables.
