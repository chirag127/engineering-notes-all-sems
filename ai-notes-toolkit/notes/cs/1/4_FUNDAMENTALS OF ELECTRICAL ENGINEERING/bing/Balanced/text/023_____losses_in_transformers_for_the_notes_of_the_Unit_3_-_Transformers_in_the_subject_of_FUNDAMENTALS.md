### Losses in Transformers

Transformers are devices that transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another by electromagnetic induction. They are used to increase or decrease the voltage and current levels of AC power systems. However, transformers are not ideal and they incur some losses during the energy conversion process. These losses reduce the efficiency and performance of the transformer. The main types of losses in transformers are:

- **Copper loss**: This is the power loss due to the resistance of the transformer windings. It is proportional to the square of the current flowing through the windings and the resistance of the windings. Copper loss can be reduced by using thicker wires or better conductors for the windings. Copper loss is also called ohmic loss, variable loss or resistive loss  .

- **Core loss**: This is the power loss due to the magnetization and demagnetization of the transformer core. It consists of two components: hysteresis loss and eddy current loss. Hysteresis loss is caused by the repeated reversal of the magnetic domains in the core material as the alternating magnetic flux changes direction. Eddy current loss is caused by the induced currents in the core that circulate in small loops and oppose the main flux. Core loss can be reduced by using better core materials with low hysteresis and high resistivity, or by laminating the core into thin sheets to increase the resistance of the eddy current paths. Core loss is also called iron loss or constant loss   .

- **Stray loss**: This is the power loss due to the leakage of the magnetic flux from the transformer. Some of the flux generated by the primary winding does not link with the secondary winding and escapes to the surrounding air or nearby metal objects. This flux causes unwanted heating and eddy currents in the transformer and its surroundings. Stray loss can be reduced by improving the design and construction of the transformer to minimize the air gaps and the leakage paths. Stray loss is also called leakage loss  .

- **Dielectric loss**: This is the power loss due to the heating of the insulation materials used in the transformer. The insulation materials have some electrical conductivity and they dissipate some energy when subjected to the electric field of the transformer. Dielectric loss can be reduced by using better insulation materials with low conductivity and high breakdown strength. Dielectric loss is also called insulation loss.

The total loss in a transformer is the sum of the copper loss, the core loss, the stray loss and the dielectric loss. The efficiency of a transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power, or the ratio of the input power minus the total loss to the input power. The efficiency of a transformer can be improved by reducing the losses as much as possible.
