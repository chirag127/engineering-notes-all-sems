
### Losses in Transformers

* Transformers are electrical components used to transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another, typically at different voltage levels. 
* Transformers are subject to a variety of losses, which can be divided into two categories: core losses and copper losses. 
* Core losses occur due to the presence of a magnetic field in the core of the transformer, and are caused by hysteresis and eddy current losses. 
* Hysteresis losses occur due to the hysteresis loop of the core material, and are proportional to the frequency of the applied voltage. 
* Eddy current losses occur due to the induced currents that circulate within the core material, and are proportional to the square of the frequency of the applied voltage. 
* Copper losses occur due to the resistance of the windings of the transformer, and are proportional to the square of the current flowing through the transformer. 
* Copper losses can be minimized by using thicker wire and increasing the number of turns in the windings. 
* To minimize core losses, the transformer core should be made of a low-loss material, such as grain-oriented silicon steel.
