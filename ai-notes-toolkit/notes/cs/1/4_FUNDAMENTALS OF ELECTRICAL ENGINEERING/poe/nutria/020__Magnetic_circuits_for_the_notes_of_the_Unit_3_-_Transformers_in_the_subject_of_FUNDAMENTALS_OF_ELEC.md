
### Magnetic Circuits for the Notes of Unit 3 - Transformers in FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

1. A transformer is a device that is used to transfer electrical energy between two or more circuits. It can be used to increase or decrease the voltage in a circuit, as well as to isolate the circuit from electrical noise.

2. Transformers are made up of two or more coils of wire, called windings, which are wrapped around a core made of iron. The number of turns in each winding determines the ratio of voltage between the primary and secondary circuits.

3. The primary winding is connected to the power source, while the secondary winding is connected to the load. The core of the transformer is magnetically linked to both windings, and the magnetic field created by the primary winding induces a voltage in the secondary winding.

4. Transformers are classified according to the type of core used. The most common types are the iron-core transformer and the air-core transformer. The iron-core transformer is the most efficient type, as it has a high magnetic permeability and low hysteresis loss.

5. Transformers can also be classified according to their application. Step-up transformers are used to increase the voltage in a circuit, while step-down transformers are used to decrease the voltage in a circuit. Isolation transformers are used to isolate two circuits from each other, while autotransformers are used to adjust the voltage in a circuit.

6. Transformers are used in a wide range of applications, from power distribution to audio systems. They are also used in various types of electrical equipment, such as motors, generators, and inverters.
