### Concept of Resonance in Series & Parallel Circuits

Resonance is an important concept in electrical engineering, especially in the analysis of AC circuits. It occurs when the frequency of an external AC source matches the natural frequency of the circuit. At resonance, the circuit exhibits a maximum response to the AC source, which can have significant practical implications. In this section, we will discuss the concept of resonance in series and parallel circuits.

#### Series Resonance

In a series resonant circuit, the inductor, capacitor, and resistor are connected in series. The circuit impedance is given by:

Z = R + j(XL - XC)

where R is the resistance, XL is the inductive reactance, and XC is the capacitive reactance. At resonance, the inductive and capacitive reactances are equal and opposite, i.e., XL = XC. Therefore, the impedance of the circuit reduces to:

Z = R

which is purely resistive. At resonance, the circuit draws the minimum current for a given voltage, and the power factor is unity. The resonance frequency is given by:

f0 = 1/(2π√(LC))

where L is the inductance and C is the capacitance of the circuit.

#### Parallel Resonance

In a parallel resonant circuit, the inductor, capacitor, and resistor are connected in parallel. The circuit impedance is given by:

Z = (R^-1 + jωC)^-1 + jωL

where R is the resistance, C is the capacitance, L is the inductance, and ω is the angular frequency of the AC source. At resonance, the imaginary part of the impedance (which is due to the reactive components) is zero, and the real part (which is due to the resistance) is maximum. Therefore, the circuit draws the maximum current for a given voltage, and the power factor is also maximum. The resonance frequency is given by:

f0 = 1/(2π√(LC))

where L is the inductance and C is the capacitance of the circuit.

#### Practical Applications

Resonant circuits are widely used in practical applications, such as in radio receivers and transmitters, filters, and oscillators. For example, a radio receiver uses a resonant circuit to tune to a particular frequency of the radio signal. Similarly, an oscillator uses a resonant circuit to generate a stable frequency signal. Filters are used to separate signals of different frequencies, and resonant circuits are an essential component of filters.

In conclusion, the concept of resonance in series and parallel circuits plays an important role in the analysis and design of AC circuits. A clear understanding of resonance is essential for electrical engineering students, as it is a fundamental concept in many practical applications.
