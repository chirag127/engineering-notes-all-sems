### Types of Batteries

1. Primary Batteries:
- Cannot be recharged.
- Examples: Alkaline batteries, Lead acid batteries.
- Used in low-drain devices like TV remotes, clocks, etc.

2. Secondary Batteries:
- Can be recharged.
- Examples: Lithium-ion batteries, Nickel-metal hydride batteries.
- Used in high-drain devices like smartphones, laptops, electric vehicles, etc.

3. Lead-acid Batteries:
- Have lead plates and sulphuric acid electrolyte.
- Used in automobiles, UPS systems, etc.
- Have high energy density but short lifespan.

4. Alkaline Batteries:
- Have alkaline electrolyte (potassium hydroxide).
- Higher energy density and longer shelf-life than lead-acid batteries.
- Used in toys, flashlights, etc.

5. Lithium-ion Batteries:
- Have lithium ions that move from positive electrode to negative electrode during discharge and back when charged.
- Higher energy density than other batteries.
- Used in consumer electronics but can explode if overcharged or short-circuited.
