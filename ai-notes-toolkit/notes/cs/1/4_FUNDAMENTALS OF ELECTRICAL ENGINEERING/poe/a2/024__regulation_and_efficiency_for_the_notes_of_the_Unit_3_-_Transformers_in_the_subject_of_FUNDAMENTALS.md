### Regulation and Efficiency

- Regulation refers to the ability of a transformer to maintain constant secondary voltage for fluctuating load conditions and primary voltage changes.
- The regulation of a transformer depends on the following factors:
- Magnetizing current: The magnetizing current required for core excitation causes the primary winding voltage to drop. This affects the regulation.
- Copper loss: The copper loss in the windings causes the winding resistance to increase with load resulting in voltage drop and poor regulation.
- Leakage reactance: The leakage reactance causes the voltage drop to increase with the load current, thereby affecting regulation.
- Efficiency refers to the ratio of output power to the input power. The efficiency of a transformer depends on:
- Core loss: The core loss increases with frequency and flux density, thereby reducing efficiency.
- Copper loss: The copper loss in the windings increases with the square of current and causes reduction in efficiency.
- Load: The efficiency is maximum at full load and reduces with fractional loads.
