### Representation of Sinusoidal waveforms – Average and effective values for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

1. Sinusoidal waveforms can be represented in three ways:
- Peak value: The maximum value the waveform attains. Denoted by Vp or Ip.
- Peak-to-peak value: The difference between the maximum and minimum values. Denoted by Vpp or Ipp.
- RMS value: The value of DC voltage or current that produces the same heating effect as the alternating quantity. Denoted by Vrms or Irms.

2. The average value of a sinusoidal waveform over one complete cycle is 0 as the positive half cycle is equal to the negative half cycle. The average value over n complete cycles is also 0.

3. The effective or RMS value of a sinusoidal waveform is the value of DC voltage or current that produces the same heating effect as the alternating quantity. RMS values are used to specify the voltage and current ratings of electrical equipment as they produce the same heating effect. The RMS value is equal to the peak value divided by √2.

4. The peak, peak-to-peak and RMS values are related as:
Vp/Vpp = 1/2 and Vp/Vrms = √2
Ip/Ipp = 1/2 and Ip/Irms = √2
