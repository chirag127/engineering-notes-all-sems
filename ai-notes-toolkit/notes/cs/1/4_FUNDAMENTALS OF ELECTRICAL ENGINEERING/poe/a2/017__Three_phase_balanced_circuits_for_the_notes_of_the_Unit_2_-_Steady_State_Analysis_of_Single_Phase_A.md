### Three phase balanced circuits

1. Three phase circuits are commonly used in power distribution systems as they have several advantages over single phase circuits like better efficiency, less voltage drops, absence of power surges etc.
2. In a three phase balanced circuit, three AC voltages having the same amplitude and frequency and a phase difference of 120 degrees are applied to the circuit. The currents flowing through the loads also have the same amplitude and are displaced from each other by 120 degrees.
3. The power in a balanced three phase circuit is constant and pulsation free. This is because at any instant, the sum of the instantaneous powers of the three phases is equal to zero.
4. The steady state analysis of balanced three phase circuits can be done easily by applying the principles and methods discussed for single phase circuits. The voltages and currents can be represented as vectors and the vector algebra can be used to solve the circuits.
5. The line currents in a balanced three phase circuit are equal in magnitude and phase shifted by 120 degrees. The line voltage between any two lines is the vector sum of the individual phase voltages.
6. Three phase transformers and motors are designed based on the principles of balanced three phase circuits. The coils of the three phases are constructed in such a way that the mutual coupling between them is the same. This results in equal current distribution and absence of circulating currents.
