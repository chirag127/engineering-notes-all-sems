## Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits

1. Steady state conditions: When a circuit reaches a steady periodic state where the current and voltage waveforms do not change with time and repeat themselves after a specific interval of time called the periodic time (T), it is said to be under steady state conditions.
2. RMS values: The heating effect produced in a resistor is proportional to the square of the current flowing through it. So we define an equivalent value of DC current called the RMS (root mean square) value which when flowed through a resistor will produce the same heating effect as an AC current.
3. Phase angle: The time delay between current and voltage waveforms is called the phase angle. It is expressed in degrees or radians. If current leads the voltage, the phase angle is positive and if voltage leads the current, the phase angle is negative.
4. Power in AC circuits: The power in an AC circuit is the measure of the rate of energy transfer. It can be either active power (average power) or reactive power (non-average power). The respective units are Watts and Volt-amperes reactive or VAR.
5. Power factor: It is the measure of how effectively the current is being used in an AC circuit to perform useful work. It is equal to the ratio of active power to the apparent power and can range from 0 to 1. A higher power factor indicates a more efficient circuit.
