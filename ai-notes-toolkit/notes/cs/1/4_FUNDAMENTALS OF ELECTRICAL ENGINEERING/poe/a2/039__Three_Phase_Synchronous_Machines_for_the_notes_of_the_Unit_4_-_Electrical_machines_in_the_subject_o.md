### Three Phase Synchronous Machines

- A three phase synchronous machine is a type of AC rotating electric machine where the rotating magnetic field of the stator interacts with the magnetic field of the rotor to produce torque.
- The rotor is made up of salient poles on which there are field windings to produce a steady magnetic field. The stator is made up of 3 phase windings which are energised by a 3 phase supply to produce a revolving magnetic field.
- The rotor magnetic field synchronously follows the revolving stator magnetic field at the same rotor speed in the same direction. The synchronism is maintained by the governor which controls the field current.
- When the frequency of supply increases, the revolving stator magnetic field moves at a faster rate. In order to maintain synchronism, the rotor speeds up and draws more current for the field as directed by the governor. Similarly when supply frequency falls, the rotor slows down and draws less field current to maintain synchronism.
- Thus the rotor speed is controlled by the frequency of supply according to the speed-torque characteristics to meet the load demand. Due to this the synchronous machines are constant speed machines.
- The synchronous machines are classified into salient pole machines and cylindrical rotor machines based on rotor pole shape. They are also classified as single excited or doubly excited based on how the field is excited.
- The applications of synchronous machines include alternators in power generating stations, synchronous condensers for power factor improvement and synchronous motors for precise constant speed drives.
