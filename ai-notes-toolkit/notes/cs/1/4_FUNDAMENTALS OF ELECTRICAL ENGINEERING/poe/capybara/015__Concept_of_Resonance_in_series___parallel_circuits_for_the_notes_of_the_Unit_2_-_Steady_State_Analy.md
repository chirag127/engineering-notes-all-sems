### Concept of Resonance in Series & Parallel Circuits

Resonance is a phenomenon that occurs in electrical circuits when the frequency of an alternating current (AC) matches the natural frequency of the circuit. This results in a significant increase in the amplitude of the current or voltage, depending on the type of circuit.

In this unit, we will focus on the concept of resonance in series and parallel circuits. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

#### Resonance in Series Circuits

- A series circuit consists of components connected in a sequential manner, one after the other.
- When the frequency of the AC source matches the natural frequency of the series circuit, resonance occurs. This natural frequency depends on the values of the inductor and capacitor in the circuit.
- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is at its minimum, which means that the current through the circuit is at its maximum.
- The voltage across the capacitor and inductor is also at its maximum at resonance, while the voltage across the resistor is at its minimum.

#### Resonance in Parallel Circuits

- A parallel circuit consists of components connected in parallel to each other, allowing current to flow through multiple paths.
- When the frequency of the AC source matches the natural frequency of the parallel circuit, resonance occurs. This natural frequency depends on the values of the inductor and capacitor in the circuit.
- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is at its maximum, which means that the current through the circuit is at its minimum.
- The voltage across the resistor is also at its maximum at resonance, while the voltage across the capacitor and inductor is at its minimum.

#### Quality Factor (Q) of Resonant Circuits

- The quality factor (Q) is a measure of the efficiency of a resonant circuit.
- In a series resonant circuit, Q is equal to the ratio of the inductive reactance to the resistance.
- In a parallel resonant circuit, Q is equal to the ratio of the resistance to the capacitive reactance.
- A higher Q value indicates a more efficient resonant circuit, with a narrower bandwidth and a sharper peak at resonance.

In conclusion, understanding the concept of resonance in series and parallel circuits is crucial for analyzing and designing electrical circuits. By knowing the natural frequency and quality factor of a resonant circuit, we can predict and control its behavior under different conditions.
