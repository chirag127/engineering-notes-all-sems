## Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits

In this unit, we will learn about the steady-state analysis of single-phase AC circuits. The following are the important points to be covered:

- AC quantities: In AC circuits, the voltage and current are varying with time. We represent them using sinusoidal waveforms. The peak value, RMS value, frequency, and phase of the waveform are the important quantities.

- Phasors: Phasors are used to represent the AC quantities in steady-state analysis. A phasor is a rotating vector that represents the sinusoidal waveform. The magnitude and phase angle of the phasor represent the peak value and phase of the waveform.

- Impedance: Impedance is the AC counterpart of resistance. It is a complex quantity that represents the opposition to the flow of AC current. The impedance of a circuit depends on the frequency and the components of the circuit.

- Ohm's law in AC circuits: Ohm's law is valid in AC circuits, but we need to use phasors and impedance instead of the actual voltage and current. The voltage and current phasors are related by the impedance of the circuit.

- Power in AC circuits: The power in AC circuits is not simply the product of voltage and current as in DC circuits. The power is a complex quantity that has two components: the real power and the reactive power. The real power is the power that is actually consumed by the circuit, while the reactive power is the power that is stored and released by the reactive components of the circuit.

- Series AC circuits: In a series AC circuit, the components are connected in series, and the same current flows through all the components. The voltage across each component depends on its impedance. We can use Kirchhoff's laws and Ohm's law to analyze series AC circuits.

- Parallel AC circuits: In a parallel AC circuit, the components are connected in parallel, and the same voltage is applied across all the components. The current through each component depends on its impedance. We can use Kirchhoff's laws and Ohm's law to analyze parallel AC circuits.

- Resonance: Resonance occurs in a circuit when the frequency of the AC source is equal to the resonant frequency of the circuit. At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is minimum, and the current is maximum. Resonance is useful in many applications, such as tuning circuits and filters.

- Frequency response: The frequency response of a circuit is the variation of its impedance with frequency. The frequency response is important in many applications, such as audio amplifiers and filters.

In conclusion, the steady-state analysis of single-phase AC circuits is an important topic in electrical engineering. It is essential for the design and analysis of many electrical systems, such as power systems, audio systems, and communication systems.
