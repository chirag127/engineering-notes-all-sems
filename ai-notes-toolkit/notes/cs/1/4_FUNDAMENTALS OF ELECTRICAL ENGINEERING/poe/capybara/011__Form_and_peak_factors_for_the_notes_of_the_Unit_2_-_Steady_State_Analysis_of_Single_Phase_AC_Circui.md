### Form and Peak Factors for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the Subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

In Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering, we will discuss the steady-state analysis of single-phase AC circuits. One of the important concepts to understand in this unit is form factor and peak factor.

Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Form factor is defined as the ratio of the RMS value of a periodic waveform to its average value over one cycle. It is denoted by the symbol 'F'.
- Mathematically, the form factor can be expressed as F = Vrms/Vavg, where Vrms is the RMS value of the waveform and Vavg is its average value.
- For a sinusoidal waveform, the form factor is equal to 1.11. This means that the RMS value of a sinusoidal waveform is 1.11 times its average value.
- Peak factor, on the other hand, is defined as the ratio of the peak value of a periodic waveform to its RMS value. It is denoted by the symbol 'P'.
- Mathematically, the peak factor can be expressed as P = Vp/Vrms, where Vp is the peak value of the waveform and Vrms is its RMS value.
- For a sinusoidal waveform, the peak factor is equal to √2. This means that the peak value of a sinusoidal waveform is √2 times its RMS value.
- Form factor and peak factor are important parameters in the analysis of AC circuits as they help to determine the power delivered by the voltage source and the current flowing through the circuit.

In conclusion, form factor and peak factor are important concepts to understand in the steady-state analysis of single-phase AC circuits. They play a crucial role in determining the power delivered by the voltage source and the current flowing through the circuit. It is important to remember the mathematical formulas and the values for form factor and peak factor for sinusoidal waveforms.
