### Three Phase Balanced Circuits

Three-phase balanced circuits are an important part of electrical engineering. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- A three-phase circuit consists of three voltage sources and three loads.
- The voltages are typically 120 degrees out of phase with each other.
- The loads must be balanced, meaning that they are equal in magnitude and impedance.
- The current in each phase is also 120 degrees out of phase with the other two phases.
- The total power in a three-phase balanced circuit is three times the power in a single-phase circuit with the same voltage and current.
- Three-phase power is used extensively in industrial settings because it is more efficient and produces less ripple in motors and other devices.
- The voltage between any two phases is called the line voltage, while the voltage between a phase and neutral is called the phase voltage.
- When analyzing three-phase circuits, it is common to use the per-phase equivalent circuit, which simplifies the calculations by reducing the circuit to a single-phase equivalent.
- The per-phase equivalent circuit consists of a single voltage source and a single load, with values that are one-third of the total values for the three-phase circuit.
- When calculating power in a three-phase circuit, it is important to use the correct formula for the type of load (i.e. resistive, capacitive, or inductive).
- In summary, three-phase balanced circuits are an important and efficient way to distribute power in industrial settings, and understanding their analysis is a key part of electrical engineering.
