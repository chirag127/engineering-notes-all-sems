### ACB for the notes of the Unit 5 - Electrical Installations in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

ACB stands for Air Circuit Breaker, which is an electrical device used in power distribution systems to protect the electrical equipment from damage due to overcurrents. Here are some important points to note about ACBs:

- ACBs are used in low voltage applications and have a rated voltage of up to 1000V.
- ACBs are designed to interrupt the flow of current in an electrical circuit when the current exceeds a pre-determined value.
- ACBs consist of a contact system, operating mechanism, trip unit, and arc chamber.
- The contact system is responsible for making and breaking the electrical circuit.
- The operating mechanism is used to open and close the contact system.
- The trip unit is responsible for sensing the overcurrent and sending a signal to the operating mechanism to open the contact system.
- The arc chamber is designed to quench the arc that is generated when the contact system is opened.
- ACBs are available in different sizes and breaking capacities to suit different applications.
- ACBs are more reliable and require less maintenance compared to other types of circuit breakers.
- ACBs are commonly used in commercial and industrial applications for protection against short circuits, overload currents, and earth faults.

In conclusion, ACBs play an important role in protecting electrical equipment from damage due to overcurrents, and are commonly used in power distribution systems. Understanding the basic principles of ACBs is essential for anyone studying electrical installations in the subject of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering.
