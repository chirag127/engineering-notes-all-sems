### Delhi 2013

- Delhi 2013 refers to the Delhi Legislative Assembly election that was held on 4 December 2013, with the result announced on 8 December resulting in formation of the Fifth Legislative Assembly of Delhi .
- The election was contested by various political parties, including the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), the Indian National Congress (INC), the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP), the Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), and others.
- The BJP won a plurality of 31 seats out of 70, closely followed by AAP, in its first election, with 28 seats. The INC, which had ruled Delhi for 15 years, was reduced to 8 seats. The BSP and others won 3 seats each .
- The election resulted in a hung assembly, as no party had a clear majority of 36 seats. The BJP, being the single largest party, was invited by the Lieutenant Governor of Delhi to form the government, but declined, citing lack of numbers. The AAP, with the outside support of the INC, formed a minority government, with Arvind Kejriwal as the Chief Minister .
- The AAP government lasted for 49 days, from 28 December 2013 to 14 February 2014, before Kejriwal resigned over the failure to pass the Jan Lokpal Bill, an anti-corruption legislation, in the assembly. The assembly was then dissolved and fresh elections were held in 2015 .
- The Delhi 2013 election was significant for several reasons, such as:
  - It marked the emergence of AAP as a new political force in Delhi and India, challenging the established parties and raising issues of corruption, governance, and public services .
  - It witnessed the highest voter turnout of 66%, reflecting the high public interest and participation in the election .
  - It exposed the decline of the INC, which had been in power at the centre and in several states, and paved the way for the BJP's victory in the 2014 general elections .
  - It demonstrated the volatility and unpredictability of the electoral outcomes in a multi-party system, and the challenges of coalition politics and governance .
