### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

- Oral communication is the process of expressing ideas, feelings, opinions, or information through spoken words.
- Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context, purpose, and audience of the message.
- Oral communication can be classified into four main types: interpersonal, group, public, and mass communication.
- Interpersonal communication is the exchange of messages between two or more people in a face-to-face or mediated setting.
- Group communication is the exchange of messages among three or more people who share a common goal, identity, or interest.
- Public communication is the exchange of messages between a speaker and a large, diverse, and often unknown audience.
- Mass communication is the exchange of messages between a source and a large, dispersed, and heterogeneous audience through a mediated channel.

- Nuances are the subtle differences or variations in meaning, expression, or tone of voice that can affect the interpretation and impact of oral communication.
- Nuances can be conveyed through verbal and nonverbal cues, such as word choice, intonation, pitch, volume, pace, pauses, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and posture.
- Nuances can enhance or diminish the clarity, credibility, and persuasiveness of oral communication, depending on how they are used and perceived by the speaker and the listener.
- Nuances can also reflect the speaker's attitude, emotion, intention, and relationship with the listener, as well as the speaker's cultural background, personality, and communication style.

- Modes of speech delivery are the different ways of presenting oral communication, depending on the level of preparation, formality, and interaction involved.
- Modes of speech delivery can be categorized into four main types: impromptu, extemporaneous, manuscript, and memorized.
- Impromptu speech delivery is the spontaneous and unrehearsed presentation of oral communication, usually in response to a question, situation, or occasion.
- Extemporaneous speech delivery is the planned and prepared presentation of oral communication, usually with the aid of notes or an outline, but without reading from a script.
- Manuscript speech delivery is the verbatim and precise presentation of oral communication, usually by reading from a written text.
- Memorized speech delivery is the exact and flawless presentation of oral communication, usually by reciting from memory a previously written text.
