### Listening modes, listening and Note taking, Types of Listening, Listening

- Listening modes are the different ways of processing and understanding the spoken information that we hear. There are four main listening modes: active, passive, critical, and empathic.
- Active listening is when we pay full attention to the speaker and try to understand their message, feelings, and intentions. We use verbal and non-verbal feedback to show our interest and comprehension. Active listening is useful for building rapport, resolving conflicts, and learning new information.
- Passive listening is when we hear the speaker but do not engage with them or their message. We may be distracted, bored, or indifferent to what they are saying. Passive listening can lead to misunderstandings, missed opportunities, and poor relationships.
- Critical listening is when we evaluate the speaker's message for its accuracy, validity, logic, and bias. We use our own knowledge, experience, and reasoning to judge the quality and reliability of the information. Critical listening is useful for making decisions, solving problems, and forming opinions.
- Empathic listening is when we try to understand the speaker's emotions, feelings, and perspectives. We use our empathy and compassion to connect with them and show our support. Empathic listening is useful for building trust, showing care, and helping others.
- Listening and note taking are skills that help us to record, organize, and review the information that we hear. They can improve our memory, comprehension, and learning outcomes. Some strategies for effective listening and note taking are:
  - Prepare before the listening session by reviewing the topic, setting a purpose, and predicting the main ideas.
  - Use a note taking system that suits your style and preference, such as outlining, mapping, charting, or summarizing.
  - Write down the key points, facts, examples, and questions that you hear, using symbols, abbreviations, and keywords.
  - Review your notes as soon as possible after the listening session, by clarifying, editing, and expanding them.
  - Use your notes to study, review, or apply the information that you learned.
- Types of listening are the different purposes and goals that we have when we listen to something. There are many types of listening, but some common ones are:
  - Informative listening is when we listen to learn something new or gain knowledge. We focus on the facts, details, and main ideas of the message. Examples of informative listening are listening to a lecture, a podcast, or a news report.
  - Persuasive listening is when we listen to be convinced or influenced by something. We focus on the arguments, evidence, and appeals of the message. Examples of persuasive listening are listening to a sales pitch, a political speech, or an advertisement.
  - Appreciative listening is when we listen to enjoy or appreciate something. We focus on the aesthetics, emotions, and creativity of the message. Examples of appreciative listening are listening to music, poetry, or a story.
  - Therapeutic listening is when we listen to help or heal someone. We focus on the feelings, needs, and problems of the speaker. Examples of therapeutic listening are listening to a friend, a counselor, or a therapist.
