### Non verbal Communication: Kinesics, Paralinguistic features of Voice-Dynamics

- Non verbal communication is the process of conveying meaning without using words. It can complement, substitute, regulate, or contradict verbal communication. Non verbal communication can be classified into different types based on the mode and channel of communication.
- Kinesics is the study of body movements, gestures, facial expressions, and eye contact as non verbal communication. Kinesics can convey emotions, attitudes, intentions, and status. Kinesics can be divided into five categories: emblems, illustrators, regulators, adaptors, and affect displays .
  - Emblems are gestures that have a direct verbal equivalent, such as nodding for yes or shaking the head for no.
  - Illustrators are gestures that accompany and enhance verbal communication, such as pointing or using hand movements to show size or shape.
  - Regulators are gestures that control the flow and pace of verbal communication, such as raising the hand to signal a turn to speak or looking away to indicate disinterest.
  - Adaptors are gestures that satisfy a personal need or cope with a situation, such as scratching, fidgeting, or playing with an object.
  - Affect displays are gestures that express emotions, such as smiling, frowning, or crying.
- Paralinguistics is the study of vocal characteristics that are separate from the actual language, such as tone, pitch, volume, rate, and quality. Paralinguistics can convey emotions, attitudes, intentions, and status. Paralinguistics can be divided into three categories: vocal qualities, vocalizations, and vocal segregates  .
  - Vocal qualities are the features of the voice that affect the overall impression, such as breathiness, nasality, resonance, or hoarseness.
  - Vocalizations are the sounds that are not part of the language, such as laughter, sighs, moans, or cries.
  - Vocal segregates are the sounds that interrupt the flow of speech, such as um, uh, ah, or eh.
- Voice dynamics is the term used to describe the variations and changes in the voice that affect the meaning and impact of verbal communication. Voice dynamics can include aspects such as stress, intonation, rhythm, and pause. Voice dynamics can convey emotions, attitudes, intentions, and status. Voice dynamics can be divided into four categories: emphasis, inflection, modulation, and pause  .
  - Emphasis is the use of stress or accent on certain words or syllables to highlight their importance or contrast.
  - Inflection is the use of pitch or tone to indicate the mood, attitude, or question type of a sentence.
  - Modulation is the use of volume or loudness to indicate the intensity, confidence, or urgency of a message.
  - Pause is the use of silence or hesitation to create a break, a transition, or an effect in verbal communication.
