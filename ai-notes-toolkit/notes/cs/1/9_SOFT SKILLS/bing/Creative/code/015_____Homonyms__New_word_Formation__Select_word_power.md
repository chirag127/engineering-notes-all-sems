### Homonyms
- Homonyms are words that have the same spelling and pronunciation, but different meanings and origins.
- For example, the word "bat" can mean a flying mammal, a wooden club, or a stroke in cricket or baseball.
- Homonyms can be classified into two types: homographs and homophones.
- Homographs are words that have the same spelling, but different pronunciation and meaning. For example, the word "lead" can be pronounced as /liːd/ to mean a metal, or as /lɛd/ to mean to guide or direct.
- Homophones are words that have the same pronunciation, but different spelling and meaning. For example, the words "sea" and "see" are pronounced the same, but have different meanings and spellings.

### New word Formation
- New word formation is the process of creating new words from existing words or parts of words.
- There are different methods of new word formation, such as derivation, compounding, blending, clipping, acronym, back-formation, conversion, and coinage.
- Derivation is the process of adding prefixes or suffixes to existing words to form new words. For example, the word "happy" can be derived into "unhappy", "happiness", or "happily".
- Compounding is the process of combining two or more existing words to form a new word. For example, the words "black" and "board" can be compounded into "blackboard".
- Blending is the process of merging parts of two or more existing words to form a new word. For example, the words "breakfast" and "lunch" can be blended into "brunch".
- Clipping is the process of shortening an existing word to form a new word. For example, the word "telephone" can be clipped into "phone".
- Acronym is the process of forming a new word from the initial letters of a phrase or a name. For example, the phrase "North Atlantic Treaty Organization" can be acronymized into "NATO".
- Back-formation is the process of forming a new word by removing a suffix or a prefix from an existing word. For example, the word "editor" can be back-formed into "edit".
- Conversion is the process of changing the word class of an existing word to form a new word. For example, the word "book" can be converted from a noun to a verb, meaning to reserve or arrange something.
- Coinage is the process of inventing a new word that has no relation to existing words. For example, the word "google" was coined by the founders of the search engine company.
