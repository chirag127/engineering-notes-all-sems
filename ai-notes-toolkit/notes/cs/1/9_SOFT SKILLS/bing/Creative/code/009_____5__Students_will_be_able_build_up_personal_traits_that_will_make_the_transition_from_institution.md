### 5. Students will be able build up personal traits that will make the transition from institution

- The transition from institution to the workplace or further education can be challenging for many students, especially those who lack the necessary personal traits to cope with the changes and demands of the new environment.
- Some of the personal traits that can help students make a successful transition are:
  - **Self-awareness**: This is the ability to recognize one's own strengths, weaknesses, interests, values, and goals, and to use this information to guide one's decisions and actions. Self-awareness can help students identify their career aspirations, choose suitable courses or programs, and seek feedback and improvement opportunities.
  - **Self-management**: This is the ability to plan, organize, prioritize, and execute tasks effectively and efficiently, and to monitor and evaluate one's own performance. Self-management can help students balance their academic, personal, and professional responsibilities, and to cope with stress, challenges, and setbacks.
  - **Communication**: This is the ability to express oneself clearly and confidently, and to listen and respond appropriately to others, using various modes and channels of communication. Communication can help students build rapport and trust with their peers, instructors, mentors, and employers, and to convey their ideas, opinions, and needs effectively.
  - **Collaboration**: This is the ability to work with others towards a common goal, and to respect and appreciate the diversity of perspectives, skills, and contributions of each team member. Collaboration can help students learn from and support each other, and to develop interpersonal and intercultural competencies that are essential for the globalized workplace.
  - **Critical thinking**: This is the ability to analyze, evaluate, and synthesize information from various sources and perspectives, and to apply logic, creativity, and evidence to solve problems and make decisions. Critical thinking can help students develop a deeper understanding of their subjects, and to apply their knowledge and skills to real-world situations and challenges.
  - **Lifelong learning**: This is the ability and willingness to continuously acquire new knowledge and skills, and to adapt to changing circumstances and demands. Lifelong learning can help students keep up with the latest developments and innovations in their fields, and to pursue their personal and professional growth and development.
