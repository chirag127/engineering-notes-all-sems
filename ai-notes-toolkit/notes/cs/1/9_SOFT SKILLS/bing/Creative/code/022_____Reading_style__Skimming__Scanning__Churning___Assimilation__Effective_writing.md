### Reading style: Skimming; Scanning; Churning & Assimilation, Effective writing

- Reading style is the way a reader approaches a text, depending on their purpose, interest, and level of comprehension.
- There are different reading styles for different situations and goals. Some of the common ones are:

  - Skimming: This is a fast and superficial reading style, where the reader only looks for the main idea, the key words, and the general structure of the text. Skimming is useful for getting an overview of a text, or for finding specific information quickly.
  - Scanning: This is a focused and selective reading style, where the reader only looks for specific details, facts, or figures in the text. Scanning is useful for finding answers to questions, or for locating relevant information for a task or a project.
  - Churning: This is a slow and deep reading style, where the reader analyzes and evaluates the text, looking for the meaning, the arguments, the evidence, and the implications. Churning is useful for understanding and critiquing a text, or for learning new concepts or skills.
  - Assimilation: This is an active and creative reading style, where the reader integrates and applies the text to their own knowledge, experience, and context. Assimilation is useful for synthesizing and creating new ideas, or for solving problems or challenges.

- Effective writing is the ability to communicate clearly, concisely, and convincingly in written form, using appropriate language, tone, and structure for the audience and the purpose.
- Some of the principles of effective writing are:

  - Know your audience: Identify who you are writing for, what they need, and what they expect from your writing. Use language and tone that suit your audience and their level of familiarity with the topic.
  - Know your purpose: Define what you want to achieve with your writing, what message you want to convey, and what action you want to elicit from your audience. Use structure and format that support your purpose and your message.
  - Be clear: Use simple and precise words, sentences, and paragraphs that convey your meaning without ambiguity or confusion. Avoid jargon, slang, or unnecessary words that may obscure your message or distract your audience.
  - Be concise: Use only the information and details that are relevant and necessary for your message and your purpose. Avoid repetition, redundancy, or filler words that may weaken your message or bore your audience.
  - Be convincing: Use logic, evidence, and examples to support your message and your purpose. Avoid errors, contradictions, or fallacies that may undermine your credibility or your argument.
