### Comprehension, reading and writing

- Comprehension is the ability to understand and interpret written or spoken texts.
- Reading is the process of decoding written symbols and extracting meaning from them.
- Writing is the process of encoding thoughts and ideas into written symbols and conveying meaning to others.
- Comprehension, reading and writing are interrelated skills that develop through exposure, practice and feedback.
- Comprehension, reading and writing are essential for academic success, communication and lifelong learning.

Some of the subskills involved in comprehension, reading and writing are:

- Phonemic awareness: the ability to identify and manipulate the sounds in spoken words.
- Phonics: the relationship between sounds and letters in written words.
- Fluency: the ability to read text accurately, quickly and with expression.
- Vocabulary: the knowledge of words and their meanings.
- Grammar: the rules and conventions of language structure and usage.
- Text structure: the organization and features of different types of texts, such as narratives, reports, arguments, etc.
- Comprehension strategies: the techniques and skills that help readers understand and remember texts, such as predicting, questioning, summarizing, etc.
- Writing process: the steps and stages of planning, drafting, revising, editing and publishing written texts.
- Writing traits: the qualities and characteristics of effective writing, such as ideas, organization, voice, word choice, sentence fluency and conventions.
- Writing genres: the different purposes and forms of writing, such as narrative, informative, persuasive, etc.
