### Delhi 2013

- Delhi 2013 refers to the Delhi Legislative Assembly election that was held on 4 December 2013, with the result announced on 8 December resulting in formation of the Fifth Legislative Assembly of Delhi.
- The election was contested by various political parties, such as the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), the Indian National Congress (INC), the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP), the Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), and others.
- The election saw a high voter turnout of 66%, the highest for the state till date.
- The election result was a hung assembly, with no party getting a clear majority of 36 seats out of 70. The BJP won a plurality of 31 seats, closely followed by the AAP, which won 28 seats in its first election. The INC won only 8 seats, while the BSP and others won 3 seats.
- The AAP leader Arvind Kejriwal defeated the incumbent chief minister Sheila Dikshit of the INC by a margin of over 25,000 votes in the New Delhi constituency.
- The BJP initially declined to form the government, citing lack of numbers and support from other parties. The AAP also refused to form the government with the support of the INC, which offered unconditional support to the AAP. The AAP sought public opinion through various means, such as phone calls, SMS, public meetings, and online surveys, to decide whether to form the government or not.
- After receiving a positive response from the public, the AAP decided to form the government with the outside support of the INC. Kejriwal was sworn in as the seventh chief minister of Delhi on 28 December 2013, along with six other ministers from the AAP.
- The AAP government lasted for 49 days, during which it implemented some of its key promises, such as providing free water, reducing electricity tariffs, ordering an audit of power distribution companies, setting up an anti-corruption helpline, and passing the Jan Lokpal Bill in the assembly. However, the government also faced several controversies, such as the midnight raid by the law minister Somnath Bharti against some African women, the protest by Kejriwal and his ministers outside the Rail Bhawan, and the refusal of the Lieutenant Governor to approve the Jan Lokpal Bill.
- The AAP government resigned on 14 February 2014, after the Jan Lokpal Bill was not allowed to be tabled in the assembly by the BJP and the INC. Kejriwal accused the two parties of colluding to protect the corrupt and said that he would seek a fresh mandate from the people.
- The Delhi assembly was kept under suspended animation for almost a year, until the Supreme Court ordered fresh elections in November 2014. The elections were held on 7 February 2015, and the results were announced on 10 February 2015. The AAP won a landslide victory, securing 67 out of 70 seats, while the BJP won only 3 seats and the INC none. Kejriwal became the chief minister of Delhi for the second time.
