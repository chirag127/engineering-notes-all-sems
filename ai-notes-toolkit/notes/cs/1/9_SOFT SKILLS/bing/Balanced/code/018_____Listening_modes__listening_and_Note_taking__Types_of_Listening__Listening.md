# Listening modes, listening and note taking, types of listening, listening

Listening is the process of receiving, understanding, evaluating, and responding to spoken or written messages. Listening is a vital skill for effective communication, learning, and relationship building. Listening can be done in different modes, depending on the purpose and context of the interaction. Here are some of the main listening modes and their characteristics:

## Informational listening
- This mode is used to gain and understand information, such as facts, data, instructions, or directions.
- The listener typically is a less active participant in the listening process and focuses on the content of the message rather than the speaker's emotions or intentions.
- One non-verbal signal that someone is in informational listening mode is that they are taking notes.
- Examples of informational listening include listening to a lecture, a podcast, a news report, or a manual.

## Critical listening
- This mode is used to form opinions, make judgments, or evaluate people and ideas.
- The listener is more active and engaged in the listening process and applies logic, reasoning, and analysis to the message.
- The listener also considers the speaker's credibility, bias, evidence, and arguments, and challenges or questions the message if necessary.
- Examples of critical listening include listening to a debate, a speech, a review, or a critique.

## Relational listening
- This mode is used to support another person or maintain a relationship.
- The listener is empathetic, attentive, and responsive to the speaker's emotions, needs, and concerns.
- The listener also uses verbal and non-verbal feedback, such as nodding, smiling, paraphrasing, or asking questions, to show interest and understanding.
- Examples of relational listening include listening to a friend, a family member, a partner, or a colleague.

## Listening for pleasure
- This mode is used to enjoy or appreciate the message, such as music, humor, or storytelling.
- The listener is relaxed, receptive, and immersed in the listening experience.
- The listener does not need to analyze, evaluate, or respond to the message, but simply enjoys the sounds, words, or emotions.
- Examples of listening for pleasure include listening to a song, a joke, a story, or a poem.

## Listening to discriminate
- This mode is used to distinguish between different sounds, words, or meanings.
- The listener is attentive, alert, and focused on the details and nuances of the message.
- The listener also compares and contrasts the message with other sounds, words, or meanings, and identifies similarities and differences.
- Examples of listening to discriminate include listening to a foreign language, a musical instrument, a dialect, or a code.

## Note taking
- Note taking is the practice of recording information from a spoken or written source, such as a lecture, a book, or a video.
- Note taking helps the listener to remember, understand, and organize the information, and to review it later.
- Note taking can be done in different ways, such as using outlines, bullet points, diagrams, charts, symbols, abbreviations, or summaries.
- Note taking can also be done using different tools, such as paper, pens, highlighters, sticky notes, laptops, tablets, or smartphones.

## Tips for effective listening and note taking
- Prepare for the listening task by setting a goal, choosing a suitable mode, and eliminating distractions.
- Pay attention to the speaker's verbal and non-verbal cues, such as tone, gestures, facial expressions, and eye contact.
- Use active listening techniques, such as asking questions, paraphrasing, summarizing, or giving feedback, to clarify and confirm your understanding.
- Take notes that are clear, concise, and relevant, and use your own words, symbols, or abbreviations to avoid plagiarism.
- Review your notes regularly and revise them if necessary, and use them to study, write, or present the information.
