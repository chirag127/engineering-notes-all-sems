# Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

Oral communication is the process of expressing ideas, feelings, opinions, or information through spoken words. Oral communication can take place in various contexts, such as interpersonal, group, public, or mass communication. Oral communication has several advantages and disadvantages compared to written communication. Some of the advantages are:

- It allows for immediate feedback and clarification.
- It can convey emotions and non-verbal cues more effectively.
- It can create rapport and trust between the speaker and the listener.
- It can be more persuasive and influential than written communication.

Some of the disadvantages are:

- It can be affected by noise, distractions, or interruptions.
- It can be misunderstood or misinterpreted due to lack of visual aids or written records.
- It can be influenced by the speaker's or the listener's biases, prejudices, or emotions.
- It can be less organized and structured than written communication.

Nuances and modes of speech delivery are two important aspects of oral communication that can affect its effectiveness and impact. Nuances refer to the subtle variations in meaning, tone, or expression that can convey different messages or impressions. Modes of speech delivery refer to the different ways of presenting oral communication, such as impromptu, extemporaneous, manuscript, or memorized. Each mode has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the purpose, audience, and situation of the communication. Some of the factors that can influence the choice of mode are:

- The level of preparation and practice required.
- The degree of spontaneity and flexibility allowed.
- The amount of control and accuracy desired.
- The level of confidence and comfort of the speaker.

The following table summarizes the main characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages of each mode of speech delivery:

| Mode | Characteristics | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Impromptu | The speaker delivers the speech without any prior preparation or notes. | It can be natural, authentic, and engaging. It can demonstrate the speaker's knowledge and expertise. | It can be disorganized, unclear, or inaccurate. It can cause anxiety and nervousness for the speaker. |
| Extemporaneous | The speaker prepares and practices the speech beforehand, but does not use a written script or notes. | It can be organized, coherent, and persuasive. It can allow for adaptation and interaction with the audience. | It can require a lot of time and effort to prepare and practice. It can be affected by memory lapses or distractions. |
| Manuscript | The speaker reads the speech from a written script or notes. | It can ensure accuracy, precision, and consistency. It can reduce the risk of forgetting or omitting important information. | It can be monotonous, boring, or unnatural. It can limit eye contact and non-verbal communication with the audience. |
| Memorized | The speaker recites the speech from memory without any notes. | It can be impressive, confident, and fluent. It can allow for more eye contact and non-verbal communication with the audience. | It can be rigid, inflexible, or artificial. It can be difficult to memorize and recall. It can be affected by memory lapses or distractions. |
