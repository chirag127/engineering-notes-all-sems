### 2. Demonstrate active listening with comprehension, and the ability to write clear and wellstructured emails and proposals.

Active listening is a skill that involves paying attention, reflecting, and responding to what the speaker is saying. It helps to build rapport, trust, and understanding with the speaker, and to avoid misunderstandings and conflicts. Active listening also improves comprehension, as it helps to clarify and retain the information that is being communicated.

Some of the benefits of active listening are:

- It shows respect and interest in the speaker and their message.
- It enhances the quality and effectiveness of communication and collaboration.
- It helps to identify and address the needs, concerns, and emotions of the speaker.
- It facilitates problem-solving and decision-making by gathering relevant information and feedback.
- It reduces errors and misunderstandings by verifying and summarizing the key points and details.

Some of the techniques of active listening are:

- Pay attention: Focus on the speaker and their message, and avoid distractions and interruptions. Maintain eye contact, nod, and use appropriate facial expressions and body language to show engagement and interest.
- Reflect: Paraphrase and repeat what the speaker has said in your own words, to show that you have understood and to confirm accuracy. Use phrases like "What I hear you saying is...", "So, you are saying that...", or "Let me see if I got this right...".
- Respond: Ask open-ended questions to elicit more information, clarification, or examples from the speaker. Use phrases like "Can you tell me more about...", "How do you feel about...", or "What do you think of...". Provide feedback and opinions in a respectful and constructive way, and avoid criticism, judgment, or advice. Use phrases like "I appreciate your perspective on...", "I agree with you on...", or "I understand your point of view, but I have a different opinion on...".
- Summarize: Review and highlight the main points and conclusions of the conversation, and check for agreement and understanding. Use phrases like "To summarize, we have discussed...", "The key takeaways from this conversation are...", or "Do we have a common understanding of...".

Writing clear and well-structured emails and proposals is another skill that involves communicating effectively and persuasively with the intended audience. It helps to convey the purpose, message, and action items of the email or proposal, and to achieve the desired outcome.

Some of the benefits of writing clear and well-structured emails and proposals are:

- It saves time and effort for both the sender and the receiver, as the email or proposal is easy to read, understand, and respond to.
- It builds credibility and professionalism, as the email or proposal is well-organized, concise, and error-free.
- It increases the chances of getting a positive response, as the email or proposal is tailored to the audience's needs, interests, and expectations.
- It fosters positive relationships and collaboration, as the email or proposal is respectful, courteous, and appreciative.

Some of the techniques of writing clear and well-structured emails and proposals are:

- Plan: Identify the purpose, audience, and goal of the email or proposal, and gather the relevant information and evidence to support your message. Outline the main points and sub-points, and organize them in a logical and coherent order.
- Write: Use a clear and concise language, and avoid jargon, slang, and abbreviations. Use a formal and polite tone, and address the receiver by their name and title. Use an appropriate salutation and closing, and sign your name and contact details. Use a descriptive and specific subject line, and include a greeting and an introduction. State the purpose and the main message of the email or proposal in the first paragraph, and provide the supporting details and evidence in the following paragraphs. Use transitions and headings to guide the reader and to create a clear structure. End with a summary and a call to action, and express your gratitude and appreciation.
- Revise: Proofread and edit your email or proposal for spelling, grammar, punctuation, and formatting errors. Check for clarity, accuracy, completeness, and consistency. Make sure your email or proposal is relevant, persuasive, and respectful. Ask for feedback from a colleague or a friend, and make the necessary changes.
