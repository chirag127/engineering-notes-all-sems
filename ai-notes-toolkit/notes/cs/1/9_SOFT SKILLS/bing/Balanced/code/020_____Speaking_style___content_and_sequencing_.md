### Speaking style ; content and sequencing

Speaking style refers to how a speaker communicates their message, such as their tone, pace, volume, and word choice. Content refers to what a speaker says, such as their main points, evidence, and examples. Sequencing refers to how a speaker organizes their content, such as their introduction, body, and conclusion.

Some points to consider when speaking are:

- Choose a style that suits your purpose, audience, and context. For example, if you are giving a formal presentation, you may want to use a more formal and authoritative style than if you are having a casual conversation.
- Use clear and concise language that is easy to understand and follow. Avoid jargon, slang, and filler words that may confuse or distract your listeners.
- Vary your style to keep your listeners engaged and interested. For example, you can use pauses, emphasis, questions, humor, and anecdotes to create contrast and highlight your key points.
- Adapt your style to feedback and cues from your listeners. For example, if you notice that your listeners are bored, confused, or disagreeing, you can adjust your style to address their concerns or questions.
- Plan your content and sequencing before you speak. For example, you can use an outline, a mind map, or a script to organize your ideas and structure your speech.
- Have a clear and specific goal for your speech. For example, you can use a thesis statement, a central question, or a problem-solution format to state your main message and purpose.
- Support your content with relevant and credible evidence and examples. For example, you can use facts, statistics, quotes, stories, or analogies to back up your claims and illustrate your points.
- Use transitions and signposts to guide your listeners through your speech. For example, you can use words, phrases, or gestures to indicate when you are moving from one point to another, summarizing, or concluding.
