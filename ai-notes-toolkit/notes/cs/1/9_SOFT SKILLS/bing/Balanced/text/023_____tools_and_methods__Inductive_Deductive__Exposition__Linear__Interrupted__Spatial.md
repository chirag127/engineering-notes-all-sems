### Tools and Methods: Inductive Deductive; Exposition; Linear; Interrupted; Spatial

- Inductive and deductive are two methods of reasoning that are based on different premises and lead to different conclusions. 
- Inductive reasoning moves from specific observations to broad generalizations, while deductive reasoning moves from general rules to specific cases. 
- Inductive reasoning aims at developing a theory, while deductive reasoning aims at testing an existing theory. 
- Inductive reasoning is more open-ended and exploratory, while deductive reasoning is more structured and logical. 
- Exposition is a method of writing that explains, informs, or describes a topic or idea. 
- Exposition can be organized in different ways, such as by definition, classification, comparison and contrast, cause and effect, or problem and solution. 
- Exposition can also use different modes of expression, such as narration, description, illustration, or argumentation. 
- Linear, interrupted, spatial, and chronological are some of the methods of paragraph writing that depend on the order of the topic sentence and the supporting sentences. 
- Linear method is when the topic sentence is placed at the beginning of the paragraph, followed by the supporting sentences in a logical sequence. 
- Interrupted method is when the topic sentence is placed in the middle or at the end of the paragraph, creating a suspense or a surprise for the reader. 
- Spatial method is when the supporting sentences describe the topic sentence in terms of its physical location, shape, size, or direction. 
- Chronological method is when the supporting sentences describe the topic sentence in terms of its time sequence, order of events, or history.
