### Comprehension, reading and writing

- Comprehension is the ability to understand and interpret written or spoken texts.
- Reading is the process of decoding written symbols and extracting meaning from them.
- Writing is the process of encoding thoughts and ideas into written symbols and conveying meaning to others.
- Comprehension, reading and writing are interrelated skills that develop through exposure, practice and feedback.
- Comprehension, reading and writing are essential for academic success, communication and lifelong learning.

Some of the subtopics that are related to comprehension, reading and writing are:

- Phonics: the relationship between sounds and letters in a language.
- Vocabulary: the words and their meanings in a language.
- Grammar: the rules and structures that govern how words are combined and used in a language.
- Fluency: the ability to read and write smoothly, accurately and with expression.
- Text structure: the organization and features of different types of texts, such as narratives, reports, arguments, etc.
- Text features: the elements that help readers navigate and understand a text, such as headings, captions, diagrams, etc.
- Comprehension strategies: the techniques that readers use to make sense of a text, such as predicting, questioning, summarizing, etc.
- Writing process: the steps that writers follow to plan, draft, revise, edit and publish their texts.
- Writing traits: the qualities that make a text effective, such as ideas, organization, voice, word choice, sentence fluency and conventions.
- Writing genres: the categories of texts that have specific purposes, audiences and formats, such as narratives, reports, arguments, etc.
