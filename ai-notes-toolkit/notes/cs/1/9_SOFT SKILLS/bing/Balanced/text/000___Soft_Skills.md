# Soft Skills

Soft skills are the attributes and personality traits that help employees interact with others and succeed in the workplace. They are often contrasted with hard skills, which are the technical or functional skills that are specific to a job or industry. Soft skills are more general and transferable across different roles and sectors. They are also sometimes called interpersonal skills, people skills, or emotional intelligence.

Some examples of soft skills are:

- Communication: The ability to express oneself clearly and effectively, both verbally and non-verbally, in various situations and with different audiences. Communication skills also include listening, giving and receiving feedback, and presenting information.
- Teamwork: The ability to collaborate and cooperate with others to achieve a common goal. Teamwork skills also include respecting diversity, resolving conflicts, delegating tasks, and supporting others.
- Problem-solving: The ability to identify, analyze, and find solutions to various challenges and issues. Problem-solving skills also include creativity, critical thinking, decision-making, and research.
- Time management: The ability to prioritize, plan, and organize one's work and personal activities in a way that maximizes productivity and efficiency. Time management skills also include setting goals, meeting deadlines, and avoiding procrastination.
- Adaptability: The ability to adjust and respond to changing circumstances and expectations. Adaptability skills also include flexibility, resilience, and learning from feedback and mistakes.
