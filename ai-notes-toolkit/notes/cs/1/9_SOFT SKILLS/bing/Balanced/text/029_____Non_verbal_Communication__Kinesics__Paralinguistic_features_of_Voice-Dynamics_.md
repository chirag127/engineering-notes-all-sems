### Non verbal Communication: Kinesics, Paralinguistic features of Voice-Dynamics

Non verbal communication is the process of sending and receiving messages without using words, either spoken or written. It can include gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, tone of voice, and other aspects of body language and vocalization. Non verbal communication can convey emotions, attitudes, intentions, and preferences, as well as complement, reinforce, or contradict verbal messages.

Kinesics is the study of body movements and facial expressions as a form of non verbal communication. It can include the following types of gestures:

- Emblems: gestures that have a direct verbal translation, such as nodding for yes or shaking the head for no.
- Illustrators: gestures that accompany speech and illustrate or emphasize what is being said, such as pointing or waving.
- Regulators: gestures that regulate the flow of conversation, such as raising the hand to signal a turn to speak or looking away to indicate disinterest.
- Adaptors: gestures that are used to manage emotions or cope with stress, such as biting the nails or tapping the foot.
- Affect displays: gestures that express feelings or emotions, such as smiling, frowning, or crying.

Head movements and posture are also part of kinesics and can convey information about the speaker's attitude, confidence, interest, and dominance. For example, tilting the head can indicate curiosity, leaning forward can show engagement, and crossing the arms can signal defensiveness.

Eye contact is another important aspect of kinesics, as it can communicate attention, interest, attraction, respect, or hostility. Eye contact can also regulate the turn-taking in a conversation, as well as convey feedback, such as nodding, blinking, or widening the eyes.

Facial expressions are the most expressive and universal form of kinesics, as they can convey a range of emotions, such as happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, or disgust. Facial expressions are often involuntary and hard to control, and can therefore reveal the true feelings of the speaker.

Paralinguistic features of voice-dynamics are the vocal aspects of non verbal communication that are separate from the actual words or language. They include the following elements:

- Voice quality: the distinctive characteristics of a person's voice, such as pitch, timbre, resonance, and breathiness.
- Rate: the speed at which a person speaks, which can indicate excitement, nervousness, boredom, or confidence.
- Pitch: the highness or lowness of a person's voice, which can indicate emotions, such as happiness, sadness, anger, or surprise.
- Volume: the loudness or softness of a person's voice, which can indicate intensity, enthusiasm, aggression, or submissiveness.
- Speaking style: the way a person speaks, such as using formal or informal language, slang, jargon, or dialect.
- Prosodic features: the variations in the tone, stress, and intonation of a person's voice, which can convey meaning, emphasis, or attitude.

Paralinguistic features of voice-dynamics can complement, modify, or contradict the verbal message, as well as express the speaker's personality, mood, or relationship with the listener. For example, a sarcastic remark can be indicated by a rising intonation, a question can be emphasized by a higher pitch, and a command can be delivered by a louder volume.
