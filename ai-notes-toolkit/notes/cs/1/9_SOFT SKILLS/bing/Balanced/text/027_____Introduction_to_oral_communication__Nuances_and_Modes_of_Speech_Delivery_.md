### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

Oral communication is the process of expressing ideas, feelings, opinions, or information through spoken words. Oral communication can take place in various contexts, such as interpersonal, group, public, or mass communication. Oral communication has several advantages and disadvantages compared to written communication. Some of the advantages are:

- It allows for immediate feedback and clarification.
- It can convey emotions and non-verbal cues more effectively.
- It can create rapport and trust between the speaker and the listener.
- It can be more persuasive and engaging than written communication.

Some of the disadvantages are:

- It can be affected by noise, distractions, or interruptions.
- It can be misunderstood or misinterpreted due to lack of visual aids or written records.
- It can be influenced by the speaker's or the listener's biases, prejudices, or emotions.
- It can be less organized and structured than written communication.

Nuances and modes of speech delivery are two important aspects of oral communication that can affect its quality and effectiveness. Nuances are the subtle variations in tone, pitch, volume, speed, or emphasis that can convey different meanings or emotions in speech. Modes of speech delivery are the different ways of presenting a speech, such as impromptu, extemporaneous, manuscript, or memorized. Each mode has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the purpose, audience, and occasion of the speech. Some of the advantages and disadvantages are:

- Impromptu speech is a speech that is delivered without any preparation or notes. It can be spontaneous, natural, and flexible, but it can also be disorganized, unclear, or unconvincing.
- Extemporaneous speech is a speech that is prepared and rehearsed in advance, but delivered with few or no notes. It can be organized, coherent, and persuasive, but it can also be affected by nervousness, forgetfulness, or lack of eye contact.
- Manuscript speech is a speech that is written out word for word and read from a paper or a teleprompter. It can be accurate, precise, and consistent, but it can also be monotonous, boring, or impersonal.
- Memorized speech is a speech that is written out word for word and recited from memory. It can be fluent, confident, and impressive, but it can also be rigid, unnatural, or robotic.
