### Techniques using Ted Talk Audio listening with script reading, Pronunciation

- Ted Talks are short, engaging, and informative presentations on various topics, delivered by experts or enthusiasts in their fields. They are available online, with audio and video recordings, as well as transcripts and subtitles in different languages.
- Listening to Ted Talks can help improve one's listening skills, vocabulary, comprehension, and general knowledge. Reading the transcripts or subtitles along with the audio can also help with spelling, grammar, and pronunciation.
- Some techniques to use Ted Talks for listening and pronunciation practice are:

  - Choose a topic that interests you, or that is related to your field of study or work. This will make the listening more enjoyable and relevant.
  - Before listening, preview the transcript or subtitles and look up any unfamiliar words or phrases. You can also read the summary or description of the talk to get an idea of the main points and purpose.
  - Listen to the talk once without reading the transcript or subtitles, and try to understand the main idea and the speaker's tone and attitude. You can also take notes of any keywords or details that you hear.
  - Listen to the talk again, this time with the transcript or subtitles, and check your understanding and notes. Pay attention to how the speaker pronounces the words, especially the stress, intonation, and rhythm. You can also highlight or underline any words or phrases that you want to remember or practice later.
  - Listen to the talk a third time, and try to repeat or shadow the speaker's words and sentences. You can pause the audio after each sentence or phrase, and repeat it aloud or in your head. Try to imitate the speaker's pronunciation, accent, and expression as closely as possible.
  - Review the talk and your notes, and summarize the main points and message of the talk in your own words. You can also write down any questions or comments that you have about the talk, or discuss it with a partner or a group.
  - Practice the words or phrases that you highlighted or underlined, and use them in your own sentences or contexts. You can also record yourself saying them and compare your pronunciation with the speaker's. You can also use online tools or apps to check your pronunciation, such as Youglish, Forvo, or Google Translate.
