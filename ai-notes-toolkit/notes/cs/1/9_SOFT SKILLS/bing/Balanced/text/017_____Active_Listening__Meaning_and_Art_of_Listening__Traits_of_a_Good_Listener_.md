### Active Listening: Meaning and Art of Listening, Traits of a Good Listener

- Active listening is a skill that involves paying full attention to the speaker, understanding their message, and responding appropriately.
- Active listening is different from passive listening, which is simply hearing what the speaker says without engaging with them or showing interest.
- Active listening is important for effective communication, building rapport, resolving conflicts, and learning new information.
- Active listening requires the following steps:
  - Prepare to listen: Clear your mind of distractions, avoid interruptions, and focus on the speaker.
  - Show interest: Use nonverbal cues such as eye contact, nodding, smiling, and leaning forward to indicate that you are listening and care about what the speaker is saying.
  - Listen attentively: Pay attention to the speaker's words, tone, body language, and emotions. Try to understand their perspective and avoid making assumptions or judgments.
  - Reflect and clarify: Paraphrase or summarize what the speaker said to check your understanding and show that you are listening. Ask open-ended questions to clarify any confusion or ambiguity.
  - Respond respectfully: Express your thoughts, feelings, opinions, or feedback in a respectful and constructive way. Acknowledge the speaker's point of view and avoid interrupting, criticizing, or arguing with them.
- Traits of a good listener include:
  - Empathy: The ability to put yourself in the speaker's shoes and understand their feelings and emotions.
  - Patience: The ability to listen without rushing, interrupting, or jumping to conclusions.
  - Open-mindedness: The ability to listen without bias, prejudice, or preconceived notions.
  - Curiosity: The ability to ask relevant and insightful questions to learn more about the speaker and their message.
  - Respect: The ability to listen without judging, mocking, or dismissing the speaker or their message.
