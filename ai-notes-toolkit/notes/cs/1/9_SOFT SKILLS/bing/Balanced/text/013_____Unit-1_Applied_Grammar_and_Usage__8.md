### Unit-1 Applied Grammar and Usage: 8

- This unit covers the following topics:
  - Subject-verb agreement
  - Pronoun-antecedent agreement
  - Parallelism
  - Modifiers
  - Sentence types and punctuation
- Subject-verb agreement is the rule that the verb must agree in number and person with the subject of a sentence. For example, "He runs fast" is correct, but "He run fast" is incorrect.
- Pronoun-antecedent agreement is the rule that the pronoun must agree in number, gender, and person with the noun or noun phrase that it refers to. For example, "She loves her dog" is correct, but "She loves his dog" is incorrect, unless the dog belongs to someone else.
- Parallelism is the rule that the elements of a sentence that are coordinated or compared must have the same grammatical form and structure. For example, "She likes reading, writing, and painting" is correct, but "She likes reading, to write, and painting" is incorrect.
- Modifiers are words or phrases that modify or describe other words or phrases in a sentence. They should be placed as close as possible to the word or phrase they modify, and they should not cause ambiguity or confusion. For example, "The man with the hat waved to me" is correct, but "Waving to me, the man with the hat left" is incorrect, because it is unclear who is waving.
