### Listening modes, listening and Note taking, Types of Listening, Listening

- Listening modes are the different ways of processing and understanding spoken information, depending on the purpose and context of listening.
- There are four main listening modes: attentive, responsive, selective, and active.
- Attentive listening is when the listener pays close attention to the speaker and tries to comprehend and remember the message without interrupting or judging.
- Responsive listening is when the listener provides feedback to the speaker, such as nodding, asking questions, or expressing agreement or disagreement.
- Selective listening is when the listener focuses on specific parts of the message that are relevant or interesting to them, and ignores or filters out the rest.
- Active listening is when the listener engages with the speaker and the message, by paraphrasing, summarizing, clarifying, or reflecting on what they hear.

- Listening and note taking are skills that help the listener to capture and retain the main points of a spoken message, especially in academic or professional settings.
- Some strategies for effective listening and note taking are:
  - Prepare before listening, by reviewing the topic, setting a goal, and predicting what the speaker might say.
  - Listen for the main ideas and supporting details, and use cues such as the speaker's tone, gestures, and transitions to identify them.
  - Take notes in an organized and concise way, using symbols, abbreviations, and outlines to save time and space.
  - Review and revise the notes after listening, by filling in the gaps, checking for accuracy, and highlighting the key points.

- Types of listening are the different purposes and contexts of listening, which require different skills and strategies from the listener.
- There are many types of listening, but some common ones are:
  - Informative listening, which is listening to learn or acquire new information or knowledge, such as in lectures, podcasts, or documentaries.
  - Critical listening, which is listening to evaluate or analyze the speaker's arguments, evidence, or credibility, such as in debates, speeches, or reviews.
  - Empathic listening, which is listening to understand or share the speaker's feelings, emotions, or experiences, such as in counseling, therapy, or friendship.
  - Appreciative listening, which is listening to enjoy or appreciate the speaker's style, creativity, or performance, such as in music, poetry, or storytelling.

- Listening is the process of receiving, attending, understanding, and responding to spoken messages.
- Listening is a complex and active skill that involves both physical and mental processes.
- Some benefits of listening are:
  - It improves communication and interpersonal skills, by enhancing the quality of interactions and relationships.
  - It enhances learning and academic performance, by increasing the retention and comprehension of information.
  - It develops critical thinking and problem-solving skills, by enabling the listener to evaluate and synthesize different perspectives and ideas.
  - It fosters empathy and compassion, by allowing the listener to connect and empathize with others' feelings and experiences.
