### 5. Students will be able build up personal traits that will make the transition from institution

- The transition from institution to the workplace or further education can be challenging for many students, especially those who lack the necessary personal traits to adapt and succeed in different environments.
- Some of the personal traits that can help students make a smooth and successful transition are:
  - Self-awareness: the ability to recognize one's strengths, weaknesses, interests, values, and goals, and to use this information to guide one's decisions and actions.
  - Self-regulation: the ability to manage one's emotions, impulses, and behaviors, and to cope with stress, challenges, and setbacks in a constructive way.
  - Self-motivation: the ability to set realistic and attainable goals, and to pursue them with persistence, enthusiasm, and optimism.
  - Self-efficacy: the belief in one's capabilities to achieve one's goals, and to overcome obstacles and difficulties.
  - Self-esteem: the sense of respect and confidence in one's worth and abilities, and the acceptance of one's flaws and limitations.
  - Self-reliance: the ability to act independently and responsibly, and to seek help and support when needed.
  - Self-improvement: the willingness to learn from feedback, mistakes, and experiences, and to seek new opportunities and challenges for personal and professional growth.
- Students can build up these personal traits by engaging in various activities and experiences, such as:
  - Participating in extracurricular activities, clubs, or volunteer work that match one's interests and values, and that provide opportunities to develop new skills, meet new people, and contribute to the community.
  - Seeking mentorship, guidance, or coaching from teachers, counselors, peers, or professionals who can offer advice, feedback, and support for one's goals and challenges.
  - Taking online courses, workshops, or seminars that can enhance one's knowledge, skills, or qualifications in a specific field or area of interest.
  - Joining professional associations, networks, or communities that can offer access to information, resources, or opportunities related to one's career or academic aspirations.
  - Seeking internships, apprenticeships, or part-time jobs that can provide practical experience, exposure, and connections in one's desired industry or sector.
  - Reflecting on one's achievements, challenges, and learnings, and setting new goals and action plans for the future.
