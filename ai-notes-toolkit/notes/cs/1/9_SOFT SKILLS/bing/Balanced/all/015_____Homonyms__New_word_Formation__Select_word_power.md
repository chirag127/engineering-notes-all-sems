# Homonyms, New word Formation, Select word power

## Homonyms
- Homonyms are words that have the same spelling and pronunciation but different meanings and origins.
- For example, the word "bat" can mean a flying mammal or a wooden club used in sports.
- Homonyms can be classified into two types: homographs and homophones.
- Homographs are words that have the same spelling but different meanings and pronunciations, such as "lead" (a metal) and "lead" (to guide).
- Homophones are words that have the same pronunciation but different spellings and meanings, such as "to", "too" and "two".
- Some examples of homonyms are:

| Word | Meaning 1 | Meaning 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bear | a large furry animal | to endure or carry |
| date | a sweet fruit | a day of the month or a romantic meeting |
| fair | just or impartial | a carnival or exhibition |
| lie | to recline or rest | to tell a falsehood |
| rose | a fragrant flower | the past tense of rise |

## New word Formation
- New word formation is the process of creating new words from existing words or parts of words.
- There are several methods of new word formation, such as:
  - Affixation: adding prefixes or suffixes to a word, such as "unhappy", "replay" or "teacher".
  - Compounding: combining two or more words to form a new word, such as "blackboard", "snowman" or "bookworm".
  - Blending: merging parts of two words to form a new word, such as "brunch", "smog" or "motel".
  - Clipping: shortening a word by deleting one or more syllables, such as "ad" (from advertisement), "flu" (from influenza) or "phone" (from telephone).
  - Acronym: forming a word from the initial letters of a phrase, such as "NASA" (from National Aeronautics and Space Administration), "LOL" (from laugh out loud) or "AIDS" (from acquired immune deficiency syndrome).
  - Conversion: changing the word class of a word without changing its form, such as "book" (a noun or a verb), "green" (an adjective or a noun) or "run" (a verb or a noun).

## Select word power
- Select word power is the ability to choose the most appropriate and effective words for a given context or purpose.
- Select word power can help to improve one's communication skills, writing skills and vocabulary.
- Some strategies to enhance one's select word power are:
  - Reading widely and regularly: reading different types of texts, such as books, magazines, newspapers, blogs, etc., can expose one to a variety of words and expressions, and help to learn their meanings, usage and connotations.
  - Using a dictionary and a thesaurus: using these tools can help to find the definitions, synonyms, antonyms, examples and origins of words, and to expand one's word choices and alternatives.
  - Learning word roots, prefixes and suffixes: learning these elements can help to understand the meanings and forms of many words, and to create new words by combining them.
  - Practicing word games and puzzles: playing games and solving puzzles, such as crosswords, anagrams, word searches, etc., can help to test and improve one's word knowledge, memory and creativity.
