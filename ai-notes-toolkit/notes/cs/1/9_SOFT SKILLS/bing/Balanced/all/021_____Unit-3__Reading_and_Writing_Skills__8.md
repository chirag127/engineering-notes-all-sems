# Unit-3: Reading and Writing Skills: 8

- This unit focuses on developing the reading and writing skills of the learners.
- Reading and writing are interrelated processes that involve comprehension, expression, and communication of ideas.
- Reading and writing skills can be improved by practicing various strategies, such as:

  - Previewing: This is the process of skimming a text to get a general idea of its main topic, purpose, and structure before reading it in detail.
  - Predicting: This is the process of making logical guesses about what will happen next or what information will be given in a text based on the clues given by the title, headings, pictures, etc.
  - Scanning: This is the process of quickly searching for specific information in a text, such as names, dates, numbers, etc.
  - Skimming: This is the process of reading a text quickly to get the main idea or the gist of it without paying attention to the details.
  - Inferring: This is the process of drawing conclusions or making interpretations based on the information given in a text and the prior knowledge of the reader.
  - Summarizing: This is the process of condensing the main points of a text into a shorter form using one's own words.
  - Paraphrasing: This is the process of rewriting a text using different words and expressions while keeping the same meaning and tone of the original text.
  - Synthesizing: This is the process of combining information from different sources or texts to create a new text or a new understanding of a topic.
  - Evaluating: This is the process of judging the quality, accuracy, relevance, and usefulness of a text based on certain criteria or standards.
  - Analyzing: This is the process of breaking down a text into its components, such as the main idea, supporting details, arguments, evidence, etc., and examining how they are related and organized.
  - Critiquing: This is the process of expressing one's opinion or perspective on a text, such as its strengths, weaknesses, biases, implications, etc., and providing reasons and evidence to support one's views.

- Reading and writing skills can be applied to various types of texts, such as:

  - Narrative texts: These are texts that tell a story or describe an event or experience, such as novels, short stories, biographies, memoirs, etc.
  - Descriptive texts: These are texts that provide details about a person, place, thing, or situation, such as essays, reports, profiles, etc.
  - Expository texts: These are texts that explain, inform, or teach about a topic, such as textbooks, articles, manuals, etc.
  - Persuasive texts: These are texts that persuade, convince, or influence the reader to accept a certain point of view, opinion, or action, such as advertisements, speeches, editorials, etc.
  - Argumentative texts: These are texts that present and support a claim or a position on a controversial issue, such as essays, debates, reviews, etc.
  - Creative texts: These are texts that express one's imagination, feelings, or emotions, such as poems, songs, stories, etc.

- Reading and writing skills can be assessed by various methods, such as:

  - Multiple-choice questions: These are questions that have four or more possible answers, and the learner has to choose the correct or the best one.
  - True/false questions: These are questions that have only two possible answers, and the learner has to decide whether the statement given is true or false.
  - Matching questions: These are questions that have two sets of items, and the learner has to match the items from one set to the items from the other set based on their relationship or similarity.
  - Fill-in-the-blank questions: These are questions that have a sentence or a paragraph with one or more words missing, and the learner has to supply the missing words.
  - Short-answer questions: These are questions that require the learner to write a brief response, usually one or a few sentences, to answer the question.
  - Essay questions: These are questions that require the learner to write a longer response, usually a paragraph or more, to answer the question or to express one's views on a topic.
