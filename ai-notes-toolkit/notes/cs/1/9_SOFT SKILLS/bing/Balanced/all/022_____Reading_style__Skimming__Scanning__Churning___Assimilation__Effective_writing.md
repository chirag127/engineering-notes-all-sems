# Reading style: Skimming; Scanning; Churning & Assimilation, Effective writing

## Skimming
- Skimming is a reading technique that involves quickly looking over a text to get a general idea of its main points and structure.
- Skimming can be useful for previewing a text before reading it in detail, or for reviewing a text after reading it thoroughly.
- Skimming can help to identify the main topic, the purpose, the audience, and the tone of a text.
- Skimming can also help to locate specific information, such as keywords, headings, subheadings, diagrams, charts, or summaries.
- Skimming can be done by reading the first and last sentences of each paragraph, or by reading only the highlighted or bolded words or phrases.

## Scanning
- Scanning is a reading technique that involves quickly searching for specific information or details in a text.
- Scanning can be useful for finding answers to questions, or for locating facts, figures, names, dates, or definitions.
- Scanning can help to save time and avoid unnecessary reading of irrelevant or redundant information.
- Scanning can be done by using the table of contents, the index, the glossary, or the references of a text, or by using visual cues, such as symbols, numbers, or colors.
- Scanning can also be done by skimming the text first to get a general idea of where the information might be located, and then reading more carefully the relevant sections.

## Churning
- Churning is a reading technique that involves repeatedly reading the same text or passage to improve comprehension or retention.
- Churning can be useful for memorizing information, or for clarifying difficult or confusing concepts or terms.
- Churning can help to reinforce learning and enhance understanding by exposing the reader to the same information from different perspectives or angles.
- Churning can be done by reading the text aloud, by paraphrasing or summarizing the text in one's own words, by writing notes or outlines of the text, or by asking and answering questions about the text.
- Churning can also be done by using different formats or media, such as audio, video, or graphics, to present the same information in different ways.

## Assimilation
- Assimilation is a reading technique that involves integrating new information from a text with one's prior knowledge and experience.
- Assimilation can be useful for developing critical thinking skills, or for applying or transferring information to new situations or contexts.
- Assimilation can help to create meaning and relevance by connecting the text to one's own life, interests, goals, or values.
- Assimilation can be done by relating the text to one's own background, culture, or worldview, by comparing and contrasting the text with other texts or sources, by evaluating the text's validity, reliability, or bias, or by synthesizing the text's main ideas or arguments.
- Assimilation can also be done by using the text as a basis for further inquiry, research, or action, by generating new questions, hypotheses, or solutions, or by creating new products or projects based on the text.

## Effective writing
- Effective writing is a writing technique that involves producing clear, coherent, and concise texts that communicate the intended message to the intended audience.
- Effective writing can be useful for achieving various purposes, such as informing, persuading, entertaining, or instructing.
- Effective writing can help to create a positive impression and a strong relationship with the reader, as well as to achieve the desired outcome or response.
- Effective writing can be done by following a writing process that involves planning, drafting, revising, editing, and proofreading, as well as by using appropriate writing strategies, such as brainstorming, outlining, researching, or peer reviewing.
- Effective writing can also be done by using appropriate writing conventions, such as grammar, spelling, punctuation, vocabulary, or style, as well as by using appropriate writing features, such as tone, voice, point of view, or structure.
