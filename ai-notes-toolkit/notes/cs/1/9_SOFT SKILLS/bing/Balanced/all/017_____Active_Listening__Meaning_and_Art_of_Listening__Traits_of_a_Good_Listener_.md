# Active Listening: Meaning and Art of Listening, Traits of a Good Listener

## Meaning of Active Listening
- Active listening is a skill that involves paying full attention to the speaker and showing interest in what they are saying.
- Active listening requires the listener to not only hear the words, but also understand the message, the emotions, and the intentions behind them.
- Active listening also involves giving feedback to the speaker, such as asking questions, paraphrasing, summarizing, or expressing agreement or disagreement.
- Active listening is important for effective communication, building rapport, resolving conflicts, and learning new information.

## Art of Listening
- The art of listening is the ability to listen with empathy, curiosity, and openness, without judging, interrupting, or imposing one's own opinions or solutions.
- The art of listening requires the listener to be aware of their own biases, assumptions, and emotions, and to avoid distractions and interruptions.
- The art of listening also requires the listener to use verbal and non-verbal cues to show interest and understanding, such as nodding, eye contact, facial expressions, and body language.
- The art of listening can enhance the quality of relationships, foster trust and respect, and create a positive and supportive environment.

## Traits of a Good Listener
- A good listener is someone who exhibits the following traits:
  - Attentiveness: A good listener pays attention to the speaker and does not let their mind wander or get distracted by other things.
  - Empathy: A good listener tries to understand the speaker's feelings, emotions, and perspectives, and shows compassion and care.
  - Open-mindedness: A good listener is willing to listen to different views and opinions, and does not judge or criticize the speaker.
  - Respect: A good listener respects the speaker's right to express themselves, and does not interrupt, argue, or dominate the conversation.
  - Feedback: A good listener provides constructive and supportive feedback to the speaker, such as asking relevant questions, paraphrasing, summarizing, or expressing agreement or disagreement.
