# Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

Oral communication is the process of expressing ideas, feelings, opinions, or information through spoken words. Oral communication can take place in various contexts, such as interpersonal, group, public, or mass communication. Oral communication can also have different purposes, such as informing, persuading, entertaining, or instructing.

Some of the nuances and modes of speech delivery are:

- **Tone**: The tone of voice is the expression of the speaker's attitude, emotion, or mood. Tone can convey confidence, enthusiasm, sincerity, sarcasm, anger, or boredom. Tone can also affect the listener's perception of the speaker's credibility, authority, or likability.
- **Pitch**: The pitch of voice is the highness or lowness of the sound. Pitch can indicate the speaker's level of interest, excitement, or urgency. Pitch can also help to emphasize certain words or phrases, or to create contrast or variety in the speech.
- **Volume**: The volume of voice is the loudness or softness of the sound. Volume can signal the speaker's degree of confidence, assertiveness, or passion. Volume can also help to capture the listener's attention, or to adjust to the size and acoustics of the room.
- **Rate**: The rate of speech is the speed at which the speaker talks. Rate can reflect the speaker's level of nervousness, preparedness, or enthusiasm. Rate can also help to create a sense of urgency, suspense, or humor in the speech.
- **Pause**: The pause is the deliberate silence or break in the speech. Pause can help the speaker to organize the thoughts, to emphasize a point, to create a dramatic effect, or to invite the listener's participation or feedback.
- **Articulation**: The articulation of speech is the clarity and accuracy of the pronunciation of the words. Articulation can affect the speaker's intelligibility, credibility, and professionalism. Articulation can also help to avoid confusion or misunderstanding of the message.
- **Fluency**: The fluency of speech is the smoothness and continuity of the delivery. Fluency can indicate the speaker's level of confidence, competence, or preparation. Fluency can also help to maintain the listener's interest and attention.
- **Nonverbal communication**: The nonverbal communication of speech is the use of body language, facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, or movements to enhance or complement the verbal message. Nonverbal communication can convey the speaker's personality, emotion, or attitude. Nonverbal communication can also help to establish rapport, trust, or credibility with the listener.

These are some of the aspects of oral communication that can influence the effectiveness and impact of the speech. By being aware of these nuances and modes of speech delivery, the speaker can improve the oral communication skills and achieve the desired outcomes.
