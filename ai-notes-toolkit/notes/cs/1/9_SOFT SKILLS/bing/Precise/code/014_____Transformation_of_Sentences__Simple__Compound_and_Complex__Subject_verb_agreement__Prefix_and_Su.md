### Transformation of Sentences: Simple, Compound and Complex, Subject-verb agreement, Prefix and Suffix, Antonyms, Synonyms, Homophones

#### Transformation of Sentences
- A sentence can be transformed by changing its structure while keeping its meaning the same.
- There are three types of sentences: simple, compound, and complex.
- A simple sentence contains only one independent clause.
- A compound sentence contains two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction.
- A complex sentence contains one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses.

#### Subject-verb agreement
- Subject-verb agreement refers to the grammatical rule that the verb must agree with the subject in number and person.
- Singular subjects take singular verbs, while plural subjects take plural verbs.
- The verb must also agree with the subject in person, meaning that if the subject is first, second, or third person, the verb must be in the corresponding form.

#### Prefix and Suffix
- A prefix is a group of letters added to the beginning of a word to change its meaning.
- A suffix is a group of letters added to the end of a word to change its meaning.
- Prefixes and suffixes are used to form new words or to change the grammatical function of a word.

#### Antonyms, Synonyms, Homophones
- Antonyms are words that have opposite meanings.
- Synonyms are words that have the same or similar meanings.
- Homophones are words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings.
