### 2. Demonstrate active listening with comprehension, and the ability to write clear and well-structured emails and proposals.

1. Active listening:
- Give the speaker your undivided attention. Make eye contact, don't look at your phone or other distractions, and listen to understand rather than just reply.
- Provide affirmation that you are listening through nodding or saying "uh-huh" periodically.
- Wait until the speaker is finished talking to reply. This allows you to fully comprehend their message and respond appropriately.
- Ask questions to clarify points or gain additional context. Paraphrase what the speaker said to confirm your understanding.

2. Writing clearly and concisely:
- Ensure your writing has a clear introduction, body, and conclusion. State the purpose and scope of your communication upfront.
- Use simple and straightforward language. Avoid unnecessary words and complex jargon.
- Organize your thoughts into a logical flow or structure. Headings and bullet points help convey this organization and make your writing easier to follow.
- Be concise but provide sufficient detail. Trim unnecessary words and sentences but do not sacrifice important context or information.

3. Structuring emails and proposals effectively:
- For emails, state the reason for your message in the first line of the subject and opening paragraph. Be direct and explicit about what you are communicating.
- For proposals, start with an executive summary that outlines the key points. Then dive into additional details in a way that builds a compelling case for your proposal. Use headings to separate major sections and keep content concise under each heading.
- Close with a clear call to action if relevant. Summarize next steps and thank the reader for their time or consideration.
