### 5. Students will be able build up personal traits that will make the transition from institution

Institutions are structured environments that provide a sense of order and direction. However, when students graduate, they enter the real world, which is much less structured, and they must adjust to it. In order to make the transition from institution to the real world, students need to develop certain personal traits. Here are some of the personal traits that students can build up to make this transition easier:

1. Adaptability: The real world is constantly changing, and students need to be able to adapt to the new situations they encounter. They should learn to be flexible and open-minded, and be willing to embrace change.

2. Problem-solving skills: In the real world, students will encounter many problems and challenges. They need to be able to identify problems, analyze them, and come up with effective solutions.

3. Time management: In the real world, time is a precious commodity. Students need to learn how to manage their time effectively, prioritize tasks, and avoid procrastination.

4. Communication skills: Communication is essential in the real world. Students need to learn how to communicate effectively, both verbally and in writing. They should also learn how to listen actively and respond appropriately.

5. Leadership skills: In the real world, students may need to take on leadership roles. They should learn how to motivate and inspire others, delegate tasks, and make decisions.

6. Self-discipline: In the real world, there is no one to remind students to do things. They need to learn how to be self-disciplined, set goals, and take responsibility for their actions.

7. Creativity: In the real world, students may encounter situations that require creative solutions. They should learn how to think creatively, generate new ideas, and take risks.

By building up these personal traits, students will be better prepared to make the transition from institution to the real world. They will be able to adapt to new situations, solve problems effectively, manage their time, communicate effectively, take on leadership roles, be self-disciplined, and think creatively. These traits will not only help them in their careers, but also in their personal lives.
