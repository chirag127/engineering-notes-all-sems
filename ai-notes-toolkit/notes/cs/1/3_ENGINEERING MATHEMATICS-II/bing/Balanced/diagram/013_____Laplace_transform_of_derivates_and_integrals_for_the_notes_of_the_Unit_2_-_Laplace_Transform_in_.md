### Laplace transform of derivatives and integrals

- Laplace transform is an integral transform that converts a function of a real variable (usually time) to a function of a complex variable (usually frequency).
- Laplace transform is useful for solving differential equations and integral equations, as well as analyzing systems such as electrical circuits, mechanical systems, and control systems.
- The Laplace transform of a function f(t) is defined as

  `L{f(t)} = F(s) = ∫∞0 f(t) e^(-st) dt`

  where s is a complex variable and the integral is taken over the positive real axis.
- The Laplace transform has some properties that make it easier to work with, such as linearity, scaling, shifting, differentiation, integration, convolution, and initial and final value theorems.
- The Laplace transform of a derivative of a function is given by

  `L{f'(t)} = sF(s) - f(0)`

  where f(0) is the initial value of f(t) at t = 0. This property can be generalized to higher order derivatives as well.
- The Laplace transform of an integral of a function is given by

  `L{∫t0 f(τ) dτ} = F(s)/s`

  where F(s) is the Laplace transform of f(t). This property can be used to solve integral equations of the form

  `f(t) = g(t) + ∫t0 k(t-τ) f(τ) dτ`

  where g(t) and k(t) are known functions.
