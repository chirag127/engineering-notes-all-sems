## Unit 3 - Sequence and Series

- A **sequence** is a list of numbers or objects that follow a certain rule or pattern. For example, 2, 4, 6, 8, ... is a sequence of even numbers.
- A **series** is the sum of the terms of a sequence. For example, 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + ... is a series of even numbers.
- There are different types of sequences and series, such as **arithmetic**, **geometric**, **harmonic**, **fibonacci**, etc. Each type has its own formula and properties.
- To find the **nth term** of a sequence, we need to know the first term and the common difference or ratio between the terms. For example, the nth term of an arithmetic sequence is given by a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)d, where a_1 is the first term and d is the common difference.
- To find the **sum** of a series, we need to know the number of terms and the first and last terms of the series. For example, the sum of an arithmetic series is given by S_n = n/2 (a_1 + a_n), where n is the number of terms, a_1 is the first term and a_n is the last term.
- Some series are **infinite**, meaning they have infinitely many terms. For example, 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... is an infinite geometric series. To find the sum of an infinite series, we need to check if the series is **convergent** or **divergent**. A series is convergent if the sum approaches a finite value as the number of terms increases. A series is divergent if the sum does not approach a finite value or does not exist.
- To check if a series is convergent or divergent, we can use various **tests**, such as the **ratio test**, the **root test**, the **integral test**, the **comparison test**, etc. Each test has its own conditions and steps to apply.
- Some series have special names and notations, such as the **sigma notation** (∑), the **factorial notation** (!), the **binomial notation** (nCr), etc. These notations help us to write and manipulate series more easily and compactly.
