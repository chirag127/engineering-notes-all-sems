# Convergence of Series

- A series is an expression of the form $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$, where $a_n$ is a sequence of terms.
- A series is convergent if the sequence of its partial sums $s_n = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i$ tends to a limit as $n$ goes to infinity . That is, $\lim_{n \to \infty} s_n = s$, where $s$ is a finite number.
- A series is divergent if the sequence of its partial sums does not have a finite limit. That is, $\lim_{n \to \infty} s_n$ does not exist, or is equal to $\pm \infty$.
- A series that is convergent is also said to be summable, and its limit is called the sum of the series.
- A series is absolutely convergent if the series of the absolute values of its terms is convergent. That is, $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |a_n|$ is convergent.
- A series that is absolutely convergent is also convergent, but the converse is not true. A series that is convergent but not absolutely convergent is called conditionally convergent.
- There are various tests and criteria to determine whether a series is convergent or divergent, such as the comparison test, the ratio test, the root test, the integral test, the alternating series test, etc.
