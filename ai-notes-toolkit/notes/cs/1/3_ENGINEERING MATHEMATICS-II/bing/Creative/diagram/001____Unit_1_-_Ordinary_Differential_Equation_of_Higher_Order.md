## Unit 1 - Ordinary Differential Equation of Higher Order

- An ordinary differential equation (ODE) is an equation that involves one or more derivatives of an unknown function with respect to a single independent variable.
- The order of an ODE is the highest order of the derivative that appears in the equation. For example, the equation `y'' + y = 0` is a second-order ODE, and the equation `y''' + y' = x` is a third-order ODE.
- A solution of an ODE is a function that satisfies the equation. For example, the function `y = sin(x)` is a solution of the equation `y'' + y = 0`.
- A general solution of an ODE is a solution that contains as many arbitrary constants as the order of the equation. For example, the general solution of the equation `y'' + y = 0` is `y = C1 sin(x) + C2 cos(x)`, where C1 and C2 are arbitrary constants.
- A particular solution of an ODE is a solution that is obtained by assigning specific values to the arbitrary constants in the general solution. For example, the particular solution of the equation `y'' + y = 0` with the initial conditions `y(0) = 1` and `y'(0) = 0` is `y = cos(x)`.
- A linear ODE is an ODE that can be written in the form `a_n(x) y^(n) + a_(n-1)(x) y^(n-1) + ... + a_1(x) y' + a_0(x) y = f(x)`, where `a_n(x), a_(n-1)(x), ..., a_0(x)` and `f(x)` are given functions of x, and `y^(n)` denotes the nth derivative of y with respect to x.
- A homogeneous linear ODE is a linear ODE with `f(x) = 0`. For example, the equation `y'' + y = 0` is a homogeneous linear ODE.
- A nonhomogeneous linear ODE is a linear ODE with `f(x) ≠ 0`. For example, the equation `y'' + y = x` is a nonhomogeneous linear ODE.
- A linear ODE of higher order can be solved by various methods, such as the method of undetermined coefficients, the method of variation of parameters, the method of reduction of order, and the method of power series.
- The method of undetermined coefficients is a method for finding a particular solution of a nonhomogeneous linear ODE with constant coefficients and a right-hand side that is a linear combination of exponential, sinusoidal, or polynomial functions. The method involves guessing a form of the particular solution based on the right-hand side, and then finding the coefficients by substituting the guess into the equation and equating the coefficients of the same terms on both sides.
- The method of variation of parameters is a method for finding a particular solution of a nonhomogeneous linear ODE with variable coefficients and a right-hand side that is any function of x. The method involves finding a general solution of the corresponding homogeneous equation, and then multiplying each term by an unknown function and setting the sum equal to the right-hand side. The unknown functions are then determined by solving a system of linear equations obtained by differentiating the equation and substituting the general solution.
- The method of reduction of order is a method for finding a general solution of a second-order linear ODE with variable coefficients and a missing y-term, that is, an equation of the form `y'' + p(x) y' + q(x) = 0`. The method involves finding one solution of the equation, and then substituting `y = v y_1`, where `y_1` is the known solution and `v` is an unknown function. The equation then reduces to a first-order linear ODE for `v`, which can be solved by the method of integrating factors.
- The method of power series is a method for finding a general solution of a linear ODE with variable coefficients and a right-hand side that is any function of x. The method involves assuming that the solution is a power series of the form `y = sum_(n=0)^∞ a_n x^n`, and then substituting the series into the equation and equating the coefficients of the same powers of x on both sides. The coefficients `a_n` are then determined by solving a recurrence relation.
