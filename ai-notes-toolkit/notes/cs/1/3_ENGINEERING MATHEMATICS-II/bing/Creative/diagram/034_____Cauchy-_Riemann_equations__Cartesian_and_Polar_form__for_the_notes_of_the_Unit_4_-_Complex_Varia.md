### Cauchy-Riemann equations (Cartesian and Polar form)

- The Cauchy-Riemann equations are a system of two partial differential equations that form a necessary and sufficient condition for a complex function to be holomorphic (complex differentiable) .
- A complex function f(z) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y) is holomorphic if and only if it satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations:
  - (1a) `u_x = v_y`
  - (1b) `u_y = -v_x`
  - where `u_x`, `u_y`, `v_x`, and `v_y` are the partial derivatives of u and v with respect to x and y, respectively.
- The Cauchy-Riemann equations can also be written in polar coordinates as:
  - (2a) `u_r = (1/r)v_θ`
  - (2b) `u_θ = -(1/r)v_r`
  - where `u_r`, `u_θ`, `v_r`, and `v_θ` are the partial derivatives of u and v with respect to r and θ, respectively, and r and θ are related to x and y by `x = r cos θ` and `y = r sin θ`.
- The Cauchy-Riemann equations allow us to check if a complex function has a complex derivative and to compute that derivative. If f(z) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y) is holomorphic, then its complex derivative is given by:
  - (3) `f'(z) = u_x + iv_x = v_y - iu_y`
  - or, in polar coordinates, by:
  - (4) `f'(z) = (u_r + iv_r)e^(iθ) = (v_θ - iu_θ)e^(iθ)`
- The Cauchy-Riemann equations also imply some important properties of holomorphic functions, such as the harmonic property, the conformal property, and the Cauchy integral formula  .
