
### 15. Determination of Rate Constant of Hydrolysis of Esters

1. Hydrolysis of esters is a chemical reaction where an ester molecule is broken down into an alcohol and an acid.

2. The rate constant of hydrolysis of esters is a measure of how quickly the reaction occurs. It is typically expressed in terms of the number of moles of ester that are broken down per second.

3. The rate constant can be determined experimentally by measuring the concentration of ester molecules over time.

4. The rate constant is affected by several factors, including the temperature and the presence of a catalyst.

5. The rate constant can be used to calculate the rate of hydrolysis of an ester at different temperatures and in the presence of a catalyst.

6. The rate constant can also be used to determine the half-life of an ester, which is the amount of time it takes for half of the ester molecules to be broken down.
