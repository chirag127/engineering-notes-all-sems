
### 4. To enable the students to understand about the preparation of different resins

1. Resin is a natural or synthetic organic compound that is typically viscous, transparent, and soluble in organic solvents.
2. Resins are typically formed through the polymerization of monomers and can be either thermoplastic or thermosetting.
3. Thermosetting resins are further classified into two types: condensation and addition.
4. Condensation resins are formed through the reaction of two or more molecules with the elimination of a small molecule like water or alcohol. Examples include phenolic and epoxy resins.
5. Addition resins are formed through the reaction of two or more molecules without the elimination of a small molecule. Examples include polyurethanes and acrylics.
6. Resins can be prepared from a variety of sources such as plants, animals, minerals, and synthetic materials.
7. Synthetic resins are produced in a laboratory by combining two or more molecules. Examples include polyvinyl chloride (PVC) and polyethylene terephthalate (PET).
8. Resins can be used in a variety of applications such as adhesives, coatings, and composites.
