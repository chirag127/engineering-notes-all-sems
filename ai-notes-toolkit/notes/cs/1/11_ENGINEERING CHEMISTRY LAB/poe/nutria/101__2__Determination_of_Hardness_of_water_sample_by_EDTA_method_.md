
### 2. Determination of Hardness of Water Sample by EDTA Method

1. Hardness of water is a measure of the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium ions present in the water.

2. EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can be used to determine the hardness of a water sample.

3. EDTA forms a complex with the calcium and magnesium ions, preventing them from forming precipitates.

4. The amount of EDTA required to form a complex with the calcium and magnesium ions is proportional to the total hardness of the sample.

5. The EDTA method of determining water hardness involves titrating the water sample with a known concentration of EDTA solution.

6. The endpoint of the titration is determined by the use of an indicator, such as Eriochrome Black T, which changes color when the sample is free of calcium and magnesium ions.

7. The total hardness of the sample is then calculated by multiplying the volume of EDTA used in the titration by the concentration of the EDTA solution.
