
### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

1. Calibration is the process of comparing a measurement instrument or system to a known standard to determine its accuracy and precision.
2. The purpose of calibration is to ensure that the instrument or system is providing accurate and reliable readings.
3. Analytical equipment and apparatus may include, but are not limited to, balances, pH meters, spectrophotometers, chromatographs, refractometers, and thermometers.
4. Calibration should be performed regularly in order to ensure accuracy and precision of the instrument or system.
5. The frequency of calibration depends on the type of instrument or system, the environment in which it is used, and the accuracy required.
6. Calibration should be performed by a qualified technician or engineer and should follow the manufacturer’s instructions.
7. The calibration procedure should include a check of the instrument or system’s accuracy, precision, linearity, and repeatability.
8. The results of the calibration should be documented and used to determine the performance of the instrument or system.
9. If the instrument or system fails to meet the requirements, it should be adjusted or repaired as necessary.
