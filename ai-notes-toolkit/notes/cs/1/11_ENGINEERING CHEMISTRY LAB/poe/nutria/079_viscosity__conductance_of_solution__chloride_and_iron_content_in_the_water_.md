
# Viscosity, Conductance of Solution, Chloride and Iron Content in Water

- Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow. It is defined as the ratio of shear stress to the rate of shear strain. Water has a relatively low viscosity, meaning it flows easily.
- Conductance is a measure of the ease with which electricity can flow through a solution. It is the reciprocal of resistance and is usually measured in siemens per meter (S/m). The conductance of a solution is affected by the presence of ions, such as chloride and iron, which can increase electrical conductivity.
- Chloride is an anion found in many natural waters, and its concentration can affect the conductivity of a solution. Chloride concentrations in water can range from 0.1 to 500 mg/L.
- Iron is another ion found in natural waters. It is present in both ferrous (Fe2+) and ferric (Fe3+) forms. Iron can affect the taste and color of water, as well as its electrical conductivity. Iron concentrations in water can range from 0.01 to 20 mg/L.
