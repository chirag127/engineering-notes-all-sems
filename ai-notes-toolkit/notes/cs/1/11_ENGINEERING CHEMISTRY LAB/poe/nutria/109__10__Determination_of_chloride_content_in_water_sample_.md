
### 10. Determination of Chloride Content in Water Sample
1. Chloride can be determined in water samples using a variety of methods, including titration, potentiometric methods, and colorimetric methods.
2. Titration is a method that involves the addition of a known quantity of a reagent to a sample of water to determine the amount of chloride present.
3. Potentiometric methods involve the use of electrodes to measure the electrical potential of the sample, which can then be used to calculate the amount of chloride present.
4. Colorimetric methods involve the use of a colorimetric reagent that reacts with the chloride in the sample to produce a color change that can be measured.
5. In order to obtain accurate results, it is important to ensure that the sample is free of interfering substances and that the sample is prepared correctly.
6. The sample should be filtered to remove any solid particles that may interfere with the analysis.
7. The sample should also be acidified to ensure that any carbonates present are converted to chloride before analysis.
8. The sample should also be diluted to ensure that the concentration of chloride is within the range of the method being used.
9. Once the sample has been prepared, the method chosen should be followed to determine the amount of chloride present in the sample.
10. The results should then be recorded and the sample discarded in accordance with local regulations.
