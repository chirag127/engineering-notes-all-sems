
### 10. Determination of Chloride Content in Water Sample

1. Chloride is an anion found in many salts, such as sodium chloride, and is an important constituent of seawater.

2. Chloride can be measured in water samples using a variety of methods, including titration and ion chromatography.

3. The most commonly used method for determining chloride content in water samples is the Mohr titration.

4. The Mohr titration involves the addition of silver nitrate to a water sample, which produces a silver chloride precipitate.

5. The amount of silver chloride precipitate is then titrated with a standard sodium thiosulfate solution to determine the amount of chloride present in the sample.

6. The amount of chloride present in the sample is then calculated using the following equation:

Chloride (mg/L) = (Titration Volume (mL) x Normality of Sodium Thiosulfate (N)) / Sample Volume (mL)

7. The result of the titration is then compared to the standard chloride concentration for the water sample.

8. The standard chloride concentration for water samples is typically between 250 and 500 mg/L.

9. If the chloride concentration is higher than the standard, it may indicate contamination from industrial or agricultural sources.

10. It is important to test water samples regularly to ensure that the chloride concentration is within the standard range.
