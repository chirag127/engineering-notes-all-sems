
### 8. Determination of the Strength of Potassium Dichromate Using Internal Indicator

1. Potassium dichromate is a strong oxidizing agent and is often used as an internal indicator in titrations.

2. To determine the strength of potassium dichromate, a titration is performed using a standard solution of sodium thiosulphate.

3. The titration is conducted by adding a known volume of sodium thiosulphate to a measured amount of potassium dichromate.

4. The endpoint of the titration is determined by observing the colour change of the potassium dichromate solution.

5. The colour change occurs when the amount of sodium thiosulphate added is equal to the amount of potassium dichromate initially added.

6. The strength of the potassium dichromate solution can then be calculated using the following equation:

`Strength (g/L) = (Volume of sodium thiosulphate used (mL) x Molarity of sodium thiosulphate (mol/L)) / (Weight (g) of potassium dichromate used)`
