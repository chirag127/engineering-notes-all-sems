### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

Bleaching powder is one of the most commonly used bleaching agents in various industries, including textile, paper, and water treatment. The active ingredient in bleaching powder is available chlorine, which is responsible for its bleaching action. The determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder is essential to ensure the quality and effectiveness of the product. The following points discuss the procedure for the determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder:

1. Principle: The determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder is based on the reaction of available chlorine with potassium iodide in the presence of an acid. The liberated iodine is then titrated against a standardized solution of sodium thiosulfate.

2. Reagents:
   - Bleaching powder
   - Potassium iodide solution
   - Dilute hydrochloric acid
   - Starch solution
   - Standardized sodium thiosulfate solution

3. Procedure:
   - Weigh accurately about 2 grams of bleaching powder and transfer it to a conical flask.
   - Add 50 ml of distilled water and 10 ml of dilute hydrochloric acid to the flask.
   - Swirl the contents of the flask until the bleaching powder dissolves completely.
   - Add 1 ml of potassium iodide solution to the flask and swirl again.
   - Allow the mixture to stand in the dark for 5 minutes.
   - Titrate the liberated iodine with standardized sodium thiosulfate solution until the yellow color disappears.
   - Add 1 ml of starch solution to the flask and continue titrating until the blue color disappears.
   - Note the volume of sodium thiosulfate solution used.

4. Calculation:
   - Calculate the amount of available chlorine in the bleaching powder using the following formula:
   Available Chlorine (%) = (Volume of sodium thiosulfate solution used x Normality of sodium thiosulfate solution x 0.003546)/Weight of bleaching powder taken

5. Precautions:
   - The bleaching powder should be stored in a dry place.
   - The potassium iodide solution should be freshly prepared.
   - The titration should be carried out in the dark.
   - The standardized sodium thiosulfate solution should be freshly prepared and standardized before use.

6. Sources of error:
   - Loss of available chlorine due to exposure to air.
   - Incomplete dissolution of bleaching powder.
   - Over-titration of the liberated iodine.

The determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder is a crucial step in ensuring the quality and effectiveness of the product. It is essential to follow the procedure carefully and take necessary precautions to obtain accurate results.
