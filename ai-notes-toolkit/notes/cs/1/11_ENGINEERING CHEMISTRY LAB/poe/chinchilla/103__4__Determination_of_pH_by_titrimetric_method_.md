### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

In analytical chemistry, pH is an essential parameter to determine the acidity or basicity of a solution. The pH value is measured on a scale from 0 to 14, with 7 being neutral, below 7 acidic and above 7 basic.

Titrimetric method is a widely used analytical technique to determine the pH of a solution. In this method, a standard solution of an acid or base is added to the solution whose pH is to be determined until the equivalence point is reached. The equivalence point is the point at which the acid and base react completely, and the pH of the solution is determined by the amount of acid or base added.

The following are the steps involved in determining the pH of a solution using titrimetric method:

1. Preparation of the solution: The solution whose pH is to be determined should be prepared and standardized according to the requirements of the analysis method.

2. Selection of the titrant: The titrant is the standard solution of an acid or base that is added to the sample solution. The titrant should be selected based on the pH range of the sample solution.

3. Calibration of the pH meter: The pH meter should be calibrated using standard buffer solutions to ensure accurate pH measurement.

4. Titration of the solution: The titration should be performed by slowly adding the titrant to the sample solution while continuously stirring the mixture. The pH of the solution should be measured using a pH meter after each addition of the titrant.

5. Determination of equivalence point: The equivalence point is the point at which the pH of the solution changes abruptly. This point can be determined by plotting the pH values against the volume of the titrant added and observing the point of inflection on the graph.

6. Calculation of pH: The pH of the solution can be calculated using the equation:

pH = pKa + log ([A-]/[HA])

where pKa is the dissociation constant of the acid, [A-] is the concentration of the conjugate base, and [HA] is the concentration of the acid.

In conclusion, the titrimetric method is a reliable and accurate technique for determining the pH of a solution. The method requires careful preparation and calibration of equipment, and precise measurement of the titrant to reach the equivalence point. The calculation of pH requires knowledge of the dissociation constant and concentrations of the acid and conjugate base.
