### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of Water Sample

Alkalinity is an important parameter of water quality, which refers to the water's ability to neutralize acids. It is determined by measuring the concentration of alkaline compounds in the water sample, such as bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides.

The determination of alkalinity of water sample is an essential procedure in water treatment and monitoring. Here are the steps involved in the process:

1. Collect a representative water sample from the source and store it in a clean container.

2. Add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator to the sample. The indicator will turn pink if the sample has a pH greater than 8.3.

3. Titrate the sample with a standard acid solution (usually sulfuric acid) until the pink color disappears. This is the endpoint of the titration.

4. Record the volume of acid solution used. This is called the alkalinity titration value.

5. Repeat the titration process using a methyl orange indicator instead of phenolphthalein. The endpoint of the titration is indicated by a color change from yellow to orange.

6. Record the volume of acid solution used. This is called the total acidity titration value.

7. Calculate the alkalinity of the water sample by subtracting the total acidity titration value from the alkalinity titration value.

Alkalinity is usually expressed in terms of milligrams of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) per liter of water. The alkalinity levels of water samples can vary depending on factors such as the source of the water, the geographical location, and the time of year.

In conclusion, the determination of alkalinity of water sample is a crucial analysis for assessing the quality of water. By following the steps outlined above, the alkalinity of a water sample can be accurately determined, providing valuable information for water treatment and monitoring.
