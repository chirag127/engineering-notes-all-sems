# 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters

In this experiment, we will determine the rate constant of hydrolysis of an ester by monitoring the change in concentration of the ester over time. The hydrolysis of an ester is a second-order reaction, and the rate of reaction can be expressed as:

rate = k [ester] [H2O]

where k is the rate constant, [ester] is the concentration of the ester, and [H2O] is the concentration of water.

## Experimental Procedure

1. Prepare a solution of the ester by adding a known amount of the ester to a fixed volume of water in a flask. Record the initial concentration of the ester solution.

2. Start a timer and begin monitoring the concentration of the ester solution at regular intervals. This can be done using a colorimeter or spectrophotometer.

3. At each time interval, take a small sample of the solution and measure its absorbance. Use Beer-Lambert law to calculate the concentration of ester at each time interval.

4. Plot a graph of ln([ester]t/[ester]0) against time, where [ester]t is the concentration of ester at time t and [ester]0 is the initial concentration of ester. The slope of the line will give the rate constant (k).

5. Repeat the experiment with different initial concentrations of the ester, keeping the volume of water constant.

6. Calculate the average value of the rate constant (k) from the different experiments.

## Precautions

1. The flask and all equipment used in the experiment should be thoroughly cleaned and dried before use.

2. The temperature of the reaction should be kept constant throughout the experiment.

3. The concentration of the ester solution should be accurately measured before starting the experiment.

4. The spectrophotometer or colorimeter should be calibrated before use to ensure accurate measurements.

5. The experiment should be repeated several times to ensure accuracy of the results.

## Conclusion

The rate constant of hydrolysis of an ester can be determined by monitoring the change in concentration of the ester over time. The rate constant can be calculated from the slope of a graph of ln([ester]t/[ester]0) against time. By repeating the experiment with different initial concentrations of the ester, the average value of the rate constant can be calculated with greater accuracy.
