### 8. Determination of the Strength of Potassium Dichromate Using Internal Indicator

Potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7) is a common oxidizing agent used in various chemical reactions. Its strength can be determined using an internal indicator, which is a substance that changes color in response to the presence of an oxidizing or reducing agent.

Here are the steps involved in determining the strength of potassium dichromate using an internal indicator:

1. Preparation of the solution: Dissolve a known quantity of potassium dichromate in distilled water to obtain a standard solution. The concentration of the solution can be calculated using a suitable analytical method.

2. Selection of the internal indicator: Choose an appropriate internal indicator that changes color in the presence of potassium dichromate. In this experiment, the internal indicator used is ferrous ammonium sulfate, which changes from green to brown in the presence of potassium dichromate.

3. Titration of the solution: Add a few drops of the internal indicator to the standard solution of potassium dichromate. The solution will turn green due to the presence of ferrous ammonium sulfate. Next, add a reducing agent, such as a solution of oxalic acid, dropwise to the potassium dichromate solution until the green color disappears and the solution turns brown.

4. Calculation of the strength of potassium dichromate: The strength of potassium dichromate can be calculated using the following formula:

Strength of K2Cr2O7 = (Volume of oxalic acid solution x Normality of oxalic acid solution x Equivalent weight of K2Cr2O7) / Volume of K2Cr2O7 solution

In this formula, the equivalent weight of K2Cr2O7 is 294 g/mol, and the normality of oxalic acid solution can be calculated using its molarity and the number of hydrogens it can donate.

5. Repeat the experiment: To ensure accuracy, the experiment should be repeated at least three times, and the average value of the strength of potassium dichromate should be calculated.
