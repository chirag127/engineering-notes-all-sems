### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of Water Sample

Alkalinity is an important parameter that indicates the ability of water to neutralize acids. It is a measure of the amount of alkaline substances present in water, such as bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions. The alkalinity of water is important in many applications, such as agriculture, industry, and domestic use. The determination of alkalinity is therefore an important test in the analysis of water quality.

The following are the steps involved in the determination of alkalinity of a water sample:

1. Collect the water sample: Collect a representative sample of the water to be tested in a clean container. The amount of water required for the test depends on the method used for the determination of alkalinity.

2. Add the indicator: Add a few drops of the indicator to the water sample. The most commonly used indicator for the determination of alkalinity is phenolphthalein. The indicator changes color to pink when the water sample reaches a pH of around 8.3.

3. Titrate the water sample: Titrate the water sample with a standardized acid solution until the endpoint is reached. The endpoint is the point at which the indicator changes color. In the case of phenolphthalein, the endpoint is reached when the water sample turns from pink to colorless.

4. Record the volume of acid used: Record the volume of the acid solution used to reach the endpoint. This volume is used to calculate the alkalinity of the water sample.

5. Calculate the alkalinity: Calculate the alkalinity of the water sample using the formula:

Alkalinity = (Volume of acid used x Normality of acid) x 50 / Volume of water sample

where:
- Volume of acid used is the volume of the standardized acid solution used to reach the endpoint.
- Normality of acid is the concentration of the standardized acid solution in normal units.
- Volume of water sample is the volume of the water sample used for the test in milliliters.

6. Interpret the results: The alkalinity of the water sample is expressed in units of mg/L or ppm of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). The acceptable range of alkalinity for drinking water is between 20-200 ppm.

In conclusion, the determination of alkalinity is an important test in the analysis of water quality. It is a measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids and is important in many applications. The steps involved in the determination of alkalinity are simple and straightforward, and the results can be easily interpreted.
