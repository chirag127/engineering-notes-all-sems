### 1. To enable the students to understand about the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments.

In this section, we will cover the basic concepts and principles of analytical instruments. Understanding these fundamental concepts is crucial for interpreting and analyzing experimental data accurately. 

Here are the key points that we will cover:

- **Measurement**: Analytical instruments are designed to measure physical or chemical properties of a sample, such as mass, volume, temperature, pH, conductivity, etc.

- **Accuracy**: Accuracy is the ability of an instrument to provide measurements that are close to the true value. It is affected by factors such as calibration, sensitivity, and precision.

- **Precision**: Precision is the degree of repeatability or reproducibility of a measurement. It is affected by factors such as instrument design, sample preparation, and data analysis.

- **Sensitivity**: Sensitivity is the smallest detectable change in the measured property. It is affected by factors such as signal-to-noise ratio, resolution, and dynamic range.

- **Selectivity**: Selectivity is the ability of an instrument to measure a specific property in the presence of other interfering substances. It is affected by factors such as sample preparation, analytical method, and instrument design.

- **Signal**: The signal is the measurable response of the instrument to the sample. It can be a physical, chemical, or electrical property.

- **Noise**: Noise is the random variation in the signal that is not related to the sample. It can be caused by external factors such as temperature, humidity, or electromagnetic interference.

- **Calibration**: Calibration is the process of adjusting the instrument to ensure accurate and precise measurements. It involves comparing the instrument response to a known standard or reference material.

- **Validation**: Validation is the process of verifying that the instrument and analytical method are suitable for the intended use. It involves testing the instrument performance on a range of samples with known properties.

By understanding these fundamental concepts, students will be able to select the appropriate analytical instrument for a given application, design experiments, and interpret the experimental results accurately.
