### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of Water Sample

Alkalinity is an important parameter that indicates the buffering capacity of water. It is defined as the ability of water to neutralize acids. Alkalinity is a critical parameter to be determined in many fields, including environmental science, water treatment, and industrial processes. In this section, we will discuss the determination of alkalinity of a water sample.

The alkalinity of water can be determined by titration of the water sample with a strong acid, such as hydrochloric acid (HCl) or sulfuric acid (H2SO4). The endpoint of the titration is determined by using a pH indicator. The most commonly used pH indicators for the determination of alkalinity are phenolphthalein and methyl orange.

The following are the steps involved in the determination of alkalinity of a water sample:

1. Take a water sample of known volume in a clean and dry conical flask.
2. Add a few drops of the pH indicator (phenolphthalein or methyl orange) to the water sample.
3. Titrate the water sample with a standardized solution of HCl or H2SO4. The acid should be added slowly with constant stirring until the endpoint is reached.
4. The endpoint is reached when the color of the pH indicator changes. For phenolphthalein, the endpoint is reached when the color changes from pink to colorless. For methyl orange, the endpoint is reached when the color changes from yellow to red.
5. Note down the volume of the acid used for titration.
6. Calculate the alkalinity of the water sample using the following formula:

   Alkalinity = (Volume of acid used x Normality of acid x 50,000) / Volume of water sample

   Where,
   Normality of acid = (Molarity of acid x Number of hydrogen ions released by the acid)

7. The alkalinity of the water sample is expressed in terms of mg/L (milligrams per liter) or ppm (parts per million) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) equivalent.

In conclusion, the determination of alkalinity of a water sample is an important analytical procedure that provides valuable information about the buffering capacity of water. It is widely used in various fields, including environmental science, water treatment, and industrial processes. The procedure involves titration of the water sample with a strong acid and using a pH indicator to determine the endpoint of the titration. The alkalinity of the water sample is then calculated using a formula and expressed in terms of mg/L or ppm of calcium carbonate equivalent.
