### Formaldehyde

- Formaldehyde is a colorless gas with a pungent odor.
- It is used in the production of various materials such as plastics, textiles, and resins.
- It is also used as a disinfectant and preservative in many products.
- Formaldehyde can cause irritation to the eyes, nose, and throat, and can even lead to cancer in high concentrations.
- The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies formaldehyde as a human carcinogen.

### Urea Formaldehyde Resin

- Urea formaldehyde resin is a synthetic material that is commonly used as an adhesive and binder in the production of particleboard, plywood, and other composite wood products.
- It is also used as a coating and finish for various materials.
- Urea formaldehyde resin is known for its high strength and durability, making it a popular choice for construction and woodworking applications.
- However, it can release formaldehyde gas over time, which can cause health problems such as respiratory irritation and cancer.

### Adipic Acid

- Adipic acid is a dicarboxylic acid that is used in the production of nylon, polyurethane, and other synthetic materials.
- It is also used as a food additive to enhance the flavor and texture of certain foods.
- Adipic acid is considered safe for use in food and other consumer products, although excessive consumption can lead to digestive problems.
- It is also a major contributor to environmental pollution, as it is a byproduct of the production of nylon and other synthetic materials.

### Paracetamol

- Paracetamol, also known as acetaminophen, is a common over-the-counter pain reliever and fever reducer.
- It works by blocking the production of prostaglandins, which are chemicals that cause pain and inflammation in the body.
- Paracetamol is considered safe for use in most people when taken in recommended doses, although excessive use can lead to liver damage.
- It is a widely used medication due to its effectiveness and low risk of side effects, and is available in various forms such as tablets, capsules, and syrups.
