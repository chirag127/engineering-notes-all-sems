#### Determination of Available Chlorine in Bleaching Powder

Bleaching powder is a commonly used compound for disinfection and bleaching purposes. It contains a mixture of calcium hypochlorite and calcium chloride, with the former being responsible for its bleaching and disinfecting properties. The amount of available chlorine in bleaching powder can be determined by the following methods:

1. **Volumetric Method:** In this method, a known volume of bleaching powder is dissolved in water, and excess potassium iodide is added to react with the available chlorine. The liberated iodine is then titrated against sodium thiosulphate using starch as an indicator. The amount of available chlorine can be calculated using the following equation:

   Available Chlorine (%) = (Volume of Na2S2O3 × Normality of Na2S2O3 × 35.5 × 100) / (Weight of Sample × 1000)

2. **Buret Method:** In this method, a known volume of bleaching powder is dissolved in water, and the solution is titrated against a standard solution of sodium thiosulphate using a buret. The end-point is indicated by the disappearance of the yellow color due to the reaction of iodine with starch. The amount of available chlorine can be calculated using the same equation as above.

3. **Colorimetric Method:** In this method, a known volume of bleaching powder is dissolved in water, and the solution is reacted with a known volume of potassium iodide. The liberated iodine is then reacted with a known volume of sodium thiosulphate, and the amount of residual thiosulphate is determined by colorimetry. The amount of available chlorine can be calculated using the following equation:

   Available Chlorine (%) = (Volume of Na2S2O3 × Normality of Na2S2O3 × 35.5 × 100) / (Weight of Sample × 1000)

It is important to note that the accuracy of the results obtained from these methods depends on the purity of the bleaching powder used and the care taken during the analysis. It is also recommended to perform multiple trials to ensure the reproducibility of the results.
