### K3

K3 is a mathematical concept that is used to classify knots. It is a knot invariant, which means that it remains the same for all knots that are equivalent by deformation. Here are some key points to understand about K3:

- K3 is a smooth projective complex surface. It is a four-dimensional complex manifold that is not a complex torus.
- K3 surfaces are named after Ernst Kummer, who first studied them in the mid-19th century.
- In topology, K3 is used to classify knots. The knot type is determined by the fundamental group of the complement of the knot in S^3, which is isomorphic to a particular lattice in K3.
- K3 surfaces have a rich geometry and topology. They are symplectic manifolds and have a hyperkähler structure.
- K3 surfaces are important in string theory, where they are used to compactify extra dimensions. In this context, K3 surfaces are often studied in the context of mirror symmetry.
- The Hodge diamond of a K3 surface is symmetric, meaning that the number of Hodge numbers of each degree is the same when the degrees are reflected across the center of the diamond.
- K3 surfaces have interesting arithmetic properties. For example, there are only finitely many K3 surfaces over any fixed number field, and they can be classified using the Torelli theorem.
