## Determination of Hardness of Water Sample by EDTA Method

Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in the water. These ions can cause various problems like scaling, corrosion, and soap scum formation. Therefore, it is important to determine the hardness of water before using it for various purposes. One of the most common and accurate methods to determine the hardness of water is the EDTA method. Here are the steps involved in this method:

1. Take a water sample of known volume and pH.
2. Add a buffer solution to maintain the pH at around 10.
3. Add a few drops of indicator solution like Eriochrome Black T or Calcon.
4. Titrate the sample with a standard solution of EDTA until the color changes from the initial color to a stable blue color.
5. Note down the volume of EDTA solution used.
6. Repeat the above steps for duplicate or triplicate samples.
7. Calculate the amount of calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample using the following formula:

   Hardness (mg/L) = (Volume of EDTA solution used x Normality of EDTA solution x 1000) / Volume of water sample taken

Some important points to keep in mind while performing the EDTA method for hardness determination are:

- Always use a calibrated burette and pipette for accurate volume measurements.
- The pH of the water sample should be maintained at around 10 for accurate results.
- The indicator solution should be added in small amounts and mixed well with the sample to avoid errors.
- The EDTA solution should be standardized before use to ensure accurate results.
- The hardness of water can be expressed in different units like ppm, mg/L, or equivalents per liter (eq/L) depending on the requirement.

Overall, the EDTA method is a simple, accurate, and widely used method for the determination of hardness of water. It is essential for various industries like food and beverage, pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics to ensure that the water used in their products is of the desired quality.
