1. **The Stroop Effect Experiment**
   - The experiment investigates the interference of a person's cognitive system when presented with conflicting information.
   - Participants are asked to name the color of ink of words that spell out a different color.
   - The experiment measures the time it takes for participants to name the ink color of the words.
   - The results show that the time taken to name the ink color of the words is longer when the word spells out a different color than the ink color.
   
2. **The Asch Conformity Experiment**
   - The experiment studies the extent to which social pressure from a majority group can influence a person's conformity to their opinions.
   - Participants are asked to match the length of a line with three other lines of different lengths, while confederates give incorrect answers.
   - The experiment measures the number of times the participant answers incorrectly to conform to the group's answer.
   - The results show that participants conform to the group's answer at least once in about 75% of trials.

3. **The Milgram Experiment**
   - The experiment investigates the extent to which individuals obey authority figures, even when the authority figure's commands conflict with their conscience.
   - Participants are asked to administer electric shocks to a person in another room, who they believe is a participant, but is actually a confederate.
   - The experiment measures the number of times the participant administers a shock, even when the confederate expresses extreme distress.
   - The results show that about 65% of participants administered the highest level of shock, even when the confederate expressed extreme distress.

4. **The Bobo Doll Experiment**
   - The experiment studies the extent to which children imitate aggressive behavior they observe in others.
   - Participants are children who watch a video of an adult model interacting aggressively with a Bobo doll.
   - The experiment measures the number of aggressive behaviors the children exhibit towards the Bobo doll after watching the video.
   - The results show that children who watched the aggressive video exhibited more aggressive behaviors towards the Bobo doll than children who watched a non-aggressive video.

5. **The Stanford Prison Experiment**
   - The experiment investigates the extent to which the roles people are assigned can influence their behavior and attitudes.
   - Participants are randomly assigned to be either guards or prisoners in a simulated prison environment.
   - The experiment measures the behavior and attitudes of participants over a period of time.
   - The results show that participants in the guard role exhibited abusive behavior towards the prisoners, while prisoners exhibited signs of extreme distress.

6. **The Hawthorne Effect Experiment**
   - The experiment studies the effect of being observed on people's behavior and performance.
   - Participants are workers in a factory who are observed during their workday.
   - The experiment measures the changes in the workers' behavior and performance due to being observed.
   - The results show that the workers' behavior and performance improved when they were being observed, even if the observation was not related to their work.

7. **The Bobath Concept Experiment**
   - The experiment examines the effectiveness of the Bobath Concept, a therapy approach for people with neurological conditions that aims to improve their movement and function.
   - Participants are people with neurological conditions who receive therapy according to the Bobath Concept.
   - The experiment measures changes in the participants' movement and function over time.
   - The results show that the Bobath Concept is effective in improving movement and function in people with neurological conditions.

8. **The Harlow Monkey Experiment**
   - The experiment studies the importance of social contact and attachment for infant monkeys.
   - Participants are infant monkeys who are separated from their mothers and placed in cages with surrogate mothers made of wire or cloth.
   - The experiment measures the monkeys' behavior and attachment to the surrogate mothers.
   - The results show that the monkeys preferred the cloth surrogate mother, even when it did not provide food, indicating the importance of social contact and attachment.

9. **The Pavlov's Dogs Experiment**
   - The experiment investigates the process of classical conditioning, where a neutral stimulus is linked with a reflex response.
   - Participants are dogs who are conditioned to associate a bell with food, causing them to salivate when the bell is rung.
   - The experiment measures the dogs' salivation response to the bell.
   - The results show that the dogs learned to associate the bell with food and exhibited a conditioned response of salivation to the bell alone.
