# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

Calibration is the process of verifying and adjusting the performance of a measuring instrument or equipment to ensure its accuracy and precision. In analytical chemistry, it is crucial to calibrate analytical instruments and apparatus before use to obtain reliable and accurate results. Here are some essential points to keep in mind for the calibration of analytical equipment and apparatus:

1. Select the appropriate calibration standards: Choose the appropriate calibration standards that are traceable to national or international standards, and ensure that they cover the range of interest.

2. Preparation of calibration standards: It is essential to prepare the calibration standards as per the manufacturer's instructions or established procedures. Ensure that the standards are stable and of known purity.

3. Calibration procedure: Follow the recommended calibration procedure provided by the manufacturer or established procedure. Ensure that the instrument is stable, and the environment is appropriate for calibration.

4. Calibration frequency: Establish a calibration frequency based on the instrument usage, stability, and drift. The frequency should be based on the manufacturer's recommendations or standard operating procedures.

5. Calibration records: Maintain accurate and complete calibration records, including calibration dates, standards used, instrument readings, and any adjustments made.

6. Verification of calibration: Verify the calibration periodically to ensure that the instrument is still within the acceptable limits. It is also essential to verify the calibration after any significant repairs or adjustments.

7. Calibration report: Prepare a calibration report that includes all the calibration details, instrument performance, and any adjustments made. The report should be reviewed and approved by the appropriate personnel.

In conclusion, calibration is a critical step in analytical chemistry to ensure the accuracy and precision of analytical instruments and apparatus. By following the above points, you can ensure that your equipment is calibrated correctly and produces reliable and accurate results.
