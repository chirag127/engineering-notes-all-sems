####### Determination of the Strength of Potassium Dichromate Using Internal Indicator

Potassium dichromate is a commonly used oxidizing agent in analytical chemistry. It is often used as a primary standard for the standardization of other oxidizing agents. The strength of potassium dichromate can be determined using an internal indicator, such as ferrous ammonium sulfate.

The following points outline the procedure for determining the strength of potassium dichromate using an internal indicator:

1. Preparation of Ferrous Ammonium Sulfate Solution: Dissolve 7.5 grams of ferrous ammonium sulfate in 500 mL of distilled water. Add 10 mL of dilute sulfuric acid and dilute the solution to 1 liter with distilled water. This solution is known as the internal indicator.

2. Preparation of Potassium Dichromate Solution: Weigh accurately about 0.4 grams of potassium dichromate and dissolve it in distilled water. Dilute the solution to 1 liter with distilled water. This solution is known as the stock solution.

3. Titration Procedure: Pipette 20 mL of the stock solution into a conical flask. Add 10 mL of internal indicator solution and dilute the mixture with distilled water to about 100 mL. Titrate the solution with standard 0.1N ferrous ammonium sulfate solution until the green color disappears and turns to a pale bluish-green color.

4. Calculation: From the volume of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution used in the titration, the strength of potassium dichromate can be calculated using the formula:

Strength of Potassium Dichromate = (Volume of Ferrous Ammonium Sulfate Solution × Normality of Ferrous Ammonium Sulfate Solution × 6.33) / Volume of Stock Solution.

The use of ferrous ammonium sulfate as an internal indicator provides accurate and precise results. This method is essential for the standardization of other oxidizing agents and for various analytical chemistry applications.
