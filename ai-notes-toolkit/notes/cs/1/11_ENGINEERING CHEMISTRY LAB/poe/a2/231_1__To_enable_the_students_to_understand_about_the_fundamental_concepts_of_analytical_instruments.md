# Fundamental Concepts of Analytical Instruments

1. Sensitivity - The ability of an analytical instrument to detect a small amount of analyte. A highly sensitive instrument can detect even a trace amount of the analyte.
2. Selectivity - The ability of an analytical instrument to detect a particular analyte in the presence of other interfering substances. An instrument with high selectivity can detect the analyte of interest even in a complex sample matrix.
3. Range - The range of concentrations of an analyte that an analytical instrument can measure accurately. The upper limit is determined by signal saturation while the lower limit is determined by instrument sensitivity.
4. Precision - The degree of reproducibility of measurements using an analytical instrument. It reflects the scatter or variance between multiple measurements of the same sample. A precise instrument will produce very similar measurements with low variance.
5. Accuracy - The closeness of the measured value to the true value of the analyte concentration. An accurate analytical instrument will produce results very close to the actual analyte concentration. Accuracy is affected by both precision and bias.
6. Robustness - The ability of an analytical instrument to remain unaffected by small changes in operating conditions or sample conditions. A robust instrument will produce consistent results even with minor fluctuations or variations.
