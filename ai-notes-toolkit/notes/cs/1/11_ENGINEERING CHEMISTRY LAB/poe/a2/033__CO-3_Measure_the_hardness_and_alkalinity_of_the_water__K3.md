### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

1. Hardness of water:
- The total concentration of calcium and magnesium salts in water is known as hardness of water.
- These salts are usually carbonates, bicarbonates, chlorides and sulfates of calcium and magnesium.
- Hard water forms scum with soap and increases the quantity of soap required for cleaning.
- Hard water is not harmful to health but causes scaling in boilers and water heaters which decreases their efficiency.
- Hardness is measured in mg/L or ppm as calcium carbonate.

2. Alkalinity of water:
- Alkalinity is the ability of water to neutralize acids.
- It is caused due to the presence of carbonates, bicarbonates and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium and sodium.
- Carbonate alkalinity is more than bicarbonate alkalinity in natural waters.
- High alkalinity leads to scale formation and affects the performance of hot water boilers and heat exchangers.
- Alkalinity is measured in mg/L as calcium carbonate.

3. Measurement of hardness and alkalinity:
- Hardness is measured by titrating the water sample against a standard EDTA solution using Eriochrome black T indicator.
- Alkalinity is measured by titrating the water sample against a standard HCl solution using methyl orange indicator.
- The volume of titrant used measures the concentration which is converted to mg/L as calcium carbonate.
