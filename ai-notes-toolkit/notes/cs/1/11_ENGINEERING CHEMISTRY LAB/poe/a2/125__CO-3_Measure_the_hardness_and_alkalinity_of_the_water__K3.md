### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

1. Water hardness refers to the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts in water. It is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) or grains per gallon (GPG).
2. The alkalinity of water refers to its ability to neutralize acids and is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) of calcium carbonate.
3. The hardness and alkalinity of water can be measured using test kits or meters. The kits use liquid reagents that change color upon reaction with the ions in the water to indicate the concentration. The meters are electronic and display the values digitally.
4. Water hardness affects the amount of soap needed for cleaning and laundering. Higher hardness requires more soap. Excessive hardness can also lead to scale buildup in pipes and water heaters.
5. The alkalinity of water affects the pH level and its corrosiveness. Low alkalinity can lead to acidic water that can damage pipes. High alkalinity can lead to scale buildup and affect the effectiveness of chlorine disinfection. The alkalinity should be in an optimum range for the water use.
