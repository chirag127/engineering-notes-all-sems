### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

1. Hardness of water: The hardness of water refers to its mineral content, specifically the amount of calcium and magnesium salts, mainly calcium bicarbonate, calcium sulphate and magnesium bicarbonate.

- Temporary hardness: It is caused by the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium hydrogen carbonates in water. It can be removed by boiling the water.
- Permanent hardness: It is caused due to the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium sulphates and chlorides in water. It cannot be removed by boiling the water.

2. Alkalinity of water: The alkalinity of water is its quantitative capacity to neutralize acids. It is mainly due to the presence of carbonate and bicarbonate salts such as sodium carbonate, sodium bicarbonate, potassium carbonate, calcium carbonate, etc. The alkalinity of natural water determines its pH value.

3. Methods to determine hardness and alkalinity:

- EDTA titration method: Used to determine total hardness and calcium hardness.
- Soap solution method: Used to determine total hardness.
- Methyl orange indicator method: Used to determine alkalinity.
- pH meter method: Used to determine both hardness and alkalinity. The pH value gives an idea about the alkalinity.

The methods involve titrating the water sample with standard solutions under specific conditions and noting the volume consumed to calculate the concentrations. Proper precautions need to be taken while collecting and storing the water samples before conducting the analysis.
