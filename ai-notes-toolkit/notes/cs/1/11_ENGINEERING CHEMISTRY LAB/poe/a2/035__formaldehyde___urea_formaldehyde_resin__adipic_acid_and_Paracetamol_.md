### Formaldehyde & Urea Formaldehyde Resin, Adipic Acid and Paracetamol

1. **Formaldehyde:** Formaldehyde (HCHO) is a colorless gas with a pungent odor. It is commonly used to produce resins used in the manufacturing of composite wood products, glues, and various building materials. Urea-formaldehyde and melamine-formaldehyde resins are produced by reacting formaldehyde with urea or melamine under specific temperature and pressure conditions. These resins are common adhesives for bonding wood products.
2. **Urea Formaldehyde Resin:** Urea formaldehyde resin is a copolymer of urea and formaldehyde. It is a thermosetting resin produced through polymerization under appropriate temperature and pH conditions. The resin is commonly used as an adhesive in the manufacturing of composite wood and board products. It has excellent adhesion to wood and the ability to produce water-resistant bonds.
3. **Adipic Acid:** Adipic acid is a white crystalline solid dicarboxylic acid. It is commonly produced through oxidation of a cyclohexane derivative. Adipic acid is a key monomer used to produce nylon 66 fibers and engineering plastics. It is also used as a flavorant and gelling aid in food production. Adipic acid has a high melting point and is stable under typical use conditions.
4. **Paracetamol:** Paracetamol is an over-the-counter acetaminophen-based pain reliever and fever reducer. It is commonly used to treat headaches, minor aches, and pains, as well as reduce fever. Paracetamol is generally well tolerated in proper dosages but can cause liver damage if overdosed. It acts as a non-opiate analgesic and antipyretic and is often used as an alternative to aspirin for patients sensitive to NSAIDs.
