### Viscosity, Conductance of Solution, Chloride and Iron Content in the Water

1. Viscosity: Viscosity refers to the thickness or thinness of a liquid. It determines the ease with which a liquid can flow. Water with high viscosity flows slowly while water with low viscosity flows rapidly. The viscosity of water is affected by temperature and impurities. Higher temperature and lower impurities decrease the viscosity of water.

2. Conductance of Solution: The ability of water to conduct electric current is known as conductance of solution. Pure water is a poor conductor of electricity but dissolved impurities like salts increase its conductance. A higher amount of dissolved salts results in higher conductance of the water solution. The conductance can be used to determine the total dissolved solids in water.

3. Chloride Content: Chloride is one of the common anions found in water that comes from the dissolution of salts. A high level of chloride is harmful for human consumption. It can give a salty taste to water and affect plants. The chloride content in water is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) and should be below 250 mg/L as per safety standards.

4. Iron Content: Iron is one of the common cations found in water that comes from the dissolution of iron salts and minerals. A high level of iron gives a reddish color to water and can stain clothes and plumbing fixtures.
