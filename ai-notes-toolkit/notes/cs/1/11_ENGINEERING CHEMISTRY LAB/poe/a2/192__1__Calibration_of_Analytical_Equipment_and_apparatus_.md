### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and apparatus.

1. Calibration is essential to ensure accuracy and precision of analytical instruments. It is a process of correcting the output of an instrument to account for any deviation from the expected value.
2. Calibration involves comparing the instrument's output for a known input (analyte concentration or quantity) to the accepted reference value and adjusting the instrument to bring the output in agreement with the reference.
3. Calibration is done using certified reference materials (CRMs) that have known composition or quantity of the analyte. The response from the instrument is compared with the accepted reference value of the CRM and adjustments are made to the instrument parameters to obtain accurate results.
4. Frequency of calibration will depend on the stability and precision of the instrument, variability in conditions and criticality of the analysis. More precise instruments may require less frequent calibration.
5. It is good laboratory practice to calibrate instruments before first use and then regularly as per the laboratory's standard operating procedures. Proper calibration and documentation helps ensure data quality.
