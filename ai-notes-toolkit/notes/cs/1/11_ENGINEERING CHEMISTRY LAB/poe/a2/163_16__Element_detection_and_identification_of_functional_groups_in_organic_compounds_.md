# 16. Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds

- Element detection: The elements present in an organic compound can be detected using various methods:
-- Combustion analysis: The compound is burnt in oxygen and the combustion products are analysed. Carbon is detected as CO2 and hydrogen as H2O.
-- Pyrolysis: The compound is heated strongly in the absence of air. The elements present break down into smaller fragments which can be analysed.
-- Spectroscopic methods: Methods like IR spectroscopy, NMR spectroscopy, UV-visible spectroscopy, etc. can be used to identify the elements present based on the characteristic spectra of C, H, O, N, halogens, etc.

- Identification of functional groups: The functional groups present in an organic compound can be identified using:
-- Colour tests: The compound is treated with specific reagents that give characteristic colours with different functional groups. For example, the Lucas test is used to detect alcohols and the Benedict's test is used to detect aldehydes and ketones.
-- Spectroscopic methods: IR spectroscopy and NMR spectroscopy can be used to identify functional groups based on their characteristic signals. For example, the C=O stretch in IR identifies a ketone and the C-H stretch identifies an alkane.
-- Characteristic reactions: The compound is treated with specific reagents and the presence of a particular functional group is identified based on a characteristic chemical reaction. For example, the presence of a carbonyl group is confirmed by its reduction with NaBH4.
