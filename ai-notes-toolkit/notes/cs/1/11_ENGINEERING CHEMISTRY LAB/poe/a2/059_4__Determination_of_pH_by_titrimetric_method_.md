#### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method.

- Titrimetric methods involve addition of titrant of known concentration to the analyte solution until an equivalence point is reached.
- The pH at equivalence point can be used to determine the pH of the analyte solution.
- Example: To determine pH of a weak acid, strong base like NaOH is used as titrant. At equivalence point, moles of acid = moles of base. The pH here can be calculated using formula: pH = pKa + log(1/([A-]/[HA]))
- The pH electrode or indicators can be used to detect the equivalence point. The pH at this point gives us the pH of the analyte solution.
- The method requires accurate preparation of solutions and proper detection of equivalence point to get precise pH value. The method can be used for both acids and bases. However, self-indicating solutions should be used to avoid errors.
- The method is simple and inexpensive but gives less precise results for higher pH values. It requires a good buffering capacity to get accurate results for wider range of pH values.
