###  Course Outcomes Bloom’s

1. Remembering: Recall or recognize information, ideas, and principles in the approximate form in which they were learned.

2. Understanding: Grasp meaning; translate or interpret information (e.g., translate an equation into words).

3. Applying: Use abstraction, concept, method, principle or theory to solve a new problem.

4. Analyzing: Break down material into component parts, determine how parts relate to one another and to the whole, note patterns and hierarchical relationships.

5. Evaluating: Make judgments about the value of ideas, works, solutions; determine criteria for judgments; assess how criteria apply.

6. Creating: Create a new or original whole out of component parts; see new connections; have insights.

The above list presents the six levels in Bloom's taxonomy, ordered from the simplest behavior (remembering) to the most complex (creating). These levels are often used to design educational objectives, assessment tasks, and learning activities.
