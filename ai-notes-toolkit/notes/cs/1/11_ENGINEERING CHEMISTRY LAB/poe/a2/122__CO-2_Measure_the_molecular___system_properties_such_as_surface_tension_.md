### CO-2 Measure the molecular / system properties such as surface tension,

1. Surface tension: It is a contractive tendency of the surface of a liquid that allows it to resist an external force. It is measured in units of force per unit length, or N/m. The high surface tension of water allows insects such as water striders to walk on its surface.

2. Viscosity: It is a measure of a fluid's resistance to gradual deformation by shear stress. It is measured in pascal-seconds (Pa⋅s). Highly viscous fluids, such as oils, flow very slowly compared to low-viscosity fluids like water. Viscosity in fluids corresponds to friction between the layers of a fluid.

3. Density: It is a measure of the amount of mass contained within a given volume. It is measured in units of mass per unit volume, such as kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m3). The density of a material depends on its molecular weight and also on the forces between its molecules. The densities of materials at a given temperature differ because of differences in their molecular weights and molecular packing.

4. Boiling point: It is the temperature at which a liquid changes into a vapor at the same pressure as the standard atmospheric pressure. The boiling point depends on the strength of the intermolecular forces between molecules of the liquid. A liquid with stronger intermolecular forces will have a higher boiling point. For example, water has hydrogen bonds and has a higher boiling point (100°C) than hydrogen sulfide (−60.8°C) which has only weak van der Waals forces between molecules.

5. Melting point: It is the temperature at which a solid changes into a liquid at standard atmospheric pressure. Like boiling point, the melting point depends on intermolecular forces between the molecules. A solid with stronger intermolecular forces will have a higher melting point. For example, the melting point of water is 0°C while that of methane is −161.5°C. Water molecules can form hydrogen bonds while methane molecules have only weak van der Waals forces between them.
