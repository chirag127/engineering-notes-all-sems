## CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

1. Hardness of water: The hardness of water refers to its calcium and magnesium content. It is usually reported as the concentration of calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
- Temporary hardness: It is caused by the presence of dissolved calcium hydrogen carbonate (Ca(HCO3)2) in water. It can be removed by boiling the water.
- Permanent hardness: It is caused by the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts primarily of sulfate and chloride in water. It cannot be removed by boiling the water.

2. Measurement of hardness of water: Hardness of water can be measured by the following methods:
- Titration method: Water is titrated against a standard solution of EDTA using Eriochrome black T indicator. 1 mg/L hardness is equivalent to 1 mL of EDTA solution.
- Complexometric method: A calibrated solution of EDTA is added to a known volume of water sample. The unreacted EDTA is then back titrated with a standard solution of calcium or magnesium salt using appropriate indicator.
- Atomic absorption spectrophotometry: The concentrations of calcium and magnesium in water are directly measured using their resonance lines.

3. Alkalinity of water: The alkalinity of water refers to its acidic neutralizing capacity. It is caused by the presence of carbonate, bicarbonate, and hydroxide ions in water. It is usually reported as the concentration of calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
- Measurement of alkalinity: The alkalinity of water can be measured by titrating the water sample against a standard solution of acid using a pH meter or indicator. The alkalinity can also be calculated if the concentrations of carbonate and bicarbonate ions are known.
