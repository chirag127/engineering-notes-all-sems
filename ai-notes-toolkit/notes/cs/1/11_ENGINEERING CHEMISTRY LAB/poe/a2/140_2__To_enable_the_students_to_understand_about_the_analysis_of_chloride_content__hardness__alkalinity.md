## To enable the students to understand about the analysis of chloride content, hardness, alkalinity

1. Chloride content
- Chlorides are dissolved salts in water containing chloride ions (Cl−).
- Common sources of chlorides are sodium chloride (table salt), potassium chloride, and magnesium chloride.
- Chloride concentration is measured in mg/L or ppm. High chloride levels can give water a salty taste and may indicate seawater intrusion or saltwater contamination.
- Excessive chloride levels can be harmful to plants and may corrode pipes or water heaters. The secondary drinking water standard is 250 mg/L.

2. Hardness
- Hardness refers to calcium and magnesium salts in water, usually as carbonates and bicarbonates.
- These minerals do not pose a health risk in low concentrations but can form scale in water heaters, boilers, and pipes.
- Hard water requires more soap for washing and can leave mineral deposits on dishes and laundry.
- Hardness is measured in mg/L of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) and is classified as soft (0-60 mg/L), moderately hard (61-120 mg/L), hard (121-180 mg/L), or very hard (over 180 mg/L).

3. Alkalinity
- Alkalinity refers to the ability of water to neutralize acids and is usually caused by carbonate, bicarbonate, hydroxide, and occasionally borate, silicate, and phosphate salts.
- The most common forms are carbonate (CO32−) and bicarbonate (HCO3−). High alkalinity can increase pH and lead to scaling.
- Alkalinity is measured in mg/L of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) and has an ideal range of 80-120 mg/L for most uses. Levels over 200 mg/L may require treatment.
