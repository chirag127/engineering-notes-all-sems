# Viscosity, Conductance and Other Water Quality Parameters

## Viscosity
- Viscosity is the measure of a fluid's resistance to flow.
- It depends on intermolecular forces between the molecules of a liquid.
- Higher the viscosity, higher is the resistance to flow.
- Water's viscosity is low, allowing it to flow easily.
- Factors affecting viscosity: Temperature (decreases with increase in temperature), Pressure (increases with increase in pressure) and Molecular weight (increases with increase in molecular weight)

## Conductance
- Conductance is the ability of a solution to conduct electric current.
- It depends on the presence of ions in the solution that can carry the current.
- Pure water has very low conductance but the presence of dissolved salts increases conductance.
- Factors affecting conductance: Nature of dissolved solute (ionic/non-ionic), Concentration of dissolved solute (increases with increase in concentration for ionic solutes), Temperature (increases with increase in temperature for electrolytes)

## Chloride and Iron Content
- Chloride and iron are commonly found dissolved ions in water that affect its conductance and other properties.
- Chloride gets into water from salt deposits and anthropogenic activities. Excess chloride can affect plants and lead to salty taste.
- Iron gets into water from rock weathering or steel corrosion. Excess iron can lead to red colour and precipitate as solids that clog pipes.
- Both chloride and iron content are monitored as part of water quality testing along with other parameters like pH, alkalinity, hardness, etc.
