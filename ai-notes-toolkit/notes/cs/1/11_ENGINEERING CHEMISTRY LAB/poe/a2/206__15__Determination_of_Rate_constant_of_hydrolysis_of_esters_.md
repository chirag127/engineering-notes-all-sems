### 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters.

1. Esters undergo hydrolysis in the presence of acid or base as a catalyst to produce alcohol and carboxylic acid. The rate of hydrolysis can be determined by measuring the amount of ester hydrolyzed per unit time.
2. The rate of reaction is directly proportional to the concentration of ester. The proportionality constant is called the rate constant (k) of the reaction.
3. Rate = k [ester]
4. Experimental procedure:
- Take a known quantity of ester in a flask.
- Add excess of water and catalyst (acid/base).
- Note the time.
- Collect samples at regular intervals of time and estimate the amount of ester reacted by titrating against standard alkali or acid.
- Plot a graph of log [ester] versus time.
- The slope gives -k.
5. Factors affecting the rate of hydrolysis:
- Nature of catalyst - acid/base
- Temperature - Rate increases with increase in temperature
- Structure of ester - Branched chain esters react faster than straight chain esters
- Solvent - Rate is faster in protic solvents like water
