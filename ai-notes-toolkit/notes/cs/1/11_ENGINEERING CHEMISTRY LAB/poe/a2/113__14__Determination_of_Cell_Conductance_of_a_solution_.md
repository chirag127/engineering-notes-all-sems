### 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a solution.

- Take two electrodes ( say platinum electrodes) and dip them in the solution to be studied. Connect the electrodes to a battery and an ammeter.
- Note the ammeter reading ( say I). This gives the current flowing through the solution.
- Measure the potential difference ( say V) across the two electrodes using a voltmeter.
- Compute the conductance of the solution using the formula:

Conductance = Current/Potential difference

= I/V

- The units of conductance are siemens (S).
- The conductivity is the reciprocal of resistance and is a measure of how well a material conducts electric current.
- The conductance depends on the nature of the solution, its concentration, temperature, etc. Pure water is a poor conductor but on dissolving salts, acids or bases, it becomes a good conductor.
