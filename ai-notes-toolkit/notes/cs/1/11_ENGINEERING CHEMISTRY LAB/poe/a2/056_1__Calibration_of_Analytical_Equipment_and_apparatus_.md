# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

1. Calibration is the process of adjusting an instrument to agree with a reference standard. It is necessary to calibrate analytical instruments periodically to ensure accuracy of measurements.
2. Calibration may involve one-point or multi-point adjustments. In one-point calibration, a single reference standard is used. In multi-point calibration, multiple standards are used across the operating range of the instrument. Multi-point calibration is more accurate but also more time-consuming and expensive.
3. The reference standards must be traceable to national standards. Commonly used reference standards include certified reference materials, volumetric glassware, and electronic multi-meters.
4. The specific steps involved in calibration vary with the type of instrument. However, the general procedure includes:

- Setting up the instrument and reference standards
- Recording instrument readings for reference standards
- Adjusting instrument parameters to correct for any deviations from reference values
- Repeating readings to ensure adjustments were accurate
- Documenting the calibration, including reference standard values and any adjustments made

5. Calibration should be performed with a standardized procedure by trained personnel on a regular schedule recommended by the instrument manufacturer or laboratory protocol. Accurate calibration is critical for ensuring data quality and analytical integrity.
