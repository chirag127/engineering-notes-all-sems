# 16. Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds

1. Element detection
- Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, halogens, sulfur can be detected using appropriate tests
- Carbon and hydrogen: Combustion method. The compound is burnt in oxygen and the carbon dioxide and water produced are collected and their volumes measured. From volumes, carbon and hydrogen percentages are calculated.
- Oxygen: Pyrogallol test. The compound is heated with pyrogallol and the oxygen consumption is noted. From amount of oxygen consumed, oxygen percentage is calculated.
- Nitrogen: Sodium fusion test. The compound is fused with metallic sodium in absence of air and the nitrogen gas evolved is collected and measured. From volume, nitrogen percentage is calculated.
- Halogens: Silver nitrate test. The compound is dissolved in sodium hydroxide and warmed with silver nitrate solution. The silver halide precipitate formed is an indication of the halogen present. The colour of precipitate indicates the specific halogen (e.g. white - chloride, yellow - bromide, pale yellow - iodide).
- Sulfur: Lead acetate test. The compound is heated with lead acetate solution. The formation of black lead sulfide precipitate is an indication of sulfur.

2. Identification of functional groups
- Alkyl halides: Give positive halogen test. Also undergo nucleophilic substitution reactions.
- Alcohols: Give positive iodoform test. Also show acid-base reactions of alcohols.
- Aldehydes: Give positive Tollens' test. Also show oxidation-reduction reactions of aldehydes.
- Ketones: Do not give positive iodoform or Tollens' test. Show oxidation-reduction reactions of ketones.
- Carboxylic acids: Give effervescence with sodium bicarbonate. Also show acid-base neutralization reactions.
- Amines: Give positive nitrous acid test. Also show acid-base reactions of amines.
