# Viscosity, Conductance and Water Impurities

## Viscosity

- Viscosity refers to the resistance of a liquid to flow.
- It is a measure of a liquid's internal friction. Higher the viscosity, higher is the resistance to flow.
- Water's viscosity is low allowing it to flow easily.
- Factors affecting viscosity: Temperature, Composition. Viscosity decreases with increase in temperature and depends on the composition of the liquid.

## Conductance

- Conductance refers to the ability of a solution to conduct electric current.
- It depends on the presence of free ions in the solution that can carry charge.
- Pure water is a poor conductor but conductance increases with addition of ionic substances like salts.
- Factors affecting conductance: Concentration of ions, Nature of ions, Temperature. Conductance increases with increase in ion concentration, depends on the mobility of ions and increases with increase in temperature.

## Chloride and Iron Content

- Chloride and iron salts are commonly found impurities in water which are measured as chloride and iron content respectively.
- High chloride or iron content imparts taste to water and stains clothes or utensils washed in such water.
- Allowable limits: Chloride: 250 mg/L; Iron: 0.3 mg/L. Water with impurity content exceeding these limits is unfit for consumption.
- These impurities can be removed using water treatment processes like reverse osmosis.
