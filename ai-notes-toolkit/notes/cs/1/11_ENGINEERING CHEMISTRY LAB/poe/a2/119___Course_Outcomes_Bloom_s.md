###  Course Outcomes Bloom’s

1.  Remembering: Recall or recognize terms, facts, information, and concepts.
2.  Understanding: Grasp the meaning of information by interpreting, summarizing, paraphrasing, classifying, reasoning, or estimating.
3.  Applying: Use information in other contexts or situations; use a concept to solve a new problem.
4.  Analyzing: Break information down into parts to explore understandings and relationships.
5.  Evaluating: Make judgments about the value or worth of information based on specific criteria.
6.  Creating: Put parts or elements together in new ways or synthesize information to create new and original communications or products.

The Bloom's taxonomy categorizes learning objectives into levels of complexity to help focus learning on higher levels of thinking. The levels start from remembering and understanding at the basic levels progressing to applying, analyzing, evaluating and creating at the higher levels requiring more complex thinking and cognitive skills. Course outcomes designed at higher levels encourage students to apply knowledge and demonstrate deeper understanding and learning.
