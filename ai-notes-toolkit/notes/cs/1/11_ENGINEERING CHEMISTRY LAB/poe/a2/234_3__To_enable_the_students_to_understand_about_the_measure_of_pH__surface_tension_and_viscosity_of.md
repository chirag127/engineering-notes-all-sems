### 3. To enable the students to understand about the measure of pH, surface tension and viscosity of

1. pH: pH is a measure of acidity or basicity of a substance. It is measured using a pH meter or pH paper which contains an indicator that changes color according to the pH. pH is measured on a scale of 0 to 14 where 0 is most acidic, 7 is neutral and 14 is most basic. Acids have pH less than 7 and bases have pH greater than 7.

2. Surface tension: Surface tension is a measure of how difficult it is to stretch the surface of a liquid to increase its surface area. It is caused by the attraction between the molecules at the surface. Surface tension can be measured using methods like rising liquid in capillary tubes, floating needles or drops, etc. Lower surface tension means the liquid can spread out more easily.

3. Viscosity: Viscosity refers to the resistance of a fluid to flow. It is a measure of the internal friction of a moving fluid. More viscous fluids move slower as their molecules move less freely. Viscosity can be measured using viscometers which determine the time taken for a fluid to flow through a tube. Higher viscosity means a liquid flows less easily.

The above points cover the key measures and descriptions of pH, surface tension and viscosity of fluids to enable students to understand these concepts.
