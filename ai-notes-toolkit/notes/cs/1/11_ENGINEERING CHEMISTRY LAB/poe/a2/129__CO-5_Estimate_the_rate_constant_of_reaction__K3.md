### CO-5 Estimate the rate constant of reaction. K3

1. A reaction has a rate law : rate = k[A]x[B]y. By measuring rates at different initial concentrations of A & B, x & y can be determined.
2. Once x & y are known, k can be estimated from the rate data at a particular [A] & [B].
3. Take the natural log of both sides: ln(rate) = lnx*ln[A] + lny*ln[B] + lnk
4. Plot ln(rate) vs ln[A] & ln[B] and determine the slopes to get x & y.
5. Then use the rate data at a particular [A] & [B] to get ln(rate), and solve for lnalnrate - ln[A]x - ln[B]y = lnk. Take the exp of both sides to get k.
6. The units of k will be M-x*N-y*sec-1 (or whatever concentration units are used). The magnitude of k indicates how fast the reaction proceeds. The larger k, the faster the reaction rate.
