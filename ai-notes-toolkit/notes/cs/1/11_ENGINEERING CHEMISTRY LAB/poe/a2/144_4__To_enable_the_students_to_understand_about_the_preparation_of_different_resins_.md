### 4. To enable the students to understand about the preparation of different resins.

1. Phenol-formaldehyde resin: Phenol and formaldehyde are reacted in the presence of an alkaline catalyst like sodium hydroxide. The molar ratio of phenol to formaldehyde is 1:1-1:2. The reaction is carried out at 60-100°C. The resulting resin is a solid that can be moulded into different shapes or used as adhesives.

2. Urea-formaldehyde resin: Urea and formaldehyde are reacted in the presence of an acid catalyst like hydrochloric acid. The molar ratio of urea to formaldehyde is 1:2-1:4. The reaction is carried out at 60-120°C. The resulting resin is a liquid that solidifies on heating. It is widely used as an adhesive for manufacturing particle boards and laminates.

3. Melamine-formaldehyde resin: Melamine and formaldehyde are reacted in the presence of an acid catalyst. The molar ratio of melamine to formaldehyde is 1:3-1:6. The reaction is carried out at 60-120°C. The resulting resin is a highly heat and water resistant solid that is used to manufacture laminates and dinnerware.

4. Polyester resin: Glycol and dibasic organic acids like phthalic acid are heated together producing a liquid thermosetting polyester resin. A catalyst like methyl ethyl ketone peroxide is added and the resin cures on heating or adding a vinyl monomer like styrene. The curing process can be accelerated using cobalt naphthenate as a catalyst. Polyester resins are used to produce glass fiber reinforced plastics.
