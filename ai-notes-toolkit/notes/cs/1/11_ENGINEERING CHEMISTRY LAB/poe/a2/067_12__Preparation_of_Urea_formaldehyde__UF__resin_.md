## 12. Preparation of Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin.

1. Urea and formaldehyde are the two monomers used to produce urea-formaldehyde (UF) resin.
2. Typically, the molar ratio of urea to formaldehyde is 1:2. An excess of formaldehyde is used to promote the polymerization reaction.
3. The monomers are heated in the presence of a basic catalyst, such as sodium hydroxide, under pressure. This causes the monomers to polymerize, forming methylene bridges between the urea molecules.
4. As the reaction proceeds, the viscosity of the reaction mixture increases until it becomes a viscous liquid or solid resin. The properties of the final UF resin depend on the extent of polymerization and degree of cross-linking.
5. The polymerization reaction can be terminated at any stage by adjusting the pH to a lower value, which deactivates the basic catalyst. The resin can then be stabilized by converting any remaining formaldehyde to harmless forms, such as by reaction with urea.
6. The properties of UF resins can be modified by incorporating other monomers into the resin or by using modifiers that react with the resin. Common modifiers include melamine, glyoxal, and acids. These modifications produce UF resins with a range of properties suited to different applications.
