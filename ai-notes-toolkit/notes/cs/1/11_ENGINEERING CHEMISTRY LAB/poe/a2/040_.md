## Blockchain

I. Introduction
- Blockchain is a distributed ledger of transactions maintained by a network of computers.
- Records are organized into groups called blocks that hold sets of individual transactions.
- New blocks are added to the blockchain in a linear, chronological order using cryptography to keep them secure.

II. Key characteristics
1. Decentralization: The blockchain network has no central authority or single administrator. The nodes work together to validate and verify transactions via the network.
2. Immutability: Records in the blockchain are irreversible and permanent. New blocks are added to the chain in a verifiable, permanent way.
3. Transparency: The blockchain is a distributed, public ledger, so transaction records are open and transparent. However, anonymity can be maintained via pseudonymous addresses.
4. Security: The blockchain uses cryptography to ensure high levels of security and verification. The decentralized, peer-to-peer network structure eliminates single points of failure.

III. Uses
- Cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin use blockchain to record monetary transactions and prevent double-spending.
- Smart contracts can be encoded into the blockchain to facilitate self-executing transactions.
- The blockchain can provide an auditable, permanent ledger for a variety of record-keeping purposes like property records, securities trades, or business contracts.
- The decentralized, trustless nature of the blockchain makes it useful for a range of applications where there are concerns about security, accountability, or fraud.
