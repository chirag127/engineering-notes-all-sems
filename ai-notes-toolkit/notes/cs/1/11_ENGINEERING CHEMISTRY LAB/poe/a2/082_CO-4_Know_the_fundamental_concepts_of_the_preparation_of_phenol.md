# CO-4 Know the fundamental concepts of the preparation of phenol

1. From cumene hydroperoxide: Cumene (isopropylbenzene) is oxidized to cumene hydroperoxide, which is then cleaved to phenol and acetone. This is an important industrial method for manufacturing phenol.

2. From benzene: Benzene can be hydroxylated to phenol using a Fenton's reagent or by reaction with nitrous acid followed by hydrolysis. However, direct hydroxylation of benzene requires high temperatures and pressures and is rarely practiced industrially.

3. From chlorobenzene: Chlorobenzene can be converted to phenol through hydrolysis or pyrolysis. In the hydrolysis, chlorobenzene is reacted with a base like sodium hydroxide to produce phenol. In the pyrolysis, chlorobenzene is decomposed by heating to high temperatures to produce hydrogen chloride and phenol.

4. From aniline: Aniline can be converted to phenol by diazotization followed by decomposition of the resulting diazonium salt. This method was used industrially in the early 20th century but is rarely practiced today due to the availability of more efficient methods.
