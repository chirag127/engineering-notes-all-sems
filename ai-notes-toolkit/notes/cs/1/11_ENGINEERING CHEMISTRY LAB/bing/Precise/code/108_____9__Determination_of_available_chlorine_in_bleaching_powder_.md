### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder

Bleaching powder, also known as calcium hypochlorite, is a chemical compound with the formula Ca(ClO)2. It is widely used as a disinfectant and bleaching agent. The available chlorine in bleaching powder refers to the amount of chlorine that can be released as a disinfectant when the powder is dissolved in water.

Here are the steps to determine the available chlorine in bleaching powder:

1. Weigh accurately about 1g of the sample of bleaching powder and transfer it to a conical flask.
2. Add 100 ml of distilled water to the flask and shake well to dissolve the powder.
3. Filter the solution through a filter paper and collect the filtrate in a conical flask.
4. Take 20 ml of the filtrate in a conical flask and add 1 ml of potassium iodide solution and 2 ml of acetic acid to it.
5. Titrate the solution against sodium thiosulphate solution using starch as an indicator.
6. Repeat the titration until concordant values are obtained.
7. Calculate the available chlorine in the sample using the formula: % available chlorine = (V * N * 35.5 * 100) / (W * 10), where V is the volume of sodium thiosulphate solution used, N is the normality of sodium thiosulphate solution, W is the weight of the sample taken, and 35.5 is the atomic weight of chlorine.

This is a brief overview of the process of determining the available chlorine in bleaching powder. It is important to follow the steps carefully and accurately to obtain reliable results.
