### 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator

1. Potassium dichromate is an oxidizing agent that can be used to determine the strength of a substance using an internal indicator.
2. An internal indicator is a substance that changes color to indicate the end point of a titration.
3. In the case of potassium dichromate, the internal indicator used is usually diphenylamine sulfonate.
4. The procedure involves adding a known volume of potassium dichromate solution to the substance being tested, and then adding the internal indicator.
5. The solution is then titrated with a reducing agent until the color change of the internal indicator indicates that the end point has been reached.
6. The volume of reducing agent used can then be used to calculate the strength of the potassium dichromate solution.
7. This method is commonly used in the analysis of iron, copper, and other metals in various samples.
8. It is important to note that the accuracy of this method depends on the purity of the potassium dichromate solution and the correct use of the internal indicator.
