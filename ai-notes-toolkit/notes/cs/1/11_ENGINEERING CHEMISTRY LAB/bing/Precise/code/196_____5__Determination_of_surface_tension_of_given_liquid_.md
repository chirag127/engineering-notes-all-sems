### 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid

Surface tension is a property of liquids that arises due to the cohesive forces between the liquid molecules. It is defined as the force acting per unit length on the surface of the liquid. There are several methods to determine the surface tension of a given liquid, including:

1. **Capillary rise method**: This method involves measuring the height to which a liquid rises in a capillary tube due to the surface tension of the liquid. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = (weight of liquid raised * radius of capillary tube) / (height of liquid rise * 2 * pi)`

2. **Maximum bubble pressure method**: This method involves measuring the maximum pressure required to blow a bubble of air through a liquid. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = (4 * maximum pressure * radius of tube) / (3 * radius of bubble)`

3. **Drop weight method**: This method involves measuring the weight of a droplet of liquid that detaches from the end of a thin tube. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = (weight of droplet * acceleration due to gravity) / (pi * radius of tube^2)`

4. **Du Nouy ring method**: This method involves measuring the force required to detach a ring from the surface of a liquid. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = (force required to detach ring) / (2 * pi * radius of ring)`

5. **Wilhelmy plate method**: This method involves measuring the force required to detach a flat plate from the surface of a liquid. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = (force required to detach plate) / (perimeter of plate)`

Each of these methods has its own advantages and limitations, and the choice of method depends on the specific requirements of the experiment. It is important to carefully calibrate the instruments and follow the experimental procedure to obtain accurate results.
