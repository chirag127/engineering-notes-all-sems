### 10. Determination of chloride content in water sample

1. Chloride content in water can be determined using a titration method known as the Mohr method.
2. In this method, a water sample is titrated with a solution of silver nitrate (AgNO3) of known concentration.
3. Potassium chromate (K2CrO4) is used as an indicator, which forms a red precipitate of silver chromate (Ag2CrO4) when all the chloride ions have reacted with the silver ions.
4. The volume of silver nitrate solution used in the titration is noted and used to calculate the concentration of chloride ions in the water sample.
5. The calculation is done using the formula: Cl- (mg/L) = (AgNO3 used (mL) x AgNO3 normality x 35.45) / sample volume (mL).
6. This method is simple, accurate, and widely used for the determination of chloride content in water samples.
7. It is important to determine the chloride content in water as high levels of chloride can have adverse effects on human health, aquatic life, and the environment.
8. The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends a maximum limit of 250 mg/L for chloride in drinking water.
9. Regular monitoring of chloride levels in water sources is necessary to ensure the safety and quality of water for consumption and other uses.
10. Other methods for the determination of chloride content in water include ion chromatography, ion-selective electrodes, and spectrophotometry.
