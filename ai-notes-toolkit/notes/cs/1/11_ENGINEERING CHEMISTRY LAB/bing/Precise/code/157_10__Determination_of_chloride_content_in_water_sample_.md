# Determination of Chloride Content in Water Sample

1. Chloride ions are one of the major inorganic anions in water and wastewater.
2. The chloride test measures the level of chloride ions in water.
3. The presence of chloride ions in water can be an indicator of pollution from human or animal waste, industrial processes, or saltwater intrusion.
4. The most common method for determining the chloride content in water is the argentometric method, also known as the Mohr method.
5. In this method, a measured volume of water sample is titrated with a standard solution of silver nitrate using potassium chromate as an indicator.
6. The end point of the titration is indicated by the formation of a red-brown precipitate of silver chromate.
7. The amount of silver nitrate used in the titration is proportional to the chloride content in the water sample.
8. The chloride content can be calculated using the formula: Cl- (mg/L) = (AgNO3 used (mL) x AgNO3 normality x 35.45) / sample volume (mL).
9. Other methods for determining the chloride content in water include ion chromatography, ion-selective electrode, and spectrophotometry.
10. It is important to regularly monitor the chloride content in water to ensure that it is within safe levels for human consumption and for the protection of aquatic life.
