# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

Calibration is the process of verifying the accuracy of a measuring instrument by comparing its readings with those of a known standard. This is important for ensuring the reliability and accuracy of analytical results.

1. Calibration is necessary for all types of analytical equipment and apparatus, including balances, pipettes, thermometers, and spectrophotometers.
2. The frequency of calibration depends on the type of equipment, its usage, and the requirements of the relevant regulatory bodies.
3. Calibration can be performed using certified reference materials, which are materials with known properties that have been certified by a recognized authority.
4. Calibration can also be performed using external standards, which are materials with known properties that have been prepared in-house or obtained from an external source.
5. Calibration should be performed according to a documented procedure, and the results should be recorded and retained for future reference.
6. Calibration should be performed by trained personnel, and the equipment should be maintained and operated according to the manufacturer's instructions.
7. If the equipment is found to be out of calibration, it should be adjusted or repaired, and the calibration should be repeated until the equipment is within the acceptable range.
