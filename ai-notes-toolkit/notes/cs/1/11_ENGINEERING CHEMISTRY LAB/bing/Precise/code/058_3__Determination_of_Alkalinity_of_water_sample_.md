# Determination of Alkalinity of Water Sample

Alkalinity is a measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids. It is an important parameter in water quality analysis, as it provides information about the buffering capacity of water. Here are the steps to determine the alkalinity of a water sample:

1. Collect a water sample in a clean container.
2. Measure the pH of the sample using a pH meter or pH test strips.
3. If the pH is above 4.5, titrate the sample with a standard solution of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) or hydrochloric acid (HCl) using a burette.
4. Record the volume of acid required to lower the pH to 4.5.
5. Calculate the alkalinity of the sample using the formula: Alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of acid used x Normality of acid x 50,000) / Volume of sample.

This method is known as the titrimetric method and is commonly used for the determination of alkalinity in water samples. It is important to note that the results obtained using this method are dependent on the accuracy of the pH measurement and the normality of the acid used for titration. Therefore, it is important to calibrate the pH meter and standardize the acid solution before performing the analysis.
