### 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid

Surface tension is a property of liquids that arises due to the cohesive forces between the liquid molecules. It is defined as the force acting per unit length on the surface of the liquid. There are several methods to determine the surface tension of a given liquid, including:

1. **Capillary rise method:** This method involves measuring the height to which a liquid rises in a capillary tube due to the surface tension of the liquid. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = (ρghr)/2`, where `ρ` is the density of the liquid, `g` is the acceleration due to gravity, `h` is the height of the liquid column, and `r` is the radius of the capillary tube.

2. **Maximum bubble pressure method:** This method involves measuring the maximum pressure required to blow a bubble from a submerged orifice. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = (4Pm)/d`, where `Pm` is the maximum pressure and `d` is the diameter of the orifice.

3. **Drop weight method:** This method involves measuring the weight of drops of liquid that detach from the end of a thin tube. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = mg/πd`, where `m` is the mass of the drop, `g` is the acceleration due to gravity, and `d` is the diameter of the tube.

4. **Du Nouy ring method:** This method involves measuring the force required to detach a ring from the surface of a liquid. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = F/4πr`, where `F` is the force and `r` is the radius of the ring.

5. **Wilhelmy plate method:** This method involves measuring the force required to detach a flat plate from the surface of a liquid. The surface tension is then calculated using the formula: `Surface tension = F/2l`, where `F` is the force and `l` is the length of the plate.

These are some of the common methods used to determine the surface tension of a given liquid. Each method has its own advantages and limitations, and the choice of method depends on the specific requirements of the experiment.
