# Determination of pH by Titrimetric Method

1. Titration is a laboratory technique used to determine the concentration of an unknown solution by reacting it with a solution of known concentration.
2. The pH of a solution can be determined by titrating it with a strong acid or base.
3. The titration curve is a graph of pH versus volume of titrant added. The equivalence point is the point at which the amount of titrant added is chemically equivalent to the amount of analyte present.
4. The pH at the equivalence point depends on the strength of the acid or base being titrated. For a strong acid-strong base titration, the pH at the equivalence point is 7. For a weak acid-strong base titration, the pH at the equivalence point is greater than 7. For a strong acid-weak base titration, the pH at the equivalence point is less than 7.
5. Indicators are used to visually signal the end point of the titration. The indicator should change color at a pH close to the equivalence point.
6. The pH can be calculated using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for weak acid-strong base and strong acid-weak base titrations.
7. The accuracy of the pH determination depends on the precision of the titration and the accuracy of the standard solution used.
