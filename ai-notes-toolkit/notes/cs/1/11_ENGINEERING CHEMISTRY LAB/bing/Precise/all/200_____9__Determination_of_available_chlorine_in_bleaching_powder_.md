# 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder

Bleaching powder, also known as calcium hypochlorite, is a chemical compound that is commonly used as a disinfectant and bleaching agent. The available chlorine in bleaching powder refers to the amount of chlorine that can be released from the compound when it reacts with an acid.

To determine the available chlorine in bleaching powder, the following steps can be followed:

1. Weigh a sample of bleaching powder and dissolve it in water.
2. Add an excess of potassium iodide (KI) to the solution. The iodide ions will react with the available chlorine to produce iodine.
3. Titrate the iodine produced with a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate, using starch as an indicator. The starch will turn blue in the presence of iodine, indicating the end point of the titration.
4. Calculate the amount of available chlorine in the sample using the volume of sodium thiosulfate used in the titration and the concentration of the sodium thiosulfate solution.

This method is known as the iodometric titration method and is commonly used to determine the available chlorine in bleaching powder. It is important to note that the results obtained using this method may vary depending on factors such as the age and storage conditions of the bleaching powder sample.
