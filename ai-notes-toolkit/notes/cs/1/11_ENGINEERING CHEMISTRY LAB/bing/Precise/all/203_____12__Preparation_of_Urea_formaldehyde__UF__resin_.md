### 12. Preparation of Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin

Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin is a type of thermosetting polymer that is commonly used in the production of particleboard, medium-density fiberboard (MDF), and other wood products. It is made by reacting urea and formaldehyde in the presence of a catalyst, typically an acid or a base.

Here are the steps involved in the preparation of UF resin:

1. Urea and formaldehyde are mixed in a reactor vessel in a molar ratio of approximately 1:1.5 to 2.5.
2. A catalyst, such as sulfuric acid or sodium hydroxide, is added to the mixture to initiate the reaction.
3. The mixture is heated to a temperature of around 90-100°C and stirred continuously.
4. The reaction is allowed to proceed for several hours until the desired degree of polymerization is achieved.
5. The resulting resin is cooled and neutralized with an appropriate base or acid to stop the reaction.
6. The resin is then filtered to remove any impurities and can be used as is or further processed to modify its properties.

It is important to note that the exact conditions and proportions of the reactants may vary depending on the desired properties of the final product. Additionally, other additives, such as fillers or pigments, may be added to the resin to improve its performance or appearance.
