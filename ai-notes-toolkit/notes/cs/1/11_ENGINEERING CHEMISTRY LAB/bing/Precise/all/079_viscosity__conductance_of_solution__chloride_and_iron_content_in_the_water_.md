# Viscosity, Conductance of Solution, Chloride and Iron Content in Water

## Viscosity
- Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow.
- The viscosity of formation water is a function of pressure, temperature, and dissolved solids.
- In general, brine viscosity increases with increasing pressure, increasing salinity, and decreasing temperature.
- Dissolved gas in the formation water at reservoir conditions generally results in a negligible effect on water viscosity .

## Conductance of Solution
- Conductance is the ability of a solution to conduct an electric current.
- The more ions present in the water, the higher the conductivity.
- High-quality deionized water has a conductivity of about 0.05 μS/cm at 25 °C.
- Typical drinking water is in the range of 200–800 μS/cm.
- Sea water is about 50 mS/cm (or 0.05 S/cm) .

## Chloride Content in Water
- Chloride ions are approximately 1.9 percent of the mass of seawater.
- The more chloride ions present in the water, the higher the conductivity.
- In general, the conductivity of waterways in the United States varies from 50 to 1500 µmhos/cm.
- Inland freshwater lake studies reveal a conductivity of 150 to 500 µmhos/cm .

## Iron Content in Water
- Iron is a naturally occurring element in water.
- Iron can be present in water in two forms: soluble ferrous iron or insoluble ferric iron.
- Water containing ferrous iron is clear and colorless because the iron is completely dissolved.
- When exposed to air in the pressure tank or atmosphere, the water turns cloudy and a reddish-brown substance begins to form.
- This sediment is the oxidized or ferric form of iron that will not dissolve in water.
