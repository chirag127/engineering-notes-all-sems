### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

Calibration is the process of verifying the accuracy of a measuring instrument by comparing it with a reference standard. This is important for ensuring that the results obtained from the instrument are accurate and reliable.

- Calibration is necessary for all types of analytical equipment and apparatus, including balances, pipettes, thermometers, and spectrophotometers.
- The frequency of calibration depends on the instrument and its usage. Some instruments may require daily calibration, while others may only need to be calibrated once a year.
- Calibration can be performed by the user, a trained technician, or an external calibration service.
- Calibration involves comparing the readings of the instrument with those of a reference standard. Any discrepancies between the two are corrected by adjusting the instrument.
- Calibration records should be maintained to demonstrate that the instrument has been properly calibrated and is functioning correctly.
- Regular calibration is essential for ensuring the accuracy and reliability of analytical results. It helps to identify any problems with the instrument and can prevent errors in the analysis.
