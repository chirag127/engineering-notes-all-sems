### 4. To enable the students to understand about the preparation of different resins.

Resins are solid or highly viscous substances that are typically convertible into polymers. They are usually mixtures of organic compounds, mainly terpenes and their derivatives. Here are some points to understand about the preparation of different resins:

1. **Natural resins**: Natural resins are typically obtained from plants. They are exuded from trees as a response to injury and are collected by tapping, cutting, or making incisions in the bark. Examples of natural resins include amber, copal, dammar, and mastic.

2. **Synthetic resins**: Synthetic resins are materials with similar properties to natural resins but are produced through chemical reactions. They are typically derived from simple hydrocarbons, such as ethylene or propylene, and are often used in the production of plastics, adhesives, and coatings.

3. **Preparation methods**: The preparation of resins varies depending on the type of resin being produced. For natural resins, the collection process involves tapping, cutting, or making incisions in the bark of trees. For synthetic resins, the preparation involves chemical reactions, often involving the polymerization of monomers.

4. **Factors affecting resin preparation**: Various factors can affect the preparation of resins, including temperature, pressure, and the presence of catalysts. These factors can influence the rate of chemical reactions and the properties of the resulting resin.

5. **Safety considerations**: The preparation of resins, particularly synthetic resins, can involve the use of hazardous chemicals and high temperatures. It is important to follow safety guidelines and wear appropriate protective equipment when working with these materials.
