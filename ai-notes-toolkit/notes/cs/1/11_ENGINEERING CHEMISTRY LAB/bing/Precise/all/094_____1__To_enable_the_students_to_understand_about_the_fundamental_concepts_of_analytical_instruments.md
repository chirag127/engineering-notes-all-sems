# Analytical Instruments

Analytical instruments are devices used to measure, analyze, and determine the physical and chemical properties of substances. These instruments are used in various fields such as chemistry, biology, physics, and engineering to provide accurate and reliable data.

1. Fundamental concepts of analytical instruments:
    - **Accuracy**: The degree to which the result of a measurement, calculation, or specification conforms to the correct value or a standard.
    - **Precision**: The degree to which repeated measurements under unchanged conditions show the same results.
    - **Sensitivity**: The capability of an instrument to respond to small changes in the quantity being measured.
    - **Selectivity**: The ability of an instrument to distinguish between different components in a sample.
    - **Resolution**: The smallest difference between two values that can be distinguished by an instrument.
    - **Calibration**: The process of establishing the relationship between the output of an instrument and the value of the input quantity.
    - **Linearity**: The ability of an instrument to produce a linear response over a range of input values.
    - **Reproducibility**: The degree to which repeated measurements of the same sample under the same conditions produce the same results.
    - **Robustness**: The ability of an instrument to maintain its performance under varying conditions.

These concepts are essential for understanding the performance and limitations of analytical instruments and for ensuring that the data obtained from these instruments is accurate and reliable. It is important for students to have a solid understanding of these concepts in order to effectively use and interpret the results obtained from analytical instruments.
