# Liquid

- A liquid is one of the four fundamental states of matter, along with solid, gas, and plasma.
- It is characterized by its ability to flow and take the shape of its container.
- Liquids have a definite volume, but not a definite shape.
- The molecules in a liquid are closely packed, but they have enough energy to move around and slide past each other.
- The attractive forces between the molecules in a liquid are weaker than in a solid, but stronger than in a gas.
- The temperature at which a substance changes from a solid to a liquid is called its melting point, and the temperature at which it changes from a liquid to a gas is called its boiling point.
- Liquids can be classified as Newtonian or non-Newtonian, depending on whether their viscosity changes with the applied shear stress.
- Some common examples of liquids include water, milk, oil, and gasoline.
- Liquids play an important role in many natural and industrial processes, such as weather patterns, biological functions, and chemical reactions.
