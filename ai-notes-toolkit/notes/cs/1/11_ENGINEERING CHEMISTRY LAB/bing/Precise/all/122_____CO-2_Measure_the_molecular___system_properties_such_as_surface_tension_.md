### CO-2 Measure the molecular / system properties such as surface tension

Surface tension is a property of liquids that arises due to the cohesive forces between the molecules at the surface of the liquid. These forces cause the liquid to behave as if it is covered by an elastic film, which resists deformation and tends to minimize the surface area.

Some methods to measure surface tension include:

1. **Maximum Bubble Pressure Method**: This method involves measuring the pressure required to force a gas bubble out of a submerged capillary tube. The pressure difference between the inside and outside of the bubble is related to the surface tension of the liquid.

2. **Drop Weight Method**: This method involves measuring the weight of a droplet of liquid that detaches from the end of a thin tube. The weight of the droplet is related to the surface tension of the liquid.

3. **Wilhelmy Plate Method**: This method involves immersing a thin, flat plate into a liquid and measuring the force required to detach it from the liquid surface. The force is related to the surface tension of the liquid.

4. **Du Nouy Ring Method**: This method involves immersing a ring into a liquid and measuring the force required to detach it from the liquid surface. The force is related to the surface tension of the liquid.

These are some of the common methods used to measure the surface tension of liquids. Each method has its own advantages and limitations, and the choice of method depends on the specific requirements of the experiment.
