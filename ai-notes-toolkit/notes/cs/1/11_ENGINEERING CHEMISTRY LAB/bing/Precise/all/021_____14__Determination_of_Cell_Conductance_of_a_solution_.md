### 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a solution

1. Cell conductance is a measure of the ability of a solution to conduct electricity.
2. It is determined by measuring the electrical resistance of the solution between two electrodes.
3. The conductance is the reciprocal of the resistance, and is measured in units of Siemens (S).
4. The cell constant is a factor that takes into account the geometry of the cell and the distance between the electrodes.
5. The cell conductance is calculated by multiplying the measured conductance by the cell constant.
6. The cell conductance is affected by the concentration and mobility of the ions in the solution, as well as the temperature of the solution.
7. The cell conductance can be used to determine the concentration of ions in a solution, or to monitor changes in the solution during a chemical reaction.
