## LIST OF EXPERIMENTS

1. **The Milgram Experiment:** A series of social psychology experiments conducted by Yale University psychologist Stanley Milgram in the 1960s, which measured the willingness of study participants to obey an authority figure who instructed them to perform acts that conflicted with their personal conscience.

2. **The Stanford Prison Experiment:** A social psychology experiment conducted at Stanford University in 1971 by Philip Zimbardo, which aimed to investigate the psychological effects of perceived power, focusing on the struggle between prisoners and prison officers.

3. **The Asch Conformity Experiment:** A series of studies conducted in the 1950s by Solomon Asch, which aimed to investigate the extent to which social pressure from a majority group could affect a person to conform.

4. **The Pavlov's Dog Experiment:** A series of experiments conducted by Russian physiologist Ivan Pavlov in the 1890s, which aimed to investigate classical conditioning, a type of learning in which an organism comes to associate stimuli.

5. **The Marshmallow Test:** A study on delayed gratification conducted by Walter Mischel in the 1960s and 1970s, which aimed to investigate the ability of children to delay gratification and the relationship between delayed gratification and future success.

6. **The Hawthorne Effect:** A series of studies conducted in the 1920s and 1930s at the Hawthorne Works factory in Cicero, Illinois, which aimed to investigate the effects of changes in working conditions on worker productivity.

7. **The Bobo Doll Experiment:** A series of studies conducted in the 1960s by Albert Bandura, which aimed to investigate the effects of observing aggressive behavior on the behavior of children.

8. **The Bystander Effect:** A social psychology phenomenon in which individuals are less likely to offer help to a victim when other people are present, first demonstrated in a series of experiments conducted by John Darley and Bibb Latané in the 1960s.

9. **The Little Albert Experiment:** A study conducted by John B. Watson and Rosalie Rayner in 1920, which aimed to investigate classical conditioning in humans by demonstrating that emotional responses could be conditioned in humans.

10. **The Rorschach Inkblot Test:** A projective psychological test developed by Hermann Rorschach in 1921, which aimed to investigate personality characteristics and emotional functioning by analyzing a person's interpretation of inkblots.
