# Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator

1. Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a double salt of iron sulfate and ammonium sulfate.
2. Its chemical formula is (NH4)2Fe(SO4)2.6H2O.
3. It is commonly used as a standard solution in redox titrations due to its stability and the ease with which it can be prepared.
4. The strength of a ferrous ammonium sulfate solution can be determined using an external indicator.
5. An external indicator is a substance that changes color at the endpoint of a titration.
6. The most commonly used external indicator for the determination of the strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate is potassium permanganate (KMnO4).
7. Potassium permanganate is a strong oxidizing agent and its solution is purple in color.
8. During the titration, the purple color of the potassium permanganate solution fades as it is reduced to colorless manganese ions.
9. The endpoint of the titration is reached when a faint pink color persists, indicating that all the ferrous ions have been oxidized to ferric ions.
10. The strength of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution can then be calculated using the volume of potassium permanganate solution used and the concentration of the potassium permanganate solution.
