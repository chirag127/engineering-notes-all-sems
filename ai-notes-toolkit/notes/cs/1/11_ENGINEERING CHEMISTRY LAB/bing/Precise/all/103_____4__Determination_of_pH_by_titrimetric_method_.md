### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

Titrimetric method is a common laboratory technique used to determine the pH of a solution. The process involves the following steps:

1. A known volume of the solution whose pH is to be determined is taken in a conical flask.
2. A suitable indicator is added to the solution. The choice of indicator depends on the pH range of the solution.
3. A standard solution of a strong acid or a strong base is taken in a burette. The choice of acid or base depends on whether the solution is acidic or basic.
4. The standard solution is slowly added to the solution in the conical flask with constant stirring.
5. The addition of the standard solution is continued until the indicator changes color, indicating the end point of the titration.
6. The volume of the standard solution used is noted.
7. The pH of the solution can be calculated using the formula pH = -log[H+], where [H+] is the concentration of hydrogen ions in the solution.

This method is simple, accurate, and can be used to determine the pH of a wide range of solutions. However, it requires the use of a suitable indicator and a standard solution of known concentration. Additionally, the end point of the titration must be accurately determined to obtain accurate results.
