### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample

Alkalinity is a measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids. It is an important parameter in water quality analysis, as it provides information about the buffering capacity of water.

Here are the steps to determine the alkalinity of a water sample:

1. Collect a water sample in a clean container.
2. Measure the pH of the water sample using a pH meter or pH test strips.
3. If the pH is above 7, the water sample is alkaline.
4. To determine the exact alkalinity of the water sample, titrate the sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid.
5. The volume of acid required to neutralize the water sample to a pH of 4.5 is used to calculate the alkalinity of the water sample.
6. The alkalinity is usually expressed in milligrams per liter (mg/L) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3).

It is important to note that the alkalinity of water can vary depending on the source and the presence of minerals and other substances that can affect the buffering capacity of water. Therefore, it is recommended to regularly test the alkalinity of water to ensure its quality.
