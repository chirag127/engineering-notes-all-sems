# Determination of Hardness of Water by EDTA Method

- Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form complexes with metal ions such as Ca2+ and Mg2+.
- EDTA titration is a method of determining the hardness of water by titrating a water sample with a standard solution of EDTA.
- The indicator used in EDTA titration is Eriochrome Black T (EBT), which is a dye that changes color from wine red to blue when it binds to metal ions.
- The pH of the water sample is adjusted to 10 ± 0.1 by adding ammonia buffer, which prevents the precipitation of metal hydroxides and enhances the color change of EBT.
- The end point of the titration is reached when the wine red color of the water sample just turns to blue, indicating that all the metal ions have been complexed by EDTA.

## Procedure for Determination of Hardness of Water by EDTA Titration

1. Pipette 20 mL of the water sample (V mL) into a 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask.
2. Dilute the sample to about 40 mL by adding distilled water.
3. Add 1 mL of ammonia buffer to adjust the pH to 10 ± 0.1.
4. Add 1 or 2 drops of EBT indicator solution. The sample should turn wine red due to the presence of metal ions.
5. Titrate the sample with a standard EDTA solution of known concentration (C mg/mL) from a burette, with constant shaking, until the color changes from wine red to blue. Record the volume of EDTA used (T mL).
6. Repeat the titration with two more aliquots of the water sample and take the average volume of EDTA used.

## Calculation of Hardness of Water by EDTA Titration

- The hardness of water (H mg/L) can be calculated by the following formula:

  H = (C × T × 1000) / V

  where C is the concentration of EDTA in mg/mL, T is the average volume of EDTA used in mL, and V is the volume of the water sample in mL.

- The hardness of water can be expressed in terms of mg/L of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) equivalent, which is the common unit for water hardness.

- The hardness of water can be classified as soft, moderately hard, hard, or very hard, depending on the concentration of CaCO3 equivalent, as shown in the table below:

| Hardness of water | Concentration of CaCO3 equivalent (mg/L) |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Soft              | < 75                                     |
| Moderately hard   | 75 - 150                                 |
| Hard              | 150 - 300                                |
| Very hard         | > 300                                    |
