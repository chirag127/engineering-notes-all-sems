# CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness and alkalinity are two important parameters that indicate the quality of water.
- Hardness measures the concentration of divalent salts, mainly calcium and magnesium, that can form deposits or scale in pipes, boilers, and appliances.
- Alkalinity measures the total amount of bases, mainly bicarbonate and carbonate, that can neutralize acids and buffer the pH of water.
- Hardness and alkalinity are related through the common ions of calcium and carbonate that can contribute to both parameters.
- Hardness can be classified into two types: temporary and permanent. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonate ions that can be removed by boiling the water. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of other ions, such as sulfate, chloride, and nitrate, that cannot be removed by boiling.
- Alkalinity can be classified into three types: carbonate, bicarbonate, and hydroxide. Carbonate alkalinity is caused by the presence of carbonate ions that can react with acids to form carbon dioxide and water. Bicarbonate alkalinity is caused by the presence of bicarbonate ions that can react with acids to form carbonate ions and water. Hydroxide alkalinity is caused by the presence of hydroxide ions that can react with acids to form water.
- The measurement of hardness and alkalinity can be done by titration methods. Titration is a technique that involves adding a known amount of a solution with a known concentration (titrant) to a sample with an unknown concentration (titrate) until a color change or an endpoint is reached.
- The measurement of hardness can be done by titrating the sample with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), which is a chelating agent that binds to the metal ions and forms a complex. The endpoint is indicated by a color change of an indicator, such as eriochrome black T, that changes from red to blue when all the metal ions are complexed.
- The measurement of alkalinity can be done by titrating the sample with a standard solution of a strong acid, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, which neutralizes the bases and lowers the pH. The endpoint is indicated by a color change of an indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange, that changes from pink to colorless or from yellow to orange, respectively, when the pH reaches a certain value.
