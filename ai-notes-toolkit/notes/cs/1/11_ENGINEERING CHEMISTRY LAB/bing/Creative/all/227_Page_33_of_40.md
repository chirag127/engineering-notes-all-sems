- The topic of this page is **the structure and function of the human eye**.
- The human eye is a complex organ that allows us to see the world around us.
- The eye consists of several parts, each with a specific role in vision. These parts are:

  - The **cornea**: a transparent layer that covers the front of the eye and refracts (bends) light rays entering the eye.
  - The **iris**: a colored ring of muscle that controls the size of the pupil, the opening that lets light into the eye.
  - The **lens**: a flexible structure that changes shape to focus light onto the retina, the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye.
  - The **ciliary body**: a ring of muscle and tissue that attaches to the lens and helps adjust its shape and position.
  - The **aqueous humor**: a clear fluid that fills the space between the cornea and the lens and provides nourishment and pressure to the eye.
  - The **vitreous humor**: a gel-like substance that fills the space between the lens and the retina and helps maintain the shape of the eye.
  - The **retina**: a thin layer of cells that converts light into electrical signals that are sent to the brain via the optic nerve.
  - The **macula**: a small area in the center of the retina that is responsible for sharp central vision and color perception.
  - The **fovea**: a tiny pit in the macula that contains the highest concentration of cone cells, the type of photoreceptors that detect color and detail.
  - The **optic nerve**: a bundle of nerve fibers that carries the visual signals from the retina to the brain.
  - The **optic disc**: a blind spot in the retina where the optic nerve exits the eye and where there are no photoreceptors.
  - The **sclera**: a tough white layer that covers the outside of the eye and protects it from injury and infection.
  - The **conjunctiva**: a thin membrane that covers the sclera and the inner surface of the eyelids and lubricates the eye.
  - The **eyelids**: movable folds of skin that cover the eye and protect it from dust, debris, and light.
  - The **eyelashes**: short hairs that grow on the edges of the eyelids and help prevent foreign objects from entering the eye.
  - The **eyebrows**: curved rows of hair above the eyes that help shade the eyes from sunlight and sweat.
  - The **lacrimal glands**: glands that produce tears that moisten and cleanse the eye and contain enzymes that fight bacteria.
  - The **lacrimal ducts**: tubes that drain the excess tears from the eye to the nose.

- The eye works by capturing and processing light in a series of steps:

  - Light rays enter the eye through the cornea, which bends them slightly and passes them to the pupil.
  - The pupil adjusts its size according to the brightness of the light, allowing more or less light into the eye.
  - The light rays then pass through the lens, which changes its shape and position to focus them onto the retina.
  - The retina contains two types of photoreceptors: rods and cones. Rods are more sensitive to light and work better in dim conditions, while cones are more sensitive to color and work better in bright conditions.
  - The photoreceptors convert the light into electrical signals that are sent to the brain via the optic nerve.
  - The brain interprets the signals and creates an image of what we see.

- The eye can also perform some adjustments to improve vision, such as:

  - Accommodation: the ability of the lens to change its shape and focus on objects at different distances.
  - Convergence: the ability of the eyes to move inward and align on the same object, creating a single image.
  - Pupillary reflex: the ability of the pupil to change its size in response to changes in light intensity, protecting the retina from damage.
  - Blinking: the ability of the eyelids to close and open rapidly, spreading tears over the eye and removing dust and debris.
  - Saccades: the ability of the eyes to make quick and involuntary movements, scanning the visual field and preventing adaptation of the photoreceptors.
