# 7. Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator.

- Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a double salt of iron(II) sulfate and ammonium sulfate, with the formula (NH4)2Fe(SO4)2·6H2O.
- It is commonly used as a reducing agent in titrations involving oxidizing agents such as potassium permanganate (KMnO4) or potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7).
- The strength of a ferrous ammonium sulfate solution can be determined by titrating it with a standard solution of an oxidizing agent, using an external indicator to detect the end point of the reaction.
- An external indicator is a substance that changes color when it comes in contact with the excess of the oxidizing agent, after the ferrous ammonium sulfate has been completely oxidized.
- One example of an external indicator is potassium ferricyanide (K3Fe(CN)6), which forms a blue precipitate of Prussian blue (Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3) with the excess of potassium permanganate, according to the reaction:

  2KMnO4 + 10K3Fe(CN)6 + 8H2O → 2MnO2 + 10K4Fe(CN)6 + 2KOH + 5Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3

- The procedure for the determination of the strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator is as follows:

  - Prepare a standard solution of potassium permanganate by dissolving a known mass of KMnO4 crystals in distilled water and diluting to a known volume.
  - Transfer a known volume of the standard KMnO4 solution to a burette and note the initial reading.
  - Transfer a known volume of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution to a conical flask and add a few drops of dilute sulfuric acid to prevent the hydrolysis of iron(II) ions.
  - Add a few drops of potassium ferricyanide solution to a small porcelain dish and place it near the mouth of the conical flask.
  - Titrate the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution with the standard KMnO4 solution, swirling the flask after each addition, until a faint blue color appears in the porcelain dish, indicating the end point of the titration.
  - Note the final reading of the burette and calculate the volume of KMnO4 solution used.
  - Repeat the titration for two more times and take the average volume of KMnO4 solution used.
  - Calculate the strength of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution using the following formula:

    Strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate = (Volume of KMnO4 × Normality of KMnO4 × Equivalent weight of ferrous ammonium sulfate) / (Volume of ferrous ammonium sulfate)

    where the equivalent weight of ferrous ammonium sulfate is 196 g/mol and the normality of KMnO4 is the molarity multiplied by the number of electrons transferred per mole of KMnO4, which is 5 in this case.
