# Determination of Cell Conductance of a Solution

- Cell conductance of a solution is the measure of how well the solution can conduct electric current.
- Cell conductance is the reciprocal of cell resistance, which is the opposition to the flow of electric current in the solution.
- Cell conductance is proportional to the concentration, charge, and mobility of the ions in the solution, which are the carriers of electric charge.
- Cell conductance is measured by using a special device called a conductance cell, which consists of two electrodes immersed in the solution and connected to a source of electric potential.
- Cell conductance is also influenced by the geometry and dimensions of the conductance cell, which are expressed by the cell constant.
- Cell constant is the ratio of the distance between the electrodes to the area of cross-section of the electrodes. It has the unit of inverse length (m^-1).
- Cell constant can be determined by measuring the cell resistance of a solution with known conductance, such as a standard solution of potassium chloride (KCl).
- Cell conductance can be calculated from the cell resistance and the cell constant by using the formula:

  Cell conductance = Cell constant / Cell resistance

- Cell conductance can also be expressed as the product of the cell constant and the specific conductance of the solution, which is the conductance of the solution per unit length and unit area.

  Cell conductance = Cell constant x Specific conductance

- Specific conductance is also known as conductivity and has the unit of siemens per meter (S/m).
- Specific conductance depends on the nature and concentration of the electrolyte in the solution, as well as the temperature and pressure of the solution.
