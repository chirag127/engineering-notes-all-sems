# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

- Calibration is the process of evaluating and adjusting the precision and accuracy of measurement equipment.
- Calibration ensures that the instruments produce valid and reliable results that are consistent with the standards and specifications.
- Calibration is essential for maintaining the quality and safety of products, processes, and services that depend on accurate measurements.
- Calibration involves comparing the output of the instrument with a known reference value, such as a certified standard or a traceable reference material.
- Calibration also involves correcting any deviation or error in the instrument's performance, such as by adjusting the settings, replacing the components, or applying a correction factor.
- Calibration should be performed at regular intervals, depending on the frequency of use, the environmental conditions, the manufacturer's recommendations, and the in-house standard operating procedures .
- Calibration should be documented and recorded, including the date, the reference value, the measured value, the correction applied, the uncertainty, and the person responsible.
- Calibration should be verified by using check standards or quality control samples, which are materials with known or assigned values that are measured before and after the calibration.
- Calibration should be done by qualified and trained personnel, using appropriate and calibrated equipment and methods.
