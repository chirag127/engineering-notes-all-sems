# 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample

- Alkalinity is the measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly caused by the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides in water.
- Alkalinity is important for water quality because it affects the pH, corrosion, and disinfection processes of water.
- Alkalinity can be determined by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, using a suitable indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange.
- The titration curve of alkalinity shows the change of pH as the acid is added to the water sample. Depending on the types and concentrations of alkaline substances in water, the titration curve may have one or two equivalence points, where the amount of acid added is equal to the amount of alkalinity in water.
- The types of alkalinity in water are classified as follows:

  - P-alkalinity: the amount of acid required to lower the pH of water to 8.3, where phenolphthalein changes color from pink to colorless. It represents the sum of hydroxides and one-half of carbonates in water.
  - M-alkalinity: the amount of acid required to lower the pH of water to 4.5, where methyl orange changes color from yellow to orange. It represents the sum of hydroxides, carbonates, and bicarbonates in water.
  - OH-alkalinity: the amount of acid required to lower the pH of water to the point where all hydroxides are neutralized. It is equal to P-alkalinity if the pH of water is above 8.3, and zero if the pH of water is below 8.3.
  - CO3-alkalinity: the amount of acid required to lower the pH of water to the point where all carbonates are neutralized. It is equal to P-alkalinity minus OH-alkalinity.
  - HCO3-alkalinity: the amount of acid required to lower the pH of water to the point where all bicarbonates are neutralized. It is equal to M-alkalinity minus P-alkalinity.

- The procedure for determining the alkalinity of water sample is as follows:

  - Take a 100 mL aliquot of the water sample in a conical flask and add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator. If the solution turns pink, record the initial pH and titrate with the standard acid solution until the color changes to colorless. Record the volume of acid used as V1. This is the P-alkalinity of the water sample.
  - If the solution does not turn pink, add a few drops of methyl orange indicator and record the initial pH. Titrate with the standard acid solution until the color changes from yellow to orange. Record the volume of acid used as V2. This is the M-alkalinity of the water sample.
  - Calculate the OH-alkalinity, CO3-alkalinity, and HCO3-alkalinity using the formulas given above.
  - Report the results in mg/L as CaCO3, using the following conversion factors:

    - 1 mL of 0.02 N H2SO4 = 1 mg/L as CaCO3
    - 1 mL of 0.02 N HCl = 1.65 mg/L as CaCO3
