# CO2: Molecular and System Properties

- Carbon dioxide (CO2) is a linear molecule with a bond length of 116.3 pm between the carbon and the oxygen atoms and a bond angle of 180 degrees.
- CO2 is a non-polar molecule with a dipole moment of zero. It has four vibrational modes: symmetric stretching, asymmetric stretching, and two degenerate bending modes.
- CO2 is a gas at standard temperature and pressure, with a density of 1.977 kg/m3 and a molar mass of 44.0095 g/mol .
- CO2 can be dissolved in water and other solvents, forming weak carbonic acid (H2CO3) and bicarbonate (HCO3-) and carbonate (CO3 2-) ions. The solubility of CO2 in water decreases with increasing temperature and increases with increasing pressure.
- CO2 can also react with some bases, such as monoethanolamine (MEA), to form carbamate salts. These reactions are reversible and depend on the temperature, pressure, and concentration of the reactants .
- Surface tension is the energy, or work, required to increase the surface area of a liquid due to intermolecular forces. Surface tension is measured in units of N/m or J/m2.
- The surface tension of pure water at 20 °C is 72.8 mN/m. The surface tension of water decreases with increasing temperature and increases with increasing salinity.
- The surface tension of CO2-loaded aqueous solutions depends on the concentration of CO2, the type and concentration of the solvent, and the temperature. For example, the surface tension of aqueous MEA solutions decreases with increasing CO2 loading and increasing MEA mass ratio, and increases with increasing temperature .
- The surface tension of CO2-loaded aqueous solutions can be measured by various methods, such as the pendant drop method, the Wilhelmy plate method, or the du Noüy ring method . These methods involve measuring the force or shape of a liquid drop or a liquid film in contact with a solid or a gas.
