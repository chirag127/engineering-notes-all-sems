# Determination of chloride content in water sample

- Chloride ions are one of the major inorganic anions in water and wastewater.
- Chloride ions can cause corrosion of metals, affect the taste of water, and increase the salinity of soil and groundwater.
- Chloride ions can be determined by various methods, such as argentometric, potentiometric, colorimetric, and gravimetric methods.
- Argentometric method is based on the precipitation of chloride ions as silver chloride by titrating with a standard silver nitrate solution using a suitable indicator.
- Potentiometric method is based on the measurement of the potential difference between a chloride ion-selective electrode and a reference electrode in a solution containing chloride ions.
- Colorimetric method is based on the formation of a colored complex between chloride ions and a reagent, such as mercuric thiocyanate, ferric thiocyanate, or mercury(II) nitrate, and measuring the absorbance of the solution at a specific wavelength.
- Gravimetric method is based on the precipitation of chloride ions as silver chloride by adding excess silver nitrate solution and filtering, washing, drying, and weighing the precipitate.

## Procedure for argentometric method (Mohr's method)

- Take 50 mL of water sample (V mL) and dilute to 100 mL in a volumetric flask.
- If the sample is highly colored, add 3 mL of aluminum hydroxide and shake well, allow to settle, filter, wash and collect the filtrate.
- Bring the sample pH to 7-8 by adding acid or alkali if necessary.
- Add 1 mL of potassium chromate indicator, which gives a yellow color to the solution.
- Titrate the solution against standard silver nitrate solution (N/50) from a burette until a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate is obtained.
- Note the volume of silver nitrate solution used (V1 mL).

## Calculation

- The reaction between chloride ions and silver nitrate is as follows:

Cl- + Ag+ -> AgCl

- The reaction between chromate ions and silver nitrate is as follows:

2CrO4^2- + 2Ag+ -> Ag2CrO4

- The equivalence point is reached when the amount of silver ions added is equal to the sum of the amount of chloride ions and one-half of the amount of chromate ions in the solution.
- The concentration of chloride ions in the water sample can be calculated by the following formula:

Cl- (mg/L) = (V1 x N x 35.45 x 1000) / V

where,

V1 = volume of silver nitrate solution used (mL)

N = normality of silver nitrate solution

35.45 = equivalent weight of chloride ion

V = volume of water sample taken (mL)

- The chloride content of the water sample can be expressed in terms of mg/L or ppm (parts per million).
