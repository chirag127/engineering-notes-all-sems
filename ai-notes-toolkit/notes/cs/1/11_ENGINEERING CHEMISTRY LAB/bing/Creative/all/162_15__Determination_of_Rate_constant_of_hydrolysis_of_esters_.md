# 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters

- Hydrolysis of esters is the reverse reaction of esterification, in which an ester reacts with water to produce a carboxylic acid and an alcohol .
- The hydrolysis of esters can be catalyzed by either an acid or a base. In acidic hydrolysis, the ester is heated with a large excess of water containing a strong-acid catalyst. In basic hydrolysis, the ester is heated with a large excess of water containing a strong-base catalyst .
- The rate of hydrolysis of esters depends on the nature of the ester, the temperature, the pH, and the concentration of the reactants. The rate law for the hydrolysis of esters can be written as:

  - For acidic hydrolysis: `rate = k[ester][H2O]`
  - For basic hydrolysis: `rate = k[ester][OH-]`

  where k is the rate constant and [ ] denotes the concentration of the species .
- The rate constant of hydrolysis of esters can be determined experimentally by measuring the concentration of the ester or one of the products as a function of time. The concentration can be measured by various methods, such as titration, spectrophotometry, conductivity, or gas chromatography .
- One example of an experiment to determine the rate constant of hydrolysis of ethyl acetate by dilute hydrochloric acid is as follows:

  - A known volume and concentration of ethyl acetate is mixed with a large excess of dilute hydrochloric acid and water in a conical flask. The flask is fitted with a reflux condenser and heated to a constant temperature in a water bath .
  - At regular intervals, a small aliquot of the reaction mixture is withdrawn and titrated against a standard solution of sodium hydroxide using phenolphthalein as an indicator. The volume of sodium hydroxide required to reach the end point is recorded .
  - The concentration of ethyl acetate at each time interval can be calculated from the volume of sodium hydroxide used and the stoichiometry of the reaction. The concentration of ethyl acetate can be plotted against time to obtain a curve that shows the decrease in concentration as the reaction proceeds .
  - The rate constant of hydrolysis of ethyl acetate can be obtained from the slope of the curve, using the equation:

    `ln[ester] = -kt + ln[ester]0`

    where [ester] is the concentration of ethyl acetate at time t, [ester]0 is the initial concentration of ethyl acetate, and k is the rate constant .
