# 5. To enable the students to understand about the synthesis of organic compounds such as adipic acid and paracetamol by conventional and green route.

- Adipic acid is a dicarboxylic acid that is used as a raw material for the production of nylon-6,6, a synthetic polymer widely used in various applications.
- Paracetamol is an analgesic and antipyretic drug that is widely used to relieve pain and fever.
- Conventional and green routes of synthesis are two different approaches to produce organic compounds from different starting materials, using different reagents, catalysts, solvents, and reaction conditions.
- Conventional routes of synthesis are usually based on the use of fossil fuels, such as benzene, as the starting material, and involve the use of hazardous chemicals, such as nitric acid, as the oxidizing agent. These routes generate a lot of waste and emissions, such as nitrogen oxides, that are harmful to the environment and human health.
- Green routes of synthesis are based on the use of renewable and biodegradable resources, such as glucose, as the starting material, and involve the use of environmentally benign chemicals, such as hydrogen peroxide, as the oxidizing agent. These routes generate less waste and emissions, and are more energy-efficient and cost-effective.

## Synthesis of adipic acid by conventional and green route

- The conventional route of synthesis of adipic acid involves the oxidation of cyclohexane, cyclohexanol, or cyclohexanone, derived from benzene, with nitric acid in the presence of a metal catalyst, such as copper or vanadium. This route produces a mixture of adipic acid and other by-products, such as glutaric acid, succinic acid, and nitrous oxide. The adipic acid is then purified by crystallization, distillation, or extraction. This route requires high temperature and pressure, and generates a lot of waste and emissions, especially nitrous oxide, which is a potent greenhouse gas and an ozone-depleting substance.
- The green route of synthesis of adipic acid involves the oxidation of cyclohexene, derived from glucose, with hydrogen peroxide in the presence of a metal catalyst, such as tungsten or molybdenum. This route produces pure adipic acid in high yield, without any by-products. The adipic acid is then recovered by simple filtration or centrifugation. This route requires low temperature and pressure, and generates no waste and emissions, except water. This route is also known as the direct oxidation of cyclohexene, and was first reported by Behr et al. in 1998.

## Synthesis of paracetamol by conventional and green route

- The conventional route of synthesis of paracetamol involves the acetylation of phenol, derived from benzene, with acetic anhydride in the presence of a catalyst, such as sulfuric acid or phosphoric acid. This route produces paracetamol and acetic acid as the main products, and some by-products, such as phenyl acetate and diacetyl. The paracetamol is then purified by crystallization, recrystallization, or chromatography. This route requires high temperature and pressure, and generates a lot of waste and emissions, especially sulfur dioxide and carbon dioxide.
- The green route of synthesis of paracetamol involves the acetylation of 4-aminophenol, derived from glucose, with acetic acid in the presence of a catalyst, such as sodium acetate or sodium bicarbonate. This route produces paracetamol and water as the only products, without any by-products. The paracetamol is then recovered by simple filtration or centrifugation. This route requires low temperature and pressure, and generates no waste and emissions, except water. This route is also known as the one-pot synthesis of paracetamol, and was first reported by Reddy et al. in 2014.
