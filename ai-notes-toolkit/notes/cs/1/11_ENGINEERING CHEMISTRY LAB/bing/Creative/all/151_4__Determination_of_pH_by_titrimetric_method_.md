# Determination of pH by titrimetric method

- Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a solution of unknown concentration (analyte) .
- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H3O+] .
- To determine the pH of a solution by titrimetric method, a pH meter can be used to monitor the change in pH as the titrant is added to the analyte  .
- A pH meter consists of a glass electrode that is sensitive to hydrogen ions and a reference electrode that provides a constant potential. The potential difference between the two electrodes is proportional to the pH of the solution .
- A plot of the pH of the solution versus the volume of the titrant added is called a titration curve. The titration curve can be used to identify the endpoint or equivalence point of the titration, where the moles of titrant are equal to the moles of analyte .
- The shape and features of the titration curve depend on the type and strength of the acid and base involved in the titration. For example, a strong acid-strong base titration curve has a sharp rise in pH near the equivalence point, while a weak acid-strong base titration curve has a more gradual rise in pH and a buffer region before the equivalence point .
- The titration curve can also be used to calculate the pH at any point of the titration, using the mole balance equation and the equilibrium constants of the acid and base .
- Titrimetric method is a useful and accurate way of determining the pH of a solution, as well as the concentration and identity of the acid and base in the solution .
