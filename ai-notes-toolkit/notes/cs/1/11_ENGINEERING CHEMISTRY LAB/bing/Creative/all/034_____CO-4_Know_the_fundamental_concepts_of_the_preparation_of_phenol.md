# CO-4 Know the fundamental concepts of the preparation of phenol

Phenol is an aromatic compound with a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to a benzene ring. It is also known as carbolic acid or phenic acid. Phenol has antiseptic and disinfectant properties and is used in the manufacture of plastics, dyes, explosives, and drugs.

There are several methods to prepare phenol from different sources, such as haloarenes, benzene, cumene, diazonium salts, and coal tar. Some of the common methods are:

- **From haloarenes:** Haloarenes are aromatic compounds with a halogen atom (-X) attached to a benzene ring. Examples of haloarenes are chlorobenzene, bromobenzene, and iodobenzene. When haloarenes are fused with sodium hydroxide (NaOH) at high temperature and pressure, sodium phenoxide (NaOC6H5) is produced. Sodium phenoxide is then converted to phenol by acidification with dilute acid or carbon dioxide (CO2). This method is also known as the Dow process .

  - **Reaction:** C6H5X + NaOH → NaOC6H5 + NaX
  - **Example:** C6H5Cl + NaOH → NaOC6H5 + NaCl
  - **Mechanism:** The halogen atom is replaced by a hydroxyl group through a nucleophilic aromatic substitution reaction. The hydroxide ion (OH-) acts as a nucleophile and attacks the carbon atom bonded to the halogen, forming a carbanion intermediate. The carbanion then loses a proton to form sodium phenoxide.

    ![Mechanism of phenol preparation from haloarenes](https://i.imgur.com/9Z8lq3Q.png)

- **From benzene:** Benzene is an aromatic compound with a six-carbon ring and three double bonds. It is the simplest and most common aromatic compound. When benzene is treated with concentrated sulfuric acid (H2SO4), benzene sulfonic acid (C6H5SO3H) is formed. Benzene sulfonic acid is then fused with sodium hydroxide at high temperature to produce sodium phenoxide, which is then acidified to yield phenol. This method is also known as the Raschig process.

  - **Reaction:** C6H6 + H2SO4 → C6H5SO3H + H2O
  - **Example:** C6H6 + H2SO4 → C6H5SO3H + H2O
  - **Mechanism:** The sulfuric acid acts as an electrophile and adds to one of the double bonds of benzene, forming a sulfonic acid group. The sulfonic acid group then loses a proton to form sodium phenoxide.

    ![Mechanism of phenol preparation from benzene](https://i.imgur.com/0v8Y1Yc.png)

- **From cumene:** Cumene is an aromatic compound with an isopropyl group (-CH(CH3)2) attached to a benzene ring. It is also known as isopropylbenzene. When cumene is oxidized by air (O2) in the presence of a catalyst, cumene hydroperoxide (C6H5C(CH3)2OOH) is formed. Cumene hydroperoxide is then cleaved by acid or base to produce phenol and acetone (CH3COCH3). This method is also known as the cumene process .

  - **Reaction:** C6H5CH(CH3)2 + O2 → C6H5C(CH3)2OOH
  - **Example:** C6H5CH(CH3)2 + O2 → C6H5C(CH3)2OOH
  - **Mechanism:** The isopropyl group is oxidized by oxygen to form a hydroperoxide group. The hydroperoxide group then breaks down to form phenol and acetone.
