# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

- Calibration is the process of evaluating and adjusting the precision and accuracy of measurement equipment.
- Calibration ensures that the equipment and apparatus are performing as expected and producing valid and reliable data.
- Calibration is required for all analytical equipment and apparatus that are used for quantitative measurements, such as balances, pipettes, thermometers, spectrophotometers, pH meters, etc .
- Calibration involves comparing the output of the equipment or apparatus with a known standard of reference, such as a certified weight, a standard solution, or a reference thermometer.
- Calibration should be done at regular intervals, depending on the frequency of use, the stability of the equipment or apparatus, the manufacturer's recommendations, and the in-house standard operating procedures .
- Calibration should be documented and recorded, including the date, time, operator, standard used, calibration results, and any corrective actions taken .
- Calibration should be verified by using check standards or quality control samples before and after the calibration.
- Calibration should be done by trained and qualified personnel, using appropriate methods and equipment .
- Calibration should be traceable to national or international standards, such as NIST, ISO, or ASTM .
