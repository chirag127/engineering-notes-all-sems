# Determination of Available Chlorine in Bleaching Powder

Bleaching powder is a compound of calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)2), calcium chloride (CaCl2) and calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2). It is commonly used as a disinfectant and a bleaching agent. The chlorine present in the bleaching powder gets reduced with time due to exposure to air and moisture. The amount of chlorine that can be liberated from the bleaching powder by the action of an acid is called the available chlorine. The percentage of available chlorine in a sample of bleaching powder can be determined by the iodometric method.

## Principle

The iodometric method is based on the reaction of chlorine with potassium iodide (KI) in an acidic medium to produce iodine (I2). The iodine is then titrated with sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) solution using starch as an indicator. The reactions are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KI} &\rightarrow 2\text{KCl} + \text{I}_2 \\
\text{I}_2 + 2\text{Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3 &\rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{S}_4\text{O}_6 + 2\text{NaI}
\end{aligned}
$$

The amount of iodine liberated is equivalent to the amount of chlorine present in the bleaching powder sample. The percentage of available chlorine can be calculated from the volume and concentration of sodium thiosulfate solution used in the titration.

## Procedure

- Weigh accurately about 0.5 g of the bleaching powder sample and transfer it to a 250 mL conical flask.
- Add about 100 mL of distilled water and shake well to dissolve the sample.
- Filter the solution through a filter paper into another 250 mL conical flask.
- Pipette out 25 mL of the filtrate into a 250 mL conical flask and add 10 mL of 10% potassium iodide solution and 5 mL of 1 M sulfuric acid.
- Titrate the liberated iodine with 0.1 N sodium thiosulfate solution using a burette until the solution becomes pale yellow.
- Add a few drops of starch solution as an indicator and continue the titration until the blue color disappears.
- Note down the volume of sodium thiosulfate solution used as the end point.
- Repeat the titration with another 25 mL aliquot of the filtrate and take the average volume of sodium thiosulfate solution used.

## Calculation

Let W be the weight of the bleaching powder sample in grams.

Let V be the average volume of 0.1 N sodium thiosulfate solution used in the titration in mL.

Let N be the normality of sodium thiosulfate solution.

The percentage of available chlorine in the bleaching powder sample can be calculated by the following formula:

$$
\text{% of available chlorine} = \frac{V \times N \times 35.5 \times 4 \times 100}{W \times 1000}
$$

where 35.5 is the equivalent weight of chlorine and 4 is the factor for 25 mL aliquot of 100 mL solution.
