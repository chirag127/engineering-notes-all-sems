# 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder

- Bleaching powder is a compound of calcium, chlorine and oxygen with the formula Ca(OCl)Cl. It is used as a disinfectant and a bleaching agent.
- Available chlorine is the amount of chlorine that can be liberated from bleaching powder when it reacts with dilute acids. It is expressed as a percentage of the mass of bleaching powder.
- The reaction of bleaching powder with dilute acid is as follows:

Ca(OCl)Cl + H2SO4 -> CaSO4 + H2O + Cl2

- The available chlorine can be determined by titrating the chlorine gas evolved with a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) using starch as an indicator. The reaction is as follows:

Cl2 + 2Na2S2O3 -> 2NaCl + Na2S4O6

- The procedure for the determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder is as follows:

  - Weigh accurately about 0.5 g of bleaching powder and transfer it to a 250 mL conical flask.
  - Add about 100 mL of distilled water and shake well to dissolve the bleaching powder.
  - Add 10 mL of 10% potassium iodide (KI) solution and 10 mL of 1 M sulfuric acid (H2SO4) to the flask. A brown color of iodine will appear due to the reaction:

Cl2 + 2KI -> 2KCl + I2

  - Titrate the liberated iodine with 0.1 N sodium thiosulfate solution until the brown color fades to pale yellow.
  - Add a few drops of starch solution as an indicator. The color will change to blue due to the formation of starch-iodine complex.
  - Continue the titration until the blue color disappears. Note the final volume of sodium thiosulfate solution used (V).
  - Repeat the procedure with a blank sample of distilled water instead of bleaching powder and note the volume of sodium thiosulfate solution used (V0).
  - Calculate the percentage of available chlorine in bleaching powder using the formula:

% available chlorine = (V0 - V) x N x 35.5 x 100 / W

where V0 and V are the volumes of sodium thiosulfate solution used for the blank and the sample, respectively, in mL; N is the normality of sodium thiosulfate solution; 35.5 is the equivalent weight of chlorine; and W is the weight of bleaching powder taken, in g.
