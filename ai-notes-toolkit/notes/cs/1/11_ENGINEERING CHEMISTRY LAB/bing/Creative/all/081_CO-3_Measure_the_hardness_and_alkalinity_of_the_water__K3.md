# CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness and alkalinity are two important parameters of water quality that indicate the concentration of dissolved minerals and bases in water respectively.
- Hardness is caused by compounds of calcium and magnesium, and by a variety of other metals, such as iron, manganese, and zinc. Hard water can cause scaling, corrosion, and reduced efficiency of water appliances and heaters.
- Alkalinity measures the total amount of bases present in water that can neutralize acids and maintain a stable pH. Alkalinity is mainly contributed by bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions, which are associated with calcium and magnesium ions. Alkalinity can buffer the water against pH changes and protect aquatic life.
- Hardness and alkalinity often occur together, because some compounds can contribute both alkalinity and hardness ions, such as calcium carbonate and magnesium carbonate. However, hardness and alkalinity are not the same, and they can vary independently depending on the source and composition of water.
- To measure the hardness and alkalinity of water, titration methods are commonly used. Titration is a technique that involves adding a known amount of a solution with a known concentration (called titrant) to a sample with an unknown concentration (called analyte) until a color change or an endpoint is reached.
- To measure the hardness of water, a titrant of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) is used, which forms a complex with the hardness ions and removes them from the solution. The endpoint is indicated by a color change of a metal indicator, such as eriochrome black T, which turns from red to blue when all the hardness ions are complexed with EDTA.
- To measure the alkalinity of water, a titrant of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) is used, which reacts with the alkalinity ions and lowers the pH of the solution. The endpoint is indicated by a color change of a pH indicator, such as phenolphthalein or bromcresol green, which turns from pink or blue to colorless when the pH reaches a certain value.
- The amount of titrant used to reach the endpoint is proportional to the concentration of the analyte in the sample. By using a calibrated burette and a known volume of sample, the hardness and alkalinity of water can be calculated in units of milligrams per liter (mg/L) as calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
