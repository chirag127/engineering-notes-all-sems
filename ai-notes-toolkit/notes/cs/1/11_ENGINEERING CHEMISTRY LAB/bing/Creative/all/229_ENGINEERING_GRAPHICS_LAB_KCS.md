# ENGINEERING GRAPHICS LAB KCS

- Engineering graphics is the discipline of creating and interpreting graphical representations of engineering objects, such as mechanical parts, structures, circuits, etc.
- Engineering graphics lab is a practical course that teaches students how to use various tools and techniques to create and analyze engineering drawings, such as sketching, orthographic projection, isometric projection, dimensioning, scaling, sectioning, etc.
- Engineering graphics lab also helps students to develop spatial visualization skills, which are essential for understanding and designing three-dimensional objects and systems.
- Engineering graphics lab is usually divided into two parts: classical geometry and computer-aided design (CAD).
- Classical geometry covers the basic concepts and principles of geometry, such as points, lines, planes, angles, polygons, circles, etc., and their applications in engineering graphics, such as construction of geometric figures, loci of points, etc.
- CAD covers the use of software applications, such as AutoCAD, SolidWorks, etc., to create and modify engineering drawings, such as 2D drafting, 3D modeling, rendering, animation, etc.
- Engineering graphics lab requires students to perform various exercises and assignments, such as drawing geometric figures, orthographic views, isometric views, sectional views, etc., using both manual and CAD methods, and to compare and evaluate their results.
- Engineering graphics lab also requires students to follow certain standards and conventions, such as ANSI, ISO, etc., for creating and presenting engineering drawings, such as symbols, abbreviations, units, formats, etc.
- Engineering graphics lab is an important and fundamental course for engineering students, as it enables them to communicate and document their ideas and designs effectively and accurately, and to understand and interpret the drawings of others.
