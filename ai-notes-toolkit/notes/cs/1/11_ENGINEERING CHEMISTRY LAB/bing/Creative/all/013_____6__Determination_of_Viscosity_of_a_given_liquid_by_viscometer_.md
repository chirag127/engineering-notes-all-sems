# 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow or deform under an applied force.
- Viscosity depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature and pressure of the fluid.
- A viscometer is a device that measures the viscosity of a fluid by observing its flow through a narrow tube or a capillary.
- The flow rate of the fluid depends on the pressure difference across the tube, the length and radius of the tube, and the viscosity of the fluid.
- The most common type of viscometer is the Ostwald viscometer, which consists of a U-shaped glass tube with two bulbs and a constant diameter capillary connecting them.
- The fluid is filled in the lower bulb and then sucked up to a mark above the upper bulb using a suction pump or a rubber bulb.
- The time taken for the fluid to flow from the upper mark to the lower mark is recorded using a stopwatch.
- The viscosity of the fluid can be calculated using the following formula:

$$\eta = \frac{\pi r^4 \rho g (t_1 - t_2)}{8 l (V_1 - V_2)}$$

where $\eta$ is the viscosity, $r$ is the radius of the capillary, $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, $t_1$ and $t_2$ are the times taken for the fluid and a reference fluid (usually water) to flow through the capillary, $l$ is the length of the capillary, and $V_1$ and $V_2$ are the volumes of the fluid and the reference fluid in the lower bulb.

- The procedure to determine the viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer is as follows:

  - Clean the viscometer thoroughly and dry it using an air jet or a filter paper.
  - Fill the lower bulb with distilled water and suck it up to the upper mark using a suction pump or a rubber bulb.
  - Allow the water to flow down through the capillary and start the stopwatch when the meniscus crosses the upper mark.
  - Stop the stopwatch when the meniscus crosses the lower mark and note down the time as $t_2$.
  - Repeat the experiment two more times and take the average time as $t_2$.
  - Empty the viscometer and rinse it with a small amount of the given liquid.
  - Fill the lower bulb with the given liquid and suck it up to the upper mark using a suction pump or a rubber bulb.
  - Allow the liquid to flow down through the capillary and start the stopwatch when the meniscus crosses the upper mark.
  - Stop the stopwatch when the meniscus crosses the lower mark and note down the time as $t_1$.
  - Repeat the experiment two more times and take the average time as $t_1$.
  - Measure the radius of the capillary using a screw gauge or a micrometer and the length of the capillary using a meter scale.
  - Measure the density of the liquid and the water using a hydrometer or a density bottle.
  - Measure the volume of the liquid and the water in the lower bulb using a measuring cylinder or a pipette.
  - Calculate the viscosity of the liquid using the formula given above.
  - Record the observations and calculations in a tabular form.
  - Report the result with proper units and significant figures.
