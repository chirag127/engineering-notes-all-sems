# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

- Calibration is the process of verifying the accuracy and precision of an analytical instrument or apparatus by comparing its measurements with known standards or reference materials.
- Calibration ensures that the instrument or apparatus is functioning properly and producing reliable and consistent results.
- Calibration is essential for maintaining the quality and validity of analytical data and for complying with regulatory requirements and quality standards.
- Calibration should be performed periodically, before and after each use, or whenever there is a change in the operating conditions or performance of the instrument or apparatus.
- Calibration should be documented and recorded, and the calibration data should be analyzed and evaluated to determine the accuracy and precision of the instrument or apparatus, and to identify any sources of error or bias.
- Calibration methods vary depending on the type and purpose of the instrument or apparatus, but generally involve the following steps:
  - Selecting appropriate standards or reference materials that have known or certified values of the parameter or property being measured, and that are compatible and traceable to the instrument or apparatus.
  - Preparing and handling the standards or reference materials according to the specified procedures and conditions, and ensuring their stability and integrity.
  - Measuring the standards or reference materials using the instrument or apparatus, and recording the observed values and any relevant information or conditions.
  - Comparing the observed values with the known or certified values of the standards or reference materials, and calculating the deviation, error, uncertainty, or bias of the instrument or apparatus.
  - Adjusting, correcting, or calibrating the instrument or apparatus based on the comparison results, and repeating the measurements until the desired accuracy and precision are achieved.
  - Reporting and documenting the calibration results, and indicating the date, time, conditions, and personnel involved in the calibration process.
  - Labeling and storing the instrument or apparatus properly, and indicating its calibration status and validity period.
