# LIST OF EXPERIMENTS

- An experiment is a scientific procedure that is carried out to test a hypothesis, observe a phenomenon, or measure a variable.
- Experiments usually involve manipulating one or more independent variables and measuring their effects on one or more dependent variables.
- Experiments can be classified into different types based on their design, purpose, or outcome. Some common types of experiments are:

  - Controlled experiments: These are experiments where only one independent variable is changed at a time, while all other variables are kept constant. The aim is to isolate the causal effect of the independent variable on the dependent variable. For example, a controlled experiment to test the effect of light intensity on plant growth would involve growing plants under different levels of light, while keeping other factors such as temperature, water, and soil constant.
  - Randomized experiments: These are experiments where the participants or units are randomly assigned to different groups or conditions. The aim is to reduce the bias or confounding factors that may affect the results. For example, a randomized experiment to test the effect of a new drug on blood pressure would involve randomly assigning patients to either receive the drug or a placebo, and measuring their blood pressure before and after the treatment.
  - Natural experiments: These are experiments where the independent variable is not manipulated by the experimenter, but rather changes naturally or due to some external factor. The aim is to exploit the natural variation in the independent variable to study its effect on the dependent variable. For example, a natural experiment to test the effect of air pollution on health would involve comparing the health outcomes of people living in areas with different levels of air pollution, while controlling for other factors such as age, income, and lifestyle.
  - Quasi-experiments: These are experiments where the independent variable is manipulated by the experimenter, but the participants or units are not randomly assigned to different groups or conditions. The aim is to approximate the causal effect of the independent variable on the dependent variable, while acknowledging the limitations and threats to validity. For example, a quasi-experiment to test the effect of a new curriculum on student achievement would involve implementing the curriculum in some schools or classes, while using the existing curriculum in others, and comparing the test scores of the students.
  - Field experiments: These are experiments that are conducted in a natural or real-world setting, rather than in a laboratory or controlled environment. The aim is to increase the external validity or generalizability of the results, while maintaining the internal validity or rigor of the design. For example, a field experiment to test the effect of a social media campaign on voter turnout would involve randomly assigning some voters to receive the campaign messages, while others do not, and measuring the actual voting behavior of the voters.
