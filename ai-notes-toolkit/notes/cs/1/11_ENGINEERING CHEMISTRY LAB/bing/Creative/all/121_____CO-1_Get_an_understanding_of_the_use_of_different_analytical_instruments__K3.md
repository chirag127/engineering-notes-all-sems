### CO-1 Get an understanding of the use of different analytical instruments. K3

- Analytical instruments are devices that are used to measure, analyze, or monitor physical, chemical, or biological properties of samples.
- Different analytical instruments have different applications, advantages, and limitations depending on the type of sample, the property of interest, and the level of accuracy and precision required.
- Some examples of analytical instruments are:

  - Spectrophotometers: These instruments measure the amount of light absorbed or emitted by a sample at different wavelengths. They can be used to determine the concentration, structure, or identity of substances in a sample based on their characteristic absorption or emission spectra.
  - Chromatographs: These instruments separate the components of a mixture based on their different affinities for a stationary phase (such as a column or a paper) and a mobile phase (such as a solvent or a gas). They can be used to identify, quantify, or purify the components of a mixture based on their retention times or elution volumes.
  - Mass spectrometers: These instruments measure the mass-to-charge ratio of ions generated from a sample by various ionization methods. They can be used to determine the molecular weight, structure, or composition of substances in a sample based on their characteristic mass spectra.
  - Electrochemical instruments: These instruments measure the electrical potential, current, or charge of a sample or an electrode in an electrochemical cell. They can be used to determine the concentration, activity, or redox state of substances in a sample based on their characteristic electrochemical behavior.
  - Microscopes: These instruments magnify the image of a sample using visible light, electrons, or other forms of radiation. They can be used to observe the morphology, structure, or function of cells, tissues, or materials in a sample based on their characteristic optical or electron properties.
