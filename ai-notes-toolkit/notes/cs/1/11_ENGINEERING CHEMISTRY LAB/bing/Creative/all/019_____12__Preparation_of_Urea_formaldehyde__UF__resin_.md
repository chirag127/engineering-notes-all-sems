# 12. Preparation of Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin

Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin is a thermosetting polymer that is widely used in adhesives, plywood, particle board, medium-density fibreboard (MDF), and molded objects. It is produced from urea and formaldehyde by a condensation reaction under alkaline or acidic conditions.

The preparation of UF resin involves three main steps :

- **Step 1**: Preparation of monomethylolurea (MMU). An aqueous solution of formaldehyde (37%) and urea (60%) are mixed in a molar ratio of 2-3:1 at pH 6-11. The mixture is heated to at least 80°C for 15-30 minutes. The reaction is exothermic and produces MMU and water as the main products. The reaction can be represented as:

```
H2N-CO-NH2 + HCHO → H2N-C(OH)(CH2OH)-NH2 + H2O
```

- **Step 2**: Preparation of dimethylolurea (DMU). The MMU solution is further heated to 90-100°C for 30-60 minutes. The reaction is also exothermic and produces DMU and water as the main products. The reaction can be represented as:

```
H2N-C(OH)(CH2OH)-NH2 + HCHO → H2N-C(OH)(CH2OH)-N(CH2OH)-CH2OH + H2O
```

- **Step 3**: Preparation of UF resin. The DMU solution is acidified to pH 0.5-3.5 by adding a mineral or organic acid such as sulfuric acid, hydrochloric acid, or formic acid. The mixture is heated to 100-110°C for 15-30 minutes. The reaction is endothermic and produces UF resin and water as the main products. The reaction can be represented as:

```
H2N-C(OH)(CH2OH)-N(CH2OH)-CH2OH + H2N-CO-NH2 → H2N-C(OH)(CH2OH)-N(CH2)-N-CO-NH2 + 2H2O
```

The UF resin can be further polymerized by heating or curing with a catalyst or a hardener to form a cross-linked network. The degree of polymerization and cross-linking affects the properties and applications of the UF resin. The UF resin has good resistance to hydrolysis, cures quickly, and has low formaldehyde emissions. However, it also has some disadvantages such as poor water resistance, low thermal stability, and brittleness.
