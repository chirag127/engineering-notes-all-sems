# Determination of Cell Conductance of a Solution

- Cell conductance of a solution is the reciprocal of its resistance, i.e. G = 1/R, where G is the conductance and R is the resistance.
- Cell conductance is measured in siemens (S) or ohms^-1^.
- Cell conductance of a solution depends on the concentration, charge, and mobility of the ions present in the solution.
- Cell conductance of a solution can be measured by using a special type of cell known as a conductivity cell and a Wheatstone bridge or a conductivity meter.
- A conductivity cell consists of two electrodes of known area and distance, immersed in the solution whose conductance is to be measured.
- The cell constant, C, is defined as the ratio of the distance between the electrodes, l, to the area of cross-section of the electrodes, a, i.e. C = l/a.
- The cell constant has units of m^-1^ and is a characteristic of the cell geometry and size.
- The cell constant can be determined by measuring the resistance of a cell filled with a solution of known conductivity, such as KCl.
- The conductivity, κ, of a solution is defined as the conductance of a unit volume of the solution, i.e. κ = G x V, where V is the volume of the solution.
- The conductivity has units of S m^-1^ and is a property of the solution.
- The conductivity of a solution can be calculated from the experimental resistance and the cell constant by using the formula: κ = C/R.
