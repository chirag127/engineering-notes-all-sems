# 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid

## Introduction

- Surface tension is a property of liquids that causes them to behave as if they have a thin elastic skin on their surface.
- Surface tension is caused by the cohesive forces between the molecules of the liquid, which tend to minimize the surface area of the liquid.
- Surface tension is measured in units of force per unit length, such as N/m or dyn/cm.
- Surface tension depends on the nature of the liquid, the temperature, and the presence of any impurities or surfactants on the surface.

## Aim

- To determine the surface tension of a given liquid by the capillary rise method.

## Theory

- The capillary rise method is based on the principle that when a liquid rises in a narrow tube (capillary), the weight of the liquid column is balanced by the upward force due to surface tension.
- The upward force due to surface tension is proportional to the circumference of the capillary and the surface tension of the liquid.
- The weight of the liquid column is proportional to the cross-sectional area of the capillary and the density of the liquid.
- Therefore, the height of the liquid column (h) is given by the formula:

  h = (4T cos θ) / (ρgr)

  where T is the surface tension of the liquid, θ is the contact angle between the liquid and the capillary wall, ρ is the density of the liquid, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and r is the radius of the capillary.

- If the liquid wets the capillary wall, the contact angle is zero and the formula simplifies to:

  h = (4T) / (ρgr)

- By measuring the height of the liquid column and the radius of the capillary, the surface tension of the liquid can be calculated.

## Apparatus

- A capillary tube of uniform bore and known radius
- A stand and clamp to hold the capillary tube vertically
- A beaker or a trough to contain the liquid
- A measuring scale or a vernier caliper to measure the height of the liquid column
- A travelling microscope or a magnifying glass to measure the diameter of the capillary
- A thermometer to measure the temperature of the liquid
- A liquid of known density, such as water, alcohol, or glycerin

## Procedure

- Clean the capillary tube and dry it with a filter paper or a cloth.
- Fix the capillary tube vertically on the stand and clamp it such that its lower end is slightly above the level of the liquid in the beaker or the trough.
- Adjust the height of the liquid in the beaker or the trough such that the liquid just touches the lower end of the capillary tube.
- Observe the rise of the liquid in the capillary tube and measure the height of the liquid column (h) with the help of the measuring scale or the vernier caliper.
- Repeat the measurement for at least three different capillaries of different radii and record the values of h and r.
- Measure the diameter of the capillaries with the help of the travelling microscope or the magnifying glass and calculate the radius of each capillary.
- Measure the temperature of the liquid with the help of the thermometer and note down the value.
- Find the density of the liquid from a standard table or calculate it using a hydrometer.
