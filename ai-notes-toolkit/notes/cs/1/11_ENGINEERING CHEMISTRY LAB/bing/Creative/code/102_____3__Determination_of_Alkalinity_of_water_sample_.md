### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample.

- Alkalinity is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids.
- Alkalinity is mainly caused by the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium, sodium, and potassium in water.
- Alkalinity is important for water quality because it affects the pH, corrosion, and disinfection processes of water.
- Alkalinity can be determined by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, using a suitable indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange.
- The titration curve of alkalinity shows the change of pH as the acid is added to the water sample. The curve has different regions depending on the types and concentrations of alkaline substances in the water sample.
- The titration curve can be divided into four regions: 
  - Region 1: The pH is above 8.3 and the water sample contains only hydroxides. The indicator is phenolphthalein and the color is pink. The endpoint is reached when the color changes to colorless. The volume of acid used is called P.
  - Region 2: The pH is between 8.3 and 4.5 and the water sample contains hydroxides and bicarbonates. The indicator is phenolphthalein and the color is colorless. The endpoint is reached when the color changes to pink. The volume of acid used is called P'.
  - Region 3: The pH is between 4.5 and 3.7 and the water sample contains bicarbonates and carbonates. The indicator is methyl orange and the color is yellow. The endpoint is reached when the color changes to orange. The volume of acid used is called M.
  - Region 4: The pH is below 3.7 and the water sample contains only carbonates. The indicator is methyl orange and the color is orange. The endpoint is reached when the color changes to red. The volume of acid used is called M'.
- The alkalinity of the water sample can be calculated from the volumes of acid used in the titration and the normality of the acid solution. The normality of the acid solution is the number of equivalents of acid per liter of solution. An equivalent of acid is the amount of acid that can donate one mole of hydrogen ions.
- The formulas for calculating the alkalinity are:

  - Total alkalinity = (P + M) x normality of acid x 50
  - Hydroxide alkalinity = P x normality of acid x 50
  - Carbonate alkalinity = (2P - M) x normality of acid x 50
  - Bicarbonate alkalinity = (M - P) x normality of acid x 50

- The units of alkalinity are milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) equivalent. 
- The alkalinity of water can be classified as follows:

  - Low alkalinity: less than 50 mg/L of CaCO3
  - Moderate alkalinity: between 50 and 150 mg/L of CaCO3
  - High alkalinity: more than 150 mg/L of CaCO3
