### Upon completion of the course the student should be able to:

- Define the basic concepts and terminology of artificial intelligence, such as agents, environments, rationality, search, knowledge representation, inference, planning, learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and robotics.
- Apply various search algorithms, such as uninformed search, informed search, local search, adversarial search, and constraint satisfaction, to solve problems that require finding optimal or near-optimal solutions.
- Represent and manipulate knowledge using propositional logic, first-order logic, and other formalisms, such as semantic networks, frames, and ontologies.
- Use inference methods, such as resolution, forward chaining, backward chaining, and probabilistic reasoning, to draw conclusions from knowledge bases.
- Design and implement planning systems that can generate sequences of actions to achieve goals in dynamic and uncertain environments.

- Analyze and process natural language texts and speech using methods such as tokenization, parsing, semantic analysis, information extraction, sentiment analysis, and dialogue systems.
- Understand and apply the basic concepts and techniques of computer vision, such as image processing, feature extraction, object recognition, face detection, and scene understanding.
- Understand and apply the basic concepts and techniques of robotics, such as kinematics, motion planning, localization, mapping, and navigation.
- Evaluate the ethical, social, and legal implications of artificial intelligence, such as its impact on human dignity, privacy, security, employment, and decision making.
