### 3. To enable the students to understand about the measure of pH, surface tension and viscosity of a liquid.

- pH is a measure of how acidic or basic a liquid is. It is defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration in moles per liter. The pH scale ranges from 0 to 14, where 0 is the most acidic, 7 is neutral, and 14 is the most basic. Some examples of liquids with different pH values are:

  - Lemon juice: pH 2
  - Vinegar: pH 3
  - Milk: pH 6.5
  - Water: pH 7
  - Baking soda solution: pH 9
  - Ammonia solution: pH 11
  - Bleach: pH 13

- Surface tension is a measure of how strongly the molecules of a liquid are attracted to each other at the surface. It is expressed in units of force per unit length, such as newtons per meter (N/m) or dynes per centimeter (dyn/cm). Surface tension causes the liquid to behave like a thin elastic membrane that resists being stretched or broken. Some examples of surface tension phenomena are:

  - Water droplets form spherical shapes because surface tension minimizes the surface area for a given volume.
  - Insects can walk on water because their weight is less than the force of surface tension that supports them.
  - Soap bubbles are formed by a thin layer of water sandwiched between two layers of soap molecules that reduce the surface tension of water.
  - Detergents and surfactants lower the surface tension of water, making it easier to wet and clean surfaces.

- Viscosity is a measure of how resistant a liquid is to flow. It is expressed in units of dynamic viscosity, such as pascal-seconds (Pa·s) or poise (P), or kinematic viscosity, such as square meters per second (m2/s) or stokes (St). Viscosity depends on the temperature, pressure, and composition of the liquid. Some examples of liquids with different viscosities are:

  - Honey: high viscosity, flows slowly and thickly
  - Water: low viscosity, flows easily and thinly
  - Oil: medium viscosity, flows moderately and smoothly
  - Glycerol: higher viscosity than water, but lower than honey
  - Mercury: lower viscosity than water, but higher than alcohol

- To measure the pH of a liquid, one can use a pH meter, a pH indicator, or a pH paper. A pH meter is an electronic device that measures the voltage difference between a reference electrode and a pH-sensitive electrode immersed in the liquid. A pH indicator is a chemical substance that changes color depending on the pH of the liquid. A pH paper is a strip of paper that is coated with a pH indicator and changes color when dipped in the liquid.

- To measure the surface tension of a liquid, one can use a force tensiometer, a drop shape analyzer, or a capillary rise method. A force tensiometer is a device that measures the force required to break or detach a liquid surface from a solid or another liquid surface. A drop shape analyzer is a device that measures the shape and size of a liquid drop hanging from a needle or resting on a surface. A capillary rise method is a technique that measures the height of a liquid column that rises in a narrow tube due to the balance of surface tension and gravity.

- To measure the viscosity of a liquid, one can use a viscometer, a rheometer, or a falling ball method. A viscometer is a device that measures the resistance of a liquid to flow through a tube or a gap under a constant pressure or a constant flow rate. A rheometer is a device that measures the deformation and stress of a liquid under a variable shear rate or a variable shear stress. A falling ball method is a technique that measures the time it takes for a ball to fall through a liquid under the influence of gravity and drag.
