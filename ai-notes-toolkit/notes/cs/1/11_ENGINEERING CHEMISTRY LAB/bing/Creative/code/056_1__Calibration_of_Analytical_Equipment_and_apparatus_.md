# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

- Calibration is the process of evaluating and adjusting the precision and accuracy of measurement equipment.
- Calibration is important for ensuring the quality and reliability of analytical results, as well as the safety of the laboratory and the environment.
- Calibration involves comparing the output of the instrument or equipment with a known reference standard, and correcting any deviation or error.
- Calibration should be performed according to standard operating procedures (SOPs) that specify the frequency, method, criteria, and documentation of the calibration process.
- The frequency of calibration depends on several factors, such as the type, usage, stability, and criticality of the instrument or equipment.
- Some examples of analytical instruments and equipment that require calibration are balances, pipettes, thermometers, pH meters, spectrophotometers, chromatographs, and titrators .
- Calibration can be done in-house by trained personnel, or by external service providers that are accredited or certified.
- Calibration records should include the identification of the instrument or equipment, the reference standard, the date and time of calibration, the calibration results, the corrective actions, and the signature of the person who performed the calibration.
