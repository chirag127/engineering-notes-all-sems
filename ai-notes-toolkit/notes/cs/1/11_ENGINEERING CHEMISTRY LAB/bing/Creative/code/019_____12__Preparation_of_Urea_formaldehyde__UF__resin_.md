### 12. Preparation of Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin

Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin is a thermosetting polymer that is widely used in adhesives, plywood, particle board, medium-density fibreboard (MDF), and molded objects. It is produced from urea and formaldehyde by a condensation reaction under alkaline or acidic conditions.

The preparation of UF resin can be divided into three main steps:

1. **Methylolation**: Urea and formaldehyde are mixed in a molar ratio of 2-3:1 at pH 6-11 and heated to at least 80°C. This results in the formation of monomethylolurea and dimethylolurea, which are the main monomers of UF resin. The reaction is reversible and depends on the temperature, pH, and concentration of the reactants.    

2. **Condensation**: The methylolureas react further with urea and formaldehyde to form linear and branched oligomers and polymers. The reaction can be catalyzed by adding a mineral or organic acid (such as sulfuric acid, hydrochloric acid, or formic acid) to lower the pH to 0.5-3.5. The degree of polymerization and the molecular weight distribution of the resin depend on the reaction time, temperature, pH, and catalyst. The condensation reaction is also reversible and can be stopped by cooling or neutralizing the mixture.    

3. **Curing**: The UF resin is applied to a substrate (such as wood or paper) and heated under pressure to form a cross-linked network. The curing process involves the elimination of water and formaldehyde from the resin molecules, which results in the formation of methylene and methylene ether bridges between the urea units. The curing conditions (such as temperature, pressure, and time) affect the properties and performance of the final product. The cured UF resin has high strength, hardness, and resistance to heat and chemicals, but also emits formaldehyde, which can cause health and environmental problems.
