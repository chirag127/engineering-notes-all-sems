# Water

Water is a substance composed of the chemical elements hydrogen and oxygen and existing in gaseous, liquid, and solid states. It is one of the most plentiful and essential of compounds. A tasteless and odourless liquid at room temperature, it has the important ability to dissolve many other substances.

Water is a tiny molecule. It consists of three atoms: two of hydrogen and one of oxygen. Water molecules cling to each other because of a force called hydrogen bonding. It's the reason why water can do amazing things.

Water is a shape-shifter. It exists in three states on Earth: liquid, gas, and solid. Liquid water is the most common form of water on Earth. It covers about 71% of the Earth's surface in oceans, lakes, rivers, and other bodies of water. Gas water is also known as water vapor. It is the invisible gas that forms when water evaporates or boils. Water vapor is present in the air and can form clouds, fog, rain, snow, and other types of precipitation. Solid water is also known as ice. It is the frozen form of water that occurs when water freezes below 0°C (32°F). Ice can form glaciers, icebergs, ice caps, snowflakes, and other shapes.

Water is the liquid that makes life on Earth possible. As water cycles from the air to the land to the sea and back again, water shapes our planet — and nearly every aspect of our lives. All living things, from tiny cyanobacteria to giant blue whales, need water to survive. Water is involved in many biological processes, such as photosynthesis, respiration, digestion, circulation, and excretion. Water also regulates the temperature of living organisms and the environment.

Water is a valuable resource for human activities. Water is used for drinking, cooking, washing, cleaning, irrigation, industry, energy, transportation, recreation, and more. Water is also a source of conflict and cooperation, as different groups of people compete for or share the limited freshwater resources on Earth. Water is a human right, as recognized by the United Nations, and a responsibility, as everyone has a role in protecting and conserving water for the present and future generations.
