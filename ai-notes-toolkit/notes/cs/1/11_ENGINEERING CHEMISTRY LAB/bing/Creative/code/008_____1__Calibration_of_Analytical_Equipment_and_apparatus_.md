### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and apparatus

Calibration is the process of evaluating and adjusting the precision and accuracy of measurement equipment. Proper calibration of an instrument allows people to have a safe working environment and produce valid data for future reference. Calibration also ensures the compliance with quality standards and regulations.

Some of the points to consider for calibration of analytical equipment and apparatus are:

- Make an annual schedule for calibrating each instrument/equipment indicating the dates when the instrument/equipment is to be calibrated.
- For all new instruments, calibrate the instrument more frequently (once in three months for a year followed by once in six months), and for existing instruments follow SOP for Calibration.
- The frequency of calibration will depend on usage, environmental conditions, stability, and criticality of the instrument/equipment. It is best to decide the intervals on the basis of in house standard operating procedure guidelines which in turn are based on equipment supplier recommendations.
- Use appropriate standards and reference materials for calibration that are traceable to national or international standards. For example, use certified mass standards for calibrating balances.
- Document the calibration results and procedures in a calibration report or certificate that includes the identification of the instrument/equipment, the date and time of calibration, the standards and reference materials used, the calibration method, the calibration data, the calibration uncertainty, and the signature of the person performing the calibration.
- Label the instrument/equipment with a calibration sticker that indicates the date of calibration, the date of next calibration, and the calibration status (pass or fail).
- If the instrument/equipment fails the calibration, take corrective actions such as repairing, adjusting, or replacing the instrument/equipment, and re-calibrate it before using it for measurements. Also, investigate the impact of the calibration failure on the previous measurements and take necessary actions to ensure the quality of the data.
- Keep the instrument/equipment clean and in good working condition. If problems with the equipment are observed, the calibration interval should be shortened or the equipment should be taken out of service.
