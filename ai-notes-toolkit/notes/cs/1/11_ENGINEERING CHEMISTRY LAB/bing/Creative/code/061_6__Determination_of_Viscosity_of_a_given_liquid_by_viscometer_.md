# 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow or deform under an applied force.
- Viscosity depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature and pressure of the fluid.
- A viscometer is a device that measures the viscosity of a fluid by observing its flow through a narrow tube or a capillary.
- The most common type of viscometer is the Ostwald viscometer, which consists of a U-shaped glass tube with two bulbs and a constant diameter capillary connecting them.
- The Ostwald viscometer works on the principle that the time taken for a fixed volume of fluid to flow through the capillary is proportional to its viscosity.
- The viscosity of a fluid can be calculated using the following formula:

    `η = kρt`

    where η is the viscosity, k is the calibration constant of the viscometer, ρ is the density of the fluid and t is the time of flow.

- The calibration constant k can be determined by using a fluid of known viscosity, such as water, and measuring its time of flow.
- The procedure for determining the viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer is as follows:

    - Clean the viscometer with distilled water and dry it with air.
    - Fill the viscometer with the liquid to be tested and immerse it in a water bath at a constant temperature.
    - Adjust the liquid level in the upper bulb to the upper mark by sucking or blowing through the side tube.
    - Open the stopcock and start the stopwatch when the liquid level reaches the lower mark in the upper bulb.
    - Stop the stopwatch when the liquid level reaches the upper mark in the lower bulb and note the time of flow.
    - Repeat the experiment at least three times and take the average time of flow.
    - Measure the density of the liquid using a hydrometer or a pycnometer.
    - Calculate the viscosity of the liquid using the formula and the calibration constant of the viscometer.
    - Record the observations and results in a tabular form.
