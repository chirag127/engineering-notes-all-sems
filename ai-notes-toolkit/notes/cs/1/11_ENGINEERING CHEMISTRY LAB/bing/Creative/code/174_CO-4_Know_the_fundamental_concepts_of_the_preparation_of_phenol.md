# CO-4 Know the fundamental concepts of the preparation of phenol

Phenol is an aromatic compound with a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to a benzene ring. It is also known as carbolic acid or phenic acid. Phenol has antiseptic and disinfectant properties and is used in the manufacture of plastics, dyes, explosives, and drugs.

There are several methods to prepare phenol from different sources, such as haloarenes, benzene, cumene, diazonium salts, and coal tar. Some of the common methods are:

- **From haloarenes**: Haloarenes are aromatic compounds with a halogen atom (-X) attached to a benzene ring. Examples of haloarenes are chlorobenzene, bromobenzene, and iodobenzene. When haloarenes are fused with sodium hydroxide at high temperature and pressure, sodium phenoxide is produced. Sodium phenoxide is then acidified with dilute acid or carbon dioxide to give phenol. This method is also known as the Dow process.

  - Example: Chlorobenzene + NaOH → Sodium phenoxide + NaCl
  - Sodium phenoxide + HCl → Phenol + NaCl

- **From benzene**: Benzene is a simple aromatic compound with six carbon atoms and six hydrogen atoms. When benzene is treated with concentrated sulfuric acid, benzene sulfonic acid is formed. Benzene sulfonic acid is then fused with sodium hydroxide to produce sodium phenoxide, which is then acidified to give phenol. This method is also known as the Raschig process.

  - Example: Benzene + H2SO4 → Benzene sulfonic acid + H2O
  - Benzene sulfonic acid + NaOH → Sodium phenoxide + Na2SO4
  - Sodium phenoxide + HCl → Phenol + NaCl

- **From cumene**: Cumene is an organic compound with the formula C6H5CH(CH3)2. It is also known as isopropylbenzene. When cumene is oxidized by air in the presence of a catalyst, cumene hydroperoxide is formed. Cumene hydroperoxide is then cleaved by dilute acid to give phenol and acetone. This method is also known as the cumene process.

  - Example: Cumene + O2 → Cumene hydroperoxide
  - Cumene hydroperoxide + H2SO4 → Phenol + Acetone

- **From diazonium salts**: Diazonium salts are organic compounds with the general formula R-N2+X-, where R is an aryl group and X is a halide ion. They are formed by the reaction of aromatic amines with nitrous acid. When diazonium salts are treated with water, they undergo hydrolysis to give phenol and nitrogen gas. This method is also known as the Sandmeyer reaction.

  - Example: C6H5NH2 + HNO2 → C6H5N2+Cl- + H2O
  - C6H5N2+Cl- + H2O → Phenol + N2 + HCl

- **From coal tar**: Coal tar is a dark brown liquid obtained from the distillation of coal. It contains various aromatic compounds, including phenol. When coal tar is fractionally distilled, phenol is obtained from the middle oil fraction at a temperature range of 443-503 K. This method is also known as the coal tar process.

  - Example: Coal tar → Fractional distillation → Phenol + Other compounds

These are some of the fundamental concepts of the preparation of phenol. Phenol is an important industrial chemical and has many applications in various fields. Phenol can also undergo various reactions, such as electrophilic aromatic substitution, oxidation, reduction, and esterification. Phenol can also form polymers, such as bakelite and nylon. Phenol is a versatile and useful compound, but it is also toxic and corrosive, so it should be handled with care.
