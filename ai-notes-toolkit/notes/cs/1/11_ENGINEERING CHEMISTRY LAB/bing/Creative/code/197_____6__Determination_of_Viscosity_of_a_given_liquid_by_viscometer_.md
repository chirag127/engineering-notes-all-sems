# 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer

## Introduction

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow or deform under an applied shear stress.
- Viscosity is important for many applications, such as lubrication, fluid dynamics, transport phenomena, and rheology.
- Viscosity can be measured by various methods, such as capillary viscometer, rotational viscometer, falling ball viscometer, etc.
- In this experiment, we will use a capillary viscometer, also known as an Ostwald viscometer, to determine the viscosity of a given liquid.

## Theory

- A capillary viscometer consists of a U-shaped glass tube with two bulbs and a narrow capillary section between them.
- The liquid to be tested is filled in the lower bulb and the upper bulb is connected to a constant pressure source, such as a water reservoir.
- The liquid flows through the capillary section due to the pressure difference and the time taken for a fixed volume of liquid to flow from one mark to another mark on the tube is measured.
- The flow rate of the liquid is inversely proportional to its viscosity, according to the Hagen-Poiseuille equation:

  $$Q = \frac{\pi r^4 \Delta P}{8 \eta L}$$

  where Q is the volumetric flow rate, r is the radius of the capillary, $\Delta P$ is the pressure difference, $\eta$ is the viscosity, and L is the length of the capillary.

- The viscosity of the liquid can be calculated by comparing the flow rate of the liquid with the flow rate of a reference fluid, such as water, whose viscosity is known at a given temperature.
- The relative viscosity of the liquid is defined as the ratio of the flow time of the liquid to the flow time of the reference fluid:

  $$\eta_r = \frac{t}{t_0}$$

  where $\eta_r$ is the relative viscosity, t is the flow time of the liquid, and $t_0$ is the flow time of the reference fluid.

- The viscosity of the liquid can be obtained by multiplying the relative viscosity with the viscosity of the reference fluid:

  $$\eta = \eta_r \eta_0$$

  where $\eta$ is the viscosity of the liquid, and $\eta_0$ is the viscosity of the reference fluid.

## Procedure

- Clean the capillary viscometer with distilled water and dry it with air.
- Fill the lower bulb of the viscometer with the liquid to be tested and immerse the viscometer in a water bath maintained at a constant temperature.
- Connect the upper bulb of the viscometer to a water reservoir and adjust the pressure difference by raising or lowering the reservoir.
- Allow the liquid to flow through the capillary section and record the time taken for the liquid to flow from the upper mark to the lower mark on the tube. Repeat the measurement at least three times and take the average value as the flow time of the liquid.
- Repeat the same steps with the reference fluid, such as water, and record the flow time of the reference fluid.
- Calculate the relative viscosity and the viscosity of the liquid using the formulas given in the theory section.

## Precautions

- Avoid air bubbles in the liquid and the capillary section, as they may affect the flow rate and the accuracy of the measurement.
- Ensure that the temperature of the water bath is constant and uniform, as the viscosity of the liquid may vary with temperature.
- Ensure that the pressure difference between the upper and lower bulbs is constant and sufficient, as the flow rate of the liquid may depend on the pressure difference.
- Ensure that the capillary viscometer is clean and free of any impurities or residues, as they may alter the flow rate and the viscosity of the liquid.
