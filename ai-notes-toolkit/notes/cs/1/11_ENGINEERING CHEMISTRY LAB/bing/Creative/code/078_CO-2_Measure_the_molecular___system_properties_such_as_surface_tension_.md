# CO2 Molecular Properties and Surface Tension

- Carbon dioxide (CO2) is a linear molecule with a bond length of 116.3 pm and a bond angle of 180 degrees.
- CO2 is a non-polar molecule with a dipole moment of zero and a symmetrical distribution of electrons.
- CO2 has a molecular weight of 44.0095 g/mol and a molar volume of 22.26 L/mol at standard temperature and pressure.
- CO2 has a critical temperature of 304.13 K and a critical pressure of 7.38 MPa.
- CO2 has a density of 1.977 kg/m3 at 0 °C and 1 atm, and a specific weight of 19.41 N/m3 at the same conditions.
- CO2 has a surface tension of 0.0148 N/m at 0 °C and 1 atm, and a surface tension of 0.0012 N/m at the critical point.
- Surface tension is the energy or work required to increase the surface area of a liquid due to intermolecular forces.
- Surface tension depends on the nature of the liquid and the solutes in the liquid, as well as the temperature and pressure.
- CO2 has weak intermolecular forces (London dispersion forces) compared to water, which has strong hydrogen bonds.
- CO2 has a lower surface tension than water, which means it is easier to spread or break the surface of CO2 than water.
- CO2 can dissolve in water and form carbonic acid, which lowers the pH and the surface tension of water.
- CO2 can also react with some solutes in water, such as monoethanolamine (MEA), and form partially carbonated solutions .
- The density and surface tension of CO2 loaded aqueous MEA solutions depend on the amine mass ratio, the CO2 loading, the temperature and the pressure .
- The density and surface tension of CO2 loaded aqueous MEA solutions generally decrease with increasing temperature and CO2 loading, and increase with increasing amine mass ratio and pressure .
