### 16. Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds

- Element detection is the process of determining the presence or absence of certain elements in an organic compound, such as carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, halogens, sulphur and phosphorus.
- Identification of functional groups is the process of determining the type and location of the specific groups of atoms that are responsible for the characteristic chemical reactions of an organic compound, such as alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, amines, etc.
- Element detection and identification of functional groups are important steps in the qualitative analysis of organic compounds, which helps to establish their structure and properties.
- Element detection can be done by various methods, such as:
  - Lassaigne's test: A general test for the detection of nitrogen, sulphur and halogens in an organic compound. It involves fusing the compound with sodium metal and extracting the sodium salts of the elements in aqueous solution. The solution is then tested for the presence of cyanide, sulphide and halide ions by various reagents.
  - Carbon and hydrogen estimation: A method to determine the percentage of carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound by burning a known mass of the compound in excess oxygen and measuring the masses of carbon dioxide and water produced.
  - Oxygen estimation: A method to determine the percentage of oxygen in an organic compound by subtracting the percentages of carbon, hydrogen and other elements from 100.
- Identification of functional groups can be done by various tests, such as:
  - Alcoholic –OH group: A functional group that consists of a hydroxyl group attached to an alkyl group. It can be detected by the following tests:
    - Lucas test: A test that involves adding a few drops of the compound to Lucas reagent (a mixture of zinc chloride and concentrated hydrochloric acid) and observing the formation of a turbid solution or a separate layer of alkyl chloride. The rate of reaction depends on the type of alcohol: tertiary alcohols react immediately, secondary alcohols react within 5 minutes, and primary alcohols do not react at room temperature.
    - Esterification test: A test that involves heating the compound with acetic acid and a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid and smelling the resulting mixture for the fruity odour of an ester.
    - Iodoform test: A test that involves adding a few drops of the compound to a solution of iodine and sodium hydroxide and observing the formation of a yellow precipitate of iodoform. The test is positive for alcohols that have a methyl group attached to the carbon bearing the hydroxyl group, such as ethanol, propan-2-ol, etc.
  - Carbonyls (Aldehydes and Ketones): Functional groups that consist of a carbon atom double-bonded to an oxygen atom. They can be detected by the following tests:
    - 2,4-dinitrophenyl hydrazine test: A test that involves adding a small amount of the compound to 2,4-dinitrophenyl hydrazine reagent and shaking well. The formation of a yellow, orange or red precipitate of 2,4-dinitrophenyl hydrazone indicates the presence of a carbonyl group. The precipitate can be further purified and recrystallized to determine the melting point and identify the specific carbonyl compound.
    - Tollen's test: A test that involves adding a few drops of the compound to a freshly prepared solution of Tollen's reagent (a mixture of silver nitrate and ammonia) and warming gently. The formation of a silver mirror on the inner surface of the test tube indicates the presence of an aldehyde group. Ketones do not react with Tollen's reagent.
    - Fehling's test: A test that involves adding a few drops of the compound to a solution of Fehling's reagent (a mixture of copper sulphate and sodium hydroxide) and heating. The formation of a red precipitate of copper oxide indicates the presence of an aldehyde group. Ketones do not react with Fehling's reagent.
