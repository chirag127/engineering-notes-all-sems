### 16. Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds.

- Organic compounds are composed of carbon, hydrogen, and other elements such as oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus, halogens, etc.
- The presence of these elements and the functional groups that they form can be detected and identified by various chemical tests.
- A functional group is a specific arrangement of atoms or bonds within a molecule that determines its chemical properties and reactions.
- Some common functional groups are alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, amides, etc.

#### 16.1 Element detection

- The detection of elements in organic compounds can be done by qualitative analysis, which involves the use of reagents that produce characteristic reactions or color changes with the elements.
- The most common methods of element detection are:

  - **Lassaigne's test**: This test is used to detect the presence of nitrogen, sulfur, and halogens in an organic compound. It involves the fusion of the organic compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube, which converts the elements into their sodium salts. The sodium extract is then tested with various reagents to detect the elements.
  - **Carbon and hydrogen detection**: The presence of carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound can be confirmed by heating the compound with copper oxide in a test tube, which produces carbon dioxide and water. The carbon dioxide can be tested with lime water, which turns milky, and the water can be tested with anhydrous copper sulfate, which turns blue.
  - **Oxygen detection**: The presence of oxygen in an organic compound can be inferred by the absence of nitrogen, sulfur, and halogens, or by the oxidation of the compound with potassium dichromate and sulfuric acid, which produces a green color.

#### 16.2 Functional group identification

- The identification of functional groups in organic compounds can be done by functional group analysis, which involves the use of specific reagents that react with the functional groups and produce characteristic products or color changes.
- The most common methods of functional group identification are:

  - **Alcohol test**: The presence of an alcohol group (-OH) in an organic compound can be detected by the following tests:
    - **Lucas test**: This test is used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols. It involves the reaction of the alcohol with Lucas reagent, which is a mixture of zinc chloride and concentrated hydrochloric acid. The formation of a turbid solution indicates the presence of a tertiary alcohol, while the formation of a turbid solution after 5 minutes indicates the presence of a secondary alcohol. A primary alcohol does not react with Lucas reagent.
    - **Iodoform test**: This test is used to detect the presence of a methyl group attached to a carbonyl group (-CO-CH3) in an organic compound, which can be an aldehyde, a ketone, or a secondary alcohol. It involves the reaction of the compound with iodine and sodium hydroxide, which produces a yellow precipitate of iodoform (CHI3) with a characteristic smell.
  - **Aldehyde and ketone test**: The presence of an aldehyde or a ketone group (>C=O) in an organic compound can be detected by the following tests:
    - **Tollen's test**: This test is used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones. It involves the reaction of the compound with Tollen's reagent, which is a solution of silver nitrate and ammonia. The formation of a silver mirror on the inner wall of the test tube indicates the presence of an aldehyde, while a ketone does not react with Tollen's reagent.
    - **Fehling's test**: This test is used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones. It involves the reaction of the compound with Fehling's reagent, which is a mixture of copper sulfate and sodium hydroxide. The formation of a brick-red precipitate of copper oxide indicates the presence of an aldehyde, while a ketone does not react with Fehling's reagent.
  - **Carboxylic acid test**: The presence of a carboxylic acid group (-COOH) in an organic compound can be detected by the following tests:
    - **Sodium bicarbonate test**: This test is used to confirm the presence of a carboxylic acid group. It involves the reaction of the compound with sodium bicarbonate, which produces carbon dioxide gas. The evolution of carbon dioxide gas can be tested with lime water, which turns milky.
    - **Esterification test**: This test is used to identify the presence of a carboxylic acid group.
