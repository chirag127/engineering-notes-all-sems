# 1. To enable the students to understand about the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments

- Analytical instruments are devices that measure, analyze, or monitor physical, chemical, biological, or environmental properties of samples.
- Analytical instruments can be classified into different categories based on their principles, applications, or techniques, such as spectroscopy, chromatography, mass spectrometry, electrochemistry, microscopy, etc.
- Analytical instruments can be used for various purposes, such as quality control, research and development, diagnosis, forensics, environmental monitoring, etc.
- Analytical instruments can have different components, such as sources, detectors, transducers, amplifiers, filters, processors, displays, etc.
- Analytical instruments can have different characteristics, such as sensitivity, selectivity, accuracy, precision, resolution, range, speed, etc.
- Analytical instruments can have different advantages and limitations, such as cost, complexity, reliability, robustness, portability, etc.

Some of the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments are:

- Signal: A signal is a representation of information that can be measured, transmitted, processed, or displayed by an analytical instrument. A signal can be analog or digital, continuous or discrete, periodic or aperiodic, deterministic or stochastic, etc.
- Noise: Noise is any unwanted or random variation in a signal that can affect the quality or accuracy of an analytical instrument. Noise can be classified into different types, such as thermal noise, shot noise, flicker noise, environmental noise, etc.
- Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR): SNR is a measure of the quality of a signal relative to the noise level. SNR can be expressed in decibels (dB) as SNR = 10 log10 (Psignal / Pnoise), where Psignal and Pnoise are the average power of the signal and the noise, respectively. A higher SNR indicates a better signal quality and a lower noise level.
- Calibration: Calibration is the process of adjusting or correcting an analytical instrument to ensure that it produces accurate and reliable results. Calibration can be done by comparing the instrument output with a known standard or reference, or by applying a known correction factor or function.
- Validation: Validation is the process of verifying that an analytical instrument meets the specifications and requirements for its intended use. Validation can be done by testing the instrument performance, accuracy, precision, repeatability, reproducibility, etc. using appropriate methods and criteria.
- Maintenance: Maintenance is the process of keeping an analytical instrument in good working condition and preventing or repairing any faults or damages. Maintenance can be done by following the manufacturer's instructions, cleaning, lubricating, replacing, or upgrading the instrument components, or by performing regular checks and tests.
