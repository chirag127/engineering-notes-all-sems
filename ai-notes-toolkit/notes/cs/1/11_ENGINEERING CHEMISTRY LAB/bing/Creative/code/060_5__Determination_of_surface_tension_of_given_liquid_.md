# 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid

- Surface tension is a property of a liquid surface that makes it act as if it were a stretched elastic membrane    .
- Surface tension is caused by the attraction of the molecules in the surface layer by the bulk of the liquid, which tends to minimize the surface area   .
- Surface tension is measured in units of force per unit length, such as newtons per meter (N/m) or dynes per centimeter (dyn/cm) .
- Surface tension is responsible for many phenomena, such as the shape of liquid drops, the ability of some insects to walk on water, and the formation of soap bubbles  .
- Surface tension can be determined by various methods, such as the capillary rise method, the drop weight method, the drop volume method, the ring method, and the bubble pressure method.
- In this experiment, we will use the capillary rise method to determine the surface tension of a given liquid.
- The capillary rise method is based on the principle that when a capillary tube of small diameter is dipped into a liquid, the liquid rises or falls inside the tube depending on the relative magnitude of the adhesive and cohesive forces.
- The adhesive force is the attraction between the liquid molecules and the tube wall, while the cohesive force is the attraction between the liquid molecules themselves.
- If the adhesive force is greater than the cohesive force, the liquid will rise inside the tube and form a concave meniscus. This is the case for water and glass.
- If the cohesive force is greater than the adhesive force, the liquid will fall inside the tube and form a convex meniscus. This is the case for mercury and glass.
- The height of the liquid column inside the tube depends on the surface tension of the liquid, the radius of the tube, the density of the liquid, and the acceleration due to gravity.
- The relationship between these quantities is given by the following formula:

$$h = \frac{2\gamma \cos \theta}{\rho gr}$$

where $h$ is the height of the liquid column, $\gamma$ is the surface tension of the liquid, $\theta$ is the contact angle between the liquid and the tube wall, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $r$ is the radius of the tube.

- To determine the surface tension of a given liquid, we need to measure the height of the liquid column, the radius of the tube, the density of the liquid, and the contact angle. The acceleration due to gravity is assumed to be constant and known.
- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:

  - Clean and dry a capillary tube of known radius and a measuring scale.
  - Dip the tube into the liquid and observe the rise or fall of the liquid inside the tube.
  - Measure the height of the liquid column from the surface of the liquid to the top of the meniscus using the scale.
  - Repeat the steps 2 and 3 for at least three different tubes of different radii.
  - Calculate the average height of the liquid column for each tube.
  - Measure the density of the liquid using a hydrometer or a balance and a graduated cylinder.
  - Estimate the contact angle between the liquid and the tube wall using a protractor or a reference table.
  - Substitute the values of the height, radius, density, and contact angle into the formula and solve for the surface tension of the liquid.
  - Report the result with the appropriate units and significant figures.
