### 7. Determination of the strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator

- Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a double salt of iron(II) sulfate and ammonium sulfate, with the formula FeSO4.(NH4)2SO4.6H2O.
- It is used as a primary standard in volumetric analysis, because it is stable in air and has a high equivalent mass.
- The strength of a ferrous ammonium sulfate solution can be determined by titrating it with a standard solution of potassium permanganate (KMnO4), which is a strong oxidizing agent.
- The reaction between ferrous ammonium sulfate and potassium permanganate is:

`5 FeSO4.(NH4)2SO4.6H2O + KMnO4 + 8 H2SO4 -> 5 Fe2(SO4)3 + K2SO4 + 2 MnSO4 + (NH4)2SO4 + 24 H2O`

- As the titration proceeds, the drop of the mixture on the tile changes color, indicating the end point.
- The color change of the external indicator depends on the type of indicator used.
- For diphenylamine sulfonate, the color change is from colorless to blue-violet.
- For starch-iodide, the color change is from yellow to blue-black.
