# Determination of chloride content in water sample

- Chloride ions in water can be determined by various methods, such as argentometric, potentiometric, or colorimetric methods.
- One of the most common and simple methods is the argentometric method, also known as Mohr's method, which involves titration of the water sample with silver nitrate solution using potassium chromate as an indicator.
- The principle of this method is based on the precipitation reaction between silver ions and chloride ions, forming a white precipitate of silver chloride:

    Ag+ + Cl- -> AgCl(s)

- The endpoint of the titration is detected by the formation of a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate, which indicates that all the chloride ions have been precipitated and the excess silver ions have reacted with the chromate ions:

    2Ag+ + CrO4^2- -> Ag2CrO4(s)

- The pH of the water sample should be adjusted to 7-8 by adding acid or alkali, as the chromate indicator is sensitive to pH changes and may give false results if the pH is too low or too high.
- The concentration of chloride ions in the water sample can be calculated from the volume of silver nitrate solution used and its molarity, using the following formula:

    Cl- (mg/L) = (AgNO3 (mL) x AgNO3 (M) x 35.45 x 1000) / V (mL)

    where AgNO3 (mL) is the volume of silver nitrate solution used in the titration, AgNO3 (M) is the molarity of silver nitrate solution, 35.45 is the atomic weight of chlorine, and V (mL) is the volume of water sample taken for the titration.

- The procedure for the determination of chloride content in water sample by Mohr's method is as follows:

    1. Take 50 mL of water sample (V mL) and dilute to 100 mL with distilled water in a volumetric flask.
    2. If the water sample is highly colored, add 3 mL of aluminum hydroxide and shake well, allow to settle, filter, wash and collect the filtrate.
    3. Bring the pH of the water sample to 7-8 by adding acid or alkali, using a pH meter or pH paper to check the pH.
    4. Add 1 mL of potassium chromate indicator to the water sample and mix well.
    5. Titrate the water sample with standard silver nitrate solution from a burette until a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate is obtained. Note the final volume of silver nitrate solution used (AgNO3 (mL)).
    6. Repeat the titration with another aliquot of water sample and take the average volume of silver nitrate solution used.
    7. Calculate the concentration of chloride ions in the water sample using the formula given above.

- The advantages of this method are that it is simple, rapid, and accurate. The disadvantages are that it requires a standard silver nitrate solution, which is unstable and needs to be standardized frequently, and that it may give erroneous results if the water sample contains other ions that can precipitate with silver ions, such as bromide, iodide, cyanide, sulfide, or thiocyanate.
