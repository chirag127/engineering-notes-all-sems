# 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters

- Hydrolysis of esters is a chemical reaction in which an ester is converted into a carboxylic acid and an alcohol by the addition of water.
- The general equation for the hydrolysis of esters is:

`R-CO-OR' + H2O -> R-COOH + R'-OH`

where R and R' are alkyl or aryl groups.

- The hydrolysis of esters can be catalyzed by either acids or bases. In this experiment, we will use an acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of ethyl acetate, which is an ester of acetic acid and ethanol.

`CH3-CO-OCH2-CH3 + H2O -> CH3-COOH + CH3-CH2-OH`

- The rate of the hydrolysis of ethyl acetate depends on the concentration of ethyl acetate, water, and the acid catalyst. The rate law for the reaction is:

`rate = k[CH3-CO-OCH2-CH3][H2O][H+]`

where k is the rate constant, and [ ] denotes the concentration of the species.

- To determine the rate constant of the hydrolysis of ethyl acetate, we will use a method called conductometry, which measures the electrical conductivity of a solution. The conductivity of a solution depends on the number and type of ions present in the solution. As the hydrolysis of ethyl acetate proceeds, the concentration of ions in the solution changes, and so does the conductivity.

- The procedure for the experiment is as follows:

  - Prepare a 0.1 M solution of ethyl acetate and a 0.1 M solution of hydrochloric acid in separate beakers.
  - Set up a conductivity meter and a burette with a clamp and a stand.
  - Fill the burette with the ethyl acetate solution and adjust the initial volume to zero.
  - Pour 50 mL of the hydrochloric acid solution into a 100 mL conical flask and place it on a magnetic stirrer with a stirring bar.
  - Connect the conductivity meter to the conical flask with electrodes and turn on the meter.
  - Record the initial conductivity of the solution.
  - Start a stopwatch and add 1 mL of the ethyl acetate solution from the burette to the conical flask. Stir the solution well and record the conductivity and the time.
  - Repeat the above step until 25 mL of the ethyl acetate solution is added. Record the final conductivity and the time.
  - Plot a graph of conductivity versus time and find the slope of the graph.
  - Use the slope of the graph and the following equation to calculate the rate constant of the hydrolysis of ethyl acetate:

  `slope = -k[CH3-CO-OCH2-CH3]0[H2O]0[H+]0`

  where [CH3-CO-OCH2-CH3]0, [H2O]0, and [H+]0 are the initial concentrations of ethyl acetate, water, and hydrochloric acid, respectively.

- The rate constant of the hydrolysis of ethyl acetate can be used to compare the reactivity of different esters and to study the effect of temperature, catalyst, and solvent on the rate of hydrolysis.
