### 2. Determination of Hardness of Water by EDTA Method

Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. These ions form insoluble precipitates with soap and interfere with the action of detergents. Hardness of water can be determined by various methods, but one of the most common and accurate methods is the complexometric titration using EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) as the titrant.

EDTA is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions by donating four pairs of electrons from its two nitrogen and four oxygen atoms. EDTA has a stronger affinity for calcium and magnesium ions than for other metal ions, so it can selectively titrate them in the presence of a suitable indicator.

The indicator used for this titration is Eriochrome Black T (EBT), which is a metallochromic indicator that changes color depending on the metal ion it is bound to. EBT forms a wine-red complex with calcium and magnesium ions at a pH of 10 ± 0.1, but when EDTA is added, it displaces EBT from the metal ions and forms a more stable complex. This causes the color of the solution to change from wine-red to blue, indicating the end point of the titration.

The procedure for the determination of hardness of water by EDTA method is as follows   :

- Take a sample volume of 20 mL (V mL) of water in a conical flask.
- Dilute the sample to 40 mL by adding 20 mL of distilled water.
- Add 1 mL of ammonia buffer to bring the pH to 10 ± 0.1.
- Add 1 or 2 drops of EBT indicator solution.
- The solution will turn wine-red due to the formation of EBT-metal complexes.
- Titrate the solution with EDTA solution of known concentration (C M) from a burette with vigorous shaking until the color changes from wine-red to blue.
- Note the volume of EDTA solution used (V E mL).
- Repeat the titration with a blank solution of distilled water and subtract the blank reading from the sample reading to get the corrected volume of EDTA solution used (V C mL).

The hardness of water can be calculated from the following formula:

Hardness of water (mg/L as CaCO3) = (V C x C M x 1000) / V

Where,

V C = corrected volume of EDTA solution used (mL)

C M = concentration of EDTA solution (mg CaCO3/mL)

V = volume of water sample (mL)

The hardness of water can be expressed in different units, such as ppm, grains per gallon, degrees Clark, degrees French, etc. The conversion factors are given below:

1 mg/L as CaCO3 = 1 ppm as CaCO3 = 0.058 grains per gallon as CaCO3 = 0.07 degrees Clark as CaCO3 = 0.1 degrees French as CaCO3

The hardness of water can be classified into different categories based on the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions, as shown in the table below:

| Category | Hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Soft     | < 75                     |
| Moderately hard | 75 - 150          |
| Hard     | 150 - 300                |
| Very hard | > 300                    |

The hardness of water can also be divided into two types: temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, which can be removed by boiling the water. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, which cannot be removed by boiling the water. The total hardness of water is the sum of temporary and permanent hardness. The temporary and permanent hardness can be determined separately by titrating the water sample with EDTA before and after boiling, respectively. The difference between the total hardness and the permanent hardness gives the temporary hardness.
