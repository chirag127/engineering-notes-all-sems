### 10. Determination of chloride content in water sample

- Chloride is one of the major anions found in natural waters. It originates from various sources, such as seawater intrusion, industrial effluents, agricultural runoff, and domestic sewage.
- Chloride can affect the taste, corrosion, and biological activity of water. Therefore, it is important to monitor and control its concentration in drinking water, wastewater, and environmental samples.
- One of the common methods for determining chloride content in water sample is the **Mohr method**. It is based on the precipitation of chloride as silver chloride (AgCl) by adding a known amount of silver nitrate (AgNO3) solution in the presence of chromate indicator.
- The chromate indicator forms a red-brown precipitate of silver chromate (Ag2CrO4) when the silver nitrate is in slight excess. This indicates the end point of the titration.
- The amount of silver nitrate used is proportional to the chloride content in the water sample. The chloride concentration can be calculated by using the following formula:

`Cl- (mg/L) = (V1 * N1 * 35.45) / V2`

where V1 is the volume of silver nitrate solution used (mL), N1 is the normality of silver nitrate solution, 35.45 is the equivalent weight of chloride (g/equivalent), and V2 is the volume of water sample taken (mL).

- The steps for performing the Mohr method are as follows:

  1. Pipette a known volume of water sample (usually 50 mL) into a conical flask.
  2. Add 1-2 mL of potassium chromate indicator solution and mix well.
  3. Titrate the sample with standard silver nitrate solution from a burette until a permanent red-brown color appears. Record the final burette reading.
  4. Repeat the titration with a blank (distilled water) and a standard (known chloride solution) to check the accuracy and precision of the method.
  5. Calculate the chloride concentration in the water sample by using the formula given above.
