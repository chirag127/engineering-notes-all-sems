# Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator

- Potassium dichromate is a primary standard substance that can be used to determine the strength of a reducing agent such as ferrous ammonium sulphate.
- The reaction between potassium dichromate and ferrous ammonium sulphate is as follows:

  $$K_2Cr_2O_7 + 6FeSO_4 + 7H_2SO_4 \rightarrow Cr_2(SO_4)_3 + 3K_2SO_4 + 6Fe_2(SO_4)_3 + 7H_2O$$

- The end point of the titration can be detected by the change in colour of the solution from green to reddish-brown, due to the formation of ferric ions.
- This colour change is the internal indicator of the titration, and no external indicator is required.
- The strength of potassium dichromate can be calculated by using the following formula:

  $$\text{Strength of potassium dichromate} = \frac{6 \times \text{Normality of ferrous ammonium sulphate} \times \text{Volume of ferrous ammonium sulphate used}}{\text{Volume of potassium dichromate used}}$$

- The normality of ferrous ammonium sulphate can be determined by titrating it against a standard solution of potassium permanganate, using phenolphthalein as an external indicator. The reaction is as follows:

  $$5FeSO_4 + KMnO_4 + 8H_2SO_4 \rightarrow 5Fe_2(SO_4)_3 + K_2SO_4 + MnSO_4 + 8H_2O$$

- The end point of the titration is marked by the disappearance of the pink colour of the permanganate solution.
- The normality of ferrous ammonium sulphate can be calculated by using the following formula:

  $$\text{Normality of ferrous ammonium sulphate} = \frac{5 \times \text{Normality of potassium permanganate} \times \text{Volume of potassium permanganate used}}{\text{Volume of ferrous ammonium sulphate used}}$$

- The procedure for the determination of the strength of potassium dichromate using internal indicator is as follows:

  - Prepare a standard solution of potassium permanganate by dissolving about 3.2 g of KMnO4 in distilled water and make up the volume to 1 L in a volumetric flask.
  - Prepare a solution of ferrous ammonium sulphate by dissolving about 7 g of FeSO4.(NH4)2SO4.6H2O in distilled water and add 20 mL of dilute sulphuric acid to prevent oxidation. Make up the volume to 250 mL in a volumetric flask.
  - Pipette out 10 mL of the ferrous ammonium sulphate solution into a conical flask and add 20 mL of dilute sulphuric acid.
  - Titrate the solution against the potassium permanganate solution, using phenolphthalein as an indicator, until the pink colour of the permanganate solution disappears. Note down the volume of potassium permanganate used.
  - Repeat the titration for two more times and take the average volume of potassium permanganate used.
  - Calculate the normality of ferrous ammonium sulphate by using the formula given above.
  - Pipette out 10 mL of the potassium dichromate solution into another conical flask and add 20 mL of dilute sulphuric acid.
  - Titrate the solution against the ferrous ammonium sulphate solution, using the colour change of the solution as the internal indicator, until the solution turns from green to reddish-brown. Note down the volume of ferrous ammonium sulphate used.
  - Repeat the titration for two more times and take the average volume of ferrous ammonium sulphate used.
  - Calculate the strength of potassium dichromate by using the formula given above.
