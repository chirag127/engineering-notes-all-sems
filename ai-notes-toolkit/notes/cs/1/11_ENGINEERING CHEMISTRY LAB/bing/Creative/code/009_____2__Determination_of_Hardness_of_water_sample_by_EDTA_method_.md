### 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method

Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. These ions form insoluble precipitates with soap and interfere with the cleaning action. Hardness of water can be classified into temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, which can be removed by boiling the water. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of chlorides, sulfates and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, which cannot be removed by boiling.

EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions in water. EDTA can be used to titrate the hardness of water by reacting with the calcium and magnesium ions and changing the color of an indicator. The indicator used is Eriochrome Black T, which is a metallochromic indicator that changes color from wine red to blue when it binds to metal ions.

The procedure for determining the hardness of water by EDTA method is as follows:

- Take a sample volume of 20 ml (V ml) of water in a conical flask.
- Dilute the sample to 40 ml by adding 20 ml of distilled water.
- Add 1 ml of ammonia buffer to bring the pH to 10 ± 0.1. The buffer consists of a mixture of ammonium chloride and ammonia solution.
- Add 1 or 2 drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator solution. The indicator will turn the solution wine red due to the presence of calcium and magnesium ions.
- Titrate the sample with EDTA solution of known concentration (C mol/L) from a burette until the color changes from wine red to blue. Note the volume of EDTA solution used (B ml).
- Repeat the titration with a blank solution of distilled water and note the volume of EDTA solution used (A ml).
- Calculate the hardness of water using the formula:

Hardness of water (mg/L as CaCO3) = (B - A) x C x 1000 / V

The hardness of water can be expressed in terms of calcium carbonate equivalent, which is the mass of calcium carbonate that would produce the same hardness as the sample. The molar mass of calcium carbonate is 100 g/mol. The molar ratio of EDTA to calcium or magnesium is 1:1. Therefore, 1 mol of EDTA can react with 100 g of calcium carbonate. Hence, the factor 1000 in the formula is used to convert the molarity of EDTA to the mass of calcium carbonate equivalent.
