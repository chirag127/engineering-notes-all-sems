# List of Experiments

An experiment is a scientific procedure that aims to test a hypothesis, answer a question, or demonstrate a known fact. Experiments usually involve manipulating one or more variables and measuring their effects on other variables. Experiments can be classified into different types based on their purpose, design, or outcome.

Some of the common types of experiments are:

- **Controlled experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher controls all the factors that could affect the outcome, except for the independent variable, which is the factor that is being tested. The independent variable is manipulated by the researcher, and the dependent variable is measured by the researcher. The dependent variable is expected to change as a result of the independent variable. A controlled experiment usually has a control group and an experimental group, which are identical in every way except for the independent variable. The control group serves as a baseline for comparison with the experimental group. An example of a controlled experiment is testing the effect of different fertilizers on plant growth.

- **Natural experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher does not manipulate any variables, but observes the effects of a natural event or phenomenon on the variables of interest. The natural event or phenomenon acts as the independent variable, and the dependent variable is measured by the researcher. The researcher cannot control the natural event or phenomenon, but can control other factors that could affect the outcome, such as the selection of the sample, the measurement of the variables, and the analysis of the data. An example of a natural experiment is studying the impact of a volcanic eruption on the climate.

- **Quasi-experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher manipulates the independent variable, but does not have full control over other factors that could affect the outcome, such as the assignment of the participants to the groups, the randomization of the groups, or the presence of confounding variables. Confounding variables are factors that are not the independent variable, but could influence the dependent variable. Quasi-experiments are often used when controlled experiments are not feasible or ethical, such as in social sciences, education, or public health. An example of a quasi-experiment is evaluating the effect of a new curriculum on student achievement.

- **Field experiments**: These are experiments that are conducted in a natural setting, rather than in a laboratory or a controlled environment. Field experiments can be controlled, natural, or quasi-experiments, depending on the degree of control the researcher has over the variables and the setting. Field experiments are often used to study the behavior of humans, animals, or plants in their natural habitats, or to test the applicability of a theory or a method in a real-world context. An example of a field experiment is testing the effectiveness of a new vaccine in a rural community.

- **Laboratory experiments**: These are experiments that are conducted in a controlled environment, such as a laboratory, where the researcher can manipulate the variables and the setting to a high degree. Laboratory experiments can be controlled, natural, or quasi-experiments, depending on the purpose and the design of the experiment. Laboratory experiments are often used to study the basic principles of science, such as physics, chemistry, or biology, or to test the validity of a hypothesis or a model. An example of a laboratory experiment is measuring the speed of light in a vacuum.
