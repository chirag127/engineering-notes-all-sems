### 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid.

- Surface tension is the property of a liquid that makes its surface behave like a stretched elastic membrane.
- Surface tension is caused by the cohesive forces between the molecules of the liquid, which tend to minimize the surface area of the liquid.
- Surface tension can be measured by various methods, such as the capillary rise method, the drop weight method, the ring method, and the stalagmometer method.
- In this experiment, we will use the stalagmometer method to determine the surface tension of a given liquid.
- A stalagmometer is a device that consists of a glass tube with a bulb at one end and a fine nozzle at the other end.
- The tube is filled with the liquid to be tested and then inverted over a graduated cylinder.
- The liquid drops from the nozzle and the number of drops and the volume of liquid collected in the cylinder are noted.
- The surface tension of the liquid is given by the formula:

$$\gamma = \frac{4\pi r^3 \rho g}{3V}$$

where $\gamma$ is the surface tension, $r$ is the radius of the nozzle, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $V$ is the volume of liquid per drop.

- To perform the experiment, we need the following apparatus:

  - A stalagmometer
  - A graduated cylinder
  - A thermometer
  - A stopwatch
  - A balance
  - A liquid to be tested (e.g. water, alcohol, glycerin, etc.)
  - A reference liquid (e.g. distilled water)

- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:

  1. Clean and dry the stalagmometer and the graduated cylinder.
  2. Fill the stalagmometer with the reference liquid up to the mark on the bulb and note the temperature of the liquid.
  3. Invert the stalagmometer over the graduated cylinder and allow the liquid to drop from the nozzle.
  4. Start the stopwatch when the first drop falls and stop it when the 50th drop falls.
  5. Note the time taken for 50 drops and the volume of liquid collected in the cylinder.
  6. Repeat the steps 2 to 5 for the liquid to be tested.
  7. Calculate the volume of liquid per drop for both the reference liquid and the liquid to be tested using the formula:

  $$V = \frac{V_c}{N}$$

  where $V$ is the volume of liquid per drop, $V_c$ is the volume of liquid collected in the cylinder, and $N$ is the number of drops.

  8. Calculate the surface tension of the liquid to be tested using the formula:

  $$\gamma = \frac{\gamma_r V_r}{V}$$

  where $\gamma$ is the surface tension of the liquid to be tested, $\gamma_r$ is the surface tension of the reference liquid, $V_r$ is the volume of liquid per drop for the reference liquid, and $V$ is the volume of liquid per drop for the liquid to be tested.

  9. Record the observations and results in a tabular form and compare the surface tension of the liquid to be tested with the theoretical values.
