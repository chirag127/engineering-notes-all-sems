### 16. Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds

- Element detection is the process of determining the presence and quantity of elements in an organic compound, such as carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, halogens, sulfur, and phosphorus.
- Identification of functional groups is the process of determining the type and location of the specific groups of atoms that are responsible for the characteristic chemical reactions of an organic compound, such as alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, amines, etc.
- Element detection and identification of functional groups are important for the analysis and classification of organic compounds, as they provide information about their structure, properties, and reactivity.
- Element detection and identification of functional groups can be done by various methods, such as:
  - Physical methods, such as solubility, melting point, boiling point, refractive index, etc.
  - Chemical methods, such as combustion analysis, Lassaigne's test, sodium fusion test, etc.
  - Spectroscopic methods, such as mass spectrometry, infrared spectroscopy, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, etc.
- Some examples of chemical tests for the detection of elements and functional groups in organic compounds are:
  - Carbon and hydrogen: These elements can be detected by heating the organic compound with copper oxide in a test tube. The carbon is oxidized to carbon dioxide, which turns lime water milky, and the hydrogen is oxidized to water, which condenses on the cooler part of the test tube.
  - Nitrogen: This element can be detected by Lassaigne's test, which involves heating the organic compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube. The nitrogen is converted to sodium cyanide, which can be extracted with water and tested with ferrous sulfate and ferric chloride solutions. The formation of a Prussian blue precipitate indicates the presence of nitrogen.
  - Oxygen: This element can be detected by heating the organic compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube. The oxygen is converted to sodium oxide, which can be extracted with water and tested with phenolphthalein indicator. The formation of a pink color indicates the presence of oxygen.
  - Halogens: These elements can be detected by sodium fusion test, which involves heating the organic compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube. The halogens are converted to sodium halides, which can be extracted with water and tested with silver nitrate solution. The formation of a white, cream, or yellow precipitate indicates the presence of chlorine, bromine, or iodine, respectively.
  - Sulfur: This element can be detected by sodium fusion test, which involves heating the organic compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube. The sulfur is converted to sodium sulfide, which can be extracted with water and tested with lead acetate solution. The formation of a black precipitate indicates the presence of sulfur.
  - Phosphorus: This element can be detected by sodium fusion test, which involves heating the organic compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube. The phosphorus is converted to sodium phosphate, which can be extracted with water and tested with ammonium molybdate solution. The formation of a yellow precipitate indicates the presence of phosphorus.
  - Alcoholic –OH group: This functional group can be detected by the following tests:
    - Lucas test: This test involves adding a few drops of the organic compound to a mixture of zinc chloride and concentrated hydrochloric acid in a test tube. The formation of a turbid solution or a separate layer indicates the presence of an alcoholic –OH group. The rate of turbidity depends on the type of alcohol: primary alcohols give turbidity after several minutes, secondary alcohols give turbidity within a few minutes, and tertiary alcohols give turbidity immediately.
    - Esterification test: This test involves adding a few drops of the organic compound to a mixture of acetic acid and concentrated sulfuric acid in a test tube. The formation of a fruity smell indicates the presence of an alcoholic –OH group. The smell is due to the formation of an ester by the reaction of the alcohol and the acetic acid.
