# Formaldehyde and Urea Formaldehyde Resin

- Formaldehyde is a colorless, flammable, strong-smelling chemical that is used in building materials and to produce many household products.
- Formaldehyde is also a naturally occurring substance in the environment. It is produced by the combustion of organic matter and by some biological processes.
- Formaldehyde can cause irritation of the eyes, nose, and throat, as well as respiratory problems and allergic reactions. It is also classified as a human carcinogen by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC).
- Urea formaldehyde (UF) is a nontransparent thermosetting resin or polymer that is produced from urea and formaldehyde.
- UF is widely used in adhesives, plywood, particle board, medium-density fibreboard (MDF), and molded objects.
- UF has good mechanical properties, water resistance, and low cost, but it also has some drawbacks, such as high formaldehyde emission, poor weatherability, and low thermal stability .
- UF often shows the appearance of crystal domains, which are influenced by the synthesis conditions, such as pH, temperature, and molar ratio of urea to formaldehyde .
- The crystallinity of UF affects its curing behavior, mechanical strength, and formaldehyde emission .
- Some modifiers, such as melamine, polyvinyl alcohol, and adipic acid dihydrazide, can be added to UF to reduce the free formaldehyde content and improve its performance.

# Adipic Acid

- Adipic acid is a white, crystalline organic compound that is mainly used as a precursor for the production of nylon.
- Adipic acid is also used as a food additive, a plasticizer, a lubricant, and a component of some polyurethanes and polyesters.
- Adipic acid is produced industrially by the oxidation of cyclohexane or cyclohexanol with nitric acid, or by the hydrolysis of cyclohexane-1,6-dione.
- Adipic acid is biodegradable and has a low toxicity, but it can cause skin and eye irritation and respiratory problems in high concentrations.
- Adipic acid is also a greenhouse gas that contributes to global warming. It has a global warming potential (GWP) of 140, which means it is 140 times more potent than carbon dioxide over a 100-year time horizon.

# Paracetamol

- Paracetamol, also known as acetaminophen, is a widely used analgesic (pain reliever) and antipyretic (fever reducer) drug.
- Paracetamol is effective for mild to moderate pain, such as headache, toothache, muscle ache, and menstrual cramps, and for reducing fever.
- Paracetamol is usually taken orally, but it can also be administered intravenously, rectally, or topically.
- Paracetamol is generally safe and well tolerated, but it can cause liver damage and allergic reactions in some cases. The maximum recommended dose of paracetamol is 4 grams per day for adults and 75 milligrams per kilogram of body weight per day for children.
- Paracetamol is metabolized in the liver by several enzymes, mainly by cytochrome P450 2E1 (CYP2E1). A small fraction of paracetamol is converted to a toxic metabolite, N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI), which is then detoxified by glutathione. However, if the glutathione level is depleted, NAPQI can accumulate and cause oxidative stress and cell death in the liver .
- Paracetamol overdose is a common cause of acute liver failure and death. The symptoms of paracetamol overdose include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and jaundice. The treatment of paracetamol overdose is the administration of N-acetylcysteine (NAC), which replenishes glutathione and enhances the elimination of NAPQI .
