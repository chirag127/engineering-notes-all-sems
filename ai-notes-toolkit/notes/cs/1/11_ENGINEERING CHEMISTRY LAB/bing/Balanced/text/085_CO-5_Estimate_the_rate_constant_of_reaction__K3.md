# CO-5 Estimate the rate constant of reaction. K3

- The rate constant of a reaction, k, is a measure of how fast the reaction proceeds at a given temperature and concentration of reactants.
- The rate constant can be determined experimentally by measuring the rate of the reaction under different conditions and using the rate law equation.
- The rate law equation relates the rate of the reaction to the concentration of the reactants and the rate constant. It has the general form:

  `rate = k[A]^m[B]^n`

  where A and B are the reactants, m and n are the order of the reaction with respect to each reactant, and k is the rate constant.
- To estimate the rate constant of a reaction, one can use the following steps:

  1. Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction and identify the reactants and products.
  2. Conduct a series of experiments to measure the rate of the reaction at different concentrations of the reactants and record the data in a table.
  3. Plot the data on a graph of rate versus concentration and determine the slope and intercept of the best-fit line.
  4. Compare the slope and intercept of the graph with the rate law equation and deduce the order of the reaction with respect to each reactant.
  5. Substitute the values of the order of the reaction and the slope or intercept of the graph into the rate law equation and solve for k.
  6. Report the value of k with the appropriate units and significant figures.

- For example, consider the following reaction:

  `2NO(g) + O2(g) -> 2NO2(g)`

  The following table shows the experimental data for the rate of this reaction at different concentrations of NO and O2 at a constant temperature:

  | [NO] (mol/L) | [O2] (mol/L) | Rate (mol/L/s) |
  | ------------ | ------------ | -------------- |
  | 0.01         | 0.01         | 0.0012         |
  | 0.02         | 0.01         | 0.0048         |
  | 0.01         | 0.02         | 0.0024         |
  | 0.02         | 0.02         | 0.0096         |

  To estimate the rate constant of this reaction, we can use the following steps:

  1. The balanced chemical equation is already given and the reactants are NO and O2.
  2. The experimental data is already given in the table.
  3. Plotting the data on a graph of rate versus [NO] at constant [O2] gives the following result:

     ![graph of rate versus NO](https://i.imgur.com/0w7f3qy.png)

     The slope of the best-fit line is 0.24 and the intercept is 0.

  4. Comparing the slope and intercept of the graph with the rate law equation, we can see that the order of the reaction with respect to NO is 2, since the rate is proportional to [NO]^2. The order of the reaction with respect to O2 can be determined by comparing the rates at different [O2] at constant [NO]. For example, when [NO] is 0.01 mol/L, the rate doubles when [O2] increases from 0.01 mol/L to 0.02 mol/L. This means that the rate is proportional to [O2]^1, and the order of the reaction with respect to O2 is 1.
  5. Substituting the values of the order of the reaction and the slope of the graph into the rate law equation, we get:

     `rate = k[NO]^2[O2]^1`

     `0.24 = k(0.01)^2(0.01)`

     Solving for k, we get:

     `k = 0.24 / (0.01)^2(0.01)`

     `k = 240 L^2/mol^2/s`

  6. The value of k is 240 L^2/mol^2/s with three significant figures and the correct units.
