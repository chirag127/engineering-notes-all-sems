### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

- Bleaching powder is a compound of calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)2), calcium chloride (CaCl2), and calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2).
- Available chlorine is the amount of chlorine that can be liberated from bleaching powder by the action of acids.
- The available chlorine in bleaching powder can be determined by iodometric titration.
- The principle of the method is that a known mass of bleaching powder is dissolved in water and treated with excess potassium iodide (KI) and hydrochloric acid (HCl).
- The liberated chlorine reacts with iodide ions to form iodine (I2), which is then titrated with standard sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) solution using starch as an indicator.
- The reactions involved are:

  - Ca(OCl)2 + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + H2O + Cl2
  - Cl2 + 2KI -> 2KCl + I2
  - I2 + 2Na2S2O3 -> 2NaI + Na2S4O6

- The procedure is as follows:

  - Weigh accurately about 0.3 g of bleaching powder and transfer it to a 250 mL conical flask.
  - Add about 100 mL of distilled water and shake well to dissolve the bleaching powder.
  - Add 10 mL of 10% KI solution and 10 mL of 1 M HCl and mix well.
  - Titrate the liberated iodine with 0.1 M Na2S2O3 solution using starch as an indicator near the end point.
  - Repeat the titration with a blank solution containing only water, KI, and HCl.
  - Calculate the percentage of available chlorine in the bleaching powder using the formula:

    - % available chlorine = (B - S) x N x 35.46 / W
    - where B is the volume of Na2S2O3 solution used for the blank, S is the volume of Na2S2O3 solution used for the sample, N is the normality of Na2S2O3 solution, W is the mass of bleaching powder taken, and 35.46 is the equivalent mass of chlorine.
