### CO-1 Get an understanding of the use of different analytical instruments. K3

- Analytical instruments are devices that are used to measure, analyze, or monitor physical, chemical, or biological properties of samples.
- Different analytical instruments have different applications, advantages, and limitations depending on the type of sample, the property of interest, and the level of accuracy and precision required.
- Some of the common analytical instruments are:

  - Spectrophotometers: These instruments measure the amount of light absorbed or emitted by a sample at different wavelengths. They can be used to determine the concentration, structure, or identity of molecules in a sample. Examples of spectrophotometers are UV-Vis, IR, NMR, and mass spectrometers.
  - Chromatographs: These instruments separate the components of a mixture based on their affinity to a stationary phase and a mobile phase. They can be used to separate, identify, or quantify the components of a mixture. Examples of chromatographs are gas chromatography (GC), liquid chromatography (LC), and thin layer chromatography (TLC).
  - Electrochemical instruments: These instruments measure the electrical properties of a sample, such as voltage, current, resistance, or conductivity. They can be used to determine the redox potential, pH, or concentration of ions in a sample. Examples of electrochemical instruments are potentiometers, pH meters, and conductivity meters.
  - Microscopes: These instruments magnify the image of a sample using lenses, light, or electrons. They can be used to observe the morphology, structure, or behavior of cells, tissues, or materials. Examples of microscopes are optical microscopes, scanning electron microscopes (SEM), and transmission electron microscopes (TEM).
