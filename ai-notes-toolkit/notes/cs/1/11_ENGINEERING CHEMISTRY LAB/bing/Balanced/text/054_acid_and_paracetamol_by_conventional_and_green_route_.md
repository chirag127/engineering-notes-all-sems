# Acid and Paracetamol by Conventional and Green Route

- Paracetamol, also known as acetaminophen, is a common painkiller and antipyretic drug that is derived from phenol.
- The conventional route for the synthesis of paracetamol involves three steps: nitration, reduction and acetylation.
  - In the first step, phenol is nitrated with nitric acid to form p-nitrophenol, which is separated from the unwanted o-nitrophenol by-product by fractional crystallization.
  - In the second step, p-nitrophenol is reduced with tin and hydrochloric acid to form p-aminophenol, which is purified by recrystallization.
  - In the third step, p-aminophenol is acetylated with acetic anhydride to form paracetamol, which is isolated by filtration and recrystallization.
- The conventional route has some drawbacks, such as low yield, high waste generation, use of hazardous reagents and solvents, and formation of undesired isomers and by-products.
- The green route for the synthesis of paracetamol aims to overcome these drawbacks by using alternative methods that are more environmentally friendly, efficient and economical.
- The green route can involve different methods, such as solvent-free synthesis, microwave-assisted synthesis, direct amidation, and enzymatic synthesis.
  - Solvent-free synthesis involves grinding the reactants in a mortar and pestle, and heating them in an oven or a microwave to induce the reaction. This method eliminates the use of solvents and reduces the reaction time and energy consumption.
  - Microwave-assisted synthesis involves irradiating the reactants in a microwave oven, which generates heat and electromagnetic fields that enhance the reaction rate and selectivity. This method also reduces the use of solvents and the reaction time and energy consumption.
  - Direct amidation involves reacting hydroquinone with acetic anhydride in the presence of a catalyst, such as sodium acetate or sodium hydroxide, to form paracetamol directly, without the intermediate steps of nitration and reduction. This method simplifies the synthetic route and avoids the formation of isomers and by-products.
  - Enzymatic synthesis involves using enzymes, such as lipases or nitrilases, to catalyze the reaction between phenol and acetic acid or acetonitrile, respectively, to form paracetamol. This method uses mild reaction conditions, biodegradable reagents, and renewable catalysts.
