### Course Outcomes Bloom’s

- Course outcomes are statements that describe what students should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course.
- Bloom’s taxonomy is a framework for classifying educational objectives into six levels of cognitive complexity: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Course outcomes should be aligned with Bloom’s taxonomy to ensure that students are challenged to develop higher-order thinking skills and not just memorize facts.
- Course outcomes should also be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) to provide clear guidance for assessment and feedback.
- Course outcomes should be written using action verbs that indicate the level of Bloom’s taxonomy and the type of performance expected from students.
- Some examples of course outcomes using Bloom’s taxonomy are:

  - Remember: Define the key terms and concepts of computer science.
  - Understand: Explain the basic principles of programming and data structures.
  - Apply: Use appropriate algorithms and data structures to solve simple problems.
  - Analyze: Compare and contrast different algorithms and data structures in terms of efficiency and suitability.
  - Evaluate: Critique the design and implementation of a software system based on given criteria.
  - Create: Develop a software system that meets the specifications and requirements of a client.
