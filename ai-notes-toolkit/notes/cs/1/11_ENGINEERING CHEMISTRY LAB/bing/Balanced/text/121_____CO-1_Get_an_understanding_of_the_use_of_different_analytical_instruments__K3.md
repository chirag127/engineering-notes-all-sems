### CO-1 Get an understanding of the use of different analytical instruments. K3

- Analytical instruments are devices that are used to measure, analyze, or monitor physical, chemical, or biological properties of samples.
- Different analytical instruments have different applications, advantages, and limitations depending on the type of sample, the property of interest, and the level of accuracy and precision required.
- Some of the common types of analytical instruments are:

  - Spectroscopic instruments: These instruments use electromagnetic radiation to interact with the sample and measure its absorption, emission, reflection, or scattering spectra. Examples are UV-Vis spectrophotometer, infrared spectrometer, atomic absorption spectrometer, etc.
  - Chromatographic instruments: These instruments use a mobile phase and a stationary phase to separate the components of a mixture based on their affinity, polarity, size, or charge. Examples are gas chromatograph, liquid chromatograph, ion chromatograph, etc.
  - Mass spectrometric instruments: These instruments use electric or magnetic fields to ionize, accelerate, deflect, and detect the mass-to-charge ratio of the sample molecules or fragments. Examples are quadrupole mass spectrometer, time-of-flight mass spectrometer, etc.
  - Electrochemical instruments: These instruments use electrodes to measure the electrical potential, current, or resistance of the sample or to induce a chemical reaction. Examples are pH meter, potentiometer, voltameter, etc.
  - Microscopic instruments: These instruments use lenses, mirrors, or detectors to magnify and visualize the structure, morphology, or behavior of the sample at a microscopic level. Examples are optical microscope, scanning electron microscope, atomic force microscope, etc.

- To get an understanding of the use of different analytical instruments, one should:

  - Know the basic principles, components, and working mechanisms of each instrument.
  - Know the sample preparation, calibration, and validation methods for each instrument.
  - Know the advantages, disadvantages, and sources of error for each instrument.
  - Know the data analysis, interpretation, and presentation techniques for each instrument.
  - Know the applications, limitations, and safety precautions for each instrument.
