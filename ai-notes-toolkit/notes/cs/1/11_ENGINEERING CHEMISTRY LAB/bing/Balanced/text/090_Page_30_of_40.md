# Nuclear Fusion

- Nuclear fusion is the process of combining two or more atomic nuclei into a larger one, releasing energy in the process.
- Nuclear fusion is the source of energy for the Sun and other stars, where hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium nuclei under high temperature and pressure.
- Nuclear fusion can also be artificially induced in devices such as tokamaks, stellarators, inertial confinement fusion, and fusion rockets.
- Nuclear fusion has the potential to provide clean, abundant, and safe energy for humanity, but it also faces many technical, economic, and environmental challenges.

## Advantages of Nuclear Fusion

- Nuclear fusion has several advantages over other energy sources, such as:

  - It produces more energy per unit mass than nuclear fission or fossil fuels, making it more efficient and economical.
  - It does not produce greenhouse gases or radioactive waste, making it more environmentally friendly and sustainable.
  - It uses abundant and widely available fuels, such as hydrogen isotopes, making it more accessible and reliable.
  - It does not pose the risk of nuclear meltdown, proliferation, or terrorism, making it more safe and secure.

## Challenges of Nuclear Fusion

- Nuclear fusion also has several challenges that need to be overcome, such as:

  - It requires extremely high temperatures and pressures to initiate and sustain the fusion reaction, making it difficult and costly to achieve and maintain.
  - It generates high-energy neutrons that can damage the fusion reactor and its components, making it challenging and expensive to operate and maintain.
  - It faces competition from other energy sources, such as renewable energy and nuclear fission, making it less attractive and viable in the current market and policy environment.
  - It requires international cooperation and coordination to share the scientific, technical, and financial resources and expertise, making it complex and slow to develop and deploy.
