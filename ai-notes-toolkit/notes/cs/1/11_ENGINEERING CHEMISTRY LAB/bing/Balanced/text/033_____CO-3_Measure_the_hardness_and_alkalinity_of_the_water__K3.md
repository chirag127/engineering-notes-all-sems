### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. It is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3) in mg/L.
- Alkalinity of water is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium and other metals. It is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3) in mg/L.
- The hardness and alkalinity of water can be measured by titration methods using standard solutions of EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) and HCl (hydrochloric acid) respectively.
- The steps for measuring the hardness and alkalinity of water are as follows:

  - Hardness:
    - Take a 50 mL sample of water in a conical flask and add a few drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator. The solution will turn wine red.
    - Titrate the solution with standard EDTA solution from a burette until the color changes from wine red to blue. Note the volume of EDTA used as V1.
    - Repeat the titration with another 50 mL sample of water and note the volume of EDTA used as V2.
    - Calculate the average volume of EDTA used as V = (V1 + V2) / 2.
    - Calculate the hardness of water as H = (V x N x 1000) / 50, where N is the normality of EDTA solution and 50 is the volume of water sample in mL.
    - Report the hardness of water in mg/L as CaCO3.

  - Alkalinity:
    - Take a 50 mL sample of water in a conical flask and add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator. The solution may turn pink if the pH is above 8.3.
    - Titrate the solution with standard HCl solution from a burette until the color changes from pink to colorless. Note the volume of HCl used as V1.
    - Add a few drops of methyl orange indicator to the same solution. The solution will turn orange or yellow.
    - Continue the titration with standard HCl solution until the color changes from orange or yellow to red. Note the volume of HCl used as V2.
    - Calculate the total volume of HCl used as V = V1 + V2.
    - Calculate the alkalinity of water as A = (V x N x 1000) / 50, where N is the normality of HCl solution and 50 is the volume of water sample in mL.
    - Report the alkalinity of water in mg/L as CaCO3.
