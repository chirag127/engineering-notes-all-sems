# 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow or deform under an applied shear stress.
- Viscosity is important for many applications, such as lubrication, fluid dynamics, transport phenomena, etc.
- A viscometer is an instrument that measures the viscosity of a fluid by measuring the force or torque required to move a surface through the fluid at a constant speed or the speed at which a surface moves through the fluid under a constant force or torque.
- There are different types of viscometers, such as capillary, rotational, falling ball, etc. Each type has its own advantages and limitations depending on the fluid properties and the measurement conditions.
- In this experiment, we will use a capillary viscometer, which consists of a glass tube with a narrow section (the capillary) and two marks on the tube to indicate the fluid level. The fluid is drawn into the tube by suction and then allowed to flow out under its own weight. The time taken for the fluid to flow between the two marks is measured and used to calculate the viscosity of the fluid.
- The capillary viscometer obeys the Hagen-Poiseuille equation, which relates the volumetric flow rate Q of a fluid through a capillary of length L and radius R to the pressure difference P across the capillary and the viscosity η of the fluid:

  Q = (πR^4 P)/(8ηL)

- Rearranging the equation, we can obtain the expression for the viscosity of the fluid:

  η = (πR^4 P)/(8QL)

- The pressure difference P can be approximated by the hydrostatic pressure due to the height difference h of the fluid column between the two marks:

  P = ρgh

  where ρ is the density of the fluid and g is the acceleration due to gravity.

- The volumetric flow rate Q can be obtained by dividing the volume V of the fluid that flows between the two marks by the time t taken for the flow:

  Q = V/t

- Substituting these expressions into the equation for the viscosity, we get:

  η = (πR^4 ρgh)/(8Vt)

- To perform the experiment, we need to know the following parameters:

  - The radius R of the capillary, which can be measured by a micrometer or a vernier caliper.
  - The length L of the capillary, which can be measured by a ruler or a meter stick.
  - The volume V of the fluid that flows between the two marks, which can be calculated by multiplying the cross-sectional area A of the tube by the distance d between the two marks:

    V = Ad

    where A = πR^2

  - The density ρ of the fluid, which can be measured by a hydrometer or a pycnometer.
  - The time t taken for the fluid to flow between the two marks, which can be measured by a stopwatch or a chronometer.
  - The height difference h of the fluid column between the two marks, which can be measured by a ruler or a meter stick.

- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:

  - Clean and dry the viscometer and the fluid container.
  - Fill the fluid container with the given liquid and place it on a stand.
  - Connect the viscometer to the fluid container by a rubber tube and a suction pump.
  - Draw the fluid into the viscometer by suction until it reaches above the upper mark.
  - Close the suction pump and disconnect the rubber tube.
  - Hold the viscometer vertically and start the stopwatch when the fluid level reaches the upper mark.
  - Stop the stopwatch when the fluid level reaches the lower mark and record the time t.
  - Repeat the steps 4 to 7 for at least three trials and take the average time t.
  - Measure the radius R, the length L, the distance d, and the height difference h of the viscometer and record the values.
  - Measure the density ρ of the fluid and record the value.
  - Calculate the volume V of the fluid that flows between the two marks using the formula V = Ad.
  - Calculate the viscosity η of the fluid using the formula η = (πR^4 ρgh)/(8Vt) and record the value.
  - Report the result with the appropriate units and significant figures.
