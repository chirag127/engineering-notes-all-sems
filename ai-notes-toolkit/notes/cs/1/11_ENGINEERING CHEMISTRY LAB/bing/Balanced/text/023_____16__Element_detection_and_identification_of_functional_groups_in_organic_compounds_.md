### 16. Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds.

- Organic compounds are composed of carbon, hydrogen, and other elements such as oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus, halogens, etc.
- The detection and identification of these elements and functional groups in organic compounds is an important part of organic chemistry.
- There are various methods and tests to detect and identify the elements and functional groups in organic compounds, such as:

  - **Combustion analysis**: This method involves burning a known mass of the organic compound in excess oxygen and measuring the masses of carbon dioxide and water produced. The percentage of carbon and hydrogen in the compound can be calculated from the masses of carbon dioxide and water, using the following equations:

    - %C = (mass of CO2 / mass of organic compound) x 12/44 x 100
    - %H = (mass of H2O / mass of organic compound) x 2/18 x 100

  - **Lassaigne's test**: This test is used to detect the presence of nitrogen, sulfur, and halogens in an organic compound. The test involves fusing a small amount of the organic compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube. The sodium metal reacts with the elements in the organic compound and forms sodium cyanide (NaCN), sodium sulfide (Na2S), and sodium halides (NaX), where X is F, Cl, Br, or I. The fused mass is then extracted with water and filtered. The filtrate is called sodium extract and is used for further tests.

    - To detect nitrogen, the sodium extract is boiled with iron(II) sulfate and sulfuric acid. The formation of a blue or green color due to the formation of ferric ferrocyanide or ferrous ferricyanide indicates the presence of nitrogen.

      - NaCN + FeSO4 + 6H2O -> Na2SO4 + Fe(OH)2 + HCN
      - 4HCN + 2Fe(OH)2 + 3FeSO4 + 2H2O -> Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 + 4Na2SO4 (Prussian blue)
      - 6HCN + Fe(OH)2 + 3FeSO4 + 4H2O -> 2Fe3[Fe(CN)6]2 + 4Na2SO4 (Turnbull's blue)

    - To detect sulfur, the sodium extract is acidified with acetic acid and lead acetate is added. The formation of a black precipitate of lead sulfide indicates the presence of sulfur.

      - Na2S + 2CH3COOH -> 2NaCH3COO + H2S
      - H2S + Pb(CH3COO)2 -> PbS + 2CH3COOH

    - To detect halogens, the sodium extract is acidified with nitric acid and silver nitrate is added. The formation of a precipitate of silver halide indicates the presence of halogens. The color of the precipitate can be used to distinguish between different halogens. Silver fluoride is soluble, silver chloride is white, silver bromide is pale yellow, and silver iodide is yellow.

      - NaX + HNO3 -> NaNO3 + HX
      - HX + AgNO3 -> AgX + HNO3

  - **Functional group tests**: These tests are used to identify the presence of specific functional groups in an organic compound, such as alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, amides, etc. Some examples of functional group tests are:

    - **Lucas test**: This test is used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols. The test involves adding a few drops of the alcohol to a solution of zinc chloride in concentrated hydrochloric acid. The formation of a turbid solution or a separate layer due to the formation of an alkyl chloride indicates the presence of an alcohol. The rate of reaction can be used to differentiate between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols. Tertiary alcohols react immediately, secondary alcohols react within 5 minutes, and primary alcohols do not react.

      - R-OH + HCl -> R-Cl + H2O

    - **Tollen's test**: This test is used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones. The test involves adding a few drops of the organic compound to a solution of silver nitrate in ammonia.
