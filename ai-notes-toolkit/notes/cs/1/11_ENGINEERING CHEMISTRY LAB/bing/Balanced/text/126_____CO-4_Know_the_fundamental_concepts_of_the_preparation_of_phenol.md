### CO-4 Know the fundamental concepts of the preparation of phenol

Phenol is an aromatic compound with a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to a benzene ring. It is also known as carbolic acid or phenic acid. Phenol has antiseptic and disinfectant properties and is used in the manufacture of plastics, dyes, explosives, and drugs.

There are various methods of preparation of phenol, which can be classified into two categories: industrial methods and laboratory methods.

#### Industrial methods of preparation of phenol

- From coal tar: Phenol is extracted from the middle oil fraction of coal tar, which is obtained by the destructive distillation of coal. The middle oil fraction is distilled at 443-503 K and phenol is separated by fractional crystallization.
- From benzene: Phenol can be prepared from benzene by the following steps:
  - Raschig's process: Benzene is reacted with hydrogen chloride and air at 523 K in the presence of an iron oxide catalyst to form chlorobenzene.
  - Hydrolysis of chlorobenzene: Chlorobenzene is fused with sodium hydroxide at 623 K and 320 atm to form sodium phenoxide, which is then acidified with dilute acid or carbon dioxide to form phenol.
- From cumene: Phenol can be prepared from cumene (isopropylbenzene) by the following steps:
  - Cumene hydroperoxide process: Cumene is oxidized by air in the presence of a cobalt catalyst to form cumene hydroperoxide, which is an unstable compound.
  - Cleavage of cumene hydroperoxide: Cumene hydroperoxide is decomposed by heating with dilute acid to form phenol and acetone.

#### Laboratory methods of preparation of phenol

- From benzene sulphonic acid: Phenol can be prepared from benzene sulphonic acid by the following steps:
  - Sulphonation of benzene: Benzene is treated with concentrated sulphuric acid at 298 K to form benzene sulphonic acid.
  - Fusion of benzene sulphonic acid: Benzene sulphonic acid is fused with sodium hydroxide at 573-623 K to form sodium phenoxide, which is then acidified with dilute acid or carbon dioxide to form phenol.
- From diazonium salts: Phenol can be prepared from diazonium salts by the following steps:
  - Diazotization of aniline: Aniline (phenylamine) is treated with nitrous acid (prepared by mixing sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid) at 273-278 K to form benzene diazonium chloride.
  - Hydrolysis of benzene diazonium chloride: Benzene diazonium chloride is treated with water at 273-278 K to form phenol and nitrogen gas.
