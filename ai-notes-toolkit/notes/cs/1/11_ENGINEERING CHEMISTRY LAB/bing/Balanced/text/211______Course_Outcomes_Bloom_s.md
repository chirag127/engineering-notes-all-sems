### Course Outcomes Bloom's Taxonomy

- Course outcomes are brief statements that describe what students will be expected to learn by the end of the course.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a model of cognitive skills used to classify educational learning objectives and is a helpful tool for the development of learning outcomes.
- Bloom's taxonomy consists of six levels of thinking, from lower to higher order: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Course outcomes should be aligned with the level of thinking that the course intends to foster in students.
- Course outcomes should use verbs that reflect the level of thinking, such as describe, explain, compare, contrast, solve, design, etc.
- Course outcomes should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound.
- Course outcomes should be communicated to students at the beginning of the course and assessed throughout the course.

Below are some examples of course outcomes that utilize Bloom's taxonomy:

- At the end of the course, students will be able to **describe** the colonization of the Americas by the British, French and Spanish (remember level).
- At the end of the course, students will be able to **explain** the main concepts and theories of social psychology (understand level).
- At the end of the course, students will be able to **apply** the principles of accounting to prepare financial statements (apply level).
- At the end of the course, students will be able to **analyze** the outcomes of the Civil War using primary and secondary sources (analyze level).
- At the end of the course, students will be able to **evaluate** the ethical implications of genetic engineering (evaluate level).
- At the end of the course, students will be able to **create** a portfolio of original poems that demonstrate their mastery of poetic techniques (create level).
