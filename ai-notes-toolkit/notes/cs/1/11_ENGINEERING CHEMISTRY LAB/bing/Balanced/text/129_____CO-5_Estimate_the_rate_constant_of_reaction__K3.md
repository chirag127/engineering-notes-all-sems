### CO-5 Estimate the rate constant of reaction. K3

- The rate constant of a reaction, denoted by k, is a measure of how fast the reaction proceeds at a given temperature and concentration of reactants and products.
- The rate constant depends on the order of the reaction, which is the sum of the exponents of the concentration terms in the rate law expression.
- For a termolecular reaction, where three molecules collide simultaneously to form products, the order of the reaction is 3 and the rate law is:

  r=k3[A][B][C]

  where r is the reaction rate, [A], [B] and [C] are the concentrations of the reactants, and k3 is the termolecular rate constant.

- The termolecular rate constant has the units of L2mol-2s-1 in the SI system, or M-2s-1 in the molar system.
- The termolecular rate constant can be estimated by using the Arrhenius equation, which relates the rate constant to the activation energy and the temperature of the reaction:

  k3=Ae-Ea/RT

  where A is the pre-exponential factor, e is the base of the natural logarithm, Ea is the activation energy, R is the universal gas constant, and T is the absolute temperature.

- The activation energy is the minimum amount of energy required for the reactants to overcome the energy barrier and form products. It can be determined experimentally by measuring the rate constant at different temperatures and plotting the natural logarithm of the rate constant versus the inverse of the temperature. The slope of the plot is equal to -Ea/R.
- The pre-exponential factor is a constant that reflects the frequency and orientation of the collisions between the reactants. It can be calculated by rearranging the Arrhenius equation and substituting the values of the rate constant, the activation energy, and the temperature at a given condition.

  A=k3eEa/RT

- To estimate the termolecular rate constant of a reaction, the following steps can be followed:

  1. Identify the reactants and products of the reaction and write the balanced chemical equation.
  2. Determine the order of the reaction and write the rate law expression.
  3. Measure the reaction rate and the concentrations of the reactants at different temperatures and calculate the rate constant at each temperature using the rate law expression.
  4. Plot the natural logarithm of the rate constant versus the inverse of the temperature and find the slope and the intercept of the plot.
  5. Use the slope to calculate the activation energy of the reaction using the formula Ea=-slope*R.
  6. Use the intercept to calculate the pre-exponential factor of the reaction using the formula A=eintercept.
  7. Use the Arrhenius equation to estimate the termolecular rate constant of the reaction at any desired temperature.
