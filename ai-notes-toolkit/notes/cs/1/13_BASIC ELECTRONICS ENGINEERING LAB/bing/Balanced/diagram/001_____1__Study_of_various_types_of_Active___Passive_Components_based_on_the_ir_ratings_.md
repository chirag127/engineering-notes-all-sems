### 1. Study of various types of Active & Passive Components based on their ratings.

- Active components are those that can generate or amplify electrical signals, such as transistors, diodes, and integrated circuits. Passive components are those that can only store or dissipate electrical energy, such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors.
- The ratings of active and passive components are the specifications that indicate their performance, characteristics, and limitations. Some common ratings are:
  - Power rating: the maximum amount of power that a component can handle without being damaged or overheated. It is usually expressed in watts (W) or milliwatts (mW).
  - Voltage rating: the maximum amount of voltage that a component can withstand across its terminals without breaking down or arcing. It is usually expressed in volts (V) or kilovolts (kV).
  - Current rating: the maximum amount of current that a component can carry without exceeding its power rating or causing excessive heat. It is usually expressed in amperes (A) or milliamperes (mA).
  - Resistance rating: the amount of opposition that a component offers to the flow of current. It is usually expressed in ohms (Ω) or kilohms (kΩ).
  - Capacitance rating: the amount of electric charge that a component can store per unit of voltage. It is usually expressed in farads (F) or microfarads (µF).
  - Inductance rating: the amount of magnetic flux that a component can generate per unit of current. It is usually expressed in henries (H) or millihenries (mH).
  - Frequency rating: the range of frequencies that a component can operate at without losing its functionality or efficiency. It is usually expressed in hertz (Hz) or kilohertz (kHz).
