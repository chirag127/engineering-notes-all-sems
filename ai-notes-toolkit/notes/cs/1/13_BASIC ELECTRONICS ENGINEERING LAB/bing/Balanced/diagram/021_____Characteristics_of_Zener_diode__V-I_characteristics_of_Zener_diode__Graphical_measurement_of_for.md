### Characteristics of Zener diode:

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode designed to reliably allow current to flow "backwards" (inverted polarity) when a certain set reverse voltage, known as the Zener voltage, is reached.
- Zener diodes are widely used as voltage references and as shunt regulators to regulate the voltage across small circuits.
- Zener diodes operate similarly to conventional diodes when in the forward-bias mode. They have a bias turn-on voltage of between 0.3 and 0.7V.
- Zener diodes are heavily doped than ordinary diodes. They have extra thin depletion region. When we apply a voltage more than the Zener breakdown voltage (can range from 1.2 volts to 200 volts), the depletion region vanishes, and large current starts to flow through the junction.
- The Zener breakdown voltage is the minimum reverse-bias voltage that causes a significant increase in the current through a Zener diode. It is also called the Zener knee voltage or the Zener voltage.
- The Zener diode has a sharp reverse breakdown voltage, which means that the voltage across the diode remains almost constant for a wide range of reverse currents. This property makes the Zener diode suitable for voltage regulation.

#### V-I characteristics of Zener diode:

- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode is a curve that shows the relationship between the voltage across the diode and the current through it.
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode can be divided into three regions: forward region, reverse region, and breakdown region.
- In the forward region, the Zener diode behaves like a normal diode. It has a low resistance and allows current to flow when the forward voltage is greater than the turn-on voltage.
- In the reverse region, the Zener diode has a high resistance and blocks the current flow when the reverse voltage is less than the Zener voltage.
- In the breakdown region, the Zener diode has a low resistance and allows a large current to flow when the reverse voltage is greater than or equal to the Zener voltage.
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode can be plotted by using a variable voltage source, a resistor, an ammeter, and a voltmeter. The resistor is used to limit the current and protect the diode from damage.

#### Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance:

- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode can be measured graphically by using the V-I characteristics curve.
- The forward resistance is the slope of the V-I curve in the forward region. It can be calculated by using the formula: Rf = ΔVf / ΔIf, where ΔVf is the change in forward voltage and ΔIf is the change in forward current.
- The reverse resistance is the slope of the V-I curve in the reverse region. It can be calculated by using the formula: Rr = ΔVr / ΔIr, where ΔVr is the change in reverse voltage and ΔIr is the change in reverse current.
- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode are usually very small and can be neglected in most applications.
