### Part A PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

This practical is not possible by virtual lab. It will be conducted only in physical mode.

- A printed circuit board (PCB) is a board that connects electronic components using conductive traces and pads. PCBs are used in various electronic devices and applications.
- The process of making a PCB involves several steps, such as designing, artwork, printing, etching, drilling, soldering, and testing.
- Artwork is the graphical representation of the circuit layout on the PCB. It shows the location and size of the components, traces, pads, holes, and other features. Artwork can be created using software tools or manually on a paper or film.
- Printing is the process of transferring the artwork onto a copper-clad board, which is a substrate with a thin layer of copper on one or both sides. Printing can be done using various methods, such as photolithography, screen printing, inkjet printing, or laser printing.
- Etching is the process of removing the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the desired traces and pads. Etching can be done using chemical solutions, such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate, or physical methods, such as milling or laser cutting.
- Drilling is the process of making holes on the board for mounting components, vias, or connectors. Drilling can be done using manual or automated drills, or laser machines. The holes should be aligned with the artwork and have the appropriate diameter and depth.
- The following steps are involved in making a simple PCB using the artwork and printing method:

  1. Design the circuit and draw the schematic diagram using a software tool or on a paper.
  2. Create the artwork for the PCB using a software tool or on a paper or film. The artwork should have the same scale as the actual board and should include the component symbols, trace widths, pad sizes, hole locations, and other details.
  3. Print the artwork on a transparent film or paper using a printer or a photocopier. The printed artwork should be clear and sharp, and should match the original artwork.
  4. Cut a copper-clad board to the desired size and shape using a saw or a cutter. The board should be clean and free of dust, grease, or scratches.
  5. Place the printed artwork on the copper side of the board and align it properly. Tape the artwork to the board using a transparent tape or a masking tape.
  6. Expose the board to a source of ultraviolet (UV) light for a few minutes. The UV light will harden the photoresist coating on the board, which is a light-sensitive material that protects the copper from etching. The areas that are covered by the artwork will remain unexposed and will be etched away later.
  7. Remove the artwork from the board and wash the board with water to remove any residue. The board should have a clear image of the circuit on the copper side.
  8. Immerse the board in an etching solution, such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate, for a few minutes. The etching solution will dissolve the unexposed copper, leaving only the exposed traces and pads. The etching time and temperature should be controlled to avoid over-etching or under-etching.
  9. Rinse the board with water and dry it with a cloth or a paper towel. The board should have a clean and smooth surface with no copper burrs or shorts.
  10. Drill the holes on the board using a manual or an automated drill, or a laser machine. The holes should be aligned with the artwork and have the appropriate diameter and depth. The drill bits should be sharp and clean, and the drilling speed and pressure should be adjusted to avoid damaging the board or the drill.
  11. Solder the components on the board using a soldering iron and a solder wire. The solder joints should be neat and secure, and the components should be oriented and placed correctly. The soldering temperature and time should be regulated to avoid overheating or cold soldering.
  12. Test the board using a multimeter, an oscilloscope, or other testing equipment. The board should function as per the circuit design and specifications. The board should be checked for any errors, such as shorts, opens, or faulty components, and corrected if needed.
