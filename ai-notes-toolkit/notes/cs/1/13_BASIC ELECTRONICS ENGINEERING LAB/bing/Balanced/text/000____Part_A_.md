## Part A:

- Part A is the first section of a document or a report that contains the introduction, the background, the objectives, and the scope of the work.
- Part A is important because it sets the context and the purpose of the document or the report, and provides an overview of what the reader can expect to find in the rest of the sections.
- Part A should be clear, concise, and informative, and should answer the following questions:
  - What is the topic or the problem that the document or the report addresses?
  - Why is the topic or the problem significant or relevant to the audience or the stakeholders?
  - What are the main goals or the aims of the document or the report?
  - What are the specific objectives or the research questions that the document or the report seeks to answer or achieve?
  - What are the limitations or the boundaries of the document or the report, such as the scope, the methodology, the assumptions, the data sources, or the ethical considerations?
- Part A should be written in a formal and academic tone, using appropriate language and terminology, and avoiding personal opinions or biases. It should also follow the formatting and referencing guidelines of the document or the report type, such as APA, MLA, or IEEE.
