# Characteristics of Zener Diode

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode that allows current to flow in both forward and reverse directions when a certain voltage, called the Zener voltage, is reached.
- Zener diodes are used as voltage references and regulators, as they can maintain a constant voltage across a circuit even when the input voltage or load current varies .

- In the forward-biased region, the Zener diode behaves like a normal diode, with a turn-on voltage of about 0.7 V for silicon and 0.3 V for germanium.
- In the reverse-biased region, the Zener diode remains non-conductive until the applied voltage reaches the Zener breakdown voltage (VZ), which can range from 1.2 V to 200 V depending on the doping level.
- At the Zener breakdown voltage, the depletion region of the diode becomes very thin and a large current flows through the diode, causing a sharp drop in the voltage across the diode.
- The Zener diode can operate in the reverse breakdown region without being damaged, as long as the current is limited by a resistor or a current source.
- The Zener diode has a negative temperature coefficient, meaning that its Zener voltage decreases as the temperature increases. This can be used to compensate for the positive temperature coefficient of other components in a circuit.
- The forward and reverse resistances of a Zener diode can be measured graphically by plotting the V-I characteristics and finding the slopes of the curves in the respective regions.
- The forward resistance (RF) is the reciprocal of the slope of the forward-biased curve, and the reverse resistance (RZ) is the reciprocal of the slope of the reverse-biased curve near the Zener voltage.
- The forward and reverse resistances are usually very small, in the order of ohms or milliohms, and can be neglected in most applications.
