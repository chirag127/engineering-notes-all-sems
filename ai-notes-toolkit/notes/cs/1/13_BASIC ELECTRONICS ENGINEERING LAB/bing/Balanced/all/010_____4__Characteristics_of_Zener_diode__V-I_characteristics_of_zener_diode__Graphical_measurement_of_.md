# 4. Characteristics of Zener diode: V-I characteristics of zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode designed to reliably allow current to flow "backwards" (inverted polarity) when a certain set reverse voltage, known as the Zener voltage, is reached.
- Zener diodes are widely used as voltage references and as shunt regulators to regulate the voltage across small circuits.
- Zener diodes operate similarly to conventional diodes when in the forward-bias mode. They have a bias turn-on voltage of between 0.3 and 0.7V.
- Zener diodes are heavily doped than ordinary diodes. They have extra thin depletion region. When we apply a voltage more than the Zener breakdown voltage (can range from 1.2 volts to 200 volts), the depletion region vanishes, and large current starts to flow through the junction.
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode are shown in the figure below. The forward characteristics are similar to a normal diode, but the reverse characteristics show a sharp breakdown at the Zener voltage. The region below the Zener voltage is called the Zener region, where the current is very small and almost constant. The region above the Zener voltage is called the avalanche region, where the current increases rapidly with the voltage.

- The forward resistance of a Zener diode is the resistance offered by the diode when it is forward biased. It can be measured by applying a small forward voltage across the diode and measuring the resulting current. The forward resistance is given by the ratio of the applied voltage to the current.
- The reverse resistance of a Zener diode is the resistance offered by the diode when it is reverse biased. It can be measured by applying a small reverse voltage across the diode and measuring the resulting current. The reverse resistance is given by the ratio of the applied voltage to the current.
- The graphical measurement of the forward and reverse resistance can be done by plotting the V-I characteristics of the diode on a graph paper and drawing a tangent to the curve at the point of interest. The slope of the tangent gives the inverse of the resistance at that point.
