# Part A PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

- PCB stands for printed circuit board, which is a thin board made of fiberglass, composite epoxy, or other laminate material.
- PCBs are used to connect electronic components such as resistors, capacitors, transistors, LEDs, etc. using conductive tracks, pads, and vias.
- PCBs can be single-sided, double-sided, or multi-layered, depending on the complexity and functionality of the circuit.
- The process of making a PCB involves the following steps:

## Artwork and printing of a simple PCB
- Artwork is the design of the PCB layout, which can be done manually or using software tools such as Eagle, KiCad, Proteus, etc.
- Artwork shows the placement and routing of the components, the size and shape of the board, the drill holes, and the copper traces.
- Artwork can be printed on a transparent film or paper using a laser printer or a photocopier.
- The printed artwork is then aligned and transferred to a copper-clad board, which is a thin sheet of copper laminated on a non-conductive substrate such as fiberglass.
- The transfer can be done using various methods such as iron-on, photoresist, or direct inkjet printing.

## Etching and drilling of PCB
- Etching is the process of removing the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the desired traces and pads.
- Etching can be done using chemical solutions such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate, which dissolve the copper when applied to the board.
- The board is first masked with a resist material, such as toner, wax, or photoresist, which protects the copper areas that need to be preserved.
- The board is then immersed in the etching solution for a certain time, depending on the concentration and temperature of the solution, and the thickness of the copper.
- The board is then rinsed with water and dried, and the resist material is removed using acetone or alcohol.
- Drilling is the process of making holes in the board for mounting the components and connecting the layers.
- Drilling can be done using a hand drill, a drill press, or a CNC machine, depending on the accuracy and speed required.
- The board is first marked with the drill locations using the artwork as a guide, and then clamped to a stable surface.
- The board is then drilled using the appropriate drill bits, which should match the size and type of the components and vias.
- The board is then cleaned and inspected for any defects or errors.
