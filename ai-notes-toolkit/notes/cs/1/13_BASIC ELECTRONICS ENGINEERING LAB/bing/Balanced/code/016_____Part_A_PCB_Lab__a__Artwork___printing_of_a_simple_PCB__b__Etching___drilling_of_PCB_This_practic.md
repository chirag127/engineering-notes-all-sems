### Part A PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

This practical is not possible by virtual lab. It will be conducted only in physical mode.

- A printed circuit board (PCB) is a board that connects electronic components using conductive traces and pads. PCBs are used in various electronic devices and applications.
- The process of making a PCB involves several steps, such as:
  - Designing the circuit and layout of the PCB using software tools or manual methods.
  - Creating the artwork or mask of the PCB, which is a negative image of the conductive traces and pads on a transparent film or paper.
  - Printing the artwork on a copper-clad board, which is a substrate with a thin layer of copper on one or both sides. The printing can be done using a laser printer, an inkjet printer, or a photocopier.
  - Etching the board, which is a chemical process that removes the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the desired traces and pads. The etching can be done using ferric chloride, ammonium persulfate, or sodium persulfate solutions.
  - Drilling the board, which is a mechanical process that creates holes for mounting the electronic components on the board. The drilling can be done using a drill press, a hand drill, or a CNC machine.
  - Finishing the board, which is a process that adds protective and decorative layers to the board, such as solder mask, silkscreen, and plating.
