### PN Junction Diode: Characteristics of PN Junction Diode-Static and Dynamic Resistance Measurement from Graph

A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It is formed by joining a p-type semiconductor and an n-type semiconductor, creating a depletion region at the junction. The depletion region acts as a potential barrier that prevents the flow of charge carriers across the junction, unless an external voltage is applied.

The current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of a PN junction diode show how the current through the diode varies with the applied voltage. The I-V characteristics can be divided into two regions: forward bias and reverse bias.

- Forward bias: When the positive terminal of the external voltage is connected to the p-type side and the negative terminal to the n-type side, the diode is said to be forward biased. In this condition, the external voltage reduces the potential barrier at the junction, allowing the charge carriers to cross the junction and flow through the diode. - Reverse bias: When the positive terminal of the external voltage is connected to the n-type side and the negative terminal to the p-type side, the diode is said to be reverse biased. In this condition, the external voltage increases the potential barrier at the junction, preventing the charge carriers from crossing the junction and flowing through the diode. The static resistance of a PN junction diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the current at a given point on the I-V characteristics. It can be calculated by using the formula:

`R = V/I`

The static resistance of a diode varies with the applied voltage and the biasing condition. For example, the static resistance of a diode in forward bias is low, while the static resistance of a diode in reverse bias is high.

The dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode is the ratio of the change in voltage to the change in current at a given point on the I-V characteristics. It can be calculated by using the formula:

`r = ΔV/ΔI`

The dynamic resistance of a diode is a measure of how the diode responds to small changes in the applied voltage. It is also related to the slope of the I-V characteristics at a given point. For example, the dynamic resistance of a diode in forward bias is small, while the dynamic resistance of a diode in reverse bias is large.
