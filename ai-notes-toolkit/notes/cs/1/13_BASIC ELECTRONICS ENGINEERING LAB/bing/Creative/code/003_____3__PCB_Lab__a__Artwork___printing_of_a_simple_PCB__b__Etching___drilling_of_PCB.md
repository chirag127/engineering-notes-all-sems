### 3. PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

- A PCB (printed circuit board) is a thin board that holds electronic components and connects them with copper traces.
- To make a PCB, you need to design the circuit layout, create the artwork, print it on a special paper, transfer it to the board, etch away the unwanted copper, and drill holes for the components.
- The steps are as follows:

  - **Artwork and printing of a simple PCB**
    - Use a software tool such as Eagle, KiCad, or Fritzing to design the circuit layout and generate the artwork file. The artwork file contains the positive or negative image of the copper traces and pads on the board.
    - Print the artwork file on a glossy paper using a laser printer. Make sure the printout is clear and has the correct size and orientation.
    - Cut out the printout and align it with the copper side of the board. Use tape to secure it in place.
    - Use an iron or a laminator to heat up the paper and transfer the toner to the board. The toner acts as a resist that protects the copper from etching.
    - Peel off the paper and check the quality of the transfer. If there are any gaps or defects, use a marker pen to fill them in.

  - **Etching and drilling of PCB**
    - Prepare a plastic container with etching solution such as ferric chloride or sodium persulfate. Wear gloves and goggles to protect yourself from the chemicals.
    - Immerse the board in the solution and gently agitate it. The solution will dissolve the exposed copper and leave the toner-covered traces intact.
    - Monitor the etching process and remove the board when all the unwanted copper is gone. Rinse the board with water and dry it with a cloth.
    - Use acetone or nail polish remover to wipe off the toner from the board. You should see the shiny copper traces and pads on the board.
    - Use a drill or a punch to make holes for the components. Use a drill bit that matches the size of the component leads. Be careful not to damage the traces or the board.
    - Clean the board and inspect it for any errors or defects. You can use a multimeter to test the continuity and resistance of the traces.

- You have successfully made a simple PCB. You can now solder the components and test the circuit.
