### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

- A P-N junction diode is a semiconductor device that consists of a p-type and an n-type region separated by a depletion region where charge is stored.
- A P-N junction diode has the ability to rectify electric current, that is, to allow current to flow in one direction only .
- A P-N junction diode has a nonlinear current-voltage (I-V) characteristic, which depends on the applied voltage and the biasing of the diode .
- A P-N junction diode can also transform light energy into electrical energy, or vice versa, depending on the mode of operation .

#### Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph

- The static resistance of a P-N junction diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the current at a given point on the I-V characteristic.
- The static resistance can be calculated by drawing a straight line from the origin to the point of interest on the I-V curve, and finding the slope of the line.
- The static resistance is different for forward and reverse biasing, and it varies with the applied voltage.
- The dynamic resistance of a P-N junction diode is the ratio of the small change in voltage to the small change in current around a given operating point on the I-V characteristic.
- The dynamic resistance can be calculated by drawing a tangent to the I-V curve at the operating point, and finding the slope of the tangent.
- The dynamic resistance is also different for forward and reverse biasing, and it varies with the applied voltage.
- The dynamic resistance is usually much smaller than the static resistance, and it represents the small-signal behavior of the diode.

#### Example

- Consider the following I-V characteristic of a P-N junction diode:

- The static resistance at point A, where V = 0.7 V and I = 10 mA, is given by:

R_s = V/I = 0.7/0.01 = 70 ohms

- The dynamic resistance at point A can be estimated by drawing a tangent to the curve at point A, and finding the slope of the tangent. The slope can be approximated by using two points on the tangent, such as B and C, where V_B = 0.8 V, I_B = 20 mA, V_C = 0.6 V, and I_C = 5 mA. The slope is given by:

r_d = (V_B - V_C)/(I_B - I_C) = (0.8 - 0.6)/(0.02 - 0.005) = 13.33 ohms

- The static and dynamic resistances are different, and they depend on the applied voltage and the biasing of the diode.
