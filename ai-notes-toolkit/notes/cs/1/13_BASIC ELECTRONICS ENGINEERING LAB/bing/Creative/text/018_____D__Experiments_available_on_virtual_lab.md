## (D) Experiments available on virtual lab

- A virtual lab is a simulated environment that allows users to perform experiments or activities without the need for physical equipment, materials, or space.
- Virtual labs can be used for various purposes, such as education, research, training, testing, or entertainment.
- Some examples of experiments available on virtual labs are:

  - Physics: Users can explore the concepts of mechanics, optics, electricity, magnetism, thermodynamics, waves, and more by manipulating virtual objects and measuring their properties and behaviors.
  - Chemistry: Users can conduct chemical reactions, analyze substances, measure pH, titrate solutions, and more by using virtual labware and chemicals.
  - Biology: Users can observe and interact with living organisms, such as cells, bacteria, plants, animals, and humans, and learn about their structures, functions, and processes.
  - Engineering: Users can design, build, and test various systems and devices, such as circuits, robots, bridges, rockets, and more by using virtual components and tools.
  - Mathematics: Users can visualize and manipulate mathematical concepts, such as geometry, algebra, calculus, statistics, and more by using virtual graphs, equations, shapes, and functions.
  - Computer Science: Users can learn and practice programming, data structures, algorithms, artificial intelligence, and more by using virtual editors, compilers, debuggers, and simulators.
  - Psychology: Users can participate in or conduct psychological experiments, such as memory, perception, cognition, emotion, and more by using virtual stimuli, tasks, and measures.
  - Art: Users can create and explore various forms of art, such as painting, drawing, sculpting, music, and more by using virtual brushes, colors, shapes, sounds, and effects.
