### 1. Study of various types of Active & Passive Components based on their ratings.

- Active components are those that can amplify or control the flow of electric current or voltage. They usually require an external source of energy to operate. Examples of active components are transistors, diodes, integrated circuits, etc.
- Passive components are those that do not amplify or control the electric current or voltage. They only store or dissipate energy. Examples of passive components are resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.
- The ratings of components are the values of their electrical parameters that specify their performance and limitations. They are usually given by the manufacturers or standards. Some common ratings of components are:
  - Resistance: The opposition to the flow of electric current, measured in ohms (Ω).
  - Capacitance: The ability to store electric charge, measured in farads (F).
  - Inductance: The ability to generate an electromotive force (emf) due to a change in current, measured in henrys (H).
  - Voltage: The potential difference between two points, measured in volts (V).
  - Current: The rate of flow of electric charge, measured in amperes (A).
  - Power: The rate of doing work or transferring energy, measured in watts (W).
  - Frequency: The number of cycles of an alternating current or voltage per second, measured in hertz (Hz).
  - Impedance: The total opposition to the flow of alternating current, measured in ohms (Ω). It is a complex quantity that consists of resistance and reactance.
  - Reactance: The opposition to the flow of alternating current due to capacitance or inductance, measured in ohms (Ω). It is a complex quantity that consists of capacitive reactance and inductive reactance.
  - Gain: The ratio of output to input of a component, such as voltage gain, current gain, or power gain. It is a dimensionless quantity that can be expressed in decibels (dB).
  - Efficiency: The ratio of output power to input power of a component, expressed as a percentage (%).
  - Bandwidth: The range of frequencies over which a component can operate effectively, measured in hertz (Hz).
  - Noise: The unwanted or random fluctuations in the output of a component, measured in decibels (dB).
  - Temperature: The measure of the average kinetic energy of the molecules of a component, measured in degrees Celsius (°C) or Kelvin (K).
