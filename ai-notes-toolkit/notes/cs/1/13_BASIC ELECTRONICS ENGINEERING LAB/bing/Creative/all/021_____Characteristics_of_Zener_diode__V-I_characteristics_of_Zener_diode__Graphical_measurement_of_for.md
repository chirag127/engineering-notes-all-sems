# Characteristics of Zener Diode

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode designed to reliably allow current to flow in both forward and reverse directions when a certain set reverse voltage, known as the Zener voltage, is reached .
- Zener diodes are widely used as voltage references and as shunt regulators to regulate the voltage across small circuits .
- Zener diodes operate similarly to conventional diodes when in the forward-bias mode. They have a bias turn-on voltage of between 0.3 and 0.7V.
- Zener diodes are heavily doped than ordinary diodes. They have extra thin depletion region. When we apply a voltage more than the Zener breakdown voltage (can range from 1.2 volts to 200 volts), the depletion region vanishes, and large current starts to flow through the junction.

- The forward resistance (Rf) can be obtained by applying a small forward voltage (Vf) across the diode and measuring the forward current (If) using an ammeter. The forward resistance is given by Rf = Vf / If.
- The reverse resistance (Rz) can be obtained by applying a reverse voltage (Vr) across the diode and measuring the reverse current (Ir) using an ammeter. The reverse resistance is given by Rz = Vr / Ir.
- The reverse resistance is usually very high (in the order of megaohms) until the breakdown voltage is reached, and then it becomes very low (in the order of ohms) as the diode conducts in the reverse direction.
