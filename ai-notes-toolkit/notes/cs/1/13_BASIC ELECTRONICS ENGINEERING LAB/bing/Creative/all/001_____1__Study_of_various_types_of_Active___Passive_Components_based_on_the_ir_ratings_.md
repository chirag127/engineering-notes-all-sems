# 1. Study of various types of Active & Passive Components based on their ratings.

## Active Components
- Active components are electronic devices that can amplify, control, or generate electric signals.
- Active components require an external source of energy to operate, such as a battery or a power supply.
- Active components can increase the power of a signal, such as a transistor or an operational amplifier.
- Active components can also perform logic operations, such as a logic gate or a microcontroller.
- Some examples of active components are:

  - Transistors: These are semiconductor devices that can act as switches or amplifiers. They have three terminals: base, collector, and emitter. The base controls the current flow between the collector and the emitter. Transistors can be classified into bipolar junction transistors (BJTs) and field-effect transistors (FETs) based on their structure and operation.
  - Diodes: These are semiconductor devices that allow current to flow in one direction only. They have two terminals: anode and cathode. The anode is connected to the positive terminal of the power supply, and the cathode is connected to the negative terminal. Diodes can be classified into rectifier diodes, zener diodes, light-emitting diodes (LEDs), photodiodes, etc. based on their function and application.
  - Integrated Circuits (ICs): These are complex circuits that contain many active and passive components on a single chip. They have multiple pins that connect to the external circuit. ICs can be classified into analog ICs, digital ICs, mixed-signal ICs, etc. based on their signal processing and functionality.
  - Sensors: These are devices that can detect physical or chemical changes and convert them into electrical signals. They have two terminals: output and ground. The output varies according to the input stimulus. Sensors can be classified into temperature sensors, pressure sensors, light sensors, etc. based on their sensing element and application.

## Passive Components
- Passive components are electronic devices that do not require an external source of energy to operate. They can only store, dissipate, or transfer electric energy.
- Passive components cannot amplify, control, or generate electric signals. They can only modify the signal characteristics, such as amplitude, frequency, phase, etc.
- Passive components can also perform filtering, impedance matching, signal coupling, etc.
- Some examples of passive components are:

  - Resistors: These are devices that oppose the flow of current and produce a voltage drop across them. They have two terminals: positive and negative. The resistance value is measured in ohms (Ω) and depends on the material, length, cross-sectional area, and temperature of the resistor. Resistors can be classified into fixed resistors, variable resistors, thermistors, etc. based on their construction and behavior.
  - Capacitors: These are devices that store electric charge and produce a voltage across them. They have two terminals: positive and negative. The capacitance value is measured in farads (F) and depends on the area, distance, and dielectric material of the plates. Capacitors can be classified into fixed capacitors, variable capacitors, electrolytic capacitors, etc. based on their construction and behavior.
  - Inductors: These are devices that store magnetic energy and produce a voltage across them. They have two terminals: positive and negative. The inductance value is measured in henrys (H) and depends on the number of turns, core material, and shape of the coil. Inductors can be classified into fixed inductors, variable inductors, air-core inductors, etc. based on their construction and behavior.
  - Transformers: These are devices that transfer electric energy from one circuit to another by electromagnetic induction. They have two or more coils: primary and secondary. The primary coil is connected to the input voltage source, and the secondary coil is connected to the output load. The voltage ratio between the primary and secondary coils depends on the turns ratio of the coils. Transformers can be classified into step-up transformers, step-down transformers, isolation transformers, etc. based on their function and application.
