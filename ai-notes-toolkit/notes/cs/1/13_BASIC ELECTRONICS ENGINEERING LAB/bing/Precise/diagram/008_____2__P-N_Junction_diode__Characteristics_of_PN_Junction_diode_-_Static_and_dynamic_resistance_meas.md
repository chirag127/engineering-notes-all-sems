### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

A P-N junction diode is a semiconductor device formed by the junction of a p-type and an n-type semiconductor. It has two terminals, the anode (p-side) and the cathode (n-side), and allows current to flow only in one direction, from the anode to the cathode.

The characteristics of a P-N junction diode can be studied by plotting its current-voltage (I-V) graph, also known as the diode characteristic curve. The graph is obtained by applying a voltage across the diode and measuring the resulting current.

- **Static Resistance:** The static resistance of a diode is the ratio of the voltage across the diode to the current flowing through it at a particular point on the I-V graph. It can be calculated using Ohm's law, R = V/I.

- **Dynamic Resistance:** The dynamic resistance of a diode is the ratio of the change in voltage to the change in current at a particular point on the I-V graph. It can be calculated as rd = dV/dI, where dV and dI are the small changes in voltage and current, respectively.

The dynamic resistance is a measure of how the diode's resistance changes with the applied voltage. It is an important parameter in AC circuits, where the voltage and current are constantly changing.

To measure the static and dynamic resistance from the I-V graph, one can draw a tangent to the curve at the point of interest. The slope of the tangent gives the dynamic resistance, while the inverse of the slope gives the static resistance.

In summary, the characteristics of a P-N junction diode can be studied by plotting its I-V graph and measuring its static and dynamic resistance from the graph. These parameters provide valuable information about the diode's behavior in a circuit.
