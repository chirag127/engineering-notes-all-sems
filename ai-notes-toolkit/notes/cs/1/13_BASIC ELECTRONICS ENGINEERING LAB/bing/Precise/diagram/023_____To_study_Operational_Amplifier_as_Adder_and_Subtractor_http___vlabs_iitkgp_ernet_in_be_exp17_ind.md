### Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

An operational amplifier, or op-amp, is a high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and a single-ended output. It is used in a variety of electronic circuits and systems, including as an adder and subtractor.

#### Adder
An adder circuit is used to add two or more input signals and produce a single output signal that is the sum of the inputs. An op-amp can be used to create an adder circuit by connecting the inputs to the inverting and non-inverting inputs of the op-amp through resistors. The output of the op-amp is then fed back to the inverting input through a resistor, creating a negative feedback loop. This configuration is known as an inverting adder.

#### Subtractor
A subtractor circuit is used to subtract one input signal from another and produce a single output signal that is the difference between the inputs. An op-amp can be used to create a subtractor circuit by connecting the inputs to the inverting and non-inverting inputs of the op-amp through resistors. The output of the op-amp is then fed back to the inverting input through a resistor, creating a negative feedback loop. This configuration is known as an inverting subtractor.

In summary, an operational amplifier can be used as an adder and subtractor by configuring the inputs and feedback loop appropriately. These circuits are commonly used in analog signal processing and other electronic systems.
