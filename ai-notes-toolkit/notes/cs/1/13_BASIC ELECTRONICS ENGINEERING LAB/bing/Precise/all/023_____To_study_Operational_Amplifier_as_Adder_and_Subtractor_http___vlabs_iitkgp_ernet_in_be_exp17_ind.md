### To study Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

1. An operational amplifier, often referred to as an op-amp, is a DC-coupled high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and, usually, a single-ended output.
2. Originally, op-amps were named because they were used to model basic mathematical operations such as addition, subtraction, integration, differentiation, etc. in electronic analog computers.
3. In an adder configuration, an op-amp can be used as a mix bus summing amplifier, as used in audio mixing consoles.
4. In a subtractor configuration, an op-amp can be used as a differential amplifier to remove common-mode signal from a differential signal source.
5. The differential amplifier configuration is a combination of the inverting and noninverting voltage amplifiers.
