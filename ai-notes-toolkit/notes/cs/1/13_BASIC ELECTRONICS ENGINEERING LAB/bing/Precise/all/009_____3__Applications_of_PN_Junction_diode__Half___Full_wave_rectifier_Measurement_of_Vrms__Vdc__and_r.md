### 3. Applications of PN Junction diode: Half & Full wave rectifier, Measurement of Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor.

A PN junction diode is a two-terminal electronic device that allows the flow of current in only one direction. It is formed by the junction of p-type and n-type semiconductor materials. The PN junction diode has several applications, including its use as a rectifier in power supplies.

#### Half-wave rectifier:

A half-wave rectifier is a circuit that uses a single diode to convert an alternating current (AC) input into a direct current (DC) output. The diode is placed in series with the AC input and the load. During the positive half-cycle of the AC input, the diode is forward-biased and allows current to flow through the load. During the negative half-cycle, the diode is reverse-biased and blocks the flow of current. As a result, the output is a pulsating DC voltage with the same frequency as the AC input.

#### Full-wave rectifier:

A full-wave rectifier is a circuit that uses two or more diodes to convert an AC input into a DC output. There are two types of full-wave rectifiers: center-tapped and bridge. In a center-tapped full-wave rectifier, the secondary winding of the transformer is center-tapped and two diodes are used. During the positive half-cycle of the AC input, one diode is forward-biased and allows current to flow through the load. During the negative half-cycle, the other diode is forward-biased and allows current to flow through the load. As a result, the output is a pulsating DC voltage with twice the frequency of the AC input.

In a bridge full-wave rectifier, four diodes are used in a bridge configuration. During both the positive and negative half-cycles of the AC input, two diodes are forward-biased and allow current to flow through the load. As a result, the output is a pulsating DC voltage with twice the frequency of the AC input.

#### Measurement of Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor:

Vrms is the root mean square value of an AC voltage. It is calculated by squaring the instantaneous values of the AC voltage, averaging the squared values over one period, and taking the square root of the average.

Vdc is the average value of a pulsating DC voltage. It is calculated by averaging the instantaneous values of the DC voltage over one period.

The ripple factor is a measure of the smoothness of the DC output of a rectifier. It is defined as the ratio of the root mean square value of the AC component of the output voltage to the average value of the DC component. A lower ripple factor indicates a smoother DC output.
