### Characteristics of Zener Diode

A Zener diode is a type of diode that is designed to operate in the reverse breakdown region. The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode can be divided into two regions: the forward region and the reverse region.

#### Forward Region
- In the forward region, the Zener diode behaves like a normal diode.
- When a forward voltage is applied, the diode starts conducting and the current increases rapidly.
- The forward resistance of the diode is very low, typically in the range of a few ohms.

#### Reverse Region
- In the reverse region, the Zener diode behaves differently from a normal diode.
- When a reverse voltage is applied, the diode does not conduct until the voltage reaches the Zener voltage (Vz).
- At the Zener voltage, the diode starts conducting and the current increases rapidly.
- The reverse resistance of the diode is very high, typically in the range of megaohms.

#### Graphical Measurement of Forward and Reverse Resistance
- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode can be measured graphically from its V-I characteristics.
- The forward resistance is calculated by taking the reciprocal of the slope of the V-I characteristic in the forward region.
- The reverse resistance is calculated by taking the reciprocal of the slope of the V-I characteristic in the reverse region.
