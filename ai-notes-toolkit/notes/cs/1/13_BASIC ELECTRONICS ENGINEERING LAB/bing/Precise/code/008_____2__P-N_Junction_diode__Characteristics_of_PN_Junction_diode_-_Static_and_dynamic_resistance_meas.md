### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

A P-N junction diode is a semiconductor device formed by the junction of a p-type and an n-type semiconductor. It has two terminals, the anode (p-side) and the cathode (n-side), and allows current to flow only in one direction, from the anode to the cathode.

The characteristics of a PN junction diode can be studied by measuring its current-voltage (I-V) relationship. This is done by applying a voltage across the diode and measuring the resulting current. The I-V characteristic of a diode is non-linear, meaning that the current does not increase linearly with the applied voltage.

The static resistance of a diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the resulting current at a particular point on the I-V characteristic. It can be calculated by drawing a tangent to the I-V curve at that point and finding the slope of the tangent. The reciprocal of the slope gives the static resistance.

The dynamic resistance of a diode is the ratio of the change in voltage to the change in current at a particular point on the I-V characteristic. It can be calculated by drawing a tangent to the I-V curve at that point and finding the slope of the tangent. The reciprocal of the slope gives the dynamic resistance.

In summary, the static and dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode can be measured from its I-V characteristic graph by drawing tangents to the curve at the desired points and finding the slopes of the tangents. The reciprocal of the slopes gives the respective resistances.
