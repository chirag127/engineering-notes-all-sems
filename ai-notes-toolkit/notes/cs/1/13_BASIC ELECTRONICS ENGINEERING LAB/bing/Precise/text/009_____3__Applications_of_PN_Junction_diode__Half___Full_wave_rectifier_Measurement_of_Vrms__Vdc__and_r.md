### 3. Applications of PN Junction diode: Half & Full wave rectifier, Measurement of Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor.

A PN junction diode is a two-terminal electronic device that allows the flow of current in only one direction. It is formed by joining a p-type semiconductor material with an n-type semiconductor material. One of the main applications of a PN junction diode is in rectification, which is the conversion of alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC).

- **Half-wave rectifier**: In a half-wave rectifier, only one half of the AC input waveform is allowed to pass through the diode, while the other half is blocked. The output is a pulsating DC waveform with the same frequency as the input AC waveform.

- **Full-wave rectifier**: In a full-wave rectifier, both halves of the AC input waveform are allowed to pass through the diode, but in opposite directions. The output is a pulsating DC waveform with twice the frequency of the input AC waveform.

- **Measurement of Vrms**: The root mean square (RMS) value of an AC voltage is the equivalent DC voltage that would produce the same heating effect in a resistor. It is calculated by squaring the instantaneous voltage values, averaging them over one period, and then taking the square root of the result.

- **Measurement of Vdc**: The average or DC value of a rectified waveform is the average value of the output voltage over one period. It is calculated by averaging the instantaneous voltage values over one period.

- **Ripple factor**: The ripple factor is a measure of the effectiveness of a rectifier in converting AC to DC. It is defined as the ratio of the RMS value of the AC component of the output voltage to the DC component of the output voltage.
