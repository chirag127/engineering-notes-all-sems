### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

A P-N junction diode is a semiconductor device formed by the junction of a p-type and an n-type semiconductor. It has two terminals, the anode (p-side) and the cathode (n-side), and allows current to flow only in one direction, from the anode to the cathode.

The characteristics of a P-N junction diode can be studied by measuring its current-voltage (I-V) relationship. This is done by applying a voltage across the diode and measuring the resulting current. The I-V curve of a P-N junction diode is non-linear, with a sharp increase in current at a certain voltage known as the "knee" voltage.

The static resistance of a P-N junction diode is defined as the ratio of the applied voltage to the resulting current at a particular point on the I-V curve. It can be calculated by drawing a tangent to the I-V curve at the point of interest and finding the slope of the tangent.

The dynamic resistance of a P-N junction diode is defined as the ratio of the change in voltage to the change in current at a particular point on the I-V curve. It can be calculated by drawing a tangent to the I-V curve at the point of interest and finding the slope of the tangent.

In summary, the characteristics of a P-N junction diode can be studied by measuring its I-V relationship and calculating its static and dynamic resistance from the I-V graph. These measurements provide valuable information about the diode's behavior and can be used to design and analyze electronic circuits that use P-N junction diodes.
