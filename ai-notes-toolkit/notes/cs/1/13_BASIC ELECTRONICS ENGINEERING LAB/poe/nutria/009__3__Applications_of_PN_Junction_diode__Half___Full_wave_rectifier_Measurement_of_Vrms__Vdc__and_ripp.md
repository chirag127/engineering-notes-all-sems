
### 3. Applications of PN Junction Diode: Half & Full Wave Rectifier
1. Half Wave Rectifier: A half wave rectifier is a type of rectifier which converts an AC voltage into a DC voltage using only one-half of the input AC voltage waveform. It consists of a single p-n junction diode connected in series with the load. During the positive half cycle of the input AC voltage, the diode is forward biased and conducts current. This allows the current to flow through the load and charge the load capacitor. During the negative half cycle of the input AC voltage, the diode is reverse biased and does not conduct current.

2. Full Wave Rectifier: A full wave rectifier is a type of rectifier which converts an AC voltage into a DC voltage using both the positive and negative half cycles of the input AC voltage waveform. It consists of two p-n junction diodes connected in series with the load. During the positive half cycle of the input AC voltage, one of the diodes is forward biased and conducts current. This allows the current to flow through the load and charge the load capacitor. During the negative half cycle of the input AC voltage, the other diode is forward biased and conducts current. This allows the current to flow through the load in the opposite direction and charge the load capacitor.

3. Measurement of Vrms, Vdc, and Ripple Factor: The peak-to-peak voltage (Vpp) of a rectified waveform can be calculated by measuring the peak voltage (Vp) and the average voltage (Vavg). The root mean square voltage (Vrms) can be calculated by taking the square root of the average voltage. The direct current (Vdc) voltage can be calculated by taking the average of the peak voltage. The ripple factor (R) of a rectified waveform can be calculated by taking the ratio of the peak-to-peak voltage to the direct current voltage.
