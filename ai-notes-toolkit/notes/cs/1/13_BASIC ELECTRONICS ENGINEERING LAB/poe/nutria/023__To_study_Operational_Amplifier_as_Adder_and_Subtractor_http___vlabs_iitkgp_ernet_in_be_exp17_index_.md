
### To Study Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor 
1. An operational amplifier (op-amp) is an integrated circuit that is used to perform mathematical operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. 
2. It is used in a wide range of applications such as signal conditioning, signal amplification, voltage regulation and data conversion. 
3. An operational amplifier can be used as an adder and subtractor by connecting two input signals to the non-inverting and inverting inputs of the op-amp. 
4. The output voltage of the op-amp is the sum or difference of the input voltages, depending on the configuration of the circuit. 
5. The two input signals can be AC or DC signals, and the output voltage is also an AC or DC signal. 
6. The output voltage of the op-amp is proportional to the difference between the input voltages, and the gain of the op-amp can be adjusted by changing the value of the feedback resistor. 
7. The two input signals can also be used to control the gain of the op-amp, allowing it to be used as an amplifier. 
8. The op-amp can also be used to convert AC signals to DC signals, and vice versa.
