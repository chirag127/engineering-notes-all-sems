### Part A PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB This practical is not possible by virtual lab. It will be conducted only in physical mode.

In this lab, you will be learning about the process of creating a Printed Circuit Board (PCB), specifically the artwork and printing of a simple PCB, as well as the etching and drilling process.

### Artwork & Printing of a Simple PCB

- The first step in creating a PCB is to design the circuit schematic diagram. This will serve as a guide for the layout of the PCB.
- Once the schematic diagram is finalized, the next step is to create the PCB layout. This is done using a software program, such as Eagle or Altium Designer.
- After the PCB layout is completed, it is time to print the artwork onto a piece of transparency film using a laser printer.
- The printed artwork is then transferred onto a copper-clad board using a heat press or iron. This creates a resist layer on the copper surface, which will protect the traces and pads during the etching process.
- The board is then exposed to a UV light source, which hardens the resist layer on the board. The unhardened resist is then removed using a developer solution, leaving only the resist layer in the shape of the PCB layout.

### Etching & Drilling of PCB

- The next step in creating a PCB is to etch the board. This is done by placing the board into an etching solution, such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate. The solution dissolves the exposed copper, leaving only the traces and pads behind.
- After the etching process is complete, the board is rinsed and dried. The resist layer is then removed using a stripping solution.
- The final step is to drill the holes for the components. This is done using a drill press, and the holes are typically plated with copper to ensure good electrical conductivity.
