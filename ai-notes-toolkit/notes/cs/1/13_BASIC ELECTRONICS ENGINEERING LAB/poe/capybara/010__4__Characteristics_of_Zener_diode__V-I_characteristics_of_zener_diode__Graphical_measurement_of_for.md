### 4. Characteristics of Zener diode: V-I characteristics of zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

The zener diode is a specialized diode that is designed to operate in the reverse breakdown region of the PN junction. It is characterized by its ability to maintain a constant voltage across its terminals, even when the current through the diode varies. The following are the characteristics of the zener diode:

#### V-I Characteristics of Zener Diode
- The V-I (voltage-current) characteristics of the zener diode are shown in a graph.
- The graph shows that the voltage across the diode remains constant (the breakdown voltage) when the current through the diode varies.
- The breakdown voltage is a function of the doping concentration of the PN junction.
- The knee of the graph is the point at which the breakdown occurs.
- The zener diode is designed to operate in the breakdown region, where the voltage is constant.

#### Graphical Measurement of Forward and Reverse Resistance
- The forward resistance of a diode is the resistance in the forward-biased direction.
- The reverse resistance of a diode is the resistance in the reverse-biased direction.
- The forward resistance can be measured by applying a small forward bias voltage to the diode and measuring the resulting current.
- The reverse resistance can be measured by applying a small reverse bias voltage to the diode and measuring the resulting current.
- The graphical method involves plotting the current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of the diode and measuring the slope of the graph in the forward and reverse regions.
- The slope of the graph in the forward region gives the forward resistance, while the slope of the graph in the reverse region gives the reverse resistance.

In conclusion, the zener diode is a specialized diode that operates in the reverse breakdown region of the PN junction. It has a constant voltage across its terminals, even when the current through the diode varies. The V-I characteristics of the zener diode are shown in a graph, and the forward and reverse resistance can be measured graphically by plotting the I-V characteristics and measuring the slope of the graph.
