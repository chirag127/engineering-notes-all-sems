## Part (C):

1. Plants take in carbon dioxide and water and, using energy from the sun, convert it into glucose (food) and oxygen. This process is called photosynthesis. The chemical equation for photosynthesis is:

6CO2 + 6H2O + sunlight energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2

2. Chlorophyll is the main pigment responsible for photosynthesis. It absorbs blue and red wavelengths of light which provide the energy for photosynthesis to occur. Chlorophyll is located in the chloroplasts of plant cells.

3. The rate of photosynthesis is limited by multiple factors, including:

- Light intensity: As light intensity increases, the rate of photosynthesis increases proportionally until other factors become limiting.
- Carbon dioxide concentration: If carbon dioxide levels are low, the rate of photosynthesis is limited by this and cannot proceed efficiently even if there is plenty of light.
- Water availability: If water is insufficient, the rate of photosynthesis will decrease. Water is necessary for photosynthesis to occur.
- Temperature: The rate of photosynthesis increases with temperature until an optimum is reached. Extremely high or low temperatures will slow the rate of photosynthesis.
- Mineral nutrients: Certain minerals such as nitrogen, magnesium and iron are required for the production of chlorophyll and photosynthetic enzymes. A lack of these minerals will limit the rate of photosynthesis.

4. The oxygen produced during photosynthesis is released into the atmosphere, providing the oxygen that all respiratory organisms need to survive. Photosynthesis is ultimately responsible for the presence of oxygen in Earth's atmosphere.
