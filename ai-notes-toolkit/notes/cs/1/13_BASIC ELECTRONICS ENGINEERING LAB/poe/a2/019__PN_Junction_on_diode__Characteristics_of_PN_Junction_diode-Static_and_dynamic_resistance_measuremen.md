### PN Junction on diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode-Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

**PN Junction:** A PN junction is formed by joining p-type and n-type semiconductors together. At the junction, holes from p-type and electrons from n-type combine and cancel each other resulting in a depletion region with no mobile charge carriers. This creates a potential barrier.

**Characteristics of PN Junction diode:**

1. Forward bias: When positive voltage is applied to p-side and negative to n-side, the potential barrier is reduced. This allows electrons and holes to recombine, causing current to flow through the diode.
2. Reverse bias: When negative voltage is applied to p-side and positive to n-side, the potential barrier is increased. This prevents electron-hole recombination and blocks current through the diode.
3. Breakdown region: At sufficiently high reverse bias, the diode breaks down and current starts flowing. This is undesirable and damages the diode.

**Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph:**
The static resistance of a diode can be measured from the forward bias region of its characteristic graph. The slope of the linear portion gives the static resistance.
The dynamic resistance is the differential of voltage over current and can be measured from the exponential portion of the forward bias curve. It decreases with increase in current.
