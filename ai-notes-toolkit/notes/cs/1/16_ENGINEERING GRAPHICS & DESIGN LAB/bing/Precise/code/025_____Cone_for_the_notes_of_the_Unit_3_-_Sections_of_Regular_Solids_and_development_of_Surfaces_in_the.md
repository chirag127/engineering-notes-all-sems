### Cone for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A cone is a three-dimensional geometric shape that tapers smoothly from a flat base to a point called the apex or vertex.
- A cone is formed by a set of line segments, half-lines, or lines connecting a common point, the apex, to all of the points on a base that is in a plane that does not contain the apex.
- The base of a cone can be any shape, but it is often a circle, which is called a right circular cone.
- The axis of a cone is the straight line passing through the apex and the center of the base.
- The slant height of a cone is the distance from the apex to any point on the base circle.
- The lateral surface area of a cone is the area of the lateral surface, which is the surface excluding the base.
- The total surface area of a cone is the sum of the lateral surface area and the area of the base.
- The volume of a cone is one third the product of the area of the base and the height of the cone.
- In engineering graphics and design, the development of the surface of a cone is used to create a flat pattern of the cone that can be cut and folded to create a physical model of the cone.
- The development of the surface of a cone is a sector of a circle with a radius equal to the slant height of the cone and an angle equal to the circumference of the base divided by the slant height.
