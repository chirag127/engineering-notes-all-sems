### Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection, where the view direction is orthogonal to the projection plane, resulting in each plane of the scene appearing in affine transformation on the viewing surface.

In orthographic projection, the object is positioned within a series of imaginary planes, known as the principal planes of projection, which are perpendicular to the line of sight. The object is then projected onto these planes to create two-dimensional views.

The three principal planes of projection are the frontal plane, the horizontal plane, and the profile plane. The frontal plane is parallel to the front view of the object, the horizontal plane is parallel to the top view of the object, and the profile plane is parallel to the side view of the object.

Orthographic projection is commonly used in engineering and technical drawing to represent objects in a clear and unambiguous manner. It is also used in computer graphics and design to create two-dimensional representations of three-dimensional models.
