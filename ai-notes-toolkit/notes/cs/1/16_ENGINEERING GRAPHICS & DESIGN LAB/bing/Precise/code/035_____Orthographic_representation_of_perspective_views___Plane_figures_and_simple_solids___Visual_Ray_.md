### Orthographic representation of perspective views — Plane figures and simple solids — Visual Ray Method

Orthographic representation is a technique used to represent three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is commonly used in engineering and technical drawings to convey the shape and size of an object.

- Plane figures and simple solids are represented using orthographic projection, which involves projecting the object onto a plane perpendicular to the line of sight.
- The visual ray method is a technique used to construct the orthographic views of an object. It involves drawing rays from the observer's eye to the edges and corners of the object, and then projecting these rays onto the plane of projection.
- This method can be used to represent both plane figures and simple solids, and is particularly useful for objects with complex shapes or irregular surfaces.

In the context of isometric projection, which is a form of axonometric projection, the visual ray method can be used to construct the isometric view of an object. This involves projecting the object onto an isometric plane, which is oriented at an angle to the line of sight.

Overall, the orthographic representation of perspective views, including the use of the visual ray method, is an important tool in the field of engineering graphics and design, allowing for the accurate and precise representation of objects in two dimensions.
