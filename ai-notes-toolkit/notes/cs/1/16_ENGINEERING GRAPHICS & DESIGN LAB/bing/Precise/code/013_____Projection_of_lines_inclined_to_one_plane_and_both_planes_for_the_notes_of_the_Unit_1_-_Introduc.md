### Projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes

In the subject of Engineering Graphics & Design Lab, Unit 1 covers the topic of Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections. One of the important concepts in this unit is the projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes.

1. A line is said to be inclined to a plane when it makes an angle with the plane other than 90 degrees.
2. When a line is inclined to one plane, its projection on that plane will be shorter than its true length.
3. The true length of the line can be determined by drawing an auxiliary plane perpendicular to the line and projecting the line onto that plane.
4. When a line is inclined to both the horizontal and vertical planes, its projections on both planes will be shorter than its true length.
5. The true length of the line and its inclinations to the horizontal and vertical planes can be determined by drawing two auxiliary planes, one perpendicular to the horizontal plane and the other perpendicular to the vertical plane, and projecting the line onto both planes.

These concepts are important to understand in order to accurately represent 3D objects in 2D drawings using orthographic projections. It is essential to practice these concepts in order to become proficient in engineering drawing.
