### Pyramid

A pyramid is a polyhedron with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common point called the apex. The base can be any polygon, such as a square, triangle, or hexagon. The triangular faces are called lateral faces.

- The height of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the base to the apex.
- A regular pyramid is a pyramid whose base is a regular polygon and whose lateral faces are congruent isosceles triangles.
- The slant height of a regular pyramid is the distance from the center of the base to the midpoint of an edge of the base.
- The lateral area of a pyramid is the sum of the areas of its lateral faces.
- The total surface area of a pyramid is the sum of its lateral area and the area of its base.
- The volume of a pyramid is one-third the product of the area of the base and the height.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, the development of the surface of a pyramid involves unfolding the lateral faces onto a flat plane. This can be useful for creating a net or pattern for constructing a physical model of the pyramid. The development of the surface can be achieved by drawing the lateral faces as separate triangles, with their bases aligned along a straight line. The triangles can then be cut out and folded along the common edges to form the pyramid.
