### Chamfer
- A chamfer is a transitional edge between two faces of an object.
- It is sometimes defined as a form of bevel, and is often created at a 45° angle between two adjoining right-angled faces.
- Chamfers are frequently used in machining, carpentry, furniture, concrete formwork, and other fields to facilitate assembly of parts, to reduce the risk of injury from sharp edges, and to improve the appearance of an edge.
- In computer-aided design, chamfers can be easily added to the edges of a 3D model, and the size and angle of the chamfer can be adjusted as needed.
- Chamfers can also be used to improve the flow of fluids or gases through pipes or other objects, by reducing turbulence and resistance at the junction of two surfaces.
- In engineering graphics and design, chamfers are often used to indicate that an edge is to be machined or finished in a specific way, and are typically represented using a symbol or notation on a technical drawing.
