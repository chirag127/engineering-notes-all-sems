## Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel associated with the engineering profession. It is used to convey ideas and information about the design, construction, and operation of objects or systems.

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection, where the view direction is orthogonal to the projection plane, resulting in each plane of the scene appearing in affine transformation on the viewing surface.

The following are some key points to remember when working with engineering drawings and orthographic projections:

1. Engineering drawings are used to communicate technical information and ideas in a clear and concise manner.
2. Orthographic projections are used to represent three-dimensional objects in two dimensions.
3. Orthographic projections are a form of parallel projection, where the view direction is orthogonal to the projection plane.
4. Each plane of the scene appears in affine transformation on the viewing surface in an orthographic projection.
5. It is important to understand and follow standard conventions and practices when creating and interpreting engineering drawings and orthographic projections.
