### Projection of Solids like Prisms

In the subject of Engineering Graphics & Design Lab, Unit 2 covers the topic of Projection of Planes and Solids. Here are some key points to remember when studying the projection of solids like prisms:

1. A prism is a polyhedron with two parallel and congruent polygonal faces, called bases, and a set of parallelogram faces, called lateral faces, connecting the corresponding sides of the bases.
2. The projection of a solid is the representation of the solid on a plane, obtained by connecting the points where the lines of sight from the observer's eye to the points on the solid meet the plane.
3. The projection of a prism can be obtained by projecting its vertices and edges onto the plane of projection.
4. The type of projection used (orthographic, isometric, etc.) will affect the appearance of the projected prism.
5. The orientation of the prism relative to the plane of projection will also affect the appearance of the projected prism.
6. It is important to carefully consider the position and orientation of the prism when creating its projection, in order to accurately represent the solid.
