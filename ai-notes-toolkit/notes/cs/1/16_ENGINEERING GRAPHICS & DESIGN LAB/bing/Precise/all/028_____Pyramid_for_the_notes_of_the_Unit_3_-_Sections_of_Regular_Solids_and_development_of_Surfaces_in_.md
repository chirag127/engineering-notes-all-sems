# Pyramid

A pyramid is a polyhedron with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common point called the apex. The base can be any polygon, and the triangular faces are called lateral faces. The height of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the base to the apex.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, the development of the surface of a pyramid involves unfolding the lateral faces onto a flat plane. This is useful for creating patterns for cutting and bending sheet metal or other materials to form the shape of the pyramid.

Here are some key points to remember when developing the surface of a pyramid:

1. The lateral faces of a pyramid are congruent isosceles triangles.
2. The slant height of a pyramid is the altitude of any of its lateral faces.
3. The development of the surface of a pyramid is a sector of a circle with a radius equal to the slant height of the pyramid.
4. The central angle of the sector is equal to the total angle of the base polygon.
5. The lateral edges of the pyramid correspond to the radii of the sector in the development.
