# Cone for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A cone is a three-dimensional geometric shape that tapers smoothly from a flat base to a point called the apex or vertex.
- A cone is formed by a set of line segments, half-lines, or lines connecting a common point, the apex, to all of the points on a base that is in a plane that does not contain the apex.
- The base of a cone can be any shape, but it is often a circle, in which case the cone is called a right circular cone.
- The axis of a cone is the straight line passing through the apex and the center of the base.
- The lateral surface of a cone is the curved surface that connects the base to the apex.
- The slant height of a cone is the distance from any point on the circle of the base to the apex, measured along the lateral surface.
- The volume of a cone is one third the product of the area of the base and the height of the cone.
- The lateral surface area of a cone is the product of the base circumference and the slant height, divided by two.
- The total surface area of a cone is the sum of the lateral surface area and the base area.
- A cone can be cut by a plane to form a conic section, such as a circle, ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola.
- The development of the surface of a cone is a sector of a circle, with the radius equal to the slant height of the cone and the central angle equal to the circumference of the base divided by the slant height.
