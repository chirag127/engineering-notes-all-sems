## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a type of axonometric projection, where the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angles between them are 120 degrees.

Some key points to remember about isometric projection are:

1. Isometric projection is a form of orthographic projection, where the projection lines are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the projection plane.
2. In isometric projection, the three principal axes of the object are equally inclined to the plane of projection.
3. Isometric projection is commonly used in technical and engineering drawings, as well as in video games and other forms of media.
4. Isometric projection can be used to represent objects in a way that is easy to understand and visually appealing.
5. Isometric projection is not the same as isometric view, which is a perspective projection where the angles between the three principal axes are not equal.

Isometric projection can be a useful tool for representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions, and is widely used in technical and engineering drawings. It is important to understand the principles of isometric projection in order to use it effectively.
