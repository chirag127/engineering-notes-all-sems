# Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

## Orthographic representation of perspective views

Orthographic representation is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection, where the view direction is orthogonal to the projection plane, resulting in each plane of the scene appearing in affine transformation on the viewing surface.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, orthographic representation is used to create detailed technical drawings of objects, such as machine parts or architectural plans. These drawings are used to communicate the design and dimensions of the object to others, such as manufacturers or builders.

## Plane figures and simple solids

Plane figures are two-dimensional shapes, such as circles, squares, and triangles. Simple solids are three-dimensional shapes, such as cubes, spheres, and cones.

In orthographic representation, plane figures and simple solids are represented using multiple views, typically a front, top, and side view. Each view shows the object as if it were being viewed from a different direction, providing a complete representation of the object.

## Visual Ray Method

The visual ray method is a technique used to create perspective drawings of objects. It involves drawing lines, called visual rays, from the object to a point representing the viewer's eye. These lines are then projected onto a picture plane to create a perspective view of the object.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, the visual ray method can be used to create more realistic representations of objects, providing a better understanding of their three-dimensional form.
