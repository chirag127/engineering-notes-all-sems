# Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection, where all the projection lines are orthogonal to the projection plane. This results in each plane of the object being projected onto the plane as a true shape, with no distortion.

In engineering drawing, orthographic projection is commonly used to represent the shape of an object in a technical drawing. It is used to provide a clear and unambiguous representation of the object, with all its details and dimensions.

Some key points to remember about orthographic projection are:

1. It is a method of representing 3D objects in 2D.
2. It is a form of parallel projection.
3. All projection lines are orthogonal to the projection plane.
4. Each plane of the object is projected as a true shape, with no distortion.
5. It is commonly used in technical drawing to provide a clear and unambiguous representation of an object.
