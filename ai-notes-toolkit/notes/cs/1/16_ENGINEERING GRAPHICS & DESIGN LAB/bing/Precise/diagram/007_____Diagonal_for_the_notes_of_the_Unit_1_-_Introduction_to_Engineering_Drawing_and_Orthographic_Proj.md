### Diagonal

- A diagonal is a straight line that connects two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon or a polyhedron.
- In a two-dimensional figure, such as a square or a rectangle, the diagonal is the line segment that connects two opposite corners.
- In a three-dimensional figure, such as a cube or a rectangular prism, the diagonal is the line segment that connects two opposite corners of the figure, passing through the interior of the figure.
- The length of the diagonal can be calculated using the Pythagorean theorem in two-dimensional figures, and using the space diagonal formula in three-dimensional figures.
- In engineering drawing, diagonals are often used to determine the center of a circle or an arc, to bisect angles, and to draw perpendicular lines.
- Diagonals are also used in orthographic projections to project the true length of an inclined line or surface.
- In isometric projection, diagonals are used to represent the true length of an inclined line or surface by drawing the diagonal of a parallelogram or a rectangle.
