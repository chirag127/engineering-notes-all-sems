## Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design

Computer Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer software to design and document a product's design process. CAD software is used to increase the productivity of the designer, improve the quality of design, improve communications through documentation, and to create a database for manufacturing.

1. **History of CAD**: The development of CAD software can be traced back to the 1960s, when Dr. Patrick J. Hanratty developed a numerical control programming system called PRONTO. This system was used to generate tool paths for numerically controlled machines. In the 1970s, the first commercial CAD systems were introduced, and by the 1980s, CAD systems were widely used in the automotive and aerospace industries.

2. **Types of CAD software**: There are many different types of CAD software available, including 2D drafting, 3D modeling, and simulation software. Some popular CAD software programs include AutoCAD, SolidWorks, and CATIA.

3. **Applications of CAD**: CAD software is used in a wide range of industries, including architecture, engineering, and construction. It is also used in the design of products such as cars, airplanes, and consumer goods.

4. **Benefits of using CAD**: The use of CAD software can provide many benefits, including increased productivity, improved design quality, and better communication. CAD software can also help to reduce errors and rework, and can facilitate the reuse of design data.

5. **Future of CAD**: The future of CAD is likely to involve the continued development of new and improved CAD software, as well as the integration of CAD with other technologies such as virtual reality and 3D printing. This will allow designers to create and test their designs in a virtual environment before they are manufactured.
