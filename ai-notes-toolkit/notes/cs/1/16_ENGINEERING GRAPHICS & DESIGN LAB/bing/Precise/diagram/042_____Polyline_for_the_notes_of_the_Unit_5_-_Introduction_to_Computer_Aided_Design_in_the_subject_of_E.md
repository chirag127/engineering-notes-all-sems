### Polyline

A polyline is a continuous line composed of one or more line segments. It is a basic element in computer-aided design (CAD) and is used to represent a wide range of geometric shapes, including lines, curves, and polygons.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, polylines are commonly used to represent the following:

1. **Wireframes:** A wireframe is a visual representation of a three-dimensional object, composed of lines and curves that represent the edges of the object. Polylines are used to create the individual lines and curves that make up the wireframe.

2. **Contours:** A contour is a line that represents a constant elevation or depth on a topographic map or a constant value on a graph. Polylines are used to create the individual contour lines.

3. **Boundaries:** A boundary is a line that defines the limits of an area or region. Polylines are used to create the individual boundary lines.

4. **Paths:** A path is a line that represents the route or trajectory of an object or entity. Polylines are used to create the individual path lines.

Polylines can be created and edited using a variety of CAD tools, including line, arc, and spline tools. They can also be modified using editing commands such as trim, extend, and fillet.

In summary, polylines are a versatile and essential element in CAD and are widely used in engineering graphics and design to represent a variety of geometric shapes and features.
