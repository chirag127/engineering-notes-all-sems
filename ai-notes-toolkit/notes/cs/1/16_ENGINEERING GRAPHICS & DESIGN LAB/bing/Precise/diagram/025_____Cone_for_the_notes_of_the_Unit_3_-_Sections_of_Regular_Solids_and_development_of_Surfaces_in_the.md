### Cone - Unit 3: Sections of Regular Solids and Development of Surfaces - ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A cone is a three-dimensional geometric shape that tapers smoothly from a flat base to a point called the apex or vertex.
- A cone is formed by a set of line segments, half-lines, or lines connecting a common point, the apex, to all of the points on a base that is in a plane that does not contain the apex.
- The base of a cone can be any shape, but it is often a circle, in which case the cone is called a right circular cone.
- The axis of a cone is the straight line passing through the apex and the center of the base.
- The lateral surface of a cone is the curved surface that connects the base to the apex.
- The slant height of a cone is the distance from any point on the circle of the base to the apex along the lateral surface.
- The volume of a cone is given by the formula V = 1/3πr²h, where r is the radius of the base, h is the height of the cone, and π is approximately equal to 3.14.
- The surface area of a cone is given by the formula A = πr² + πr√(r² + h²), where r is the radius of the base, h is the height of the cone, and π is approximately equal to 3.14.
- A cone can be cut by a plane to form different sections, such as a circle, an ellipse, a parabola, or a hyperbola.
- The development of the surface of a cone is a sector of a circle with radius equal to the slant height of the cone and central angle equal to the circumference of the base divided by the slant height.
