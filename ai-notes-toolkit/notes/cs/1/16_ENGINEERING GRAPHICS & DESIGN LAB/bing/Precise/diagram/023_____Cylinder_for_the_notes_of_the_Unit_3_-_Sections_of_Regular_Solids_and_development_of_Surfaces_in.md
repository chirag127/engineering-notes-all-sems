### Cylinder
A cylinder is a three-dimensional geometric shape with two parallel circular bases and a curved surface connecting them. It is a type of prism and is classified as a right circular cylinder if the axis joining the centers of the bases is perpendicular to the bases, and as an oblique cylinder otherwise.

Some key points to remember about cylinders in the context of Engineering Graphics & Design Lab are:
- The development of a cylinder involves unfolding the curved surface into a flat plane.
- The development of the lateral surface of a right circular cylinder is a rectangle.
- The height of the rectangle is equal to the height of the cylinder, and the width is equal to the circumference of the base.
- The development of the lateral surface of an oblique cylinder is a parallelogram.
- The true length of the oblique element is equal to the slant height of the cylinder, and the width is equal to the circumference of the base.
- The development of the entire surface of a cylinder includes the development of the two bases, which are circles, in addition to the development of the lateral surface.
