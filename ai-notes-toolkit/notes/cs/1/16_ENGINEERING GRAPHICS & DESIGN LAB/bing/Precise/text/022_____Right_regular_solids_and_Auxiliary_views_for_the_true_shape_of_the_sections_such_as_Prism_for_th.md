### Right Regular Solids and Auxiliary Views for the True Shape of the Sections such as Prism

Right regular solids are three-dimensional shapes that have identical regular polygon faces. These include the Platonic solids, which are the tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, and icosahedron. These shapes have the same number of faces meeting at each vertex and the same length of edges.

Auxiliary views are used to show the true shape of a section or feature that is not parallel to any of the principal planes of projection. These views are projected onto an auxiliary plane, which is perpendicular to the feature being shown.

In the case of a prism, an auxiliary view can be used to show the true shape of a section that is not parallel to any of the principal planes of projection. This can be useful when trying to determine the development of the surface of the prism.

Here are some key points to remember when using auxiliary views to determine the true shape of a section of a prism:

1. The auxiliary plane must be perpendicular to the feature being shown.
2. The auxiliary view is projected onto the auxiliary plane.
3. The true shape of the section will be shown in the auxiliary view.
4. The development of the surface can be determined using the true shape of the section shown in the auxiliary view.

It is important to understand the use of auxiliary views when working with right regular solids such as prisms. This knowledge can help in determining the true shape of sections and the development of surfaces.
