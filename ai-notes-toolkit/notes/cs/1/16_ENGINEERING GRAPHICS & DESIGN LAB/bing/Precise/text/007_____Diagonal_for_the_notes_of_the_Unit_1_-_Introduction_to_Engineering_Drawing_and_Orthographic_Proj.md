### Diagonal

- A diagonal is a line segment that connects two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon.
- In a two-dimensional plane, a diagonal can be defined as a line segment joining two non-consecutive points of a polygon.
- In a three-dimensional space, a diagonal can be defined as a line segment joining two non-consecutive points of a polyhedron.
- In an n-dimensional space, a diagonal can be defined as a line segment joining two non-consecutive points of an n-dimensional polytope.
- In the context of engineering drawing and orthographic projections, diagonals can be used to determine the true length of a line or the true angle between two lines.
- Diagonals can also be used to construct auxiliary views, which are used to show the true shape of an inclined surface.
- In an isometric drawing, diagonals can be used to determine the orientation of an object in three-dimensional space.
- Diagonals can also be used to construct perspective drawings, which are used to represent three-dimensional objects in two-dimensional space.
