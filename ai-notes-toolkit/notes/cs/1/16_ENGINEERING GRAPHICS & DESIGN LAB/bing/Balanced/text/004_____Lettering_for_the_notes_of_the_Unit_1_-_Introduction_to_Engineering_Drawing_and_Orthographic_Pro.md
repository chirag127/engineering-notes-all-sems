### Lettering for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Lettering is the process of writing titles, subtitles, symbols, dimension values, notes, and other elements on a drawing.
- Lettering is used to specify details of an object on a drawing.
- Lettering is one type of freehand drawing, which means it is done without using any instruments or tools.
- Lettering should be clear, legible, uniform, and consistent in size and style.
- Lettering should follow some general rules and guidelines, such as:

  - The line thickness of the letter should appear as if it is obtained in one stroke of the pencil with uniform pressure, but not so much that it cuts grooves in the drawing sheet.
  - Words should be placed one letter apart.
  - The letters should be spaced in such a way that the area between letters is almost equal.
  - The height of the letters should be proportional to the size of the drawing.
  - The letters should be aligned horizontally or vertically, depending on the orientation of the drawing.
  - The letters should be written in capital letters, unless otherwise specified.
  - The letters should be written in a standard font, such as Gothic, Roman, or Italic.
  - The letters should be written in a single stroke, without lifting the pencil or retracing the lines.
  - The letters should be written with a sharp pencil or a pen of suitable size and color.
  - The letters should be written on a smooth and clean drawing sheet, without any smudges or erasures.

- Lettering can be classified into two types, based on the shape of the letters:

  - Vertical lettering: The letters are written with vertical strokes, and the height of the letters is equal to the width of the strokes. This type of lettering is commonly used for titles, subtitles, and dimension values.
  - Inclined lettering: The letters are written with inclined strokes, and the height of the letters is greater than the width of the strokes. This type of lettering is commonly used for notes, symbols, and abbreviations.

- Lettering can also be classified into two types, based on the slope of the letters:

  - Normal lettering: The letters are written with a slope of 75 degrees from the horizontal line. This type of lettering is suitable for most drawings and purposes.
  - Italic lettering: The letters are written with a slope of 15 degrees from the vertical line. This type of lettering is used for emphasis, contrast, or decoration.

- Lettering can be done by using different methods, such as:

  - Freehand lettering: The letters are written by using the eye and hand coordination, without any aids or guides. This method requires practice and skill, but it is fast and flexible.
  - Guide lettering: The letters are written by using some guides, such as grids, lines, or templates. This method ensures accuracy and uniformity, but it is slow and rigid.
  - Computer lettering: The letters are written by using a computer software, such as AutoCAD, SolidWorks, or SketchUp. This method offers convenience and variety, but it may lack personal touch and creativity.

- Lettering is an essential part of engineering drawing, as it conveys information and instructions that cannot be expressed by graphical means. Lettering should be done with care and attention, following the standards and conventions of the field. Lettering should also be done with neatness and elegance, reflecting the quality and professionalism of the work.
