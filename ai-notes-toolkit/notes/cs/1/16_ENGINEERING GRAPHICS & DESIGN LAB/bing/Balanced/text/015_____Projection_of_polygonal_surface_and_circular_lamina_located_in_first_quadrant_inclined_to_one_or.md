### Projection of polygonal surface and circular lamina located in first quadrant inclined to one or both reference planes

- A polygonal surface is a plane surface bounded by straight lines, such as a triangle, a square, a pentagon, etc.
- A circular lamina is a plane surface bounded by a circle, such as a coin, a disc, a wheel, etc.
- Projection of a plane surface is the outline of the surface as seen from a given direction, such as the top view or the front view.
- To project a plane surface, we need to know its position and orientation with respect to the reference planes, namely the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP).
- The position of a plane surface can be specified by the distances of its corners or center from the reference planes, measured along the perpendiculars.
- The orientation of a plane surface can be specified by the angles of inclination of the surface or its edges with the reference planes, measured in the planes containing them.
- A plane surface is said to be located in the first quadrant if it is above HP and in front of VP.
- A plane surface is said to be inclined to a reference plane if it makes an angle other than 90° with that plane.
- A plane surface is said to be perpendicular to a reference plane if it makes an angle of 90° with that plane.
- A plane surface is said to be parallel to a reference plane if it makes an angle of 0° or 180° with that plane.
- To project a plane surface inclined to one or both reference planes, we can use the following steps:
  - Draw the projections of the plane surface in its true shape and size on the reference plane to which it is perpendicular or parallel, or on an auxiliary plane parallel to the surface.
  - Project the corners or the center of the plane surface from the reference plane or the auxiliary plane to the other reference plane, along the perpendiculars or the projectors.
  - Join the projected points to get the outline of the plane surface on the other reference plane.
  - If the plane surface is inclined to both reference planes, we can also use the method of rotation to project it. This involves rotating the plane surface about one of its edges or axes until it becomes parallel or perpendicular to one of the reference planes, and then projecting it on both reference planes. The angle of rotation is equal to the angle of inclination of the plane surface with the reference plane about which it is rotated.
