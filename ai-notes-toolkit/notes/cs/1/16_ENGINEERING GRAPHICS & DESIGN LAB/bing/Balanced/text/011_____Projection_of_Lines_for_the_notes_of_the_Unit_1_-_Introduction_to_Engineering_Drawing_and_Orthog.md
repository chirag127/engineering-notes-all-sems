### Projection of Lines

- A projection of a line is a geometrically represented image of the line obtained on a plane or surface.
- A line can be projected on different planes, such as the horizontal plane (HP), the vertical plane (VP), the profile plane (PP), or the auxiliary plane (AP).
- The projection of a line on a plane depends on the position and orientation of the line with respect to the plane.
- There are four possible cases of projection of a line on a plane:
  - Case 1: The line is parallel to both the plane and the projector (the line of sight).
  - Case 2: The line is parallel to the plane but inclined to the projector.
  - Case 3: The line is inclined to the plane but parallel to the projector.
  - Case 4: The line is inclined to both the plane and the projector.
- The projection of a line on a plane can be determined by projecting the end points of the line on the plane and joining them.
- The projection of a line on a plane can be classified as true length (TL), apparent length (AL), or foreshortened length (FL) depending on the angle between the line and the projector :
  - True length: The projection of a line on a plane is equal to the actual length of the line when the line is perpendicular to the projector.
  - Apparent length: The projection of a line on a plane is less than the actual length of the line when the line is inclined to the projector.
  - Foreshortened length: The projection of a line on a plane is zero when the line is parallel to the projector.
- The projection of a line on a plane can be represented by a line segment, a point, or a trace (the point of intersection of the line and the plane) depending on the case of projection .
- The projection of a line on a plane can be used to determine the true length, the inclination, and the traces of the line.
- The projection of a line on a plane can be shown by using different types of lines, such as continuous thin line, continuous thick line, dashed line, or dotted line depending on the visibility and importance of the projection .
- The projection of a line on a plane can be illustrated by using orthographic projection, isometric projection, or perspective projection depending on the purpose and the effect of the projection .
