### Isometric scales for the notes of the Unit 4 - Isometric Projection in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees.
- Isometric projection is used by engineers, technical illustrators, and occasionally, architects to show the dimensions and shape of an object without distortion.
- Isometric projection can also create a unique and eye-catching graphic design style, known as isometric design, that shows an object from a bird’s eye angle.
- To draw an isometric projection, an isometric scale is needed to convert the actual dimensions of the object to the isometric dimensions on the drawing plane.
- The isometric scale is constructed as follows:

  - Draw a horizontal line OA.
  - Through O draw OP and OQ at 30º and 45º to the horizontal respectively.
  - On OQ mark the actual scale in mm.
  - Draw verticals from each of the division points on the actual scale to cut OP at the corresponding divisions.
  - The divisions on OP are the isometric scale.

- The isometric scale can be used to measure the isometric length of any line parallel to the three isometric axes.
- The isometric scale can also be used to measure the isometric radius of any circle on the isometric planes.
- The isometric scale is convenient and practical for drawing isometric projections of simple objects with regular dimensions.
- However, for complex objects with irregular dimensions, the use of isometric scale is impractical and inconvenient.
- In such cases, the isometric projection can be drawn by using the isometric axes and angles, without using the isometric scale.
