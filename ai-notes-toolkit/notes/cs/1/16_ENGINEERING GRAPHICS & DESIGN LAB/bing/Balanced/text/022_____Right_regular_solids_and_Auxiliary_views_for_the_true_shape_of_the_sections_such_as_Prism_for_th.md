### Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism

- A right regular solid is a three-dimensional geometric figure whose faces are congruent regular polygons and whose edges are perpendicular to the faces. Examples of right regular solids are cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and cones.
- An auxiliary view is a view that shows the true shape and size of a surface that is not parallel to any of the principal planes of projection. Auxiliary views are useful for showing the details of inclined or oblique surfaces in an object.
- To draw an auxiliary view of a right regular solid, such as a prism, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the front, top, and right side views of the prism in orthographic projection, using dotted lines to show hidden edges.
  - Identify the inclined surface or surfaces that need to be shown in true shape and size. In this case, it is the top surface of the prism.
  - Draw a reference line perpendicular to the inclined surface in one of the principal views, usually the front view. This line is called the folding line, and it represents the edge of the auxiliary plane of projection.
  - Project the vertices of the inclined surface from the front view to the folding line, and label them with letters.
  - Draw a line perpendicular to the folding line, and extend it to the right. This line is called the hinge line, and it represents the axis of rotation of the auxiliary plane of projection.
  - Swing the auxiliary plane of projection about the hinge line until it is parallel to the inclined surface. This will result in a view that shows the true shape and size of the inclined surface.
  - Project the vertices of the inclined surface from the folding line to the auxiliary plane of projection, and label them with the same letters as in the front view.
  - Connect the vertices of the inclined surface in the auxiliary plane of projection, and shade the surface if desired. This is the auxiliary view of the prism.
  - Add dimensions, notes, and symbols as needed to the auxiliary view and the principal views.
