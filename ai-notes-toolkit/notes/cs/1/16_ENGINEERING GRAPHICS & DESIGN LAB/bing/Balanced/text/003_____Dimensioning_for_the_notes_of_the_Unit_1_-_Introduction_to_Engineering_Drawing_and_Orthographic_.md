### Dimensioning

- Dimensioning is the process of adding measurements and other information to a drawing to specify the size, shape, location, and features of an object.
- Dimensioning is essential for engineering drawings, as it allows the fabrication, inspection, and assembly of the object according to the design specifications.
- Dimensioning follows certain rules and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency of the drawings.
- Some of the basic rules and conventions of dimensioning are:

  - Dimensions should be placed outside the outline of the object, unless there is not enough space or it would reduce the clarity of the drawing.
  - Dimensions should be aligned with the dimension lines, which are thin, continuous lines with arrowheads at both ends to indicate the extent of the dimension.
  - Dimensions should be placed above the dimension lines, except for angular dimensions, which are placed in the arc of the angle.
  - Dimensions should be given in millimeters (mm) or meters (m), unless otherwise specified.
  - Dimensions should be given to the nearest 0.1 mm for sizes up to 100 mm, and to the nearest 1 mm for sizes above 100 mm, unless otherwise specified.
  - Dimensions should not be repeated or duplicated on the same drawing or on different views of the same object.
  - Dimensions should not be derived from other dimensions, such as by adding or subtracting, unless they are clearly marked as reference dimensions, which are given in parentheses.
  - Dimensions should not cross or coincide with other dimension lines, extension lines, or outlines of the object.
  - Extension lines are thin, continuous lines that extend from the outline of the object to the dimension lines, with a small gap between them.
  - Extension lines should not extend beyond the dimension lines, unless they are used to dimension a feature that is hidden or not visible in the view.
  - Dimensions should be placed in the most descriptive view of the object, where the shape and size of the feature are clearly shown.
  - Dimensions should be arranged in a logical order, from general to specific, and from overall to detail.
  - Dimensions should be grouped or aligned whenever possible, to avoid clutter and confusion.
  - Dimensions should be placed parallel to the feature being dimensioned, or to the horizontal or vertical axis of the drawing, unless otherwise specified.
  - Dimensions should be placed on the same side of the object as the feature being dimensioned, unless it would reduce the clarity of the drawing.
  - Dimensions should be placed symmetrically about the centerline or axis of symmetry of the object, if applicable.
  - Dimensions should be placed with respect to the datum or reference plane of the object, if applicable.
  - Datum or reference plane is a plane or surface that is used as a basis for measuring or locating other features of the object.
  - Dimensions should be placed with respect to the functional or manufacturing requirements of the object, such as tolerances, fits, surface finish, etc., if applicable.
  - Tolerances are the allowable variations in the dimensions of the object, which are specified by adding upper and lower limits to the nominal dimension, or by using symbols or abbreviations.
  - Fits are the degree of tightness or looseness between two mating parts, such as a shaft and a hole, which are specified by using standard codes or symbols.
  - Surface finish is the quality or texture of the surface of the object, which is specified by using symbols or abbreviations.
