### Circle

A circle is a two-dimensional shape that consists of all the points that are equidistant from a fixed point called the center. The distance from the center to any point on the circle is called the radius. The longest chord of the circle that passes through the center is called the diameter. The diameter is twice the radius.

Some of the properties of circles are:

- The circumference of a circle is the perimeter or the boundary of the circle. It is equal to 2πr, where r is the radius and π is a constant approximately equal to 3.14.
- The area of a circle is the region enclosed by the circle. It is equal to πr^2, where r is the radius and π is a constant approximately to 3.14.
- A sector of a circle is a region bounded by two radii and an arc of the circle. The area of a sector is proportional to the central angle subtended by the arc. The area of a sector is given by A = (θ/360)πr^2, where θ is the central angle in degrees and r is the radius.
- A segment of a circle is a region bounded by a chord and an arc of the circle. The area of a segment is the difference between the area of the sector and the area of the triangle formed by the chord and the radii. The area of a segment is given by A = (θ/360)πr^2 - (1/2)r^2sinθ, where θ is the central angle in degrees and r is the radius.
- A tangent to a circle is a line that touches the circle at exactly one point. The point of contact is called the point of tangency. The tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.
- A chord of a circle is a line segment that joins two points on the circle. The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the center of the circle. Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the center. The longest chord of a circle is the diameter.
- An arc of a circle is a portion of the circumference of the circle. The measure of an arc is equal to the central angle subtended by the arc. The length of an arc is proportional to the measure of the arc. The length of an arc is given by L = (θ/360)2πr, where θ is the measure of the arc in degrees and r is the radius.
- A central angle of a circle is an angle whose vertex is the center of the circle and whose sides are radii. The measure of a central angle is equal to the measure of the arc subtended by the angle.
- An inscribed angle of a circle is an angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords of the circle. The measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of the arc subtended by the angle. An inscribed angle that subtends a semicircle is a right angle.
- A circumscribed angle of a circle is an angle whose sides are tangents to the circle. The measure of a circumscribed angle is half the difference of the measures of the arcs intercepted by the angle and its vertical angle. A circumscribed angle that intercepts a semicircle is a right angle.
