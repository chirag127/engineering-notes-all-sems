## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method of creating a three-dimensional representation of an object on a two-dimensional plane, such as a paper or a screen.
- Isometric projection preserves the relative proportions and angles of the object's edges, faces, and features, but not the actual sizes or distances.
- Isometric projection is commonly used in engineering, architecture, and video games to show the overall shape and appearance of an object without distortion or perspective.
- Isometric projection can be created by following these steps:
  - Choose an isometric axis, which is a diagonal line that represents the direction of viewing the object.
  - Draw a box around the object that is parallel to the isometric axis and the three coordinate axes (x, y, and z).
  - Project the vertices of the object onto the faces of the box by drawing lines perpendicular to the isometric axis.
  - Remove the box and any hidden lines or faces of the object that are not visible from the isometric axis.
  - Add any details or features of the object that are visible from the isometric axis.
- Isometric projection has some advantages and disadvantages compared to other types of projection, such as orthographic, oblique, or perspective projection.
  - Advantages:
    - Isometric projection is easy to draw and measure using a 30-degree angle and a grid.
    - Isometric projection shows the object's shape and appearance clearly and consistently from any direction.
    - Isometric projection can be easily converted to other types of projection by changing the angle or the scale of the grid.
  - Disadvantages:
    - Isometric projection does not show the true sizes or distances of the object's features, which may cause confusion or errors in some applications.
    - Isometric projection does not show the depth or perspective of the object, which may reduce the realism or the aesthetic appeal of the representation.
