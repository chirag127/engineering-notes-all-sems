### Sections of Solids

- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane, called the section plane or cutting plane.
- Sections of solids are used to show the hidden or internal parts of an object in technical drawings.
- Sections of solids can also help to find the true shape of the cut surface, the volume and the centroid of the solid.
- Some common types of solids are prisms, pyramids, cylinders and cones. These solids can have different positions and orientations with respect to the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP).
- The section plane can be perpendicular or inclined to one or both of the reference planes (HP and VP).
- The true shape of the section is the shape that is seen when the cut surface is parallel to the plane of projection.
- To draw the sectional views of solids, the following steps are generally followed:

  - Draw the projections of the solid in the given position and orientation.
  - Draw the section plane in the front view and the top view, and mark the points where it intersects the edges of the solid.
  - Project the sectional points from the front view to the top view, or vice versa, depending on the given view.
  - Draw the outline of the cut surface in the given view, and shade it with diagonal lines.
  - Draw the true shape of the section by rotating the cut surface until it is parallel to the plane of projection.
  - Mark the dimensions and the symbols of the section plane and the true shape of the section.
