### Principles of Engineering Graphics and their significance

- Engineering graphics is the graphical representation of engineering objects and concepts using lines, symbols, dimensions, and views.
- Engineering graphics is essential for communicating engineering ideas and designs in a clear, concise, and accurate manner.
- Engineering graphics follows certain principles and standards to ensure consistency, clarity, and readability of the drawings.
- Some of the principles of engineering graphics are:

  - Projection: The process of representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional plane by projecting its points along parallel or converging lines.
  - View: The representation of an object as seen from a particular direction or position.
  - Dimensioning: The process of specifying the size, shape, location, and orientation of the features of an object using numbers, symbols, and lines.
  - Tolerancing: The process of specifying the allowable variation in the dimensions and geometry of an object to ensure its proper function and fit.
  - Sectioning: The process of cutting an object along a plane and showing the internal features of the object in the view.
  - Hatching: The process of filling the sectioned areas of an object with parallel lines to indicate the material or surface texture of the object.
  - Symbol: A conventional sign or notation used to represent a specific feature, condition, or instruction on a drawing.
  - Centerline: A thin line used to mark the centers of circles and the axes of symmetrical parts, such as cylinders and bolts.
  - Break line: A line used to show where an object is broken to save drawing space or reveal interior features. Break lines come in two forms: a freehand thick line, and a long, ruled thin line with zigzags.

- Engineering graphics is significant for the following reasons:

  - It helps engineers to visualize, analyze, and design engineering objects and systems.
  - It facilitates the communication and collaboration among engineers, technicians, manufacturers, and customers.
  - It provides a precise and unambiguous description of the specifications and requirements of engineering objects and systems.
  - It serves as a legal document and a record of engineering work and innovation.
