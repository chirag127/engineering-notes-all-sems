### Dim style for the notes of the Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Computer Aided Design (CAD) is a manufacturing process that allows us to digitally create 2D drawings or 3D models of future products.
- CAD helps designers and engineers visualize a product’s construction, before fabricating it.
- CAD also enables the analysis and optimization of the product’s performance, functionality, and quality.
- CAD software can be used for various applications, such as mechanical engineering, architecture, civil engineering, electrical engineering, etc.
- CAD software can be classified into two categories: 2D CAD and 3D CAD.
- 2D CAD software allows the creation of flat drawings of objects, such as floor plans, schematics, diagrams, etc.
- 3D CAD software allows the creation of realistic and detailed models of objects, such as parts, assemblies, structures, etc.
- 3D CAD software can also support solid modeling, which is the representation of an object as a collection of solid geometric primitives, such as cylinders, spheres, cubes, etc.
- Solid modeling can be used for conceptual design, detailed design, and engineering analysis of products.
- Some examples of 3D CAD software are Creo Parametric, SolidWorks, AutoCAD, Inventor, etc .
- CAD software can be integrated with other tools, such as computer-aided engineering (CAE), computer-aided manufacturing (CAM), and product lifecycle management (PLM), to form a complete product development system.
- CAD software can improve the efficiency, accuracy, and quality of the design process, as well as reduce the cost and time required for product development.
