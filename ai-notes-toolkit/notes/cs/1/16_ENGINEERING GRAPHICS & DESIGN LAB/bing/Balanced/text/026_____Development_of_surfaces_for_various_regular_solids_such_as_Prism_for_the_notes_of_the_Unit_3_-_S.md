### Development of surfaces for various regular solids such as Prism

- Development of surfaces is a technique of representing the shape of a three-dimensional object on a plane surface by unfolding or unrolling its surfaces.
- Development of surfaces can be used for designing and making patterns of sheet metal, paper, cardboard, etc. that can be cut and folded to form the desired solid object.
- Development of surfaces can be classified into four methods: parallel-line development, radial-line development, triangulation development, and approximate development.
- Parallel-line development is used for developing prisms and cylinders, in which all the edges or generators of the lateral surfaces are parallel to each other.
- Radial-line development is used for developing pyramids and cones, in which the edges or generators of the lateral surfaces converge to a point (the vertex or the axis).
- Triangulation development is used for developing solids with curved surfaces, such as spheres, torus, etc. by dividing the surface into small triangular facets and developing each facet separately.
- Approximate development is used for developing solids with complex or irregular surfaces, such as helix, paraboloid, etc. by using approximate methods such as tangent plane method, zone method, etc.

- To develop the surface of a prism, the following steps are followed:

  - Draw the front view and the top view of the prism in the first and third quadrants, respectively.
  - Draw the true shape and size of the base of the prism by projecting it on a plane parallel to the base (the auxiliary plane).
  - Draw the development of the base by transferring the dimensions of the true shape along a horizontal line (the reference line).
  - Draw the development of the lateral surface by projecting the lengths of the edges of the prism on the reference line and drawing perpendiculars to the reference line equal to the width of the prism.
  - Join the ends of the perpendiculars to form the development of the lateral surface.
  - Cut along the outline of the development and fold along the reference line to form the prism.
