### Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design

- Computer-aided design (CAD) is a manufacturing process that allows us to digitally create 2D drawings or 3D models of future products.
- CAD helps designers and engineers visualize a product’s construction, before fabricating it.
- CAD also enables the analysis and optimization of the product’s performance, functionality, and quality.
- CAD software can be classified into two categories: 2D CAD and 3D CAD.
  - 2D CAD software allows the creation of flat or planar drawings, such as floor plans, schematics, or blueprints.
  - 3D CAD software allows the creation of solid or surface models, which can be viewed and manipulated from any angle.
- Some examples of CAD software are AutoCAD, SolidWorks, Creo Parametric, CATIA, and SketchUp.
- Engineering graphics is the discipline of creating and interpreting graphical representations of physical objects, systems, or processes.
- Engineering graphics uses CAD software to create and manipulate geometric shapes, dimensions, annotations, and views.
- Engineering graphics also uses CAD software to perform engineering analysis, such as stress, strain, heat transfer, fluid flow, or kinematics.
- Engineering graphics is a foundation to engineering design and analysis, as it helps engineers communicate their ideas, specifications, and solutions.
- Engineering graphics also helps engineers develop their spatial reasoning, geometric modeling, and vector skills.
- Some topics covered in engineering graphics are orthographic projection, isometric projection, section views, auxiliary views, dimensioning, tolerancing, and assembly drawings .
