### Conversion of pictorial view into orthographic projection

- A pictorial view is a 3-D view of an object that shows how it looks in reality. There are three types of pictorial views: perspective, isometric and oblique.
- An orthographic projection is a collection of 2-D views of different sides of an object, such as the front, top, side, etc. that show the shape and dimensions of the object .
- To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, the following steps can be followed:
  - Identify the principal view of the object, which is the view that shows the most features and details of the object. Usually, this is the front view, but it can vary depending on the object.
  - Draw the principal view on a plane of projection, which is a flat surface that is perpendicular to the line of sight. Use a scale and a ruler to draw the outline and dimensions of the object accurately.
  - Draw the other views on adjacent planes of projection, which are parallel to the principal plane of projection. Use projection lines, which are perpendicular to the planes of projection, to transfer the dimensions and features from the principal view to the other views. The number and position of the other views depend on the projection method, which can be either first-angle or third-angle projection .
  - Label the views and add any necessary dimensions, symbols, notes and title block to complete the drawing. Follow the standards and conventions of engineering drawing to ensure clarity and consistency.
