### Isometric projections of simple and combination of solids

- Isometric projection is a method of graphic representation of three-dimensional objects, used by engineers, technical illustrators, and architects.
- Isometric projection shows the object as it appears to the eye from a particular direction, with all the dimensions preserved at the same scale.
- Isometric projection is obtained by rotating the object 45 degrees about a vertical axis, then tilting it forward until the body diagonal appears as a point in the front view.
- Isometric projection can be used to draw simple geometrical solids, such as prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and cones, as well as combinations of any two or more of these solids.
- Isometric projection can also be used to show the truncation of a solid by a cutting plane inclined to the horizontal plane (HP).
- To draw an isometric projection of a solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the isometric axes, which are three mutually perpendicular lines that meet at a point called the origin. The axes are usually labeled as X, Y, and Z.
  - Draw the isometric scale, which is a line divided into equal parts that represent the true lengths of the object along the axes. The isometric scale is usually drawn at an angle of 30 degrees to the horizontal.
  - Locate the vertices of the solid on the isometric axes, using the isometric scale and the true dimensions of the object.
  - Join the vertices to form the edges and faces of the solid, using straight lines or curves as required.
  - Shade or color the faces of the solid to show the depth and orientation of the object.
- To draw an isometric projection of a combination of solids, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the isometric projection of each solid separately, using the same origin and isometric scale.
  - Align the solids according to the given position and orientation, using the isometric axes as guides.
  - Erase the hidden edges and faces of the solids, using dotted lines or dashes to indicate them if necessary.
  - Shade or color the visible faces of the solids to show the depth and orientation of the object.
