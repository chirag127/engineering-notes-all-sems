# Cone for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A cone is a three-dimensional solid that has a circular base and a curved surface that tapers to a vertex.
- A cone can be cut by a plane in different ways to produce different conic sections, such as circle, ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola.
- A conic section is a curve formed by the intersection of a plane and a cone.
- The shape of the conic section depends on the angle and position of the cutting plane relative to the cone.
- The eccentricity of a conic section is a measure of how much the curve deviates from being circular. It is defined as the ratio of the distance from any point on the curve to the focus and the distance from the same point to the directrix.
- The focus is a fixed point inside the cone, and the directrix is a fixed line outside the cone, such that the ratio of the distances is constant for any point on the curve.
- The eccentricity of a circle is zero, the eccentricity of an ellipse is between zero and one, the eccentricity of a parabola is one, and the eccentricity of a hyperbola is greater than one.
- The development of a surface is the process of unfolding or flattening a curved surface into a plane.
- The development of a surface is useful for designing and manufacturing objects that have curved shapes, such as cans, cones, pipes, etc.
- The development of a surface can be obtained by drawing the true shape and size of each face of the solid on a plane, and then cutting and joining them along the edges.
- The development of a cone is a sector of a circle, whose radius is equal to the slant height of the cone, and whose angle is equal to the base circumference of the cone divided by the slant height of the cone.
- The development of a sectioned cone is a part of the sector of a circle, whose radius is equal to the slant height of the cone, and whose angle is equal to the arc length of the sectioned base divided by the slant height of the cone.
- The development of a sectioned cone can be obtained by drawing the true shape of the section on the sector of the circle, and then cutting along the boundary of the section.
