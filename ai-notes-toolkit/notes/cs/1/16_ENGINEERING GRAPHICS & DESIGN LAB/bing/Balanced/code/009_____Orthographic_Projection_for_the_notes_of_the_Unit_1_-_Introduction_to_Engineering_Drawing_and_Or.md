### Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is commonly used in engineering graphics to communicate the shape and dimensions of an object. Orthographic projection has the following characteristics:

- The object is viewed along parallel lines that are perpendicular to the plane of the drawing.
- The object is projected onto three principal planes: the horizontal plane, the frontal plane, and the profile plane.
- The three principal planes are mutually perpendicular and intersect at a point called the station point.
- The three views obtained from the three principal planes are called the top view, the front view, and the side view, respectively.
- The three views are aligned according to the first-angle projection or the third-angle projection, depending on the convention used.
- The three views show the true shape and size of the object, except for the foreshortened features that are parallel to the plane of projection.
- The hidden edges and surfaces of the object are indicated by dashed lines, called hidden lines.
- The center lines, dimension lines, extension lines, and leader lines are used to annotate the views with the necessary information.
