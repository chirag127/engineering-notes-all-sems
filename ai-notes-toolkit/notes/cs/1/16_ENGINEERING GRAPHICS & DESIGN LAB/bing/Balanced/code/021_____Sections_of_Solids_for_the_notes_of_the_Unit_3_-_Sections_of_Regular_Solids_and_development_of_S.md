### Sections of Solids

- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane, called the section plane or cutting plane.
- Sectional views are used to show the hidden or internal features of a solid object in a technical drawing.
- To draw the sectional view of a solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the projections of the solid in the given position and orientation.
  - Draw the section plane in one of the views, usually the front view, and mark the points where it intersects the edges of the solid.
  - Project these points to the other view, usually the top view, and join them to get the outline of the section.
  - Shade the sectioned area with cross-hatching lines to indicate the cut surface.
  - Draw the true shape of the section by projecting the points on the section outline to a plane parallel to the section plane.
- The true shape of the section is the actual shape of the cut surface as seen from a direction perpendicular to the section plane.
- The section plane can be parallel, perpendicular, or inclined to one or both of the reference planes (HP and VP).
- The section plane can also be offset, meaning that it does not pass through the center or axis of the solid, but through some other points.
- The section plane can also be curved, meaning that it is not a straight line, but a curve or a combination of curves.
- The section plane can also be partial, meaning that it does not cut the entire solid, but only a part of it.
- The section plane can also be broken, meaning that it consists of two or more planes that are not continuous, but have gaps or breaks between them.
- The section plane can also be revolved, meaning that it is rotated about an axis to get different views of the section.
- The section plane can also be removed, meaning that it is not shown in the drawing, but only the section outline and the true shape of the section are shown.
- The most common solids that are sectioned in engineering graphics are prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and cones.
