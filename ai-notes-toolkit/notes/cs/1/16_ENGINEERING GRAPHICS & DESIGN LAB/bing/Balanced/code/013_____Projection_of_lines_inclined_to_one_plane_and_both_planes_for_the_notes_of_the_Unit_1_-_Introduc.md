### Projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes

- A line is a geometrical figure that has only one dimension, namely length, and no thickness.
- A line can be represented by two points, one at each end, called the extremities of the line.
- A line can be parallel, perpendicular, or inclined to a plane, depending on the angle it makes with the plane.
- A plane is a flat surface that extends infinitely in all directions and has two dimensions, namely length and breadth, and no thickness.
- A plane can be represented by three points that are not collinear, or by a line and a point that is not on the line.
- A plane can be horizontal, vertical, or oblique, depending on its orientation with respect to the ground level.
- In engineering drawing, two planes are commonly used to represent the three-dimensional objects, namely the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP).
- The HP is parallel to the ground level and the VP is perpendicular to the ground level.
- The line of intersection of the HP and the VP is called the reference line (RL) or the ground line (GL).
- The projection of a line on a plane is the locus of the points where the perpendiculars drawn from the line to the plane meet the plane.
- The projection of a line on the HP is called the top view (TV) or the plan, and the projection of a line on the VP is called the front view (FV) or the elevation.
- The projection of a line can be classified into four types, depending on its inclination to the HP and the VP, as follows:

  - Type 1: The line is parallel to both the HP and the VP. In this case, the TV and the FV are equal in length and parallel to the RL.
  - Type 2: The line is inclined to one plane and parallel to the other. In this case, the TV or the FV is equal in length to the line and parallel to the RL, and the other view is shorter and perpendicular to the RL.
  - Type 3: The line is perpendicular to one plane and inclined to the other. In this case, the TV or the FV is a point, and the other view is equal in length to the line and inclined to the RL.
  - Type 4: The line is inclined to both the HP and the VP. In this case, the TV and the FV are both shorter and inclined to the RL.

  - Step 2: Draw the perpendiculars from A and B to the HP and the VP and mark the points where they meet the planes as a and b for the TV and a' and b' for the FV.

- The traces of a line are the points where the line meets the HP and the VP. They are denoted by HT and VT respectively.
- The traces of a line can be used to find the true length and the true inclination of the line, as well as to locate the line in space.
- The traces of a line can be found by the following methods:

  - Method 1: Rotate the line about its HT until it is parallel to the VP. In this position, the FV of the line will be equal to its true length and the VT will coincide with the FV.
  - Method 2: Rotate the line about its VT until it is parallel to the HP. In this position, the TV of the line will be equal to its true length and the HT will coincide with the TV.
