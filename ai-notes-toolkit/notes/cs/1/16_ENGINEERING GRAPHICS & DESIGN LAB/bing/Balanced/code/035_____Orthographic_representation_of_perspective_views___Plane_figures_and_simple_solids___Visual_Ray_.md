### Orthographic representation of perspective views

- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by projecting its points onto a plane or a set of planes.
- Orthographic projection is also called parallel projection because the projection lines are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the projection plane.
- Orthographic projection can be used to show the shape and size of an object accurately and without distortion.
- Orthographic projection is commonly used in engineering and technical drawings to show the details and dimensions of an object from different views.
- The most common types of orthographic views are the front, top, and side views, which are aligned along the principal axes of the object.
- The front view shows the object as seen from the front, the top view shows the object as seen from above, and the side view shows the object as seen from the right or left.
- The orthographic views are arranged in a standard layout called the first-angle projection or the third-angle projection, depending on the convention used in different regions of the world.
- The first-angle projection places the object in the first quadrant, between the projection planes, and the third-angle projection places the object in the third quadrant, behind the projection planes.
- The orthographic views can be used to construct a perspective view of the object, which shows the object as seen from a point of view that is not perpendicular to any of the projection planes.
- A perspective view gives a more realistic impression of the object, but it also introduces some distortion and foreshortening of the object's dimensions.
- A perspective view can be obtained by using the visual ray method, which involves drawing imaginary rays from the eye of the observer to the corners and edges of the object, and then projecting them onto a picture plane that is parallel to the front view.
- The intersection points of the rays and the picture plane form the vertices of the perspective view, which can then be connected by straight lines to form the edges and faces of the object.
- The visual ray method can be applied to plane figures and simple solids, such as rectangles, triangles, circles, cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, and spheres.
- The visual ray method can also be used to find the true shape and size of a plane figure or a curved surface that is inclined to the projection planes, by projecting it onto a plane that is perpendicular to the line of sight of the observer.
