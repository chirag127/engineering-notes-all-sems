### Chamfer

- A chamfer is an angle on the edge of a workpiece.
- It is often created at a 45° angle between two adjoining right-angled faces.
- It is a form of bevel, but not all bevels are chamfers.
- It is used for mainly for protecting the chamfered object as well as anyone who might come in contact with the object.
- It can also improve the appearance of the object, facilitate assembly, or allow for a tighter joint.
- Chamfers can be applied to the outside of the part, where a hole breaks through a surface, or where any two surfaces meet.
- Chamfers can be classified into two types: linear and circular.
- Linear chamfers are straight and uniform, while circular chamfers are curved and variable.
- Chamfers can be measured by the length of the edge or the angle of the chamfer.
- Chamfers can be created by various methods, such as machining, grinding, filing, or molding.
