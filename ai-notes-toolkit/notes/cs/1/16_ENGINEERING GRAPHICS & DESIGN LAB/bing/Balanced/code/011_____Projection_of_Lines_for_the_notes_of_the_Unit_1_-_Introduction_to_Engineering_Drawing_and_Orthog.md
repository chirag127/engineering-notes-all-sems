### Projection of Lines

- A line is a basic element of engineering drawing that represents the edge or axis of a solid object or the path of a moving point.
- A line can be defined by two points in space, or by its direction and true length.
- A projection of a line is the image of the line on a plane of projection, such as the horizontal plane (HP) or the vertical plane (VP).
- A projection of a line can be classified into three types: parallel, perpendicular, and inclined.
- A parallel line is a line that is parallel to one of the principal planes of projection, such as the HP or the VP. Its projection on that plane is equal to its true length, and its projection on the other plane is a point.
- A perpendicular line is a line that is perpendicular to one of the principal planes of projection, such as the HP or the VP. Its projection on that plane is a point, and its projection on the other plane is equal to its true length.
- An inclined line is a line that is neither parallel nor perpendicular to any of the principal planes of projection, such as the HP or the VP. Its projections on both planes are shorter than its true length, and form an angle with the reference lines.
- To draw the projections of a line, the following steps are followed:
  - Locate the end points of the line on the given planes of projection, using the given dimensions or coordinates.
  - Draw the projections of the end points on the respective planes, using the reference lines and the scale.
  - Join the projections of the end points on each plane with a straight line, using a ruler.
  - Label the projections of the line and the end points with suitable notations, such as AB for the line, A and B for the end points, A' and B' for the projections on the HP, and A'' and B'' for the projections on the VP.
  - Indicate the true length of the line and the inclination angles with the planes, if required, using a protractor and a compass.
