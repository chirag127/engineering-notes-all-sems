### Isometric scales for the notes of the Unit 4 - Isometric Projection in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees.
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the general shape and dimensions of an object, but it does not preserve the true lengths or angles of the object.
- Isometric scale is a tool for constructing isometric projections of objects by using a reduced scale along the isometric axes.
- The isometric scale is constructed as follows:

  - Draw a horizontal line OA.
  - Through O draw OP and OQ at 30º and 45º to the horizontal respectively.
  - On OQ mark the actual scale in mm.
  - Draw verticals from each of the division points on the actual scale to cut OP at the corresponding divisions.
  - The points on OP form the isometric scale.

- The isometric scale is used to measure the lengths of the isometric lines in the isometric projection of an object.
- The isometric scale is smaller than the actual scale by a factor of 0.816 (the cosine of 30º).
- To draw an isometric projection of an object, the following steps are followed:

  - Draw the isometric axes (X, Y, and Z) using the isometric scale.
  - Draw the isometric outline of the object by projecting the corners and edges of the object along the isometric axes.
  - Draw the isometric details of the object by projecting the features and curves of the object along the isometric axes.
  - Erase the unwanted lines and darken the visible lines.

- Here is an example of an isometric projection of a cube with 50 mm edges:

```
    /\
   /  \
  /    \
 /      \
/________\  Z
\        /\
 \      /  \
  \    /    \
   \  /      \
    \/________\ Y
    X
```

- The isometric projection of the cube has the following dimensions:

  - X = 50 mm x 0.816 = 40.8 mm
  - Y = 50 mm x 0.816 = 40.8 mm
  - Z = 50 mm x 0.816 = 40.8 mm

- The angles between the isometric axes are 120 degrees.
