### Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism

- A **right regular solid** is a three-dimensional shape that has congruent and regular polygonal faces, and whose edges are perpendicular to the faces. Examples of right regular solids are cubes, tetrahedrons, octahedrons, etc. 
- A **prism** is a special type of right regular solid that has two identical and parallel polygonal faces, called bases, and rectangular faces, called lateral faces, that connect the bases. The shape of the base determines the name of the prism, such as triangular prism, square prism, hexagonal prism, etc. 
- An **auxiliary view** is a projection of an object onto a plane that is not parallel to any of the principal planes (front, top, or side). Auxiliary views are used to show the true shape and size of a slanted or inclined surface or feature of an object. 
- To draw an auxiliary view of a prism, the following steps are followed  :
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the prism in orthographic projection, showing the hidden edges as dashed lines.
  - Identify the slanted or inclined surface or feature that needs to be shown in true shape and size. Draw a reference line perpendicular to the slant on the front view and the top view.
  - Draw an auxiliary plane parallel to the slanted surface or feature, and project the vertices of the slant onto the plane from the front view and the top view. Label the points on the auxiliary plane.
  - Draw the true shape of the slanted surface or feature by connecting the points on the auxiliary plane. This is the auxiliary view of the prism.
  - If the prism is sectioned by a cutting plane, the true shape of the section can also be shown on the auxiliary plane by projecting the intersection points of the cutting plane and the prism from the front view and the top view. The sectioned area can be shaded or hatched to indicate the cut surface.
