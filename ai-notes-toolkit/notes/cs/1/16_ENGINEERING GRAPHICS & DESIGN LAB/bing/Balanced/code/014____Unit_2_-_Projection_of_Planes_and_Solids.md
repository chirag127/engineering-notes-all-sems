# Unit 2 - Projection of Planes and Solids

- A plane is a two-dimensional object having length and breadth only. Its thickness is always neglected. Various shapes of plane figures are considered such as square, rectangle, circle, pentagon, hexagon, etc.
- A solid is a three-dimensional object having length, breadth and thickness. It is completely bounded by a surface or surfaces, which may be curved or plane. The shape of a solid is described orthographically by drawing its two orthographic projections, usually, on the two principal planes of projection i.e., HP and VP.
- Projection of planes and solids is the process of representing their true shape and size on a plane of projection, such as a sheet of paper, by using a method of drawing called orthographic projection.
- Orthographic projection is a method of drawing in which the object is viewed at right angles to the plane of projection, and the lines of sight are perpendicular to the plane of projection. The object is projected onto the plane of projection by connecting the points where the lines of sight pierce the projection plane.
- There are two types of orthographic projection: first angle projection and third angle projection. In first angle projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant, between the HP and the VP. In third angle projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant, below the HP and behind the VP. The choice of projection depends on the convention followed in different countries or regions.
- The projection of planes and solids can be classified into different cases based on the position and orientation of the object with respect to the planes of projection. The cases are as follows:
  - Simple position: The axis of the solid is perpendicular to one of the planes of projection and parallel to the other.
  - Inclined position: The axis of the solid is inclined to one of the planes of projection and parallel to the other.
  - Oblique position: The axis of the solid is inclined to both the planes of projection.
- The projection of planes and solids can also be classified into different types based on the shape of the object. The types are as follows:
  - Prisms: A prism is a solid whose bases are identical, parallel and polygonal, and whose lateral faces are parallelograms. The projection of a prism depends on the shape of its base and the position of its axis.
  - Pyramids: A pyramid is a solid whose base is a polygon and whose lateral faces are triangles meeting at a common vertex called the apex. The projection of a pyramid depends on the shape of its base and the position of its axis.
  - Cylinders: A cylinder is a solid whose bases are identical, parallel and circular, and whose lateral surface is a curved surface. The projection of a cylinder depends on the position of its axis.
  - Cones: A cone is a solid whose base is a circle and whose lateral surface is a curved surface meeting at a common vertex called the apex. The projection of a cone depends on the position of its axis.
  - Spheres: A sphere is a solid whose surface is a curved surface equidistant from a fixed point called the center. The projection of a sphere is always a circle, regardless of its position.
- Section of solids is the process of cutting a solid by a cutting plane and showing the shape of the cut surface on the plane of projection. The cut surface is called the section plane and the shape of the cut surface is called the sectional view. The purpose of sectioning is to reveal the internal details of the solid that are hidden in the normal projection. The section plane can be parallel, perpendicular or inclined to the axis of the solid. The section plane can also be passed through the center or offset from the center of the solid. The section plane can be represented by a line called the cutting plane line, which shows the direction and position of the section plane. The sectional view can be represented by hatching, which is a series of thin parallel lines drawn on the cut surface to indicate the material of the solid.
