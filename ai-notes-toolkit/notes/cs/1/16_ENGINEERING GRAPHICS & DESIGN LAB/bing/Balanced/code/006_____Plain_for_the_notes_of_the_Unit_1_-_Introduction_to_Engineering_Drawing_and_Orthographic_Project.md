### Notes for Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used to communicate design ideas, dimensions, shapes, and methods for the creation of an object or a system .
- Engineering drawing follows certain standards and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency of the drawings.
- Orthographic projection is a technique of representing a three-dimensional object by multiple two-dimensional views, each showing only two of the three dimensions (width, height, depth) .
- Orthographic projection is based on the principle that parallel lines from the object are perpendicular to the plane of projection.
- There are two types of orthographic projection: first angle projection and third angle projection.
- In first angle projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant, between the observer and the plane of projection. The front view is projected on the plane of projection, and then the plane is unfolded to the right to obtain the right side view, and to the bottom to obtain the top view.
- In third angle projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant, behind the plane of projection. The front view is projected on the plane of projection, and then the plane is unfolded to the left to obtain the left side view, and to the top to obtain the top view.

- The choice of orthographic projection depends on the convention followed in different regions of the world. Generally, first angle projection is used in Europe, Asia, and Australia, while third angle projection is used in North America.
- Orthographic projection is useful for showing the shape, size, and features of an object in a clear and unambiguous way. It is also the basis for other types of projections, such as isometric, oblique, and perspective projections.
