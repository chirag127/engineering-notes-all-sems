### Cylinder

A cylinder is a solid with two circular bases of equal size and shape that are parallel to each other and connected by a curved surface. The axis of the cylinder is the line joining the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases.

#### Sections of Cylinder

A section of a cylinder is the shape obtained when the cylinder is cut by a plane. The shape of the section depends on the orientation and position of the cutting plane. There are three types of sections of a cylinder:

- Parallel section: A section obtained when the cutting plane is parallel to the axis of the cylinder. The shape of the parallel section is a circle equal to the base of the cylinder.
- Normal section: A section obtained when the cutting plane is perpendicular to the axis of the cylinder. The shape of the normal section is a rectangle whose length is equal to the circumference of the base and whose width is equal to the height of the cylinder.
- Inclined section: A section obtained when the cutting plane is neither parallel nor perpendicular to the axis of the cylinder. The shape of the inclined section is an ellipse whose major axis is equal to the diameter of the base and whose minor axis is equal to the height of the cylinder multiplied by the cosine of the angle of inclination of the cutting plane to the horizontal plane.

#### Development of Cylinder

The development of a cylinder is the process of unfolding the curved surface of the cylinder into a flat plane. The development of a cylinder is useful for making patterns for cutting and bending sheet metal or paper. The development of a cylinder can be obtained by following these steps:

- Draw a rectangle whose length is equal to the circumference of the base and whose width is equal to the height of the cylinder. This rectangle represents the development of the lateral surface of the cylinder.
- Divide the rectangle into a number of equal parts along its length. The number of parts depends on the accuracy required for the development. The more parts, the more accurate the development.
- Draw a circle whose diameter is equal to the base of the cylinder. This circle represents the development of the base of the cylinder.
- Divide the circle into the same number of equal parts as the rectangle. The points of division on the circle correspond to the points of division on the rectangle.
- Draw radial lines from the center of the circle to the points of division on the circle. These lines represent the generators of the cylinder.
- Cut out the rectangle and the circle along their boundaries. The rectangle and the circle are the developments of the lateral surface and the base of the cylinder, respectively.
- Join the corresponding points of division on the rectangle and the circle with straight lines. These lines represent the elements of the cylinder.
- Fold the rectangle along the elements and join the opposite edges of the rectangle. The rectangle forms the lateral surface of the cylinder.
- Fold the circle along the generators and attach it to the rectangle. The circle forms the base of the cylinder.
