## Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate design ideas, specifications, and dimensions of physical objects or systems.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into two types: artistic drawing and technical drawing. Artistic drawing is used to express aesthetic or creative ideas, while technical drawing is used to convey precise and accurate information about the shape, size, and features of an object or system.
- Engineering drawing can be further divided into two categories: pictorial drawing and orthographic drawing. Pictorial drawing is a three-dimensional representation of an object or system, while orthographic drawing is a two-dimensional representation of an object or system.
- Orthographic drawing is also known as orthographic projection, which is a method of projecting the features of a three-dimensional object onto two or more planes perpendicular to each other. The planes are called principal planes, and the views obtained on these planes are called principal views.
- The principal planes are usually the front, top, and right side planes, and the principal views are usually the front, top, and right side views. The front view shows the width and height of the object, the top view shows the width and depth of the object, and the right side view shows the height and depth of the object.
- Orthographic drawing follows a set of rules and conventions to ensure clarity and consistency in the representation of objects and systems. Some of these rules and conventions are:

  - The object or system should be placed in the first quadrant of the three-dimensional space, with the front view facing the observer.
  - The object or system should be aligned with the principal planes, and the principal views should be aligned with each other.
  - The object or system should be drawn in full scale, unless otherwise specified.
  - The object or system should be drawn with thin, continuous, and uniform lines, unless otherwise specified.
  - The object or system should be drawn with hidden lines, center lines, dimension lines, extension lines, leader lines, and cutting-plane lines as needed, following the standard symbols and formats.
  - The object or system should be dimensioned and labeled according to the standard units and abbreviations, following the standard rules and practices.
  - The object or system should be drawn with proper views, sections, and details as needed, following the standard methods and techniques.
