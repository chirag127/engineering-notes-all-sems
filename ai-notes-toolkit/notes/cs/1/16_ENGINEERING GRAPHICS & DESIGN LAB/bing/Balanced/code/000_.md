# Nuclear Fusion

- Nuclear fusion is the process of combining two or more atomic nuclei into a larger one, releasing energy in the process.
- Nuclear fusion is the source of energy for the Sun and other stars, where hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium nuclei under high temperature and pressure.
- Nuclear fusion can also be artificially induced in devices such as tokamaks, stellarators, inertial confinement fusion, and fusion rockets.
- Nuclear fusion has the potential to provide clean, abundant, and safe energy for humanity, but it also faces many technical, economic, and environmental challenges.

## Conditions for Nuclear Fusion

- For nuclear fusion to occur, the nuclei must overcome the electrostatic repulsion between their positive charges and get close enough to allow the strong nuclear force to bind them together.
- The minimum energy required to overcome the electrostatic repulsion is called the Coulomb barrier, which depends on the charge and mass of the nuclei.
- The Coulomb barrier can be lowered by increasing the temperature and pressure of the nuclei, which increases their kinetic energy and the probability of tunneling through the barrier.
- The temperature and pressure required for fusion depend on the type of nuclei involved, but typically range from millions to billions of degrees Celsius and from millions to billions of atmospheres.

## Types of Nuclear Fusion

- The most common type of nuclear fusion is the proton-proton chain, which occurs in the Sun and other low-mass stars. It involves four hydrogen nuclei (protons) fusing into one helium nucleus, releasing two positrons, two neutrinos, and gamma rays.
- The proton-proton chain has three steps:

  - Two protons fuse into a deuterium nucleus (one proton and one neutron), releasing a positron and a neutrino.
  - A deuterium nucleus and a proton fuse into a helium-3 nucleus (two protons and one neutron), releasing a gamma ray.
  - Two helium-3 nuclei fuse into a helium-4 nucleus (two protons and two neutrons), releasing two protons.

- The net reaction of the proton-proton chain is:

  - 4H -> He + 2e+ + 2v + 6γ

- The energy released by the proton-proton chain is about 26.7 MeV per helium-4 nucleus, or about 4.3 x 10^-12 J.

- Another type of nuclear fusion is the CNO cycle, which occurs in the cores of high-mass stars. It involves carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen nuclei acting as catalysts for the fusion of hydrogen nuclei into helium nuclei, releasing gamma rays and neutrinos.
- The CNO cycle has four steps:

  - A carbon-12 nucleus and a proton fuse into a nitrogen-13 nucleus, releasing a gamma ray.
  - A nitrogen-13 nucleus decays into a carbon-13 nucleus, releasing a positron and a neutrino.
  - A carbon-13 nucleus and a proton fuse into a nitrogen-14 nucleus, releasing a gamma ray.
  - A nitrogen-14 nucleus and a proton fuse into a oxygen-15 nucleus, releasing a gamma ray.
  - An oxygen-15 nucleus decays into a nitrogen-15 nucleus, releasing a positron and a neutrino.
  - A nitrogen-15 nucleus and a proton fuse into a carbon-12 nucleus and a helium-4 nucleus, releasing a gamma ray.

  - 4H -> He + 2e+ + 2v + 3γ
