### Classification of solids

- A solid is an object that has three dimensions, i.e., length, breadth and height.
- Solids can be classified into two types: **crystalline solids** and **amorphous solids**.
- Crystalline solids are solids that have a regular and orderly arrangement of atoms, ions or molecules in a three-dimensional space. They have well-defined shapes, sharp melting points and characteristic properties.
- Amorphous solids are solids that have a random and irregular arrangement of atoms, ions or molecules in a three-dimensional space. They have no definite shape, no sharp melting point and variable properties.
- Crystalline solids can be further classified into four types based on the nature of the bonding between the constituent particles: **ionic solids**, **metallic solids**, **covalent solids** and **molecular solids**.
- Ionic solids are solids that consist of cations and anions held together by strong electrostatic forces. They have high melting points, high hardness, poor electrical conductivity and brittle nature.
- Metallic solids are solids that consist of metal atoms held together by metallic bonds. They have variable melting points, high electrical and thermal conductivity, ductility, malleability and metallic lustre.
- Covalent solids are solids that consist of atoms held together by covalent bonds. They have very high melting points, high hardness, poor electrical conductivity and directional properties.
- Molecular solids are solids that consist of molecules held together by intermolecular forces. They have low melting points, low hardness, poor electrical conductivity and soft nature.
- In engineering graphics, solids are represented by their projections on two perpendicular planes, called the **horizontal plane (HP)** and the **vertical plane (VP)** .
- The projections of solids can be obtained by different methods, such as **change of position method**, **change of reference line method**, **auxiliary plane method** and **section plane method** .
- The projections of solids can be used to study their shapes, dimensions, orientations and relationships with other objects .
- The projections of solids can also be used to create **solid models**, which are mathematical and computer models of three-dimensional shapes that can be manipulated, analyzed and displayed.
