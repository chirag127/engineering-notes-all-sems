# Cone for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A cone is a solid that has a circular base and a single vertex that is not in the same plane as the base.
- A cone can be cut by a plane to produce different conic sections, such as circles, ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas.
- The conic section depends on the angle and position of the cutting plane relative to the axis and the base of the cone.
- The development of a cone is the process of unfolding the curved surface of the cone into a flat plane.
- The development of a cone can be done by cutting the cone along a generator (a line from the vertex to the base) and spreading the surface on a plane.
- The development of a cone is a sector of a circle with radius equal to the slant height of the cone and central angle equal to the angle subtended by the base of the cone at the vertex.
- The development of a sectioned cone is the process of unfolding the part of the cone that is cut by a plane into a flat plane.
- The development of a sectioned cone can be done by cutting the cone along two generators that pass through the points where the cutting plane meets the base of the cone and spreading the surface on a plane.
- The development of a sectioned cone is a segment of a circle with radius equal to the slant height of the cone and central angle equal to the angle subtended by the sectioned base of the cone at the vertex.
- The true shape of the sectioned cone can be obtained by drawing a perpendicular from the vertex to the cutting plane and projecting the points on the sectioned curve to the perpendicular line.
