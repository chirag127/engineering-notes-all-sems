### Cone

A cone is a solid generated by revolving a right-angled triangle about one of its sides, which is called the axis of the cone. The base of the cone is a circle whose center lies on the axis. The vertex of the cone is the point where the axis intersects the surface of the cone.

#### Sections of a cone

When a cone is cut by a plane, the intersection of the plane and the cone is called a conic section. There are four types of conic sections: circle, ellipse, parabola and hyperbola. The shape of the conic section depends on the angle and position of the cutting plane.

- If the cutting plane is perpendicular to the axis of the cone, the conic section is a circle. This is the simplest case of a section of a cone.
- If the cutting plane is inclined to the axis of the cone, but does not pass through the vertex, the conic section is an ellipse. This is the most common case of a section of a cone.
- If the cutting plane is parallel to a generator of the cone, the conic section is a parabola. This is a special case of a section of a cone, where the cutting plane touches the cone at one point only.
- If the cutting plane is inclined to the axis of the cone, and passes through the vertex, the conic section is a hyperbola. This is another special case of a section of a cone, where the cutting plane cuts the cone into two parts.

#### Development of a cone

The development of a cone is the process of unfolding the surface of the cone into a flat plane. The development of a cone is a sector of a circle, whose radius is equal to the slant height of the cone, and whose angle is equal to the angle subtended by the base of the cone at the vertex.

To draw the development of a cone, follow these steps:

- Draw the front view and the top view of the cone, showing the axis, the base and the vertex.
- Draw a line perpendicular to the axis, passing through the vertex. This line is called the reference line.
- Divide the base of the cone into a number of equal parts, and number them as 1, 2, 3, ..., n.
- Draw radial lines from the vertex to the points on the base, and measure the length of each line. This length is the slant height of the cone, and is the same for all the lines.
- Draw a circle with radius equal to the slant height of the cone, and mark the center as O.
- Draw a line from O to any point on the circle, and mark it as OA. This line is the reference line of the development.
- From O, draw an arc with radius equal to the radius of the base of the cone, and cut the circle at two points, B and C. The angle AOB is equal to the angle subtended by the base of the cone at the vertex, and is the angle of the sector of the development.
- Divide the arc BC into the same number of equal parts as the base of the cone, and number them as 1, 2, 3, ..., n.
- Join O to the points on the arc, and extend the lines to cut the circle at points 1', 2', 3', ..., n'.
- The sector A1'2'3'...n'C is the development of the cone.
