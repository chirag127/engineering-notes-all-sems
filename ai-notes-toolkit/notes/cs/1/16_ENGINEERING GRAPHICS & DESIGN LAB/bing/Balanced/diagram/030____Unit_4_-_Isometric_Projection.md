## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions.
- It is a type of axonometric projection, which means that the three principal axes are equally inclined to the plane of projection, and the scale along each axis is the same.
- Isometric projection preserves the proportions and angles of the object, but distorts the shape and size of the faces.
- To draw an isometric projection of an object, the following steps are followed:

  - Choose a suitable isometric scale and draw three mutually perpendicular lines, called isometric axes, representing the length, width and height of the object.
  - Project the vertices of the object along the isometric axes and mark the points on the scale.
  - Join the points to form the edges and faces of the object.
  - Shade or hatch the faces to show the depth and orientation of the object.

- An example of an isometric projection of a cube is shown below:

```
       /\
      /  \
     /____\
    /\    /\
   /  \  /  \
  /____\/____\
  \    /\    /
   \  /  \  /
    \/____\/
```

- Isometric projection is useful for engineering and architectural drawings, as it shows the overall shape and dimensions of the object without perspective distortion.
- However, it is not suitable for showing the true shape and size of the faces, as they appear distorted and foreshortened. For example, a square face appears as a rhombus in isometric projection.
- To show the true shape and size of the faces, other types of projection, such as orthographic projection or oblique projection, are used.
