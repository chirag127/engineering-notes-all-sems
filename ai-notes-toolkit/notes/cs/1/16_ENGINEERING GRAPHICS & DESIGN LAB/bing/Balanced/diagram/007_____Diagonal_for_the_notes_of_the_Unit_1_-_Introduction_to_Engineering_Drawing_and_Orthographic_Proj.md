### Diagonal

A diagonal is a straight line that connects two opposite corners of a polygon. In engineering drawing, diagonals are used for various purposes, such as:

- Drawing a diagonal scale to measure small fractions of the unit of measurement with higher accuracy . A diagonal scale represents a unit into three different multiples, such as meters, centimeters, and millimeters. To draw a diagonal scale, the following steps are involved:

  - Finding the length of the scale based on the given data and the required accuracy.
  - Dividing the length of the scale horizontally into equal parts, corresponding to the main divisions of the unit (such as meters).
  - Dividing the first part of the scale vertically into equal parts, corresponding to the sub-divisions of the unit (such as centimeters).
  - Dividing the last part of the scale vertically into equal parts, corresponding to the smallest divisions of the unit (such as millimeters).
  - Joining the points from the sub-divisions to the successive next sub-divisions on the last vertical line. These lines form the diagonals of the scale.
  - Marking the values of the unit and its multiples along the horizontal and vertical lines of the scale.

- Drawing section lines or cross-hatching to indicate the area that has been theoretically cut in a section view . A section view reveals the interior features of an object or a structure by cutting it along a plane. Section lines are thin and are usually drawn at a 45-degree angle to the major outline of the object. The spacing between lines should be uniform. Different types of section lines are used to represent different materials or components.

- Drawing center lines or axis lines to mark the centers of circles and the axes of symmetrical parts, such as cylinders and bolts. Center lines are thin and consist of alternating long and short dashes. They should extend beyond the outline of the part and terminate with arrowheads.

- Drawing break lines to show where an object is broken to save drawing space or reveal interior features. Break lines come in two forms: a freehand thick line, and a long, ruled thin line with zigzags. The former is used for irregular or curved breaks, and the latter is used for straight or cylindrical breaks.
