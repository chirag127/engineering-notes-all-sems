### Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design

- Computer Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer software to create, modify, analyze, and optimize a design.
- CAD can be used for various purposes, such as engineering, architecture, art, animation, and manufacturing.
- CAD software can create 2D drawings or 3D models of a design, and can also provide information such as dimensions, materials, tolerances, and assembly instructions.
- CAD software can also perform simulations, tests, and calculations on a design, such as stress analysis, fluid dynamics, and thermal analysis.
- CAD software can also generate output files that can be used for printing, machining, or fabrication of a design, such as STL, DXF, or G-code files.
- CAD software can improve the efficiency, accuracy, and quality of a design process, and can also facilitate communication, collaboration, and documentation of a design.
- Some examples of CAD software are AutoCAD, SolidWorks, Onshape, SketchUp, and Blender.
- Engineering Graphics is the discipline of creating and interpreting graphical representations of engineering designs, such as sketches, drawings, and diagrams.
- Engineering Graphics can use conventional methods, such as pencil and paper, or digital methods, such as CAD software.
- Engineering Graphics can follow certain standards and conventions, such as geometric construction, orthographic projection, isometric view, sectional view, detailed view, geometric dimensioning and tolerancing, and engineering drawing.
- Engineering Graphics can help engineers to visualize, communicate, and document their designs, and can also help them to analyze and solve engineering problems.
