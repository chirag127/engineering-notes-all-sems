### Projection of polygonal surface and circular lamina located in first quadrant inclined to one or both reference planes

- Projection of planes is the process of drawing the views of a plane surface on the reference planes, such as horizontal plane (HP) and vertical plane (VP).
- A plane surface can be a polygonal surface (such as a triangle, square, hexagon, etc.) or a circular lamina (a thin disc).
- A plane surface can be located in any of the four quadrants, but in this topic we will focus on the first quadrant, which is the space above HP and in front of VP.
- A plane surface can be inclined to one or both reference planes, which means that its surface makes an angle with the reference plane(s) other than 90 degrees.
- To draw the projection of a plane surface, we need to know the following information:
  - The size and shape of the plane surface
  - The position of the plane surface with respect to the reference planes (such as the distance of an edge or a point from HP and VP)
  - The inclination of the plane surface with respect to the reference planes (such as the angle of the surface or an edge with HP or VP)
- Depending on the information given, we can use different methods to draw the projection of a plane surface, such as:
  - Change of position method: This method involves rotating the plane surface about an axis parallel to one of the reference planes until it becomes perpendicular to the other reference plane. Then, we can draw the true shape of the plane surface on the reference plane and project it on the other reference plane. Finally, we can rotate the plane surface back to its original position and draw the final projections.
  - Auxiliary plane method: This method involves drawing an auxiliary plane (AP) perpendicular to one of the reference planes and passing through the plane surface. Then, we can draw the true shape of the plane surface on the AP and project it on the reference planes. Finally, we can remove the AP and draw the final projections.
