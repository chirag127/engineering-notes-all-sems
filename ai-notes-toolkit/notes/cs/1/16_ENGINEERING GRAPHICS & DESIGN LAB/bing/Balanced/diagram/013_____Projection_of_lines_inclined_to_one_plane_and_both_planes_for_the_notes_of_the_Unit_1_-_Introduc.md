### Projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes

- A line is a geometrical object that has length but no width or thickness.
- A line can be represented by two points, A and B, that are its extremities.
- A line can be projected on two reference planes, the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP), that are perpendicular to each other.
- The projections of a line on the HP and VP are called the top view and the front view, respectively.
- The projections of a line can be obtained by drawing perpendiculars from the points A and B to the HP and VP.
- The projections of a line can show the true length, inclination, and traces of the line, depending on its position in space relative to the HP and VP.
- A line can be parallel, perpendicular, or inclined to one or both of the reference planes.

#### Projection of a line inclined to one plane and parallel to the other

- A line is inclined to one plane if it makes an angle other than 0° or 90° with that plane.
- A line is parallel to a plane if it makes an angle of 0° with that plane.
- A line can be inclined to the HP and parallel to the VP, or vice versa.
- The projection of a line inclined to one plane and parallel to the other will show the true length of the line on the plane to which it is parallel, and the true inclination of the line on the plane to which it is inclined.
- The projection of a line inclined to one plane and parallel to the other will also show the traces of the line on the plane to which it is inclined.
- The traces of a line are the points where the line intersects the reference planes.
- The traces of a line can be found by extending the line until it meets the reference planes.
- The traces of a line can help to locate the position of the line in space.

##### Example

- Consider a line AB of 80 mm length, inclined at 45° to the HP and parallel to the VP.
- The point A is 20 mm above the HP and 30 mm in front of the VP.
- Draw the projections of the line AB.

###### Solution

- To draw the projections of the line AB, follow these steps:

  1. Draw the reference line XY that represents the intersection of the HP and VP.
  2. Draw the front view of the line AB by projecting the points A and B on the VP. Since the line is parallel to the VP, the front view will show the true length of the line, 80 mm. Mark the front view as a'b'.
  3. Draw the top view of the line AB by projecting the points A and B on the HP. Since the line is inclined to the HP, the top view will show the apparent length of the line, which is less than the true length. The apparent length can be calculated using trigonometry: 80 mm x cos 45° = 56.57 mm. Mark the top view as ab.
  4. Draw the inclination of the line AB on the HP by drawing an arc of radius 80 mm from the point a and cutting the reference line XY at the point t. The angle at t is the true inclination of the line, 45°. Mark the angle as θ.
  5. Draw the trace of the line AB on the HP by joining the point b to the point t. The trace is the point where the line intersects the HP. Mark the trace as ht.
  6. Draw the dimensions and labels of the projections.
