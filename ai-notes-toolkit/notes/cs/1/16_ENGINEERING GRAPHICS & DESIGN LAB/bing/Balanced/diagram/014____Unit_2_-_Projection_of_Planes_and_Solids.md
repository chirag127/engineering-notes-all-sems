## Unit 2 - Projection of Planes and Solids

- A plane is a flat, two-dimensional surface that extends infinitely in all directions. A solid is a three-dimensional object that has length, width and height.
- A projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional plane, such as a sheet of paper or a computer screen.
- There are different types of projections, such as orthographic, isometric, oblique and perspective, depending on the angle and position of the plane relative to the object.
- Orthographic projection is a type of projection where the plane is perpendicular to the object, and the object is viewed from one or more directions, such as front, top and side. Orthographic projection preserves the true shape and size of the object, but does not show depth or perspective.
- Isometric projection is a type of projection where the plane is inclined at an angle of 30 degrees to the horizontal and the object is viewed from a corner. Isometric projection shows the three dimensions of the object, but distorts the shape and size of the object.
- Oblique projection is a type of projection where the plane is parallel to the object, and the object is viewed from an angle other than 90 degrees. Oblique projection shows the true shape and size of the front face of the object, but distorts the other faces of the object.
- Perspective projection is a type of projection where the plane is perpendicular to the line of sight of the viewer, and the object is viewed from a finite distance. Perspective projection shows the realistic appearance of the object, but does not preserve the true shape and size of the object.

- To draw the projection of a plane or a solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Choose a suitable type of projection, depending on the purpose and the given information.
  - Draw the reference planes, such as the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP), and the axes, such as the x-axis, y-axis and z-axis.
  - Draw the object in its true shape and size, and mark the important points, such as the corners, the center and the dimensions.
  - Project the object on the reference planes, and draw the projection lines perpendicular to the planes.
  - Label the projection points, such as A', B', C' and D', and the projection lines, such as a', b', c' and d'.
  - Draw the projection views, such as the front view, the top view and the side view, and show the hidden lines, such as A'-B', B'-C' and C'-D', with dashed lines.
  - Indicate the inclination of the object, if any, with the angle of inclination, such as θ, and the direction of inclination, such as clockwise or anticlockwise.
  - Indicate the distance of the object from the reference planes, if any, with the distance, such as h, and the direction of distance, such as above or below.
  - Indicate the scale of the projection, if any, with the scale factor, such as 1:2, and the unit of measurement, such as cm.
  - Indicate the name of the object, the type of projection, and the projection views, such as Rectangle, Orthographic Projection, Front View, Top View and Side View.
