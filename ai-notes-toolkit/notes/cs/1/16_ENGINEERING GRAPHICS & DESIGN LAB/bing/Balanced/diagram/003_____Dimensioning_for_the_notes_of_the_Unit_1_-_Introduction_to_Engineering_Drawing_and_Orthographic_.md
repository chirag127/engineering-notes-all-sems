### Dimensioning

- Dimensioning is the process of adding measurements and other information to a drawing to specify the size, shape, location, and features of an object.
- Dimensioning is essential for communicating the design intent and ensuring the accuracy and quality of the product.
- Dimensioning follows some standard rules and conventions to avoid ambiguity and confusion.
- Some of the basic principles of dimensioning are:

  - Dimensions should be placed on the view that shows the feature most clearly and completely.
  - Dimensions should be aligned with the dimension lines and placed above them, except for circular features, which can have the dimensions placed inside or outside the circle.
  - Dimensions should be placed outside the outline of the object, unless there is not enough space or it would make the drawing unclear.
  - Dimensions should not be duplicated or implied by other dimensions or scale.
  - Dimensions should be placed at right angles to the dimension lines, unless the dimension lines are parallel or inclined to 30°, 45°, or 60°, in which case the dimensions can be placed parallel to the dimension lines.
  - Dimensions should be given in millimeters, unless otherwise specified.
  - Dimensions should be rounded off to the nearest 0.5 mm, unless a higher degree of accuracy is required.
  - Dimensions should be placed in such a way that they can be read from the bottom or right side of the drawing, unless otherwise specified.
  - Dimensions should be grouped and arranged in a logical order, with the most important dimensions given first and the least important ones given last.
  - Dimensions should be separated by a minimum gap of 10 mm from each other and from the object outline.
  - Dimensions should not cross each other or the extension lines, unless unavoidable.
  - Dimensions should be attached to the extension lines by short dashes, called dimension origin indicators, which extend 1 mm beyond the extension lines.
  - Extension lines should extend 2 mm beyond the dimension lines, unless they coincide with the object outline or another extension line.
  - Dimension lines should be thin, continuous lines with arrowheads at both ends, pointing towards the dimension. The arrowheads should be 3 mm long and 1 mm wide, and should touch the extension lines or the object outline.
  - Leader lines should be thin, continuous lines with arrowheads at one end, pointing towards the feature to be dimensioned. The leader lines should be inclined at 30°, 45°, or 60° to the horizontal or vertical, and should not be horizontal, vertical, or parallel to the dimension lines. The leader lines should touch the feature at its center or near its edge, and should not cross the object outline or other leader lines.
