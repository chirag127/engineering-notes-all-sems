### Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees .
- Isometric projection is intended to combine the illusion of depth with the clarity and accuracy of a plan view.
- Isometric projection can be used to draw various shapes, such as cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, spheres, etc.
- Isometric projection has some advantages and disadvantages over other types of projections, such as:

  - Advantages:
    - It preserves the true dimensions and angles of the object.
    - It is easy to construct and measure.
    - It gives a clear and realistic view of the object.
  - Disadvantages:
    - It may cause distortion or confusion in some cases, such as when the object has curved or inclined surfaces.
    - It may not show the hidden details or features of the object.
    - It may not be suitable for complex or irregular objects.

- There are two methods of drawing isometric projection: isometric scale method and box method.

  - Isometric scale method:
    - It involves using an isometric scale to measure and mark the lengths of the isometric lines.
    - An isometric scale is a scale that shows the true lengths of the lines in isometric projection.
    - An isometric scale can be constructed by drawing a 30-degree angle from a horizontal line and dividing it into equal parts.
    - The isometric scale can be used to transfer the dimensions from the orthographic views to the isometric view.
  - Box method:
    - It involves drawing a box that encloses the object and then drawing the object inside the box.
    - The box can be drawn by using the isometric axes and the dimensions of the object.
    - The box can be used as a guide to draw the shape and features of the object.
    - The box can be erased or hidden after the object is drawn.
