### Projection of Lines

- A projection of a line is a geometrically represented image of the line obtained on a plane or surface.
- A line can be projected on different planes, such as the horizontal plane (HP), the vertical plane (VP), the profile plane (PP), or the auxiliary plane (AP).
- The projection of a line on a plane depends on the position and orientation of the line relative to the plane.
- There are four possible cases of projection of a line on a plane:
  - Case 1: The line is parallel to both the plane and the projector (the line of sight).
  - Case 2: The line is parallel to the plane but inclined to the projector.
  - Case 3: The line is inclined to the plane but parallel to the projector.
  - Case 4: The line is inclined to both the plane and the projector.
- The projection of a line on a plane can be determined by projecting the end points of the line on the plane and joining them.
- The length of the projection of a line on a plane is equal to the true length of the line if and only if the line is parallel to the projector.
- The angle between the projection of a line and a reference line on the plane is called the inclination of the line to the plane.
- The projection of a line on a plane can be used to find the true length, true inclination, and true shape of the line and the plane.
- The projection of a line on a plane can be represented by using different methods, such as the first angle projection, the third angle projection, or the isometric projection.
- The projection of a line on a plane is an important concept in engineering drawing and graphics, as it helps to visualize and communicate the shape and dimensions of an object or a structure.
