### Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is commonly used in engineering graphics to communicate the shape and dimensions of an object. Orthographic projection consists of three main steps:

- Choosing a projection plane that is perpendicular to the direction of viewing.
- Drawing parallel lines from the object to the projection plane.
- Transferring the points where the lines intersect the plane to a two-dimensional drawing.

Orthographic projection can produce different views of the same object, depending on the orientation of the projection plane. The most common views are:

- Front view: the projection plane is parallel to the front face of the object.
- Top view: the projection plane is parallel to the top face of the object.
- Side view: the projection plane is parallel to the side face of the object.

Other views, such as isometric, oblique, or auxiliary, can also be obtained by changing the angle or position of the projection plane.

Some advantages of orthographic projection are:

- It preserves the true shape and size of the object in each view.
- It is easy to measure and dimension the object in each view.
- It follows a standard convention that is widely used in engineering and technical drawings.

Some disadvantages of orthographic projection are:

- It requires multiple views to show the complete shape of the object.
- It can be confusing or ambiguous if the object has hidden or overlapping features.
- It does not show the depth or perspective of the object.
