### Scales for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Scales are used to measure and represent the actual dimensions of an object or a part of it on a drawing.
- Scales can be classified into three types: plain scales, diagonal scales, and vernier scales.
- Plain scales are used to measure and show two units of the same system, such as meters and centimeters, or feet and inches. A plain scale consists of a main scale and a sub-scale, divided into equal parts. For example, a plain scale of 1:100 means that 1 cm on the drawing represents 100 cm on the object.
- Diagonal scales are used to measure and show three units of the same system, such as meters, decimeters, and centimeters, or feet, inches, and fractions of an inch. A diagonal scale consists of a main scale, a sub-scale, and a set of diagonal lines, which divide the sub-scale into smaller parts. For example, a diagonal scale of 1:50 means that 1 cm on the drawing represents 50 cm on the object, and each sub-division of the sub-scale represents 1 cm on the object.
- Vernier scales are used to measure and show distances inside a unit and its two significant subdivisions, such as millimeters and tenths of a millimeter, or inches and hundredths of an inch. A vernier scale consists of a main scale and a sliding scale, which has a smaller number of divisions than the main scale. The difference between the two scales is called the least count, which is the smallest distance that can be measured by the vernier scale. For example, a vernier scale of 1:1 means that 1 mm on the drawing represents 1 mm on the object, and the least count is 0.1 mm.
- Engineering scales are a type of plain scales that are used to measure and show distances in engineering drawings. Engineering scales are usually marked with the following ratios: 1:10, 1:20, 1:30, 1:40, 1:50, and 1:60. Each ratio corresponds to one edge of the engineering scale, and each edge is divided into 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, or 60 equal parts. For example, an engineering scale of 1:10 means that 1 cm on the drawing represents 10 cm on the object, and each division of the edge represents 1 cm on the object.
