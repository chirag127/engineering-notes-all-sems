# Cylinder and cone when the axis is inclined to one reference plane by change of position method

- A cylinder is a solid of revolution generated by rotating a rectangle about one of its sides. A cone is a solid of revolution generated by rotating a right triangle about one of its legs.
- The projection of a cylinder or a cone on a plane is an ellipse if the axis of the solid is inclined to the plane. If the axis is perpendicular to the plane, the projection is a circle. If the axis is parallel to the plane, the projection is a rectangle or a triangle, respectively.
- The change of position method is a technique to draw the projections of a solid when its axis is inclined to one reference plane (either HP or VP) and parallel to the other. It involves the following steps:
  - Draw the projections of the solid in its simple position, i.e., when its axis is perpendicular to both HP and VP.
  - Rotate the solid about an axis parallel to the reference plane to which the solid's axis is inclined, until the solid's axis becomes parallel to the other reference plane. This is called the auxiliary position of the solid.
  - Draw the projections of the solid in its auxiliary position, using the traces of the axis and the base circle or triangle as reference points.
  - Rotate the solid back to its original position, keeping the axis inclined to the reference plane. This is called the true position of the solid.
  - Draw the projections of the solid in its true position, using the traces of the axis and the base ellipse as reference points.
  - Erase the unwanted lines and mark the dimensions and labels.
