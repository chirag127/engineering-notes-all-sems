# Engineering Scales

- Engineering scales are tools that are used to measure and represent the dimensions of objects in engineering drawings.
- Engineering scales can be used to create drawings that are larger or smaller than the actual objects, depending on the purpose and accuracy of the drawing.
- Engineering scales can also be used to measure distances and angles on existing drawings, such as maps, plans, and blueprints.
- Engineering scales are usually made of plastic or metal, and have different markings and divisions on each side or edge.
- There are different types of engineering scales, such as:

  - Plain scale: A plain scale is a line that is divided into equal parts, and one of the parts is further subdivided into smaller fractions. A plain scale can be used to represent two units, such as meters and centimeters, or feet and inches .
  - Diagonal scale: A diagonal scale is a plain scale that has diagonal lines drawn across the subdivisions. A diagonal scale can be used to represent three units, such as meters, decimeters, and centimeters, or feet, inches, and eighths of an inch .
  - Comparative scale: A comparative scale is a scale that shows the ratio between two different units, such as kilometers and miles, or meters and yards. A comparative scale can be used to compare the distances or sizes of different objects or places .
  - Vernier scale: A vernier scale is a scale that has a movable part that slides along the fixed part. A vernier scale can be used to measure very small fractions of a unit, such as tenths or hundredths of a millimeter, or inch, by using the principle of alignment of the markings .
  - Scale of chords: A scale of chords is a scale that has a circular arc divided into equal parts, and each part is marked with the length of the chord subtending the corresponding angle. A scale of chords can be used to measure angles or to construct angles of a given size .

- Some examples of engineering scales are:

  - Engineer's scale: An engineer's scale is a type of plain scale that is used to measure and draw roads, water mains, and topographical features. An engineer's scale has different ratios, such as 1 inch = 10 feet, or 1 inch = 50 feet, or 1:10, or 1:50 .
  - Architect's scale: An architect's scale is a type of plain scale that is used to measure and draw buildings and structures. An architect's scale has different ratios, such as 1/4 inch = 1 foot, or 1/8 inch = 1 foot, or 1:48, or 1:96 .
  - Metric scale: A metric scale is a type of plain scale that is used to measure and draw objects in the metric system. A metric scale has different ratios, such as 1:1, or 1:2, or 1:5, or 1:10, or 1:20, or 1:50, or 1:100, or 1:200, or 1:500, or 1:1000, or 1:2000, or 1:5000, or 1:10000.
  - Map scale: A map scale is a type of comparative scale that is used to show the relationship between the distance on a map and the actual distance on the ground. A map scale can be expressed as a verbal statement, such as "one inch equals one mile", or a fraction, such as 1/63360, or a graphic representation, such as a bar or a ruler.
  - Protractor: A protractor is a type of scale of chords that is used to measure and draw angles. A protractor has a semi-circular or circular arc divided into 180 or 360 degrees, and each degree is marked with the length of the chord subtending the angle.
