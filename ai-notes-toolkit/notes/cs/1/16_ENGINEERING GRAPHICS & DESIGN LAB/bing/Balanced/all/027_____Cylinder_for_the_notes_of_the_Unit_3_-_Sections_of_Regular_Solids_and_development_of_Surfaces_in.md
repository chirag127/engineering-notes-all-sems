# Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid that has two circular bases and a curved surface that connects them. The bases are parallel and congruent, and the axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases.

Some points to note about cylinders are:

- A cylinder can be classified as a right cylinder or an oblique cylinder, depending on whether the axis is perpendicular or oblique to the bases.
- A cylinder can also be classified as a circular cylinder or an elliptical cylinder, depending on whether the bases are circles or ellipses.
- A cylinder can be cut by a plane in different ways, resulting in different sections, such as circles, ellipses, parabolas, or hyperbolas.
- A cylinder can be unfolded or developed into a rectangular or trapezoidal sheet, depending on whether it is a right circular cylinder or an oblique circular cylinder.

Some examples of cylinders in engineering graphics are:

- A pipe or a tube is a right circular cylinder that has a hollow interior.
- A cone is a special case of a circular cylinder that has one of the bases reduced to a point.
- A frustum is a portion of a circular cylinder that is cut by two parallel planes, resulting in a truncated cone.
- A cylinder can be drawn in isometric view by using ellipses for the bases and straight lines for the sides.
- A cylinder can be drawn in orthographic projection by using circles for the bases and rectangles for the sides.
