## Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces

- A regular solid is a three-dimensional shape that has all its faces congruent and regular, such as a cube, a tetrahedron, or a sphere.
- A section of a regular solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane. For example, a section of a cube can be a square, a rectangle, a hexagon, or a triangle, depending on the orientation of the cutting plane.
- A development of a surface is a flat pattern that can be folded or bent to form the surface of a solid. For example, a development of a cube is a square net with six congruent squares.
- To find the section of a regular solid, one can use the following steps:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid, using orthographic projection.
  - Draw the cutting plane on the front view and the top view, using dashed lines.
  - Mark the points where the cutting plane intersects the edges of the solid, and label them with letters.
  - Draw the section view by joining the corresponding points with straight lines, using solid lines.
  - Shade the section view to indicate the cut portion of the solid.
- To find the development of a surface, one can use the following steps:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid, using orthographic projection.
  - Identify the faces of the solid that are visible from the front view and the top view, and label them with numbers.
  - Draw the development of the surface by unfolding the solid along its edges, and drawing the shapes of the faces on a flat plane, using solid lines.
  - Indicate the folding lines by using dashed lines, and label them with the corresponding numbers of the faces.
  - Show the dimensions of the development, using arrowheads and leader lines.
