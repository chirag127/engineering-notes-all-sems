# Diagonal

- A diagonal is a line segment that joins two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon or a polyhedron.
- In engineering drawing, diagonals are used to construct true shapes and true lengths of inclined surfaces in orthographic projections.
- Diagonals are also used to find the center of a circle or an arc in orthographic projections.
- To construct a diagonal, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the orthographic views of the given object, showing the inclined surface as a foreshortened figure.
  - Draw a diagonal on the inclined surface in the top view or the front view, depending on the orientation of the surface.
  - Project the diagonal to the other views, where it will appear as a point or a line.
  - Draw a perpendicular line from the diagonal point or line to the reference plane (horizontal or vertical) in the other views.
  - The point where the perpendicular line meets the reference plane is the true length of the diagonal.
  - The true shape of the inclined surface can be obtained by drawing a line parallel to the diagonal through the true length point, and joining it with the other vertices of the surface.
