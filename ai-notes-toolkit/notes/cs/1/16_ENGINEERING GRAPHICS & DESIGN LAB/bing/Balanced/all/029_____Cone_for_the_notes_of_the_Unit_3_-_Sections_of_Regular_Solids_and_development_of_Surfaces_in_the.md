# Cone for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A cone is a solid that has a circular base and a single vertex that is not in the same plane as the base.
- A cone can be cut by a plane to produce different conic sections, such as circle, ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola.
- The conic section depends on the angle and position of the cutting plane relative to the axis and vertex of the cone.
- The development of a cone is the process of unfolding the curved surface of the cone into a flat plane.
- The development of a cone can be done by cutting the cone along a generator (a line that passes through the vertex and the base) and spreading the surface on a plane.
- The development of a cone is a sector of a circle whose radius is equal to the slant height of the cone and whose angle is equal to the angle subtended by the base of the cone at the vertex.
- The development of a sectioned cone is the process of unfolding the part of the cone that is cut by a plane into a flat plane.
- The development of a sectioned cone can be done by cutting the cone along a generator and spreading the surface on a plane, and then drawing the true shape of the section on the development.
- The true shape of the section can be found by projecting the points of intersection of the section plane and the cone on the base of the cone, and then transferring them to the development using the arc length method.
