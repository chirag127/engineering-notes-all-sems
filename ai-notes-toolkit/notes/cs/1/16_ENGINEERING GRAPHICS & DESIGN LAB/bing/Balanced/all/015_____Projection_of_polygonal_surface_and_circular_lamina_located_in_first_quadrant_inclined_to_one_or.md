# Projection of polygonal surface and circular lamina located in first quadrant inclined to one or both reference planes

- A polygonal surface is a plane surface bounded by straight lines, such as a triangle, a square, a pentagon, etc.
- A circular lamina is a plane surface bounded by a circle, such as a coin, a disc, a wheel, etc.
- Projection of a plane surface is the rectilinear parallel projection of the surface onto a plane, such as the horizontal plane (HP) or the vertical plane (VP).
- The projected area of a plane surface is the area of the projection on the plane, which depends on the orientation of the surface with respect to the plane.
- The true shape and size of a plane surface can be obtained by projecting it on a plane parallel to the surface.
- The true inclination of a plane surface with respect to a plane can be obtained by projecting the surface on a plane perpendicular to the plane of inclination.

## Steps to draw the projection of a polygonal surface or a circular lamina located in first quadrant inclined to one or both reference planes

- Assume a suitable position and orientation of the plane surface in the first quadrant, such that it is inclined to one or both reference planes.
- Draw the top view and the front view of the plane surface, showing the visible and hidden edges or boundaries.
- Mark the points of intersection of the edges or boundaries with the reference planes, and project them on the other view.
- Draw the traces of the plane surface on the reference planes, if any.
- Measure the projected lengths and angles of the edges or boundaries in both views, and compare them with the actual dimensions of the plane surface.
- Measure the inclination of the plane surface with respect to the reference planes, using the traces or the projected angles.
- If required, draw the auxiliary view of the plane surface on a plane parallel to the surface, to show its true shape and size.
- If required, draw the auxiliary view of the plane surface on a plane perpendicular to the plane of inclination, to show its true inclination.

- Example 1: A square lamina of 40 mm side is inclined to HP at 30° and to VP at 45°. Draw its projections.
  - Assume the square lamina ABCD is in the first quadrant, with its corner A on HP and its diagonal AC on VP.
  - Draw the top view of the square lamina as a rhombus abcd, with ab = 40 mm and ∠bad = 30°. Mark the point e where the diagonal ac intersects HP, and the point f where the diagonal bd intersects HP.
  - Draw the front view of the square lamina as a rectangle a'b'c'd', with a'b' = 40 mm and a'd' = 40 mm. Mark the point e' where the diagonal ac intersects VP, and the point f' where the diagonal bd intersects VP.
  - Project the points e and f on the front view as e' and f', and join them to form the horizontal trace HT of the square lamina on VP.
  - Project the points e' and f' on the top view as e and f, and join them to form the vertical trace VT of the square lamina on HP.
  - Measure the projected lengths and angles of the edges and diagonals in both views, and compare them with the actual dimensions of the square lamina.
  - Measure the inclination of the square lamina with respect to HP and VP, using the traces or the projected angles. The inclination with respect to HP is ∠bad = 30°, and the inclination with respect to VP is ∠a'b'e' = 45°.
  - If required, draw the auxiliary view of the square lamina on a plane parallel to the surface, by rotating the front view about the line a'e' until it coincides with the horizontal trace HT. The auxiliary view will show the true shape and size of the square lamina as a square ABCD, with AB = 40 mm and ∠BAD = 90°.
  - If required, draw the auxiliary view of the square lamina on a plane perpendicular to the plane of inclination, by rotating the top view about the line ae until it coincides with the vertical trace VT.
