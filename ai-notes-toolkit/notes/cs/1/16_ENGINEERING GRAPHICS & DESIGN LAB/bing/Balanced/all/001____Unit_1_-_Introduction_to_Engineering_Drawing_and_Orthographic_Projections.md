## Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate technical information, such as dimensions, shapes, materials, and manufacturing processes, of physical objects and systems.
- Engineering drawing can be done by hand or by using computer-aided design (CAD) software.
- Engineering drawing follows a set of standards and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency of the drawings.
- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by projecting its views on parallel planes perpendicular to the principal axes of the object.
- Orthographic projection can be classified into two types: first-angle projection and third-angle projection, depending on the relative positions of the object and the planes of projection.
- First-angle projection is used in Europe, Asia, and Australia, while third-angle projection is used in North America.
- In first-angle projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant, and the views are projected onto the planes as if the object is behind the planes. The front view is on the right, the top view is below, and the left view is on the left.
- In third-angle projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant, and the views are projected onto the planes as if the object is in front of the planes. The front view is on the left, the top view is above, and the right view is on the right.
- The views are aligned with each other by using projection lines, which are dashed lines that connect the corresponding points on the views.
- The views are labeled with letters, such as F for front view, T for top view, L for left view, and R for right view.
- The views are drawn to the same scale, unless otherwise specified.
- The views are arranged in a standard layout, such as the one shown below, to facilitate the reading and interpretation of the drawing.

```
    T
L   F   R
    B
```

- B is the bottom view, which is optional and usually omitted unless it shows important features of the object.
