# Circle

A circle is a two-dimensional shape that consists of all the points that are equidistant from a fixed point called the center. The distance from the center to any point on the circle is called the radius. A line segment that joins two points on the circle and passes through the center is called the diameter. The diameter is twice the radius. A circle has no corners or edges and is perfectly round.

Some of the properties of a circle are:

- The circumference of a circle is the perimeter or the length of the boundary of the circle. The circumference of a circle is given by the formula C = 2πr, where r is the radius and π is a constant that is approximately equal to 3.14.
- The area of a circle is the amount of space enclosed by the circle. The area of a circle is given by the formula A = πr^2, where r is the radius and π is a constant that is approximately equal to 3.14.
- A chord of a circle is a line segment that joins any two points on the circle. The longest chord of a circle is the diameter. A chord that is perpendicular to the radius at its endpoint is called a tangent. A tangent is a line that touches the circle at exactly one point and does not intersect the circle anywhere else.
- An arc of a circle is a part of the circumference of the circle. The length of an arc is proportional to the angle subtended by the arc at the center of the circle. The angle subtended by an arc at the center of the circle is called the central angle. The measure of a central angle in degrees is equal to the measure of the arc in degrees. The measure of a central angle in radians is equal to the length of the arc divided by the radius of the circle.
