# Creation of engineering models and their presentation in standard 2D blueprint form

- Engineering models are representations of physical objects or systems that capture their essential features and behavior. They can be used for analysis, design, simulation, testing, or communication purposes.
- Engineering models can be created using various methods, such as sketching, drafting, CAD (computer-aided design), or CAE (computer-aided engineering) software. The choice of method depends on the complexity, accuracy, and purpose of the model.
- Engineering models can be presented in different forms, such as 2D blueprints, 3D wireframes, 3D shaded solids, or 3D meshes. The choice of form depends on the level of detail, clarity, and realism required by the audience or the application.
- 2D blueprints are drawings that show the shape, size, and dimensions of an object or a system in two dimensions. They are often used for manufacturing, assembly, or inspection purposes. They follow standard conventions and symbols, such as ISO and ANSI, for coordinate dimensioning and tolerancing, geometric dimensioning and tolerancing, and projection methods.
- 2D blueprints can be created manually or automatically from 3D models using CAD software. The advantages of 2D blueprints are that they are easy to read, print, and share, and that they can convey precise and unambiguous information. The disadvantages are that they may not show the depth, perspective, or appearance of the object or system, and that they may require multiple views or sections to show all the features.
- 2D blueprints can be classified into different types, such as orthographic projections, isometric projections, oblique projections, perspective projections, sectional views, auxiliary views, detail views, or assembly drawings. The choice of type depends on the complexity, orientation, and visibility of the features of the object or system.
