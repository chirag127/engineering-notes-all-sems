# Notes for Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

## Engineering Drawing
- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate technical information.
- Engineering drawing consists of symbols, lines, dimensions, notes, and views that represent the shape, size, and specifications of an object or a system.
- Engineering drawing follows certain standards and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and uniformity of the drawings.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into two types: sketching and drafting.
  - Sketching is a freehand drawing technique that uses simple tools such as pencils, erasers, and paper. Sketching is used for quick and rough representation of ideas, concepts, and designs.
  - Drafting is a precise and detailed drawing technique that uses specialized tools such as rulers, compasses, protractors, and software. Drafting is used for final and formal representation of products, structures, and systems.

## Orthographic Projections
- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by projecting its features onto parallel planes.
- Orthographic projection is based on the principle that the lines of projection are perpendicular to the planes of projection.
- Orthographic projection can be classified into two types: first-angle projection and third-angle projection.
  - First-angle projection is a method of orthographic projection where the object is placed in the first quadrant, between the horizontal and vertical planes of projection. The front view is projected onto the vertical plane, the top view is projected onto the horizontal plane, and the side view is projected onto the plane perpendicular to the horizontal and vertical planes. The planes of projection are unfolded such that the horizontal plane is below the vertical plane, and the side plane is on the left of the vertical plane.
  - Third-angle projection is a method of orthographic projection where the object is placed in the third quadrant, behind the horizontal and vertical planes of projection. The front view is projected onto the vertical plane, the top view is projected onto the horizontal plane, and the side view is projected onto the plane perpendicular to the horizontal and vertical planes. The planes of projection are unfolded such that the horizontal plane is above the vertical plane, and the side plane is on the right of the vertical plane.
- Orthographic projection follows certain rules and conventions to represent the shape, size, and orientation of the object and its features. Some of these rules and conventions are:
  - The views are aligned with each other along the projection lines.
  - The dimensions are given in the view where the feature is clearly visible and measurable.
  - The hidden lines are shown by dashed lines, and the center lines are shown by chain lines.
  - The scale of the drawing is indicated in the title block or the notes section.
  - The symbols and abbreviations are used to indicate the type of projection, the surface finish, the tolerances, and the units of measurement.
