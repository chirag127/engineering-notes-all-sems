# Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is commonly used in engineering graphics to communicate the shape and dimensions of an object. Orthographic projection is also known as orthogonal projection.

## Principles of Orthographic Projection

- Orthographic projection is based on the principle of parallel projection, which means that the projection lines are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the projection plane.
- Orthographic projection consists of three principal views: the top view, the front view, and the side view. Each view shows the object as if it is viewed from a different direction, such as above, in front, or from the side.
- The three principal views are arranged according to the first-angle or the third-angle projection system. In the first-angle system, the object is placed in the first quadrant, and the views are projected onto the adjacent planes. In the third-angle system, the object is placed in the third quadrant, and the views are projected onto the opposite planes.
- The three principal views are aligned with each other using projection lines, which are dashed lines that connect the corresponding points on the views. The projection lines help to show the relationship between the views and the object.
- The three principal views are drawn to the same scale, which means that the dimensions of the object are preserved in the projection. The scale can be chosen according to the size and complexity of the object, and it should be indicated on the drawing.

## Advantages and Disadvantages of Orthographic Projection

- Orthographic projection has some advantages over other methods of representation, such as perspective projection or isometric projection. Some of the advantages are:
  - Orthographic projection is simple and easy to construct, as it only requires straight lines and basic geometric shapes.
  - Orthographic projection is accurate and precise, as it shows the true shape and size of the object without distortion or foreshortening.
  - Orthographic projection is standardized and widely used in engineering and technical fields, as it follows a set of rules and conventions that are universally understood.
- Orthographic projection also has some disadvantages and limitations, such as:
  - Orthographic projection requires multiple views to show the complete shape and features of the object, which can be confusing and time-consuming to draw and interpret.
  - Orthographic projection does not show the depth or the three-dimensional appearance of the object, which can make it difficult to visualize or appreciate the object.
  - Orthographic projection may not be suitable for complex or irregular objects, as it may require many views or auxiliary views to show all the details and angles of the object.
