- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate technical information, such as design specifications, dimensions, tolerances, materials, and manufacturing processes.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into two types: artistic drawing and technical drawing. Artistic drawing is used to express aesthetic or creative ideas, while technical drawing is used to convey precise and accurate information.
- Engineering drawing can be further divided into two categories: freehand sketching and instrument drawing. Freehand sketching is done without using any instruments, such as rulers, compasses, or protractors. Instrument drawing is done with the help of instruments, such as drawing boards, T-squares, set squares, scales, and pencils.
- Engineering drawing follows certain standards and conventions to ensure clarity, consistency, and accuracy. Some of the common standards and conventions are:
  - Drawing sheet size and layout: The drawing sheet size and layout should follow the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) or the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) specifications. The drawing sheet should have a title block, a border, and a grid of reference lines.
  - Drawing scales: The drawing scale is the ratio of the size of the object on the drawing to the actual size of the object. The drawing scale should be chosen according to the size and complexity of the object and the available space on the drawing sheet. The drawing scale should be indicated on the drawing sheet, preferably in the title block.
  - Drawing views: The drawing views are the projections of the object on different planes. The drawing views should show the shape, size, and features of the object clearly and completely. The drawing views should follow the orthographic projection method, which is based on the principle of projecting the object on six mutually perpendicular planes, called the principal planes of projection. The six principal planes of projection are: the front plane, the top plane, the bottom plane, the left plane, the right plane, and the back plane. The drawing views can be classified into two types: multiview drawing and pictorial drawing. Multiview drawing shows two or more views of the object on different planes, such as the front view, the top view, and the side view. Pictorial drawing shows one view of the object on a single plane, such as the isometric view, the oblique view, or the perspective view.
  - Drawing lines: The drawing lines are used to represent the edges, contours, boundaries, and dimensions of the object. The drawing lines should follow the line types, line weights, and line styles specified by the ISO or the BIS standards. Some of the common line types are: visible line, hidden line, center line, extension line, dimension line, leader line, cutting plane line, section line, and break line.
  - Drawing symbols: The drawing symbols are used to represent the details, features, and specifications of the object that are not easily shown by the drawing lines. The drawing symbols should follow the standard symbols specified by the ISO or the BIS standards. Some of the common drawing symbols are: surface finish symbol, welding symbol, datum symbol, geometric tolerance symbol, and material symbol.
  - Drawing dimensions: The drawing dimensions are used to indicate the size and location of the object and its features. The drawing dimensions should follow the dimensioning rules, methods, and formats specified by the ISO or the BIS standards. Some of the common dimensioning methods are: aligned dimensioning, unidirectional dimensioning, chain dimensioning, parallel dimensioning, and angular dimensioning.
  - Drawing notes: The drawing notes are used to provide additional information or instructions that are not shown by the drawing lines, symbols, or dimensions. The drawing notes should be clear, concise, and consistent. The drawing notes should be placed near the relevant drawing views or features, and should be separated from the drawing lines by a gap or a leader line. The drawing notes should follow the standard abbreviations, lettering, and numbering specified by the ISO or the BIS standards.
