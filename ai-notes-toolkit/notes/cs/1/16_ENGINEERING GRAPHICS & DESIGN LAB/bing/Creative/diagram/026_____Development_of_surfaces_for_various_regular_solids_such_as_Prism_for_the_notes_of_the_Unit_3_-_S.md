### Development of Surfaces for Various Regular Solids such as Prism

- Development of surfaces is a technique of representing the shape of a three-dimensional object on a plane surface by unfolding or unrolling its surfaces.
- Development of surfaces is useful for designing and manufacturing sheet metal objects, such as containers, boxes, pipes, etc.
- Development of surfaces can be done by different methods, such as parallel-line method, radial-line method, triangulation method, and approximate method.
- Parallel-line method is used for developing prisms and cylinders, in which all the edges or generators of the lateral surfaces are parallel to each other.
- Radial-line method is used for developing pyramids and cones, in which the edges or generators of the lateral surfaces converge to a point (the apex or the vertex).
- Triangulation method is used for developing solids with curved surfaces, such as spheres, torus, etc., by dividing the surface into small triangles and developing each triangle separately.
- Approximate method is used for developing solids with complex or irregular shapes, such as helix, screw, etc., by using approximate shapes, such as polygons, circles, etc.

- To develop the surface of a prism, the following steps are followed:

  1. Draw the front view and the top view of the prism in the first angle projection.
  2. Draw a horizontal line AB below the front view, which will be the base line of the development.
  3. Mark the width of the base of the prism on the line AB, and divide it into equal parts, corresponding to the number of sides of the base. Label the points as A, B, C, D, etc.
  4. Draw perpendiculars from the points A, B, C, D, etc., and mark the heights of the prism on them. Label the points as A', B', C', D', etc.
  5. Join the points A'B', B'C', C'D', etc., to form the development of the lateral surface of the prism.
  6. To develop the top and bottom surfaces of the prism, draw arcs with centers at A and B, and radii equal to the distances of the top and bottom corners of the prism from the axis. Cut the arcs by the perpendiculars from A and B, and label the points as E, F, G, H, etc.
  7. Join the points E, F, G, H, etc., to form the development of the top and bottom surfaces of the prism.
