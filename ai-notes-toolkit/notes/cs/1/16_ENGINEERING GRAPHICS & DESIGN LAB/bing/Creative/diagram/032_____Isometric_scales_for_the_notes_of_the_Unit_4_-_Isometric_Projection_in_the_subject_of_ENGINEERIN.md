# Isometric scales for the notes of the Unit 4 - Isometric Projection in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees.
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the overall shape and dimensions of an object without distortion or perspective.
- Isometric projection can be drawn by using an isometric scale, which is a device that converts the actual measurements of an object into the corresponding distances on the isometric plane.
- The isometric scale is constructed as follows:

  - Draw a horizontal line OA.
  - Through O draw OP and OQ at 30º and 45º to the horizontal respectively.
  - On OQ mark the actual scale in mm.
  - Draw verticals from each of the division points on the actual scale to cut OP at the corresponding divisions.
  - The points on OP form the isometric scale.

- The isometric scale can be used to draw isometric projections by measuring the distances along the three axes (X, Y, and Z) using the isometric scale and plotting them on the isometric plane.
- The isometric plane is a plane that contains the three isometric axes and forms an angle of 35.26º with the horizontal plane.
- The isometric plane can be drawn by using a 30º set square and a 45º set square as shown below:

```
    /| 30º
   / |
  /  |
 /   |
/____| 45º
```

- The isometric plane can be divided into four quadrants by drawing two perpendicular lines through the origin O, one horizontal and one vertical.
- The isometric projection of an object can be drawn by following these steps:

  - Choose a suitable position for the object on the isometric plane and mark the origin O.
  - Draw the isometric axes X, Y, and Z from O at 120º to each other.
  - Measure the length, width, and height of the object along the actual scale and transfer them to the isometric scale.
  - Plot the points on the isometric plane using the isometric scale and the isometric axes.
  - Join the points to form the isometric projection of the object.
  - Add details and dimensions as required.
