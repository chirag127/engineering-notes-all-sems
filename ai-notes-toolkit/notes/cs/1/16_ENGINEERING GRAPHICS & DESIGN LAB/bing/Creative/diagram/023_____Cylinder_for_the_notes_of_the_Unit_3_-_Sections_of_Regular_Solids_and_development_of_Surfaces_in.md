### Cylinder

A cylinder is a solid that has two circular bases of equal radius and a curved surface that connects them. The axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases.

Some points to remember when drawing a cylinder in engineering graphics are:

- To draw the top and bottom bases, use ellipses that are perpendicular to the axis of the cylinder. The major axis of the ellipse should be equal to the diameter of the base, and the minor axis should be equal to the projection of the diameter on the plane of the ellipse. 
- To draw the curved surface, use vertical lines that are parallel to the axis of the cylinder and connect the corresponding points on the top and bottom ellipses. The length of these lines should be equal to the height of the cylinder. 
- To draw the centerline of the cylinder, use a dashed line that passes through the centers of the top and bottom ellipses. The centerline should be perpendicular to the plane of the ellipse and coincide with the axis of the cylinder. 
- To draw the isometric view of a cylinder, use a 30-degree angle for the major axis of the ellipse and a 150-degree angle for the minor axis. The centerline of the cylinder should be parallel to the isometric axis. 
- To draw the development of a cylinder, use a rectangle that has a length equal to the circumference of the base and a width equal to the height of the cylinder. The rectangle should be divided into equal parts by vertical lines that correspond to the points on the ellipse. The development of the cylinder can be obtained by cutting the cylinder along a vertical line and unfolding it on a plane.
