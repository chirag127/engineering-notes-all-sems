### Transformation of Projections

- A projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional (3D) object on a two-dimensional (2D) plane, such as a sheet of paper or a computer screen.
- A projection is produced by connecting the points where the lines of sight from an observer to the object pierce the projection plane. As a result, the 3D object is transformed into a 2D view.
- There are different types of projections, depending on the orientation of the projection plane and the direction of the lines of sight. Some common types are:
  - Parallel projection: The lines of sight are assumed to be parallel, and the projection plane is perpendicular to a principal axis of the object. This type of projection preserves the true shape and size of the object, but distorts the angles and distances. Parallel projection is often used for engineering drawings and drafting.
  - Perspective projection: The lines of sight converge at a single point, called the center of projection or the eye point. The projection plane is perpendicular to the line joining the center of projection and the center of the object. This type of projection mimics the way humans see the world, and creates a realistic impression of depth and distance. However, it also distorts the shape and size of the object.
  - Oblique projection: The lines of sight are parallel, but the projection plane is not perpendicular to any principal axis of the object. This type of projection creates a distorted view of the object, but allows some of its features to be seen more clearly. Oblique projection is often used for pictorial drawings and sketches.
- A transformation of projection is a change of the projection type, the projection plane, or the center of projection. This can be done by applying a matrix multiplication to the coordinates of the object, or by using geometric constructions. A transformation of projection can alter the appearance of the object, but not its intrinsic properties.
- Some examples of transformation of projection are:
  - Projective transformation: A transformation that preserves the collinearity and the cross-ratio of points, but not the parallelism or the angles. This type of transformation can be used to convert between different perspective projections, or between a perspective projection and a parallel projection.
  - Affine transformation: A transformation that preserves the parallelism and the ratios of distances, but not the angles or the lengths. This type of transformation can be used to convert between different parallel projections, or between a parallel projection and an oblique projection.
  - Orthographic transformation: A transformation that preserves the angles and the lengths, but not the ratios of distances. This type of transformation can be used to convert between different orthographic projections, which are a special case of parallel projection where the projection plane is perpendicular to one of the principal planes of the object.
