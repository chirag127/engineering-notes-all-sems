### Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design

- Computer Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer software to design and document a product's design process .
- CAD software can create, modify, analyze, or optimize a design, and can also generate graphical symbols such as points, lines, curves, planes and shapes .
- CAD software can increase the productivity of the designer, improve the quality of design, improve communications through documentation, and create a database for manufacturing .
- CAD software can also be integrated with other computer systems, such as Computer Aided Manufacturing (CAM), Computer Aided Engineering (CAE), Product Lifecycle Management (PLM), and Building Information Modeling (BIM).
- Some examples of CAD software are AutoCAD, SolidWorks, CATIA, Creo, Inventor, SketchUp, and Revit.
- Some benefits of using CAD software are:
  - Faster and more accurate design process
  - Easier modification and revision of designs
  - Better visualization and simulation of designs
  - Enhanced collaboration and communication among designers and stakeholders
  - Reduced errors and costs in design and manufacturing
  - Increased innovation and creativity in design
- Some challenges of using CAD software are:
  - High initial cost and maintenance of software and hardware
  - Need for training and skill development of designers
  - Dependence on computer systems and data security
  - Ethical and legal issues in design ownership and intellectual property
  - Social and environmental impacts of design and manufacturing
