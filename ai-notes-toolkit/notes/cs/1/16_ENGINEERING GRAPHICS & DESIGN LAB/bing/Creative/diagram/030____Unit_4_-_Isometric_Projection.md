## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions.
- It is a type of axonometric projection, which means that the three principal axes are equally inclined to the plane of projection, and the scale along each axis is the same.
- Isometric projection preserves the angles and proportions of the object, but distorts the distances and shapes.
- To draw an isometric projection, one can use an isometric grid, which consists of equilateral triangles arranged in a hexagonal pattern.
- Alternatively, one can use an isometric cube, which is a cube with its edges parallel to the principal axes, and its faces inclined at 30 degrees to the horizontal plane.
- To draw an isometric projection of an object, one can follow these steps:
  - Choose a suitable orientation for the object, such that its faces are parallel or perpendicular to the principal axes.
  - Draw the isometric cube that contains the object, using 30-degree angles for the inclined edges.
  - Draw the outline of the object within the isometric cube, using horizontal and vertical lines for the edges parallel to the axes, and 30-degree angles for the inclined edges.
  - Erase the parts of the isometric cube that are hidden by the object, and add any details or shading as needed.
