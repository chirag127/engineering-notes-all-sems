### Projection of Lines

- A line is a basic element of engineering drawing that represents the path of a moving point.
- A line can be defined by its two end points, its length, and its direction or inclination with respect to the reference planes.
- The reference planes are the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP) that are perpendicular to each other and intersect along a line called the reference line (RL).
- The projection of a line on a plane is the shortest distance between the line and the plane, measured along a perpendicular from the line to the plane.
- The projection of a line on the HP is called the top view or plan, and the projection of a line on the VP is called the front view or elevation.
- The projections of a line can be classified into four types based on the relative positions of the line and the reference planes:

  - Parallel to both planes: The line is parallel to both the HP and the VP, and its projections are equal in length and parallel to the RL.
  - Perpendicular to one plane and parallel to the other: The line is perpendicular to one of the reference planes and parallel to the other, and its projection on the perpendicular plane is a point, while its projection on the parallel plane is equal in length and parallel to the line.
  - Inclined to one plane and parallel to the other: The line is inclined to one of the reference planes and parallel to the other, and its projection on the inclined plane is shorter than the true length of the line, while its projection on the parallel plane is equal in length and parallel to the line.
  - Inclined to both planes: The line is inclined to both the HP and the VP, and its projections on both planes are shorter than the true length of the line and inclined to the RL.

- To draw the projections of a line, the following steps are followed:

  - Locate the end points of the line on the reference planes using given dimensions or coordinates.
  - Draw the projections of the end points on the HP and the VP, and join them by straight lines to get the top view and the front view of the line.
  - Mark the true length of the line and the angles of inclination with the reference planes using auxiliary planes or traces.
  - Label the projections of the line and the end points with suitable notations and dimensions.
