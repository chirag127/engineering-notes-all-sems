## Introduction

- A pyramid is a polyhedron that has a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common vertex.
- The base can be any regular or irregular polygon, such as a square, a triangle, a pentagon, etc.
- The triangular faces are called lateral faces, and the common vertex is called the apex.
- The height of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the apex to the base plane.
- The slant height of a pyramid is the distance from the apex to the midpoint of an edge of the base.
- The axis of a pyramid is the line joining the apex and the centroid of the base.

## Sections of Regular Solids

- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane.
- The plane that cuts the solid is called the cutting plane or the section plane.
- The shape of the section depends on the orientation and position of the cutting plane and the shape of the solid.
- A section can be classified as a true section or an apparent section.
- A true section is the shape obtained when the cutting plane is perpendicular to the axis of the solid.
- An apparent section is the shape obtained when the cutting plane is inclined to the axis of the solid.
- To draw the true shape of a section, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid with the cutting plane.
  - Project the points of intersection of the cutting plane and the solid on the front view and the top view.
  - Draw a line parallel to the axis of the solid and passing through the centroid of the base in the top view.
  - Mark the points of intersection of the cutting plane and the solid on this line.
  - Draw a perpendicular line from each point to the base of the solid in the top view.
  - Transfer these points to the front view along the projectors.
  - Draw the true shape of the section by joining these points in the front view.

## Development of Surfaces

- A development of a surface is the unfolded or flattened shape of the surface that can be cut and folded to form the original surface.
- A development of a surface is useful for making patterns or templates for cutting and bending materials to form the surface.
- To draw the development of a surface, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid with the cutting plane.
  - Divide the surface into a number of equal parts or elements, such as rectangles, triangles, sectors, etc.
  - Mark the points of division on the front view and the top view.
  - Draw a reference line on the front view and the top view, parallel to the axis of the solid.
  - Draw the development of the base of the solid by transferring the points of division from the top view to a horizontal line.
  - Draw the development of the lateral surface of the solid by transferring the points of division from the front view to a vertical line.
  - Join the points of division on the vertical line to the corresponding points on the horizontal line by straight lines or arcs, depending on the shape of the surface.
  - Cut along the outer boundary of the development and fold along the inner lines to form the surface.
