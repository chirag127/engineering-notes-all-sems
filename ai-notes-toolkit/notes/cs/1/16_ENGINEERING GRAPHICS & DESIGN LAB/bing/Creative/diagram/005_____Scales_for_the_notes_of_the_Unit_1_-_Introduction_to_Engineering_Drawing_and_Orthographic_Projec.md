### Scales for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- A scale is a ratio that compares the measurements of the drawing to the actual measurements of the object.
- Scales are used in engineering drawings to represent the size and shape of the object accurately and precisely.
- Scales can be classified into three types: full scale, reduced scale, and enlarged scale.
- Full scale drawings show the actual size of the object. For example, a scale of 1:1 means that one unit on the drawing is equal to one unit on the object.
- Reduced scale drawings show the object smaller than its actual size. For example, a scale of 1:10 means that one unit on the drawing is equal to ten units on the object. Reduced scales are used when the object is too large to fit on the drawing sheet.
- Enlarged scale drawings show the object larger than its actual size. For example, a scale of 10:1 means that one unit on the drawing is equal to one-tenth of a unit on the object. Enlarged scales are used when the object is too small to show the details clearly.
- Scales can also be classified into two categories: plain scales and diagonal scales.
- Plain scales are used to measure distances in one unit or its fractions. For example, a plain scale of 1:100 can measure distances in meters or centimeters.
- Diagonal scales are used to measure distances in two units or their fractions. For example, a diagonal scale of 1:100 can measure distances in meters and decimeters, or centimeters and millimeters.
- Scales can also be classified into four standards: representative fraction, engineering scale, architectural scale, and metric scale.
- Representative fraction (RF) is a scale that expresses the ratio of the drawing to the object as a fraction. For example, a scale of 1:100 can be written as RF = 1/100.
- Engineering scale is a scale that expresses the ratio of the drawing to the object as a decimal. For example, a scale of 1:100 can be written as 1" = 100'.
- Architectural scale is a scale that expresses the ratio of the drawing to the object as a fraction of an inch. For example, a scale of 1:100 can be written as 1/8" = 1'-0".
- Metric scale is a scale that expresses the ratio of the drawing to the object in meters. For example, a scale of 1:100 can be written as 1 cm = 1 m.
- Scales are important in engineering drawing because they allow the designer to communicate the dimensions and proportions of the object clearly and accurately to the fabricator or constructor. Scales also help the reader to visualize the object and its features. Scales should be chosen carefully to suit the purpose and size of the drawing. Scales should also be indicated on the drawing for reference.
