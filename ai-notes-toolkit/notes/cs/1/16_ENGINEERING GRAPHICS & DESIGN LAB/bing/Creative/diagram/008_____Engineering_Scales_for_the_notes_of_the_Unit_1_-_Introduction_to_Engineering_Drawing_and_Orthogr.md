### Engineering Scales

- Engineering scales are rulers that produce ratio drawings of objects or plans .
- Engineering scales are commonly used for drafting and blueprint creation in engineering fields  .
- Engineering scales are usually 300 mm long, which is a little more than 12 inches. The extra space on the ends is meant to provide a safety margin.
- Engineering scales are shaped like long triangular prisms, with each side of each point marked with a different scale for measuring .
- Engineering scales have six scales on each prism, divided into decimalized fractions of an inch .
- The six scales on each prism are 1:10, 1:20, 1:30, 1:40, 1:50 and 1:60, unless otherwise stated. These ratios mean that one unit on the scale represents 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 or 60 units on the actual object or plan.
- The first graduation on an engineering scale is the zero mark. The zero mark is aligned with the starting point of the measurement on the drawing.
- To use an engineering scale, one must choose the appropriate scale for the drawing, align the zero mark with the starting point, and read the measurement at the end point.
