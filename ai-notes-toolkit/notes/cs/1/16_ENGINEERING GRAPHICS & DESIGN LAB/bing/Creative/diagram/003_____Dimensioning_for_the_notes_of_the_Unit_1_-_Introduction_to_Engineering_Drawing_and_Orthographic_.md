### Dimensioning

- Dimensioning is the process of adding numerical values to a drawing to indicate the size, shape, and location of the features of an object.
- Dimensioning is essential for communicating the design intent and specifications of an object to the manufacturers, engineers, and end-users.
- Dimensioning follows certain rules and standards to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency of the drawings.
- Some of the basic rules and standards for dimensioning are:

  - Use a single dimensioning system, either metric or imperial, and do not mix them in the same drawing.
  - Place dimensions outside the views, unless they are too large or interfere with other dimensions or views.
  - Align dimensions with the dimension lines and place them above the dimension lines, except for angular dimensions, which are placed in the arc.
  - Use horizontal and vertical dimension lines, and avoid inclined or curved dimension lines, unless necessary for circular or angular features.
  - Use leader lines to connect dimensions to the features, and make them straight and inclined at 30° or 60° to the horizontal or vertical.
  - Use arrowheads or dots to indicate the ends of the dimension lines and the points of the leader lines.
  - Use extension lines to extend the features beyond the outline of the object and leave a small gap between them and the outline.
  - Avoid crossing or overlapping dimension lines, extension lines, leader lines, or other lines in the drawing.
  - Use chain or continuous dimensioning for linear dimensions, and avoid dimensioning the same feature twice or using unnecessary dimensions.
  - Use aligned or parallel dimensioning for circular or angular dimensions, and place them in the most descriptive view.
  - Use auxiliary views or sections for dimensioning features that are not clearly visible or measurable in the principal views.
  - Use symbols, abbreviations, and notes to indicate special features or requirements, such as diameter, radius, chamfer, countersink, counterbore, thread, surface finish, tolerance, etc.
