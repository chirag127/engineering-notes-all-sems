### Geometry and topology of engineered components

- Geometry is the branch of mathematics that studies the properties, measurements, and relationships of points, lines, angles, surfaces, and solids in space.
- Topology is the branch of mathematics that studies the properties of shapes that are preserved under continuous deformations, such as stretching, twisting, bending, and shrinking, but not tearing or gluing.
- Geometry and topology are closely related, as many geometric concepts can be generalized and abstracted to topological ones, and many topological problems can be approached by geometric methods.
- In engineering, geometry and topology are important for designing and analyzing the shape, size, structure, and function of components, systems, and materials.
- Some examples of geometry and topology in engineering are:

  - CAD (Computer Aided Design) is the use of computer software to create, modify, analyze, and optimize geometric models of engineering components and systems.
  - CAM (Computer Aided Manufacturing) is the use of computer software to control and automate the fabrication of engineering components and systems from geometric models.
  - CAE (Computer Aided Engineering) is the use of computer software to simulate, test, and optimize the performance and behavior of engineering components and systems based on their geometric models.
  - CAGD (Computer Aided Geometric Design) is the use of computer software to create and manipulate curves, surfaces, and solids that are used for geometric modeling in CAD, CAM, and CAE.
  - FEA (Finite Element Analysis) is a numerical method for solving partial differential equations that arise from physical problems involving engineering components and systems, by discretizing their geometric domains into finite elements and applying boundary conditions and loads.
  - BIM (Building Information Modeling) is a process of creating and managing digital representations of physical and functional characteristics of buildings and infrastructure, using geometric and topological data, as well as other information such as materials, costs, schedules, and energy consumption.
  - Topological optimization is a method for finding the optimal shape and structure of engineering components and systems, by minimizing or maximizing a given objective function subject to certain constraints, using techniques from topology, such as homology, cohomology, and Morse theory.
  - Topological data analysis is a method for extracting meaningful features and patterns from complex and high-dimensional data sets, using techniques from topology, such as persistent homology, mapper, and Reeb graphs.
