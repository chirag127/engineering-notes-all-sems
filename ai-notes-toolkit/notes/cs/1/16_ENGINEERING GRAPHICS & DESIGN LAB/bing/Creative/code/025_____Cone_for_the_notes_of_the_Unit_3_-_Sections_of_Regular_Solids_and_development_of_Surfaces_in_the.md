### Cone

A cone is a three-dimensional solid that has a circular base and a curved surface that tapers to a point called the vertex or apex. The height of the cone is the perpendicular distance from the base to the vertex. The slant height of the cone is the distance from the vertex to any point on the edge of the base. The radius of the base is the distance from the center of the base to any point on the edge of the base.

#### Sections of a cone

A section of a cone is the curve obtained by cutting the cone with a plane. Depending on the position and orientation of the plane, different types of sections can be obtained. The most common types of sections are:

- Circle: This is obtained when the plane is perpendicular to the axis of the cone and passes through the base. The circle has the same radius as the base of the cone.

- Ellipse: This is obtained when the plane is inclined to the axis of the cone and cuts both the base and the curved surface. The ellipse has two foci, which are the points where the plane intersects the axis of the cone.

- Parabola: This is obtained when the plane is parallel to a generator of the cone and cuts the base and the curved surface. The parabola has a focus, which is the point where the plane intersects the axis of the cone, and a directrix, which is the line where the plane intersects the base of the cone.

- Hyperbola: This is obtained when the plane is inclined to the axis of the cone and cuts only the curved surface. The hyperbola has two foci, which are the points where the plane intersects the axis of the cone, and two asymptotes, which are the lines where the plane approaches the generators of the cone.

#### Development of a cone

The development of a cone is the process of unfolding the curved surface of the cone into a flat plane. The development of a cone is a sector of a circle, whose radius is equal to the slant height of the cone and whose angle is equal to the angle subtended by the base of the cone at the vertex. The development of a cone can be used to find the surface area of the cone, which is equal to the area of the sector.

To draw the development of a cone, the following steps can be followed:

- Draw the front view and the top view of the cone, showing the dimensions of the base radius, the height, and the slant height.

- Draw an arc of radius equal to the slant height of the cone, with the center at the vertex of the cone.

- Divide the base of the cone into equal parts, and mark the points on the arc corresponding to the divisions.

- Join the points on the arc to the vertex of the cone, forming the sector.

- Cut out the sector and fold it along the radial lines to form the cone.

#### Development of a sectioned cone

The development of a sectioned cone is the process of unfolding the part of the curved surface of the cone that is cut by a plane into a flat plane. The development of a sectioned cone is a segment of a sector of a circle, whose radius is equal to the slant height of the cone and whose angle is equal to the angle subtended by the base of the cone at the vertex. The development of a sectioned cone can be used to find the area of the sectioned surface of the cone, which is equal to the area of the segment.

To draw the development of a sectioned cone, the following steps can be followed:

- Draw the front view and the top view of the cone, showing the dimensions of the base radius, the height, and the slant height.

- Draw the section plane and the true shape of the section, which can be a circle, an ellipse, a parabola, or a hyperbola.

- Draw an arc of radius equal to the slant height of the cone, with the center at the vertex of the cone.

- Divide the base of the cone into equal parts, and mark the points on the arc corresponding to the divisions.

- Join the points on the arc to the vertex of the cone, forming the sector.

- Mark the points on the arc corresponding to the intersection of the section plane and the base of the cone.

- Join the points on the arc to the vertex of the cone, forming the segment.

- Cut out the segment and fold it along the radial lines to form the sectioned cone.
