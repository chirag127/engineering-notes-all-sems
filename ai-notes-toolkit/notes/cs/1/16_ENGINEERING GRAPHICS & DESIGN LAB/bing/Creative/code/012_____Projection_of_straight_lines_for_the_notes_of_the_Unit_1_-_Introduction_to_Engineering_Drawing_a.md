# Projection of Straight Lines

## Introduction

A straight line is the shortest distance between two points. It can be represented by a line segment with two end points. A projection of a straight line is the image of the line on a plane or a surface. The projection of a straight line depends on the position and orientation of the line with respect to the plane or the surface.

## Types of Projections

There are two types of projections of straight lines:

- Orthographic projection: It is the projection of a straight line on a plane that is perpendicular to the line of sight. The orthographic projection of a straight line is a point or a line segment, depending on whether the line is parallel or inclined to the plane.
- Oblique projection: It is the projection of a straight line on a plane that is not perpendicular to the line of sight. The oblique projection of a straight line is a line segment, regardless of the orientation of the line.

## Terminology

Some terms related to the projection of straight lines are:

- Projector: It is a line that joins a point on the straight line to its projection on the plane or the surface.
- True length: It is the actual length of the straight line, measured along the line.
- Apparent length: It is the length of the projection of the straight line on the plane or the surface, measured along the projection.
- True inclination: It is the angle between the straight line and a reference plane or direction, measured in the plane containing the line and the reference.
- Apparent inclination: It is the angle between the projection of the straight line and a reference plane or direction, measured in the plane of projection.

## Methods of Projection

There are two methods of projection of straight lines:

- First angle projection: It is the method of projection in which the object is placed in the first quadrant, between the observer and the planes of projection. The planes of projection are assumed to be transparent and folded to form a single plane. The projection of the object on the vertical plane is placed below the projection of the object on the horizontal plane.
- Third angle projection: It is the method of projection in which the object is placed in the third quadrant, behind the planes of projection. The planes of projection are assumed to be opaque and unfolded to form a single plane. The projection of the object on the vertical plane is placed above the projection of the object on the horizontal plane.

## Steps of Projection

The steps of projection of a straight line are:

- Draw the projections of the end points of the straight line on the horizontal and the vertical planes, using the first or the third angle projection method.
- Join the projections of the end points on each plane to obtain the projections of the straight line on the horizontal and the vertical planes.
- Measure the apparent lengths and the apparent inclinations of the projections of the straight line on the horizontal and the vertical planes.
- Compare the apparent lengths and the apparent inclinations with the true length and the true inclination of the straight line to determine the position and the orientation of the straight line with respect to the planes of projection.
