- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees.
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the general shape and dimensions of an object, but it does not preserve the true lengths or angles of the object.
- To draw an isometric projection, an isometric scale is needed to convert the actual lengths of the object to the corresponding lengths on the drawing.
- The isometric scale is constructed as follows:
  - Draw a horizontal line OA.
  - Through O draw OP and OQ at 30º and 45º to the horizontal respectively.
  - On OQ mark the actual scale in mm.
  - Draw verticals from each of the division points on the actual scale to cut OP at the corresponding divisions.
  - The points on OP are the isometric scale divisions.
- The isometric scale can be used to measure the isometric lengths of the object by placing it parallel to the isometric axes.
- The isometric scale is also used to draw circles and arcs in isometric projection by using a compass or a template.
- The isometric scale is convenient and practical for drawing isometric projections of simple objects, but it may not be suitable for complex or large objects.
- In such cases, other methods of isometric projection, such as using a grid or a CAD software, may be preferred .
