### Fillet

- A fillet is a curved surface that connects two adjacent faces or edges of a solid object, such as a mechanical part or a 3D model.
- Fillets are used to smooth sharp corners, reduce stress concentrations, improve aesthetics, and facilitate manufacturing processes such as casting, molding, and machining.
- Fillets can be classified into two types: internal and external. Internal fillets are concave curves that connect two interior faces or edges, while external fillets are convex curves that connect two exterior faces or edges.
- Fillets can be dimensioned in technical drawings using the radius (R) of the curve and the number of fillets (if they are symmetrical or equal) as shown below:

![Fillet dimensioning example](https://mechanicalland.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/How-To-Show-Fillets-And-Rounds-In-Technical-Drawings-1.jpg)

- Fillets can be quickly designed onto parts using 3D solid modeling engineering CAD software by invoking the function and picking edges of interest.
- Fillets are commonly used geometrical features in technical drawings. In most of the technical drawings created, fillets are generally of the same size. Instead of showing all the fillets with individual arrows and dimensions, you can indicate it on a side of the technical drawing page such as “FILLETS R4 UNLESS OTHERWISE INDICATED”.
