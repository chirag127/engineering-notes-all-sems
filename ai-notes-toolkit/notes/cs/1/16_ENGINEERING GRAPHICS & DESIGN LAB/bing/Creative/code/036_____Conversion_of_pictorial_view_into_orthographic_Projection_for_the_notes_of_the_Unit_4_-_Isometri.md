### Conversion of pictorial view into orthographic projection

- A pictorial view is a 3-D view of an object that shows how it looks in reality. There are three types of pictorial views: perspective, isometric and oblique.
- An orthographic projection is a 2-D view of an object that shows only one side or face of the object at a time. There are usually three orthographic views: front, top and right.
- To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, you need to follow these steps:
  - Identify the principal planes of projection, which are the vertical, horizontal and profile planes that are perpendicular to each other and intersect at a point called the station point.
  - Align the object with the principal planes of projection, such that the front view is parallel to the vertical plane, the top view is parallel to the horizontal plane and the right view is parallel to the profile plane.
  - Project the object onto the principal planes of projection, using parallel lines that are perpendicular to the planes. These lines are called projectors or projection lines.
  - Transfer the projected views onto a drawing sheet, using a suitable scale and orientation. The front view is usually placed at the center, the top view is above the front view and the right view is to the right of the front view.
  - Add dimensions, labels, symbols and notes to the orthographic views, following the standards and conventions of engineering drawing.
