### Sections of Solids

- A section of a solid is the shape of the surface obtained by cutting the solid by a plane, called the section plane or cutting plane.
- Sections of solids are used to show the hidden or internal parts of an object in technical drawings.
- Sections of solids can also help to find the true shape of the cut surface, the volume and the centroid of the solid.
- Some common types of solids are prisms, pyramids, cylinders and cones. They can be cut by different types of section planes, such as parallel, perpendicular, inclined or oblique.
- To draw the sectional view of a solid, the following steps are followed:

  - Draw the projections of the solid in the given position and orientation.
  - Draw the section plane in the front view and the top view, and mark the points where it intersects the edges of the solid.
  - Project the sectional points from the front view to the top view, or vice versa, depending on the given view.
  - Draw the outline of the cut surface in the given view, and shade it with diagonal lines to indicate the section.
  - Draw the visible parts of the solid in the other view, and erase the hidden lines behind the section plane.
  - Label the sectional view and the section plane with letters.

- To find the true shape of the section, the following steps are followed:

  - Draw the projections of the solid and the section plane as before.
  - Draw a line perpendicular to the section plane from any point on the cut surface, and mark the point where it meets the plane. This is called the hinge line or the reference line.
  - Imagine folding the solid along the hinge line, so that the cut surface coincides with the section plane.
  - Draw the true shape of the section on the section plane, using the distances of the sectional points from the hinge line as radii.
  - Label the true shape of the section with the same letters as the sectional view.
