### Engineering Scales

- Engineering scales are used to measure and represent the dimensions of objects in engineering drawings.
- Engineering scales are based on the principle of similarity, which states that two similar figures have proportional lengths, areas and volumes.
- Engineering scales are expressed as ratios, such as 1:10, 1:50, 1:100, etc. The ratio indicates the relationship between the actual size of the object and the size of its representation on the drawing.
- Engineering scales can be classified into three types: plain scales, diagonal scales and vernier scales.
- Plain scales are used to measure and represent linear dimensions, such as length, width and height. Plain scales have two parts: a primary scale and a secondary scale. The primary scale shows the main divisions and the secondary scale shows the sub-divisions.
- Diagonal scales are used to measure and represent fractional dimensions, such as 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, etc. Diagonal scales have three parts: a primary scale, a secondary scale and a diagonal line. The primary scale shows the main divisions, the secondary scale shows the sub-divisions and the diagonal line shows the fractional parts.
- Vernier scales are used to measure and represent very small dimensions, such as 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, etc. Vernier scales have two parts: a main scale and a vernier scale. The main scale shows the main divisions and the vernier scale shows the sub-divisions. The vernier scale slides along the main scale and has a zero mark that coincides with one of the main scale divisions. The difference between the main scale division and the vernier scale division gives the exact measurement.
