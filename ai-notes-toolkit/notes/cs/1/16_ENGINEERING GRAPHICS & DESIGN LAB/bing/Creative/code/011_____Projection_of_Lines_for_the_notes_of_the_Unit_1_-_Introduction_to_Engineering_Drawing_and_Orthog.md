### Projection of Lines

- A projection of a line is a geometrically represented image of the line obtained on a plane or surface.
- A line can be projected on different planes, such as the horizontal plane (HP), the vertical plane (VP), the profile plane (PP), or the auxiliary plane (AP).
- The projection of a line on a plane depends on the position and orientation of the line relative to the plane.
- The projection of a line can be classified into three types based on the angle between the line and the plane:
  - Parallel projection: when the line is parallel to the plane, the projection is equal to the true length of the line.
  - Perpendicular projection: when the line is perpendicular to the plane, the projection is a point.
  - Inclined projection: when the line is inclined to the plane, the projection is shorter than the true length of the line.
- The projection of a line can also be classified into four types based on the angle between the line and the reference planes (HP and VP):
  - First angle projection: when the line is inclined to both HP and VP, the projection is called a first angle projection. The line is in the first quadrant of the space.
  - Second angle projection: when the line is inclined to HP and parallel to VP, the projection is called a second angle projection. The line is in the second quadrant of the space.
  - Third angle projection: when the line is inclined to VP and parallel to HP, the projection is called a third angle projection. The line is in the third quadrant of the space.
  - Fourth angle projection: when the line is parallel to both HP and VP, the projection is called a fourth angle projection. The line is in the fourth quadrant of the space.
- The projection of a line can be drawn using the following steps:
  - Draw the reference planes (HP and VP) and the line of intersection (XY) between them.
  - Locate the end points of the line on the reference planes using the given distances from XY.
  - Draw the projectors from the end points of the line to the plane of projection (HP or VP).
  - Mark the points where the projectors intersect the plane of projection as the end points of the projected line.
  - Join the end points of the projected line with a straight line.
  - Label the projected line and its end points with appropriate symbols and dimensions.
