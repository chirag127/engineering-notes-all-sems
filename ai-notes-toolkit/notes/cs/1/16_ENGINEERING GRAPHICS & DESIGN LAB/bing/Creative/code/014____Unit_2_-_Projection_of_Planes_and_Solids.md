# Unit 2 - Projection of Planes and Solids

## Introduction

- A plane is a two dimensional object having length and breadth only. Its thickness is always neglected. Various shapes of plane figures are considered such as square, rectangle, circle, pentagon, hexagon, etc. 
- A solid is a three dimensional object having length, breadth and thickness. It is completely bounded by a surface or surfaces, which may be curved or plane. The shape of a solid is described orthographically by drawing its two orthographic projections, usually, on the two principal planes of projection i.e., HP and VP. 
- Projection of planes and solids is the process of representing their true shape and size on a plane of projection, such as a sheet of paper, by using a method of orthographic projection. 

## Types of Planes and Solids

- Planes can be classified into two types: regular and irregular. Regular planes are those whose sides are equal and angles are right angles, such as square, rectangle, etc. Irregular planes are those whose sides are unequal and angles are not right angles, such as triangle, trapezium, etc. 
- Solids can be classified into two types: polyhedra and solids of revolution. Polyhedra are solids whose faces are plane polygons, such as cube, prism, pyramid, etc. Solids of revolution are solids obtained by rotating a plane figure about an axis, such as cylinder, cone, sphere, etc. 

## Types of Positions of Planes and Solids

- Planes and solids can be placed in different positions with respect to the principal planes of projection (HP and VP). The positions can be classified into three types: perpendicular, parallel and inclined.  
- Perpendicular position: A plane or a solid is said to be in a perpendicular position when its surface or axis is perpendicular to one of the principal planes and parallel to the other. In this position, the projection on the parallel plane shows the true shape and size of the plane or the base of the solid, while the projection on the perpendicular plane shows the true thickness of the plane or the height of the solid.  
- Parallel position: A plane or a solid is said to be in a parallel position when its surface or axis is parallel to both the principal planes. In this position, the projections on both the planes show the true shape and size of the plane or the base of the solid, but they are foreshortened.  
- Inclined position: A plane or a solid is said to be in an inclined position when its surface or axis is inclined to both the principal planes. In this position, the projections on both the planes are foreshortened and do not show the true shape and size of the plane or the base of the solid.  

## Methods of Projection of Planes and Solids

- There are two methods of projection of planes and solids: first angle projection and third angle projection. 
- First angle projection: In this method, the object is placed in the first quadrant, i.e., between the observer and the planes of projection. The front view is projected on the VP and the top view is projected on the HP. The views are drawn as if the planes of projection are folded towards the observer. 
- Third angle projection: In this method, the object is placed in the third quadrant, i.e., behind the planes of projection. The front view is projected on the VP and the top view is projected on the HP. The views are drawn as if the planes of projection are unfolded away from the observer.
