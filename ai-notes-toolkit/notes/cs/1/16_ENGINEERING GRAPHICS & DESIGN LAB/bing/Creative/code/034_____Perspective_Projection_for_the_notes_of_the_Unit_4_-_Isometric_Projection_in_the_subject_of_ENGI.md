### Perspective Projection

- Perspective projection is a type of projection that shows a three-dimensional object as it appears to the human eye from a certain point of view.
- Perspective projection is used to create realistic and artistic drawings of objects and scenes, but it is not suitable for engineering drawings that require accurate dimensions and shapes.
- Perspective projection is based on the principle that the lines of projection from the object to the eye (or the center of projection) are not parallel, but converge at a single point.
- The center of projection can be at a finite distance from the object (one-point, two-point, or three-point perspective) or at an infinite distance (parallel perspective).
- The distance between the center of projection and the projection plane (or the picture plane) determines the degree of foreshortening and distortion of the object in the perspective projection.
- The closer the center of projection is to the projection plane, the more distorted and exaggerated the object appears. The farther the center of projection is from the projection plane, the more realistic and natural the object appears.
- The perspective projection can be classified into two types: linear perspective and curvilinear perspective.
- Linear perspective is the most common type of perspective projection, where the projection plane is a flat surface and the lines of projection are straight.
- Curvilinear perspective is a type of perspective projection where the projection plane is a curved surface and the lines of projection are curved. This type of perspective projection can create a wide-angle or a fish-eye effect.
