### Diagonal Scale

A diagonal scale is a type of graphical scale that is used to measure small fractions of the unit of measurement in engineering and cartographic drawings. It can represent a unit into three different multiples, such as meters, centimeters, and millimeters, or other units accordingly. It is based on the principle of similar triangles, where the ratio of corresponding sides is constant.

The steps involved in drawing a diagonal scale are:

- Finding the length of the scale, which is equal to the product of the largest unit and the number of divisions required.
- Drawing a horizontal line and dividing it into equal parts, each representing the largest unit.
- Drawing a vertical line at the left end of the horizontal line and dividing it into equal parts, each representing the smallest unit.
- Drawing a vertical line at the right end of the horizontal line and extending it above the horizontal line.
- Joining the points from the subdivisions on the left vertical line to the successive next subdivisions on the right vertical line, forming diagonal lines.
- Labeling the scale with the units and the values.

- Locating the point on the diagonal line that corresponds to the next largest unit of the length.
