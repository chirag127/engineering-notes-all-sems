### Scales for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- A scale is a ratio that compares the dimensions of a drawing to the actual dimensions of the object.
- A scale can be expressed as a fraction, a decimal, or a verbal statement.
- A scale can be used to reduce or enlarge the size of a drawing, depending on the purpose and the available space.
- A scale can also be used to measure distances and dimensions on a drawing, by using a scale ruler or a scale factor.
- There are different types of scales, such as:

  - Representative fraction (RF) scale: This is a scale that uses a fraction to express the ratio between the drawing and the object. For example, 1:50 means that one unit on the drawing represents 50 units on the object.
  - Plain scale: This is a scale that uses a single unit of measurement, such as millimeters, centimeters, or meters, to express the ratio between the drawing and the object. For example, 1 cm = 1 m means that one centimeter on the drawing represents one meter on the object.
  - Diagonal scale: This is a scale that uses a diagonal line to divide a plain scale into smaller units of measurement, such as millimeters or fractions of a centimeter. For example, a diagonal scale of 1 cm = 1 m can be divided into 10 equal parts, each representing 10 cm on the object.
  - Vernier scale: This is a scale that uses a sliding scale to measure fractions of a unit on a plain scale, such as tenths or hundredths of a centimeter. For example, a vernier scale of 1 cm = 1 m can measure up to 0.01 cm on the object.
  - Comparative scale: This is a scale that compares the dimensions of two or more objects or drawings, using the same unit of measurement. For example, a comparative scale of 1 cm = 1 m and 1 cm = 2 m can show the relative sizes of two objects or drawings that have different scales.

- To choose an appropriate scale for a drawing, some factors to consider are:

  - The size and shape of the object
  - The purpose and function of the drawing
  - The available space and paper size
  - The level of detail and accuracy required
  - The standard conventions and practices of the field or discipline

- To draw a scale on a drawing, some steps to follow are:

  - Decide the type and value of the scale
  - Mark the origin and the length of the scale on the drawing
  - Divide the scale into equal or proportional parts, depending on the type of scale
  - Label the scale with the unit of measurement and the ratio
  - Use the scale to measure or draw distances and dimensions on the drawing
