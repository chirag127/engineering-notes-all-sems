### Projection of Point

- Projection in engineering drawing is a technique for representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional drawing.
- A point is the simplest geometric element that has no dimensions, only a position in space.
- A plane of projection (i.e., an image or picture plane) is an imaginary flat plane upon which the image is projected.
- The projection is produced by connecting the points where the lines of sight pierce the projection plane. As a result, the 3D object is transformed into a 2D view.
- If the distance from the observer to the object is infinite, then the projection lines are assumed to be parallel, and the projection is called a parallel projection.
- Parallel projections can be classified into orthographic projections and oblique projections, depending on the orientation of the projection plane and the projection lines.
- Orthographic projections are those in which the projection plane is perpendicular to the projection lines. Oblique projections are those in which the projection plane is inclined to the projection lines.
- Orthographic projections are further divided into multiview projections and axonometric projections, depending on the number and arrangement of the projection planes.
- Multiview projections are those in which the object is projected onto two or more mutually perpendicular planes, such as the front, top, and side views. Axonometric projections are those in which the object is projected onto a single plane that is inclined to all three principal axes of the object, such as the isometric, dimetric, and trimetric views.
- To project a point onto a plane, a perpendicular line is drawn from the point to the plane. The point where the line intersects the plane is the projection of the point.
- The projection of a point can be represented by a small circle or a dot on the plane.
- The projection of a point can vary depending on the position of the point, the orientation of the plane, and the type of projection.
- For example, in an orthographic projection, the projection of a point on the front view is the same as its x and y coordinates, and the projection of a point on the top view is the same as its x and z coordinates.
- In an oblique projection, the projection of a point on the plane is obtained by applying a scaling factor to its depth coordinate, depending on the angle of the projection.
- In an axonometric projection, the projection of a point on the plane is obtained by applying a rotation matrix to its coordinates, depending on the angles of the axes.
