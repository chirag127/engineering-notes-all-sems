### Diagonal

- A diagonal is a line segment that joins two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon or a polyhedron.
- In engineering drawing, diagonals are used to check the accuracy of the dimensions and angles of a figure.
- Diagonals are also used to divide a figure into equal parts or to locate the center of a circle or an arc.
- To draw a diagonal, a ruler and a compass are required.
- The steps to draw a diagonal are as follows:
  - Mark the vertices of the figure with letters, such as A, B, C, D, etc.
  - Choose any two non-adjacent vertices, such as A and C, and draw a line segment AC using a ruler.
  - Measure the length of AC using a ruler and mark the midpoint M using a compass.
  - Repeat the same process for any other pair of non-adjacent vertices, such as B and D, and draw a line segment BD using a ruler.
  - Measure the length of BD using a ruler and mark the midpoint N using a compass.
  - The point where the two line segments AC and BD intersect is the diagonal point P.
  - Label the point P and draw a circle with P as the center and PM or PN as the radius using a compass.
  - The circle is the diagonal circle and it passes through all the vertices of the figure.
  - The line segments AP, BP, CP, and DP are the diagonals of the figure.
