### Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A right regular solid is a solid whose base is a regular polygon and whose axis is perpendicular to the base. Examples of right regular solids are prisms, cylinders, pyramids, and cones.
- A section is a view of an object as if it were cut by a plane. A sectional view shows the internal details of the object that are hidden in the normal views.
- An auxiliary view is a view that is projected from an existing view along a direction that is not parallel to any of the principal planes. An auxiliary view shows the true shape and size of a surface that is inclined or oblique to the principal planes.
- To draw an auxiliary view of a section of a right regular solid, such as a prism, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the front, top, and right side views of the solid in orthographic projection.
  - Draw the cutting plane on the front view and project it to the top and right side views.
  - Draw the sectional view of the solid on the front view by hatching the cut area and removing the hidden lines.
  - Identify the reference edge on the sectional view that is parallel to the inclined surface of the solid. This edge will be the folding line for the auxiliary view.
  - Draw a line perpendicular to the reference edge and extend it beyond the sectional view. This line will be the auxiliary plane for the auxiliary view.
  - Project the points on the sectional view along the auxiliary plane and mark their distances from the reference edge. These points will be the vertices of the auxiliary view.
  - Connect the vertices of the auxiliary view with straight lines to form the true shape of the section. Label the auxiliary view as A-A.
  - Draw the dimension and annotation lines for the auxiliary view.
