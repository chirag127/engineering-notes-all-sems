### Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees .
- Isometric projection is intended to combine the illusion of depth with the clarity and accuracy of a plan view.
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the general shape and dimensions of an object, but it does not preserve the true lengths or angles of the object.
- Isometric projection can be drawn by using an isometric grid or by using a 30-degree set square and a compass.
- There are two methods of isometric projection: isometric scale method and full size method.
  - Isometric scale method: In this method, the true lengths of the object are reduced by a factor of 0.816 to obtain the isometric lengths. The isometric scale can be constructed by drawing a line at 30 degrees to the horizontal and dividing it into equal parts. Then, perpendiculars are drawn from each division point to the horizontal line. The distances between the perpendiculars are the isometric lengths corresponding to the true lengths.
  - Full size method: In this method, the true lengths of the object are transferred directly to the isometric axes without any reduction. The isometric axes are drawn at 30 degrees to the horizontal and the true lengths are measured along them. This method is simpler and faster than the isometric scale method, but it results in a distorted image of the object.
- Some advantages of isometric projection are :
  - It gives a realistic view of the object.
  - It shows all the three dimensions of the object in one view.
  - It is easy to draw and understand.
  - It can be used to show hidden details and cut sections of the object.
- Some examples of isometric projection are :
  - A cube: A cube can be drawn in isometric projection by drawing a rhombus with 120-degree angles and then drawing four parallel lines from each vertex to form the other faces of the cube.
  - A cylinder: A cylinder can be drawn in isometric projection by drawing two ellipses with the same major axis and then connecting them with two parallel lines to form the curved surface of the cylinder.
  - A cone: A cone can be drawn in isometric projection by drawing an ellipse with the major axis equal to the diameter of the base of the cone and then drawing a line from the center of the ellipse to the vertex of the cone. The line should be perpendicular to the plane of the ellipse.
