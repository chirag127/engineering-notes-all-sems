### Cylinder

- A cylinder is a solid with two circular bases of equal size and shape that are parallel to each other and connected by a curved surface.
- The axis of a cylinder is the line joining the centers of the bases. The height of a cylinder is the distance between the bases.
- The lateral surface of a cylinder is the curved surface that connects the bases. The lateral area of a cylinder is the area of the lateral surface.
- The total surface area of a cylinder is the sum of the areas of the bases and the lateral surface.

### Sections of Cylinder

- A section of a cylinder is the intersection of the cylinder with a plane. The shape of the section depends on the position and orientation of the plane.
- If the plane is perpendicular to the axis of the cylinder, the section is a circle. This is called a right section or a normal section.
- If the plane is parallel to the axis of the cylinder, the section is a rectangle. This is called a parallel section.
- If the plane is inclined to the axis of the cylinder, the section is an ellipse. This is called an oblique section or a non-normal section.
- If the plane passes through the axis of the cylinder, the section is a semi-circle. This is called a diametral section.

### Development of Cylinder

- The development of a cylinder is the process of unfolding the lateral surface of the cylinder on a plane. The development of a cylinder is a rectangle whose length is equal to the circumference of the base and whose width is equal to the height of the cylinder.
- To draw the development of a cylinder, follow these steps:
  - Draw a circle representing the base of the cylinder and divide it into equal parts. The number of parts depends on the accuracy required. More parts will give a more accurate development.
  - Draw a horizontal line and mark a point A on it. From point A, draw a vertical line equal to the height of the cylinder and mark the end point B.
  - From point A, draw a horizontal line equal to the circumference of the base and mark the end point C. Join BC to complete the rectangle ABCD.
  - Transfer the divisions of the circle to the line AC by measuring the arc lengths with a compass. Label the points as 1, 2, 3, ..., n.
  - Draw perpendicular lines from the points 1, 2, 3, ..., n to the line BD and label the points as 1', 2', 3', ..., n'.
  - The rectangle ABCD is the development of the cylinder. The points 1, 2, 3, ..., n and 1', 2', 3', ..., n' correspond to the points on the edge of the lateral surface of the cylinder.
