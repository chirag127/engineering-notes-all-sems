### Orthographic representation of perspective views — Plane figures and simple solids — Visual Ray Method

- Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions by using parallel lines that are perpendicular to the projection plane .
- Perspective projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions by using converging lines that meet at a point of perspective .
- Orthographic representation of perspective views is the process of converting a perspective view of an object into an orthographic view by using visual rays.
- Visual rays are imaginary lines that connect the points on the object to the point of perspective.
- Plane figures are two-dimensional shapes that have length and width, such as circles, triangles, squares, etc.
- Simple solids are three-dimensional shapes that have length, width and height, such as cubes, spheres, cylinders, cones, etc.
- To draw an orthographic representation of a perspective view of a plane figure or a simple solid, follow these steps:
  - Identify the point of perspective and the projection plane in the perspective view.
  - Draw visual rays from the point of perspective to the vertices or edges of the figure or solid.
  - Mark the intersections of the visual rays and the projection plane as the corresponding vertices or edges of the figure or solid in the orthographic view.
  - Connect the vertices or edges in the orthographic view to form the shape of the figure or solid.
  - Label the orthographic view with the name of the figure or solid and the dimensions if given.
