### Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is commonly used in engineering graphics to communicate the shape and dimensions of an object. Orthographic projection is also known as orthogonal projection.

Some points to note about orthographic projection are:

- Orthographic projection is a form of parallel projection, in which the projection lines are perpendicular to the projection plane.
- Orthographic projection consists of three principal views: the front view, the top view, and the right view. These views are also called the principal planes of projection.
- The front view shows the object as seen from the front, the top view shows the object as seen from the top, and the right view shows the object as seen from the right.
- The three views are aligned with each other along the horizontal and vertical axes. The front view is placed below the top view, and the right view is placed to the right of the front view.
- The three views are drawn to the same scale and show the true shape and size of the object.
- The three views are sufficient to represent the object completely, unless the object has hidden features or curved surfaces. In that case, additional views or auxiliary views may be required.
- The three views are also called the orthographic views or the orthographic projections of the object.
