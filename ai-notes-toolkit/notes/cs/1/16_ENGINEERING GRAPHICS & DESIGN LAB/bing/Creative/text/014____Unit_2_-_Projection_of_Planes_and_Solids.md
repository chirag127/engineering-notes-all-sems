## Unit 2 - Projection of Planes and Solids

- A plane is a two dimensional object having length and breadth only. Its thickness is always neglected. Various shapes of plane figures are considered such as square, rectangle, circle, pentagon, hexagon, etc.
- A solid is a three dimensional object having length, breadth and thickness. It is completely bounded by a surface or surfaces, which may be curved or plane. The shape of a solid is described orthographically by drawing its two orthographic projections, usually, on the two principal planes of projection i.e., HP and VP.
- The projection of a plane or a solid is the image or picture of the object on a plane of projection, which is an imaginary flat plane upon which the image is projected. The projection is produced by connecting the points where the lines of sight pierce the projection plane.
- The projection of a plane or a solid depends on its position and orientation with respect to the planes of projection. There are different types of positions, such as perpendicular, parallel, inclined, oblique, etc.
- The projection of a plane or a solid also depends on the type of projection method used, such as first angle projection or third angle projection. In first angle projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant, between the observer and the planes of projection. In third angle projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant, behind the planes of projection.
- The projection of a plane or a solid may show the true shape and size of the object or its base, or it may show an elliptical or foreshortened shape, depending on the angle of inclination of the object or its axis with respect to the planes of projection .
- The projection of a plane or a solid may also require section views, which are obtained by cutting the object by a cutting plane and removing the portion of the object in front of the cutting plane. Section views are used to show the internal details of the object, such as holes, cavities, ribs, etc.
- The projection of a plane or a solid follows certain rules and conventions, such as naming the views, drawing the center lines, hidden lines, cutting plane lines, section lines, etc. The projection of a plane or a solid also requires proper scaling, dimensioning, and labeling of the views .
