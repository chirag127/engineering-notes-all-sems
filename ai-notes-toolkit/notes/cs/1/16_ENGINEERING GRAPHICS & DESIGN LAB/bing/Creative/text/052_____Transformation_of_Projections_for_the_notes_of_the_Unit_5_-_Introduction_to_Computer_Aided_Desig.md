### Transformation of Projections

- A projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional (3D) object on a two-dimensional (2D) plane, such as a sheet of paper or a computer screen.
- A projection is obtained by drawing lines of sight from an observer's eye to the points on the object and marking the points where these lines intersect the projection plane.
- A projection can be classified into two types: parallel and perspective, depending on the distance and orientation of the observer from the object.
- A parallel projection is a projection where the lines of sight are assumed to be parallel, and the projection plane is perpendicular to a principal axis of the object. Parallel projections are commonly used in engineering and technical drawings, as they preserve the true shape and size of the object.
- A perspective projection is a projection where the lines of sight converge at a single point, called the center of projection or the vanishing point. Perspective projections are more realistic and natural, as they mimic how the human eye perceives the 3D world.
- A transformation of projections is a process of changing the orientation or position of the projection plane or the object, resulting in a different view of the object. Transformation of projections can be used to show different aspects of the object, such as the front, top, side, or isometric views.
- A transformation of projections can be performed by applying a matrix multiplication to the coordinates of the object, using a specific projection matrix that depends on the type and parameters of the projection.
- A projection matrix is a 4x4 matrix that maps the homogeneous coordinates of a 3D point to the homogeneous coordinates of a 2D point on the projection plane.
- A projection matrix can be derived by applying the principles of similar triangles, using the ratios of the corresponding sides of the triangles formed by the lines of sight and the projection plane.
- A projection matrix can be decomposed into three components: a scaling matrix, a shear matrix, and a perspective matrix, each representing a different aspect of the projection.
- A scaling matrix is a matrix that scales the coordinates of the object by a factor, depending on the distance of the object from the projection plane.
- A shear matrix is a matrix that shifts the coordinates of the object by an amount, depending on the angle of the projection plane with respect to the principal axes of the object.
- A perspective matrix is a matrix that divides the coordinates of the object by a factor, depending on the distance of the object from the center of projection.
- A transformation of projections can be classified into four types: orthographic, oblique, axonometric, and perspective, depending on the type and parameters of the projection.
- An orthographic projection is a parallel projection where the projection plane is perpendicular to one of the principal axes of the object, and the lines of sight are parallel to the other two axes. Orthographic projections are simple and easy to construct, as they do not involve any scaling, shearing, or perspective effects.
- An oblique projection is a parallel projection where the projection plane is perpendicular to one of the principal axes of the object, but the lines of sight are not parallel to the other two axes. Oblique projections are more expressive and dynamic than orthographic projections, as they show more sides of the object, but they also distort the shape and size of the object.
- An axonometric projection is a parallel projection where the projection plane is not perpendicular to any of the principal axes of the object, and the lines of sight are equally inclined to all three axes. Axonometric projections are more realistic and balanced than oblique projections, as they show the object in a 3D perspective, but they also preserve the proportions and angles of the object.
- A perspective projection is a projection where the lines of sight converge at a single point, called the center of projection or the vanishing point. Perspective projections are the most natural and realistic way of representing a 3D object, as they mimic how the human eye perceives the 3D world, but they also introduce foreshortening and distortion effects.
