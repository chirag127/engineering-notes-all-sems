### Dimensioning

- Dimensioning is the process of adding measurements and other information to a drawing to specify the size, shape, location, and features of an object.
- Dimensioning is essential for communicating the design intent and ensuring the accuracy and functionality of the product.
- Dimensioning follows certain rules and conventions that are widely accepted and standardized by various organizations, such as ANSI, ISO, ASME, etc.
- Dimensioning consists of four elements: dimension lines, extension lines, dimension values, and dimension symbols.

  - Dimension lines are thin, solid lines that indicate the direction and extent of a dimension. They are terminated by arrowheads or tick marks at both ends.
  - Extension lines are thin, solid lines that extend from the outline of the object to the dimension line. They indicate the points or features that are being dimensioned. They should not touch the object or cross the dimension lines.
  - Dimension values are numerical values that indicate the size or distance of a dimension. They are usually placed above or below the dimension line, and aligned with it. They may be accompanied by units, prefixes, suffixes, or tolerances.
  - Dimension symbols are graphical symbols that provide additional information about a dimension, such as the type, method, or reference of measurement. They are usually placed before, after, or above the dimension value.

- Dimensioning should follow the principles of clarity, completeness, consistency, and economy.

  - Clarity means that the dimensions should be easy to read and understand, without ambiguity or confusion. The dimensions should be placed in the most appropriate view, with adequate spacing and orientation. The dimensions should not be repeated, unless necessary for clarity.
  - Completeness means that the dimensions should provide all the information needed to manufacture and inspect the product, without leaving any gaps or assumptions. The dimensions should cover all the features and details of the object, and indicate the tolerances and specifications required.
  - Consistency means that the dimensions should follow the same rules and conventions throughout the drawing, and conform to the standards and practices of the industry or field. The dimensions should use the same units, symbols, formats, and styles, and avoid any contradictions or conflicts.
  - Economy means that the dimensions should be as few and simple as possible, without compromising the clarity and completeness. The dimensions should avoid unnecessary or redundant information, and use the most efficient and effective methods of dimensioning.
