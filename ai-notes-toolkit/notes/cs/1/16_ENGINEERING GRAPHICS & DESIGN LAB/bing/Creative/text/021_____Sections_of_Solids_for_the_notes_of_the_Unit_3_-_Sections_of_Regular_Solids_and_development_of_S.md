### Sections of Solids

- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid by a plane, called the section plane or cutting plane, and removing the part of the solid between the plane and the observer.
- Sections of solids are used to show the hidden or internal features of an object in technical drawings.
- Sections of solids can also help to find the true shape of the cut surface, the volume and the centroid of the solid.
- The most common solids used in engineering graphics are prisms, pyramids, cylinders and cones.
- The section plane can be perpendicular or inclined to one or both of the principal planes (HP and VP).
- The true shape of the section is the shape of the cut surface as seen from a direction perpendicular to the section plane.
- The true shape of the section can be obtained by projecting the sectional points on the section plane to a plane parallel to it.
- The true shape of the section can also be used to locate the position of the section plane, if the sectional points are known.
- The following steps are generally followed to draw the sectional views of solids   :

  1. Draw the line XY, which represents the trace of the section plane on the principal plane.
  2. Draw the top view and the front view of the solid, and name the corners or points of the solid.
  3. Draw projectors from each corner or point of the solid through XY, and mark the points where they intersect the section plane.
  4. Draw the sectional view of the solid by joining the sectional points in the order of their appearance in the top view or the front view.
  5. Draw the true shape of the section by projecting the sectional points on a plane parallel to the section plane, and joining them in the same order as in the sectional view.
  6. Shade the sectional view and the true shape of the section with diagonal lines to indicate the cut surface.
