# Circle

A circle is a two-dimensional shape that consists of all the points that are equidistant from a fixed point called the center. The distance from the center to any point on the circle is called the radius. The longest chord of a circle is called the diameter, which is twice the radius. A circle has no corners or edges and is perfectly symmetrical.

Some of the properties of a circle are:

- The circumference of a circle is the perimeter or the length of the boundary of the circle. It is given by the formula C = 2πr, where r is the radius and π is a constant approximately equal to 3.14.
- The area of a circle is the region enclosed by the circle. It is given by the formula A = πr^2, where r is the radius and π is a constant approximately to 3.14.
- The angle subtended by an arc at the center of a circle is twice the angle subtended by the same arc at any point on the circle. This is called the central angle theorem.
- The angle subtended by a semicircle at any point on the circle is a right angle. This is called the inscribed angle theorem.
- The perpendicular from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. This is called the perpendicular bisector theorem.
- Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the center and at any point on the circle. This is called the congruent chords theorem.
- The sum of the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral (a quadrilateral whose vertices lie on a circle) is 180 degrees. This is called the cyclic quadrilateral theorem.

Some examples of circles in real life are:
