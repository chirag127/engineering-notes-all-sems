# Projection of Solids like Prisms

- A solid is a three-dimensional object that has length, breadth and height.
- A prism is a solid that has two congruent and parallel faces called bases and the other faces are parallelograms called lateral faces.
- The projection of a solid is the representation of its shape and size on a plane surface, such as a sheet of paper.
- The projection of a solid can be obtained by using orthographic projection or isometric projection methods.
- Orthographic projection is a method of drawing the front view, top view and side view of a solid on three mutually perpendicular planes, called the principal planes of projection.
- Isometric projection is a method of drawing a three-dimensional solid on a plane surface, such that the three axes of the solid are equally inclined to the plane of projection and the scale along each axis is the same.
- The projection of a solid like a prism can be obtained by following these steps:

  - Draw the front view of the prism by projecting its base on the vertical plane (VP) and its height on the horizontal plane (HP).
  - Draw the top view of the prism by projecting its base on the HP and its height on the VP.
  - Draw the side view of the prism by projecting its base on the profile plane (PP) and its height on the VP.
  - If the axis of the prism is inclined to one of the principal planes, use the change of position method to rotate the prism about an axis perpendicular to the plane of inclination until the axis becomes parallel to the plane.
  - If the prism is cut by a plane inclined to one of the principal planes, draw the true shape of the section by projecting the intersection points of the cutting plane and the lateral faces of the prism on a plane perpendicular to the cutting plane.
  - If the prism is drawn in isometric projection, use the isometric scale to measure the dimensions of the prism along the isometric axes and draw the isometric outline of the prism by connecting the corresponding vertices.
