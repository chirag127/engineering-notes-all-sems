# Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism

- A right regular solid is a three-dimensional solid whose faces are congruent regular polygons and whose edges are perpendicular to the faces.
- Examples of right regular solids are prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, and spheres.
- A prism is a right regular solid with two parallel and congruent polygonal bases and rectangular lateral faces.
- An auxiliary view is a view that shows the true shape and size of a surface that is not parallel to any of the principal planes of projection.
- An auxiliary view is projected from an existing view, usually the front, top, or side view, along a reference line that is perpendicular to the inclined surface.
- An auxiliary view can be symmetrical or unilateral, depending on whether the reference line divides the inclined surface into two equal parts or not.
- An auxiliary view can be used to show the true shape of the section of a prism that is cut by a plane that is not parallel to any of the principal planes of projection.
- To draw an auxiliary view of the section of a prism, follow these steps:
  - Draw the front, top, and side views of the prism, showing the cutting plane in each view.
  - Draw a reference line perpendicular to the cutting plane in the front view, and extend it to the top and side views.
  - Project the points where the cutting plane intersects the edges of the prism from the front view to the top and side views, and connect them to form the apparent shape of the section in each view.
  - Draw an auxiliary plane perpendicular to the reference line, and project the points of the apparent shape of the section from the top and side views to the auxiliary plane, and connect them to form the true shape of the section in the auxiliary view.
