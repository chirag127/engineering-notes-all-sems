# Projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes

- A line is a geometrical object that has length but no width or thickness.
- A line can be represented by two points, A and B, that are its extremities.
- A line can be projected on two reference planes, the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP), that are perpendicular to each other.
- The projections of a line on the HP and VP are called the top view and the front view, respectively.
- The projections of a line can be obtained by drawing perpendiculars from the points A and B to the HP and VP.
- The projections of a line can be classified into four types, depending on its inclination to the HP and VP:

  - Type 1: Line parallel to both the planes
  - Type 2: Line inclined to one plane and parallel to the other
  - Type 3: Line perpendicular to one plane and inclined to the other
  - Type 4: Line inclined to both the planes

- The inclination of a line to a plane can be measured by the angle between the line and its projection on that plane.
- The true length of a line can be found by measuring the length of its projection on a plane that is parallel to the line.
- The traces of a line are the points where the line intersects the HP and VP.
- The traces of a line can be used to locate the position of the line in space and to find its true inclination to the planes.
