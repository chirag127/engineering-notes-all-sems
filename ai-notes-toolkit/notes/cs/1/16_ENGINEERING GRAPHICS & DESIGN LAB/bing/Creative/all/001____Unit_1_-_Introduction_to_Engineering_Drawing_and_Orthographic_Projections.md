## Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate technical information, such as design specifications, dimensions, tolerances, materials, and manufacturing processes.
- Engineering drawing follows a set of standards and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and uniformity of the drawings.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into two types: artistic drawing and technical drawing. Artistic drawing is used to express the aesthetic and creative aspects of an object, while technical drawing is used to convey the functional and structural aspects of an object.
- Technical drawing can be further divided into two categories: pictorial drawing and orthographic drawing. Pictorial drawing is a three-dimensional representation of an object, such as isometric, oblique, or perspective drawing. Orthographic drawing is a two-dimensional representation of an object, such as front, top, and side views.
- Orthographic projection is a method of projecting the features of a three-dimensional object onto two-dimensional planes, called principal planes. The principal planes are perpendicular to each other and form a box around the object, called the projection box. The projection box has six faces: front, top, bottom, left, right, and back.
- The front, top, and right faces of the projection box are called the principal views, and they are the most commonly used views in engineering drawing. The bottom, left, and back faces are called the auxiliary views, and they are used to show additional details or features that are not visible in the principal views.
- The principal views are arranged in a standard layout, called the first-angle projection or the third-angle projection, depending on the relative positions of the object and the projection box. In the first-angle projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant of the projection box, and the views are unfolded clockwise. In the third-angle projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant of the projection box, and the views are unfolded counterclockwise.
- The first-angle projection is commonly used in Europe, Asia, and Australia, while the third-angle projection is commonly used in North America. The projection method should be indicated on the drawing by a symbol, such as ⅃ for the first-angle projection and ⅂ for the third-angle projection.
- The principal views should be aligned with each other along the projection lines, which are the imaginary lines that connect the corresponding points on the object and the views. The projection lines should be parallel to the principal planes and perpendicular to the line of sight, which is the imaginary line that connects the eye of the observer and the point on the object.
- The principal views should be drawn to the same scale, which is the ratio of the size of the drawing to the size of the actual object. The scale should be chosen to fit the drawing on the paper and to show the details clearly. The scale should be indicated on the drawing by a fraction, such as 1:10, or a statement, such as "Scale 1:10".
- The principal views should be labeled with the names of the planes, such as "Front View", "Top View", and "Right View". The labels should be placed below or beside the views, and aligned horizontally or vertically. The labels should be written in capital letters, and the font size should be consistent throughout the drawing.
