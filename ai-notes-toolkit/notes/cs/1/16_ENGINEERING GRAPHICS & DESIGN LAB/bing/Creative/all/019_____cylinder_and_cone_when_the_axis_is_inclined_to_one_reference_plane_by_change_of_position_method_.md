# Cylinder and cone when the axis is inclined to one reference plane by change of position method

- A cylinder or a cone is said to have its axis inclined to one reference plane when the angle between the axis and the reference plane is not 90 degrees.
- To draw the projections of such a cylinder or a cone, we can use the change of position method, which involves the following steps:
  - Step 1: Draw the projections of the cylinder or the cone when the axis is perpendicular to the reference plane, i.e., when the angle between the axis and the reference plane is 90 degrees. This is called the true shape of the cylinder or the cone.
  - Step 2: Rotate the cylinder or the cone about a point on the axis until the axis makes the given angle with the reference plane. This is called the change of position. The point of rotation is called the center of rotation. The angle of rotation is equal to the difference between 90 degrees and the given angle of inclination.
  - Step 3: Draw the projections of the cylinder or the cone after the change of position. This is called the apparent shape of the cylinder or the cone. The apparent shape will be an ellipse in both the top view and the front view.
  - Step 4: Mark the dimensions and the angles of the projections and label them accordingly.
