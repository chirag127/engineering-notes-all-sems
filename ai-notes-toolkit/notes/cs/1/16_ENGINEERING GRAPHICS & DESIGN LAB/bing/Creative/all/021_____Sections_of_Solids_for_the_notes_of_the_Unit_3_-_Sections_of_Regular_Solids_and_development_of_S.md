# Sections of Solids

- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid by a plane, called the section plane or cutting plane, and removing the portion between the plane and the observer.
- Sections of solids are used to show the hidden or internal parts of an object in technical drawings.
- Sections of solids can also help to find the true shape of the cut surface, the volume and the centroid of the solid.
- The section plane can be perpendicular or inclined to one or both of the reference planes (HP and VP).
- The section plane can also be parallel, perpendicular or oblique to the axis of the solid.
- The true shape of the section is the shape that is obtained when the section plane is parallel to the plane of projection.
- The true shape of the section can be found by drawing projectors from the points of intersection of the section plane and the outline of the solid to the plane of projection.
- The true shape of the section can also be found by rotating the solid until the section plane becomes parallel to the plane of projection.
- The common types of solids that are used in engineering graphics are prisms, pyramids, cylinders and cones.
