# Lettering for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Lettering is the process of writing or drawing letters, numbers, symbols, or other characters on a drawing or a document.
- Lettering is an essential part of engineering drawing and design, as it conveys information, dimensions, specifications, notes, and instructions to the reader or the user of the drawing or the document.
- Lettering should be clear, legible, uniform, and consistent throughout the drawing or the document.
- Lettering should follow certain standards and guidelines, such as the size, shape, style, spacing, alignment, and orientation of the characters.
- Lettering should also follow certain conventions and abbreviations, such as the use of capital letters, symbols, units, and prefixes.
- Lettering should be done with the help of suitable instruments, such as pencils, pens, stencils, templates, or computer software.
- Lettering should be done on a suitable surface, such as paper, tracing paper, tracing cloth, or drafting film.
- Lettering should be done with care and accuracy, as errors or corrections may affect the quality and appearance of the drawing or the document.

## Types of lettering

- There are two main types of lettering used in engineering drawing and design: single-stroke lettering and freehand lettering.
- Single-stroke lettering is the type of lettering that is done with the help of instruments, such as stencils, templates, or computer software. It is also called mechanical or instrumental lettering. It is characterized by uniform and precise characters that are made with a single stroke of the pencil or the pen. It is used for titles, headings, labels, dimensions, and specifications on the drawing or the document.
- Freehand lettering is the type of lettering that is done without the help of instruments, such as stencils, templates, or computer software. It is also called artistic or manual lettering. It is characterized by varied and expressive characters that are made with multiple strokes of the pencil or the pen. It is used for notes, instructions, comments, and sketches on the drawing or the document.

## Styles of lettering

- There are two main styles of lettering used in engineering drawing and design: vertical lettering and inclined lettering.
- Vertical lettering is the style of lettering that is done with the characters standing upright and perpendicular to the baseline. It is also called upright or normal lettering. It is used for titles, headings, labels, dimensions, and specifications on the drawing or the document.
- Inclined lettering is the style of lettering that is done with the characters slanting at an angle to the baseline. It is also called slanting or italic lettering. It is used for notes, instructions, comments, and sketches on the drawing or the document.

- The size of lettering is usually expressed in millimeters (mm) or fractions of an inch (in). The standard sizes of lettering used in engineering drawing and design are 2.5 mm, 3.5 mm, 5 mm, 7 mm, 10 mm, 14 mm, and 20 mm. The corresponding sizes in inches are 3/32 in, 1/8 in, 3/16 in, 1/4 in, 3/8 in, 1/2 in, and 3/4 in.
- The size of lettering should be consistent throughout the drawing or the document, except for the titles and the headings, which may be larger or bolder than the rest of the lettering. The size of lettering should also be uniform within a word, a line, or a paragraph. The size of lettering should not be changed abruptly or randomly on the drawing or the document.
