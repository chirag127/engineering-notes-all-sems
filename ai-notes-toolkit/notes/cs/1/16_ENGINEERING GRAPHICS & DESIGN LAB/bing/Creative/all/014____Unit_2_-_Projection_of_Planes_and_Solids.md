# Unit 2 - Projection of Planes and Solids

## Introduction

- A plane is a two dimensional object having length and breadth only. Its thickness is always neglected. Various shapes of plane figures are considered such as square, rectangle, circle, pentagon, hexagon, etc. 
- A solid is a three dimensional object having length, breadth and thickness. It is completely bounded by a surface or surfaces, which may be curved or plane. The shape of a solid is described orthographically by drawing its two orthographic projections, usually, on the two principal planes of projection i.e., HP and VP. 
- The projection of a plane or a solid is the image or picture of the object on a plane of projection. The projection is produced by connecting the points where the lines of sight pierce the projection plane. 
- The projection of a plane or a solid depends on its position and orientation with respect to the planes of projection. The position and orientation are described by the following terms:
  - Reference line: A horizontal line drawn on the VP, which is used to measure the distances of the object from the VP.
  - Inclination: The angle made by the surface or the axis of the object with the planes of projection.
  - Trace: The line of intersection of the surface or the axis of the object with the planes of projection.

## Projection of Planes

- The projection of a plane shows the true shape and size of the plane only when the plane is parallel to the plane of projection. Otherwise, the projection shows an ellipse or a foreshortened shape of the plane. 
- The projection of a plane can be obtained by the following methods:
  - Method of auxiliary plane: A plane perpendicular to the given plane and parallel to the plane of projection is assumed. The projection of the given plane on the auxiliary plane is the same as the projection of the given plane on the plane of projection. 
  - Method of successive parallel positions: The given plane is rotated about an edge or a diagonal until it becomes parallel to the plane of projection. The projection of the plane in the final position is the same as the projection of the plane in the initial position. 

## Projection of Solids

- The projection of a solid shows the true shape and size of its base only when the axis of the solid is parallel to the plane of projection. Otherwise, the projection shows an ellipse or a foreshortened shape of the base. 
- The projection of a solid can be obtained by the following methods:
  - Method of auxiliary plane: A plane perpendicular to the axis of the solid and parallel to the plane of projection is assumed. The projection of the solid on the auxiliary plane is the same as the projection of the solid on the plane of projection. 
  - Method of successive parallel positions: The solid is rotated about an axis perpendicular to its axis until its axis becomes parallel to the plane of projection. The projection of the solid in the final position is the same as the projection of the solid in the initial position. 

## Section of Solids

- A section of a solid is the portion of the solid that is cut by a cutting plane. The section plane is assumed to be transparent and the solid is viewed from the direction of the cutting plane. 
- The section of a solid shows the true shape and size of the internal surfaces of the solid that are exposed by the cutting plane. The section also reveals the shape and size of any voids or cavities inside the solid. 
- The section of a solid can be obtained by the following steps:
  - Draw the projections of the solid in the given position and orientation.
  - Draw the cutting plane and mark its trace on the projections of the solid.
  - Remove the portion of the solid that is cut by the cutting plane and shade the sectioned area.
  - Draw the projections of the sectioned solid and show the hidden edges or surfaces by dashed lines.
