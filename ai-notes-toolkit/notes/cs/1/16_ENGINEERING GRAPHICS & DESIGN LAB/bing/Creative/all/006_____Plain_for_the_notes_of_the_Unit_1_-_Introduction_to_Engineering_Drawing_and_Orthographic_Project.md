# Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

## Engineering Drawing
- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate technical information, such as design specifications, dimensions, tolerances, materials, and manufacturing processes.
- Engineering drawing is also a legal document that defines the rights and obligations of the parties involved in the design, production, and use of a product or a system.
- Engineering drawing follows certain standards and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and uniformity of the drawings.

## Orthographic Projections
- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by projecting its features onto a series of planes perpendicular to each other.
- Orthographic projection is also called orthogonal projection or multiview projection.
- Orthographic projection is based on the principle that parallel lines remain parallel and perpendicular lines remain perpendicular when projected onto a plane.
- Orthographic projection consists of three principal views: front view, top view, and right side view. These views show the shape, size, and position of the object from different directions.
- Orthographic projection may also include additional views, such as left side view, bottom view, rear view, sectional view, auxiliary view, and detail view. These views provide more information about the object that cannot be shown in the principal views.
- Orthographic projection follows certain rules and conventions to ensure consistency, completeness, and readability of the drawings. Some of these rules and conventions are:

  - The object should be placed in the first quadrant of the coordinate system, with the front view facing the observer.
  - The object should be aligned with the principal planes of projection, which are the horizontal plane (HP), the vertical plane (VP), and the profile plane (PP).
  - The object should be oriented such that the most descriptive features are shown in the principal views.
  - The views should be arranged according to the third-angle projection method, which means that the top view is placed above the front view, and the right side view is placed to the right of the front view.
  - The views should be aligned with each other, such that the corresponding features are projected on the same level or line.
  - The views should be drawn to the same scale, unless otherwise specified.
  - The views should be labeled with the name of the view and the direction of sight.
  - The views should be dimensioned and annotated according to the standards and conventions of engineering drawing.
