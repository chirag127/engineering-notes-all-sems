# Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and Development of Surfaces

- A **regular solid** is a three-dimensional figure whose faces are congruent regular polygons. Examples of regular solids are cube, tetrahedron, octahedron, dodecahedron, and icosahedron.
- A **section** of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane. The plane is called the **section plane** and the shape is called the **sectional view** or the **true shape of section**.
- The **development** of a surface of a solid is the unfolding or unrolling of the surface on a plane. The development shows the shape and size of the surface in its true dimensions. The development is useful for making patterns for sheet metal work, paper craft, etc.

## Sections of Regular Solids

- To draw the sectional view of a regular solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the projections of the solid in its given position.
  - Draw the section plane in its given position and inclination.
  - Mark the points where the section plane cuts the edges of the solid.
  - Join the points to form the outline of the section.
  - Project the outline of the section on the other view.
  - Shade the sectioned part of the solid to indicate the cut surface.
- The true shape of section can be obtained by rotating the section plane to become parallel to the plane of projection. The outline of the section will then appear in its true size and shape.
- The true shape of section depends on the shape of the solid and the position and inclination of the section plane. Some examples are:
  - A section of a cube by a plane parallel to one of its faces is a square.
  - A section of a cube by a plane perpendicular to one of its diagonals is a regular hexagon.
  - A section of a tetrahedron by a plane parallel to one of its faces is an equilateral triangle.
  - A section of a tetrahedron by a plane perpendicular to one of its edges is an isosceles triangle.
  - A section of a cone by a plane parallel to its base is a circle.
  - A section of a cone by a plane perpendicular to its axis is an ellipse.
  - A section of a cone by a plane passing through its vertex and making an angle with its axis is a parabola.
  - A section of a cone by a plane parallel to its slant edge is a triangle.
  - A section of a cylinder by a plane parallel to its base is a circle.
  - A section of a cylinder by a plane perpendicular to its axis is a rectangle.
  - A section of a cylinder by a plane oblique to its axis is an ellipse.

## Development of Surfaces

- To draw the development of a surface of a solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the projections of the solid in its given position.
  - Divide the surface to be developed into a number of small parts, such as triangles, rectangles, trapezoids, etc. These parts are called the **elements** of the surface.
  - Mark the points where the elements meet the edges of the solid. These points are called the **joints** of the surface.
  - Number the elements and the joints in a systematic order.
  - Draw a base line on a plane and mark a point on it as the starting point of the development.
  - Draw the first element of the surface with one of its sides coinciding with the base line and passing through the starting point. The length and angle of the sides should be the same as in the projection of the solid.
  - Draw the adjacent elements of the surface by joining their corresponding joints to the first element. The length and angle of the sides should be the same as in the projection of the solid.
  - Repeat the process until all the elements of the surface are drawn. The last element should join the first element to complete the development.
  - Indicate the fold lines and the dimensions of the development.
- The development of a surface depends on the shape of the solid and the position and orientation of the surface. Some examples are:
  - The development of the lateral surface of a prism is a rectangle. The length of the rectangle is equal to the perimeter of the base of the prism. The width of the rectangle is equal to the height of the prism.
  - The development of the lateral surface of a pyramid is a polygon. The number of sides of the polygon is equal to the number of faces of the pyramid
