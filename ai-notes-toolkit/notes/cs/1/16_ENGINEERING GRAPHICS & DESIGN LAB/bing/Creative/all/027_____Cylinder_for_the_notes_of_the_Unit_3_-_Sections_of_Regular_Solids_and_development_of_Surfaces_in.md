# Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid that has two circular bases and a curved surface that connects them. The bases are parallel and congruent, and the axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases.

Some points to remember about cylinders are:

- A cylinder can be classified as a right cylinder or an oblique cylinder, depending on whether the axis is perpendicular or slanted to the bases.
- A cylinder can also be classified as a circular cylinder or an elliptical cylinder, depending on whether the bases are circles or ellipses.
- A cylinder can be cut by a plane in different ways, resulting in different sections. Some common sections are:
  - A circle, when the plane is parallel to the bases and passes through the axis.
  - An ellipse, when the plane is parallel to the bases and does not pass through the axis.
  - A rectangle, when the plane is perpendicular to the axis and passes through the center of the bases.
  - A parallelogram, when the plane is perpendicular to the axis and does not pass through the center of the bases.
  - A hyperbola, when the plane is oblique to the axis and passes through both bases.
  - A parabola, when the plane is oblique to the axis and passes through one base and the curved surface.
- A cylinder can be unfolded or developed into a flat shape by cutting along a line on the curved surface and spreading it out. The development of a cylinder is a rectangle whose length is equal to the circumference of the base and whose width is equal to the height of the cylinder. The development can be used to find the surface area of the cylinder.
