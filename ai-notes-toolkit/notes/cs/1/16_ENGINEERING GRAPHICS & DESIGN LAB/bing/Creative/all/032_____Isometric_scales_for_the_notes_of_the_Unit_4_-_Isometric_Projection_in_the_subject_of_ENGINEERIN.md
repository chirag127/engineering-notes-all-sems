# Isometric scales for the notes of the Unit 4 - Isometric Projection in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees.
- Isometric projection is used by engineers, technical illustrators, and occasionally, architects to show the dimensions and shape of an object in a clear and realistic way.
- Isometric projection can also be used for creating isometric design, which is a facet of graphic design that shows three-dimensional objects in two-dimensional planes.
- Isometric design can create a unique and eye-catching visual effect that can be used for logos, icons, illustrations, animations, and more.
- To draw an isometric projection, one needs to use an isometric scale, which is a tool that helps to convert the actual measurements of an object into the corresponding isometric measurements.
- The isometric scale is constructed as follows:
  - Draw a horizontal line OA.
  - Through O draw OP and OQ at 30º and 45º to the horizontal respectively.
  - On OQ mark the actual scale in mm.
  - Draw verticals from each of the division points on the actual scale to cut OP at the corresponding divisions.
  - The points on OP form the isometric scale.

![Isometric scale](https://www.theengineerspost.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/isometric-scale.png)

- To use the isometric scale, one needs to follow these steps:
  - Draw the isometric axes X, Y, and Z at 120º to each other.
  - Locate the points on the isometric axes using the isometric scale.
  - Join the points to form the isometric projection of the object.

![Isometric projection](https://www.theengineerspost.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/isometric-projection-example.png)

- Isometric projection has some advantages and disadvantages over other types of projections:
  - Advantages:
    - It shows the true shape and size of the object along the three principal directions.
    - It is easy to draw and understand.
    - It preserves the angles and proportions of the object.
  - Disadvantages:
    - It does not show the true lengths of the object along the oblique directions.
    - It may cause distortion or confusion when the object has curved or inclined surfaces.
    - It may not be suitable for complex or irregular objects.
