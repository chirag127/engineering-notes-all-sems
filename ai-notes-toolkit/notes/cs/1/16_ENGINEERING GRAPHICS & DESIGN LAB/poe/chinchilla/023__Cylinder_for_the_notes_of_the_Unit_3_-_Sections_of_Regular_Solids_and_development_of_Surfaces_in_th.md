### Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid shape that has two parallel and congruent circular bases connected by a curved lateral surface. It is a regular solid that is commonly used in engineering and design. In this section, we will discuss the properties of a cylinder and how to develop its surface.

#### Properties of a Cylinder

1. Bases: A cylinder has two circular bases that are congruent and parallel to each other. The radius of the bases is denoted by 'r'.

2. Height: The height of a cylinder is the distance between the two bases. It is denoted by 'h'.

3. Lateral Surface: The lateral surface of a cylinder is the curved surface that connects the two bases. It can be unwrapped and flattened into a rectangle.

4. Axis: The axis of a cylinder is a line that passes through the center of the two circular bases. It is perpendicular to the bases.

5. Volume: The volume of a cylinder can be calculated using the formula V = πr^2h, where 'π' is the constant pi.

6. Surface Area: The total surface area of a cylinder can be calculated using the formula SA = 2πr^2 + 2πrh, where 'π' is the constant pi.

#### Development of Surface of a Cylinder

To develop the surface of a cylinder, we need to unwrap the lateral surface and flatten it into a rectangle. The length of the rectangle is equal to the circumference of the base (2πr) and the height is equal to the height of the cylinder (h). The rectangle is then cut along one side and rolled out to create the lateral surface of the cylinder.

The development of the two circular bases can also be created by unwrapping them and flattening them into circles. The diameter of the circle is equal to the diameter of the base (2r).

#### Applications of a Cylinder

Cylinders are commonly used in engineering and design for various applications such as:

1. Pipes and tubes for fluid transport
2. Pistons and cylinders in engines
3. Hydraulic and pneumatic systems
4. Pressure vessels in chemical and industrial processes
5. Rollers and bearings in machinery
