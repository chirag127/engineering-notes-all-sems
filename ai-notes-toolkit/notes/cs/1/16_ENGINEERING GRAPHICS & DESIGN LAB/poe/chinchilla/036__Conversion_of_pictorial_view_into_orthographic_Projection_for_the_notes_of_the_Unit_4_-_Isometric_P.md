### Conversion of Pictorial View into Orthographic Projection

In the field of engineering graphics and design, pictorial views are often used to represent 3D objects on a 2D plane. However, for accurate and detailed representation, orthographic projection is preferred. The process of converting pictorial views into orthographic projection involves the following steps:

1. Identify the principal planes - The first step in converting a pictorial view into orthographic projection is to identify the principal planes of the object. These planes are usually the top, front, and side views.

2. Sketch the object in each principal plane - Once the principal planes have been identified, sketch the object in each plane. This involves drawing the object as it would appear if it were viewed from that plane only. 

3. Transfer the dimensions - After sketching the object in each principal plane, transfer the dimensions from the pictorial view to the orthographic projection. This involves measuring the distances and angles between different points on the object and transferring them to the corresponding views in the orthographic projection.

4. Add hidden lines - In orthographic projection, hidden lines are used to show the parts of the object that are not visible in any of the principal views. Add hidden lines to the orthographic projection to accurately represent the object.

5. Finalize the drawing - Once all the dimensions and hidden lines have been added, finalize the drawing by adding any necessary annotations, such as notes on the materials or manufacturing processes used to create the object.

By following these steps, a pictorial view can be accurately converted into an orthographic projection. This process is essential for creating detailed technical drawings used in engineering and design.
