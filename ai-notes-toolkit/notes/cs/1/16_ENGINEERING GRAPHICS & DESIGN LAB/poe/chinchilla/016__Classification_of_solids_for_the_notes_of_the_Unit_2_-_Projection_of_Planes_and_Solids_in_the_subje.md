### Classification of Solids

In the study of Engineering Graphics and Design, it is essential to understand the different types of solids and their properties. Solids are classified based on their shapes, edges, vertices, and faces. Here are the classifications of solids:

1. **Polyhedra:** These are three-dimensional objects that have flat faces, straight edges, and sharp corners or vertices. Examples of polyhedra include cubes, pyramids, prisms, and dodecahedrons.

2. **Non-polyhedra:** These are three-dimensional objects that do not have flat faces, straight edges, or sharp corners or vertices. Examples of non-polyhedra include spheres, cones, cylinders, and toroids.

3. **Regular solids:** These are three-dimensional objects that have faces, edges, and vertices that are all the same shape and size. Examples of regular solids include cubes, tetrahedrons, and octahedrons.

4. **Irregular solids:** These are three-dimensional objects that do not have faces, edges, and vertices that are all the same shape and size. Examples of irregular solids include spheres, cones, and cylinders.

5. **Prisms:** These are three-dimensional objects that have two parallel faces that are congruent polygons and other faces that are parallelograms. Examples of prisms include rectangular prisms and triangular prisms.

6. **Pyramids:** These are three-dimensional objects that have a polygon base and triangular faces that meet at a common vertex. Examples of pyramids include square pyramids and triangular pyramids.

7. **Spheres:** These are three-dimensional objects that are perfectly round and have no flat faces, edges, or vertices.

8. **Cylinders:** These are three-dimensional objects that have two parallel circular bases and a curved surface that connects the bases.

9. **Cones:** These are three-dimensional objects that have a circular base and a curved surface that tapers to a point or vertex.

Understanding the classification of solids is essential in the study of Engineering Graphics and Design. It allows us to accurately represent and project these objects in two-dimensional drawings and models.
