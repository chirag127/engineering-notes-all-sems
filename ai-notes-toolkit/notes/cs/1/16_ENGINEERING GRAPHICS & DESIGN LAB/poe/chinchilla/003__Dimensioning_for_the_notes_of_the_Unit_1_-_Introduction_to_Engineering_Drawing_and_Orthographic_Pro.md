### Dimensioning

Dimensioning is an important aspect of engineering drawing as it provides information about the size and location of the features of a part. The following points cover the basics of dimensioning:

1. **Types of Dimensions:** There are two types of dimensions - size dimensions and location dimensions. Size dimensions specify the size of a feature while location dimensions specify the location of a feature relative to a reference.

2. **Dimensioning Techniques:** There are three common techniques for dimensioning - aligned dimensioning, unidirectional dimensioning, and baseline dimensioning. Aligned dimensioning is used when the dimensions are placed in line with the dimension lines. Unidirectional dimensioning is used when the dimensions are placed horizontally and read from the bottom of the drawing. Baseline dimensioning is used when the dimensions are placed on a baseline and read from the left side of the drawing.

3. **Dimension Lines:** Dimension lines should be thin, continuous lines that terminate with arrowheads. The arrowheads should be placed at the ends of the dimension line and should be drawn to the same size as the stroke width of the dimension line.

4. **Extension Lines:** Extension lines are used to extend the dimension line to the edge of the feature being dimensioned. They should be thin, continuous lines that do not touch the feature being dimensioned.

5. **Dimension Text:** Dimension text should be placed above the dimension line and centered between the extension lines. The text should be clear and legible and should not touch any other lines on the drawing.

6. **Units of Measurement:** All dimensions should include units of measurement, such as millimeters, inches, or degrees. The units should be placed in parentheses following the dimension text.

7. **Tolerances:** Tolerances specify the allowable variation in the size or location of a feature. They should be placed on the dimension line and separated from the dimension text by a slash (/). The tolerance value should be placed before the tolerance symbol (±) and should be followed by the tolerance grade, such as IT or ANSI.

8. **Overall Dimensions:** Overall dimensions provide the total size of a part and are placed outside the drawing frame. They are usually placed in brackets and should be preceded by the words "OVERALL DIMENSIONS" in capital letters.

In conclusion, dimensioning is a crucial aspect of engineering drawing that provides information about the size and location of features. By following these basic guidelines, you can ensure that your dimensioning is clear, accurate, and easy to understand.
