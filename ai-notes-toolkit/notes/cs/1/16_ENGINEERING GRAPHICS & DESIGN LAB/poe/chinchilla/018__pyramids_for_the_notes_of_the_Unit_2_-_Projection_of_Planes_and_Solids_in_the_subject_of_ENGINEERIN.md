### Pyramids

A pyramid is a three-dimensional solid that has a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common vertex. In this section, we will discuss the different types of pyramids and their projections.

#### Types of Pyramids

1. Triangular Pyramid - A pyramid with a triangular base and three triangular faces.

2. Square Pyramid - A pyramid with a square base and four triangular faces.

3. Pentagonal Pyramid - A pyramid with a pentagonal base and five triangular faces.

4. Hexagonal Pyramid - A pyramid with a hexagonal base and six triangular faces.

#### Projections of Pyramids

The projections of pyramids can be found by following the same principles as those for projecting other solids. The two main types of projections are:

1. Orthographic Projection - This is a two-dimensional representation of the three-dimensional object, where the object is viewed from different directions.

2. Isometric Projection - This is a three-dimensional representation where the object is viewed from a fixed angle, and all the edges are drawn to scale.

#### Orthographic Projections of Pyramids

To project a pyramid orthographically, we need to determine the position of the pyramid with respect to the three reference planes - the horizontal plane (HP), the vertical plane (VP), and the profile plane (PP). The steps involved in projecting a pyramid orthographically are:

1. Determine the position of the pyramid with respect to the reference planes.

2. Draw the base of the pyramid in its true shape and size in the profile plane (PP).

3. Project the corners of the base on the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP) to obtain the base's projections.

4. Draw the height of the pyramid from the vertex perpendicular to the base's plane.

5. Project the apex of the pyramid on the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP) to obtain the vertex's projections.

6. Draw the triangular faces of the pyramid by joining the base's vertices to the vertex's projection.

#### Isometric Projections of Pyramids

To draw the isometric projection of a pyramid, we need to first draw the base of the pyramid in its true shape and size. Then, we can draw the triangular faces by projecting the vertices of the base to the apex at a fixed angle. The steps involved in drawing an isometric projection of a pyramid are:

1. Draw the base of the pyramid in its true shape and size.

2. Draw the height of the pyramid from the vertex perpendicular to the base's plane.

3. Draw the isometric axes and locate the base and the vertex at appropriate positions.

4. Project the vertices of the base to the apex by joining them with lines at a fixed angle.

5. Draw the triangular faces by joining the projected vertices to the apex.
