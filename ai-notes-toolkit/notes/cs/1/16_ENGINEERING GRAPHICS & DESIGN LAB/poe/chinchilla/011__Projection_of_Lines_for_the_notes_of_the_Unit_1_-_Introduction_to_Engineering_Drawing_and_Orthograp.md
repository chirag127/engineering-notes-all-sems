### Projection of Lines

Engineering drawing is an essential tool that engineers use to communicate their ideas and designs. It is a language that enables the transfer of technical information from one person to another. One of the fundamental concepts in engineering drawing is the projection of lines. In this section, we will discuss what projection of lines is and its importance in engineering drawing.

#### What is Projection of Lines?

Projection of lines is the process of determining the image of a line on a plane or a surface by projecting it perpendicular to that plane or surface. In other words, it is the representation of a line on a plane or surface by its true length and direction. The projection of lines is used extensively in engineering drawing to create accurate and precise representations of objects and structures.

#### Types of Projection of Lines

There are two types of projection of lines:

1. Orthographic Projection: In orthographic projection, the object is projected onto a plane perpendicular to its axis. This projection is used to represent three-dimensional objects on two-dimensional surfaces.

2. Oblique Projection: In oblique projection, the object is projected onto a plane at an angle to its axis. This projection is used to represent three-dimensional objects on two-dimensional surfaces by showing the front, top, and side views of the object.

#### Importance of Projection of Lines

The projection of lines is an essential concept in engineering drawing for the following reasons:

1. It enables the creation of accurate and precise representations of objects and structures.

2. It facilitates the communication of technical information between engineers and other stakeholders.

3. It helps engineers visualize and analyze the design of objects and structures.

4. It is a fundamental concept in the study of engineering drawing and is used extensively in the engineering field.

In conclusion, the projection of lines is a crucial concept in engineering drawing that enables the creation of accurate and precise representations of objects and structures. Understanding this concept is essential for anyone studying or working in the engineering field.
