### Orthographic representation of perspective views — Plane figures and simple solids — Visual Ray Method

Engineering Graphics and Design Lab covers a wide range of topics related to engineering design and drawing. One of the key concepts covered in this subject is the Orthographic representation of perspective views, which includes the Plane figures and simple solids.

In this unit, we will be discussing the Visual Ray Method for representing orthographic views of perspective figures. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Orthographic projection is used to represent three-dimensional objects on a two-dimensional plane. This method is widely used in engineering design and drawing.
- In Orthographic projection, we represent the object in three different views, namely top view, front view, and side view. These views are drawn by projecting the object on three mutually perpendicular planes.
- Perspective views, on the other hand, are drawn to represent the object as it appears to the observer. These views are not necessarily mutually perpendicular and they provide a more realistic view of the object.
- To represent perspective views using orthographic projection, we use the Visual Ray Method. This method involves drawing visual rays from the observer's eye to the object and projecting the points at which these rays intersect the object on the three mutually perpendicular planes.
- To draw a perspective view of a plane figure or a simple solid using the Visual Ray Method, we first draw the object in its true shape and size. We then draw visual rays from the observer's eye to the object and project the points at which these rays intersect the object on the three mutually perpendicular planes.
- Once we have the three views of the object, we can use them to draw the orthographic projection of the object. This involves projecting the points from the perspective views onto the corresponding plane in the orthographic projection.
- It is important to note that the Visual Ray Method is only applicable for drawing perspective views of simple solids and plane figures. For more complex objects, other methods may be used.

In conclusion, the Visual Ray Method is an important technique for representing orthographic views of perspective figures. By understanding this method, we can accurately represent three-dimensional objects on a two-dimensional plane, which is crucial in engineering design and drawing.
