## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

1. Isometric projection is a method of 3D rendering of a figure on 2D drawing/surface. It maintains the relative proportions and angles in drawing.
2. In isometric projection, an object is drawn in 3D view but the three axes are shown at equal angles(i.e. 120 degrees). This means the scale along each axis is different while the angles between the axes remain equal.
3. To draw an isometric projection of a figure:
- Choose one corner of the figure to be the origin. Mark the three axes at 120 degrees to each other.
- Draw vertical and horizontal lines parallel to the corresponding axes at required intervals to divide the figure into sections.
- Draw lines joining the corresponding points on each axis to get the isometric view.
- Erase the guidelines to get the final drawing.
4. Applications of isometric projection:
- It is useful for illustrating 3D structures/objects on 2D surfaces.
- It helps in perceiving the 3D form of an object.
- It is commonly used in technical drawings, illustrations, and video game designs.
5. Limitations: There can be ambiguity in determining the dimensions and shape of the object. The drawing does not show all the details of the object.
