### Perspective Projection for the notes of the Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

1. Perspective projection is a graphical technique to represent three-dimensional objects on a two-dimensional surface.
2. In perspective projection, objects appear smaller as their distance increases from the viewer.
3. There are two major types of perspective projections:
	- Parallel perspective projection: Parallel lines remain parallel in the projection.
	- Convergent perspective projection: Parallel lines converge at a vanishing point on the horizon.
4. Isometric projection is a type of parallel perspective projection with the following properties:
	- The three principal axes of the object are equally foreshortened.
	- The three principal angles between the axes are 120 degrees.
	- The projected shape appears similar to the shape of the original object.
5. To draw an isometric view of an object:
	- Draw the isometric axes: two diagonals at 30 degrees and a vertical line.
	- Mark equal spacing for all three axes depending on the scale required.
	- Project the edges of the object on the isometric axes.
	- Connect the projected points to get the isometric view.
