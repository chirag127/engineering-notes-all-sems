### Geometry and topology of engineered components

- Points: Zero-dimensional entity that indicates a location in space.
- Lines: One-dimensional entity defined by two points. Can be straight, curved, etc.
- Planes: Two-dimensional entity defined by three non-collinear points or a line and a point not on the line.
- Angles: Measurement of rotation between two lines, planes or vectors. Can be acute, obtuse, right, etc.
- Intersections: Meeting of two or more entities (lines, planes, etc.) resulting in common points, lines or planes.
- Parallelism: Two or more entities that do not intersect and remain equidistant at all points.
- Perpendicularity: Intersection of two entities at right angles (90 degrees).
- Curves: One-dimensional entities defined by mathematical functions. Can be circular, parabolic, etc.
- Surfaces: Two-dimensional entities defined by curves, lines or functions. Can be planar, cylindrical, spherical, etc.
- Solids: Three-dimensional entities bounded by surfaces. Can be primitives (cube, sphere, etc.) or complex shapes.
- Topology: Study of properties preserved under bending, twisting and stretching transformations. Includes concepts like connectivity, holes, inside/outside, etc.
