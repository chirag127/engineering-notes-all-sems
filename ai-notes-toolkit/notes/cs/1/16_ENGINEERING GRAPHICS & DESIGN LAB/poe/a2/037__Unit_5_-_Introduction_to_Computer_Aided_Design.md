## Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design

1. Computer Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer technology to aid in the creation, modification, analysis, or optimization of a design. CAD software is used to increase the productivity of the designer, improve the quality of design, improve communications through documentation, and to create a database for manufacturing.
2. CAD applications are typically used to design complex structures, products, and parts. CAD techniques are widely used to create plans for buildings, transportation systems, machinery, electronics, and many other products. CAD allows rapid creation of design alternatives that can then be evaluated to determine the optimal solution. CAD has greatly improved the efficiency and accuracy of the design process.
3. Advantages of CAD:
    - Save time in creation of technical drawings.
    - Ability to create and edit complex designs quickly and accurately.
    - Drawings are easy to modify and update.
    - Enforces drawing standards and conventions.
    - Permits client participation in the design process.
    - Drawings can be easily scaled for different views.
    - Databases can be created for manufacturing and material requirements.
4. Disadvantages of CAD:
    - Initial cost of software and hardware can be high.
    - Learning to use the software effectively takes time.
    - Dependence on computers means risk of software bugs or technical problems.
    - Easier to make undetected errors or introduce flaws in the design.
    - Difficulty in translating electronic files to hard copy drawings.
