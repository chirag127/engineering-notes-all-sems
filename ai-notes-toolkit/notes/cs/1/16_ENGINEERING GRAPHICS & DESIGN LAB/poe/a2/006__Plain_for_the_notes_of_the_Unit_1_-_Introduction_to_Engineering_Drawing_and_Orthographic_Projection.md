### Plain notes for Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

1. Engineering drawing: It is a language of technical communication which is used to convey ideas, information and requirements about a product. It provides a permanent and detailed record of the designer's intent about a product.

2. Types of drawings:
- Sketches: Rough freehand drawings to quickly explore ideas.
- Detail drawings: Shows specific part of an object in detail.
- Assembly drawings: Shows how different components fit together.
- Working drawings: Shows all details of an object needed for manufacturing.

3. Views: To represent a 3D object on a 2D drawing sheet, multiple views are used. The most common types of views are:
- Front view: Looking at the front face of the object.
- Top view: Looking down at the object from the top.
- Side view: Looking at the side of the object.
- Isometric view: 3D representation of the object with the same scale along x, y and z axes.

4. Projections: The technique of representing 3D objects on a 2D surface is known as projection. The most common types are:
- Orthographic projection: Parallel projection onto perpendicular planes. Leads to orthographic views.
- Perspective projection: Divergent projection from a point. Gives a 3D effect but distorts shapes.

5. Sections: The internal details of an object that are not visible from the external view are shown using sections. Sections are obtained by cutting the object with an imaginary cutting plane and viewing the surface that is exposed.
