### Orthographic Projection

- Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It projects an object onto a drawing surface (paper) by drawing parallel lines from the object to the drawing surface.
- The parallel lines are perpendicular to the drawing surface. This results in a unidirectional view of the object.
- To fully represent a 3D object, multiple orthographic projections are used. Typically, front, top and side views are used.
- Each view shows what the object would look like if it was sliced at those specific orientations. This gives the drafter key information needed to recreate the 3D object.
- Objects are projected independently along the vertical and horizontal planes. This means overlapping views can be shown without distortion.
- Hidden lines are used to show features of an object that are not visible in a particular view. Hidden lines are typically dashed or dotted lines.
- Section views can be used to show internal features. This involves theoretically cutting the object with a cutting plane and displaying what is visible.
- Advantages - Precise, versatile, no distortion or perspective effects. Useful for design and manufacturing.
- Disadvantages - Can be difficult to visualise 3D form from 2D projections. Hidden lines may be unclear.
