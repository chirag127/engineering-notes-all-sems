## Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

1. Engineering drawing is a type of technical drawing that is used to fully and clearly define requirements for engineered items.
2. Engineering drawings provide a complete understanding of the item including dimensions, tolerances, materials, finishes, and other important details.
3. Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It shows an object as viewed from multiple sides projecting the object onto a set of planes.
4. There are six views required to show all sides of an object: front, back, top, bottom, right side, and left side.
5. Views are arranged relative to each other according to standard projection practices. Adjacent views share common projection lines and sides.
6. break lines or cutting planes are used to show internal features of an object that would otherwise be hidden. They indicate where the object has been cut to show the internal part.
7. Dimensioning and tolerancing are also important components of engineering drawings. Dimensions display measurable quantities while tolerances indicate acceptable variation in dimension.
8. Learning orthographic projection and engineering drawing conventions is essential for communicating design ideas and requirements. With practice, these skills can be mastered.
