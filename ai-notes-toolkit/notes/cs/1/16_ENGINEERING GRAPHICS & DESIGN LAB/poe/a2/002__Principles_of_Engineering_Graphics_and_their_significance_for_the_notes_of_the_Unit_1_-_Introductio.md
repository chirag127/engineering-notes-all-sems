### Principles of Engineering Graphics and their significance for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB.

1. Principle of Orthogonality: All the projecting lines in an orthographic projection are perpendicular to the plane of projection. This results in views that are undistorted and have true shapes and sizes.
2. Principle of Projection: The projections of an object on more than one planes can be obtained by projecting the object onto those planes. This forms the basis of obtaining multiple views of an object in orthographic projection.
3. Principle of Views: To completely represent an object, a minimum of 3 views, namely front view, top view and side view (or right side view) are necessary to convey all the details and features of the object. Additional views may be required for complex objects.
4. Principle of Sectioning: An object can be imagined to be cut by one or more planes to expose the internal details, especially for complex objects. The shapes exposed as a result of cutting are then projected onto the planes of projection to obtain the sectional views. This helps in understanding the internal details, features and constructions of an object.

The above principles form the basis for representing objects pictorially through orthographic projections. An understanding of these principles is essential to be able to draw orthographic projections of any given object and to interpret the drawings. The notes of Unit 1 will elaborate on these principles with illustrations and examples for a better understanding.
