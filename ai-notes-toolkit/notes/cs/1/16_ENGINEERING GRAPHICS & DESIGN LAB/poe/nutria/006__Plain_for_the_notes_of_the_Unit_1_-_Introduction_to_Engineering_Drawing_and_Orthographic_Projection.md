
### Plain for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

1. Engineering drawing is the language of communication between engineers and technicians. It is a language of symbols, lines, letters, and numbers.

2. Orthographic projections are a type of drawing used to represent a three-dimensional object in two dimensions. The object is projected onto a plane, usually the plane of projection, and the resulting drawing is called an orthographic projection.

3. The three types of orthographic projections are first angle, third angle, and oblique. The first angle projection is the most commonly used in the United States, while the third angle projection is the most commonly used in Europe.

4. The drawing is composed of views (front, top, and side) of the object. Each view is represented by a plane, either the plane of projection or a cutting plane.

5. The drawing must contain all the necessary information for the object to be constructed. This includes dimensions, notes, and any other special instructions.

6. The drawing must be constructed according to the conventions of the particular industry. This includes the use of symbols, lines, letters, and numbers.

7. The drawing must be dimensioned correctly. This includes the use of linear, angular, and radial dimensions.

8. The drawing must be neat and legible. This includes the use of lettering, line weights, and the use of a scale.
