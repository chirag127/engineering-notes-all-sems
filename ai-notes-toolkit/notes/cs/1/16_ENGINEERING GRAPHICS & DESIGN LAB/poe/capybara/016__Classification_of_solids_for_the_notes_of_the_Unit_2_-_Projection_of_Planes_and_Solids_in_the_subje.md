### Classification of Solids

In Engineering Graphics and Design, solids are classified based on their shape and symmetry. The classification is important in understanding the properties of solids and their projections in different planes. The following are the different types of solids:

1. **Prism**: A prism is a solid object with two identical ends and flat sides. The sides are usually parallel, and the shape of the ends determines the type of prism. Examples of prisms include rectangular, triangular, and hexagonal prisms.

2. **Pyramid**: A pyramid is a solid object with a polygonal base and triangular sides that meet at a point called the apex. The shape of the base determines the type of pyramid. Examples of pyramids include square, triangular, and pentagonal pyramids.

3. **Cylinder**: A cylinder is a solid object with two identical circular ends and a curved surface. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the two circular ends. Examples of cylinders include soda cans, water bottles, and pipes.

4. **Cone**: A cone is a solid object with a circular base and a curved surface that tapers to a point called the apex. The height of the cone is the distance between the base and the apex. Examples of cones include traffic cones, ice cream cones, and party hats.

5. **Sphere**: A sphere is a solid object with a curved surface that is the same distance from a fixed point called the center. The radius of the sphere is the distance from the center to any point on the surface. Examples of spheres include balls, marbles, and globes.

6. **Ellipsoid**: An ellipsoid is a solid object with a curved surface that is generated by rotating an ellipse about its major or minor axis. The ellipsoid has three axes of symmetry, and its shape can be described by three radii. Examples of ellipsoids include eggs, footballs, and planets.

7. **Torus**: A torus is a solid object with a curved surface that is generated by rotating a circle about an axis that is perpendicular to the plane of the circle. The torus has two radii, the distance from the center of the tube to the center of the torus, and the distance from the center of the torus to the center of the circle. Examples of tori include doughnuts, lifebuoys, and tires.

In conclusion, the classification of solids is essential in engineering graphics and design as it helps in creating accurate projections of solids in different planes. Understanding the properties of each type of solid is also vital in designing and manufacturing different products.
