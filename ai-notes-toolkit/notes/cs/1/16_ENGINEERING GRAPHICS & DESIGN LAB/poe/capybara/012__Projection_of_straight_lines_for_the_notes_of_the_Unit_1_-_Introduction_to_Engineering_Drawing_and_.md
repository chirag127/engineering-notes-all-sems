### Projection of straight lines for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

In engineering drawing, the projection of straight lines is a crucial concept that forms the basic foundation for creating accurate and precise technical drawings. Here are some important points to keep in mind when studying the projection of straight lines:

- A straight line can be projected onto a plane in different ways. The type of projection used depends on the purpose of the drawing and the information that needs to be conveyed.
- The two most commonly used types of projection are orthographic projection and isometric projection. Orthographic projection is used to create 2D views of a 3D object, while isometric projection is used to create a 3D view of an object.
- In orthographic projection, a straight line can be projected onto each of the three primary planes (front, top, and side) to create a 2D representation of the object. These projections are known as the front view, top view, and side view.
- The projection of a straight line onto a plane can be determined using a projection line, which is a line drawn from the endpoint of the straight line to the plane of projection. The point where the projection line intersects the plane of projection is the projected endpoint of the straight line.
- To project a line that is not parallel to any of the primary planes, it is necessary to create an auxiliary view. An auxiliary view is a view that is created by projecting the object onto a plane that is not one of the primary planes. This allows the projection of the line to be determined more accurately.
- When projecting a line, it is important to ensure that the projection is accurate and to scale. This requires a good understanding of the principles of projection and the ability to visualize the object in three dimensions.

By mastering the concepts of projection of straight lines, you will be able to create accurate and precise technical drawings that convey important information about the object being represented.
