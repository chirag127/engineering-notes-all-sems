### Pyramid

A pyramid is a three-dimensional geometric shape that has a polygonal base and triangular faces that converge to a single point at the top, known as the apex. Here are some key points to remember about pyramids in the context of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB:

- A pyramid can have any polygon as its base, including triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, and so on. The number of sides in the base determines the name of the pyramid. For example, a pyramid with a square base is called a square pyramid, while one with a triangular base is called a triangular pyramid.
- The height of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the apex to the base. The slant height is the distance from the apex to the midpoint of an edge on the base.
- The lateral area of a pyramid is the sum of the areas of its triangular faces. The total surface area includes the base as well. The formula for the lateral area is 1/2 * perimeter of base * slant height, while the formula for the total surface area is base area + lateral area.
- The volume of a pyramid is given by the formula 1/3 * base area * height. This formula applies to all pyramids, regardless of the shape of the base.
- Pyramids can be used to model a wide range of structures in engineering and architecture, including buildings, bridges, and even underground tunnels. The ability to calculate the surface area and volume of pyramids accurately is essential in these fields.

In conclusion, the pyramid is a fundamental shape in geometry and has many applications in engineering and architecture. Understanding how to calculate its surface area and volume is crucial in solving real-world problems.
