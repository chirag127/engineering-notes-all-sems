### Lettering for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB.

- Lettering is an essential aspect of engineering drawing and orthographic projections that is often overlooked but is crucial for clear communication of design ideas and specifications.
- The purpose of lettering is to convey information in a clear and concise manner, and it should be done with careful attention to detail and consistency.
- The three main types of lettering used in engineering drawings are capital letters, lowercase letters, and numerals. 
- Capital letters are used for titles, headings, and other prominent text, while lowercase letters are used for annotations and dimensions. 
- Numerals are used for numerical values and measurements.
- The most commonly used lettering style in engineering drawings is Gothic lettering, which is characterized by its block-like appearance and uniform stroke width.
- Other lettering styles that may be used include Roman, Italic, and Script.
- When lettering, it is important to use the correct size and spacing to ensure legibility and clarity. 
- The height of capital letters should be approximately 1/8 inch for drawings up to 11 inches in size, and 1/4 inch for larger drawings. 
- Lowercase letters should be approximately two-thirds the height of capital letters.
- The spacing between letters should be equal to the width of the stroke of the letter, and the spacing between words should be approximately equal to the width of the letter "o".
- It is also important to use consistent lettering throughout the drawing to avoid confusion and ensure clarity.
- In summary, lettering is a critical aspect of engineering drawing and orthographic projections that should not be overlooked. Using the correct size, spacing, and style of lettering will help to ensure clear and effective communication of design ideas and specifications.
