## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

Isometric projection is a method of visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. In this technique, all three axes are equally foreshortened, which means that the angles between any two of them are 120 degrees.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying isometric projection:

- Isometric projection is often used in technical drawing to show three-dimensional objects in a way that is easy to understand.
- The three axes of an isometric projection are typically labeled as X, Y, and Z.
- The angles between the three axes in an isometric projection are always 120 degrees.
- Isometric projections are not true representations of the object being depicted, as they do not account for perspective or scale.
- However, isometric projections are useful for showing the general shape and orientation of an object, as well as its relationship to other objects in a design.
- To create an isometric projection, you will need to use a set of guidelines to ensure that your drawing is accurate.
- Some common guidelines for creating an isometric projection include using a 30-degree set square, drawing parallel lines, and using the same scale for all three axes.
- Isometric projections can be created by hand using drafting tools, or by using computer-aided design (CAD) software.
- Isometric projections are commonly used in engineering, architecture, and product design to create technical drawings and schematics.
- Mastery of isometric projection is an important skill for anyone interested in pursuing a career in technical drawing or design.
