### Isometric Projections of Simple and Combination of Solids

Isometric projection is a type of pictorial drawing in which all three dimensions of an object are shown in a single view. In this projection, all the edges of an object are drawn parallel to each other, and at equal angles with the plane of projection. This technique is widely used in engineering graphics and design to communicate complex ideas in a clear and concise manner. 

In this unit, we will focus on the isometric projections of simple and combination of solids. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

#### Isometric Projections of Simple Solids

1. Isometric projection of a cube: A cube has six faces, each of which is a square. To draw an isometric projection of a cube, draw a square as the base and then extend the sides to create a three-dimensional shape.

2. Isometric projection of a sphere: A sphere is a three-dimensional object with a curved surface. To draw an isometric projection of a sphere, first draw a circle as the base and then draw a series of curved lines to create the three-dimensional shape.

3. Isometric projection of a cylinder: A cylinder is a three-dimensional object with a circular base and a curved surface. To draw an isometric projection of a cylinder, first draw a circle as the base and then extend the sides to create a three-dimensional shape.

#### Isometric Projections of Combination of Solids

1. Isometric projection of a cube and cylinder: To draw an isometric projection of a combination of a cube and a cylinder, draw the cube first and then add the cylinder on top of it.

2. Isometric projection of a cube and sphere: To draw an isometric projection of a combination of a cube and a sphere, draw the cube first and then add the sphere on top of it.

3. Isometric projection of a cylinder and sphere: To draw an isometric projection of a combination of a cylinder and a sphere, draw the cylinder first and then add the sphere on top of it.

In conclusion, isometric projection is an important technique in engineering graphics and design. By mastering the isometric projections of simple and combination of solids, you will be able to communicate complex ideas effectively in your designs and drawings.
