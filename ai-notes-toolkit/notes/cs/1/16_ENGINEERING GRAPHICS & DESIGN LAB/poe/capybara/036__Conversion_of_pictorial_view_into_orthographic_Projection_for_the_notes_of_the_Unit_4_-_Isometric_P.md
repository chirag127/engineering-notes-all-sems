### Conversion of pictorial view into orthographic Projection

In the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB, it is important to understand the process of converting a pictorial view into an orthographic projection. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Pictorial views are often used in engineering drawings to provide a realistic representation of an object. However, for certain purposes, such as manufacturing or assembly, orthographic projections are preferred.
- Orthographic projections are two-dimensional representations of an object that show all three principal views: front, top, and side. These views are created by projecting the object onto a plane that is perpendicular to each of the principal axes.
- To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, the first step is to identify the principal axes of the object. These are typically the x, y, and z axes.
- Next, the object is rotated so that each principal axis is aligned with one of the three planes of projection: the xy plane, the xz plane, and the yz plane. This creates the three principal views.
- The front view is created by projecting the object onto the xy plane. The top view is created by projecting the object onto the xz plane. The side view is created by projecting the object onto the yz plane.
- In order to ensure that the three views are consistent with each other, it is important to maintain the same scale and orientation in each view. This means that the same proportion of the object should be shown in each view, and that the orientation of the object should be consistent across all three views.
- Once the three principal views have been created, they can be arranged on a drawing sheet to create an orthographic projection of the object. This projection can then be used for manufacturing, assembly, or other purposes.

In conclusion, the conversion of pictorial views into orthographic projections is an important skill for anyone studying engineering graphics and design. By following the steps outlined above, it is possible to create accurate and consistent representations of objects that can be used for a variety of purposes.
