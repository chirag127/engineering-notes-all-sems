### Orthographic representation of perspective views — Plane figures and simple solids — Visual Ray Method

In the Unit 4 of Engineering Graphics & Design Lab, we will be studying about Orthographic representation of perspective views, Plane figures, and simple solids using the Visual Ray Method. Here are some important points to keep in mind:

- Orthographic Projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions. It is also known as Engineering Drawing or Technical Drawing.

- Perspective Projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional surface, such as a piece of paper or a computer screen. It is used in art, architecture, and photography.

- The Visual Ray Method is a technique used to create an isometric projection of a three-dimensional object. It involves drawing lines from the object to the viewer's eye to create the illusion of depth and dimension.

- In Orthographic Projection, we draw the object as if it were projected onto a plane that is perpendicular to each of the three primary axes (x, y, and z) of the object.

- To create an Orthographic Projection, we need to have an understanding of the different views of the object, such as the top view, front view, and side view.

- In Plane figures, we use Orthographic Projection to represent objects that have only two dimensions, such as triangles, rectangles, and circles.

- In Simple Solids, we use Orthographic Projection to represent objects that have three dimensions, such as cubes, cylinders, and spheres.

- The Visual Ray Method is used to create an isometric projection of a three-dimensional object. Isometric Projection is a type of Orthographic Projection that represents the object from multiple angles, rather than just one.

- To create an isometric projection using the Visual Ray Method, we first draw the object in its normal position. Then, we draw lines from each corner of the object to the viewer's eye. Finally, we use these lines to create the illusion of depth and dimension in the projection.

By understanding the concepts of Orthographic Projection, Perspective Projection, and the Visual Ray Method, we can create accurate and detailed representations of complex objects and designs.
