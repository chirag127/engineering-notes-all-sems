# Business Correspondence and Report Writing

- Business correspondence is the exchange of written or oral messages between people who are involved in a business relationship. It can be formal or informal, internal or external, routine or special, depending on the purpose, audience, tone, and medium of communication.
- Report writing is the process of presenting information, analysis, findings, or recommendations in a clear, concise, and logical manner. It can be oral or written, formal or informal, descriptive or analytical, depending on the objective, scope, and nature of the report.
- Business correspondence and report writing are essential skills for professionals who need to communicate effectively with various stakeholders, such as customers, suppliers, employees, managers, investors, regulators, etc.
- Some of the common types of business correspondence are:
  - Letters: written messages that are sent or received by mail or email. They can be classified into various categories, such as inquiry, order, complaint, adjustment, sales, goodwill, application, recommendation, etc.
  - Memos: short and informal written messages that are used for internal communication within an organization. They can be used for various purposes, such as informing, requesting, instructing, reminding, persuading, etc.
  - Emails: electronic messages that are sent or received through the internet or a network. They can be used for both internal and external communication, and can have various formats, such as plain text, HTML, attachments, etc.
  - Faxes: printed messages that are transmitted or received through a telephone line or a network. They can be used for both internal and external communication, and can have various formats, such as letter, memo, form, etc.
  - Notices: written announcements that are displayed or circulated for public information. They can be used for various purposes, such as informing, inviting, warning, etc.
  - Circulars: written messages that are distributed to a large number of people within or outside an organization. They can be used for various purposes, such as informing, instructing, persuading, etc.
  - Agendas: written lists of items that are to be discussed or decided in a meeting. They can be used for various purposes, such as planning, organizing, controlling, etc.
  - Minutes: written records of the proceedings and decisions of a meeting. They can be used for various purposes, such as documenting, reporting, evaluating, etc.
- Some of the common types of business reports are:
  - Progress reports: written updates on the status and achievements of a project, task, or activity. They can be used for various purposes, such as monitoring, reviewing, evaluating, etc.
  - Feasibility reports: written assessments of the viability and suitability of a proposed project, plan, or solution. They can be used for various purposes, such as exploring, analyzing, recommending, etc.
  - Research reports: written presentations of the results and findings of a systematic investigation or study. They can be used for various purposes, such as discovering, explaining, interpreting, etc.
  - Audit reports: written evaluations of the accuracy and reliability of the financial statements and records of an organization or entity. They can be used for various purposes, such as verifying, attesting, assuring, etc.
  - Proposal reports: written proposals of a project, plan, or solution that are submitted for approval, funding, or acceptance. They can be used for various purposes, such as proposing, persuading, convincing, etc.
  - Annual reports: written summaries of the performance and achievements of an organization or entity over a year. They can be used for various purposes, such as informing, reporting, disclosing, etc.
- Some of the common features of business correspondence and report writing are:
  - Purpose: the specific goal or objective of the communication. It should be clear, relevant, and realistic.
  - Audience: the intended recipients or readers of the communication. They should be identified, analyzed, and addressed appropriately.
  - Tone: the attitude or emotion that is conveyed by the communication. It should be suitable, consistent, and respectful.
  - Medium: the channel or mode of communication. It should be selected, designed, and used effectively.
  - Format: the structure and layout of the communication. It should be organized, logical, and attractive.
  - Content: the information and data that are presented in the communication. They should be accurate, reliable, and complete.
