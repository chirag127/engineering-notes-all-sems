# Students should practice the language with variety of activities and exercises based on employability skills. Interactive and Communicative Practical with emphasis on Oral Presentation/Spoken Communication, based on International Phonetic Alphabets (I.P.A.)

- Language is a vital skill for employability in the globalized world. It enables people to communicate effectively, express their ideas and opinions, and collaborate with others in different contexts and situations.
- To master a language, students need to practice it with a variety of activities and exercises that are based on employability skills. Employability skills are the abilities and attributes that employers look for in potential employees, such as teamwork, problem-solving, creativity, communication, and digital literacy.
- Some examples of activities and exercises that can help students practice the language and develop employability skills are:
  - Role-playing scenarios that simulate real-life situations in the workplace, such as customer service, negotiation, or conflict resolution.
  - Group projects that require students to research, plan, and present a topic related to their field of study or interest, using different media and formats.
  - Debates and discussions that encourage students to express their views, listen to others, and provide constructive feedback.
  - Games and quizzes that test students' vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation, as well as their general knowledge and cultural awareness.
  - Online platforms and tools that allow students to interact with native speakers, exchange information, and practice the language in authentic contexts.
- One of the key aspects of practicing the language is the interactive and communicative practical, which emphasizes oral presentation and spoken communication. Oral presentation and spoken communication are essential skills for employability, as they demonstrate students' confidence, clarity, and persuasiveness.
- To improve their oral presentation and spoken communication skills, students need to pay attention to the International Phonetic Alphabets (I.P.A.), which are a system of symbols that represent the sounds of a language. The I.P.A. can help students to:
  - Pronounce words correctly and avoid misunderstandings or miscommunication.
  - Identify and produce the sounds that are specific to a language or a dialect, such as the /θ/ sound in English or the /r/ sound in Spanish.
  - Learn the stress, intonation, and rhythm patterns of a language, which convey meaning and emotion.
  - Compare and contrast the sounds of different languages and accents, and appreciate the diversity and richness of languages.
- To practice the I.P.A., students can use the following resources and strategies:
  - Dictionaries and online tools that provide the I.P.A. transcription of words and sentences, as well as audio recordings and examples.
  - Charts and diagrams that illustrate the articulation and classification of the sounds, such as the place and manner of production, the voicing, and the vowel quality.
  - Exercises and drills that help students to recognize, transcribe, and produce the sounds, such as minimal pairs, tongue twisters, and dictation.
  - Feedback and correction from teachers, peers, or native speakers, who can point out the errors and suggest improvements.
  - Self-monitoring and self-evaluation, which involve recording and listening to one's own speech, and identifying the strengths and weaknesses.
