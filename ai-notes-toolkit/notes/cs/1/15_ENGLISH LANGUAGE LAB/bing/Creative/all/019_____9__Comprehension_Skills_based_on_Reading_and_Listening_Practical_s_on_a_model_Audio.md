# 9. Comprehension Skills based on Reading and Listening Practical’s on a model Audio

- Comprehension skills are the ability to understand and interpret information from various sources, such as texts, speeches, images, etc.
- Reading and listening are two of the most common and important sources of information in academic and professional contexts.
- Reading comprehension is the ability to read a text and extract its main idea, supporting details, inferences, and implications.
- Listening comprehension is the ability to listen to an audio and identify its purpose, tone, structure, and key points.
- Practicing reading and listening comprehension skills can help improve one's vocabulary, grammar, critical thinking, and communication skills.
- A model audio is a sample of an authentic or simulated speech that can be used for listening comprehension practice.
- A model audio can be based on various topics, such as news, lectures, interviews, conversations, etc.
- A model audio can have different levels of difficulty, depending on the speed, accent, vocabulary, and complexity of the speech.
- A model audio can be accompanied by questions, tasks, or activities that test the listener's comprehension skills.
- Some examples of questions, tasks, or activities based on a model audio are:

  - Multiple choice questions: The listener has to choose the correct answer from a list of options based on the audio.
  - True or false questions: The listener has to decide whether a statement is true or false based on the audio.
  - Fill in the blanks: The listener has to complete a sentence or a paragraph with the missing words based on the audio.
  - Matching: The listener has to match words, phrases, or sentences with their meanings, synonyms, or examples based on the audio.
  - Sequencing: The listener has to arrange the events, steps, or stages of a process in the correct order based on the audio.
  - Summarizing: The listener has to write a brief summary of the main idea and the key points of the audio.
  - Paraphrasing: The listener has to rewrite a sentence or a paragraph from the audio in their own words, without changing the meaning.
  - Inferring: The listener has to draw conclusions or make predictions based on the audio.
  - Evaluating: The listener has to express their opinion or judgment about the audio, using evidence and reasoning.
  - Comparing and contrasting: The listener has to identify the similarities and differences between two or more audios, or between an audio and a text.
