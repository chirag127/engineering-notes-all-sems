# Software based self-guided learning to provide the required English language proficiency to students from an employability and career readiness standpoint

- Software based self-guided learning is a type of learning that uses computer software to deliver personalized and adaptive instruction to learners, without the need for a human teacher or tutor.
- The software can provide feedback, assessment, and guidance to learners based on their performance, preferences, and goals.
- The software can also track and monitor the learners' progress and achievements, and provide them with reports and certificates.
- The software can be accessed online or offline, through various devices such as computers, tablets, or smartphones.
- The software can be designed for different levels, domains, and purposes of English language learning, such as general, academic, or professional English.
- The software can also incorporate various features and elements to enhance the learning experience, such as multimedia, gamification, social interaction, and artificial intelligence.

## The software should align to Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) and deliver a CEFR level – B2 upon completion

- The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) is a set of standards that describe the levels of language proficiency for learners of foreign languages.
- The CEFR defines six levels of proficiency, from A1 (beginner) to C2 (proficient), based on the learners' ability to communicate in different situations and contexts.
- The CEFR also provides descriptors and criteria for each level, covering the four skills of listening, reading, speaking, and writing, as well as the aspects of language use such as grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation.
- The CEFR is widely used and recognized by educational institutions, employers, and organizations around the world, as a common reference for language learning, teaching, and assessment.
- The CEFR level B2 is considered as the upper-intermediate level of English language proficiency, and it corresponds to the following description:

  - Can understand the main ideas of complex texts on both concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in his/her field of specialization.
  - Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain for either party.
  - Can produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects and explain a viewpoint on a topical issue giving the advantages and disadvantages of various options.

- The software should align to the CEFR by providing learners with the appropriate content, tasks, and feedback that match their level and goals, and by measuring their progress and achievements according to the CEFR standards and criteria.
- The software should deliver a CEFR level B2 upon completion by ensuring that the learners have acquired the knowledge and skills required for this level, and by providing them with a valid and reliable certificate that attests their level of proficiency.
