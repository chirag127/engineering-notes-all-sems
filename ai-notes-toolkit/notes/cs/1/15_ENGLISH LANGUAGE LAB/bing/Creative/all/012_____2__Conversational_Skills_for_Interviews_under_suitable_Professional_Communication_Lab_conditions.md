# 2. Conversational Skills for Interviews under suitable Professional Communication Lab conditions with emphasis on Kinesics

- Conversational skills are the ability to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in various situations, such as interviews, meetings, presentations, etc.
- Interviews are formal interactions between an interviewer and an interviewee, where the interviewer evaluates the interviewee's suitability for a job, a project, a scholarship, or any other opportunity.
- Professional communication lab conditions are the settings and equipment that simulate a real-life interview scenario, such as a video conference, a microphone, a camera, a screen, etc.
- Kinesics is the study of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, and movement, and how they convey meaning and emotion in communication.
- Emphasis on kinesics means paying attention to how one's body language affects the impression and message one gives to the interviewer, and how to use it to enhance one's conversational skills.

Some points to remember when using conversational skills for interviews under suitable professional communication lab conditions with emphasis on kinesics are:

- Prepare well for the interview by researching the organization, the role, the interviewer, and the common questions and topics that might be asked.
- Dress appropriately and professionally for the interview, and make sure the background and lighting are clear and suitable for the video conference.
- Greet the interviewer politely and confidently, and introduce yourself briefly and clearly.
- Maintain a positive and respectful tone and attitude throughout the interview, and avoid using slang, jargon, filler words, or negative expressions.
- Use active listening skills, such as nodding, smiling, paraphrasing, asking questions, and giving feedback, to show interest and engagement in the conversation.
- Use appropriate body language, such as leaning forward, keeping an open posture, gesturing naturally, and maintaining eye contact, to convey confidence, enthusiasm, and sincerity.
- Avoid distracting or negative body language, such as fidgeting, crossing arms, touching face, looking away, or slouching, to avoid giving the impression of nervousness, boredom, or dishonesty.
- Answer the questions clearly and concisely, and provide relevant examples and evidence to support your points. Use the STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) method to structure your answers.
- Ask relevant and thoughtful questions at the end of the interview, and thank the interviewer for their time and consideration.
- Follow up with a thank-you email or note, and restate your interest and qualifications for the opportunity.
