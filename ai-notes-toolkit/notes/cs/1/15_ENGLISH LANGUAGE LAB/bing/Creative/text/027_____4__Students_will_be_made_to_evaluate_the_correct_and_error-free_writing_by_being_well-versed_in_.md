To evaluate the correct and error-free writing, students need to be well-versed in the rules of English grammar. Some of the rules that students should know and apply are:

- Subject-verb agreement: The subject and the verb of a sentence must agree in number and person. For example, "He writes well." (singular subject and verb) and "They write well." (plural subject and verb).
- Pronoun-antecedent agreement: The pronoun and the antecedent (the word that the pronoun refers to) must agree in number, gender, and person. For example, "She likes her job." (singular feminine pronoun and antecedent) and "They like their jobs." (plural pronoun and antecedent).
- Sentence structure: A sentence must have a subject and a predicate (the part that tells what the subject does or is). A sentence can be simple (one independent clause), compound (two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction or a semicolon), complex (one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses), or compound-complex (two or more independent clauses and one or more dependent clauses). For example, "She studied hard and passed the exam." (compound sentence) and "Because she studied hard, she passed the exam." (complex sentence).
- Punctuation: Punctuation marks are used to separate and clarify the parts of a sentence, such as commas, periods, colons, semicolons, quotation marks, apostrophes, hyphens, dashes, parentheses, and brackets. For example, "He said, 'I'm hungry.'" (quotation marks to indicate direct speech) and "It's a beautiful day." (apostrophe to indicate contraction).
- Spelling: Spelling is the correct way of writing a word according to the standard rules of the language. Spelling errors can affect the meaning and credibility of the writing. For example, "accept" (to receive or agree) and "except" (to exclude or leave out) are two different words with different spellings and meanings.
- Capitalization: Capitalization is the use of uppercase letters for certain words or parts of words in a sentence, such as the first word of a sentence, proper nouns, titles, acronyms, and abbreviations. For example, "She works at Microsoft." (capitalization of a proper noun) and "She works as a CEO." (capitalization of an abbreviation).
