### 4. Presentation Skills for Technical Paper/Project Reports/ proposals based on proper Stress and Intonation Mechanics

- Presentation skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and confidently with an audience, using appropriate verbal and non-verbal cues, visual aids, and structure.
- Technical paper/project reports/proposals are types of academic or professional documents that present the results of research, analysis, design, or development of a specific topic or problem.
- Stress and intonation mechanics are the aspects of speech that convey emphasis, emotion, attitude, and meaning through variations in loudness, pitch, and duration of syllables and words.
- To improve presentation skills for technical paper/project reports/proposals based on proper stress and intonation mechanics, one should follow these steps:

  - Plan the presentation: Identify the purpose, audience, and main message of the presentation. Outline the main points and supporting details. Organize the presentation into a clear introduction, body, and conclusion. Choose the appropriate format, style, and tone for the presentation. Select and prepare the visual aids and materials.
  - Practice the presentation: Rehearse the presentation several times, preferably in front of a mirror, a recorder, or a friend. Pay attention to the content, delivery, and timing of the presentation. Adjust the stress and intonation of the speech to emphasize the key words, phrases, and sentences. Use pauses, pitch changes, and volume variations to create interest and clarity. Avoid monotone, mumbling, or rushing the speech. Seek feedback and improve the presentation accordingly.
  - Deliver the presentation: Arrive early and check the equipment and the room. Dress appropriately and professionally. Greet the audience and introduce yourself and the topic. Establish eye contact, posture, and gestures that convey confidence and engagement. Speak clearly, loudly, and fluently. Use stress and intonation to convey the meaning and tone of the presentation. Use transitions, signposts, and summaries to guide the audience through the presentation. Engage the audience with questions, examples, and anecdotes. Conclude the presentation with a summary, a recommendation, or a call to action. Thank the audience and invite questions or comments. Handle the questions or comments politely and effectively.
