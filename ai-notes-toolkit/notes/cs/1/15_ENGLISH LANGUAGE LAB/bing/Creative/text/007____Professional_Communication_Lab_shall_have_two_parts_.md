## Professional Communication Lab shall have two parts:

- Part A: This part consists of oral communication skills and focuses on the following aspects:

  - Phonetics: The study of speech sounds, their production, transcription and classification.
  - Pronunciation: The correct and standard way of speaking words in a language.
  - Accent: The distinctive way of pronouncing a language, especially one associated with a particular country, region or social class.
  - Intonation: The rise and fall of the voice in speaking, especially as this affects the meaning and expression of sentences.
  - Stress: The emphasis given to a word or syllable in speaking, either by pronouncing it louder, higher or longer than others, or by changing the pitch or tone of the voice.
  - Rhythm: The pattern of sounds and silences in speech, created by the alternation of stressed and unstressed syllables and pauses.
  - Fluency: The ability to speak or write a language easily and smoothly, without hesitation, repetition or errors.
  - Coherence: The quality of being logical and consistent in speech or writing, and of forming a unified whole.
  - Cohesion: The use of words, phrases and grammatical devices to connect and relate sentences and paragraphs in speech or writing.
  - Paraphrasing: The act of expressing the meaning of something using different words, especially to achieve greater clarity or brevity.
  - Summarizing: The act of giving a brief and concise account of the main points of something, omitting details and examples.
  - Presentation skills: The ability to deliver a speech or a report to an audience effectively and confidently, using appropriate language, voice, body language and visual aids.

- Part B: This part consists of written communication skills and focuses on the following aspects:

  - Vocabulary: The set of words that a person knows or uses in a language, or that are available in a language or subject.
  - Grammar: The system and structure of a language, including the rules of word formation, word order, sentence structure and punctuation.
  - Spelling: The way of writing a word using the correct letters in the correct order.
  - Punctuation: The marks, such as commas, periods and question marks, that are used in writing to separate sentences and clauses, and to indicate pauses, emphasis and intonation.
  - Style: The way of writing or speaking that is characteristic of a particular person, group, period or genre, and that reflects their choices of words, sentence structures, tone and register.
  - Format: The way of arranging and presenting written or printed material, such as margins, fonts, headings, paragraphs and lists, according to a set of conventions or standards.
  - Citation: The act of acknowledging the source of information, ideas or words that are not one's own, by providing the author's name, date of publication and page number, or a link to the online source, in a specific style, such as APA, MLA or Chicago.
  - Plagiarism: The act of copying or using someone else's work or ideas without giving proper credit, and presenting them as one's own, either intentionally or unintentionally.
  - Editing: The act of reviewing and correcting a written or printed text, by checking for errors, inconsistencies, clarity, accuracy and completeness, and making changes or suggestions for improvement.
  - Proofreading: The act of reading a written or printed text carefully and attentively, to find and mark any errors or mistakes, such as spelling, grammar, punctuation and formatting, before the final publication or submission.
