### 2. Conversational Skills for Interviews under suitable Professional Communication Lab conditions with emphasis on Kinesics.

- Conversational skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in various contexts and situations, such as interviews.
- Interviews are formal interactions between an interviewer and an interviewee, where the interviewer evaluates the interviewee's suitability for a job, a project, a scholarship, or any other opportunity.
- Professional communication lab conditions are the settings and environments that simulate the real-world scenarios of interviews, such as mock interviews, video interviews, group discussions, etc.
- Kinesics is the study of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, and movement, and how they convey meaning and emotion in communication.
- Emphasis on kinesics means paying attention to and using the non-verbal cues of kinesics to enhance the verbal messages and create a positive impression in interviews.

Some points to remember while developing conversational skills for interviews under suitable professional communication lab conditions with emphasis on kinesics are:

- Prepare well for the interview by researching the organization, the role, the interviewer, and the common questions and topics that may be asked or discussed.
- Dress appropriately and professionally for the interview, according to the culture and norms of the organization and the industry.
- Arrive on time and greet the interviewer politely and confidently, with a firm handshake and a smile.
- Maintain good eye contact with the interviewer throughout the interview, but do not stare or look away too often. Eye contact shows interest, attention, and respect.
- Use gestures and facial expressions to emphasize and complement your verbal messages, but do not overdo them or use them inappropriately. Gestures and facial expressions can show enthusiasm, confidence, and sincerity, but they can also reveal nervousness, boredom, or dishonesty.
- Avoid crossing your arms, fidgeting, touching your face, or slouching, as these can indicate defensiveness, insecurity, or lack of interest. Instead, adopt a relaxed, open, and upright posture, and use your hands to gesture naturally and moderately.
- Match your tone, volume, and speed of speech to the situation and the interviewer, and avoid using filler words, slang, or jargon. Speak clearly, concisely, and coherently, and use appropriate language and vocabulary.
- Listen actively and attentively to the interviewer, and show that you understand and appreciate their questions and comments by nodding, smiling, or giving verbal feedback. Do not interrupt, argue, or contradict the interviewer, but ask for clarification or repetition if needed.
- Answer the questions honestly, positively, and relevantly, and provide examples or evidence to support your claims. Avoid giving vague, generic, or negative answers, and focus on your strengths, achievements, and goals.
- Ask relevant and insightful questions to the interviewer at the end of the interview, to show your interest, curiosity, and enthusiasm for the opportunity. Avoid asking questions that are too personal, trivial, or already answered.
- Thank the interviewer for their time and consideration, and express your interest and excitement for the opportunity. Follow up with a thank-you email or note, and restate your qualifications and suitability for the role.
