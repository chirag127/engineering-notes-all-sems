### 3. To train students to use the correct and error-free writing by being well versed in rules of English grammar.

- Writing is a skill that requires practice and feedback. Students need to learn the rules of English grammar and apply them in their writing to avoid errors and communicate effectively.
- Some of the rules of English grammar are:

  - Subject-verb agreement: The verb must agree with the subject in number and person. For example, "He runs fast." (singular subject, singular verb) and "They run fast." (plural subject, plural verb).
  - Sentence structure: A sentence must have a subject and a predicate. A subject is the person or thing that performs the action or is described by the predicate. A predicate is the part of the sentence that tells what the subject does or is. For example, "She sings beautifully." (subject: she, predicate: sings beautifully).
  - Punctuation: Punctuation marks are used to separate sentences, clauses, phrases, and words. They help to clarify the meaning and tone of the writing. Some of the common punctuation marks are:

    - Period (.) : Used to end a sentence or an abbreviation. For example, "I like pizza." and "Mr. Smith is a teacher."
    - Comma (,) : Used to separate items in a list, clauses in a compound sentence, or phrases that modify the main clause. For example, "She bought apples, oranges, and bananas." and "He was late, because he missed the bus."
    - Question mark (?) : Used to end a sentence that asks a question. For example, "What is your name?" and "Do you like chocolate?"
    - Exclamation mark (!) : Used to end a sentence that expresses strong emotion or surprise. For example, "Wow, that was amazing!" and "Stop, thief!"
    - Quotation marks (") : Used to enclose the exact words of a speaker or a writer. For example, "She said, "I am hungry." and "The title of the book is "The Catcher in the Rye.""
    - Apostrophe (') : Used to show possession or contraction. For example, "This is John's book." and "She's my friend."
    - Colon (:) : Used to introduce a list, a quotation, or an explanation. For example, "The ingredients are: flour, sugar, eggs, and butter." and "He said: "I have a dream.""
    - Semicolon (;) : Used to separate two independent clauses that are closely related in meaning or to separate items in a list that contain commas. For example, "She loves reading; he prefers watching movies." and "I visited Paris, France; Rome, Italy; and London, England."
    - Hyphen (-) : Used to join two or more words that form a compound word or to separate syllables of a word that is divided at the end of a line. For example, "She is a well-known author." and "The word is pro-nun-ci-a-tion."
    - Dash (--) : Used to indicate a sudden break or change in thought or to emphasize a word or phrase. For example, "He was a good friend--until he betrayed me." and "She is very smart--and modest too."

- To train students to use the correct and error-free writing by being well versed in rules of English grammar, teachers can:

  - Provide clear and concise explanations and examples of the rules of English grammar.
  - Assign regular writing tasks that require students to apply the rules of English grammar in different contexts and genres.
  - Give constructive and specific feedback on students' writing, pointing out the errors and suggesting ways to improve them.
  - Encourage students to revise and edit their writing, using tools such as dictionaries, grammar checkers, and peer review.
  - Expose students to various forms of authentic and quality writing, such as books, articles, blogs, and essays, and discuss the grammar and style of the writers.
  - Create a positive and supportive learning environment that fosters students' confidence and interest in writing.
