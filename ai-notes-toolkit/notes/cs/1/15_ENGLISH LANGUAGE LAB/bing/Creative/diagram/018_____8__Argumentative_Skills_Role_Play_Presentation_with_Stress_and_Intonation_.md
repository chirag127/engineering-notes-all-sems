### 8. Argumentative Skills/Role Play Presentation with Stress and Intonation

- Argumentative skills are the ability to present and defend a position or opinion on an issue using logical reasoning and evidence.
- Role play presentation is a type of oral communication where the speaker assumes a specific role or character and interacts with other speakers or audience members in a simulated situation.
- Stress and intonation are the features of speech that convey the speaker's attitude, emotion, emphasis, and tone.
- To improve your argumentative skills and role play presentation with stress and intonation, you should:

  - Identify the purpose and audience of your argument or role play. What are you trying to achieve and who are you trying to persuade or entertain?
  - Research and organize your information and evidence. Use credible sources and logical arguments to support your claims and counterclaims. Arrange your points in a clear and coherent structure, such as introduction, body, and conclusion.
  - Use appropriate language and style for your argument or role play. Choose words and expressions that suit your role, topic, and audience. Avoid informal, vague, or offensive language. Use rhetorical devices, such as repetition, contrast, or analogy, to enhance your message.
  - Use stress and intonation to convey your meaning and attitude. Stress the important words and syllables in your sentences to show emphasis, contrast, or new information. Use intonation to show your feelings, questions, or statements. For example, use a rising intonation to show surprise, doubt, or interest, and a falling intonation to show certainty, agreement, or finality.
  - Practice and rehearse your argument or role play. Record yourself and listen to your speech. Check if your stress and intonation are clear and appropriate. Ask for feedback from others and make improvements.
