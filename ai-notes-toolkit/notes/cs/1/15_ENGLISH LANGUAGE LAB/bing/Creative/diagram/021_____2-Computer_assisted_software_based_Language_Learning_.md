### 2-Computer assisted software based Language Learning:

- Computer assisted software based language learning (CASLL) is a subset of computer assisted language learning (CALL), which is an approach to teaching and learning languages that uses computers and other technologies to present, reinforce, and assess material to be learned, or to create environments where teachers and learners can interact with one another and the outside world.
- CASLL focuses on the use of software applications that are specifically designed for language learning purposes, such as vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, reading, writing, listening, and speaking. Some examples of CASLL software are Rosetta Stone, Duolingo, Fluenz, and Lingoda.
- CASLL has some advantages and disadvantages in enhanced language learning. Some of the advantages are:
  - It can provide individualized, adaptive, and interactive learning experiences that cater to the learners' needs, preferences, and pace.
  - It can offer immediate and consistent feedback, correction, and reinforcement that can motivate and guide the learners.
  - It can expose the learners to authentic and diverse language input and output, such as texts, audio, video, images, and speech.
  - It can facilitate collaborative and communicative learning activities, such as peer feedback, group work, and online discussions.
  - It can enhance the learners' autonomy, self-regulation, and metacognitive skills, such as goal setting, planning, monitoring, and evaluating.
- Some of the disadvantages are:
  - It can be costly, time-consuming, and technically challenging to develop, maintain, and update the software applications.
  - It can require adequate infrastructure, equipment, and support to ensure the quality and accessibility of the software applications.
  - It can pose some pedagogical, ethical, and social issues, such as the appropriateness, validity, and reliability of the software content and assessment, the privacy and security of the learners' data and identity, and the impact of the software on the learners' motivation, attitude, and interaction.
  - It can not replace the human element and the social context of language learning, such as the role of the teacher, the culture, and the community.
