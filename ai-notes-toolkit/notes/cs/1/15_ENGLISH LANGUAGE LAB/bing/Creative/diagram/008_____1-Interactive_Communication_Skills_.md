### 1-Interactive Communication Skills:

- Interactive communication skills are the abilities that enable a person to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in various situations, such as conversations, meetings, presentations, interviews, etc.
- Interactive communication skills involve both verbal and non-verbal elements, such as words, tone, body language, facial expressions, gestures, eye contact, etc.
- Interactive communication skills are important for building rapport, trust, understanding, and cooperation with others, as well as for expressing oneself clearly, confidently, and persuasively.
- Some of the key interactive communication skills are:

  - Active listening: This means paying attention to what the other person is saying, showing interest and empathy, asking questions, paraphrasing, and giving feedback.
  - Asking open-ended questions: These are questions that invite the other person to share more information, opinions, feelings, or ideas, rather than giving a yes or no answer. For example, "What do you think about this project?" instead of "Do you like this project?"
  - Giving constructive feedback: This means providing specific, positive, and negative comments on the other person's performance, behavior, or work, with the intention of helping them improve or learn. For example, "You did a great job on the report, but I noticed some spelling errors that you need to correct."
  - Assertiveness: This means expressing one's needs, opinions, feelings, or rights in a respectful and confident way, without being aggressive or passive. For example, "I appreciate your offer, but I have already made other plans."
  - Negotiation: This means finding a mutually acceptable solution to a conflict or disagreement, by compromising, collaborating, or persuading. For example, "I understand your point of view, but I think we can both benefit from this option."
  - Persuasion: This means influencing the other person's attitudes, beliefs, or actions, by using logical arguments, emotional appeals, or evidence. For example, "If you join our team, you will have more opportunities to grow and learn."
  - Presentation: This means delivering a clear, engaging, and informative speech or talk to an audience, by using appropriate language, structure, visuals, and delivery techniques. For example, "Today, I will talk about the benefits of our new product."
