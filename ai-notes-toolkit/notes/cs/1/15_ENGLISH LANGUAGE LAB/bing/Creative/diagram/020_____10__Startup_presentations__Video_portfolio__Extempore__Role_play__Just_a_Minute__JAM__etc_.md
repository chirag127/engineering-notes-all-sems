### 10. Startup presentations, Video portfolio, Extempore, Role play, Just a Minute (JAM) etc.

These are some of the common types of activities that are used to assess the communication skills, creativity, and personality of candidates in various fields. Here are some brief descriptions and tips for each of them:

- **Startup presentations**: These are short pitches that showcase an idea for a new business or product. They usually include the problem, the solution, the market, the revenue model, the team, and the competitive advantage. The purpose of a startup presentation is to convince potential investors, customers, or partners to support the idea. Some tips for a successful startup presentation are:

  - Use a clear and catchy title that summarizes the main value proposition of the idea.
  - Use simple and engaging language that avoids jargon and technical terms.
  - Use visuals, such as graphs, charts, images, or videos, to illustrate the key points and make the presentation more appealing.
  - Focus on the benefits and outcomes of the idea, not the features and details.
  - Anticipate and address the possible questions and objections from the audience.
  - Practice and rehearse the presentation before delivering it.

- **Video portfolio**: This is a collection of short videos that showcase the skills, achievements, and personality of a candidate. It can be used for applying to jobs, scholarships, or educational programs. The purpose of a video portfolio is to demonstrate the candidate's abilities and potential in a more dynamic and personal way than a resume or a cover letter. Some tips for a successful video portfolio are:

  - Choose the best examples of your work that are relevant to the position or opportunity you are applying for.
  - Use a good quality camera, microphone, and lighting to record the videos.
  - Keep the videos short, concise, and focused. Avoid rambling or repeating yourself.
  - Use a clear and confident voice and maintain eye contact with the camera.
  - Be yourself and show your passion and enthusiasm for the work you do.
  - Edit and polish the videos to remove any errors, pauses, or distractions.

- **Extempore**: This is a spontaneous speech that is given on a topic that is assigned on the spot. It can be used for testing the candidate's ability to think on their feet, express their thoughts clearly, and handle pressure. The purpose of an extempore speech is to showcase the candidate's knowledge, logic, and creativity on a given topic. Some tips for a successful extempore speech are:

  - Take a few seconds to think of a main idea and a structure for your speech. You can use a simple format, such as introduction, body, and conclusion, or a more specific one, such as problem, cause, effect, and solution.
  - Use examples, facts, statistics, or anecdotes to support your points and make your speech more interesting.
  - Use transitions, such as firstly, secondly, moreover, however, or in conclusion, to connect your points and make your speech more coherent.
  - Use a confident and expressive tone and body language to convey your message and engage the audience.
  - Avoid filler words, such as um, uh, like, or you know, and use pauses instead to collect your thoughts.

- **Role play**: This is a simulated situation that involves acting out a specific role or scenario. It can be used for testing the candidate's interpersonal skills, problem-solving skills, and adaptability. The purpose of a role play is to evaluate how the candidate would behave and react in a real-life situation. Some tips for a successful role play are:

  - Read and understand the instructions and the objectives of the role play carefully.
  - Research and prepare for the role and the scenario beforehand, if possible. Learn about the background, the context, the expectations, and the challenges of the situation.
  - Stay in character and act according to the role and the scenario. Do not break the fourth wall or deviate from the script.
  - Use appropriate verbal and non-verbal communication skills to interact with the other participants and achieve the desired outcome.
  - Be flexible and creative in dealing with any unexpected events or complications that may arise during the role play.
