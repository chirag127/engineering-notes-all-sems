#### Students should practice the language with variety of activities and exercises based on employability skills. Interactive and Communicative Practical with emphasis on Oral Presentation/Spoken Communication, based on International Phonetic Alphabets (I.P.A.)

- Language is a vital skill for employability in the globalized world. It enables communication, collaboration, and creativity in various contexts and domains.
- To master a language, students need to practice it with a variety of activities and exercises that are relevant, engaging, and challenging. These activities and exercises should be based on employability skills, such as teamwork, problem-solving, critical thinking, and intercultural awareness.
- Interactive and communicative practicals are effective ways to practice the language with other learners and native speakers. They involve authentic tasks, scenarios, and simulations that require students to use the language in meaningful and purposeful ways.
- Oral presentation and spoken communication are essential skills for employability, especially in the fields of business, education, and media. They require students to express their ideas, opinions, and arguments clearly, confidently, and persuasively.
- International Phonetic Alphabets (I.P.A.) are a system of symbols that represent the sounds of any language. They help students to improve their pronunciation, accent, and intonation, as well as to understand the differences and similarities between languages.
- Some examples of activities and exercises based on employability skills and I.P.A. are:

  - Role-playing a job interview, a sales pitch, or a negotiation, using appropriate language and I.P.A. symbols to indicate the stress, rhythm, and intonation of the speech.
  - Creating and delivering a presentation on a topic related to the students' field of study or interest, using visual aids, gestures, and I.P.A. symbols to enhance the clarity and impact of the message.
  - Participating in a group discussion or debate on a controversial or current issue, using relevant vocabulary, grammar, and I.P.A. symbols to express and support their views, as well as to listen and respond to others.
  - Reading and listening to texts from different genres, sources, and accents, and identifying and transcribing the sounds and words using I.P.A. symbols, as well as analyzing the meaning, tone, and purpose of the texts.
  - Playing games and quizzes that test and reinforce the students' knowledge of I.P.A. symbols, such as matching, sorting, filling in the blanks, and guessing the word or sound.
