### 4. To cultivate relevant technical style of communication and presentation at their work place and also for academic uses.

Technical style of communication and presentation is the way of conveying information and ideas in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, using appropriate language, format, and visuals for a specific audience and purpose. Technical style of communication and presentation is important for both work place and academic uses, as it can help to:

- Achieve the intended goals and objectives of the communication or presentation, such as informing, persuading, instructing, or entertaining.
- Enhance the credibility and professionalism of the communicator or presenter, as well as the organization or institution they represent.
- Avoid misunderstandings, errors, or confusion that may arise from ambiguous, inaccurate, or incomplete information or messages.
- Save time and resources by reducing the need for clarification, repetition, or correction of the communication or presentation.
- Increase the engagement and satisfaction of the audience or recipients, as well as their comprehension and retention of the information or ideas.

To cultivate relevant technical style of communication and presentation, one should:

- Analyze the purpose, audience, and context of the communication or presentation, and tailor the content, tone, and format accordingly.
- Use clear, concise, and precise language, avoiding jargon, slang, or unnecessary words that may obscure or confuse the message.
- Use appropriate grammar, spelling, punctuation, and vocabulary, following the conventions and standards of the chosen language and field of study or work.
- Use logical and coherent structure, organization, and transitions, following the principles of introduction, body, and conclusion, and using headings, subheadings, bullet points, or numbering to highlight the main points and subpoints.
- Use relevant and accurate information, data, facts, and evidence, citing the sources and references properly and ethically, and avoiding plagiarism, fabrication, or distortion of the information or ideas.
- Use suitable and effective visuals, such as graphs, charts, tables, diagrams, images, or videos, to complement, illustrate, or emphasize the information or ideas, and to appeal to the visual learners or preferences of the audience or recipients.
- Use appropriate and consistent format, style, and layout, following the guidelines and requirements of the chosen medium, platform, or genre of the communication or presentation, such as report, memo, email, letter, proposal, essay, article, poster, slide, or video.
- Use appropriate and respectful tone, voice, and body language, considering the cultural, social, and emotional aspects of the communication or presentation, and avoiding offensive, biased, or inappropriate expressions or gestures.
- Use feedback and evaluation, seeking and providing constructive and honest comments and suggestions on the strengths and weaknesses of the communication or presentation, and using them to improve and refine the technical style of communication and presentation.
