#### Software based self-guided learning to provide the required English language proficiency to students from an employability and career readiness standpoint. The software should align to Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) and deliver a CEFR level – B2 upon completion.

- Software based self-guided learning is a form of education that uses computer programs and applications to deliver personalized and interactive instruction to learners, without the need for a teacher or a classroom.
- The software should provide the required English language proficiency to students from an employability and career readiness standpoint, meaning that it should help them develop the skills and knowledge that are relevant and useful for their future careers and workplaces, such as communication, collaboration, problem-solving, critical thinking, and digital literacy.
- The software should align to Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), which is a set of standards that describe the levels of language proficiency for different languages, from A1 (beginner) to C2 (proficient). The software should deliver a CEFR level – B2 upon completion, which means that the learners should be able to understand the main ideas of complex texts on both concrete and abstract topics, interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain for either party, and produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects and explain a viewpoint on a topical issue giving the advantages and disadvantages of various options.
- The software should have the following features and characteristics to achieve its objectives:

  - It should be adaptive, meaning that it should adjust the level of difficulty and the content of the lessons according to the learner's progress and performance, providing feedback and guidance along the way.
  - It should be engaging, meaning that it should use multimedia, gamification, and social elements to motivate and retain the learner's interest and attention, and to make the learning experience enjoyable and rewarding.
  - It should be comprehensive, meaning that it should cover all the four skills of language learning: listening, speaking, reading, and writing, as well as grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation, and provide ample opportunities for practice and assessment.
  - It should be flexible, meaning that it should allow the learner to access the software anytime, anywhere, and on any device, and to choose the pace, order, and duration of the lessons according to their preferences and needs.
  - It should be relevant, meaning that it should provide authentic and meaningful contexts and scenarios for language use, and expose the learner to a variety of accents, registers, and styles of English, especially those that are related to their career goals and interests.
