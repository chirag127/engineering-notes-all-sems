# 3. Communication Skills for Seminars/Conferences/Workshops with emphasis on Paralinguistic/Kinesics

- Communication skills are essential for effective participation in seminars, conferences and workshops, where one has to present, discuss and interact with others on a specific topic or theme.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features are aspects of nonverbal communication that complement and enhance the verbal message, such as voice quality, pitch, volume, rate, pauses, facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, posture and movement.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features can convey various meanings and emotions, such as interest, enthusiasm, confidence, boredom, confusion, anger, surprise, etc. They can also influence the perception and impression of the speaker and the listener, such as credibility, competence, professionalism, rapport, etc.
- Some tips for using paralinguistic and kinesic features effectively in seminars, conferences and workshops are:

  - Use a clear, loud and varied voice that suits the purpose, audience and context of the presentation or discussion. Avoid monotone, mumbling, stuttering, filler words and vocalized pauses.
  - Use appropriate facial expressions that match the tone and content of the speech. Avoid frowning, grimacing, yawning, rolling eyes and showing boredom or disinterest.
  - Use eye contact to establish connection, engagement and feedback with the audience. Avoid staring, looking away, looking down or looking at notes too often.
  - Use gestures to emphasize, illustrate and clarify the verbal message. Avoid excessive, distracting, repetitive or inappropriate gestures that may confuse or offend the audience.
  - Use posture and movement to show confidence, openness and energy. Avoid slouching, leaning, crossing arms or legs, fidgeting, pacing or standing still.
