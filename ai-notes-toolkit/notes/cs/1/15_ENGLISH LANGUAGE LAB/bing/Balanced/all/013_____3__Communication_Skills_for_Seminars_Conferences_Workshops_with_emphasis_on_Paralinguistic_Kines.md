# 3. Communication Skills for Seminars/Conferences/Workshops with emphasis on Paralinguistic/Kinesics

- Communication skills are essential for effective participation in seminars, conferences and workshops, where one has to present, discuss and interact with others on a specific topic or theme.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features are aspects of nonverbal communication that complement and enhance the verbal message, such as voice quality, pitch, volume, rate, pauses, facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, posture and movement.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features can convey information about the speaker's attitude, emotion, intention, confidence, credibility, interest, rapport and feedback, as well as clarify, emphasize, modify or contradict the verbal message.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features can also influence the listener's perception, comprehension, response and evaluation of the speaker and the message, as well as the overall communication process and outcome.
- Some examples of paralinguistic and kinesic features and their functions in communication are:

  - Voice quality: The tone, timbre, resonance and clarity of the voice can indicate the speaker's mood, personality, health and professionalism. For example, a clear, warm and pleasant voice can create a positive impression and engage the listener, while a harsh, nasal or monotonous voice can have the opposite effect.
  - Pitch: The highness or lowness of the voice can signal the speaker's emotion, attitude and emphasis. For example, a high pitch can express excitement, surprise, anger or fear, while a low pitch can express calmness, seriousness, authority or boredom. A varied pitch can also add interest and expression to the speech, while a flat pitch can make it dull and boring.
  - Volume: The loudness or softness of the voice can indicate the speaker's confidence, intention and attention. For example, a loud voice can express confidence, assertiveness, enthusiasm or urgency, while a soft voice can express shyness, politeness, hesitation or intimacy. A loud voice can also attract and maintain the listener's attention, while a soft voice can lose it or invite interruption.
  - Rate: The speed or pace of the speech can signal the speaker's emotion, attitude and knowledge. For example, a fast rate can express excitement, nervousness, impatience or competence, while a slow rate can express calmness, boredom, uncertainty or ignorance. A fast rate can also convey a lot of information in a short time, while a slow rate can allow more time for processing and understanding.
  - Pauses: The breaks or silences in the speech can indicate the speaker's thinking, feeling and emphasis. For example, a pause can express hesitation, doubt, confusion or emotion, while a deliberate pause can create suspense, anticipation, attention or emphasis. A pause can also allow the speaker to breathe, organize, revise or check the message, as well as give the listener time to respond, ask or comment.
  - Facial expressions: The movements and changes of the face can indicate the speaker's emotion, attitude and reaction. For example, a smile can express happiness, friendliness, approval or agreement, while a frown can express sadness, anger, disapproval or disagreement. A raised eyebrow can express surprise, curiosity, skepticism or doubt, while a wink can express humor, flirtation, complicity or secrecy.
  - Eye contact: The direction and duration of the gaze can indicate the speaker's interest, attention and rapport. For example, a direct eye contact can express confidence, honesty, respect or interest, while an indirect eye contact can express shyness, dishonesty, disrespect or disinterest. A prolonged eye contact can also create intimacy, trust or dominance, while a brief eye contact can create distance, detachment or submission.
  - Gestures: The movements and positions of the hands, arms, head and body can indicate the speaker's intention, meaning and emphasis. For example, a pointing gesture can express direction, instruction, accusation or attention, while a nodding gesture can express agreement, affirmation, understanding or encouragement. A waving gesture can express greeting, farewell, invitation or dismissal, while a shrugging gesture can express indifference, uncertainty, confusion or apology.
  - Posture: The way the speaker stands or sits can indicate the speaker's confidence, attitude and status.
