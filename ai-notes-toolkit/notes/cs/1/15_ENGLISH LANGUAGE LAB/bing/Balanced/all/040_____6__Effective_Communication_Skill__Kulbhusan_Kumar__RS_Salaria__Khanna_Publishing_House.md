# 6. Effective Communication Skill, Kulbhusan Kumar, RS Salaria, Khanna Publishing House

- Effective communication skill is the ability to convey information and ideas clearly, accurately, and persuasively to others, using verbal and non-verbal means.
- Effective communication skill is essential for personal and professional success, as it helps to build trust, rapport, and understanding among people, and to achieve desired outcomes and goals.
- Effective communication skill involves four main components: sender, message, channel, and receiver. The sender is the person who initiates the communication, the message is the information or idea that is being communicated, the channel is the medium or mode of communication, and the receiver is the person who receives and interprets the message.
- Effective communication skill also requires the following elements: feedback, context, noise, and barriers. Feedback is the response or reaction of the receiver to the message, which helps the sender to evaluate the effectiveness of the communication. Context is the situation or environment in which the communication takes place, which influences the meaning and interpretation of the message. Noise is any disturbance or interference that affects the quality or clarity of the message, such as background sounds, distractions, or technical problems. Barriers are any obstacles or factors that hinder or prevent effective communication, such as language differences, cultural differences, emotional states, or biases.
- Effective communication skill can be improved by following some general principles and guidelines, such as:
  - Know your purpose and audience: Before communicating, you should have a clear idea of what you want to achieve and who you are communicating with, and tailor your message accordingly.
  - Be clear and concise: You should use simple, direct, and precise language, and avoid unnecessary or ambiguous words, jargon, or slang. You should also organize your message in a logical and coherent manner, and use appropriate transitions and signposts to guide the receiver.
  - Be respectful and courteous: You should show respect and consideration for the receiver's feelings, opinions, and values, and avoid any offensive or insensitive language or behavior. You should also use polite and positive words, expressions, and tone, and acknowledge the receiver's feedback and concerns.
  - Be attentive and responsive: You should listen actively and attentively to the receiver's message, and show interest and engagement by using non-verbal cues, such as eye contact, nodding, or smiling. You should also provide constructive and timely feedback, and ask relevant and clarifying questions, to ensure mutual understanding and agreement.
  - Be adaptable and flexible: You should be aware of the context and the channel of communication, and adjust your message and style accordingly. You should also be open-minded and willing to accept different perspectives and viewpoints, and to modify or revise your message if needed.
