# 2. Conversational Skills for Interviews under suitable Professional Communication Lab conditions with emphasis on Kinesics.

- Conversational skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in various situations, such as interviews.
- Interviews are formal interactions between an interviewer and an interviewee, where the interviewer evaluates the interviewee's suitability for a job, a project, a scholarship, or any other opportunity.
- Professional communication lab conditions are the settings and equipment that simulate a real-life interview scenario, such as a video conference, a phone call, or a face-to-face meeting.
- Kinesics is the study of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and posture, and how they convey meaning and emotion in communication.
- Conversational skills for interviews under suitable professional communication lab conditions with emphasis on kinesics include:

  - Preparing for the interview by researching the organization, the role, and the interviewer, and rehearsing possible questions and answers.
  - Dressing appropriately and professionally for the interview, according to the culture and norms of the organization and the role.
  - Arriving on time and checking the technical aspects of the communication lab, such as the microphone, the camera, the internet connection, and the lighting.
  - Greeting the interviewer politely and confidently, and introducing oneself briefly and clearly.
  - Maintaining a positive and respectful attitude throughout the interview, and avoiding negative or defensive language.
  - Listening actively and attentively to the interviewer's questions and comments, and responding with relevant and concise answers that highlight one's skills, achievements, and motivations.
  - Using appropriate body language and gestures to support one's verbal communication, such as nodding, smiling, leaning forward, and gesturing with the hands.
  - Making eye contact with the interviewer to show interest and engagement, and avoiding looking away, down, or at the screen.
  - Modulating one's voice to convey confidence, enthusiasm, and clarity, and avoiding monotone, mumbling, or shouting.
  - Asking relevant and insightful questions at the end of the interview to show one's curiosity and interest in the organization and the role.
  - Thanking the interviewer for their time and consideration, and following up with a thank-you email or message.
