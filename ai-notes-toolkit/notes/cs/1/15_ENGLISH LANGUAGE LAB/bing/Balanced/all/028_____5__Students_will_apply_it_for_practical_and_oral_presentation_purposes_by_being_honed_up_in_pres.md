# Practical and Oral Presentation Skills

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

- Identify the purpose and audience of their presentations
- Plan and structure their presentations using appropriate tools and techniques
- Deliver their presentations with confidence, clarity and enthusiasm
- Use voice-dynamics, body language and visual aids to enhance their presentations
- Handle questions and feedback from the audience effectively
- Evaluate their own and others' presentations and identify areas for improvement
- Develop interpersonal communication skills and positive attitude for professional success

## Content

### 1. Purpose and Audience Analysis

- Before preparing a presentation, students should analyze the purpose and audience of their presentation.
- The purpose of a presentation is the main goal or message that the presenter wants to convey to the audience.
- The audience of a presentation is the group of people who will listen to and watch the presentation.
- Students should consider the following questions when analyzing the purpose and audience of their presentation:

  - What is the main goal or message of the presentation?
  - Who are the audience members and what are their backgrounds, interests, needs and expectations?
  - How much do the audience members know about the topic of the presentation?
  - What are the audience members' attitudes and opinions towards the topic of the presentation?
  - How can the presenter engage and persuade the audience members?

- Students should tailor their presentation content, style and delivery according to the purpose and audience of their presentation.

### 2. Planning and Structuring a Presentation

- After analyzing the purpose and audience of their presentation, students should plan and structure their presentation using appropriate tools and techniques.
- A presentation should have a clear and logical structure that consists of three main parts: introduction, body and conclusion.
- The introduction of a presentation should:

  - Capture the attention and interest of the audience
  - Establish the credibility and rapport of the presenter
  - Provide an overview of the presentation topic and outline the main points
  - State the purpose and objectives of the presentation

- The body of a presentation should:

  - Develop the main points of the presentation in a coherent and organized manner
  - Support the main points with relevant and credible evidence, such as facts, statistics, examples, anecdotes, quotes, etc.
  - Use transitions, signposts and summaries to guide the audience through the presentation
  - Address potential counterarguments, objections or questions from the audience

- The conclusion of a presentation should:

  - Summarize the main points and restate the purpose and objectives of the presentation
  - Emphasize the key takeaways and implications of the presentation
  - Provide a call to action or a recommendation for the audience
  - Thank the audience and invite questions and feedback

- Students should use appropriate tools and techniques to plan and structure their presentation, such as:

  - Brainstorming, mind mapping, outlining, etc. to generate and organize ideas
  - Researching, evaluating, citing, etc. to gather and present evidence
  - Drafting, revising, editing, etc. to refine and polish the presentation content
  - Practicing, rehearsing, timing, etc. to prepare for the presentation delivery

### 3. Delivering a Presentation

- After planning and structuring their presentation, students should deliver their presentation with confidence, clarity and enthusiasm.
- Students should use voice-dynamics, body language and visual aids to enhance their presentation delivery.
- Voice-dynamics refers to the use of vocal elements, such as volume, pitch, tone, pace, pause, etc. to convey meaning and emotion in a presentation.
- Students should use voice-dynamics to:

  - Adjust their volume according to the size and acoustics of the room, the distance from the audience and the importance of the message
  - Vary their pitch to avoid monotony and emphasize key words and phrases
  - Express their tone to match the mood and intention of the message
  - Control their pace to suit the complexity and urgency of the message
  - Use pauses to create suspense, signal a change of topic or allow the audience to process the information

- Body language refers to the use of non-verbal elements, such as eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture, movement, etc. to communicate and interact with the audience in a presentation.
