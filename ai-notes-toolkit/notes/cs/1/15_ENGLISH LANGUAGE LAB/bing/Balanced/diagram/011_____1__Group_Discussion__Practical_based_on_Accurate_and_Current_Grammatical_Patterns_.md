### 1. Group Discussion: Practical based on Accurate and Current Grammatical Patterns.

- A group discussion is a form of oral communication where a group of people exchange their views and opinions on a given topic.
- A group discussion can have various purposes, such as problem-solving, decision-making, brainstorming, or learning.
- A group discussion can also be used to assess the communication skills, interpersonal skills, critical thinking skills, and leadership skills of the participants.
- To participate effectively in a group discussion, one should follow some basic rules and etiquette, such as:
  - Be prepared with the topic and do some research beforehand.
  - Listen actively and respectfully to others and do not interrupt or dominate the conversation.
  - Speak clearly, confidently, and politely and use appropriate language and tone.
  - Express your views with relevant facts, examples, and arguments and avoid vague or irrelevant statements.
  - Acknowledge and appreciate the positive points of others and disagree constructively and politely if needed.
  - Summarize the main points and conclusions of the discussion at the end.
- One of the most important aspects of a group discussion is the use of accurate and current grammatical patterns. This means that one should use the correct forms and structures of the language and avoid grammatical errors and mistakes.
- Using accurate and current grammatical patterns can help one to communicate effectively and clearly and convey the intended meaning and message.
- Using accurate and current grammatical patterns can also help one to create a positive impression and demonstrate one's knowledge and proficiency in the language.
- Some of the common grammatical patterns that one should be familiar with and use correctly in a group discussion are:
  - Subject-verb agreement: The subject and the verb of a sentence should agree in number and person. For example, "He is a good speaker." (singular subject and verb) "They are good speakers." (plural subject and verb)
  - Tense and aspect: The tense and aspect of a verb indicate the time and duration of an action or state. One should use the appropriate tense and aspect according to the context and meaning. For example, "I have read this book." (present perfect tense, indicating a completed action in the past with a present relevance) "I am reading this book." (present continuous tense, indicating an ongoing action in the present)
  - Modals: Modals are auxiliary verbs that express possibility, ability, permission, obligation, advice, etc. One should use the correct modal according to the function and meaning. For example, "You can join the discussion." (modal of ability or permission) "You should join the discussion." (modal of advice or obligation)
  - Conditionals: Conditionals are sentences that express a hypothetical or real situation and its possible result. One should use the correct conditional type according to the degree of certainty and reality. For example, "If you study hard, you will pass the exam." (first conditional, indicating a real and likely situation and result) "If I were you, I would join the discussion." (second conditional, indicating a hypothetical and unlikely situation and result)
  - Passive voice: Passive voice is a way of forming sentences where the subject is acted upon by the verb, rather than performing the action. One should use the passive voice when the focus is on the action or the object, rather than the doer or the agent. For example, "This book was written by J.K. Rowling." (passive voice, focusing on the book and the writer) "J.K. Rowling wrote this book." (active voice, focusing on the writer and the action)
  - Reported speech: Reported speech is a way of expressing what someone said or thought without using their exact words. One should use the reported speech when quoting or paraphrasing someone else's words or thoughts in a group discussion. For example, "She said that she liked the book." (reported speech, using the past tense and the conjunction 'that') "She said, 'I like the book.'" (direct speech, using the present tense and quotation marks)
