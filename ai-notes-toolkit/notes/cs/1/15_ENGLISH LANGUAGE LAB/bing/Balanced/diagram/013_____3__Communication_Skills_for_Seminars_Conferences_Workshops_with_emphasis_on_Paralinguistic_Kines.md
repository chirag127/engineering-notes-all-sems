### 3. Communication Skills for Seminars/Conferences/Workshops with emphasis on Paralinguistic/Kinesics

- Seminars, conferences and workshops are formal events where people share their knowledge, ideas and opinions on a specific topic or theme.
- Communication skills are essential for effective participation and presentation in these events, as they help to convey the message clearly, confidently and persuasively.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic skills are two aspects of communication skills that focus on the non-verbal elements of speech and body language, respectively.
- Paralinguistic skills include the use of voice quality, pitch, volume, rate, pauses, stress, intonation and accent to enhance the meaning and emotion of the spoken words.
- Kinesic skills include the use of facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, posture, movement and proxemics to reinforce the verbal message and establish rapport with the audience.
- Some examples of paralinguistic and kinesic skills for seminars, conferences and workshops are:

  - Use a clear, loud and confident voice to project authority and credibility.
  - Vary your pitch, volume and rate to create interest and emphasis.
  - Use pauses and stress to highlight key points and signal transitions.
  - Use appropriate intonation and accent to convey the tone and attitude of your speech.
  - Use facial expressions and eye contact to show your enthusiasm, sincerity and engagement with the audience.
  - Use gestures and movement to illustrate and support your ideas and arguments.
  - Use posture and proxemics to show your confidence, openness and respect for the audience and the speakers.
  - Avoid paralinguistic and kinesic behaviors that may distract, confuse or offend the audience, such as:

    - Using a monotone, low or fast voice that may sound boring, nervous or unclear.
    - Using excessive or inappropriate pauses, stress, intonation or accent that may sound hesitant, uncertain or rude.
    - Using facial expressions and eye contact that may show your boredom, disinterest or hostility towards the audience or the speakers.
    - Using gestures and movement that may be too vague, exaggerated or irrelevant to your speech.
    - Using posture and proxemics that may show your lack of confidence, closedness or disrespect for the audience and the speakers.
