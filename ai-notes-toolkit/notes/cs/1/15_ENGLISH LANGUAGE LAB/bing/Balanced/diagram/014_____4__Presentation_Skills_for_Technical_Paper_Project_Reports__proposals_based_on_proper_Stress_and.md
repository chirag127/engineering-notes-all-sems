### 4. Presentation Skills for Technical Paper/Project Reports/ proposals based on proper Stress and Intonation Mechanics

- Presentation skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and confidently with an audience, using appropriate verbal and non-verbal cues, visual aids, and technology.
- Technical paper/project reports/proposals are types of academic or professional documents that present the results of research, analysis, design, or development of a specific topic or problem.
- Stress and intonation mechanics are the aspects of speech that convey emphasis, emotion, attitude, and meaning through variations in loudness, pitch, and duration of syllables and words.
- To improve your presentation skills for technical paper/project reports/proposals based on proper stress and intonation mechanics, you should:

  - Know your purpose, audience, and context. Define the main goal, message, and scope of your presentation. Identify the needs, expectations, and background of your audience. Consider the time, place, and format of your presentation.
  - Plan and structure your content. Outline the main points, subpoints, and supporting details of your presentation. Organize your content into a clear and logical sequence, such as introduction, body, and conclusion. Use transitions, signposts, and summaries to connect and highlight your ideas.
  - Design and prepare your visual aids. Choose the appropriate type and amount of visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, images, videos, or animations, to enhance your presentation. Make sure your visual aids are clear, relevant, consistent, and attractive. Practice using your visual aids with your speech and technology.
  - Practice and rehearse your presentation. Practice your presentation aloud, preferably in front of a mirror, a recorder, or a friend. Pay attention to your stress and intonation patterns, and adjust them according to the meaning and importance of your words. Use stress to emphasize key words, contrast information, or show emotion. Use intonation to indicate questions, statements, or commands, and to show attitude, interest, or uncertainty. Avoid monotone, flat, or rising intonation that can make your presentation sound boring, unclear, or unfinished. Rehearse your presentation several times until you feel confident and comfortable with your content, delivery, and timing.
  - Deliver your presentation with confidence and enthusiasm. On the day of your presentation, dress appropriately, arrive early, and check your equipment and materials. Greet your audience, introduce yourself and your topic, and state your main goal and message. Speak clearly, loudly, and fluently, using proper stress and intonation mechanics to convey your meaning and attitude. Maintain eye contact, facial expressions, and body language that show your confidence and enthusiasm. Engage your audience with questions, examples, stories, or humor, if appropriate. Summarize your main points, restate your main message, and thank your audience at the end of your presentation. Answer any questions or comments from your audience politely and professionally.
