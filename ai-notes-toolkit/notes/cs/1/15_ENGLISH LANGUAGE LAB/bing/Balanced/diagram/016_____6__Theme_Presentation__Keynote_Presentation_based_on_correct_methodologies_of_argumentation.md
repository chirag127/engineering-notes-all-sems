### 6. Theme Presentation/ Keynote Presentation based on correct methodologies of argumentation

- A theme presentation or keynote presentation is a type of speech that aims to convey a central message or idea to the audience, usually in a persuasive or inspirational manner.
- A theme presentation or keynote presentation should have a clear and specific purpose, a well-defined audience, a coherent structure, and a strong delivery.
- A theme presentation or keynote presentation should also follow the correct methodologies of argumentation, which are the principles and techniques of reasoning and persuading others with logic and evidence.
- Some of the correct methodologies of argumentation are:

  - Claim: A claim is a statement that expresses the speaker's position or opinion on an issue or topic. A claim should be debatable, relevant, and supported by evidence.
  - Evidence: Evidence is the information or data that supports the claim. Evidence can be factual, statistical, anecdotal, testimonial, or analogical. Evidence should be reliable, credible, sufficient, and relevant.
  - Warrant: A warrant is the logical connection or explanation that shows how the evidence supports the claim. A warrant can be based on common sense, values, principles, or rules. A warrant should be clear, valid, and consistent.
  - Counterargument: A counterargument is a statement that challenges or opposes the claim or a part of the argument. A counterargument should be acknowledged, refuted, or conceded by the speaker.
  - Conclusion: A conclusion is the final statement that summarizes the main points of the argument and restates the claim. A conclusion should be concise, memorable, and call for action or further reflection.

- An example of a theme presentation or keynote presentation based on correct methodologies of argumentation is:

  - Claim: Artificial intelligence (AI) is beneficial for humanity and should be embraced and developed further.
  - Evidence: AI can perform tasks that are too complex, tedious, or dangerous for humans, such as medical diagnosis, scientific research, disaster relief, and education. AI can also enhance human creativity, productivity, and well-being by providing assistance, feedback, and entertainment. AI can also help solve global challenges, such as climate change, poverty, and health care.
  - Warrant: AI can improve the quality of life and the progress of society by augmenting human capabilities and solving human problems. AI can also foster human values, such as curiosity, innovation, and collaboration, by enabling new forms of communication, learning, and expression.
  - Counterargument: AI can pose risks and threats to humanity and should be regulated and controlled. AI can cause unemployment, inequality, and discrimination by replacing human workers, creating digital divides, and biasing decisions. AI can also harm human dignity, autonomy, and privacy by manipulating, deceiving, or surveilling humans. AI can also challenge human morality, identity, and culture by creating ethical dilemmas, existential crises, and social conflicts.
  - Refutation: AI can be designed and used in ways that minimize or prevent the potential harms and maximize or enhance the potential benefits. AI can be aligned with human values, interests, and goals by following ethical principles, standards, and guidelines. AI can also be accountable, transparent, and explainable by providing mechanisms for oversight, feedback, and justification. AI can also be respectful, cooperative, and inclusive by recognizing human diversity, rights, and preferences.
  - Conclusion: AI is a powerful and promising technology that can bring positive impacts and opportunities to humanity and the world. Therefore, we should embrace and develop AI further, while also ensuring that it is ethical, responsible, and human-centered.
