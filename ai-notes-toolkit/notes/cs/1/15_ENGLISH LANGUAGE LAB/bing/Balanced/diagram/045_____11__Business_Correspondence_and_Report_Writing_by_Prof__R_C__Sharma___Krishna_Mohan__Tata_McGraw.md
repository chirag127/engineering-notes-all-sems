# 11. Business Correspondence and Report Writing

## Introduction

Business correspondence and report writing are essential skills for professionals who need to communicate effectively with various stakeholders, such as customers, suppliers, employees, managers, investors, regulators, etc. Business correspondence refers to the written communication that takes place in a business context, such as letters, memos, emails, faxes, etc. Report writing refers to the process of presenting information, analysis, and recommendations in a structured and logical format, such as proposals, feasibility studies, progress reports, etc.

## Objectives

The main objectives of business correspondence and report writing are:

- To convey information clearly, concisely, and accurately.
- To persuade, influence, or request action from the reader.
- To build and maintain good relationships with the reader.
- To project a positive and professional image of the writer and the organization.

## Principles

The following principles should guide the business correspondence and report writing:

- Plan before writing. Identify the purpose, audience, and key messages of the communication. Gather and organize the relevant information and data. Outline the main points and structure of the document.
- Adapt to the reader. Consider the reader's needs, expectations, and preferences. Use appropriate tone, style, and language. Avoid jargon, slang, and technical terms that the reader may not understand. Provide sufficient background and context for the reader to follow the communication.
- Be clear and concise. Use simple and direct sentences. Avoid ambiguity, vagueness, and redundancy. Use headings, subheadings, bullet points, tables, graphs, and charts to organize and highlight the information. Use transitions and connectors to link the ideas and paragraphs.
- Be accurate and credible. Check the facts, figures, and sources of the information. Use reliable and relevant evidence to support the claims and arguments. Acknowledge the limitations and assumptions of the analysis. Cite the references and sources of the information.
- Be courteous and respectful. Use polite and positive words and expressions. Avoid offensive, rude, or aggressive language. Address the reader by name and title. Use appropriate salutations and closings. Express appreciation and gratitude for the reader's attention and cooperation.
- Revise and edit. Review the draft of the document for content, organization, style, and language. Check for errors in grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting. Use tools such as spell checkers, grammar checkers, and style guides to improve the quality of the document. Seek feedback from others and make necessary changes.
