### 2. Conversational Skills for Interviews under suitable Professional Communication Lab conditions with emphasis on Kinesics

- Conversational skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in a variety of situations, such as interviews.
- Interviews are formal interactions between an interviewer and an interviewee, where the interviewer evaluates the interviewee's suitability for a job, a project, a scholarship, or any other opportunity.
- Professional communication lab conditions are the settings and equipment that simulate a realistic and professional interview environment, such as a quiet room, a microphone, a camera, a computer, and a projector.
- Kinesics is the study of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and posture, and how they convey meaning and emotion in communication.
- Some of the conversational skills for interviews under suitable professional communication lab conditions with emphasis on kinesics are:

  - Prepare well for the interview by researching the organization, the role, and the interviewer, and by rehearsing your answers to common questions.
  - Dress appropriately and professionally for the interview, and avoid wearing anything that is too casual, flashy, or distracting.
  - Arrive on time and greet the interviewer with a firm handshake, a smile, and eye contact.
  - Sit comfortably and confidently, and maintain a good posture throughout the interview. Avoid slouching, fidgeting, crossing your arms, or leaning too far back or forward.
  - Use gestures and facial expressions that match your words and tone, and that show your interest, enthusiasm, and confidence. Avoid gestures and expressions that are too exaggerated, repetitive, or negative, such as pointing, shrugging, rolling your eyes, or frowning.
  - Maintain eye contact with the interviewer, but do not stare or look away too often. Eye contact shows your attention, respect, and honesty, and helps you build rapport with the interviewer.
  - Listen actively and attentively to the interviewer's questions and comments, and show your understanding by nodding, smiling, or paraphrasing. Avoid interrupting, talking over, or ignoring the interviewer.
  - Speak clearly and fluently, and use a polite and professional language. Avoid using slang, jargon, filler words, or inappropriate humor. Adjust your volume, speed, and pitch according to the situation and the interviewer's feedback.
  - Answer the questions directly and concisely, and provide relevant examples and evidence to support your points. Avoid giving vague, incomplete, or dishonest answers, or rambling on too long.
  - Ask relevant and insightful questions at the end of the interview, and show your interest and curiosity in the organization and the role. Avoid asking questions that are too personal, trivial, or negative, or that have already been answered.
  - Thank the interviewer for their time and consideration, and end the interview with a handshake, a smile, and eye contact. Follow up with a thank-you email or letter, and restate your interest and qualifications for the role.
