### 2. To acquaint students with specific dimensions of communication skills i.e. Reading, Writing, Listening, Thinking and Speaking.

Communication skills are essential for academic and professional success. They enable students to express themselves clearly, confidently, and persuasively in various contexts and situations. Communication skills can be divided into five specific dimensions: reading, writing, listening, thinking, and speaking. Each dimension has its own purpose, process, and challenges. Students need to be acquainted with these dimensions and practice them regularly to improve their communication competence. Here are some brief descriptions of each dimension:

- Reading: Reading is the process of extracting meaning from written texts. It involves decoding symbols, recognizing words, understanding sentences, and interpreting texts. Reading requires attention, concentration, comprehension, and critical thinking skills. Reading also helps students to expand their vocabulary, knowledge, and perspectives. Students can improve their reading skills by reading a variety of texts, such as books, articles, reports, essays, etc., and by applying different reading strategies, such as skimming, scanning, summarizing, analyzing, evaluating, etc.

- Writing: Writing is the process of producing written texts. It involves encoding meaning, choosing words, constructing sentences, and organizing texts. Writing requires planning, drafting, revising, editing, and proofreading skills. Writing also helps students to communicate their ideas, arguments, and opinions in a clear, coherent, and convincing manner. Students can improve their writing skills by writing for different purposes, audiences, and genres, such as academic, professional, creative, etc., and by following the conventions of grammar, spelling, punctuation, style, etc.

- Listening: Listening is the process of receiving and interpreting oral messages. It involves hearing, attending, understanding, remembering, and responding. Listening requires focus, patience, empathy, and feedback skills. Listening also helps students to learn from others, to build rapport, and to resolve conflicts. Students can improve their listening skills by listening to a variety of sources, such as lectures, presentations, conversations, podcasts, etc., and by applying different listening techniques, such as active, passive, critical, empathic, etc.

- Thinking: Thinking is the process of generating and evaluating ideas. It involves observing, questioning, reasoning, analyzing, synthesizing, and judging. Thinking requires creativity, logic, curiosity, and reflection skills. Thinking also helps students to solve problems, to make decisions, and to innovate. Students can improve their thinking skills by thinking about different topics, issues, and scenarios, and by applying different thinking methods, such as deductive, inductive, lateral, etc.

- Speaking: Speaking is the process of delivering oral messages. It involves articulating, modulating, projecting, and gesturing. Speaking requires confidence, clarity, fluency, and coherence skills. Speaking also helps students to share their thoughts, feelings, and experiences with others, to persuade, to inform, and to entertain. Students can improve their speaking skills by speaking in different situations, such as formal, informal, individual, group, etc., and by practicing different speaking skills, such as pronunciation, intonation, stress, etc.
