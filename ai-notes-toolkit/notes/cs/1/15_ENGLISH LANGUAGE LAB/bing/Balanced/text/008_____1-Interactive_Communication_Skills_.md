### 1-Interactive Communication Skills:

- Interactive communication skills are the abilities that enable a person to effectively exchange information, ideas, feelings, and feedback with others in various contexts, such as face-to-face, online, or in groups.
- Interactive communication skills include listening, speaking, reading, writing, and nonverbal communication, as well as the use of appropriate language, tone, and etiquette.
- Interactive communication skills are essential for building rapport, trust, and understanding with others, as well as for achieving personal, academic, and professional goals.
- Some examples of interactive communication skills are:

  - Asking open-ended questions to elicit more information or opinions from others.
  - Paraphrasing or summarizing what others have said to check for comprehension and show interest.
  - Giving constructive feedback to others in a respectful and supportive manner.
  - Using active listening techniques, such as nodding, eye contact, and verbal cues, to show attention and engagement.
  - Adapting one's communication style and language to suit the audience, purpose, and situation.
  - Using appropriate body language, gestures, and facial expressions to convey meaning and emotion.
  - Avoiding distractions, interruptions, and multitasking when communicating with others.
  - Being aware of and respecting the cultural, social, and personal differences and preferences of others.
  - Seeking clarification or confirmation when unsure or confused about something.
  - Expressing one's own thoughts, feelings, and needs clearly and assertively, without being aggressive or passive.
