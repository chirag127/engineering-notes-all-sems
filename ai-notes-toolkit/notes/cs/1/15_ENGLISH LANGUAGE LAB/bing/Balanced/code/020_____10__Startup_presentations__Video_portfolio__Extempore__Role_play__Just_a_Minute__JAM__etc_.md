# 10. Startup presentations, Video portfolio, Extempore, Role play, Just a Minute (JAM) etc.

## Startup presentations
- A startup presentation is a brief and persuasive speech that introduces a new business idea, product, or service to potential investors, customers, or partners.
- The purpose of a startup presentation is to convince the audience that the startup has a viable and profitable solution to a problem or need in the market.
- A startup presentation typically consists of the following elements:
  - A hook: a catchy and memorable opening that grabs the attention of the audience and makes them curious about the startup.
  - A problem: a clear and concise statement of the pain point or gap that the startup aims to address or fill.
  - A solution: a description of the product or service that the startup offers, how it works, and how it differs from existing alternatives.
  - A market: an overview of the target market, the size of the opportunity, and the competitive landscape.
  - A business model: an explanation of how the startup generates revenue, what are the costs and margins, and what are the key metrics and milestones.
  - A team: a brief introduction of the founders and key members of the startup, their backgrounds, skills, and roles.
  - A call to action: a clear and compelling request for the audience to take the next step, such as investing, buying, or partnering with the startup.

## Video portfolio
- A video portfolio is a collection of short videos that showcase one's skills, talents, achievements, or interests in a specific field or domain.
- The purpose of a video portfolio is to demonstrate one's abilities, personality, and potential to prospective employers, clients, or collaborators.
- A video portfolio typically consists of the following elements:
  - A introduction: a brief and engaging video that introduces oneself, the purpose of the portfolio, and the main themes or categories of the videos.
  - A selection of videos: a curated and organized set of videos that highlight one's best work, projects, or experiences in the chosen field or domain. The videos should be relevant, diverse, and high-quality.
  - A conclusion: a short and concise video that summarizes the portfolio, thanks the audience, and provides contact information and links to further resources.

## Extempore
- Extempore is a form of impromptu speaking that requires one to deliver a speech on a given topic without any prior preparation or notes.
- The purpose of extempore is to test one's ability to think on one's feet, organize one's thoughts, and communicate effectively under pressure.
- Extempore typically consists of the following elements:
  - A topic: a word, phrase, or question that is randomly assigned or chosen by the speaker or the audience.
  - A structure: a logical and coherent framework that guides the speech, such as introduction, body, and conclusion, or problem, solution, and benefit.
  - A content: a relevant and informative content that covers the main points, arguments, or examples related to the topic.
  - A delivery: a confident and clear delivery that engages the audience, maintains eye contact, uses appropriate gestures, and modulates voice, tone, and pace.

## Role play
- Role play is a form of interactive and experiential learning that involves acting out a scenario or situation by assuming the roles of different characters or stakeholders.
- The purpose of role play is to develop one's skills, knowledge, or attitudes in a specific context or domain, such as communication, negotiation, leadership, or teamwork.
- Role play typically consists of the following elements:
  - A scenario: a realistic and relevant situation that poses a challenge, problem, or dilemma for the participants.
  - A roles: a set of characters or stakeholders that have different perspectives, interests, or goals in the scenario.
  - A objectives: a set of learning outcomes or objectives that the participants aim to achieve through the role play, such as understanding, applying, or evaluating a concept, theory, or skill.
  - A feedback: a constructive and reflective feedback that is given by the facilitator, peers, or self after the role play, to assess the performance, identify the strengths and weaknesses, and suggest the areas for improvement.
