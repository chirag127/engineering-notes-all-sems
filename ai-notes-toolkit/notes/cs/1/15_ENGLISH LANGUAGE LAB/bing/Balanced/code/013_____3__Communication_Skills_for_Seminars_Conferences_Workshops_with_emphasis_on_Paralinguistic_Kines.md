### 3. Communication Skills for Seminars/Conferences/Workshops with emphasis on Paralinguistic/Kinesics

- Communication skills are essential for effective participation in seminars, conferences and workshops, where one has to present, discuss and interact with other professionals or experts in the field.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features are the aspects of spoken communication that do not involve words, but convey meaning and emotion through voice, gesture, facial expression and body language.
- Paralinguistic features include tone, pitch, volume, speed, pause, stress and intonation of voice. They can indicate the speaker's attitude, mood, confidence, interest, emphasis and intention.
- Kinesic features include eye contact, posture, movement, gesture, facial expression and proxemics (the use of space and distance). They can indicate the speaker's relationship, emotion, attitude, attention, agreement and feedback.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features can enhance or undermine the verbal message, depending on how they are used and interpreted. They can also vary across cultures and contexts, so one has to be aware of the norms and expectations of the audience and the situation.
- Some tips for using paralinguistic and kinesic features effectively in seminars, conferences and workshops are:

  - Use a clear, loud and confident voice that matches the content and purpose of your speech. Avoid monotone, mumbling, shouting or whispering.
  - Use appropriate stress and intonation to highlight key points, questions, contrasts and emotions. Avoid flat, rising or falling intonation that can confuse or bore the listeners.
  - Use pauses and variations in speed to create rhythm, coherence and emphasis. Avoid speaking too fast, too slow or without breaks.
  - Use eye contact to engage with the audience, show interest and confidence, and check for understanding and feedback. Avoid looking away, staring or blinking too much.
  - Use posture and movement to show energy, enthusiasm and professionalism. Avoid slouching, leaning, fidgeting or crossing your arms or legs.
  - Use gestures and facial expressions to support, illustrate and emphasize your verbal message, and to show emotion and attitude. Avoid excessive, inappropriate or contradictory gestures and expressions that can distract or offend the audience.
  - Use proxemics to establish rapport, trust and respect with the audience and the speakers. Avoid invading, avoiding or violating the personal or social space of others.
