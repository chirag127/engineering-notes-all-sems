### 1-Interactive Communication Skills:

- Interactive communication skills are the abilities that enable a person to effectively exchange information, ideas, feelings, and feedback with others in various contexts, such as face-to-face, online, or in groups.
- Interactive communication skills include verbal and non-verbal communication, listening, questioning, clarifying, summarizing, paraphrasing, empathizing, and giving and receiving feedback.
- Interactive communication skills are important for building rapport, trust, and understanding with others, as well as for resolving conflicts, negotiating, persuading, and influencing others.
- Some examples of interactive communication skills are:
  - Verbal communication: using clear, concise, and appropriate language, tone, and volume to convey a message or express an opinion.
  - Non-verbal communication: using body language, facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, and posture to reinforce or modify a verbal message or show emotions.
  - Listening: paying attention to what others are saying, showing interest and curiosity, and avoiding distractions or interruptions.
  - Questioning: asking open-ended, closed-ended, or probing questions to elicit information, opinions, or feedback from others, or to check understanding or clarify a point.
  - Clarifying: restating or rephrasing what others have said to ensure accuracy and avoid misunderstandings or confusion.
  - Summarizing: giving a brief overview of the main points or key ideas of a conversation or a presentation, or highlighting the main conclusions or outcomes.
  - Paraphrasing: using different words to restate what others have said, to show comprehension, or to emphasize a point or a perspective.
  - Empathizing: showing sensitivity and compassion to others' feelings, needs, or situations, and acknowledging or validating their emotions or experiences.
  - Giving feedback: providing constructive, specific, and timely comments or suggestions to others to help them improve their performance, behavior, or attitude.
  - Receiving feedback: accepting, appreciating, and learning from the comments or suggestions of others, and responding positively and professionally.
