##### Introduction 
- The measurement of power in a three-phase circuit is essential for the proper functioning of various electrical systems.
- The two-wattmeter method is commonly used to measure power in three-phase circuits.
- This method involves using two wattmeters to measure the power in each of the three phases and then calculating the total power.

##### Two-wattmeter method
- The two-wattmeter method involves connecting two wattmeters in the circuit.
- One wattmeter is connected between the first phase and the second phase, while the other wattmeter is connected between the second phase and the third phase.
- The voltage across each phase and the current through each phase are measured using voltmeters and ammeters, respectively.
- The wattmeters measure the power in each phase, and the total power is calculated by adding the two readings.

##### Power factor for star-connected load
- The power factor for a star-connected load can be determined using the two-wattmeter method.
- The power factor is given by the formula cos(Φ) = (P₁ - P₂) / 3V_I cos(θ), where P₁ and P₂ are the readings of the two wattmeters, V is the line voltage, I is the line current, and θ is the phase angle between voltage and current.

##### Power factor for delta-connected load
- The power factor for a delta-connected load can also be determined using the two-wattmeter method.
- The power factor is given by the formula cos(Φ) = (P₁ - P₂) / 3V_L cos(θ), where P₁ and P₂ are the readings of the two wattmeters, V is the phase voltage, L is the line voltage, I is the line current, and θ is the phase angle between voltage and current.

##### Conclusion
- The two-wattmeter method is an effective way to measure power in three-phase circuits.
- By using this method, the power factor for both star and delta-connected loads can also be determined.
- Proper measurement of power and power factor is essential for efficient operation of electrical systems.
