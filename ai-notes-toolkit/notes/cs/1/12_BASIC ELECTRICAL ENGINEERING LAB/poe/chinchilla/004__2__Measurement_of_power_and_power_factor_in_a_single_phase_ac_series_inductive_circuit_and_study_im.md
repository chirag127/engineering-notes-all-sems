## 2. Measurement of power and power factor in a single phase ac series inductive circuit and study improvement of power factor using capacitor

In this topic, we will discuss the measurement of power and power factor in a single-phase AC series inductive circuit and study the improvement of power factor using a capacitor. Let's dive into the details:

1. Power Measurement: 
    - The power in an AC circuit is given by the product of the voltage and current, i.e., P = VI.
    - The unit of power is watts (W).
    - In an AC circuit, power is not constant, and it fluctuates with time due to the alternating nature of the current and voltage.
    - To measure the average power in an AC circuit, we use a wattmeter.

2. Wattmeter:
    - A wattmeter is an instrument used to measure the average power in an AC circuit.
    - It consists of two coils, one fixed (current coil) and one movable (potential coil).
    - The current coil is connected in series with the load, and the potential coil is connected across the load.
    - The wattmeter measures the product of the current and voltage, i.e., the power consumed by the load.

3. Power Factor:
    - The power factor (PF) of an AC circuit is defined as the cosine of the angle between the voltage and current waveforms.
    - It is a measure of the efficiency of the circuit in utilizing the power supplied to it.
    - The power factor of an AC circuit lies between 0 and 1.
    - A power factor of 1 indicates that the circuit is consuming all the power supplied to it, whereas a power factor of 0 indicates that the circuit is not consuming any power.

4. Power Factor Improvement:
    - In an AC circuit with inductive loads, the power factor is generally low.
    - To improve the power factor, we can add a capacitor in parallel with the load.
    - The capacitor supplies the reactive power required by the load, thus reducing the reactive power supplied by the source.
    - This results in an improvement in the power factor of the circuit.

5. Calculation of Capacitance:
    - To calculate the capacitance required to improve the power factor of the circuit, we use the following formula:
    
        C = (tan φ1 - tan φ2) / (2πfV^2)
        
        where 
        - C is the capacitance in farads (F)
        - φ1 is the angle between the voltage and current waveforms before adding the capacitor
        - φ2 is the angle between the voltage and current waveforms after adding the capacitor
        - f is the frequency of the AC supply in hertz (Hz)
        - V is the voltage of the AC supply in volts (V)
        
6. Conclusion:
    - In this topic, we discussed the measurement of power and power factor in a single-phase AC series inductive circuit.
    - We also studied the improvement of power factor using a capacitor.
    - Adding a capacitor in parallel with the load results in a reduction in reactive power and an improvement in the power factor of the circuit.
