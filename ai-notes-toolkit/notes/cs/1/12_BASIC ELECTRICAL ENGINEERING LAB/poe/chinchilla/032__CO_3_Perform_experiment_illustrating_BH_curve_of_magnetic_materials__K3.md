##### CO 3 Perform experiment illustrating BH curve of magnetic materials. K3

Magnetic materials are widely used in various applications such as transformers, motors, generators, and magnetic storage devices. The magnetic properties of these materials are characterized by the BH curve, which shows the relationship between magnetic field strength (H) and magnetic flux density (B). In this experiment, we will illustrate the BH curve of magnetic materials by performing the following steps:

1. Equipment and materials required:
   - Magnetic material sample (e.g., iron, steel, nickel)
   - Electromagnet or permanent magnet
   - Ammeter
   - Voltmeter
   - Power supply
   - Magnetic field strength meter (optional)
   - Magnetic flux density meter (optional)

2. Set up the experimental apparatus:
   - Connect the power supply to the magnet.
   - Connect the ammeter and voltmeter in series with the magnet.
   - Place the magnetic material sample in the magnet's magnetic field.
   - Ensure that the magnetic field is uniform and stable.

3. Measure the magnetic field strength and magnetic flux density:
   - Measure the magnetic field strength with the magnetic field strength meter (optional).
   - Measure the voltage across the sample with the voltmeter.
   - Calculate the current flowing through the sample using Ohm's law.
   - Calculate the magnetic field strength from the current and the number of turns in the magnet.
   - Measure the magnetic flux density with the magnetic flux density meter (optional) or calculate it using the formula B = μ0H, where μ0 is the permeability of free space.

4. Plot the BH curve:
   - Vary the magnetic field strength by changing the current in the magnet.
   - Measure the corresponding magnetic flux density values for each magnetic field strength value.
   - Plot the BH curve by plotting magnetic flux density (B) on the y-axis and magnetic field strength (H) on the x-axis.
   - Repeat the measurements and plot the curve for different magnetic materials.

5. Analyze the BH curve:
   - The BH curve shows the magnetic properties of the material, such as saturation, remanence, and coercivity.
   - Saturation is the point at which the magnetic material cannot be magnetized further.
   - Remanence is the residual magnetic flux density after the magnetic field is removed.
   - Coercivity is the magnetic field strength required to reduce the magnetic flux density to zero.

By performing this experiment, we can illustrate the BH curve of magnetic materials and understand their magnetic properties. This knowledge is essential for designing and optimizing magnetic devices for various applications.
