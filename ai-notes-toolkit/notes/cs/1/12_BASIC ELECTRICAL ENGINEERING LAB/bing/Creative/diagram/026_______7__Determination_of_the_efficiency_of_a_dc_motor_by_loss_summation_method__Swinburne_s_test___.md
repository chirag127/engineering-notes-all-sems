##### 7. Determination of the efficiency of a dc motor by loss summation method (Swinburne's test).

- Swinburne's test is an indirect method of testing of DC machines, named after Sir James Swinburne.
- In this method, the losses are measured separately and the efficiency at any desired load is predetermined   .
- This test is the simplest method of testing of shunt and compound DC machines which have constant field flux .
- This test can be performed by running the machine as a motor or as a generator.
- The advantages of this test are:
  - It is easy and economical as no load is required  .
  - It does not cause heating of the machine as the power input is small  .
  - It can be used to determine the efficiency at any load and speed  .
- The disadvantages of this test are:
  - It does not account for the temperature rise of the machine at full load  .
  - It assumes that the losses are constant at all loads, which may not be true for armature reaction and stray load losses  .
  - It cannot be used for series DC machines as the field flux varies with load  .

- The procedure of Swinburne's test is as follows:
  - The machine is run at rated speed and voltage with no load  .
  - The voltmeter V, ammeter A and wattmeter W are used to measure the no-load voltage, current and power input respectively  .
  - The field rheostat Rf is adjusted to obtain the rated voltage  .
  - The armature rheostat Ra is varied to obtain different values of armature current and the corresponding values of power input are noted  .
  - The no-load losses are calculated as the product of no-load voltage and current  .
  - The armature copper losses are calculated as the product of armature resistance and square of armature current  .
  - The total losses are calculated as the sum of no-load losses and armature copper losses  .
  - The efficiency at any load is calculated as the ratio of output power to input power, where output power is the difference of input power and total losses  .
