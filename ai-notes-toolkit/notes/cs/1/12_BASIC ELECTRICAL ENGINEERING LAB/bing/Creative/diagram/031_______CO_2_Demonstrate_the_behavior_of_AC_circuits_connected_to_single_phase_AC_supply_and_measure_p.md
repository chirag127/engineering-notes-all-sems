- An AC circuit is a circuit that carries alternating current (AC), which is a current that changes its direction and magnitude periodically.
- A single phase AC supply is a type of AC supply that has only one voltage waveform and one current waveform, which are in phase with each other.
- A three phase AC supply is a type of AC supply that has three voltage waveforms and three current waveforms, which are 120 degrees out of phase with each other.
- The behavior of AC circuits depends on the type and combination of the circuit elements, such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, and transformers.
- The circuit elements can be classified into two categories: passive and active. Passive elements do not generate or control the voltage or current in the circuit, while active elements do.
- The passive elements can be further classified into three types: resistive, capacitive, and inductive. Resistive elements oppose the flow of current and dissipate power as heat. Capacitive elements store electric charge and energy in an electric field. Inductive elements store magnetic flux and energy in a magnetic field.
- The active elements can be further classified into two types: sources and loads. Sources provide the voltage or current to the circuit, while loads consume the power from the circuit.
- The behavior of AC circuits can be analyzed using various methods, such as phasor diagrams, complex numbers, impedance, admittance, power factor, and power triangle.
- A phasor diagram is a graphical representation of the voltage and current waveforms in an AC circuit, using vectors that rotate at a constant angular frequency. The length of the vector represents the peak value of the waveform, and the angle of the vector represents the phase angle of the waveform.
