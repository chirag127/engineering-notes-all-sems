#### (A) Hardware based experiments

- Hardware based experiments are experiments that involve the use of physical devices, components, or systems to test a hypothesis, measure a phenomenon, or demonstrate a concept.
- Hardware based experiments can be classified into different types, such as:
  - **Simulation experiments**: These are experiments that use software or hardware models to mimic the behavior of a real system or environment. For example, a flight simulator can be used to test the performance of an aircraft or a pilot under different conditions.
  - **Benchtop experiments**: These are experiments that use small-scale or simplified versions of a system or device to test its functionality or properties. For example, a breadboard can be used to test the logic of an electronic circuit or a wind tunnel can be used to test the aerodynamics of a vehicle.
  - **Field experiments**: These are experiments that use real-world settings or scenarios to test a system or device in its intended environment or application. For example, a drone can be used to test the feasibility of aerial photography or a solar panel can be used to test the efficiency of renewable energy.
- Hardware based experiments can have different purposes, such as:
  - **Exploratory experiments**: These are experiments that aim to discover new phenomena, principles, or relationships that are not well understood or established. For example, an experiment can be conducted to explore the effects of quantum entanglement on communication or encryption.
  - **Confirmatory experiments**: These are experiments that aim to verify or validate a hypothesis, theory, or model that is already proposed or assumed. For example, an experiment can be conducted to confirm the existence of gravitational waves or the Higgs boson.
  - **Comparative experiments**: These are experiments that aim to compare or contrast the performance, characteristics, or outcomes of different systems, devices, or methods. For example, an experiment can be conducted to compare the accuracy of different machine learning algorithms or the durability of different materials.
