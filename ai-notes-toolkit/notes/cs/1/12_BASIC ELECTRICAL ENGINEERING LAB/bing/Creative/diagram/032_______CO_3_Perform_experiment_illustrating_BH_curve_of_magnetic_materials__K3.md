##### CO 3 Perform experiment illustrating BH curve of magnetic materials. K3

- A BH curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between the magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetic flux density (B) of a magnetic material.
- The BH curve can be used to characterize the magnetic properties of a material, such as its permeability, coercivity, saturation, hysteresis, and remanence.
- To perform an experiment illustrating the BH curve of a magnetic material, the following steps are required:
  - Prepare a solenoid coil with a known number of turns and a variable current source. Insert a sample of the magnetic material inside the coil.
  - Connect a gaussmeter to measure the magnetic flux density (B) inside the coil. The magnetic field strength (H) can be calculated from the current and the number of turns using the formula H = NI/L, where N is the number of turns, I is the current, and L is the length of the coil.
  - Vary the current from zero to a maximum value and record the corresponding values of B and H. Plot the points on a graph paper to obtain the first quadrant of the BH curve.
  - Reverse the direction of the current and repeat the previous step. Plot the points on the same graph paper to obtain the second quadrant of the BH curve.
  - Repeat the steps for the third and fourth quadrants of the BH curve by reversing the direction of the current alternately.
  - The resulting graph should look like this:

```
B
^
|        /
|       / 
|      /  
|     /   
|    /    
|   /     
|  /      
| /       
|/        
|--------->
H
```

- The shape and size of the BH curve depend on the type and quality of the magnetic material. Some common features of the BH curve are:
  - The slope of the curve indicates the permeability of the material, which is the ratio of B to H. A higher slope means a higher permeability and a lower reluctance.
  - The point where the curve intersects the B-axis is called the saturation point, which is the maximum value of B that the material can attain. Beyond this point, increasing H does not increase B significantly.
  - The point where the curve intersects the H-axis is called the coercivity, which is the value of H required to reduce B to zero. A higher coercivity means a higher resistance to demagnetization.
  - The area enclosed by the curve is called the hysteresis loop, which represents the energy loss due to the reversal of magnetization. A larger loop means a higher hysteresis loss and a lower efficiency.
  - The value of B that remains after reducing H to zero is called the remanence, which is the residual magnetization of the material. A higher remanence means a higher permanent magnetism.
