##### CO 4 Calculate efficiency of a single phase transformer and DC machine. K4

- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is defined as the ratio of the output power to the input power. It is expressed as a percentage or a decimal fraction. The input and output power are measured in the same unit, such as kilowatts (kW) or watts (W).
- The efficiency of a single phase transformer can be calculated by the following formula:

    `η = (Pout / Pin) x 100%`

    where η is the efficiency, Pout is the output power, and Pin is the input power.

- The efficiency of a single phase transformer depends on the losses that occur in the transformer. There are two main types of losses: core losses and copper losses.

    - Core losses are the losses due to the magnetic properties of the core material. They consist of hysteresis losses and eddy current losses. Hysteresis losses are caused by the repeated magnetization and demagnetization of the core as the alternating current changes direction. Eddy current losses are caused by the induced currents that circulate within the core due to the changing magnetic flux. Core losses are constant and independent of the load.
    - Copper losses are the losses due to the resistance of the windings. They consist of primary copper losses and secondary copper losses. Primary copper losses are the power dissipated in the primary winding due to the primary current. Secondary copper losses are the power dissipated in the secondary winding due to the secondary current. Copper losses are proportional to the square of the load current.

- The efficiency of a single phase transformer can be maximized by minimizing the core losses and the copper losses. This can be achieved by using high quality core material, reducing the thickness of the core laminations, increasing the cross-sectional area of the windings, and reducing the length of the windings.

- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is usually high, ranging from 95% to 99%. For large power transformers with very low losses, the efficiency can be as high as 99.7%.

- The efficiency of a single phase transformer can be determined by measuring the input and output power under loaded conditions. However, this method is not very accurate as the wattmeter readings may suffer errors of 1% to 2%. A more accurate method is to measure the core losses and the copper losses separately and then use the following formula:

    `η = (Pin - Pcore - Pcu) / Pin x 100%`

    where Pcore is the core loss and Pcu is the copper loss.

- An example of calculating the efficiency of a single phase transformer is given below:

    - A single-phase transformer has a maximum efficiency of 98%. The core losses are 80 W and the equivalent winding resistance as seen from the primary side is 0.5 Ω. The rated current on the primary side is 25 A.
    - The input power is given by:

        `Pin = Vp x Ip`

        where Vp is the primary voltage and Ip is the primary current.

        Assuming a primary voltage of 240 V, the input power is:

        `Pin = 240 x 25 = 6000 W`

    - The output power is given by:

        `Pout = Vs x Is`

        where Vs is the secondary voltage and Is is the secondary current.

        Assuming a secondary voltage of 120 V and a load resistance of 6 Ω, the secondary current is:

        `Is = Vs / Rl = 120 / 6 = 20 A`

        The output power is:

        `Pout = 120 x 20 = 2400 W`

    - The copper loss is given by:

        `Pcu = Ip^2 x Rp`

        where Rp is the equivalent winding resistance as seen from the primary side.

        The copper loss is:

        `Pcu = 25^2 x 0.5 = 312.5 W`

    - The efficiency is given by:

        `η = (Pout / Pin) x 100%`

        The efficiency is:

        `η = (2400 / 6000) x 100% = 40%`

    - Alternatively, the efficiency can be calculated by:

        `η = (Pin - Pcore - Pcu) / Pin x 100%`

        The efficiency is:

        `η = (6000 - 80 - 312.5) / 6000 x 100% = 93.6%`
