## DC Machine

- A DC machine is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa.
- It consists of two main parts: the stator and the rotor.
- The stator is the stationary part that contains the field windings, which produce a magnetic field when energized by a DC source.
- The rotor is the rotating part that contains the armature windings, which carry the current induced by the magnetic field.
- The armature windings are connected to a commutator, which is a cylindrical device that consists of segments of copper bars insulated from each other.
- The commutator rotates with the rotor and allows the current to flow in the same direction in the external circuit by changing the connections of the armature windings to the brushes.
- The brushes are stationary conductors that make contact with the commutator segments and transfer the current to or from the external circuit.
- The direction of rotation of the rotor depends on the direction of the current in the armature windings and the polarity of the field windings.
- A DC machine can operate as a motor or a generator depending on the direction of the power flow.

## Three Phase Induction Machine

- A three phase induction machine is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa using the principle of electromagnetic induction.
- It consists of two main parts: the stator and the rotor.
- The stator is the stationary part that contains the stator windings, which are connected to a three phase AC supply.
- The stator windings produce a rotating magnetic field that rotates at a synchronous speed, which depends on the frequency of the supply and the number of poles of the machine.
- The rotor is the rotating part that contains the rotor windings, which are either short-circuited or connected to an external resistance.
- The rotor windings are either wound or embedded in the rotor core, which is made of laminated steel to reduce eddy current losses.
- The rotor windings are either of the squirrel cage type or the wound type.
- The squirrel cage type has bars of copper or aluminum that are short-circuited at both ends by end rings, forming a cage-like structure.
- The wound type has coils of insulated wire that are connected to slip rings, which allow the connection of an external resistance or a DC source.
- The rotating magnetic field induces an electromotive force (emf) in the rotor windings, which causes a current to flow in them.
- The current in the rotor windings interacts with the magnetic field and produces a torque that causes the rotor to rotate.
- The rotor speed is less than the synchronous speed, and the difference is called the slip.
- The slip determines the amount of power transferred from the stator to the rotor and the torque developed by the machine.
- A three phase induction machine can operate as a motor or a generator depending on the direction of the power flow and the slip.
