# 4. Connection and measurement of power consumption of a fluorescent lamp (tube light).

- A fluorescent lamp (or tube light) is a type of electric light that uses low-pressure mercury vapor and phosphor coating to produce visible light.
- A fluorescent lamp consists of a glass tube filled with inert gas (usually argon) and a small amount of mercury, with electrodes at both ends.
- A fluorescent lamp requires a ballast to provide the necessary voltage and current to start and operate the lamp.
- A ballast can be either magnetic or electronic, depending on the type and frequency of the lamp.
- A magnetic ballast uses an iron core and a coil to create a magnetic field that induces a voltage across the lamp terminals.
- An electronic ballast uses a circuit that converts the mains voltage to a high-frequency alternating current (AC) that drives the lamp.
- A fluorescent lamp also requires a starter to initiate the discharge of the mercury vapor inside the tube.
- A starter can be either a glow switch or an electronic device, depending on the type and frequency of the lamp.
- A glow switch starter consists of a bimetallic strip and a neon lamp that heats up and bends when the lamp is switched on, closing the circuit and allowing a current to flow through the lamp.
- An electronic starter uses a capacitor and a resistor to create a pulse of voltage that triggers the lamp ignition.

  - Connect the lamp, the ballast, and the starter in series with the mains supply, as shown in the figure below.

  - Use a multimeter to measure the voltage (V) across the lamp terminals and the current (I) flowing through the lamp.
  - Use the formula P = VI to calculate the power (P) consumed by the lamp in watts (W).
  - Alternatively, use a wattmeter to measure the power directly.

- Some factors that affect the power consumption of a fluorescent lamp are:

  - The type and frequency of the lamp and the ballast, which determine the voltage and current required to operate the lamp.
  - The temperature and pressure of the gas inside the tube, which affect the resistance and conductivity of the lamp.
  - The age and condition of the lamp, which affect the efficiency and luminosity of the lamp.
  - The ambient light and humidity, which affect the starting and operating characteristics of the lamp.
