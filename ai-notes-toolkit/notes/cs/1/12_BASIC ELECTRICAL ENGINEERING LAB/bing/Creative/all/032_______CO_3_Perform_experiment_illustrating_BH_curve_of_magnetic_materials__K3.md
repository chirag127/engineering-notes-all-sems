# CO 3 Perform experiment illustrating BH curve of magnetic materials. K3

- The BH curve of a magnetic material is a plot of the magnetic flux density B versus the magnetic field intensity H.
- The BH curve shows the relationship between the magnetization of the material and the applied magnetic field.
- The BH curve can be used to measure the magnetic properties of the material, such as permeability, coercivity, saturation, hysteresis, and remanence.
- To perform the experiment, you will need the following equipment and materials:
  - A solenoid coil with a variable current source and an ammeter
  - A magnetic material sample that can fit inside the coil
  - A search coil with a voltmeter
  - A magnetometer or a gaussmeter
- The steps of the experiment are as follows:
  - Connect the solenoid coil to the current source and the ammeter, and the search coil to the voltmeter.
  - Place the magnetic material sample inside the solenoid coil, and the search coil around the sample.
  - Adjust the current in the solenoid coil to zero, and record the reading of the voltmeter. This is the initial induced voltage in the search coil, V0.
  - Increase the current in the solenoid coil gradually, and record the readings of the ammeter and the voltmeter. The current in the solenoid coil is proportional to the magnetic field intensity H, and the induced voltage in the search coil is proportional to the rate of change of the magnetic flux density B.
  - Plot the values of B versus H on a graph paper, where B is calculated as B = (V - V0) / (N * A), where V is the induced voltage, N is the number of turns of the search coil, and A is the cross-sectional area of the sample.
  - Repeat the steps from 4 to 5, but in the opposite direction, by decreasing the current in the solenoid coil gradually until it reaches zero, and then reversing the polarity of the current source. This will trace the hysteresis loop of the magnetic material.
  - Measure the magnetic flux density of the sample using the magnetometer or the gaussmeter, and compare it with the value obtained from the graph. This will give you the remanence of the material, which is the residual magnetization after the applied field is removed.
  - Find the value of H that corresponds to the point where B becomes zero on the graph. This will give you the coercivity of the material, which is the amount of reverse field required to demagnetize the material.
  - Find the value of B that corresponds to the point where the graph becomes horizontal on both sides. This will give you the saturation of the material, which is the maximum magnetization that the material can attain.
  - Find the ratio of B to H at any point on the graph. This will give you the permeability of the material, which is the measure of how easily the material can be magnetized.
