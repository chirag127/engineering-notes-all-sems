# Determination of the efficiency of a dc motor by loss summation method (Swinburne's test)

- The efficiency of a dc motor is defined as the ratio of output (mechanical) power to input (electrical) power. 
- The efficiency of a dc motor can be measured by direct or indirect methods. 
- The direct method requires measuring the input power by a power analyzer and the output power by a speed and torque meter. 
- The indirect method, also known as the loss summation method or Swinburne's test, involves determining the losses of the dc motor without loading it. 
- The losses of a dc motor can be classified into constant losses and variable losses. 
- The constant losses are the losses that do not depend on the load, such as core loss, friction loss, and windage loss. 
- The variable losses are the losses that depend on the load, such as copper loss in the armature and the field windings. 
- The Swinburne's test is performed by running the dc motor at no load and rated speed, and measuring the no-load current and voltage. 
- The no-load input power is equal to the sum of the constant losses and the shunt field copper loss. 
- The armature copper loss can be calculated by multiplying the armature resistance and the square of the rated current. 
- The total losses can be obtained by adding the no-load input power and the armature copper loss. 
- The efficiency of the dc motor can be calculated by subtracting the total losses from the rated output power and dividing by the rated output power. 
- The Swinburne's test has the advantages of convenience, economy, and accuracy, as it does not require loading the motor or measuring the output power. 
- The Swinburne's test has the limitations of being applicable only to shunt and compound motors, and not accounting for the temperature rise and stray load losses that occur at full load.
