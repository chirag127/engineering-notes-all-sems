# 3. Study of phenomenon of resonance in RLC series circuit and obtain resonant frequency.

- An RLC series circuit consists of a resistor (R), an inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) connected in series to an alternating voltage source.
- The current (I) in the circuit is the same for all the elements and is given by Ohm's law: I = V/Z, where V is the source voltage and Z is the total impedance of the circuit.
- The impedance (Z) of the circuit is a complex quantity that depends on the frequency (f) of the source voltage and the values of R, L and C. It is given by Z = R + j(XL - XC), where j is the imaginary unit, XL is the inductive reactance and XC is the capacitive reactance.
- The inductive reactance (XL) is proportional to the frequency and the inductance: XL = 2πfL. The capacitive reactance (XC) is inversely proportional to the frequency and the capacitance: XC = 1/(2πfC).
- The phase angle (φ) of the circuit is the angle between the source voltage and the current. It is given by tanφ = (XL - XC)/R. The phase angle determines the power factor (cosφ) of the circuit, which is a measure of how efficiently the circuit transfers power from the source to the load.
- Resonance is a special condition in which the circuit behaves as a pure resistor, meaning that the current is in phase with the voltage and the power factor is 1. Resonance occurs when the inductive reactance is equal to the capacitive reactance: XL = XC. This implies that the frequency of the source voltage is equal to the resonant frequency (fr) of the circuit, which is given by fr = 1/(2π√LC).
- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is equal to the resistance: Z = R. This means that the current in the circuit is maximum and is given by I = V/R. The voltage across the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase, and they cancel each other. The voltage across the resistor is equal to the source voltage.
- The quality factor (Q) of the circuit is a measure of how sharp the resonance is. It is given by Q = XL/R = XC/R = 1/(R√C/L). The higher the Q, the narrower the bandwidth (Δf) of the circuit, which is the range of frequencies around the resonant frequency for which the circuit behaves as a resonator. The bandwidth is inversely proportional to the Q: Δf = fr/Q.
