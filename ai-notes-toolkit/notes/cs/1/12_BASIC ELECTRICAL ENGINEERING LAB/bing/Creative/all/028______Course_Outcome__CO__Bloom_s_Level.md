#### Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Level

- A course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what a learner should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course or a unit of instruction.
- A CO should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART).
- A CO should also align with the program outcomes (POs) and the institutional outcomes (IOs) of the educational institution.
- A CO should be written using an action verb that indicates the level of cognitive skill or ability required by the learner to achieve the outcome.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a framework that classifies different levels of cognitive skills or abilities, from lower-order to higher-order, as follows:
  - Remember: The learner can recall or recognize facts, terms, concepts, or procedures.
  - Understand: The learner can explain, interpret, summarize, or paraphrase information or ideas.
  - Apply: The learner can use or apply knowledge or skills to solve problems or perform tasks.
  - Analyze: The learner can break down information or ideas into components or relationships, or identify patterns or trends.
  - Evaluate: The learner can judge, critique, or assess the quality, value, or effectiveness of information, ideas, or arguments.
  - Create: The learner can generate, produce, or synthesize new or original information, ideas, or products.
- Bloom's level of a CO indicates the level of cognitive skill or ability that the learner is expected to demonstrate after completing the course or the unit of instruction.
- Bloom's level of a CO should be consistent with the level of difficulty and complexity of the course content and the assessment methods.
- Bloom's level of a CO should also match the level of the POs and the IOs that the CO supports or contributes to.
- Bloom's level of a CO can be determined by using a verb list or a verb chart that maps different action verbs to different levels of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Examples of COs with different Bloom's levels are:

  - CO1: Define the basic concepts and principles of accounting. (Remember)
  - CO2: Explain the accounting cycle and the financial statements. (Understand)
  - CO3: Apply the accounting rules and standards to record business transactions and prepare financial reports. (Apply)
  - CO4: Analyze the financial performance and position of a company using ratio analysis and common-size analysis. (Analyze)
  - CO5: Evaluate the ethical and social implications of accounting decisions and practices. (Evaluate)
  - CO6: Create a budget and a cash flow forecast for a small business. (Create)
