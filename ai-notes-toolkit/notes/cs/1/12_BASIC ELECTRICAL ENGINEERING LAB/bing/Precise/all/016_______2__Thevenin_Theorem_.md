##### 2. Thevenin Theorem

Thevenin's Theorem is a method used to simplify complex electrical circuits. It states that any linear, bilateral circuit can be replaced by an equivalent circuit consisting of a single voltage source and a single resistor.

The steps to apply Thevenin's Theorem are as follows:
1. Identify the load resistor in the circuit.
2. Remove the load resistor from the circuit.
3. Calculate the open-circuit voltage across the terminals where the load resistor was connected. This is the Thevenin voltage.
4. Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit as seen from the open terminals. This is the Thevenin resistance.
5. Replace the original circuit with the Thevenin equivalent circuit, consisting of the Thevenin voltage source in series with the Thevenin resistance.
6. Reconnect the load resistor to the Thevenin equivalent circuit.

Thevenin's Theorem can be used to analyze circuits with multiple sources and multiple resistors. It is a powerful tool for simplifying complex circuits and making them easier to analyze.
