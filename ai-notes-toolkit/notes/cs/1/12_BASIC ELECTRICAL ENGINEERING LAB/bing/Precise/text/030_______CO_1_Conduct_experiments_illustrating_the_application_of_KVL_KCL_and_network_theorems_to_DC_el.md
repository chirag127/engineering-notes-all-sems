##### CO 1 Conduct experiments illustrating the application of KVL/KCL and network theorems to DC electrical circuits. K3

- KVL (Kirchhoff's Voltage Law) states that the sum of the voltage drops around a closed loop in a circuit is equal to the voltage supplied to the loop.
- KCL (Kirchhoff's Current Law) states that the sum of the currents entering a node in a circuit is equal to the sum of the currents leaving the node.
- Network theorems, such as Thevenin's theorem and Norton's theorem, can be used to simplify complex circuits and make it easier to analyze them.
- To conduct experiments illustrating the application of KVL/KCL and network theorems to DC electrical circuits, one can set up a simple circuit with a power source, resistors, and measuring devices such as a voltmeter and an ammeter.
- By measuring the voltage drops across the resistors and the currents flowing through them, one can verify the validity of KVL and KCL.
- By applying network theorems, one can simplify the circuit and make predictions about its behavior, which can then be verified through experimentation.
