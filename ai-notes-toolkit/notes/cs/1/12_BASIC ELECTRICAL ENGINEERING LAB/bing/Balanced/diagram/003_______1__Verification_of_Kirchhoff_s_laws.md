##### 1. Verification of Kirchhoff’s laws

- Kirchhoff's laws are two rules that describe the conservation of electric charge and energy in a circuit. They are:

  - Kirchhoff's current law (KCL): The algebraic sum of all currents entering and exiting a node must equal zero. This means that the current that flows into a junction is equal to the current that flows out of it.

  - Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL): The algebraic sum of all the voltages around a closed loop must equal zero. This means that the total voltage drop across all the components in a loop is equal to the total voltage rise from the sources in the loop.

- To verify these laws experimentally, we need to measure the currents and voltages in a circuit using appropriate instruments, such as ammeters, voltmeters, and multimeters.

- For KCL, we can choose any node in the circuit and add up the currents entering and leaving that node. The result should be zero or very close to zero, depending on the accuracy of the measurements.

- For KVL, we can choose any closed loop in the circuit and add up the voltages across all the components in that loop. The result should be zero or very close to zero, depending on the accuracy of the measurements.
