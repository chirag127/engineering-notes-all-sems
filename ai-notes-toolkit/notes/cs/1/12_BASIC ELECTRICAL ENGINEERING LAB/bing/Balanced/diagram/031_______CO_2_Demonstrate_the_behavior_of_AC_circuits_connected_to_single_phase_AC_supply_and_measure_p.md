- An AC circuit is a circuit that carries alternating current, which is the current that changes its direction and magnitude periodically.
- A single phase AC supply is a type of power supply that delivers a single sinusoidal voltage and current to a load. A single phase AC supply requires two wires: one phase and one neutral.
- A three phase AC supply is a type of power supply that delivers three sinusoidal voltages and currents to a load, with a phase difference of 120 degrees between them. A three phase AC supply requires three phase wires and one neutral wire.
- The behavior of AC circuits depends on the type and combination of the load elements, such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors. The load elements can affect the voltage, current, power, and power factor of the AC circuit.
- The power in an AC circuit is the rate of energy transfer from the source to the load. The power in an AC circuit can be calculated by multiplying the voltage and the current, and taking into account the power factor, which is the ratio of the real power to the apparent power. The power factor can range from 0 to 1, depending on the phase difference between the voltage and the current.
