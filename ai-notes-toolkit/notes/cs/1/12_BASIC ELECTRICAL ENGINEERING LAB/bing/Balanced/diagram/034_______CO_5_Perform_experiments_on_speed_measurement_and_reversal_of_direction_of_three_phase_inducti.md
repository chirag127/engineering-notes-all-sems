##### CO 5 Perform experiments on speed measurement and reversal of direction of three phase induction motor and Identify the type of DC and AC machines based on their construction. K4

- A three phase induction motor is a type of AC machine that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy by using the principle of electromagnetic induction.
- The speed of a three phase induction motor depends on the frequency of the AC supply, the number of poles in the stator winding, and the slip between the stator and rotor magnetic fields.
- The speed of a three phase induction motor can be measured by using a tachometer, a stroboscope, or a frequency meter.
- The direction of rotation of a three phase induction motor can be reversed by interchanging any two of the three supply terminals. This changes the phase sequence of the stator winding and reverses the direction of the rotating magnetic field.
- An experiment to demonstrate the speed measurement and reversal of direction of a three phase induction motor can be performed by using the following steps:
  - Connect the three phase induction motor to a three phase AC supply and a suitable load.
  - Measure the speed of the motor by using a tachometer, a stroboscope, or a frequency meter and record the value.
  - Reverse the direction of rotation of the motor by interchanging any two of the supply terminals and observe the change in the speed and direction of the motor.
  - Repeat the steps for different values of frequency and load and tabulate the results.
- DC and AC machines are classified based on their construction into different types, such as:
  - DC machines: 
    - Separately excited: The field winding is supplied by a separate DC source and the armature winding is connected to the load.
    - Self excited: The field winding is connected in series, shunt, or compound with the armature winding and the same DC source supplies both the windings.
    - Permanent magnet: The field winding is replaced by permanent magnets and the armature winding is connected to the load.
  - AC machines: 
    - Synchronous: The rotor speed is equal to the synchronous speed, which is determined by the frequency of the AC supply and the number of poles in the stator winding. The rotor can be a salient pole or a cylindrical type.
    - Induction: The rotor speed is less than the synchronous speed and the difference is called the slip. The rotor can be a squirrel cage or a wound type.
    - Universal: The motor can operate on both AC and DC supply. The stator winding is similar to a DC machine and the rotor winding is similar to an AC machine.
