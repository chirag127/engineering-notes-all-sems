##### 7. Determination of the efficiency of a dc motor by loss summation method (Swinburne's test).

- The efficiency of a dc motor is the ratio of output (mechanical) power to input (electrical) power.
- The efficiency can be determined by direct or indirect methods. The direct method requires measuring the input and output power on load, while the indirect method requires measuring the losses without loading the machine.
- The indirect method is also known as the loss summation method or Swinburne's test. It is convenient and economical, especially for large machines.
- The loss summation method involves running the dc motor at no load and measuring the input current and voltage. The input power is equal to the sum of the losses in the machine, which are constant losses and variable losses.
- The constant losses are the core loss, the friction and windage loss, and the shunt field loss. They are independent of the load and can be calculated from the no-load test.
- The variable losses are the armature copper loss and the series field loss. They depend on the load current and can be calculated from the rated current of the machine.
- The efficiency of the dc motor can be calculated by subtracting the losses from the input power and dividing by the input power. Alternatively, the efficiency can be calculated by dividing the output power by the input power, where the output power is the difference between the input power and the losses.
- The efficiency of the dc motor is maximum when the variable losses are equal to the constant losses. This condition can be used to find the optimal load current for the machine.
