#### K1 – Remember, K2 – Understand, K3 – Apply, K4 – Analyze, K5 – Evaluate, K6 – Create

- These are the six levels of cognitive learning according to Bloom's taxonomy, a framework for classifying educational objectives and outcomes.
- The levels are arranged in a hierarchy, from lower-order to higher-order thinking skills, and each level requires the mastery of the previous one.
- The levels are:

  - **K1 – Remember**: This level involves recalling facts, terms, definitions, concepts, or procedures from memory. Examples of tasks at this level are: listing, naming, identifying, labeling, matching, or reproducing.
  - **K2 – Understand**: This level involves explaining, interpreting, summarizing, paraphrasing, or illustrating the meaning of information. Examples of tasks at this level are: describing, explaining, classifying, comparing, or exemplifying.
  - **K3 – Apply**: This level involves using knowledge or skills in new or different situations, or solving problems by applying rules, methods, principles, or procedures. Examples of tasks at this level are: applying, solving, demonstrating, calculating, or performing.
  - **K4 – Analyze**: This level involves breaking down information into parts, examining the relationships, causes, effects, or implications of each part, or identifying patterns, trends, or gaps in the information. Examples of tasks at this level are: analyzing, comparing, contrasting, categorizing, or outlining.
  - **K5 – Evaluate**: This level involves making judgments, assessments, or critiques based on criteria, standards, or evidence, or supporting or defending a position or opinion. Examples of tasks at this level are: evaluating, judging, critiquing, rating, or recommending.
  - **K6 – Create**: This level involves generating, producing, designing, or constructing new or original products, ideas, solutions, or outcomes, or synthesizing or integrating information from different sources or perspectives. Examples of tasks at this level are: creating, designing, developing, inventing, or composing.

- The levels of cognitive learning can be used to design learning objectives, activities, assessments, or feedback for learners, or to measure the level of learning achieved by learners.
