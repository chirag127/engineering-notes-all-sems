
### Working principles of hydraulic turbines (Pelton Wheel and Francis)& pumps (Centrifugal and Reciprocating) and their classifications and hydraulic lift for the notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

* Hydraulic turbines are machines that convert the potential energy of a falling water stream into mechanical energy. The two most common types of hydraulic turbines are the Pelton wheel and the Francis turbine.

* The Pelton wheel is a type of impulse turbine that consists of a series of buckets mounted around the circumference of a wheel. The water is directed onto the buckets at a high velocity, causing them to rotate and converting the energy of the water into mechanical energy.

* The Francis turbine is a type of reaction turbine that consists of a series of curved blades mounted around the circumference of a wheel. The water is directed onto the blades at a high velocity, causing them to rotate and converting the energy of the water into mechanical energy.

* Pumps are machines that convert mechanical energy into the potential energy of a liquid. The two most common types of pumps are the centrifugal pump and the reciprocating pump.

* The centrifugal pump is a type of rotodynamic pump that consists of an impeller mounted on a shaft. The impeller is driven by a motor and rotates, causing the liquid to be drawn in and then ejected at a high velocity.

* The reciprocating pump is a type of positive displacement pump that consists of a piston mounted on a cylinder. The piston is driven by a motor and reciprocates, causing the liquid to be drawn in and then ejected at a high pressure.

* Hydraulic lifts are machines that convert the potential energy of a liquid into mechanical energy. The hydraulic lift consists of a cylinder filled with a liquid, such as oil or water, and a piston. When the piston is pushed down, the liquid is forced out of the cylinder, causing the lift to move upwards. When the piston is pulled up, the liquid is drawn back into the cylinder, causing the lift to move downwards.
