
### Hookes’ Law

Hookes’ law is a fundamental law of mechanics that states that the force required to extend or compress a spring is proportional to the distance the spring is extended or compressed. The law is named after Robert Hooke, who first proposed it in 1676.

In mathematical terms, Hookes’ law states that the force (F) is proportional to the displacement (x) of a spring, according to the equation:

F = -kx

Where k is the spring constant, which is a measure of the stiffness of the spring.

Hookes’ law is a useful tool for understanding the behavior of many mechanical systems, including pendulums, springs, and other objects that experience a restoring force proportional to the displacement. It is also useful for calculating the forces in compression and tension members, such as beams and columns.
