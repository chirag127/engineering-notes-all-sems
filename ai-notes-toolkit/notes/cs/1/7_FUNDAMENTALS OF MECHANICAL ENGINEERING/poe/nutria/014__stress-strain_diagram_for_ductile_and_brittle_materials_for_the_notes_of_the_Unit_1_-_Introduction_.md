

### Stress-Strain Diagram for Ductile and Brittle Materials

* Stress-strain diagrams are graphical representations of the relationship between the stress applied to a material and the resulting strain.
* Ductile materials are able to undergo large amounts of deformation before failure, while brittle materials fail with relatively little deformation.
* The stress-strain diagram for ductile materials typically follows a curved line, while the diagram for brittle materials is usually linear.
* On a stress-strain diagram, the elastic limit is the point at which the material begins to deform permanently.
* The yield point is the point at which the material begins to deform plastically and the ultimate tensile strength is the maximum stress a material can withstand before failure.
* The strain hardening region is the area after the yield point where the material becomes stronger as it is deformed more.
* The ductile-to-brittle transition temperature is the temperature at which a ductile material becomes brittle.
