## Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications

1. Fluids and Fluid Mechanics: A fluid is a substance that deforms continuously under the application of shear stress, no matter how small. Fluid mechanics is the study of forces acting on fluids and the motion of fluids. It has applications in many engineering fields.
2. Properties of Fluids: The three key properties that characterize a fluid are:
- Density: Mass per unit volume. Determines buoyancy effects and flow rates.
- Viscosity: Resistance to flow. Determines amount of shear stress required to induce flow.
- Surface Tension: Tendency of liquid surfaces to shrink to minimum surface area. Important in capillarity and droplet formation.
3. Pressure and Pressure Gradient: Pressure is a normal force per unit area. A pressure gradient is the change in pressure over a distance and causes fluid flow. The relationship between pressure, pressure gradient, density, and gravity is described by hydrostatic equations.
4. Fluid Flow: Fluid flow may be laminar or turbulent. Laminar flow is characterized by smooth, parallel fluid motion while turbulent flow is chaotic and irregular. The Reynolds number is used to predict flow regime. Continuity equation describes conservation of mass in fluid flow.
5. Applications: Fluid mechanics has applications such as pipe flow, flow meters, pumps, turbomachinery, aerodynamics, and blood flow. Understanding fluid forces is crucial in engineering design of spacecraft, aircraft, ships, and other applications.
