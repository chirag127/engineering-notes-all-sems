### Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity

- Dynamic viscosity (μ): It is a measure of a fluid's resistance to shear stress. It is proportional to the shear stress produced by a given velocity gradient.
- Units: Pascal-second (Pa-s) or N-s/m2 or kg/ms
- Kinematic viscosity (ν): It is the ratio of dynamic viscosity to density. It is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow.
- Units: m2/s
- The ratio of dynamic to kinematic viscosity is density (ρ).
- Viscosity decreases with increasing temperature for most fluids.
- Viscosity increases with increasing molecular weight and intermolecular attractions between molecules.
- Liquids have lower viscosity than solids and gases have lower viscosity than liquids.
- High viscosity fluids flow slowly while low viscosity fluids flow rapidly.
- Kinematic and dynamic viscosity both give an indication of a fluid's resistance to flow with dynamic viscosity being an absolute measure and kinematic viscosity being relative to density.
