### Its meaning and application for the notes of the Unit 3 - Introduction to Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

1. Refrigeration is the process of removing heat from a low-temperature reservoir and rejecting it to a high-temperature reservoir. The refrigeration system employs the vapor-compression cycle and moves heat from a low temperature to high temperature.
2. The vapor-compression refrigeration cycle is made up of four components: evaporator, compressor, condenser, and expansion valve. The refrigerant enters the evaporator as a liquid and absorbs the heat from the surrounding. It gets vaporized in the process. The vapor is then compressed in the compressor to high pressure. The high-pressure vapor enters the condenser and rejects the heat to the atmosphere. It gets condensed into a liquid. The liquid refrigerant passes through the expansion valve to reduce its pressure. It again enters the evaporator and repeats the cycle.
3. The coefficient of performance (COP) is the ratio of the heat removed to the work done. A higher COP means the system is more efficient. The COP can be increased by increasing the evaporating temperature or decreasing the condensing temperature. A higher temperature difference between the evaporator and condenser leads to a lower COP.
4. The applications of refrigeration and air conditioning include:
- Domestic refrigerators and freezers for storing food and perishables.
- Air conditioning for human comfort in residential and commercial buildings.
- Industrial refrigeration for processes like separation, liquefaction, and preservation.
- Transportation refrigeration for trucks, trains, and ships to carry perishable goods.
