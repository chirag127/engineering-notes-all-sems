### Concept of Measurement

- Measurement is the process of quantifying observations of physical properties. It involves comparing an unknown quantity with a known quantity of the same kind.
- The known quantity is called the standard or unit and the process results in a number and a unit that can be compared to other measurements.
- The International System of Units (SI) is the modern form of the metric system and is the most widely used system of measurement. It comprises seven base units: length (meter), mass (kilogram), time (second), electric current (ampere), temperature (kelvin), amount of substance (mole), and luminous intensity (candela).
- Derived units are units that can be expressed in terms of the base units, for example, velocity (meters/second), acceleration (meters/second^2), pressure (pascals), energy (joules), power (watts), etc.
- To make meaningful measurements, the instruments used must have a smaller uncertainty than the quantity being measured. The precision and accuracy of measurements can be improved using highly precise instruments and techniques.
- Significant figures convey the precision of a measurement and are used to report the result of a measurement. The number of significant figures in a number matches the precision of the instrument used.
- Scientific notation is commonly used to write very large and very small numbers in a more convenient way. It enables simpler computations and comparisons.
- Repeatability and reproducibility are important concepts associated with making measurements. Repeatability refers to the closeness of agreement between successive measurements of the same quantity under the same conditions. Reproducibility refers to the closeness of agreement between measurements of the same quantity under changed conditions, especially changed operators, instruments, and elapsed time.
