### Introduction to Measurement

- Measurement is the process of quantifying physical variables in the real world. It involves comparing the unknown quantity with a standard unit, such as length, mass, time, etc.
- The standard units are defined by international bodies like International System of Units (SI) to achieve uniformity worldwide.
- The basic SI units are:
-- Length - meter (m)
-- Mass - kilogram (kg)
-- Time - second (s)
-- Electric current - ampere (A)
-- Temperature - kelvin (K)
-- Amount of substance - mole (mol)
-- Luminous intensity - candela (cd)
- Derived units are obtained from the basic units and have dimensions that can be expressed in terms of the basic units, e.g. - velocity (m/s), acceleration (m/s2), force (N), energy (J), etc.
- To measure a physical quantity, we need a standard, a method of comparison, and a device to make the comparison. The devices used are called measuring instruments or gauges.
- The accuracy and precision of a measurement depend on the instrument used and how it is operated. Accuracy refers to how close a measurement is to the true value. Precision refers to the reproducibility of a measurement.
- Significant figures convey the precision of a measurement. The number of significant figures in a measurement equals the number of digits known with confidence plus one estimated digit.
