### Pascal's Law and Continuity Equation

Pascal's Law states that pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions. This means that any change in pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and to the walls of the containing vessel.

Continuity equation is a statement of conservation of mass for an incompressible fluid. It states that the rate of flow into a system must equal the rate of flow out of the system. Mathematically,

$$
\dfrac{\text{d}V}{\text{d}t} + \nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0
$$

Where $V$ is volume, $t$ is time and $\vec{v}$ is velocity.

The continuity equation can also be written in terms of mass flow rate:

$$
\dfrac{\text{d}{\rho V}}{\text{d}t} + \nabla \cdot(\rho \vec{v}) = 0
$$

Where $\rho$ is density. The continuity equation relates the velocity and density of a moving fluid and ensures that mass is conserved. It is useful in deriving the Navier-Stokes equations which describe fluid flow.

The points are:
1. Pascal's Law: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.
2. Continuity equation: Rate of flow in = Rate of flow out. Ensures conservation of mass.
3. Can be written in terms of volume or mass flow rate. Relates velocity and density.
4. Useful in deriving Navier-Stokes equations for fluid flow.
