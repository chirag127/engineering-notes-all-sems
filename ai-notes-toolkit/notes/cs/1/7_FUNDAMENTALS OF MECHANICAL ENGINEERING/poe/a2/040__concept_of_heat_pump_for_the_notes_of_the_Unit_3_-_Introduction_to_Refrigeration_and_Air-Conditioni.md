### Concept of Heat Pump

- A heat pump is a device that transfers heat energy from a source of lower temperature to a destination of higher temperature.
- It uses external electrical or mechanical work to accomplish the transfer of energy, thereby increasing the temperature of the heat sink, while reducing the temperature of the heat source.
- The basic components of a heat pump are: evaporator, compressor, condenser and expansion valve.
- The working fluid (refrigerant) undergoes phase changes in the evaporator and condenser. In the evaporator, the refrigerant absorbs heat and evaporates. In the condenser, it releases heat and condenses.
- The compressor increases the pressure of the refrigerant vapour, thereby increasing its temperature. The expansion valve decreases the pressure, thereby decreasing the temperature of the refrigerant.
- The efficiency of a heat pump is expressed in terms of Coefficient of Performance (COP). It is the ratio of heat absorbed to the work done. A higher COP means higher efficiency.
- The applications of heat pumps include space heating, space cooling, water heating, etc. They can transfer 3 to 4 times more heat than the work input. Thus, they are more efficient than furnaces or air conditioners.
