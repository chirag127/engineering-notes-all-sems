### EV batteries

- Lithium-ion batteries are the most popular batteries used in electric vehicles (EVs) due to their high energy density. Some of the commonly used lithium-ion batteries in EVs are:
- Lithium Nickel Manganese Cobalt Oxide (NMC): Has a high energy density and can operate at high temperatures but more expensive. Used in Tesla Model S and X.
- Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP): Has a lower energy density but cheaper and more stable. Used in BYD e6 and BAIC EU260.
- Lithium Nickel Cobalt Aluminum Oxide (NCA): Has a high energy density and can operate at high currents but more expensive. Used in Tesla Model 3.
- The capacity and voltage of the lithium-ion batteries depend on the materials used and the battery pack configuration. The battery pack consists of many individual lithium-ion cells connected in series and parallel to achieve the required voltage and capacity.
- The batteries degrade over time due to factors like heat, use and age. The range of an EV reduces as the battery capacity decreases. However, EVs have a minimum range that must be achieved throughout the battery lifetime. Battery management systems are used to optimally use the batteries to achieve the required lifetime and range.
- The cost of lithium-ion batteries has reduced significantly over the years due to improvements in technology and mass production. However, the batteries still constitute a major portion of the total cost of an EV. More research is being done to develop cheaper and more efficient batteries to make EVs more affordable.
