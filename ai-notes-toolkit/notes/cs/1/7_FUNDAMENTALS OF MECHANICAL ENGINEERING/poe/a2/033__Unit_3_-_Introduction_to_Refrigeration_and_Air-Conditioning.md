## Unit 3 - Introduction to Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning

1. Refrigeration and Air Conditioning:
- Refrigeration is the process of removing heat from a substance or space and moving it to a place where it is unobjectionable.
- Air Conditioning refers to control of temperature, humidity, purity, and motion of air in an enclosed space.
- Refrigeration and Air Conditioning systems have wide applications in domestic as well as industrial fields.

2. Basic Refrigeration System:
- A basic refrigeration system consists of a compressor, a condenser, an expansion valve, and an evaporator.
- The compressor compresses the refrigerant vapor and increases its pressure. The high-pressure vapor is condensed in the condenser and converted into a liquid.
- The liquid refrigerant passes through the expansion valve, where its pressure is reduced. The low-pressure liquid enters the evaporator, absorbs heat from the surrounding space, and gets evaporated.
- The cycle then repeats as the evaporated refrigerant vapor is drawn into the compressor.

3. Thermodynamic principles involved:
- The basis of refrigeration is the thermodynamic principle that absorption or removal of heat from a space results in lowering its temperature.
- The properties of refrigerants and the components involved enable the absorption and rejection of heat, leading to the transfer of thermal energy and the production of the desired cooling effect.
