 Here are the notes on Newtonian and Non-Newtonian fluid for the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications:

### Newtonian Fluid

- A Newtonian fluid is a fluid in which the shear stress is directly proportional to the shear rate.
- The viscosity of a Newtonian fluid remains constant for a given temperature and pressure.
- Examples: Water, gasoline, air, etc.
- The relationship between shear stress (τ) and shear rate (γ) is given by τ = μγ, where μ is the coefficient of viscosity.

### Non-Newtonian Fluid

- A non-Newtonian fluid is a fluid in which the viscosity changes with shear rate.
- The relationship between shear stress and shear rate is non-linear.
- Examples: Paints, nail polish, ketchup, etc.
- Types:
    - Shear-thickening fluid: Viscosity increases with increase in shear rate. Example: Cornstarch and water mixture.
    - Shear-thinning fluid: Viscosity decreases with increase in shear rate. Example: Blood and solutions of polymers like polyacrylamide.
- The non-Newtonian behavior arises due to the structure of the fluid. The structural changes with applied shear lead to the variation in viscosity.
