### Resultant of force system- concurrent and non-concurrent coplanar forces

In mechanics, force is described as an action that is capable of changing an object's motion. When multiple forces are applied to an object, the resultant force is the single force that has the same effect on the object as all the individual forces combined. In this section, we will discuss the resultant of force systems, specifically concurrent and non-concurrent coplanar forces.

#### Concurrent force system

A concurrent force system is a system in which all the forces have a common point of intersection. The resultant force of a concurrent force system can be found by using the graphical or analytical method.

##### Graphical method

To find the resultant force using the graphical method, we can use the following steps:

1. Draw a vector diagram of all the forces in the system.
2. Draw a line from the origin to the end of the last force vector.
3. The length of this line represents the magnitude of the resultant force, and the angle between this line and the positive x-axis represents the direction of the resultant force.

##### Analytical method

To find the resultant force using the analytical method, we can use the following steps:

1. Resolve all the forces into their x and y components.
2. Find the sum of all the x-components and all the y-components separately.
3. The magnitude of the resultant force is given by the square root of the sum of the squares of the x and y components, and the direction is given by the arctan of the y-component divided by the x-component.

#### Non-concurrent force system

A non-concurrent force system is a system in which the forces do not have a common point of intersection. The resultant force of a non-concurrent force system can be found by using the parallelogram law or the triangle law of vector addition.

##### Parallelogram law

To find the resultant force using the parallelogram law, we can use the following steps:

1. Draw a vector diagram of all the forces in the system.
2. Draw a parallelogram using the two adjacent force vectors as adjacent sides.
3. Draw the diagonal of the parallelogram, which represents the magnitude and direction of the resultant force.

##### Triangle law

To find the resultant force using the triangle law, we can use the following steps:

1. Draw a vector diagram of all the forces in the system.
2. Choose any force vector as the starting point and draw a line parallel to the next force vector.
3. Draw a line from the starting point to the end of the second force vector.
4. The resultant force is represented by the third side of the triangle formed by these two lines.

In conclusion, the resultant force of a force system can be found by using graphical or analytical methods for concurrent force systems, and by using the parallelogram law or triangle law for non-concurrent force systems.
