### Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids

Fluids can be classified into two categories: Newtonian and Non-Newtonian fluids. Here are some key points about these fluids:

#### Newtonian Fluids
- Newtonian fluids follow the law of viscosity, which states that the shear stress is directly proportional to the rate of shear strain.
- The viscosity of Newtonian fluids remains constant irrespective of the shear rate.
- Examples of Newtonian fluids include water, air, and oil.

#### Non-Newtonian Fluids
- Non-Newtonian fluids do not follow the law of viscosity, and their viscosity depends on the shear rate.
- There are different types of Non-Newtonian fluids, such as shear-thinning, shear-thickening, and viscoelastic fluids.
- Shear-thinning fluids have a decreasing viscosity with increasing shear rate, while shear-thickening fluids have an increasing viscosity with increasing shear rate.
- Viscoelastic fluids exhibit both viscous and elastic behavior, and their behavior depends on the rate and duration of deformation.
- Examples of Non-Newtonian fluids include ketchup, toothpaste, and blood.

Understanding the behavior of fluids is crucial in the field of mechanical engineering, as it plays a significant role in designing and analyzing various systems and processes.
