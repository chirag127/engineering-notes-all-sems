### Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids

Fluids can be classified into two main categories: Newtonian and Non-Newtonian fluids. The main difference between these two types of fluids is the way they respond to applied stress, such as shear stress.

#### Newtonian Fluids

Newtonian fluids are characterized by a linear relationship between shear stress and shear rate. This means that the viscosity of the fluid remains constant regardless of the shear rate applied to it. Examples of Newtonian fluids include water, air, and most gases.

Some important properties of Newtonian fluids are:

- Constant viscosity
- Shear stress is directly proportional to shear rate
- No yield stress
- No thixotropy
- No time-dependent behavior

#### Non-Newtonian Fluids

Non-Newtonian fluids, on the other hand, do not have a linear relationship between shear stress and shear rate. The viscosity of these fluids changes with the applied shear rate, and the relationship between the two is often nonlinear. Examples of non-Newtonian fluids include blood, ketchup, and paint.

Some important properties of non-Newtonian fluids are:

- Variable viscosity
- Shear stress is not directly proportional to shear rate
- Yield stress may be present
- Thixotropy may be present
- Time-dependent behavior may be present

Non-Newtonian fluids can be further classified into different categories based on their behavior under stress. Some common types of non-Newtonian fluids include:

- Shear-thinning fluids: These fluids become less viscous as the shear rate increases. Examples include ketchup and blood.
- Shear-thickening fluids: These fluids become more viscous as the shear rate increases. Examples include cornstarch and quicksand.
- Bingham plastics: These are non-Newtonian fluids that exhibit a yield stress, meaning that they do not flow until a certain stress is applied. Examples include toothpaste and mayonnaise.

Understanding the behavior of fluids is crucial in many engineering applications. By classifying fluids into Newtonian and non-Newtonian categories, engineers can better predict how different fluids will behave under various conditions and design systems accordingly.
