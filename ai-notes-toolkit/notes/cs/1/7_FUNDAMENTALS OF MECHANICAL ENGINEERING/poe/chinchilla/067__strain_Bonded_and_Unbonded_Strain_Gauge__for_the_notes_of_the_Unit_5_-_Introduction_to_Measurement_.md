### Strain (Bonded and Unbonded Strain Gauge)

Strain is defined as the ratio of change in length to the original length of a material under stress. It is expressed in terms of microstrain (µε).

Strain gauges are used to measure strain in a material. They are classified into two types: bonded and unbonded strain gauges.

#### Bonded Strain Gauge

Bonded strain gauges are used in applications where high accuracy and precision are required. They are made of a thin metal foil with resistance wire attached to it. The gauge is bonded to the surface of the material being measured using a special adhesive. When the material is subjected to stress, the strain gauge experiences a change in resistance, which is proportional to the strain. This change in resistance is measured using a Wheatstone bridge circuit.

Advantages:
- High accuracy and precision
- Can be used to measure strains in small areas

Disadvantages:
- Difficult to install
- Cannot be reused once installed
- Requires a stable and controlled environment for accurate measurements

#### Unbonded Strain Gauge

Unbonded strain gauges are used in applications where the material being measured is subjected to high strain levels. They consist of a length of wire that is twisted or wound into a coil. When the material is subjected to stress, the coil experiences a change in length, which is proportional to the strain. This change in length is measured using a frequency counter or oscilloscope.

Advantages:
- Easy to install
- Can be reused multiple times
- Can measure high strain levels

Disadvantages:
- Lower accuracy and precision compared to bonded strain gauges
- Cannot be used to measure strains in small areas

In conclusion, bonded and unbonded strain gauges are used to measure strain in materials. Bonded strain gauges are used in applications where high accuracy and precision are required, while unbonded strain gauges are used in applications where the material being measured is subjected to high strain levels. It is important to choose the appropriate type of strain gauge for the specific application to ensure accurate measurements.
