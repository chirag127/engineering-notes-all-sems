### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine

In this section, we will discuss the construction and working of two-stroke and four-stroke SI (Spark Ignition) and CI (Compression Ignition) engines.

#### Two-stroke SI Engine

- The two-stroke SI engine has two strokes (one upstroke and one downstroke) in one revolution of the crankshaft.
- The engine has a simpler construction and fewer parts compared to a four-stroke engine.
- The fuel and air mixture is drawn into the crankcase during the downstroke, and as the piston moves upwards, the mixture is compressed and transferred to the combustion chamber.
- The spark plug ignites the mixture, and the resulting explosion pushes the piston down, completing the power stroke.
- The upward movement of the piston during the next stroke pushes the exhaust gases out of the cylinder.
- The lubrication of the engine is done by mixing oil with the fuel.
- The two-stroke engine is commonly used in small engines such as chainsaws, mopeds, and some motorcycles.

#### Four-stroke SI Engine

- The four-stroke SI engine has four strokes (one intake, one compression, one power, and one exhaust stroke) in two revolutions of the crankshaft.
- The engine has a more complex construction and more parts compared to a two-stroke engine.
- The fuel and air mixture is drawn into the cylinder during the intake stroke, and as the piston moves upward, the mixture is compressed during the compression stroke.
- The spark plug ignites the mixture, and the resulting explosion pushes the piston down, completing the power stroke.
- The upward movement of the piston during the exhaust stroke pushes the exhaust gases out of the cylinder.
- The lubrication of the engine is done by a separate oil system, and the oil is pumped to various parts of the engine.
- The four-stroke engine is commonly used in automobiles, trucks, and other large vehicles.

#### Two-stroke CI Engine

- The two-stroke CI engine has two strokes (one upstroke and one downstroke) in one revolution of the crankshaft.
- The engine has a simpler construction and fewer parts compared to a four-stroke engine.
- In a two-stroke CI engine, the air is compressed during the downstroke, and fuel is injected into the compressed air during the upstroke.
- The resulting combustion pushes the piston down, completing the power stroke.
- The upward movement of the piston during the next stroke pushes the exhaust gases out of the cylinder.
- The lubrication of the engine is done by mixing oil with the fuel.
- The two-stroke CI engine is commonly used in small diesel engines such as chainsaws, generators, and some motorcycles.

#### Four-stroke CI Engine

- The four-stroke CI engine has four strokes (one intake, one compression, one power, and one exhaust stroke) in two revolutions of the crankshaft.
- The engine has a more complex construction and more parts compared to a two-stroke engine.
- In a four-stroke CI engine, the air is drawn into the cylinder during the intake stroke, and as the piston moves upward, the air is compressed during the compression stroke.
- Fuel is injected into the compressed air during the power stroke, and the resulting combustion pushes the piston down, completing the power stroke.
- The upward movement of the piston during the exhaust stroke pushes the exhaust gases out of the cylinder.
- The lubrication of the engine is done by a separate oil system, and the oil is pumped to various parts of the engine.
- The four-stroke CI engine is commonly used in large diesel engines such as ships, locomotives, and heavy-duty trucks.

In conclusion, the construction and working of two-stroke and four-stroke SI and CI engines have their own unique characteristics and advantages. It is important to understand the differences between these engines to choose the right type of engine for a particular application.
