### Equilibrium Equations and Support Reactions

In the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, Unit 1 - Introduction to Mechanics, equilibrium equations and support reactions are important concepts to understand.

1. Equilibrium equations are mathematical expressions that describe the balance of forces and moments acting on a body or a system of bodies.
2. These equations are based on Newton's laws of motion, which state that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.
3. In two-dimensional problems, there are three equilibrium equations: the sum of the forces in the x-direction must be zero, the sum of the forces in the y-direction must be zero, and the sum of the moments about any point must be zero.
4. In three-dimensional problems, there are six equilibrium equations: the sum of the forces in the x, y, and z directions must be zero, and the sum of the moments about the x, y, and z axes must be zero.
5. Support reactions are the forces and moments that a support exerts on a body to keep it in equilibrium.
6. Common types of supports include fixed supports, pinned supports, and roller supports.
7. The type of support determines the number and direction of the support reactions.
8. To solve for the support reactions, the equilibrium equations are used in conjunction with the free body diagram of the body or system of bodies.
