### Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles
#### FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

##### Difference between Two-Stroke and Four-Stroke IC Engines:

1. **Cycle completion:** A two-stroke engine completes a power cycle in two strokes of the piston (one up and one down movement) while a four-stroke engine completes a power cycle in four strokes of the piston (two up and two down movements).
2. **Valve mechanism:** Two-stroke engines do not have a dedicated valve mechanism while four-stroke engines have a complex valve mechanism to control the intake and exhaust of gases.
3. **Power output:** Two-stroke engines have a higher power-to-weight ratio and can produce more power with a smaller engine size compared to four-stroke engines.
4. **Fuel efficiency:** Four-stroke engines are generally more fuel-efficient than two-stroke engines.
5. **Lubrication:** Two-stroke engines require a mixture of oil and fuel for lubrication while four-stroke engines have a separate lubrication system.
6. **Emissions:** Two-stroke engines generally produce more emissions than four-stroke engines due to incomplete combustion of the fuel-oil mixture.

##### Difference between SI and CI Engines:

1. **Ignition:** SI engines use a spark plug to ignite the air-fuel mixture while CI engines use the heat generated by the compression of the air-fuel mixture to ignite it.
2. **Fuel:** SI engines generally use gasoline as fuel while CI engines use diesel.
3. **Compression ratio:** CI engines have a higher compression ratio than SI engines.
4. **Efficiency:** CI engines are generally more efficient than SI engines due to their higher compression ratio.
5. **Noise:** CI engines are generally noisier than SI engines due to the higher pressure and temperature of the combustion process.
6. **Emissions:** CI engines generally produce less CO and HC emissions than SI engines but produce more NOx and particulate matter emissions.
