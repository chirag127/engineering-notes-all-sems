### Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications
#### Pressure
- Pressure is defined as the force exerted per unit area.
- The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa), which is equivalent to one newton per square meter (N/m²).
- Pressure can be measured using various instruments, such as a manometer or a barometer.
- In fluid mechanics, pressure is an important concept as it is used to describe the behavior of fluids at rest or in motion.
- The pressure at a point within a fluid is the same in all directions, this is known as Pascal's principle.
- The pressure in a fluid increases with depth due to the weight of the fluid above it.
- The pressure difference between two points in a fluid can cause the fluid to flow from the point of higher pressure to the point of lower pressure.
- The Bernoulli's equation describes the relationship between the pressure, velocity, and elevation of a fluid in motion.
- Pressure can also be used to calculate the force exerted by a fluid on an object, such as the buoyant force or the drag force.
