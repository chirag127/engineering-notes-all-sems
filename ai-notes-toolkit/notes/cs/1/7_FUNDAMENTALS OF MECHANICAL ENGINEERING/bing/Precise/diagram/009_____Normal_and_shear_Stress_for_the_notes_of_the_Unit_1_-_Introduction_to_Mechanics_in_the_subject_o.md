### Normal and Shear Stress

#### Introduction to Mechanics - Unit 1: Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering

- **Stress** is defined as the internal resistance offered by a body to an external force.
- It is a measure of the intensity of the internal forces acting within a deformable body.
- Stress is expressed in terms of force per unit area.
- There are two main types of stress: **normal stress** and **shear stress**.

#### Normal Stress
- Normal stress is the stress that acts perpendicular to the plane of the cross-section of the body.
- It is denoted by the symbol σ (sigma).
- Normal stress can be either tensile or compressive.
- Tensile stress is the stress that tends to pull the particles of the body apart.
- Compressive stress is the stress that tends to push the particles of the body together.

#### Shear Stress
- Shear stress is the stress that acts parallel to the plane of the cross-section of the body.
- It is denoted by the symbol τ (tau).
- Shear stress is the stress that tends to cause the particles of the body to slide over one another.
- It is the stress that causes deformation by shear strain.

#### Conclusion
- Normal and shear stress are two main types of stress that act on a deformable body.
- Normal stress acts perpendicular to the plane of the cross-section of the body, while shear stress acts parallel to it.
- Normal stress can be either tensile or compressive, while shear stress causes deformation by shear strain.
