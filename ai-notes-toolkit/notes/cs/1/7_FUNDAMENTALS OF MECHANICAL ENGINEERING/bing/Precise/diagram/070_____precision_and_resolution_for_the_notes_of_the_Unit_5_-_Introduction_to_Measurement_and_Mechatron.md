### Precision and Resolution

Precision and resolution are two important concepts in the field of measurement and mechatronics, particularly in the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering.

- **Precision** refers to the degree of repeatability of a measurement. It is a measure of how close the results of multiple measurements of the same quantity are to each other. A high degree of precision indicates that the measurement process is consistent and produces little random error.

- **Resolution** refers to the smallest change in the quantity being measured that can be detected by the measuring instrument. It is determined by the smallest division or scale interval on the instrument. A high resolution instrument can detect smaller changes in the quantity being measured.

These two concepts are closely related but not the same. It is possible for an instrument to have high resolution but low precision, or vice versa. For example, a digital scale with a resolution of 0.1 grams may be able to detect small changes in weight, but if it consistently gives different readings for the same object, it has low precision.

In the context of Unit 5 - Introduction to Measurement and Mechatronics, understanding the concepts of precision and resolution is important for selecting and using appropriate measuring instruments and for interpreting measurement data. It is also important for designing and building mechatronic systems that rely on accurate and precise measurements.
