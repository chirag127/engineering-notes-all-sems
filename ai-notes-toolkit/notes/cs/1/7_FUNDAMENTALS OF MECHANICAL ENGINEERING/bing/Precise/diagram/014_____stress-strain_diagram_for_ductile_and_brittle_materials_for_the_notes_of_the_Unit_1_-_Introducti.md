### Stress-Strain Diagram for Ductile and Brittle Materials

A stress-strain diagram is a graphical representation of the relationship between the stress and strain of a material. It is used to determine the mechanical properties of a material, such as its strength, ductility, and toughness.

#### Ductile Materials
- Ductile materials are materials that can undergo large plastic deformation before failure.
- The stress-strain diagram for a ductile material typically has a linear elastic region, followed by a non-linear plastic region.
- In the elastic region, the material obeys Hooke's Law, where the stress is proportional to the strain.
- In the plastic region, the material undergoes permanent deformation.
- The point at which the material transitions from elastic to plastic behavior is called the yield point.
- The maximum stress that the material can withstand before failure is called the ultimate tensile strength.

#### Brittle Materials
- Brittle materials are materials that undergo little to no plastic deformation before failure.
- The stress-strain diagram for a brittle material typically has a linear elastic region, followed by a sudden fracture.
- In the elastic region, the material obeys Hooke's Law, where the stress is proportional to the strain.
- The maximum stress that the material can withstand before failure is called the ultimate tensile strength.
- Unlike ductile materials, brittle materials do not have a distinct yield point.
