### Elastic Constants and their Relationship

Elastic constants are the material properties that describe the relationship between stress and strain in a material. There are several elastic constants, including:

1. **Young's Modulus (E):** This is the ratio of tensile stress to tensile strain within the elastic limit. It is a measure of the stiffness of a material.

2. **Shear Modulus (G):** This is the ratio of shear stress to shear strain within the elastic limit. It is a measure of the rigidity of a material.

3. **Bulk Modulus (K):** This is the ratio of hydrostatic stress to volumetric strain within the elastic limit. It is a measure of the compressibility of a material.

4. **Poisson's Ratio (ν):** This is the ratio of lateral strain to axial strain within the elastic limit. It is a measure of the degree to which a material tends to expand in directions perpendicular to the direction of compression.

These elastic constants are related to each other through various equations. For example, the relationship between Young's Modulus, Shear Modulus, and Poisson's Ratio is given by the equation:

E = 2G(1 + ν)

The relationship between Bulk Modulus, Shear Modulus, and Poisson's Ratio is given by the equation:
