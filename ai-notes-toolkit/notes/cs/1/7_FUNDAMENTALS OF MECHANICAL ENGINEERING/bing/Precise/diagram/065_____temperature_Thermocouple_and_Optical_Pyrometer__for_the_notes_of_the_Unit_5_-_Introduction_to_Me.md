### Unit 5 - Introduction to Measurement and Mechatronics in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING
#### Temperature (Thermocouple and Optical Pyrometer)

1. A thermocouple is a temperature sensor that consists of two dissimilar metal wires joined at one end, which generates a voltage when the temperature of one of the junctions differs from the reference temperature at the other junction.
2. The voltage produced by a thermocouple is proportional to the temperature difference between the two junctions, and the relationship between voltage and temperature is determined by the Seebeck effect.
3. Thermocouples are widely used in industrial and scientific applications due to their low cost, durability, and wide temperature range.
4. An optical pyrometer is a non-contact temperature measurement device that measures the temperature of an object by detecting the thermal radiation emitted by the object.
5. Optical pyrometers are commonly used in high-temperature applications, such as in the steel and glass industries, where contact temperature measurement is not possible or practical.
6. The accuracy of an optical pyrometer depends on several factors, including the emissivity of the object being measured, the wavelength of the thermal radiation being detected, and the calibration of the instrument.
