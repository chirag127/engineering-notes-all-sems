### Error in Measurements

In the context of the Unit 5 - Introduction to Measurement and Mechatronics in the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, error in measurements refers to the difference between the measured value and the true value of the quantity being measured.

There are several sources of error in measurements, including:

1. **Instrumental errors:** These errors arise from the limitations of the measuring instrument, such as the resolution or accuracy of the instrument.

2. **Environmental errors:** These errors are caused by external factors such as temperature, humidity, and air pressure, which can affect the measurement.

3. **Observational errors:** These errors are caused by the observer, such as parallax error or misreading the instrument.

4. **Random errors:** These errors are unpredictable and arise from unknown sources. They can be reduced by taking multiple measurements and averaging the results.

It is important to understand and account for these sources of error in order to improve the accuracy and reliability of measurements.
