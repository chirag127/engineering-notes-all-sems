### Electric vehicles and hybrid vehicles

Electric vehicles (EVs) and hybrid vehicles are two types of vehicles that are powered by electricity. They differ in their fuel type and the way they generate electricity.

- **Electric vehicles (EVs)** have a battery instead of a gasoline tank, and an electric motor instead of an internal combustion engine. The battery can be recharged with electricity, which comes from many generating sources, from the burning of fossil fuels to renewable sources like solar, wind, and hydro power.

- **Hybrid vehicles** are a combination of gasoline and electric vehicles, so they have a battery, an electric motor, a gasoline tank, and an internal combustion engine. They are entirely fueled by gasoline and derive the electricity they need through brake regeneration and the harnessing of excess engine power.

- **Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs)** are a type of hybrid vehicle that can be recharged by plugging into an external source of electricity, as well as by their internal combustion engine.

These vehicles offer a more environmentally friendly and sustainable alternative to traditional gasoline-powered vehicles. They produce fewer emissions and can be more cost-effective to operate in the long run. However, the availability of charging infrastructure and the initial cost of the vehicles can be barriers to their widespread adoption.
