### Strain (Bonded and Unbonded Strain Gauge)

Strain is defined as the deformation of a body due to an applied force. It is a measure of the change in length of a material per unit length. Strain gauges are devices used to measure strain on an object. There are two types of strain gauges: bonded and unbonded.

1. Bonded Strain Gauge: A bonded strain gauge is attached directly to the surface of the object being measured. The gauge consists of a wire or foil grid that is bonded to a thin backing material. As the object deforms, the grid deforms with it, causing a change in the electrical resistance of the grid. This change in resistance is measured and used to calculate the strain on the object.

2. Unbonded Strain Gauge: An unbonded strain gauge is not attached directly to the object being measured. Instead, it consists of a wire or foil grid that is suspended between two supports. As the object deforms, it causes the supports to move, which in turn causes the grid to deform. This deformation causes a change in the electrical resistance of the grid, which is measured and used to calculate the strain on the object.

Both types of strain gauges are commonly used in the field of mechanical engineering for the measurement of strain in materials and structures. They are an important tool in the study of the mechanical behavior of materials and the design of mechanical systems.
