### Hookes’ law

Hooke's law is a principle of physics that states that the force needed to extend or compress a spring by some distance is proportional to that distance. It is expressed mathematically as F = kx, where F is the force applied to the spring, x is the displacement of the spring from its equilibrium position, and k is the spring constant, a measure of the stiffness of the spring.

- Hooke's law applies to many materials, not just springs, and is a fundamental principle of elasticity.
- The spring constant, k, is a measure of the stiffness of the spring and is determined by the material and geometry of the spring.
- The displacement, x, is the change in length of the spring from its equilibrium position.
- Hooke's law is an idealization and is only approximately true for small displacements. For larger displacements, the force-displacement relationship may become nonlinear.
- Hooke's law is named after the 17th-century British physicist Robert Hooke, who first formulated the law in 1660.
