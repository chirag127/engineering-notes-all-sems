### IC Engine

An internal combustion engine (IC engine) is a type of heat engine where the combustion of fuel occurs within the engine, producing mechanical work. This mechanical work is used to power vehicles, machines, and other devices.

Some key points to note about IC engines are:

1. IC engines convert chemical energy from fuel into mechanical energy through a series of small explosions or combustions.
2. The fuel-air mixture is compressed and ignited, causing the gases to expand and push a piston, which in turn rotates a crankshaft to produce mechanical work.
3. IC engines can be classified into two main types: spark-ignition (SI) engines and compression-ignition (CI) engines.
4. SI engines use a spark plug to ignite the fuel-air mixture, while CI engines rely on the heat generated by the compression of the fuel-air mixture to ignite it.
5. IC engines can also be classified based on the number of strokes per cycle: two-stroke and four-stroke engines.
6. Two-stroke engines complete a power cycle in two strokes of the piston, while four-stroke engines complete a power cycle in four strokes of the piston.
7. IC engines are widely used in transportation, including cars, trucks, motorcycles, and boats, as well as in other applications such as generators and pumps.
