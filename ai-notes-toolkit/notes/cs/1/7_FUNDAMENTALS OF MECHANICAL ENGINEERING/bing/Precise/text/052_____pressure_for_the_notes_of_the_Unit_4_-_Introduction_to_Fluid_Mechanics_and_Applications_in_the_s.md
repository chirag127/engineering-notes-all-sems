### Pressure - Unit 4: Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications - Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering

1. Pressure is defined as the force exerted per unit area.
2. The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa), which is equal to one newton per square meter (N/m²).
3. Pressure can be measured using various instruments, such as a manometer or a barometer.
4. In fluid mechanics, pressure is an important concept as it is used to describe the behavior of fluids at rest or in motion.
5. The pressure at a point within a fluid is the same in all directions, this is known as Pascal's principle.
6. The pressure in a fluid increases with depth due to the weight of the fluid above it.
7. The pressure difference between two points in a fluid can cause the fluid to flow from the point of higher pressure to the point of lower pressure.
8. The Bernoulli's equation describes the relationship between the pressure, velocity, and elevation of a fluid in motion.
9. Applications of pressure in fluid mechanics include the design of hydraulic systems, the calculation of buoyant forces, and the analysis of fluid flow in pipes and channels.
