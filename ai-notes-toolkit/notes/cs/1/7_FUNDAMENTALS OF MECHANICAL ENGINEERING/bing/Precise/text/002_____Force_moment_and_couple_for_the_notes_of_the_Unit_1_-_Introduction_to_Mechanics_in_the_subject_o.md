### Force moment and couple

- **Force moment** is the measure of the rotational effect of a force on an object.
- It is calculated by multiplying the magnitude of the force by the perpendicular distance from the line of action of the force to the axis of rotation.
- The unit of force moment is Newton-meter (N-m).
- A **couple** is a pair of forces of equal magnitude, acting in opposite directions, whose lines of action do not coincide.
- The effect of a couple is to produce a rotation without translation.
- The moment of a couple is the same about any point and is equal to the product of the magnitude of one of the forces and the perpendicular distance between their lines of action.
- The moment of a couple is a free vector, meaning it is not associated with a specific point and can be moved anywhere in space without changing its effect.
- The moment of a couple is also known as the **torque** or **twist**.
- The unit of torque is also Newton-meter (N-m).
- The concept of force moment and couple is important in the study of mechanics and is used in the analysis of structures, machines, and other mechanical systems.
