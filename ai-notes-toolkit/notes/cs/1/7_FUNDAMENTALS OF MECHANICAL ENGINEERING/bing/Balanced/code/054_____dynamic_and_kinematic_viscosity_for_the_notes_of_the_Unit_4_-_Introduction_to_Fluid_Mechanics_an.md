### Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity

- Viscosity is a property of fluids that measures their resistance to flow. It is also known as the internal friction of fluids.
- There are two types of viscosity: dynamic (or absolute) viscosity and kinematic viscosity .
- Dynamic viscosity evaluates the internal resistance of a fluid to flow when a force is applied. It is a measure of how much force is needed to make the fluid flow at a certain rate .
- Kinematic viscosity describes the ratio of dynamic viscosity to density. It is a measure of how fast the fluid moves when a certain force is applied. It incorporates the fluid's weight or density as part of its measurement .
- The SI unit of dynamic viscosity is N s/m2 or Pa s, while the SI unit of kinematic viscosity is m2/s .
- The dynamic viscosity of a fluid depends on the substance and its temperature, while the kinematic viscosity depends on both the substance, temperature and density .
- Examples of fluids with high dynamic viscosity are honey, glycerin, and motor oil. Examples of fluids with low dynamic viscosity are water, air, and gasoline.
- Examples of fluids with high kinematic viscosity are honey, glycerin, and motor oil. Examples of fluids with low kinematic viscosity are water, air, and gasoline.
- Dynamic and kinematic viscosity are important concepts in fluid mechanics and applications, such as lubrication, flow measurement, heat transfer, and fluid dynamics.
