### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine

- SI stands for spark ignition and CI stands for compression ignition. These are two types of internal combustion engines that use different methods to ignite the fuel-air mixture in the cylinder.
- A two stroke engine completes one cycle of operation in two strokes of the piston or one revolution of the crankshaft. A four stroke engine completes one cycle of operation in four strokes of the piston or two revolutions of the crankshaft.
- The four strokes of a four stroke engine are intake, compression, power and exhaust. The two strokes of a two stroke engine are compression and power (or expansion).
- In a four stroke SI engine, the intake stroke draws the fuel-air mixture into the cylinder through the intake valve. The compression stroke compresses the mixture and the spark plug ignites it at the end of the stroke. The power stroke produces work by expanding the hot gases and pushing the piston down. The exhaust stroke expels the burnt gases through the exhaust valve.
- In a four stroke CI engine, the intake stroke draws only air into the cylinder through the intake valve. The compression stroke compresses the air to a high pressure and temperature. The fuel is injected into the cylinder near the end of the stroke and ignites spontaneously due to the heat of the compressed air. The power stroke produces work by expanding the hot gases and pushing the piston down. The exhaust stroke expels the burnt gases through the exhaust valve.
- In a two stroke SI engine, the compression stroke compresses the fuel-air mixture in the cylinder and the spark plug ignites it at the end of the stroke. The power stroke produces work by expanding the hot gases and pushing the piston down. At the same time, the fuel-air mixture is drawn into the crankcase through the inlet port. The exhaust stroke expels the burnt gases through the exhaust port and transfers the fresh mixture from the crankcase to the cylinder through the transfer port.
- In a two stroke CI engine, the compression stroke compresses only air in the cylinder and the fuel is injected into the cylinder near the end of the stroke and ignites spontaneously due to the heat of the compressed air. The power stroke produces work by expanding the hot gases and pushing the piston down. At the same time, the air is drawn into the crankcase through the inlet port. The exhaust stroke expels the burnt gases through the exhaust port and transfers the fresh air from the crankcase to the cylinder through the transfer port.
- Two stroke engines have simpler valve systems and fewer moving parts, and are more power dense than four stroke engines. However, since they require oil to be mixed with the fuel, and some of the fuel mixture escapes unused, they pollute more than a comparable four stroke engine .
- Four stroke engines have more complex valve systems and more moving parts, and are less power dense than two stroke engines. However, they have better fuel efficiency and lower emissions than two stroke engines .
