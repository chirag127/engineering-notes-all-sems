### Chargers
- A charger is a device that converts electrical energy into chemical energy stored in a battery or a capacitor.
- A charger can be classified into two types: constant current (CC) and constant voltage (CV).
- A constant current charger delivers a fixed amount of current to the battery or capacitor until it reaches a certain voltage level, then switches to a lower current or turns off.
- A constant voltage charger delivers a fixed voltage to the battery or capacitor until it reaches a certain current level, then switches to a lower voltage or turns off.
- A charger can also have different modes of operation, such as trickle, float, boost, and equalization.
- A trickle charger delivers a very low current to the battery or capacitor to maintain its charge level and prevent self-discharge.
- A float charger delivers a slightly higher current than a trickle charger to compensate for the losses due to internal resistance and leakage.
- A boost charger delivers a high current to the battery or capacitor to quickly charge it up to a certain voltage level, then switches to a lower current or voltage mode.
- An equalization charger delivers a higher voltage than a normal charger to the battery or capacitor to balance the charge level of each cell and remove sulfation.
- A charger can also have different features, such as temperature compensation, reverse polarity protection, short circuit protection, overcharge protection, and indicator lights.
