# Temperature (Thermocouple and Optical Pyrometer)

- Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the molecules in a substance.
- Temperature measurement is important for many industrial processes, such as metallurgy, chemical engineering, and manufacturing.
- There are different methods of measuring temperature, depending on the range, accuracy, and application required.
- Two common methods of temperature measurement are thermocouples and optical pyrometers.

## Thermocouple

- A thermocouple is a device that consists of two different metals joined at one end, forming a junction.
- When the junction is heated or cooled, a voltage is generated across the two metals, proportional to the temperature difference between the junction and the other end.
- The voltage can be measured by a voltmeter or a thermometer, and calibrated to give the temperature of the junction.
- Thermocouples are widely used for measuring temperatures from -200°C to 2000°C, with high accuracy and fast response time.
- Thermocouples are also cheap, rugged, and easy to install and use.
- However, thermocouples have some disadvantages, such as:
  - They require a reference temperature at the other end, which may be affected by ambient conditions or errors in measurement.
  - They are susceptible to corrosion, oxidation, and contamination, which may alter their characteristics and reduce their accuracy.
  - They have a nonlinear relationship between voltage and temperature, which requires complex calibration and compensation.
  - They have a low voltage output, which may be affected by noise and interference.

## Optical Pyrometer

- An optical pyrometer is a device that measures the temperature of a hot object by comparing its brightness or color to a known standard.
- The principle of an optical pyrometer is based on the fact that all objects emit electromagnetic radiation according to their temperature, and the wavelength and intensity of the radiation depend on the temperature.
- An optical pyrometer consists of an optical system, a detector, and a display or indicator.
- The optical system focuses the radiation from the target object onto the detector, which converts it into an electrical signal.
- The display or indicator shows the temperature of the target object, based on the calibration of the detector and the optical system.
- Optical pyrometers are used for measuring high temperatures, from 500°C to 3000°C, without contact or interference with the target object.
- Optical pyrometers have some advantages, such as:
  - They are fast, accurate, and reliable, as they do not depend on the physical properties or conditions of the target object.
  - They are safe, as they do not require contact or proximity to the target object, which may be hazardous or inaccessible.
  - They are simple, as they do not require complex calibration or compensation, and can be easily adjusted for different emissivities or wavelengths.
- However, optical pyrometers also have some disadvantages, such as:
  - They are affected by the emissivity of the target object, which is the ratio of the actual radiation emitted by the object to the ideal radiation emitted by a black body at the same temperature. The emissivity varies with the material, surface condition, and wavelength of the radiation, and may be unknown or variable.
  - They are affected by the ambient radiation, which may interfere with the radiation from the target object, and cause errors or fluctuations in the measurement.
  - They are affected by the optical system, which may introduce errors or distortions due to the lens, filter, or mirror used, or due to dust, smoke, or moisture in the air.
