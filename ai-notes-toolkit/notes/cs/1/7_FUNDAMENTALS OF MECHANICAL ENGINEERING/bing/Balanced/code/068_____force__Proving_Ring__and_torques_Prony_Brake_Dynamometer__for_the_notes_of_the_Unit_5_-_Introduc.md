# Force (Proving Ring)

- A proving ring is a device used to measure force. It consists of an elastic ring of known diameter with a measuring device located in the center of the ring.
- The ring is deformed by the applied force and the deformation is measured by the device, which can be a dial gauge, a micrometer, or a strain gauge.
- The force is proportional to the deformation according to Hooke's law, and the proportionality constant is called the calibration factor of the ring.
- Proving rings come in a variety of sizes and are made of a steel alloy. They are calibrated by applying known forces and recording the corresponding deformations.
- Proving rings are used in various applications, such as soil testing, pile testing, load testing of structures, and calibration of other force-measuring devices.

# Torques (Prony Brake Dynamometer)

- A Prony brake dynamometer is a device used to measure torque, or turning force, transmitted by a rotating shaft. It consists of a clamp that applies friction to the shaft and a lever arm that measures the force required to hold the clamp.
- The clamp can be made of wood, metal, or other materials, and can be adjusted to vary the friction. The lever arm can be attached to a spring balance or a standard mass to measure the force.
- The torque is calculated by multiplying the force measured at the end of the lever arm by the lever arm length: Torque = Force x Distance.
- The power, or the rate of doing work, is calculated by multiplying the torque by the angular speed of the shaft: Power = Torque x Angular Speed.
- Prony brake dynamometers are simple and inexpensive devices that can be used to measure the power output of engines, motors, and other rotating machines.
