## Unit 3 - Introduction to Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning

- Refrigeration and air-conditioning are the processes of maintaining a desired temperature and humidity level in an enclosed space by removing or adding heat from or to that space.
- Refrigeration and air-conditioning use the **refrigeration cycle**, which is a series of thermodynamic processes that transfer heat from a low-temperature region to a high-temperature region using a **refrigerant** as the working fluid.
- The refrigeration cycle consists of four main components: **compressor**, **condenser**, **expansion device**, and **evaporator**.
- The refrigeration cycle can be explained as follows :
  - The refrigerant enters the compressor as a low-pressure, low-temperature vapor and is compressed to a high-pressure, high-temperature vapor.
  - The refrigerant leaves the compressor and flows through the condenser, where it releases heat to the surrounding air or water and condenses to a high-pressure, high-temperature liquid.
  - The refrigerant passes through the expansion device, which reduces its pressure and temperature and causes some of it to flash to a vapor. This process is called **throttling** or **expansion with flashing**.
  - The refrigerant enters the evaporator as a low-pressure, low-temperature liquid-vapor mixture and absorbs heat from the space to be cooled, such as a refrigerator or a room. The refrigerant evaporates to a low-pressure, low-temperature vapor and returns to the compressor to complete the cycle.
- The performance of a refrigeration or air-conditioning system is measured by the **coefficient of performance (COP)**, which is the ratio of the useful heat transfer to the work input. The COP can be expressed as:

  - COP = QL / W for refrigeration, where QL is the heat removed from the low-temperature region and W is the work done by the compressor.
  - COP = QH / W for air-conditioning, where QH is the heat delivered to the high-temperature region and W is the work done by the compressor.
- The properties of air that are relevant for air-conditioning are its **specific heat capacity (Cp)**, which is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of one kilogram of air by one degree Celsius, and its **humidity ratio (w)**, which is the mass of water vapor per unit mass of dry air. The Cp of air is about 1.005 kJ/kg.K and the w of air varies depending on the temperature and the relative humidity.
- Air-conditioning systems can be classified into two types: **all-air systems** and **air-water systems**. All-air systems use only air as the medium for heat transfer and distribution, while air-water systems use a combination of air and water for heat transfer and distribution.
