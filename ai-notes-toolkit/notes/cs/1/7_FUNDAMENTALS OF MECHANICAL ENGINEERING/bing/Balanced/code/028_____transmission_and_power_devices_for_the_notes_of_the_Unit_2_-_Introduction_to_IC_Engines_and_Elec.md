### Transmission and Power Devices for IC Engines and Electric Vehicles

- A transmission is a machine that transmits power from the energy source (electric motor or internal combustion engine) to the wheels of a vehicle.
- A transmission can have different types of gears, such as manual, automatic, or continuously variable transmission (CVT), which change the speed and torque of the output shaft according to the driving conditions.
- A power device is a component that converts, controls, or distributes the electrical energy in a vehicle, such as a battery, an alternator, a motor, or a power split device.
- A power split device is a special type of transmission that can operate the vehicle with electric motor power and the IC engine separately or can also combine the power from both sources. It also acts as a CVT and eliminates the need for any other transmission. It is used in some hybrid vehicles, such as the Toyota Prius.
- An electric motor is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. It can be powered by a battery or a fuel cell. It can have different types, such as induction, permanent magnet, or switched reluctance motors, which have different characteristics and efficiencies.
- An internal combustion engine is a device that converts chemical energy from fuel into mechanical energy. It can have different types, such as gasoline, diesel, or hybrid engines, which have different configurations, emissions, and performances.
- A battery is a device that stores electrical energy in the form of chemical energy. It can have different types, such as lead-acid, nickel-metal hydride, or lithium-ion batteries, which have different capacities, voltages, and lifetimes.
- An alternator is a device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. It is used to charge the battery and power the electrical devices in a vehicle. It can have different types, such as claw-pole, Lundell, or claw-pole with permanent magnets, which have different outputs and efficiencies.
