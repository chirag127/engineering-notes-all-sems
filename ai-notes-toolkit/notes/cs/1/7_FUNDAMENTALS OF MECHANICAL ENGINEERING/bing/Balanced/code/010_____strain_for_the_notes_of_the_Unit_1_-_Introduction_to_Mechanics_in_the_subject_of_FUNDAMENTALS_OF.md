### Strain

Strain is a measure of the amount of deformation that an object undergoes due to an applied force. Strain is a dimensionless quantity that is calculated by dividing the change in length or shape of an object by its original length or shape. Strain can be positive or negative, depending on whether the object is stretched or compressed.

There are three main types of strain:

- Longitudinal strain: This is the strain that occurs when an object is subjected to a force that is parallel to its length. Longitudinal strain is defined as the ratio of the change in length to the original length of the object. For example, if a metal rod of length 10 cm is stretched by 1 cm, its longitudinal strain is 0.1.
- Volumetric strain: This is the strain that occurs when an object is subjected to a force that changes its volume. Volumetric strain is defined as the ratio of the change in volume to the original volume of the object. For example, if a balloon of volume 100 cm^3 is inflated to 120 cm^3, its volumetric strain is 0.2.
- Shearing strain: This is the strain that occurs when an object is subjected to a force that causes it to slide or twist. Shearing strain is defined as the ratio of the change in angle to the original angle of the object. For example, if a book of thickness 2 cm is tilted by 10 degrees, its shearing strain is 0.087 radians.
