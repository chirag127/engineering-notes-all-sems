### Normal and Shear Stress

- Normal stress is the stress that acts perpendicular to the cross-sectional area of a material. It arises from the normal force, the component of force vector that is perpendicular to the material cross section. Normal stress can be either tensile or compressive, depending on the direction of the normal force. Tensile stress causes elongation of the material, while compressive stress causes shortening of the material.
- Shear stress is the stress that acts parallel to the cross-sectional area of a material. It arises from the shear force, the component of force vector that is parallel to the material cross section. Shear stress causes deformation of the material, such as twisting, sliding, or shearing .
- Normal and shear stress are related by the following equation:

![\tau = \frac{VQ}{Ib}](https://latex.codecogs.com/png.latex?%5Ctau%20%3D%20%5Cfrac%7BVQ%7D%7BIb%7D "\tau = \frac{VQ}{Ib}")

where:

  - τ is the shear stress
  - V is the shear force
  - Q is the first moment of area
  - I is the second moment of area
  - b is the width of the cross section

- Examples of normal and shear stress are:

  - A rope that is hanging from the ceiling by a light fixture. The rope experiences a tensile normal stress due to the weight of the fixture, and a shear stress due to the friction between the rope and the ceiling.
  - A beam that is supported by two columns and loaded by a weight in the middle. The beam experiences a compressive normal stress at the top and a tensile normal stress at the bottom, and a shear stress along the length of the beam.
  - A bolt that is holding two plates together. The bolt experiences a tensile normal stress due to the clamping force of the plates, and a shear stress due to the torque applied to the bolt.
