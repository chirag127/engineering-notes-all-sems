### Introduction

- Fluid mechanics is the branch of science that deals with the behavior of fluids (liquids and gases) at rest and in motion.
- Fluid mechanics has many applications in engineering, such as aerodynamics, hydraulics, fluid power, heat transfer, etc.
- Fluid mechanics can be divided into two main branches: fluid statics and fluid dynamics.
- Fluid statics is the study of fluids at rest or in equilibrium. It deals with the concepts of pressure, buoyancy, hydrostatic forces, etc.
- Fluid dynamics is the study of fluids in motion. It deals with the concepts of velocity, acceleration, flow rate, continuity, energy, momentum, viscosity, turbulence, etc.
- Fluid mechanics is governed by three fundamental principles: conservation of mass, conservation of energy, and conservation of momentum. These principles are expressed by mathematical equations, such as the continuity equation, the Bernoulli equation, and the Navier-Stokes equation.
- Fluid mechanics is also influenced by various external factors, such as gravity, surface tension, capillarity, compressibility, etc.
- Fluid mechanics is a complex and challenging subject that requires a good understanding of mathematics, physics, and engineering. It also requires experimental and computational methods to analyze and solve fluid problems.
