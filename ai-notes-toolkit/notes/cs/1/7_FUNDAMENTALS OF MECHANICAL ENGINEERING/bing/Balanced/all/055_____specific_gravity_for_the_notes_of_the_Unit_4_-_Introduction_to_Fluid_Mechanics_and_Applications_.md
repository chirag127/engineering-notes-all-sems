# Specific Gravity

- Specific gravity is a dimensionless quantity that compares the density of a substance with the density of a reference substance, usually water.
- Specific gravity can be calculated using the following formula  :

    $$SG = \frac{\rho_{substance}}{\rho_{water}}$$

    - Where SG is the specific gravity, $\rho_{substance}$ is the density of the substance, and $\rho_{water}$ is the density of water.
- The unit of specific gravity is the same as the unit of density, which is kg/m$^3$ or g/cm$^3$.
- The specific gravity of water at 4°C is 1, because the density of water at that temperature is 1000 kg/m$^3$ or 1 g/cm$^3$ .
- The specific gravity of a substance can be used to determine if it will sink or float in water. A substance with a specific gravity less than 1 will float, while a substance with a specific gravity greater than 1 will sink.
- For example, the specific gravity of gold is 19.3, which means that gold is 19.3 times denser than water. Therefore, gold will sink in water.
- Some factors that affect the specific gravity of a substance are temperature, pressure, and purity. The specific gravity of a substance will change with the change in these factors.
