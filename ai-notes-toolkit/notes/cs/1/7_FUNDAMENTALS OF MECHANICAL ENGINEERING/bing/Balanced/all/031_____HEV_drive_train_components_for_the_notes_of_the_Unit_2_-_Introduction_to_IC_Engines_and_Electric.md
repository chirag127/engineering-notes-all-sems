# HEV drive train components

A hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) is a type of hybrid vehicle that combines a conventional internal combustion engine (ICE) system with an electric propulsion system (hybrid vehicle drivetrain). The key components in an HEV drive train are :

- **Internal combustion engine**: The primary power source of an HEV is a conventional engine. It can run on gasoline, diesel, or other fuels. The engine provides mechanical power to the wheels or to a generator that produces electrical power.
- **Electric motor**: The secondary power source of an HEV is the electric motor. It converts electrical energy from the battery pack or the generator into mechanical power that drives the wheels. The electric motor can also act as a generator during braking or coasting, recovering kinetic energy and storing it in the battery pack.
- **Battery pack**: A battery pack powers the electric motor. It stores electrical energy that can be used to supplement or replace the engine power. The battery pack can be recharged by the engine, the electric motor, or an external source (in plug-in hybrid vehicles).
- **Generator**: It is an essential component found in the series hybrid vehicle. It converts mechanical power from the engine into electrical power that can be used by the electric motor or stored in the battery pack. The generator allows the engine to operate at its optimal efficiency and emissions level, regardless of the vehicle speed or load.
- **Transmission**: Typically, hybrid vehicles use conventional transmissions similar to petrol or diesel cars. The transmission transfers the power from the engine and/or the electric motor to the wheels. Depending on the type of hybrid vehicle, the transmission can be a manual, automatic, continuously variable, or power-split device.
- **Control board**: It is the brain of the hybrid vehicle. It monitors and regulates the operation of the drive train components. It determines the optimal power split between the engine and the electric motor, based on the driver's input and the vehicle's condition. It also controls the charging and discharging of the battery pack, the regenerative braking, and the start-stop function of the engine.
