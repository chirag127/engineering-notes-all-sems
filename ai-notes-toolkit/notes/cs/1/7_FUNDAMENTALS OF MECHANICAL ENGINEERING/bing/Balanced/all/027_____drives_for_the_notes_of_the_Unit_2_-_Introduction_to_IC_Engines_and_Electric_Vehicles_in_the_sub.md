# Drives for IC Engines and Electric Vehicles

- A drive is a system that transfers power from a source to a load, such as a vehicle's wheels.
- There are two main types of drives for vehicles: internal combustion engine (ICE) drives and electric vehicle (EV) drives.
- ICE drives use fuel, such as gasoline or diesel, that combusts inside a combustion chamber with the help of an oxidizer, such as air, to produce mechanical power. The power is then transmitted to the wheels through a transmission, a clutch, a differential, and an axle. ICE drives are widely used in conventional vehicles, such as cars, trucks, buses, motorcycles, etc.
- EV drives use one or more electric motors that convert electrical energy from a battery or another source into mechanical power. The power is then transmitted to the wheels through a single or multiple gear ratio, a differential, and an axle. EV drives are used in electric vehicles, such as battery electric vehicles (BEVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs), etc.
- The main advantages of EV drives over ICE drives are:
  - Higher efficiency: EV drives can convert more than 90% of the electrical energy into mechanical power, while ICE drives can only convert about 20-30% of the fuel energy into mechanical power. EV drives also have less energy losses due to friction, heat, and noise.
  - Lower emissions: EV drives do not produce tailpipe emissions, such as carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides, particulate matter, etc., that contribute to air pollution and climate change. EV drives can also reduce greenhouse gas emissions if the electricity is generated from renewable sources, such as solar, wind, hydro, etc.
  - Better performance: EV drives can provide instant torque and acceleration, as well as smoother and quieter operation. EV drives can also enable regenerative braking, which means the electric motor can operate in reverse and recapture some of the kinetic energy of the vehicle and charge the battery, thereby extending the driving range and reducing brake wear.
- The main disadvantages of EV drives over ICE drives are:
  - Higher cost: EV drives are more expensive than ICE drives, mainly due to the high cost of batteries, which account for a large portion of the vehicle's price. EV drives also require more complex and sophisticated control systems and components, such as inverters, converters, chargers, etc.
  - Limited range: EV drives have a shorter driving range than ICE drives, as the battery capacity and performance are affected by various factors, such as temperature, state of charge, driving conditions, etc. EV drives also depend on the availability and accessibility of charging stations, which are still limited in some areas.
  - Longer charging time: EV drives take longer to recharge than ICE drives, which can be refueled in a few minutes. EV drives can take several hours to fully charge, depending on the battery size and the charging power. EV drives also face the risk of battery degradation and capacity loss over time and use.
