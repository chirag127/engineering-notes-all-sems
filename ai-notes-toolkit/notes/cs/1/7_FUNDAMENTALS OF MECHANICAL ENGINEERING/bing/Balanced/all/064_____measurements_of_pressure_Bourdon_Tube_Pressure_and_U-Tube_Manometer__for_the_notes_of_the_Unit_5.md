# Measurements of Pressure (Bourdon Tube Pressure and U-Tube Manometer)

- Pressure is the force per unit area exerted by a fluid on a surface.
- Pressure can be measured by various devices that use the principle of balancing the fluid force with a known force.
- Two common devices for measuring pressure are the Bourdon tube pressure gauge and the U-tube manometer.

## Bourdon Tube Pressure Gauge

- A Bourdon tube pressure gauge consists of a hollow metal tube that is bent into a circular or elliptical shape and closed at one end.
- The other end of the tube is connected to a pointer that moves over a calibrated scale.
- When the tube is exposed to a fluid pressure, it tends to straighten out due to the internal force of the fluid.
- The amount of straightening is proportional to the fluid pressure and is indicated by the pointer on the scale.
- The Bourdon tube pressure gauge can measure both gauge and absolute pressure, depending on the reference pressure at the closed end of the tube.
- The Bourdon tube pressure gauge can measure pressure up to 1600 bar and is compatible with almost any fluid medium.

## U-Tube Manometer

- A U-tube manometer consists of a U-shaped tube filled with a liquid of known density, such as mercury or water.
- One end of the tube is connected to the fluid whose pressure is to be measured, and the other end is open to the atmosphere or another reference pressure.
- The difference in height of the liquid columns in the two arms of the tube is proportional to the pressure difference between the two ends of the tube.
- The U-tube manometer can measure both gauge and differential pressure, depending on the reference pressure at the open end of the tube.
- The U-tube manometer can measure pressure up to a few bar and is suitable for low-pressure and low-viscosity fluids  .
