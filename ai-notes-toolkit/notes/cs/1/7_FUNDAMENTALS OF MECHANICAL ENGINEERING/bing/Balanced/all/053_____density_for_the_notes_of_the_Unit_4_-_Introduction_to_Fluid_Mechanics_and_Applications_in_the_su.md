# Density

- Density is the measurement of how tightly a material is packed together. It is defined as the mass per unit volume .
- The symbol for density is D or ρ (the Greek letter rho).
- The formula for density is d = M / V, where d is density, M is mass, and V is volume  .
- Density is commonly expressed in units of grams per cubic centimetre (g/cm3) or kilograms per cubic metre (kg/m3). For example, the density of water is 1 g/cm3 or 1000 kg/m3, and Earth’s density is 5.51 g/cm3 or 5510 kg/m3  .
- Density can also be expressed in other units, such as pounds per cubic foot (lb/ft3) or ounces per cubic inch (oz/in3), depending on the context and the system of units used.
- Density is an intensive property, which means it does not depend on the size or shape of the object. For example, a gold coin and a gold bar have the same density, even though they have different masses and volumes.
- Density can be used to identify substances or to compare the compactness of different materials. For example, iron, platinum, and lead are examples of dense materials, while air, helium, and styrofoam are examples of less dense materials .
- Density can also be affected by temperature and pressure. For example, water expands when it freezes, so ice has a lower density than liquid water. Similarly, air becomes denser when it is compressed, so air at sea level has a higher density than air at high altitudes.
