# Comfort Conditions

- Comfort conditions are the conditions of the indoor environment that are conducive to human health and well-being .
- Comfort conditions depend on various factors, such as temperature, humidity, air movement, air purity, and personal preferences .
- Comfort conditions are defined by the ASHRAE Standard 55, which specifies the acceptable ranges of these factors for different types of spaces and activities.
- Comfort conditions can be achieved by using refrigeration and air conditioning systems, which can control the temperature, humidity, and quality of the air supplied to the indoor spaces .
- Refrigeration and air conditioning systems can be classified into two types: comfort cooling and precision cooling .
- Comfort cooling systems are designed to provide comfortable conditions for human occupants, and have a lower and more variable heat load and a higher need for dehumidification .
- Precision cooling systems are designed to provide precise and stable conditions for sensitive equipment or processes, and have a higher and more constant heat load and a lower need for dehumidification .
- Comfort cooling systems can be further divided into two types: central systems and unitary systems.
- Central systems use a single or multiple refrigeration units to produce chilled water or air, which is then distributed to various zones or rooms through pipes or ducts.
- Unitary systems use individual refrigeration units to cool and condition the air for each zone or room, and do not require any distribution network.
- Some examples of comfort cooling systems are window air conditioners, split air conditioners, packaged air conditioners, fan coil units, and air handling units.
