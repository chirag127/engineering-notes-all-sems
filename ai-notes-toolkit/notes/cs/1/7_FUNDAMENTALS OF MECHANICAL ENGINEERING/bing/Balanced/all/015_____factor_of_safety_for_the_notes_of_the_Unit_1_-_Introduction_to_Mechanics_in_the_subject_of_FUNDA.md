# Factor of Safety

- Factor of safety (FoS) is a measure of how much stronger a system is than it needs to be for an intended load.
- It is the ratio of the ultimate strength (or structural capacity) of a material or a member to the actual working stress or the maximum permissible stress when in use  .
- It is a dimensionless quantity that indicates the margin of safety or the degree of reliability of a design.
- It is also known as safety factor (SF) and is sometimes expressed as a percentage.
- It is calculated using detailed analysis, testing, or empirical formulas based on the type of material, load, and application .
- It is influenced by various factors such as material properties, environmental conditions, manufacturing defects, human errors, and design uncertainties .
- It is usually chosen based on codes, standards, regulations, or engineering judgment depending on the level of risk and consequence of failure .
- It is typically greater than one, meaning that the system can withstand more load than the intended one without failure.
- It can also be less than one, meaning that the system is intentionally designed to fail at a certain load to protect other components or to trigger a safety mechanism.
- It can vary depending on the type of load (static, dynamic, impact, etc.), the type of failure (yielding, buckling, fatigue, etc.), and the type of material (brittle, ductile, composite, etc.) .
- It can be expressed as:

FoS = ultimate strength / working stress

or

FoS = allowable stress / actual stress

or

FoS = load capacity / applied load
