# Fluid Properties for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the Subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering

Fluid mechanics is the science concerned with the response of fluids to forces exerted upon them. Fluids are substances that can flow and deform under applied shear stress, such as liquids and gases. The study of fluid mechanics is important for many applications in engineering, such as hydraulic systems, aerodynamics, fluid power, and heat transfer.

The various properties of fluids that are relevant for fluid mechanics are  :

- **Density or mass density**: The density or mass density of a fluid is defined as the ratio of the mass of a fluid to the volume of the fluid. It is denoted by the symbol $\rho$ (rho) and has the units of kg/m$^3$. Density is a measure of how much mass is contained in a given volume of fluid. Density varies with temperature and pressure for most fluids, except for incompressible fluids, such as water, which have a constant density.

- **Specific weight or weight density**: The specific weight or weight density of a fluid is defined as the ratio of the weight of a fluid to the volume of the fluid. It is denoted by the symbol $\gamma$ (gamma) and has the units of N/m$^3$. Specific weight is a measure of how much weight is exerted by a given volume of fluid due to gravity. Specific weight is related to density by the equation $\gamma = \rho g$, where $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

- **Specific volume**: The specific volume of a fluid is defined as the ratio of the volume of a fluid to the mass of the fluid. It is denoted by the symbol $v$ and has the units of m$^3$/kg. Specific volume is a measure of how much space is occupied by a given mass of fluid. Specific volume is the inverse of density, i.e., $v = 1/\rho$.

- **Specific gravity**: The specific gravity of a fluid is defined as the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a standard fluid, usually water at 4°C or air at standard atmospheric conditions. It is denoted by the symbol $S$ or $SG$ and has no units. Specific gravity is a measure of how heavy a fluid is compared to a reference fluid. Specific gravity is dimensionless and independent of the system of units.

- **Viscosity**: The viscosity of a fluid is defined as the measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow or deformation under applied shear stress. It is denoted by the symbol $\mu$ (mu) and has the units of Pa$\cdot$s or N$\cdot$s/m$^2$. Viscosity is a property that characterizes the internal friction of a fluid. Viscosity depends on the molecular structure and interactions of the fluid, as well as the temperature and pressure. Viscosity affects the flow behavior and energy losses of fluids in motion.

These are the main properties of fluids that are used in fluid mechanics. Other properties, such as surface tension, vapor pressure, compressibility, and thermal conductivity, may also be relevant for some applications. The knowledge of these properties is essential to make the study of fluid mechanics comprehensive and to solve practical problems involving fluids.
