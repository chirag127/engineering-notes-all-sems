# EV batteries

- EV batteries are the devices that store electrical energy and power the electric motors in electric vehicles (EVs).
- EV batteries are usually rechargeable and can be charged from external sources or from regenerative braking, which converts kinetic energy into electricity when the vehicle slows down.
- The most common type of EV battery is lithium-ion, which uses lithium metal and other materials as electrodes and an electrolyte solution as the medium for ion transfer.
- Lithium-ion batteries have high energy density, meaning they can store a lot of energy in a small volume and weight. They also have low self-discharge rate, meaning they can retain their charge for a long time when not in use.
- Other types of EV batteries include lead-acid, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, zinc-air, and sodium nickel chloride, but they are less common due to their lower performance, higher cost, or environmental issues.
- EV batteries have several advantages over internal combustion engines (ICEs), such as lower emissions, higher efficiency, lower noise, and lower maintenance.
- However, EV batteries also have some challenges, such as high cost, limited range, long charging time, degradation over time, and recycling issues.
- EV batteries are constantly being improved by research and innovation, such as developing new materials, increasing capacity, reducing weight, enhancing safety, and lowering cost.
- Some of the emerging technologies for EV batteries include solid-state batteries, which use solid electrolytes instead of liquid ones, and wireless charging, which uses electromagnetic induction to transfer power without physical contact.
