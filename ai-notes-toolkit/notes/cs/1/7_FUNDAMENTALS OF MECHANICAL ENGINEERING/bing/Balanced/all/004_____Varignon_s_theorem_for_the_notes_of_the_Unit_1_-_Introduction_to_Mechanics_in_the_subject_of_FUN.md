# Varignon's Theorem

- Varignon's theorem is a useful tool in scalar moment calculations. It states that the moment of a resultant of two concurrent forces about any point is equal to the algebraic sum of the moments of its components about the same point .
- Varignon's theorem can be extended to any number of concurrent forces. It can also be applied to couples, since a couple can be considered as two equal and opposite forces.
- Varignon's theorem can be used to simplify the calculation of moments when the perpendicular distance is hard to determine or when the forces are not in the same plane.
- Varignon's theorem can be derived from the definition of moment and the principle of superposition. The proof is based on the fact that the moment of a force about a point is equal to the cross product of the position vector of the point of application of the force and the force vector .
- Varignon's theorem can also be interpreted as a statement in Euclidean geometry, that deals with the construction of a particular parallelogram, the Varignon parallelogram, from an arbitrary quadrilateral. It is named after Pierre Varignon, whose proof was published posthumously in 1731.
