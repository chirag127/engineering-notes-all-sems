# Introduction to Measurement

- Measurement is the process of **comparing** an unknown quantity with a known quantity .
- The result of a measurement is a **numeric value** with certain **units** .
- Measurement is **fundamental** to the sciences, engineering, construction, and other technical fields, as well as to almost all everyday activities.
- Measurement involves a **definition** of the quantity that is to be measured, and a **comparison** with some known quantity of the same kind.
- If the object or quantity to be measured is not accessible for direct comparison, it is **converted** or “transduced” into an analogous measurement signal.
- Measurement can be classified into different types, such as **direct** and **indirect**, **absolute** and **relative**, **static** and **dynamic**, etc.
- Measurement can also be categorized by the **physical quantity** that is being measured, such as **length**, **mass**, **capacity** (volume), **temperature**, **force**, **pressure**, **speed**, **electricity**, etc .
- Measurement requires the use of **instruments** or **devices** that can produce reliable and accurate results, such as **rulers**, **scales**, **thermometers**, **gauges**, **meters**, **sensors**, etc .
- Measurement also involves the use of **standards** or **units** that are universally accepted and consistent, such as the **International System of Units (SI)**, which is based on seven base units: **meter** (m) for length, **kilogram** (kg) for mass, **second** (s) for time, **ampere** (A) for electric current, **kelvin** (K) for temperature, **mole** (mol) for amount of substance, and **candela** (cd) for luminous intensity .
- Measurement also requires the use of **conversion** or **transformation** methods that can change the units or scales of a measurement, such as **multiplication** or **division** by a factor, **addition** or **subtraction** of an offset, **exponentiation** or **logarithm**, **trigonometry**, etc .
- Measurement also involves the **analysis** and **interpretation** of the measurement results, such as **error** or **uncertainty** estimation, **statistical** or **graphical** representation, **comparison** or **correlation** with other measurements, **inference** or **prediction** of the underlying phenomena, etc.
