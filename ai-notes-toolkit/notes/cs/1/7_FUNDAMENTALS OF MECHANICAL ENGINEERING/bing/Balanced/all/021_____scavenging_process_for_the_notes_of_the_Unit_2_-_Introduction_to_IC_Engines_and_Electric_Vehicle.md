# Scavenging Process

- Scavenging is the process of replacing the exhaust gas in a cylinder of an internal combustion engine with the fresh air/fuel mixture (or fresh air, in the case of direct-injection engines) for the next cycle.
- Scavenging is necessary for the engine’s proper fuel combustion and better power output .
- Scavenging occurs in ic engine during the overlapping of valves (Opens at the same time) in which burnt gases are released out of the cylinder with the entering of fresh charge inside the engine at the same time.
- There are three types of scavenging used in two stroke IC engines:
  - Uniflow scavenging: The fresh charge enters from one end of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the other end. This type of scavenging has the highest efficiency and the least short-circuiting of the fresh charge.
  - Cross-flow scavenging: The fresh charge enters from one side of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the opposite side. This type of scavenging has a lower efficiency and a higher short-circuiting of the fresh charge than uniflow scavenging .
  - Loop or reverse scavenging: The fresh charge enters from the bottom of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the top. This type of scavenging has the lowest efficiency and the highest short-circuiting of the fresh charge among the three types .
- The scavenging efficiency is the ratio of the mass of fresh charge trapped in the cylinder to the mass of fresh charge supplied to the cylinder. It is a measure of how well the scavenging process removes the exhaust gas and fills the cylinder with the fresh charge.
