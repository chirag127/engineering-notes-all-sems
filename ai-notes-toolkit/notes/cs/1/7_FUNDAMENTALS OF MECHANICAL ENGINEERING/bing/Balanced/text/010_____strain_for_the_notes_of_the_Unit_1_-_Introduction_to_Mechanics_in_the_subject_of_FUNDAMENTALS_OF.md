### Strain

Strain is a measure of the deformation or change in shape of a body due to an applied force. Strain is a dimensionless quantity and has no units. Strain can be positive or negative, depending on whether the body is elongated or compressed by the force.

There are three main types of strain:

- Longitudinal strain: This is the ratio of the change in length to the original length of a body when it is subjected to a tensile or compressive force along its length. Longitudinal strain is denoted by the Greek letter epsilon (ε). A positive longitudinal strain indicates that the body is stretched, while a negative longitudinal strain indicates that the body is compressed. For example, a rubber band that is pulled by a force has a positive longitudinal strain, while a spring that is pushed by a force has a negative longitudinal strain.

- Volumetric strain: This is the ratio of the change in volume to the original volume of a body when it is subjected to a uniform pressure in all directions. Volumetric strain is denoted by the Greek letter beta (β). A positive volumetric strain indicates that the body is expanded, while a negative volumetric strain indicates that the body is contracted. For example, a balloon that is inflated by air has a positive volumetric strain, while a balloon that is deflated by air has a negative volumetric strain.

- Shearing strain: This is the ratio of the change in angle to the original angle between two perpendicular lines in a body when it is subjected to a tangential force along its surface. Shearing strain is denoted by the Greek letter gamma (γ). A positive shearing strain indicates that the body is twisted, while a negative shearing strain indicates that the body is untwisted. For example, a book that is pushed by a force along its edge has a positive shearing strain, while a book that is pulled by a force along its edge has a negative shearing strain.
