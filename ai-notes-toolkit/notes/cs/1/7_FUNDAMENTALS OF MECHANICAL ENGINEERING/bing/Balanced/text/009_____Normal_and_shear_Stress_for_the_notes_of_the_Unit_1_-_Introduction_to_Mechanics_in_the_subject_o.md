### Normal and Shear Stress

- Normal stress is the stress that acts perpendicular to the cross-sectional area of a material. It arises from the normal force, the component of force vector that is perpendicular to the material cross section. Normal stress can be either tensile or compressive, depending on the direction of the normal force. Tensile stress causes elongation or stretching of the material, while compressive stress causes shortening or squeezing of the material .
- Shear stress is the stress that acts parallel to the cross-sectional area of a material. It arises from the shear force, the component of force vector that is parallel to the material cross section. Shear stress causes deformation or sliding of the material along the plane of the cross section  .
- Examples of normal stress are the stress in a rope that supports a weight, the stress in a column that supports a load, and the stress in a bone that supports the body weight. Examples of shear stress are the stress in a bolt that connects two plates, the stress in a beam that resists bending, and the stress in a fluid that flows through a pipe  .
- The units of normal and shear stress are the same as the units of pressure, which are pascals (Pa) in SI units and pounds per square inch (psi) in imperial units. The formula for normal stress is σ = F/A, where σ is the normal stress, F is the normal force, and A is the cross-sectional area. The formula for shear stress is τ = F/A, where τ is the shear stress, F is the shear force, and A is the cross-sectional area .
