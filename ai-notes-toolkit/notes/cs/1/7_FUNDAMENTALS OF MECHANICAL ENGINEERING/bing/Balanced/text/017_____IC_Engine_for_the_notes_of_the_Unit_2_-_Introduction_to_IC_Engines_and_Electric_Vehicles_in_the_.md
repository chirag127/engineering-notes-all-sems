### IC Engine

- An IC engine (internal combustion engine) is a type of heat engine that converts the heat energy released during combustion of fuel into mechanical work  .
- The combustion takes place within the engine cylinder, which is an integral part of the working fluid flow circuit.
- The working fluid is usually a mixture of air and fuel, which is compressed, ignited, and expanded in the cylinder, pushing the piston and producing power.
- The IC engine can use liquid or gaseous fuels, such as gasoline, diesel, natural gas, propane, biodiesel, or ethanol .
- The IC engine can be classified into two main types: spark ignition (SI) engine and compression ignition (CI) engine .
  - In an SI engine, the fuel-air mixture is ignited by a spark plug at the end of the compression stroke.
  - In a CI engine, the air is compressed to a high pressure and temperature, and the fuel is injected and ignited by the heat of compression.
- The IC engine can also be classified based on the number of strokes, cylinders, valves, arrangement, cooling, and application .
  - A stroke is the movement of the piston from one end of the cylinder to the other.
  - A four-stroke engine completes one cycle of intake, compression, power, and exhaust in four strokes of the piston, while a two-stroke engine completes one cycle in two strokes.
  - The number of cylinders determines the power output and smoothness of the engine. A single-cylinder engine has one cylinder, while a multi-cylinder engine has two or more cylinders arranged in different configurations, such as inline, V, W, or radial.
  - The number of valves controls the flow of the working fluid into and out of the cylinder. A two-valve engine has one intake and one exhaust valve per cylinder, while a four-valve engine has two intake and two exhaust valves per cylinder.
  - The arrangement of the cylinders and valves affects the performance and efficiency of the engine. A cross-flow engine has the intake and exhaust valves on opposite sides of the cylinder, while a reverse-flow engine has them on the same side. A hemispherical engine has the valves arranged in a dome-shaped combustion chamber, while a flat engine has them arranged in a flat or wedge-shaped combustion chamber.
  - The cooling of the engine prevents overheating and damage to the components. An air-cooled engine uses air as the cooling medium, while a water-cooled engine uses water or a mixture of water and antifreeze as the cooling medium.
  - The application of the engine determines the design and specifications of the engine. An automobile engine is used to power a vehicle, while a stationary engine is used to power a generator, pump, or other equipment. A marine engine is used to propel a boat or ship, while an aircraft engine is used to propel an airplane or helicopter. A rocket engine is used to propel a rocket or spacecraft, while a jet engine is used to propel a jet or turbine.
