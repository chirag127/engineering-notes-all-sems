### Working principles of hydraulic turbines (Pelton Wheel and Francis) and pumps (Centrifugal and Reciprocating) and their classifications and hydraulic lift

- A hydraulic turbine is a device that converts the kinetic and potential energy of water into mechanical work. A hydraulic pump is a device that converts the mechanical work of an engine or motor into hydraulic energy. 
- The working principle of a hydraulic turbine is based on Newton's law of motion, which states that a force is directly proportional to the change in momentum of a fluid. When water flows through a turbine, it changes its direction and velocity, thus imparting a force on the turbine blades. The turbine blades then rotate and transfer the torque to a shaft, which can be connected to a generator or a load. 
- The working principle of a hydraulic pump is based on the principle of displacement, which states that for each rotation of the pumping component, a fixed amount of fluid is displaced or delivered. The pumping component and the pump housing have a precise fit that allows for consistent delivery of fluid throughout each cycle. The fluid is then pressurized and sent to the hydraulic system.  
- Hydraulic turbines and pumps can be classified into different types based on various criteria, such as the direction of flow, the head and speed, the specific speed, the number of stages, the type of impeller, etc. Some of the common types of hydraulic turbines are Pelton wheel, Francis, Kaplan, and propeller turbines. Some of the common types of hydraulic pumps are centrifugal, reciprocating, gear, vane, and piston pumps.    
- A hydraulic lift is a device that uses the principle of Pascal's law, which states that the pressure in a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions. A hydraulic lift consists of two pistons of different diameters connected by a pipe filled with fluid. When a force is applied to the smaller piston, it creates a pressure that is transmitted to the larger piston, which then lifts a load. The ratio of the forces is equal to the ratio of the areas of the pistons.
