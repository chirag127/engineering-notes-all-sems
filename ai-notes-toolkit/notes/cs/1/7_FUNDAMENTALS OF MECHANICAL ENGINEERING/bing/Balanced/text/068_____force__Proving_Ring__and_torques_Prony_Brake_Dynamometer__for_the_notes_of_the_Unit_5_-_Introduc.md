### Force (Proving Ring) and Torques (Prony Brake Dynamometer) for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Introduction to Measurement and Mechatronics in the Subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering

- Force is a physical quantity that causes a change in the motion, shape, or deformation of an object. Force can be measured by various methods, such as using a spring balance, a load cell, or a proving ring.
- A proving ring is a device used to measure force. It consists of an elastic ring of known diameter with a measuring device located in the center of the ring. Proving rings come in a variety of sizes. They are made of a steel alloy.
- The principle of a proving ring is based on the Hooke's law, which states that the deformation of an elastic body is proportional to the applied force. When a force is applied to the ring, it causes a slight change in its diameter, which is detected by the measuring device. The measuring device can be a dial gauge, a strain gauge, or a load cell.
- The advantages of a proving ring are that it is simple, robust, accurate, and easy to calibrate. The disadvantages are that it is sensitive to temperature changes, it has a limited range of measurement, and it requires a correction factor for the effect of gravity.
- Torque is a physical quantity that measures the tendency of a force to rotate an object about an axis. Torque can be measured by various methods, such as using a torsion balance, a torque meter, or a prony brake dynamometer.
- A prony brake dynamometer is a device used to measure the torque and power of a rotating shaft. It consists of a frictional brake that clamps around the shaft and a lever arm that measures the braking force. The brake can be adjusted to vary the amount of resistance on the shaft .
- The principle of a prony brake dynamometer is based on the balance of torques. When the brake is applied to the shaft, it causes a frictional force that opposes the rotation of the shaft. The frictional force is measured by the lever arm, which is attached to a spring balance or a load cell. The torque is calculated by multiplying the frictional force by the radius of the shaft. The power is calculated by multiplying the torque by the angular speed of the shaft .
- The advantages of a prony brake dynamometer are that it is simple, inexpensive, and easy to use. The disadvantages are that it is inaccurate, it causes a lot of heat and wear on the shaft, and it wastes a lot of energy in the form of heat .
