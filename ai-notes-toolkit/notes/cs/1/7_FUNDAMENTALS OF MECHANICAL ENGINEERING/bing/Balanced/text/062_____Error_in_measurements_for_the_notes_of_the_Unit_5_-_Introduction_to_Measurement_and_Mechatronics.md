### Error in measurements

- Error in measurements is the difference between the true value and the measured value of a physical quantity.
- Error in measurements can affect the accuracy and precision of the results and introduce uncertainty in the analysis.
- Error in measurements can be classified into two major types: systematic errors and random errors.

#### Systematic errors

- Systematic errors are errors that are consistent and predictable in their direction and magnitude.
- Systematic errors are caused by faults or limitations in the measuring instrument, the method, or the observer.
- Systematic errors can be detected and corrected by calibrating the instrument, using a different method, or improving the observation technique.
- Systematic errors can also be estimated by comparing the results with a known standard or a reference value.
- Examples of systematic errors are zero error, parallax error, scale error, etc.

#### Random errors

- Random errors are errors that are unpredictable and vary in their direction and magnitude.
- Random errors are caused by factors that are beyond the control of the observer, such as environmental conditions, fluctuations in the instrument, or human errors.
- Random errors can be reduced by increasing the number of measurements, using statistical methods, or averaging the results.
- Random errors can also be quantified by calculating the mean, standard deviation, or confidence interval of the results.
- Examples of random errors are reading error, rounding error, human error, etc.
