### Varignon's theorem

- Varignon's theorem is a theorem of French mathematician Pierre Varignon (1654–1722), published in 1687 in his book Projet d'une nouvelle mécanique.
- The theorem states that the torque of a resultant of two concurrent forces about any point is equal to the algebraic sum of the torques of its components about the same point .
- Mathematically, the theorem can be expressed as:

$$\vec{M}_O = \vec{M}_{O_1} + \vec{M}_{O_2}$$

where $\vec{M}_O$ is the moment of the resultant force $\vec{R}$ about point O, $\vec{M}_{O_1}$ is the moment of force $\vec{F}_1$ about point O, and $\vec{M}_{O_2}$ is the moment of force $\vec{F}_2$ about point O.

- The theorem can be extended to any number of concurrent forces, as follows:

$$\vec{M}_O = \sum_{i=1}^n \vec{M}_{O_i}$$

where $\vec{M}_O$ is the moment of the resultant force $\vec{R}$ about point O, and $\vec{M}_{O_i}$ is the moment of force $\vec{F}_i$ about point O.

- The theorem can be proved by using the definition of moment as the cross product of position vector and force vector, and the properties of cross product and vector addition.

- Varignon's theorem is extremely useful in calculating the moments of complex force systems, such as the centroid and centre of mass of rigid bodies, and the centre of pressure of hydrostatic forces on submerged objects.
