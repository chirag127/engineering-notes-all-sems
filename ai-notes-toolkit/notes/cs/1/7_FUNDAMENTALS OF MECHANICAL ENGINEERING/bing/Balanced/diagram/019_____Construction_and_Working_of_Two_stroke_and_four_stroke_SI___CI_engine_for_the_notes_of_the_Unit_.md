# Construction and Working of Two Stroke and Four Stroke SI and CI Engine

## Introduction

An internal combustion (IC) engine is a type of engine that converts chemical energy of a fuel into mechanical energy by burning it inside a combustion chamber. The mechanical energy is then used to power various machines, such as vehicles, generators, pumps, etc. There are two main types of IC engines: spark ignition (SI) engines and compression ignition (CI) engines. SI engines use a spark plug to ignite a mixture of air and fuel, while CI engines use high pressure and temperature to ignite only air and inject fuel later. Both SI and CI engines can be classified into two stroke and four stroke engines, depending on the number of piston strokes required to complete one cycle of operation.

## Two Stroke Engine

A two stroke engine is an engine that completes one cycle of operation in two piston strokes, or one revolution of the crankshaft. It has only one power stroke for every revolution, which makes it more powerful and simpler than a four stroke engine. However, it also has more emissions, lower efficiency, and shorter lifespan than a four stroke engine. A two stroke engine can be either SI or CI, depending on the type of fuel and ignition system used.

### Construction of a Two Stroke Engine

A two stroke engine consists of a cylinder, a piston, a connecting rod, a crankshaft, a spark plug (for SI engine) or a fuel injector (for CI engine), and a few ports for air intake, fuel transfer, and exhaust. The cylinder is fitted with a cover at one end, where the spark plug or the fuel injector is placed, and a sealed crankcase at the other end, which acts as a pump for the air-fuel mixture or the air. The cylinder has three ports: an inlet port, a transfer port, and an exhaust port. The inlet port is connected to the crankcase and allows the air-fuel mixture or the air to enter the cylinder. The transfer port is also connected to the crankcase and allows the air-fuel mixture or the air to move from the crankcase to the cylinder. The exhaust port is connected to the exhaust system and allows the burnt gases to leave the cylinder.

### Working of a Two Stroke Engine

The working of a two stroke engine can be divided into two phases: suction-compression phase and power-exhaust phase. These phases are explained below:

- Suction-Compression Phase: In this phase, the piston moves from the top dead center (TDC) to the bottom dead center (BDC), creating a partial vacuum in the crankcase. This causes the inlet port to open and the air-fuel mixture or the air to enter the crankcase. At the same time, the piston compresses the air-fuel mixture or the air in the cylinder. When the piston reaches the BDC, the transfer port opens and the compressed air-fuel mixture or the air moves from the crankcase to the cylinder, pushing the burnt gases out of the exhaust port. The spark plug (for SI engine) or the fuel injector (for CI engine) then ignites the air-fuel mixture or the fuel, initiating the combustion process.

- Power-Exhaust Phase: In this phase, the piston moves from the BDC to the TDC, due to the high pressure and temperature generated by the combustion. This produces a power stroke, which rotates the crankshaft and delivers mechanical energy. When the piston reaches the TDC, the exhaust port opens and the burnt gases escape from the cylinder. The cycle then repeats.

## Four Stroke Engine

A four stroke engine is an engine that completes one cycle of operation in four piston strokes, or two revolutions of the crankshaft. It has only one power stroke for every two revolutions, which makes it less powerful and more complex than a two stroke engine. However, it also has less emissions, higher efficiency, and longer lifespan than a two stroke engine. A four stroke engine can be either SI or CI, depending on the type of fuel and ignition system used.

### Construction of a Four Stroke Engine

A four stroke engine consists of a cylinder, a piston, a connecting rod, a crankshaft, a spark plug (for SI engine) or a fuel injector (for CI engine), and a few valves for air intake, fuel injection, and exhaust. The cylinder is fitted with a cover at one end, where the spark plug or the fuel injector is placed, and a cylinder head at the other end, which houses the valves.
