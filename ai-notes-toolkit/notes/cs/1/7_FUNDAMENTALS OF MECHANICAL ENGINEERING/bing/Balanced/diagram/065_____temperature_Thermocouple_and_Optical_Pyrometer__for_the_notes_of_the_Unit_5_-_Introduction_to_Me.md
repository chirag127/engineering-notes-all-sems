### Temperature Measurement

- Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the molecules of a substance.
- Temperature measurement is important for many industrial, scientific and domestic applications.
- There are different methods and devices for measuring temperature, depending on the range, accuracy and environment of the measurement.
- Two common devices for temperature measurement are thermocouple and optical pyrometer.

### Thermocouple

- A thermocouple is a device that consists of two dissimilar metals joined at one end, forming a junction.
- When the junction is heated or cooled, a small electric voltage is generated across the two metals, due to the Seebeck effect.
- The voltage is proportional to the temperature difference between the junction and the other ends of the metals, which are kept at a constant reference temperature.
- By measuring the voltage and using a calibration table, the temperature of the junction can be determined.
- Thermocouples are widely used for measuring temperatures from -200°C to 2500°C, with high accuracy and fast response.
- Thermocouples are also simple, rugged and inexpensive devices that can withstand harsh environments.

### Optical Pyrometer

- An optical pyrometer is a device that measures the temperature of a hot object by comparing its brightness or color with a standard source of light, such as a filament lamp.
- The principle of optical pyrometry is based on the fact that all objects emit electromagnetic radiation according to their temperature, and the wavelength and intensity of the radiation depend on the temperature.
- An optical pyrometer has a lens, a filter, an eyepiece and an adjustable lamp, arranged in such a way that the image of the lamp filament is superimposed on the image of the hot object.
- The operator adjusts the current of the lamp until the brightness or color of the filament matches the brightness or color of the object, and then reads the temperature from a scale calibrated with the lamp current.
- Optical pyrometers are used for measuring temperatures above 700°C, where contact methods are impractical or inaccurate.
- Optical pyrometers are also non-contact, fast and easy to use devices, but they require a clear line of sight and a known emissivity of the object.
