### Scavenging Process

- Scavenging is the process of replacing the exhaust gas in a cylinder of an internal combustion engine with the fresh air/fuel mixture (or fresh air, in the case of direct-injection engines) for the next cycle.
- Scavenging is necessary for the engine's proper fuel combustion and better power output. It also reduces the engine's emissions and improves its efficiency.
- Scavenging occurs in the two-stroke engines during the overlapping of valves (opens at the same time) in which burnt gases are released out of the cylinder with the entering of fresh charge inside the engine at the same time.
- There are three types of scavenging used in two-stroke engines:
  - Uniflow scavenging: The fresh charge enters from one end of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the other end. This is the most efficient type of scavenging as it prevents the mixing of fresh and exhaust gases.
  - Cross-flow scavenging: The fresh charge enters from one side of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the opposite side. This type of scavenging is simple and cheap, but it causes some loss of fresh charge and incomplete removal of exhaust gas.
  - Loop or reverse scavenging: The fresh charge enters from the bottom of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the top. This type of scavenging creates a loop of fresh charge that pushes the exhaust gas out of the cylinder. This type of scavenging is better than cross-flow, but it still causes some mixing of fresh and exhaust gases.
- Scavenging can be improved by using a supercharger or a turbocharger, which increase the pressure of the fresh charge and force it into the cylinder. Scavenging can also be improved by using a scavenging pump, which creates a vacuum in the cylinder and sucks out the exhaust gas.
- Scavenging efficiency is the ratio of the mass of fresh charge trapped in the cylinder to the mass of fresh charge supplied to the cylinder. It is a measure of how well the scavenging process is performed. Scavenging efficiency can be affected by factors such as the valve timing, the valve size, the piston speed, the cylinder geometry, and the scavenging system design.
