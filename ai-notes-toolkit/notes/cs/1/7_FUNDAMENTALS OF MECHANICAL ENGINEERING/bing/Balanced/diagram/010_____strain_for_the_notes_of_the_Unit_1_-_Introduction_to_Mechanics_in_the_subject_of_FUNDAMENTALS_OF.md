### Strain

Strain is a measure of the amount of deformation that an object undergoes due to an applied force. Strain is a dimensionless quantity that is calculated by dividing the change in length or shape of an object by its original length or shape. Strain can be positive or negative, depending on whether the object is stretched or compressed.

There are three main types of strain:

- **Longitudinal strain**: This is the strain that occurs when an object is subjected to a normal force (perpendicular to its surface) that causes a change in its length. Longitudinal strain is defined as the ratio of the change in length to the original length of the object. For example, if a metal rod of length 10 cm is stretched by 0.1 cm, its longitudinal strain is 0.1/10 = 0.01.

- **Volumetric strain**: This is the strain that occurs when an object is subjected to a force that causes a change in its volume. Volumetric strain is defined as the ratio of the change in volume to the original volume of the object. For example, if a balloon of volume 1000 cm^3 is inflated by 100 cm^3, its volumetric strain is 100/1000 = 0.1.

- **Shearing strain**: This is the strain that occurs when an object is subjected to a tangential force (parallel to its surface) that causes a change in its shape. Shearing strain is defined as the ratio of the change in angle to the original angle of the object. For example, if a square of side 10 cm is distorted by a force that makes one of its angles 91 degrees, its shearing strain is (91-90)/90 = 0.011.

The formula for strain is:

Strain = (Change in dimension) / (Original dimension)

The symbol for strain is the Greek letter epsilon (ε).

The unit for strain is none, as it is a dimensionless quantity. However, sometimes strain is expressed as a percentage, by multiplying the strain value by 100. For example, a strain of 0.01 can be written as 1%.
