# Types of supports (Hinge, Roller) and loads (Point, UDL, UVL) for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Mechanics in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

## Supports

- Supports are devices that connect a structural member to the ground or to another part of the structure and provide resistance to forces and moments.
- Supports can be classified into four types: simple, roller, hinge, and fixed   .
- Simple support: A simple support allows rotation about any axis and translation (horizontal and vertical movement) in any direction. It does not provide any reaction force or moment. It is generally not used in structures.
- Roller support: A roller support allows rotation about any axis and translation in any direction parallel to the surface on which it rests. It provides a reaction force perpendicular to the surface, but no moment. It is commonly used for beams and bridges.
- Hinge support: A hinge support allows rotation about any axis, but prevents translation in any direction. It provides two reaction forces, one horizontal and one vertical, but no moment. It is commonly used for frames and trusses .
- Fixed support: A fixed support prevents rotation about any axis and translation in any direction. It provides two reaction forces, one horizontal and one vertical, and a reaction moment. It is commonly used for columns and walls .

## Loads

- Loads are forces or moments that act on a structural member and cause deformation, stress, or displacement.
- Loads can be classified into three types: point, uniformly distributed (UDL), and uniformly varying (UVL) loads.
- Point load: A point load is a load that acts at a single point on a structural member. It can be either concentrated or distributed. A concentrated load is a load that acts over a very small area, such as a weight or a force. A distributed load is a load that acts over a finite area, such as a pressure or a moment.
- UDL load: A UDL load is a load that acts uniformly over a length of a structural member. It can be either constant or varying. A constant UDL load is a load that has the same magnitude and direction throughout the length, such as a self-weight or a wind load. A varying UDL load is a load that changes in magnitude and/or direction along the length, such as a snow load or a temperature load.
- UVL load: A UVL load is a load that acts uniformly over a length of a structural member, but varies linearly from zero at one end to a maximum value at the other end. It can be either triangular or trapezoidal. A triangular UVL load is a load that has a zero value at one end and a maximum value at the other end, such as a beam supported by a cable. A trapezoidal UVL load is a load that has a nonzero value at both ends and a maximum value somewhere in between, such as a beam supported by a spring.
