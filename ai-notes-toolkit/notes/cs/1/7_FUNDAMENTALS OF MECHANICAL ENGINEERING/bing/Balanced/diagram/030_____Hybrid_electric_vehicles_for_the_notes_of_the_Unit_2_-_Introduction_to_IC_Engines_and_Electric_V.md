### Hybrid electric vehicles

- A hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) is a type of hybrid vehicle that combines a conventional internal combustion engine (ICE) system with an electric propulsion system (hybrid vehicle drivetrain)  .
- The presence of the electric powertrain is intended to achieve either better fuel economy than a conventional vehicle or better performance  .
- A hybrid electric vehicle cannot be plugged in to charge the battery. Instead, the battery is charged through regenerative braking and by the internal combustion engine .
- There are different types of hybrid electric vehicles, such as parallel hybrids, series hybrids, power-split hybrids, and plug-in hybrids (PHEVs)  .
- Parallel hybrids have both the ICE and the electric motor connected to the mechanical transmission, and can operate using either or both sources of power .
- Series hybrids have only the electric motor connected to the wheels, and use the ICE to generate electricity for the motor and the battery .
- Power-split hybrids have a power-split device that allows the ICE and the electric motor to provide power to the wheels directly or through a generator, depending on the driving conditions .
- Plug-in hybrids (PHEVs) are a combination of gasoline and electric vehicles, so they have a battery, an electric motor, a gasoline tank, and an internal combustion engine. PHEVs use both gasoline and electricity as fuel sources, and can be plugged in to an external power source to charge the battery .
- Hybrid electric vehicles have several advantages over conventional vehicles, such as lower emissions, higher fuel efficiency, reduced dependence on fossil fuels, and improved performance   .
- Hybrid electric vehicles also have some challenges, such as higher initial cost, complexity of the hybrid system, battery weight and size, and limited driving range on electric mode   .
- Some examples of hybrid electric vehicles are the Toyota Prius, the Honda Insight, the Ford Fusion Hybrid, the Kia Niro Plug-In Hybrid, and the Hyundai Ioniq Hybrid   .
