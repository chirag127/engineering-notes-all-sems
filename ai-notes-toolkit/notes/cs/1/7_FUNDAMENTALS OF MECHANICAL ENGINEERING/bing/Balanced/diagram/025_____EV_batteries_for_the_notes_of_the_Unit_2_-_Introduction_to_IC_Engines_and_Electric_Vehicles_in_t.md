### EV batteries

- EV batteries are the devices that store electrical energy and power the electric motors in electric vehicles (EVs).
- The most common type of EV battery is lithium-ion, which has high energy density, long cycle life, and fast charging capability.
- Other types of EV batteries include lead-acid, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, zinc-air, and sodium nickel chloride, which have different advantages and disadvantages in terms of cost, performance, safety, and environmental impact.
- EV batteries are composed of several components, such as cells, modules, packs, management systems, and thermal systems, which work together to provide optimal performance and safety.
- EV batteries are subject to degradation over time and use, which affects their capacity, power, and efficiency. Factors that influence battery degradation include temperature, state of charge, depth of discharge, charging rate, and calendar age.
- EV batteries can be recycled or reused after their end of life, which can reduce the environmental impact and the demand for raw materials. Recycling involves extracting valuable metals and materials from the battery, while reuse involves repurposing the battery for other applications, such as energy storage or backup power.
