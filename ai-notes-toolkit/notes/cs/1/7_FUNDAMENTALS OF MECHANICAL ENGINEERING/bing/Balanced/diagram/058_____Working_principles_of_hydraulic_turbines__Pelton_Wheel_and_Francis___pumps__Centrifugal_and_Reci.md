### Working principles of hydraulic turbines (Pelton Wheel and Francis) and pumps (Centrifugal and Reciprocating) and their classifications and hydraulic lift

- A hydraulic turbine is a device that converts the kinetic and potential energy of water into mechanical work. A hydraulic pump is a device that converts the mechanical work into hydraulic energy by increasing the pressure and flow of a fluid.
- The working principle of a hydraulic turbine is based on Newton's law of motion, which states that a force is directly proportional to the change in momentum of a fluid. When a fluid passes through a turbine, it changes its direction and velocity, thus imparting a force on the turbine blades. The turbine blades then rotate and transfer the force to a shaft, which can be connected to a generator or other machinery.
- The working principle of a hydraulic pump is based on the principle of displacement, which states that for each rotation of the pumping component, the same amount of fluid is displaced or delivered. The pumping component creates a vacuum at the pump inlet, which allows the atmospheric pressure to push the fluid from the reservoir into the pump. The pumping component then pushes the fluid out of the pump outlet, increasing its pressure and flow.
- There are different types of hydraulic turbines and pumps, depending on the design, configuration, and application. Some of the common types are:

  - Pelton wheel turbine: This is a type of impulse turbine, which means that the fluid enters the turbine with high velocity and low pressure, and leaves the turbine with low velocity and low pressure. The fluid is directed by a nozzle onto the buckets of a wheel, which are shaped like spoons. The fluid changes its direction by 180 degrees, thus transferring its momentum to the wheel. The Pelton wheel turbine is suitable for high head and low flow applications, such as mountainous regions.
  - Francis turbine: This is a type of reaction turbine, which means that the fluid enters and leaves the turbine with both pressure and velocity changes. The fluid is guided by a spiral casing and a set of guide vanes onto the runner, which consists of curved blades. The fluid exerts a pressure and a tangential force on the blades, causing the runner to rotate. The Francis turbine is suitable for medium head and medium flow applications, such as dams and reservoirs.
  - Centrifugal pump: This is a type of rotodynamic pump, which means that the fluid is accelerated by a rotating impeller. The impeller consists of a set of vanes that are attached to a hub. The fluid enters the pump axially and is pushed radially outward by the centrifugal force. The fluid then exits the pump through a volute or a diffuser, which converts the kinetic energy into pressure energy. The centrifugal pump is suitable for low head and high flow applications, such as irrigation and water supply.
  - Reciprocating pump: This is a type of positive displacement pump, which means that the fluid is moved by a piston or a plunger that reciprocates in a cylinder. The piston or the plunger creates a vacuum in the cylinder during the suction stroke, which allows the fluid to enter the pump through a valve. The piston or the plunger then pushes the fluid out of the pump through another valve during the discharge stroke. The reciprocating pump is suitable for high head and low flow applications, such as oil and gas production and injection.
- A hydraulic lift is a device that uses a hydraulic pump and a hydraulic cylinder to lift or lower a load. The hydraulic pump supplies pressurized fluid to the hydraulic cylinder, which extends or retracts a piston rod that is connected to the load. The hydraulic lift works on the principle of Pascal's law, which states that the pressure in a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions. This means that the force exerted by the fluid on the piston is proportional to the area of the piston and the pressure of the fluid. By using different sizes of pistons, the hydraulic lift can achieve a mechanical advantage, which is the ratio of the output force to the input force.
