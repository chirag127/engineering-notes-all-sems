### Fluids properties for the notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

- A fluid is a substance that deforms continuously (change in shape due to relative motion) under the action of shear force.
- Fluids can be classified as Newtonian or Non-Newtonian based on the viscosity which changes with respect to shear stress.
- Some of the physical properties of fluids are as follows:
  - Viscosity: It is the resistance offered by fluids to deformations, and which tends to impede fluidity. It is usually represented in Pa.s or N.s/m^2.
  - Density: It is the measure of the amount of matter in a given volume, it is usually represented in kg/m^3.
  - Specific Weight: It is the weight of a unit volume of fluid, it is usually represented in N/m^3.
  - Specific Gravity: It is the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a standard fluid (usually water or air), it is a dimensionless quantity.
  - Bulk Modulus: It is the measure of the compressibility of a fluid, it is the ratio of the change in pressure to the fractional change in volume, it is usually represented in Pa.
  - Kinematic Viscosity: It is the ratio of the dynamic viscosity to the density of a fluid, it is usually represented in m^2/s.
  - Surface Tension: It is the force per unit length acting along an imaginary line drawn on the free surface of a fluid, it is usually represented in N/m.
  - Capillarity: It is the phenomenon of rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube due to the combined effect of surface tension and adhesion, it is usually represented in m.
- Fluids can be modeled as ideal or real depending on the assumptions made about their properties and behavior.
  - Ideal fluids are incompressible, inviscid, irrotational, and have no internal friction or external forces acting on them.
  - Real fluids are compressible, viscous, rotational, and have internal friction and external forces acting on them.
- Fluids can be analyzed using different approaches such as continuum, molecular, or statistical.
  - Continuum approach assumes that fluids are continuous and homogeneous, and that the properties of a fluid element are independent of its size and shape.
  - Molecular approach considers the discrete nature of fluids and the interactions between the molecules or atoms that make up the fluid.
  - Statistical approach uses the concepts of probability and statistics to describe the behavior of fluids at the microscopic level.
