### Varignon's theorem

- Varignon's theorem is a principle of mechanics that relates the moments of several concurrent forces to the moment of their resultant force.
- It states that the moment of the resultant of two or more concurrent forces about any point is equal to the algebraic sum of the moments of the individual forces about the same point .
- Mathematically, if **F** is the resultant of **F**<sub>1</sub>, **F**<sub>2</sub>, ..., **F**<sub>n</sub>, and **r** is the position vector of any point, then

  **M** = **r** x **F** = **r** x (**F**<sub>1</sub> + **F**<sub>2</sub> + ... + **F**<sub>n</sub>) = **r** x **F**<sub>1</sub> + **r** x **F**<sub>2</sub> + ... + **r** x **F**<sub>n</sub> = **M**<sub>1</sub> + **M**<sub>2</sub> + ... + **M**<sub>n</sub>

  where **M** is the moment of the resultant force, and **M**<sub>i</sub> is the moment of the i-th force.

- Varignon's theorem can be used to simplify the calculation of moments of several forces by finding their resultant force first, or to find the resultant force by equating the moments of the individual forces and the resultant force.
- Varignon's theorem can also be applied to couples, since a couple is a system of two equal and opposite forces. The moment of a couple is independent of the point of application, and is equal to the product of the magnitude of one force and the perpendicular distance between the two forces.
- Varignon's theorem can also be extended to three dimensions, where the moments and the forces are vector quantities, and the cross product is used to find the moment of a force about a point.
- Varignon's theorem is named after Pierre Varignon, a French mathematician who published it in 1687 in his book *Projet d'une nouvelle mécanique*. However, the theorem was also known to earlier mathematicians, such as Simon Stevin and Isaac Newton.
- Varignon's theorem should not be confused with another theorem of the same name in Euclidean geometry, which deals with the construction of a parallelogram from an arbitrary quadrilateral.
