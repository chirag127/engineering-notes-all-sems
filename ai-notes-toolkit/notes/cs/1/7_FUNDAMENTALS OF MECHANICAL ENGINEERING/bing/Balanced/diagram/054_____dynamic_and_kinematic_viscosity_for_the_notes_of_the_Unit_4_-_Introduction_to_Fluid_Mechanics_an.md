### Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity

- Viscosity is a property of fluids that measures their resistance to flow. It is also known as the internal friction of fluids.
- There are two types of viscosity: dynamic and kinematic.
- Dynamic viscosity, which is also known as absolute viscosity, evaluates the internal resistance of a fluid to flow; in contrast, kinematic viscosity describes the ratio of dynamic viscosity to density.
- Dynamic viscosity is a measure of force, while kinematic viscosity is a measure of velocity.
- The SI unit of dynamic viscosity is Pascal-second (Pa-s), while the SI unit of kinematic viscosity is square meter per second (m2/s).
- The dynamic viscosity of liquids decreases as their temperature increases, while the dynamic viscosity of gases increases as their temperature rises.
- The kinematic viscosity of fluids depends on both their dynamic viscosity and their density. The density of fluids changes with temperature and pressure, so the kinematic viscosity also varies accordingly.
- The relationship between dynamic and kinematic viscosity is given by the formula:

  `ν = μ / ρ`

  where

  `ν` is the kinematic viscosity (m2/s)

  `μ` is the dynamic viscosity (Pa-s)

  `ρ` is the density (kg/m3)

- Examples of fluids with high dynamic viscosity are honey, molasses, and glycerin. Examples of fluids with low dynamic viscosity are water, air, and gasoline.
- Examples of fluids with high kinematic viscosity are honey, motor oil, and maple syrup. Examples of fluids with low kinematic viscosity are water, ethanol, and acetone.
