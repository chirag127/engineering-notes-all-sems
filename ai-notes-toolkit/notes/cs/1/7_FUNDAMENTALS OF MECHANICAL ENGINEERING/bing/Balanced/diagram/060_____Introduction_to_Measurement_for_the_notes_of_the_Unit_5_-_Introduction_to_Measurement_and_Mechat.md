### Introduction to Measurement

- Measurement is the process of **comparing** an unknown quantity with a known quantity of the same kind.
- The result of a measurement is a **numeric value** with certain **units**.
- Measurement is **fundamental** to the sciences, engineering, construction, and other technical fields, as well as to almost all everyday activities.
- Measurement involves a **definition** of the quantity that is to be measured, and a **comparison** with a known quantity or a **standard**.
- If the object or quantity to be measured is not accessible for direct comparison, it is converted or **transduced** into an analogous measurement **signal**.
- Some examples of quantities that can be measured are: length, mass, capacity (volume), and temperature.
- Some examples of units that can be used for measurement are: meter, kilogram, liter, and degree Celsius.
- Some examples of instruments that can be used for measurement are: ruler, balance, measuring cup, and thermometer.
- Measurement can be classified into different types, such as direct and indirect, absolute and relative, and scalar and vector.
- Measurement can also be affected by various factors, such as accuracy, precision, error, and uncertainty.
