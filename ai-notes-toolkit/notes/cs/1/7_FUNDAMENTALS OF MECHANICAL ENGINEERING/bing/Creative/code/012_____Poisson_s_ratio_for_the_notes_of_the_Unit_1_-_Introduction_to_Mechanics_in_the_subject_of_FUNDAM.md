### Poisson's ratio

- Poisson's ratio is a measure of the Poisson effect, the deformation (expansion or contraction) of a material in directions perpendicular to the specific direction of loading.
- Poisson's ratio is defined as the negative ratio of the transverse strain to the longitudinal strain of a material when it is stretched or compressed .
- Mathematically, Poisson's ratio can be represented as:

  μ = - εₗₐₜ/εₗₒₙ₉

  Where,

  μ = Poisson's ratio

  εₗₐₜ = Lateral strain

  εₗₒₙ₉ = Longitudinal strain

- Poisson's ratio is a dimensionless quantity and has no unit.
- Poisson's ratio can vary from -1 to 0.5 depending on the material and the loading condition.
- Poisson's ratio is positive for most materials, which means they contract in the transverse direction when stretched in the longitudinal direction, and vice versa.
- Poisson's ratio is zero for materials that do not change their volume when deformed, such as cork and rubber.
- Poisson's ratio is negative for materials that expand in the transverse direction when stretched in the longitudinal direction, such as some types of foams and auxetic materials.
- Poisson's ratio is important for understanding the mechanical behavior of materials and designing structures and devices that use them.
