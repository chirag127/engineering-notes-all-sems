### Types of supports (Hinge, Roller) and loads (Point, UDL, UVL)

- Supports are devices that connect a structural member to the ground or to another part of the structure and provide resistance to forces and moments.
- Supports can be classified into four main types: simple, roller, hinge, and fixed.
- Simple supports are free to rotate and translate in any direction. They can only resist a vertical force. They are also called knife-edge supports.
- Roller supports are free to rotate and translate along the surface upon which the roller rests. They can resist a vertical force but not a horizontal force or a moment. They are commonly used for bridges and beams.
- Hinge supports are capable of resisting forces acting in any direction of the plane, but not out of the plane. They can also resist a moment about the axis perpendicular to the plane. They allow rotation about any axis in the plane. They are also called pin supports.
- Fixed supports can resist vertical and horizontal forces as well as moments. They restrain both rotation and translation. They are also called built-in or cantilever supports.
- Loads are external forces or moments that act on a structural member and cause deformation, stress, or displacement.
- Loads can be classified into three main types: point, uniformly distributed (UDL), and uniformly varying (UVL).
- Point loads are concentrated forces or moments that act at a single point on a structural member. They are also called concentrated loads. They can cause high stress and deflection at the point of application.
- UDLs are loads that are distributed uniformly over a length or area of a structural member. They are also called uniform loads. They can cause a constant bending moment and a linearly varying shear force along the length of the member.
- UVLs are loads that are distributed non-uniformly over a length or area of a structural member. They are also called varying loads. They can cause a parabolic bending moment and a linearly varying shear force along the length of the member.
