### EV batteries

- EV batteries are the devices that store electrical energy and power the electric motors in electric vehicles (EVs).
- EV batteries are usually rechargeable and can be charged from external sources or from regenerative braking, which converts kinetic energy into electricity during deceleration.
- The most common type of EV battery is lithium-ion, which has high energy density, long cycle life, and low self-discharge rate. Lithium-ion batteries are composed of positive and negative electrodes, an electrolyte, and a separator.
- Other types of EV batteries include lead-acid, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, zinc-air, and sodium nickel chloride, but they have lower performance, higher cost, or environmental issues compared to lithium-ion batteries.
- EV batteries vary in size, shape, capacity, voltage, and chemistry depending on the vehicle design, performance, and range requirements. EV batteries are typically modular and consist of several cells connected in series and parallel to form a battery pack.
- EV batteries have several advantages over internal combustion engines, such as lower emissions, higher efficiency, lower maintenance, and lower noise. However, EV batteries also face some challenges, such as high cost, limited availability, safety risks, and recycling issues .
- EV batteries are expected to improve in the future with new technologies, such as solid-state batteries, which use solid electrolytes instead of liquid ones and offer higher energy density, faster charging, and better safety.
