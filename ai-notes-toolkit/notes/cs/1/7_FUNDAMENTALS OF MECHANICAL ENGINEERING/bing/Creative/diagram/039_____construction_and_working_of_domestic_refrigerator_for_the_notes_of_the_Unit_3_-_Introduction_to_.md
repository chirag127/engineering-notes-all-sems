### Construction and working of domestic refrigerator

A domestic refrigerator is a device that uses the vapor compression cycle to maintain a low temperature inside a cabinet where food items can be stored. The main components of a domestic refrigerator are:

- **Compressor**: A hermetically sealed compressor is located at the lower part of the refrigerator. It compresses the refrigerant gas (usually R-134a or R-600a) and increases its pressure and temperature. The compressor is driven by an electric motor and has a thermostat to control its operation.  
- **Condenser**: A fin and tube type condenser is located at the back of the refrigerator. It releases the heat from the hot refrigerant gas to the surrounding air and condenses it into a high-pressure liquid. The condenser is air-cooled and has a fan to enhance the heat transfer.  
- **Capillary tube**: A capillary tube is a thin and long tube that connects the condenser and the evaporator. It acts as an expansion device and reduces the pressure and temperature of the refrigerant liquid before it enters the evaporator. The capillary tube also controls the flow rate of the refrigerant.  
- **Evaporator**: A fin and tube type evaporator is located in the freezer box in the form of a coil. The refrigerant liquid evaporates in the evaporator by absorbing heat from the contents of the refrigerator in the cabinet. The evaporator is also connected to an accumulator that collects any liquid refrigerant that may not have evaporated completely.  
- **Drier and strainer**: A drier and strainer is a device that removes any moisture and impurities from the refrigerant before it enters the capillary tube. It prevents any blockage or corrosion in the refrigeration system. 

The working principle of a domestic refrigerator is as follows:

1. The compressor compresses the refrigerant gas and increases its pressure and temperature. The hot refrigerant gas flows through the condenser and dissipates its heat to the surrounding air. The refrigerant gas condenses into a high-pressure liquid. 
2. The high-pressure liquid refrigerant flows through the drier and strainer and then through the capillary tube. The capillary tube reduces the pressure and temperature of the refrigerant liquid. The refrigerant liquid enters the evaporator as a low-pressure liquid. 
3. The low-pressure liquid refrigerant evaporates in the evaporator by absorbing heat from the contents of the refrigerator in the cabinet. The refrigerant gas becomes cold and flows through the accumulator and then back to the compressor. The cycle repeats. 
4. The thermostat monitors the temperature inside the refrigerator and switches on or off the compressor as needed. The thermostat maintains a desired temperature range inside the refrigerator.
