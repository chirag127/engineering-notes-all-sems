### Equilibrium Equations and Support Reactions

- Equilibrium equations are the conditions that must be satisfied for a structure or a body to be in a state of static equilibrium, meaning that there is no net force or moment acting on it.
- The equilibrium equations for a two-dimensional (planar) structure or body are:

  - Sum of forces in the x-direction is zero: $\sum F_x = 0$
  - Sum of forces in the y-direction is zero: $\sum F_y = 0$
  - Sum of moments about any point is zero: $\sum M = 0$

- The equilibrium equations for a three-dimensional (spatial) structure or body are:

  - Sum of forces in the x-direction is zero: $\sum F_x = 0$
  - Sum of forces in the y-direction is zero: $\sum F_y = 0$
  - Sum of forces in the z-direction is zero: $\sum F_z = 0$
  - Sum of moments about the x-axis is zero: $\sum M_x = 0$
  - Sum of moments about the y-axis is zero: $\sum M_y = 0$
  - Sum of moments about the z-axis is zero: $\sum M_z = 0$

- Support reactions are the forces and moments that are exerted by the supports on a structure or a body to keep it in equilibrium.
- The types of support reactions depend on the types of supports, such as:

  - A pin or hinge support can provide two reaction forces, one in the x-direction and one in the y-direction, but no moment.
  - A roller or rocker support can provide one reaction force, either in the x-direction or the y-direction, but no moment.
  - A fixed or built-in support can provide two reaction forces, one in the x-direction and one in the y-direction, and one reaction moment.
  - A cable or rope support can provide one reaction force, along the direction of the cable or rope, but no moment.

- The number of support reactions for a structure or a body is equal to the number of restraints or constraints imposed by the supports.
- The number of support reactions for a planar structure or body cannot exceed three, otherwise the structure or body is statically indeterminate, meaning that the equilibrium equations are not sufficient to determine the support reactions.
- The number of support reactions for a spatial structure or body cannot exceed six, otherwise the structure or body is statically indeterminate, meaning that the equilibrium equations are not sufficient to determine the support reactions.
- To compute the support reactions for a structure or a body, the following steps are recommended:

  - Draw a free-body diagram of the structure or the body, showing all the applied forces and moments, and the support reactions as unknowns.
  - Choose a convenient coordinate system and label the directions and signs of the forces and moments.
  - Apply the equilibrium equations to the free-body diagram and solve for the unknown support reactions.
  - Check the results for consistency and accuracy.
