### Measurements of Pressure (Bourdon Tube Pressure and U-Tube Manometer)

- Pressure is the force exerted per unit area by a fluid on a surface. It can be measured in various units, such as Pascal (Pa), bar, psi, etc.
- Pressure measurement is important for many applications, such as fluid mechanics, hydraulics, pneumatics, meteorology, etc.
- There are different types of pressure, such as absolute pressure, gauge pressure, differential pressure, etc. Absolute pressure is the pressure relative to a perfect vacuum. Gauge pressure is the pressure relative to the atmospheric pressure. Differential pressure is the difference between two pressures measured at different points.
- There are different devices for measuring pressure, such as manometers, bourdon gauges, pressure transducers, etc. Manometers are devices that use a column of liquid to measure pressure. Bourdon gauges are devices that use a curved metal tube to measure pressure. Pressure transducers are devices that use electrical signals to measure pressure.
- U-Tube Manometer is a type of manometer that consists of a U-shaped tube filled with a liquid, such as mercury, water, oil, etc. One end of the tube is connected to the point where the pressure is to be measured, and the other end is open to the atmosphere or another reference pressure. The difference in the liquid levels in the two arms of the tube indicates the pressure difference between the two points. The pressure difference can be calculated by using the formula:

  $$p_d = \gamma h = \rho g h$$

  where $p_d$ is the pressure difference, $\gamma$ is the specific weight of the liquid, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $h$ is the height difference of the liquid levels.

- Bourdon Tube Pressure Gauge is a type of pressure gauge that consists of a curved metal tube, such as brass, bronze, steel, etc. The tube is closed at one end and connected to the point where the pressure is to be measured at the other end. The tube tends to straighten when the pressure inside it increases, and vice versa. The change in the shape of the tube is transmitted to a pointer or a dial by a linkage mechanism. The pointer or the dial indicates the pressure measured by the tube. The pressure can be calibrated by using a standard pressure source, such as a manometer.
