### Density

- Density is a physical property that measures how much mass is contained in a given volume of a substance.
- The symbol for density is ρ (the lower case Greek letter rho) or D, and the unit is kg/m^3^ (kilogram per cubic meter) or g/cm^3^ (gram per cubic centimeter).
- The formula for density is ρ = m / V, where m is mass and V is volume.
- Density can be used to identify substances, compare the compactness of different materials, and calculate other properties such as specific gravity, pressure, and buoyancy.
- Density is affected by temperature and pressure. When a substance is heated, its volume usually expands and its density decreases. When a substance is compressed, its volume decreases and its density increases.
- Density is an intensive property, which means it does not depend on the amount of substance. For example, a small piece of iron and a large piece of iron have the same density, because they have the same mass per unit volume.
