### IC Engine

An IC engine is a machine that transforms the energy released from the combustion of fuel into mechanical energy. The motion of the piston is then converted into rotary motion by a crankshaft, which can be used to drive a wide range of machines and vehicles.

#### Classification of IC Engines

IC engines can be classified according to different criteria, such as:

- According to the type of fuel used:

  - Gas engine: uses gaseous fuels such as natural gas, biogas, hydrogen, etc.
  - Petrol engine: uses petrol or gasoline as fuel
  - Light oil engine: uses kerosene or aviation fuel as fuel
  - Heavy oil engine: uses diesel or fuel oil as fuel

- According to the working cycle:

  - Otto cycle engine: uses spark ignition (SI) to initiate the combustion of the fuel-air mixture
  - Diesel cycle engine: uses compression ignition (CI) to ignite the fuel injected into the compressed air
  - Dual combustion engine: uses both SI and CI in different stages of the cycle

- According to the speed of the engine:

  - Low-speed engine: has a speed less than 300 RPM
  - Medium-speed engine: has a speed between 300 and 1000 RPM
  - High-speed engine: has a speed more than 1000 RPM

- According to the number of strokes:

  - Two-stroke engine: completes one cycle in two strokes of the piston
  - Four-stroke engine: completes one cycle in four strokes of the piston

- According to the number of cylinders:

  - Single-cylinder engine: has only one cylinder and piston
  - Multi-cylinder engine: has more than one cylinder and piston arranged in different configurations, such as inline, V, W, radial, etc.

- According to the valve arrangement:

  - Overhead valve engine: has the valves located in the cylinder head above the piston
  - Side valve engine: has the valves located in the cylinder block beside the piston
  - Sleeve valve engine: has the valves replaced by sliding sleeves that control the gas flow

- According to the cooling system:

  - Air-cooled engine: uses air as the cooling medium and has fins or ribs on the cylinder walls to increase the heat transfer
  - Water-cooled engine: uses water as the cooling medium and has a radiator, pump, and fan to circulate the water around the engine

- According to the application:

  - Stationary engine: used for power generation, pumping, etc.
  - Automotive engine: used for vehicles such as cars, trucks, buses, etc.
  - Marine engine: used for ships, boats, etc.
  - Locomotive engine: used for trains, trams, etc.
  - Aircraft engine: used for airplanes, helicopters, etc.

#### Components of IC Engines

The main components of an IC engine are:

- Cylinder: the cylindrical chamber in which the piston moves and the combustion takes place
- Piston: the cylindrical part that reciprocates inside the cylinder and transmits the force of the combustion to the crankshaft
- Connecting rod: the rod that connects the piston to the crankshaft
- Crankshaft: the shaft that converts the reciprocating motion of the piston into rotary motion
- Cylinder head: the part that covers the top of the cylinder and contains the valves, spark plug, injector, etc.
- Valves: the parts that control the intake and exhaust of the gases in the cylinder
- Spark plug: the device that produces a spark to ignite the fuel-air mixture in SI engines
- Injector: the device that injects the fuel into the cylinder in CI engines
- Camshaft: the shaft that operates the valves through cams and followers
- Flywheel: the wheel that stores the rotational energy of the crankshaft and smooths out the fluctuations in speed
- Carburetor: the device that mixes the fuel and air in the correct proportion for SI engines
- Fuel pump: the device that delivers the fuel from the tank to the carburetor or injector
- Air filter: the device that removes the dust and impurities from the air before entering the engine
