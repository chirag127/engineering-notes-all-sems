### Difference between two-stroke and four-stroke IC engines

- A two-stroke engine is an engine that completes a power cycle with two strokes of the piston during only one crankshaft revolution.
- A four-stroke engine is an engine that completes a power cycle with four strokes of the piston during two crankshaft revolutions.
- The main difference between two-stroke and four-stroke engines is in the method of filling the fresh air-fuel mixture in the cylinder and removing the burned gases from the cylinder.
- In a two-stroke engine, the intake and exhaust processes occur simultaneously during the crankshaft's one revolution, while in a four-stroke engine, they occur separately during the crankshaft's two revolutions.
- A two-stroke engine can produce power after one cycle, while a four-stroke engine can produce power after two cycles.
- A two-stroke engine has a simpler design, lighter weight, higher power-to-weight ratio, and lower cost than a four-stroke engine .
- A four-stroke engine has better fuel efficiency, lower emissions, more torque at lower rpm, and longer durability than a two-stroke engine .

### Difference between SI and CI engines

- SI stands for spark ignition, while CI stands for compression ignition.
- In an SI engine, the air-fuel mixture is ignited by a spark plug at the end of the compression stroke, while in a CI engine, the air-fuel mixture is ignited by the heat of compression at the end of the compression stroke.
- An SI engine can use gasoline, ethanol, or natural gas as fuel, while a CI engine can use diesel, biodiesel, or vegetable oil as fuel.
- An SI engine has a lower compression ratio, higher octane number, lower thermal efficiency, and higher specific fuel consumption than a CI engine.
- A CI engine has a higher compression ratio, lower octane number, higher thermal efficiency, and lower specific fuel consumption than an SI engine.
- An SI engine has a simpler fuel injection system, lower noise, lower maintenance, and lower emissions of nitrogen oxides (NOx) than a CI engine.
- A CI engine has a more complex fuel injection system, higher noise, higher maintenance, and higher emissions of particulate matter (PM) and sulfur oxides (SOx) than an SI engine.
