### Chargers for Electric Vehicles

- Electric vehicles (EVs) need chargers to replenish their batteries and extend their driving range.
- There are different types and levels of EV chargers, depending on the power source, the charging speed, and the connector type.
- The main types of EV chargers are:

  - Level 1 chargers: These are the most common and basic chargers that can plug into a standard 120-volt (120V) AC wall outlet. They usually come with the vehicle at purchase and can deliver about 1.4 kilowatts (kW) of power, providing 4 miles of driving range per hour of charging. Level 1 chargers are suitable for overnight or long-term charging at home or work, but they are not very efficient or convenient for fast or frequent charging.  
  - Level 2 chargers: These are the most popular and versatile chargers that can plug into a 240-volt (240V) AC outlet or a dedicated charging station. They can deliver up to 19.2 kW of power, providing 10 to 60 miles of driving range per hour of charging. Level 2 chargers are ideal for daily or regular charging at home, work, or public places, as they can fully charge an EV in 4 to 8 hours. Level 2 chargers are also compatible with most EV models and offer various smart features, such as Wi-Fi connectivity, scheduling, monitoring, and remote control.   
  - Level 3 chargers: These are the fastest and most advanced chargers that can use a high-voltage (480V) DC current or a three-phase AC current. They can deliver up to 350 kW of power, providing 180 to 240 miles of driving range per hour of charging. Level 3 chargers are also known as DC fast chargers or superchargers, and they can fully charge an EV in 15 to 30 minutes. Level 3 chargers are mainly designed for highway or long-distance travel, and they are usually located at commercial or public stations. However, Level 3 chargers are not compatible with all EV models and require special adapters or connectors.  

- The main types of EV charger connectors are:

  - SAE J1772: This is the standard connector for Level 1 and Level 2 chargers in North America. It is compatible with all EV models and can deliver up to 19.2 kW of power. 
  - Tesla: This is the proprietary connector for Tesla vehicles and chargers. It can deliver up to 250 kW of power and can be used with Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 chargers. Tesla vehicles can also use other connectors with adapters. 
  - CHAdeMO: This is the standard connector for Level 3 chargers in Japan and some other countries. It is compatible with some EV models, such as Nissan Leaf and Mitsubishi Outlander, and can deliver up to 62.5 kW of power. 
  - CCS (Combined Charging System): This is the standard connector for Level 3 chargers in Europe and some other countries. It is compatible with most EV models, such as BMW i3 and Chevrolet Bolt, and can deliver up to 350 kW of power. CCS connectors have two plugs: one for Level 1 and Level 2 charging (similar to SAE J1772) and one for Level 3 charging.
