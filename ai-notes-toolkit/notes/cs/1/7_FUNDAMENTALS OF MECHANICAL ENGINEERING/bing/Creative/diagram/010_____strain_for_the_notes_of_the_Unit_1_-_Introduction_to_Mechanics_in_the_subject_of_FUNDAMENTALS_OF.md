### Strain

Strain is a measure of the deformation of a body due to an applied force. It is defined as the ratio of the change in dimension of the body to the original dimension. Strain is a dimensionless quantity and has no units. Strain can be positive or negative, depending on whether the body is elongated or compressed by the force.

There are three main types of strain:

- Longitudinal strain: This is the strain produced when a body is subjected to a tensile or compressive force along its length. It is defined as the ratio of the change in length to the original length of the body. For example, when a rubber band is stretched, it undergoes longitudinal strain.

- Volumetric strain: This is the strain produced when a body is subjected to a uniform pressure in all directions. It is defined as the ratio of the change in volume to the original volume of the body. For example, when a balloon is inflated, it undergoes volumetric strain.

- Shearing strain: This is the strain produced when a body is subjected to a force that causes it to slide or twist along a plane. It is defined as the ratio of the displacement of the plane to the perpendicular distance between the planes. For example, when a book is tilted, it undergoes shearing strain.
