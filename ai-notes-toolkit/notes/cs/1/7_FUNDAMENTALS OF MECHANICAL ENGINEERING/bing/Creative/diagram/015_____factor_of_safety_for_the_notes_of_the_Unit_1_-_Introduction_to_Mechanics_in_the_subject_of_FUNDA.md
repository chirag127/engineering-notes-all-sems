### Factor of Safety

- The factor of safety (FoS) is a measure of how much stronger a system is than it needs to be for an intended load.
- It is the ratio of the ultimate strength (or structural capacity) of a system to the actual applied load (or working stress)  .
- It is also known as the safety factor (SF) and is used interchangeably with FoS .
- It is a dimensionless quantity that indicates the margin of safety or reliability of a design .
- It is calculated using detailed analysis, testing, or empirical data  .
- It is critical to structures and often termed as the backbone of the system .
- It depends on various factors such as the type of material, the loading condition, the environmental condition, the design standard, and the level of risk  .
- It is usually greater than one, meaning that the system is stronger than the minimum required .
- A higher FoS means a higher safety margin, but also a higher cost, weight, or complexity of the system .
- A lower FoS means a lower safety margin, but also a lower cost, weight, or complexity of the system .
- The optimal FoS depends on the trade-off between safety and efficiency of the system .
- Some common values of FoS for different applications are:

| Application | FoS |
| ----------- | --- |
| Aircraft structures | 1.5 |
| Bridges | 2.0 |
| Buildings | 2.0 |
| Cranes | 3.0 |
| Elevators | 10.0 |
| Pressure vessels | 4.0 |
| Shafts | 2.0 |
| Springs | 1.2 |
