### Dew point temperature
- Dew point temperature is the temperature at which the air becomes saturated with water vapor, assuming constant air pressure and water content  .
- When the air is cooled below the dew point temperature, the water vapor in the air will condense to form liquid water, known as dew .
- Dew point temperature depends on the amount of water vapor in the air. The more water vapor the air contains, the higher the dew point temperature is  .
- Dew point temperature is an indicator of the humidity of the air. The higher the dew point temperature, the more humid the air is  .
- Dew point temperature can be measured using a device called a hygrometer, which compares the temperature of a wet-bulb thermometer and a dry-bulb thermometer .
- Dew point temperature is important for refrigeration and air-conditioning systems, because it affects the cooling capacity, energy efficiency, and moisture control of the systems. 
- For example, if the dew point temperature of the air entering a refrigeration system is higher than the temperature of the evaporator coil, the water vapor in the air will condense on the coil, reducing the heat transfer and increasing the power consumption of the system.
- Similarly, if the dew point temperature of the air entering an air-conditioning system is higher than the temperature of the cooling coil, the water vapor in the air will condense on the coil, increasing the humidity and discomfort of the conditioned space.
- Therefore, it is desirable to maintain the dew point temperature of the air below the temperature of the cooling coil in both refrigeration and air-conditioning systems.
