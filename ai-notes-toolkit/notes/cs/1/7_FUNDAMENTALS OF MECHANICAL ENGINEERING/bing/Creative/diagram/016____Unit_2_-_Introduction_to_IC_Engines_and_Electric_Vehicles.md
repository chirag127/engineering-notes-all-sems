## Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles

- An **IC engine** or **internal combustion engine** is a type of engine that converts the chemical energy of a fuel into mechanical energy by burning it inside a combustion chamber.
- An **electric vehicle** or **EV** is a type of vehicle that uses an electric motor and a battery instead of an IC engine and a fuel tank to propel itself.
- IC engines and electric vehicles have different advantages and disadvantages in terms of performance, efficiency, emissions, cost, maintenance, and environmental impact.
- Some of the main differences between IC engines and electric vehicles are:

| IC engines | Electric vehicles |
| --- | --- |
| - Use gasoline or diesel as fuel | - Use electricity as fuel |
| - Produce harmful emissions such as carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides, and particulate matter | - Produce zero tailpipe emissions, but may have indirect emissions from electricity generation |
| - Have lower efficiency, as a lot of energy is lost as heat and noise | - Have higher efficiency, as electric motors have fewer moving parts and less friction |
| - Have higher maintenance costs, as they require regular oil changes, spark plugs, filters, etc. | - Have lower maintenance costs, as they have fewer components and fluids to replace |
| - Have longer range and shorter refueling time | - Have shorter range and longer charging time |
| - Have more variety and availability in the market | - Have less variety and availability in the market |
| - Have lower initial cost, but higher operating cost | - Have higher initial cost, but lower operating cost |

- There are also different types of electric vehicles, such as:

  - **Battery electric vehicles (BEVs)**, which run solely on electricity stored in batteries and have no IC engine or fuel tank.
  - **Hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs)**, which use both an IC engine and an electric motor, and can switch between them or use them together depending on the driving conditions.
  - **Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs)**, which are similar to HEVs, but have larger batteries that can be charged from an external source and can run longer on electricity alone.
  - **Fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs)**, which use hydrogen as fuel and generate electricity through a chemical reaction in a fuel cell, and have an electric motor and a battery as well.

- Electric vehicles are considered to be more environmentally friendly and energy efficient than IC engines, but they also face some challenges, such as:

  - The availability and accessibility of charging stations and electricity grids.
  - The cost and performance of batteries, especially in cold weather.
  - The environmental impact of battery production and disposal.
  - The consumer preferences and perceptions of electric vehicles .

- IC engines and electric vehicles are both evolving and improving over time, and may coexist or compete in the future transportation market.
