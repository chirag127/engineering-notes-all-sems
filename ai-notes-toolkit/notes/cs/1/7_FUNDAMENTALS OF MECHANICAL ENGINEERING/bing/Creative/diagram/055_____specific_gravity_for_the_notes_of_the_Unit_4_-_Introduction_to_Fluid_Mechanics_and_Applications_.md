### Specific Gravity

- Specific gravity is a dimensionless quantity that compares the density of a substance with the density of a reference substance, usually water.
- Specific gravity can be calculated using the following formula  :

    `Specific Gravity = Density of substance / Density of water`

- The unit of specific gravity is the same as the unit of density, which is kg/m³ or g/cm³. However, since specific gravity is a ratio, it has no units and is simply a number.
- Specific gravity can be used to determine the relative purity of a substance, the concentration of a solution, or the buoyancy of an object in a fluid.
- Specific gravity depends on the temperature and pressure of both the substance and the water, as these factors affect their densities. The standard temperature and pressure for water are 4 °C and 1 atm, respectively, where water has a density of 1000 kg/m³ or 1 g/cm³.
- Some examples of specific gravity of common substances are:

    - Gold: 19.3
    - Iron: 7.9
    - Ice: 0.92
    - Ethanol: 0.79
    - Air: 0.0012

- To calculate the specific gravity of a substance, we need to measure its mass and volume, and divide them by the mass and volume of water at the same temperature and pressure. For example, if we have a block of iron with a mass of 790 g and a volume of 100 cm³, and water at 4 °C and 1 atm, we can find the specific gravity of iron as follows:

    `Specific Gravity of iron = (Density of iron) / (Density of water)`

    `= (Mass of iron / Volume of iron) / (Mass of water / Volume of water)`

    `= (790 g / 100 cm³) / (1000 g / 1000 cm³)`

    `= 7.9 / 1`

    `= 7.9`
