### Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity

- Viscosity is a property of fluids that measures their resistance to flow. It is also known as the internal friction of fluids.
- There are two types of viscosity: dynamic (or absolute) viscosity and kinematic viscosity.
- Dynamic viscosity evaluates the internal resistance of a fluid to flow when a force is applied. It is given by the ratio of shear stress to shear rate in a fluid.
- Dynamic viscosity has the units of N s/m2 or Pa s in SI units, or poise (P) or centipoise (cP) in CGS units.
- Dynamic viscosity depends on the type and temperature of the fluid, and is independent of the fluid's density.
- Kinematic viscosity describes the ratio of dynamic viscosity to density. It is given by the ratio of the fluid's velocity to the applied force in a fluid.
- Kinematic viscosity has the units of m2/s or stokes (St) or centistokes (cSt) in SI units.
- Kinematic viscosity depends on both the type and temperature of the fluid, and the fluid's density.
- Two fluids with the same value of dynamic viscosity can have different values of kinematic viscosity based on their density and vice versa.
- Examples of fluids with high dynamic viscosity are honey, glycerin, and motor oil. Examples of fluids with low dynamic viscosity are water, air, and gasoline.
- Examples of fluids with high kinematic viscosity are honey, glycerin, and motor oil. Examples of fluids with low kinematic viscosity are water, air, and gasoline.
- Dynamic and kinematic viscosity are important parameters in fluid mechanics and applications, such as lubrication, flow measurement, heat transfer, and fluid power.
