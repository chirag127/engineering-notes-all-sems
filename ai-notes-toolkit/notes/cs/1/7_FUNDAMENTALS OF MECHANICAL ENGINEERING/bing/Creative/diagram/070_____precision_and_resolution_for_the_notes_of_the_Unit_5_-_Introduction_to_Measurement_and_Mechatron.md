### Precision and resolution

- Precision is the amount of information that is conveyed in terms of digits. It refers to the resolution or limit of the measurement.
- Precision is independent of accuracy. Accuracy denotes how close the measured value is to the true value of a given quantity.
- Precision is also an instrument's degree of repeatability - how reliably it can reproduce the same measurement over and over.
- Resolution is the smallest increment an instrument can detect and display - hundredths, thousandths, millionths.
- Resolution is the total weighing range of a scale divided by the readability of the display.
- Resolution is related to the sensitivity of the instrument - how well it can respond to small changes in the input quantity.
- Resolution is also affected by the number of significant figures or decimal places that the instrument can display.
- A high-resolution instrument has a large number of divisions on the scale, while a low-resolution instrument has a small number of divisions.
- A high-precision instrument has a small variation in the repeated measurements, while a low-precision instrument has a large variation.
- A high-accuracy instrument has a small deviation from the true value, while a low-accuracy instrument has a large deviation.

Some examples of precision and resolution are:

- A digital thermometer that can measure temperature up to 0.01°C has a high resolution, but it may not be accurate if it is not calibrated properly.
- A balance that can weigh up to 0.001 g has a high precision, but it may not be accurate if it is affected by external factors such as wind or vibration.
- A ruler that can measure length up to 0.1 mm has a low resolution, but it may be accurate if it is made of a standard material and has clear markings.
- A stopwatch that can measure time up to 0.01 s has a high precision, but it may not be accurate if it is affected by human error or battery power.
