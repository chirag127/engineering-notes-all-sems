### Basic definition of engine and components

An engine is a machine that converts one or more forms of energy into mechanical force and motion. It is the 'heart' of an automobile, as it provides the power that drives the road wheels and other ancillary components . 

The basic components of an engine are :

- **Cylinder block**: The main structure of the engine that contains the cylinders, where the fuel-air mixture is burned and the pistons move up and down.
- **Cylinder head**: The part that covers the top of the cylinder block and contains the valves, spark plugs, and fuel injectors.
- **Pistons**: The cylindrical parts that move up and down inside the cylinders and transmit the force of combustion to the connecting rods.
- **Valves**: The parts that control the intake and exhaust of the fuel-air mixture and the exhaust gases in the cylinders. They are operated by the camshaft and the valve springs.
- **Connecting rods**: The parts that connect the pistons to the crankshaft and transfer the reciprocating motion of the pistons to the rotary motion of the crankshaft.
- **Crankshaft**: The part that converts the reciprocating motion of the pistons into rotary motion and delivers it to the transmission and the road wheels.
- **Oil pan**: The part that holds the engine oil and lubricates the moving parts of the engine.
- **Oil pump**: The part that pumps the engine oil from the oil pan to the various parts of the engine that need lubrication.
- **Water pump**: The part that pumps the coolant from the radiator to the engine block and the cylinder head to keep the engine from overheating.
- **Intake manifold**: The part that distributes the air or the fuel-air mixture to the cylinders.
- **Exhaust manifold**: The part that collects the exhaust gases from the cylinders and sends them to the exhaust system.
- **Timing belt or chain**: The part that synchronizes the movement of the camshaft and the crankshaft and ensures the proper timing of the valve opening and closing.
- **Camshaft**: The part that operates the valves by means of the cam lobes and the rocker arms.
- **Rocker arms**: The parts that transfer the motion of the camshaft to the valves and open and close them.
- **Spark plugs**: The parts that ignite the fuel-air mixture in the cylinders by producing a spark at the right time.
- **Fuel injectors**: The parts that spray the fuel into the cylinders or the intake manifold in a precise amount and timing.
- **Engine mounts and cushions**: The parts that support the engine and isolate it from the chassis and the body of the vehicle to reduce noise and vibration.
- **Engine torque strut**: The part that prevents the engine from twisting or rocking due to the torque generated by the crankshaft.
- **Flywheel and flywheel ring gear**: The parts that smooth out the fluctuations in the crankshaft speed and provide a surface for the starter motor to engage and start the engine.
- **Harmonic balancer**: The part that reduces the torsional vibrations of the crankshaft and protects it from damage.
- **Timing cover**: The part that covers and protects the timing belt or chain and the gears or sprockets that drive it.
- **Valve cover**: The part that covers and seals the top of the cylinder head and prevents oil leakage.
- **Fuel pump**: The part that pumps the fuel from the fuel tank to the fuel injectors or the carburetor.
- **Carburetor**: The part that mixes the air and the fuel in the proper ratio and delivers it to the intake manifold. It is used in older engines that do not have fuel injection.
- **Distributor**: The part that distributes the high-voltage current from the ignition coil to the spark plugs in the correct firing order. It is used in older engines that do not have electronic ignition.
