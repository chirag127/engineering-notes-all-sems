### Calibration

- Calibration is the action or process of comparing an instrument or experimental readings with a standard of known accuracy and adjusting the instrument or readings accordingly .
- Calibration ensures that the instrument or readings are accurate, reliable, and consistent with the intended measurements.
- Calibration is usually performed by using a calibration standard, which is a device or material that has a known or assigned value of a physical quantity, such as length, mass, temperature, voltage, etc.
- The calibration standard is normally traceable to a national or international standard held by a metrology body, such as the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in the US or the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM) in France.
- The calibration process involves two steps:
  - In the first step, a relation is established between the quantity values with measurement uncertainties provided by the calibration standard and the corresponding indications with associated measurement uncertainties of the instrument or readings to be calibrated.
  - In the second step, this relation is used to obtain a measurement result from an indication of the instrument or readings.
- Calibration can be performed in different ways, depending on the type and purpose of the instrument or readings. Some common methods are:
  - Zero calibration: adjusting the instrument or readings to show zero when there is no input or stimulus.
  - Span calibration: adjusting the instrument or readings to show the full range of the expected input or stimulus.
  - Linearity calibration: checking and correcting the instrument or readings for any deviation from a straight line relationship between the input or stimulus and the output or response.
  - Accuracy calibration: comparing the instrument or readings with a more accurate calibration standard and correcting any error or bias.
  - Precision calibration: checking and improving the repeatability and consistency of the instrument or readings.
- Calibration is an essential part of quality assurance and control in measurement and mechatronics. It helps to reduce errors, improve performance, and ensure compliance with standards and regulations.
