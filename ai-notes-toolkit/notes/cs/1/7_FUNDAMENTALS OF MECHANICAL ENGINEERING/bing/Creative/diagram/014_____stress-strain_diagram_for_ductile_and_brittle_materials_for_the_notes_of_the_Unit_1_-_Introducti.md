# Stress-Strain Diagram for Ductile and Brittle Materials

- A stress-strain diagram is a graphical representation of the relationship between the applied stress and the resulting strain in a material under loading.
- The stress is plotted on the vertical axis and the strain is plotted on the horizontal axis.
- The shape and features of the stress-strain diagram can reveal important information about the mechanical properties and behavior of the material.
- Ductile materials are those that can undergo large plastic deformation before fracture, such as metals, polymers, and some ceramics.
- Brittle materials are those that fracture with little or no plastic deformation, such as glass, concrete, and some ceramics.

## Stress-Strain Diagram for Ductile Material

- A typical stress-strain diagram for a ductile material is shown below.

![Stress-strain diagram for ductile material](https://material-properties.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/stress-strain-curve-ductile-material.png)

- The diagram can be divided into several regions, each corresponding to a different stage of deformation.
- The initial linear region (OA) is called the elastic region, where the material behaves according to Hooke's law and the deformation is reversible. The slope of this region is the elastic modulus or Young's modulus of the material.
- The point A is called the proportional limit, where the stress-strain relationship ceases to be linear.
- The point B is called the elastic limit, where the material starts to exhibit permanent deformation or plasticity. The stress at this point is called the yield stress or yield strength of the material.
- The region between B and C is called the plastic region, where the material undergoes plastic deformation and the stress increases with increasing strain. The material becomes harder and stronger due to work hardening or strain hardening.
- The point C is called the ultimate stress or ultimate strength of the material, where the material reaches its maximum load-bearing capacity.
- The region between C and D is called the necking region, where the material undergoes localized deformation and the cross-sectional area decreases rapidly. The stress decreases with increasing strain due to the reduction in area.
- The point D is called the fracture point, where the material breaks or ruptures. The stress at this point is called the fracture stress or fracture strength of the material.
- The strain at this point is called the fracture strain or ductility of the material, which is a measure of how much the material can stretch before breaking.

## Stress-Strain Diagram for Brittle Material

- A typical stress-strain diagram for a brittle material is shown below.

![Stress-strain diagram for brittle material](https://physicsteacher.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/stress-strain-curve-brittle-material.png)

- The diagram is similar to the ductile material, except that the elastic region is much larger and the plastic region is very small or nonexistent.
- The material behaves elastically until it reaches the elastic limit, where it fractures suddenly and catastrophically.
- The stress at the fracture point is called the fracture stress or fracture strength of the material, which is usually much higher than the yield stress or yield strength of the material.
- The strain at the fracture point is called the fracture strain or brittleness of the material, which is a measure of how little the material can deform before breaking.
