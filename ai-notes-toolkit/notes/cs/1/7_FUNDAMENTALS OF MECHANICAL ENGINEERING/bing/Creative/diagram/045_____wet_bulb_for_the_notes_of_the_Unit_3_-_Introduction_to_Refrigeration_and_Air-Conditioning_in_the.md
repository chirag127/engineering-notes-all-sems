### Wet bulb temperature

- Wet bulb temperature is the temperature read by a thermometer covered in water-soaked cloth over which air is passed.
- Wet bulb temperature is the lowest temperature that can be reached by the evaporation of water into the air at a constant pressure.
- Wet bulb temperature is the temperature of a parcel of air cooled to saturation by the evaporation of water into it, with the latent heat supplied by the parcel.
- Wet bulb temperature is the temperature of an object that can be achieved through evaporative cooling, assuming good air flow and that the ambient air temperature remains the same.
- Wet bulb temperature is measured by wrapping a wet wick around the bulb of a thermometer and the measured temperature corresponds to the wet bulb temperature.
- Wet bulb temperature is an important parameter in psychrometrics, the study of the thermodynamic properties of moist air and its effect on materials and human comfort.
- Wet bulb temperature is used to calculate the relative humidity, dew point temperature, humid heat, enthalpy, and other properties of moist air.
- Wet bulb temperature is also used to determine the cooling capacity of evaporative coolers, cooling towers, and other devices that use water evaporation to lower the temperature of air.
- Wet bulb temperature is influenced by the dry bulb temperature, the amount of water vapor in the air, and the air pressure.
- Wet bulb temperature can be calculated from the dry bulb temperature and the relative humidity using empirical formulas or psychrometric charts.
- Wet bulb temperature can also be measured directly using a sling psychrometer, a device that consists of two thermometers, one dry and one wet, that are swung in the air to measure the air temperature and the wet bulb temperature.
- Wet bulb temperature is lower than the dry bulb temperature, unless the air is saturated, in which case they are equal.
- Wet bulb temperature is a measure of the adiabatic saturation temperature, the temperature that a parcel of air would have if it were cooled adiabatically (without heat transfer) by evaporation of water until it reached saturation.
- Wet bulb temperature is not the same as the dew point temperature, the temperature at which the air becomes saturated when cooled at constant pressure.
- Wet bulb temperature is always higher than the dew point temperature, unless the air is saturated, in which case they are equal.
- Wet bulb temperature is a function of the dry bulb temperature and the humidity ratio, the ratio of the mass of water vapor to the mass of dry air.
- Wet bulb temperature can be used to estimate the heat stress on humans and animals, as it reflects the ability of the body to cool itself by sweating.
- Wet bulb temperature can also be used to estimate the risk of heat stroke, as it indicates the limit of the body's thermoregulation.
- Wet bulb temperature can vary widely depending on the location, season, time of day, and weather conditions.
- Wet bulb temperature can be affected by factors such as solar radiation, wind speed, cloud cover, and altitude.
