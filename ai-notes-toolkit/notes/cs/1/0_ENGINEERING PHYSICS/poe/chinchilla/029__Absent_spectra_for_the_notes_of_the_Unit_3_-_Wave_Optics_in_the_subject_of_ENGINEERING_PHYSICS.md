### Absent Spectra for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the Subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Wave optics is a fascinating branch of physics that deals with the behavior of light as a wave. One of the most interesting phenomena in wave optics is the absent spectra, which occurs when certain wavelengths of light are missing in a spectrum.

Here are some key points to help you understand absent spectra in wave optics:

- An absent spectrum is a spectrum that is missing certain wavelengths of light. This can occur when light passes through a medium that absorbs certain wavelengths.
- Absent spectra are often observed in the spectra of stars. The gases in the stars' atmospheres absorb certain wavelengths of light, creating gaps in the spectrum.
- The study of absent spectra can provide important information about the composition of stars and other celestial bodies. By analyzing the gaps in the spectra, scientists can determine which elements are present in the atmosphere of the star.
- Absent spectra can also be observed in the spectra of gases and liquids. When light passes through a gas or liquid, certain wavelengths are absorbed by the molecules in the medium. This creates gaps in the spectrum that can be used to identify the molecules present in the medium.
- The study of absent spectra has many practical applications. For example, it can be used in the analysis of atmospheric pollutants and the detection of impurities in chemicals.
- Absent spectra can be observed using a spectroscope, which is an instrument that separates light into its component wavelengths. By analyzing the spectrum produced by the spectroscope, scientists can identify which wavelengths are missing and use this information to learn about the medium through which the light has passed.

In conclusion, absent spectra are an important phenomenon in wave optics that have many practical applications. By studying absent spectra, scientists can learn about the composition of stars, gases, and liquids, and use this information to solve a variety of problems in fields such as astronomy, chemistry, and environmental science.
