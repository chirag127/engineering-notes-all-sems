### Relation between Electric and Magnetic Fields of an Electromagnetic Wave

In the study of Electromagnetic Field Theory, understanding the relationship between electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave is crucial. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves, which means that the electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of wave propagation.
- The electric field and magnetic field are in phase with each other, which means that they reach their maximum and minimum values at the same time and location.
- The ratio of the electric field strength to the magnetic field strength, also known as the wave impedance or characteristic impedance, is a constant value in free space and is given by the expression Z = E/H, where E is the electric field strength and H is the magnetic field strength.
- The direction of the electric field is perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation, while the direction of the magnetic field is perpendicular to both the electric field and the direction of wave propagation.
- The electric and magnetic fields are related to each other through Maxwell's equations, specifically the Ampere-Maxwell law and the Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.
- In a vacuum, the speed of light is equal to the ratio of the electric and magnetic field strengths, or c = E/H, where c is the speed of light in a vacuum. This relationship is known as the electromagnetic wave equation.

Understanding the relationship between electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave is essential in many areas of engineering and physics, including telecommunications, optics, and electronics. It is important to remember that the two fields are interdependent and cannot exist without each other.
