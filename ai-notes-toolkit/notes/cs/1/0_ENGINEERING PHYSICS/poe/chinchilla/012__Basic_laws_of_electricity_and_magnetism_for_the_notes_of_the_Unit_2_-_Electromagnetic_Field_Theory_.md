### Basic Laws of Electricity and Magnetism

The study of electromagnetism is crucial in the field of engineering physics, as it forms the foundation for many modern technologies. Here are some of the basic laws of electricity and magnetism that you need to understand:

1. Coulomb's Law: This law states that the force between two charged particles is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
    - F = k(q₁q₂)/r², where F is the force, q₁ and q₂ are the charges, r is the distance between them, and k is the Coulomb constant.

2. Gauss's Law: This law states that the electric flux through any closed surface is proportional to the electric charge enclosed within the surface.
    - Φᴇ = q/ε₀, where Φᴇ is the electric flux, q is the charge enclosed within the surface, and ε₀ is the electric constant.

3. Ampere's Law: This law relates the magnetic field around a closed loop to the electric current passing through that loop.
    - ∮ B·ds = μ₀I, where B is the magnetic field, ds is the differential element of the path, I is the electric current passing through the loop, and μ₀ is the magnetic constant.

4. Faraday's Law: This law states that a changing magnetic field induces an electromotive force (EMF) in a conductor.
    - ε = -dΦᵐ/dt, where ε is the EMF, Φᵐ is the magnetic flux, and dt is the change in time.

5. Lenz's Law: This law states that the direction of the induced EMF is such that it opposes the change that produced it.
    - The induced current always flows in a direction that creates a magnetic field that opposes the change in the original magnetic field.

These laws form the basis for understanding the behavior of electric and magnetic fields, and their interactions with each other. By mastering these laws, you will be able to analyze and design complex electrical and electronic systems.
