### Particle in a one-Dimensional box

In quantum mechanics, a particle in a one-dimensional box is a fundamental example of a quantum system. In this system, a particle is confined to move within a one-dimensional region, or box, by potential barriers that are infinite in height at the edges of the box.

#### Properties of the system
- The particle has discrete energy levels, which are quantized and depend on the size of the box.
- The wave function of the particle must be zero at the edges of the box, which imposes boundary conditions on the wave function.
- The probability of finding the particle at a particular point in the box depends on the square of the wave function, which is known as the probability density.
- The system is a model for many physical systems, including atoms, molecules, and semiconductors.

- The ground state, or lowest energy level, is n=1, and its energy is non-zero.

#### Applications
- The one-dimensional box model is used to study the behavior of electrons in semiconductors, which are important for electronic devices.
- The model is also used to study the behavior of atoms and molecules in quantum chemistry.
- The one-dimensional box can be extended to two-dimensional and three-dimensional boxes, which are used to model more complex physical systems.
