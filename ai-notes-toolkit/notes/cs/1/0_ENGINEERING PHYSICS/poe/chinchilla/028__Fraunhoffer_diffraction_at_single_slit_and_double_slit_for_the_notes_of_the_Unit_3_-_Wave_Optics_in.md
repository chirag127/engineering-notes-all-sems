### Fraunhoffer Diffraction at Single Slit and Double Slit

In wave optics, Fraunhoffer diffraction refers to the phenomenon of light bending and spreading out when it passes through a narrow slit or a series of slits. This phenomenon can be observed in both single and double slit configurations. Here are some important points to understand about Fraunhoffer diffraction at single slit and double slit:

#### Fraunhoffer Diffraction at Single Slit

- When a plane wave of light passes through a narrow slit, diffraction occurs due to the bending of the wavefronts at the edges of the slit. This results in the spreading out of the wavefronts and the formation of a diffraction pattern on a screen placed behind the slit.
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern is given by the single-slit diffraction formula I = (I_0)(sinθ/θ)^2, where I_0 is the intensity of the incident light, θ is the angle of diffraction, and λ is the wavelength of the incident light.
- The diffraction pattern consists of a bright central maximum and a series of alternating bright and dark fringes on either side of the maximum. The distance between adjacent fringes is given by d sinθ = mλ, where d is the width of the slit, m is an integer representing the order of the fringe, and λ is the wavelength of the incident light.

#### Fraunhoffer Diffraction at Double Slit

- When a plane wave of light passes through two narrow slits separated by a distance d, interference occurs between the waves passing through each slit. This results in the formation of an interference pattern on a screen placed behind the double slit.
- The intensity of the interference pattern is given by the double-slit interference formula I = (I_0)(cosπd sinθ/λ)^2, where I_0 is the intensity of the incident light, θ is the angle of diffraction, d is the distance between the slits, and λ is the wavelength of the incident light.
- The interference pattern consists of a central maximum and a series of alternating bright and dark fringes on either side of the maximum. The distance between adjacent fringes is given by d sinθ = mλ, where d is the distance between the slits, m is an integer representing the order of the fringe, and λ is the wavelength of the incident light.

In summary, Fraunhoffer diffraction at single slit and double slit is a fundamental concept in wave optics that explains the bending and spreading out of light when it passes through narrow slits. Understanding this phenomenon is essential for engineers and physicists working in fields such as optics, telecommunications, and photonics.
