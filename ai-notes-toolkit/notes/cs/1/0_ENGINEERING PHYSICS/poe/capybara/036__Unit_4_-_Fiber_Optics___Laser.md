## Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser

In this unit, we will be discussing the basics of fiber optics and lasers. Here are some key points to remember:

### Fiber Optics

- Fiber optics is a technology that uses thin, flexible fibers made of glass or plastic to transmit light signals.
- These fibers are capable of transmitting data over long distances with very little signal loss or interference.
- The technology is widely used in telecommunications, internet services, and cable television.
- The advantages of fiber optics over traditional copper wires include faster data transfer rates, lower interference, and greater bandwidth.
- The basic components of a fiber optic system include a transmitter, fiber optic cable, and a receiver.
- Fiber optic cables are available in single-mode and multi-mode varieties, each with its own set of advantages and disadvantages.

### Lasers

- A laser is a device that emits a highly focused beam of light.
- The word "laser" is actually an acronym that stands for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.
- Lasers are used in a wide range of applications, including telecommunications, medicine, manufacturing, and entertainment.
- Lasers can be classified into different types based on their operating wavelengths, power levels, and applications.
- The basic components of a laser system include an active medium, an excitation mechanism, and an optical resonator.
- The advantages of lasers over traditional light sources include higher brightness, greater coherence, and a narrower beam width.
- Some common types of lasers include gas lasers, solid-state lasers, and semiconductor lasers.

In conclusion, fiber optics and lasers are two important technologies that have revolutionized the way we communicate, work, and live. Understanding the basics of these technologies is essential for anyone interested in pursuing a career in telecommunications, engineering, or related fields.
