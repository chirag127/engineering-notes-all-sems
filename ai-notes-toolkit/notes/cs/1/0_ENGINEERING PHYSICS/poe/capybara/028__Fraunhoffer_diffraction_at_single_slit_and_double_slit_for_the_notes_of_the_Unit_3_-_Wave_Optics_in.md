### Fraunhoffer Diffraction at Single Slit and Double Slit

Fraunhoffer diffraction is a phenomenon that occurs when a wave passes through an aperture or an obstacle. This phenomenon is studied in the field of wave optics and is an important topic in Engineering Physics. In this section, we will discuss Fraunhoffer diffraction at single slit and double slit.

#### Single Slit Diffraction

Single slit diffraction occurs when a wave passes through a narrow slit. The wavefronts bend around the edges of the slit and interfere with each other. This interference results in a pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen placed behind the slit. The pattern is known as the diffraction pattern.

The diffraction pattern depends on the width of the slit and the wavelength of the wave. The narrower the slit, the wider the diffraction pattern. Similarly, the longer the wavelength of the wave, the wider the diffraction pattern.

#### Double Slit Diffraction

Double slit diffraction occurs when a wave passes through two parallel slits. The wavefronts diffract through both the slits and interfere with each other. This interference results in a pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen placed behind the slits. The pattern is known as the interference pattern.

The interference pattern depends on the distance between the slits, the width of the slits, and the wavelength of the wave. The distance between the slits determines the spacing between the fringes. The width of the slits determines the intensity of the fringes. The longer the wavelength of the wave, the wider the interference pattern.

#### Conclusion

Fraunhoffer diffraction at single slit and double slit is an important topic in the field of wave optics. It is essential for the understanding of the behavior of waves when they pass through narrow apertures or obstacles. The diffraction and interference patterns that result from Fraunhoffer diffraction are used in various applications, such as in the construction of diffraction gratings and interferometers.
