# Interference in uniform and wedge shaped thin films

## Introduction

Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves of the same frequency and phase, resulting in a new wave pattern. Interference can be constructive or destructive, depending on the relative phase difference between the waves. Interference can occur in any type of wave, such as sound, light, water, etc.

One of the common examples of interference of light waves is the thin film interference. A thin film is a layer of material with a thickness that is comparable to the wavelength of light. When light is incident on a thin film, some of it is reflected from the top surface and some of it is transmitted into the film. The transmitted light can then be reflected from the bottom surface of the film and emerge out of the film. The reflected light from the top and bottom surfaces can interfere with each other, producing a pattern of bright and dark fringes.

The thin film interference depends on the following factors:

- The thickness of the film
- The wavelength of light
- The angle of incidence of light
- The refractive indices of the film and the surrounding media

## Interference in uniform thin films

A uniform thin film is a film with a constant thickness. A common example of a uniform thin film is a soap bubble or an oil slick on water. When white light is incident on a uniform thin film, different wavelengths of light undergo different phase shifts upon reflection and transmission, resulting in different interference conditions. Therefore, the film appears to have different colors depending on the viewing angle and the film thickness.

The condition for constructive interference in a uniform thin film is given by:

$$2t = (m + \frac{1}{2})\lambda_n$$

where $t$ is the film thickness, $m$ is an integer, $\lambda_n$ is the wavelength of light in the film, and $n$ is the refractive index of the film.

The condition for destructive interference in a uniform thin film is given by:

$$2t = m\lambda_n$$

where $t$, $m$, $\lambda_n$, and $n$ are the same as above.

## Interference in wedge shaped thin films

A wedge shaped thin film is a film with a varying thickness, such as a thin air gap between two glass plates. When monochromatic light is incident on a wedge shaped thin film, the film thickness changes along the direction of the wedge, resulting in different path differences and phase shifts for the reflected light from the top and bottom surfaces. Therefore, the interference pattern consists of straight parallel fringes that are perpendicular to the edge of the wedge.

The condition for constructive interference in a wedge shaped thin film is given by:

$$t = (m + \frac{1}{2})\frac{\lambda}{2}$$

where $t$ is the film thickness at the point of observation, $m$ is an integer, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light in air.

The condition for destructive interference in a wedge shaped thin film is given by:

$$t = m\frac{\lambda}{2}$$

where $t$, $m$, and $\lambda$ are the same as above.

The fringe width, or the distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes, is given by:

$$\beta = \frac{\lambda L}{2d}$$

where $L$ is the length of the wedge, and $d$ is the distance between the wedge and the screen.
