# Dispersive Power

- Dispersive power is a measure of the ability of a transparent medium to separate different colors of light by refraction  .
- Dispersive power is denoted by the symbol \uD835\uDF14 and is defined as the ratio of the difference in refractive indices of the medium for two specified widely differing wavelengths divided by the refractive index at some specified intermediate wavelength minus 1  .
- Mathematically, dispersive power is given by the formula:

$$\uD835\uDF14 = \frac{\uD835\uDCD1_\uD835\uDCD9 - \uD835\uDCD1_\uD835\uDCD6}{\uD835\uDCD1_\uD835\uDCD7 - 1}$$

where \uD835\uDCD1_\uD835\uDCD9, \uD835\uDCD1_\uD835\uDCD7, and \uD835\uDCD1_\uD835\uDCD6 are the refractive indices of the medium for the violet, yellow, and red wavelengths of light, respectively.

- Dispersive power indicates how much a medium can disperse light of different wavelengths. The higher the value of \uD835\uDF14, the more the medium can spread out light into its constituent colors. The lower the value of \uD835\uDF14, the less the medium can spread out light into its constituent colors.
- Dispersive power is an important property of optical devices such as prisms and lenses, as it affects the quality and clarity of the images formed by them. For example, a prism with a high dispersive power can be used to produce a clear and colorful spectrum of light, while a lens with a low dispersive power can reduce the chromatic aberration or color distortion in the images formed by it.
