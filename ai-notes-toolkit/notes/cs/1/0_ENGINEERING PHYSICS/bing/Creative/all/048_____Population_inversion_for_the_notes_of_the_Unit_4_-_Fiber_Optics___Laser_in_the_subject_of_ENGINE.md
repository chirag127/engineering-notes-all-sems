# Population inversion

- Population inversion is the redistribution of atomic energy levels that takes place in a system so that laser or maser action can occur.
- Normally, a system of atoms is in thermal equilibrium and there are always more atoms in low energy states than in higher ones. This is described by the Boltzmann distribution.
- To achieve population inversion, a system of atoms must be excited by an external source of energy, such as an electric current or a flash of light, so that more atoms are in higher energy states than in lower ones.
- Population inversion is a necessary condition for all lasers, regardless of their power or wavelength, because all lasers operate on the same basic principle of a chain reaction.
- Excited atoms emit photons when they decay to lower energy states. These photons can stimulate other excited atoms to emit photons of the same frequency, phase, and direction, creating a coherent beam of light. This is the laser action.
- Ground-state atoms absorb photons and reduce the intensity of the beam. This is the loss mechanism.
- To maintain population inversion and laser action, the system must be continuously pumped by the external source of energy.
