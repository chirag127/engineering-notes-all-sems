# Dispersion in Fiber Optics

- Dispersion in an optical fiber is the spreading of light pulses when the wave travels through an optical fiber from an end to another.
- It results in some variation of the actually transmitted wave through an optical fiber.
- Due to the dispersion of light waves, various adverse effects are noticed on the signal being transmitted, such as distortion, attenuation, and noise.
- Dispersion distorts signals and limits the data rate of digital signals sent over fiber optic cable.
- Dispersion can be classified into three types: material dispersion, waveguide dispersion, and intermodal dispersion .

## Material Dispersion
- Material dispersion is caused by a change in fiber optic material’s refractive index with different wavelength.
- The higher the index, the slower the light travels.
- Material dispersion is also known as chromatic dispersion, as it depends on the color or frequency of light.
- Material dispersion can be reduced by using graded-index fibers, which have a varying refractive index in the core.

## Waveguide Dispersion
- Waveguide dispersion is due to the distribution of light between core and cladding.
- It depends on the geometry and refractive index profile of the fiber.
- Waveguide dispersion is also influenced by the mode of propagation of light, such as single-mode or multimode.
- Waveguide dispersion can be minimized by optimizing the fiber design and choosing the appropriate wavelength of light.

## Intermodal Dispersion
- Intermodal dispersion is caused by the different propagation speeds of different modes of light in a multimode fiber.
- It results in a broadening of the pulse as different modes arrive at different times at the receiver.
- Intermodal dispersion is also called modal dispersion or differential mode delay.
- Intermodal dispersion can be eliminated by using single-mode fibers, which only allow one mode of light to propagate.
