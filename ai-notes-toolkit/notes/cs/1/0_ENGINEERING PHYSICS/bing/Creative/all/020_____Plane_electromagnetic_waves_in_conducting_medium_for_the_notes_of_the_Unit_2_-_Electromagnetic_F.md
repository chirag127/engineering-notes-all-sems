# Plane electromagnetic waves in conducting medium

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has constant amplitude and direction of electric and magnetic fields in any plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A conducting medium is a medium that has a finite conductivity (\uD835\uDF82), which means that it allows electric currents to flow through it when an electric field is applied.
- When a plane electromagnetic wave propagates in a conducting medium, it experiences attenuation and phase shift due to the presence of free charges and currents in the medium.
- The wave equation for a plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium is given by:

$$\nabla^2 \mathbf{E} - \mu \epsilon \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2} - \mu \sigma \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} = 0$$

where \uD835\uDF41 is the permeability, \uD835\uDF3A is the permittivity, and \uD835\uDF82 is the conductivity of the medium.

- A general solution for the electric field of a plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium is given by:

$$\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t) = \mathbf{E}_0 e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \omega t)}$$

where \uD835\uDF0E0 is the amplitude, \uD835\uDF11 is the wave vector, and \uD835\uDFB0 is the angular frequency of the wave.

- The wave vector \uD835\uDF11 and the angular frequency \uD835\uDFB0 are related by the complex wave number \uD835\uDF0E, which is given by:

$$k = \sqrt{\mu \epsilon \omega^2 + i \mu \sigma \omega}$$

- The complex wave number \uD835\uDF0E can be written as \uD835\uDF0E = \uD835\uDF0E' + i\uD835\uDF0E", where \uD835\uDF0E' and \uD835\uDF0E" are the real and imaginary parts of \uD835\uDF0E, respectively. The real part \uD835\uDF0E' represents the phase constant, and the imaginary part \uD835\uDF0E" represents the attenuation constant of the wave.
- The phase constant \uD835\uDF0E' determines the phase velocity and the wavelength of the wave, which are given by:

$$v_p = \frac{\omega}{k'}$$

$$\lambda = \frac{2 \pi}{k'}$$

- The attenuation constant \uD835\uDF0E" determines the intensity and the penetration depth of the wave, which are given by:

$$I = I_0 e^{-2 k" z}$$

$$\delta = \frac{1}{2 k"}$$

where \uD835\uDF1A0 is the initial intensity, and \uD835\uDFB4 is the distance traveled by the wave in the z-direction.

- The penetration depth \uD835\uDFB4 is the distance at which the intensity of the wave drops to 1/e (about 37%) of its initial value. It depends on the frequency and the conductivity of the medium. Higher frequency and higher conductivity lead to smaller penetration depth, which means that the wave is more attenuated in the medium.
- The magnetic field of a plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium can be obtained from the electric field by using the relation:

$$\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{\omega} \mathbf{k} \times \mathbf{E}$$

where \uD835\uDF1B is the magnetic field vector.
