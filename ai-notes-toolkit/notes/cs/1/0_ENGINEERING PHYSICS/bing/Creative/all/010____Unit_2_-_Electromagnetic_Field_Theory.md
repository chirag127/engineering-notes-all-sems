# Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory

- Electromagnetic field theory is the study of the interaction between electric and magnetic fields, and the sources that produce them, such as electric charges and currents .
- Electromagnetic fields are described by Maxwell's equations, which are a set of four differential equations that relate the electric and magnetic fields to their sources and to each other  .
- Maxwell's equations can be written in different forms, such as integral form, differential form, or vector form, depending on the context and the convenience .
- Maxwell's equations can be used to derive various laws and phenomena in electromagnetism, such as Coulomb's law, Gauss's law, Ampere's law, Faraday's law, Lenz's law, Biot-Savart law, Lorentz force, electromagnetic induction, electromagnetic waves, electromagnetic radiation, and electromagnetic spectrum  .
- Electromagnetic fields can be classified into two types: static and dynamic. Static fields are produced by stationary charges or steady currents, and do not vary with time. Dynamic fields are produced by moving or accelerating charges or changing currents, and vary with time .
- Electromagnetic fields can be measured by various instruments, such as voltmeters, ammeters, galvanometers, electrometers, magnetometers, compasses, and oscilloscopes .
- Electromagnetic fields can have various effects on matter, such as polarization, magnetization, induction, attraction, repulsion, heating, and radiation .
- Electromagnetic fields can also have various applications in science, engineering, and technology, such as electric motors, generators, transformers, solenoids, electromagnets, relays, switches, circuits, antennas, radio, television, radar, lasers, fiber optics, and medical imaging .
