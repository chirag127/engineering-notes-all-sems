# De-Broglie Concept of Matter Waves

- De-Broglie concept of matter waves is based on the idea that matter has a dual nature, i.e., it can exhibit both particle-like and wave-like properties depending on the situation .
- De-Broglie proposed that when matter is in motion, it is associated with a wave of a certain wavelength, called the de-Broglie wavelength, given by the formula  :

  $$\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$$

  where $\lambda$ is the de-Broglie wavelength, $h$ is the Planck's constant, and $p$ is the momentum of the matter.
- De-Broglie wavelength is inversely proportional to the momentum of the matter, which means that the faster the matter moves, the shorter its wavelength becomes. Conversely, the slower the matter moves, the longer its wavelength becomes  .
- De-Broglie wavelength is also inversely proportional to the mass of the matter, which means that the heavier the matter is, the shorter its wavelength becomes. Conversely, the lighter the matter is, the longer its wavelength becomes  .
- De-Broglie concept of matter waves can explain the quantization of the angular momentum of the electron in the hydrogen atom, which was postulated by Bohr's quantum theory. According to de-Broglie, the electron in the hydrogen atom can be regarded as a standing wave that can only have certain wavelengths that fit into the circumference of the orbit .
- De-Broglie concept of matter waves can also explain the phenomenon of electron diffraction, which is the bending of electron beams when they pass through a narrow slit or a crystal lattice. This shows that electrons can behave like waves and interfere with each other .
- De-Broglie concept of matter waves is a generalization of the wave-particle duality of light, which was established by Einstein's photon theory and Compton's formula. According to de-Broglie, not only light but also matter can have both wave-like and particle-like properties  .
