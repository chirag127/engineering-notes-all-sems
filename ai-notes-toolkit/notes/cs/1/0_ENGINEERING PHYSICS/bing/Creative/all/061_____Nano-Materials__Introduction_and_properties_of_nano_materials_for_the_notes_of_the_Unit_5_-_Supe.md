# Nano-Materials: Introduction and Properties of Nano Materials

- Nano-materials are materials that have at least one dimension of less than 100 nanometers (nm) in size  .
- The size of nano-materials affects their physical and chemical properties, such as conductivity, reactivity, optical, magnetic, and mechanical properties  .
- Nano-materials can be classified based on their dimensions, shapes, structures, compositions, and applications .
- Some examples of nano-materials are nano-filaments, nano-tubes, nano-wires, nano-cables, nano-thin films, dendrimers, quantum dots, nano-particles, nano-shells, nano-rods, nano-cones, etc .
- Nano-materials have many advantages and disadvantages, depending on their properties and applications .
  - Some advantages are:
    - Higher strength and durability
    - Enhanced electrical and thermal conductivity
    - Improved catalytic and sensor performance
    - Lower weight and cost
    - New functionalities and features
  - Some disadvantages are:
    - Potential toxicity and environmental impact
    - Difficulties in fabrication and characterization
    - Lack of standardization and regulation
    - Ethical and social issues
- Nano-materials have a wide range of applications in various fields, such as electronics, energy, medicine, biotechnology, cosmetics, textiles, aerospace, etc  .
