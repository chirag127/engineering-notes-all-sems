# Spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation

- Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state decays to a lower energy state and emits a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states .
- Spontaneous emission occurs without any external influence and is random in direction, phase, and polarization  .
- Spontaneous emission is the source of natural light, such as the light from stars, fireflies, and fluorescent materials.
- Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state is induced to emit a photon of the same energy, phase, polarization, and direction as an incident photon that has the same frequency as the energy difference between the two states  .
- Stimulated emission occurs when the incident photon interacts with the excited atom or molecule and causes it to drop to a lower energy state, releasing another photon that is identical to the incident photon  .
- Stimulated emission is the basis of laser operation, as it allows the amplification of light by creating a population inversion of atoms or molecules in the excited state .
