# Spectra with grating

- A **grating** is an optical device that consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits or grooves on a transparent or reflective surface. It is used to produce a **spectrum** of light by diffracting the incident light into different directions according to the wavelength.
- A **spectrum** is the distribution of light intensity as a function of wavelength or frequency. It can be **continuous**, **emission** or **absorption** depending on the source of light and the medium through which it passes.
- A **diffraction grating spectrometer** is an instrument that uses a grating to analyze the spectrum of a light source. It consists of a **collimator**, a **grating**, an **adjustable telescope** and a **scale**. The collimator produces a parallel beam of light from the source, the grating diffracts the light into different orders of spectra, the telescope focuses on a particular order of spectrum and the scale measures the angular position of the spectral lines.
- The **grating equation** relates the wavelength of light, the angle of incidence, the angle of diffraction and the grating spacing. It is given by:

$$d(\sin i + \sin r) = n\lambda$$

where $d$ is the grating spacing, $i$ is the angle of incidence, $r$ is the angle of diffraction, $n$ is the order of spectrum and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light.

- The **resolving power** of a grating is a measure of its ability to separate two closely spaced spectral lines. It is given by:

$$R = \frac{\lambda}{\Delta \lambda} = Nn$$

where $\lambda$ is the average wavelength of light, $\Delta \lambda$ is the minimum wavelength difference that can be resolved, $N$ is the total number of slits or grooves on the grating and $n$ is the order of spectrum.

- The **dispersion** of a grating is a measure of its ability to spread out different wavelengths of light. It is given by:

$$D = \frac{d\cos r}{n}$$

where $d$ is the grating spacing, $r$ is the angle of diffraction and $n$ is the order of spectrum.

- The **angular dispersion** of a grating is the rate of change of angle of diffraction with respect to wavelength. It is given by:

$$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = \frac{n}{d\cos r}$$

where $d$ is the grating spacing, $r$ is the angle of diffraction, $n$ is the order of spectrum and $\theta$ is the angular position of the spectral line.

- The **linear dispersion** of a grating is the rate of change of linear position of the spectral line on the screen or detector with respect to wavelength. It is given by:

$$\frac{dx}{d\lambda} = f\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = \frac{fn}{d\cos r}$$

where $f$ is the focal length of the telescope, $x$ is the linear position of the spectral line and the other symbols have the same meaning as before.

- The **free spectral range** of a grating is the wavelength range between two successive orders of spectrum. It is given by:

$$\Delta \lambda = \frac{\lambda}{n}$$

where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light and $n$ is the order of spectrum.

- The **blaze angle** of a grating is the angle of incidence that produces the maximum intensity in a particular order of spectrum. It depends on the shape and orientation of the slits or grooves on the grating surface. A **blazed grating** is a grating that has slits or grooves that are shaped like sawteeth or triangles to enhance the intensity of a desired order of spectrum.
