# Fibre Optics: Principle and construction of optical fiber

- Fibre optics is the science of transmitting data, voice, and images by the passage of light through thin, transparent fibers.
- Optical fibers are flexible, transparent fibers made by drawing glass or plastic to a diameter slightly thicker than that of a human hair.
- Optical fibers work on the principle of total internal reflection of light, which means that no part of the light signal gets refracted or transmitted to the second medium, but instead, all of the signal is retained inside the fiber .
- The condition for total internal reflection is that the light must travel from an optically denser medium to an optically rarer medium, and the angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle for the boundary.
- The construction of an optical fiber consists of three main parts: the core, the cladding, and the protective layer .
  - The core is the innermost part of the fiber, where the light is transmitted. It is made of glass or plastic with a high refractive index .
  - The cladding is the outer layer of the core, which surrounds and protects it. It is made of glass or plastic with a lower refractive index than the core, so that the light is reflected back into the core at the core-cladding interface .
  - The protective layer is the outermost layer of the fiber, which covers the cladding and provides mechanical strength and insulation. It is usually made of plastic or rubber .
- The diameter of the core and the cladding vary depending on the type and application of the optical fiber. The typical values are 8 μm for the core and 125 μm for the cladding.
- The types of optical fibers are mainly classified based on the number of modes or paths that the light can take inside the fiber. The two main types are single-mode fibers and multimode fibers.
  - Single-mode fibers have a very thin core, which allows only one mode or path of light to propagate. They have a higher bandwidth and lower attenuation than multimode fibers, but they are more expensive and require more precise alignment of the light source and the fiber.
  - Multimode fibers have a thicker core, which allows multiple modes or paths of light to propagate. They have a lower bandwidth and higher attenuation than single-mode fibers, but they are cheaper and easier to connect and operate.
- The uses of optical fibers are mainly in the fields of telecommunications, data transmission, medical imaging, sensing, and illumination.
  - In telecommunications, optical fibers are used to transmit voice and data signals over long distances and at high speeds, with minimal loss and interference.
  - In data transmission, optical fibers are used to link computers and devices within local area networks, as well as to connect different networks and servers.
  - In medical imaging, optical fibers are used to deliver light and images to and from the internal organs and tissues of the body, using devices such as endoscopes and lasers.
  - In sensing, optical fibers are used to measure physical parameters such as temperature, pressure, strain, and vibration, by detecting the changes in the light properties inside the fiber.
  - In illumination, optical fibers are used to provide flexible and decorative lighting for various applications such as architecture, art, and entertainment.
