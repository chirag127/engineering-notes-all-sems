# Laser applications

Laser is an acronym for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation. It is a device that produces a coherent beam of light with high intensity and low divergence. Lasers have many applications in various fields of science, engineering, medicine, industry, and entertainment. Some of the common laser applications are:

- **Transmission and processing of information**: Lasers are used to transmit and process information in optical communication systems, such as fiber optics, optical disks, barcode scanners, laser printers, and holography. Lasers can also be used to store and retrieve data in optical memory devices, such as compact discs (CDs) and digital versatile discs (DVDs).

- **Precise delivery of energy**: Lasers are used to deliver energy precisely and selectively to a target, such as in laser surgery, laser welding, laser cutting, laser drilling, laser marking, and laser engraving. Lasers can also be used to generate high temperatures and pressures, such as in laser fusion, laser heating, and laser propulsion. Lasers can also be used to induce chemical reactions, such as in laser spectroscopy, laser isotope separation, and laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy. 

- **Alignment, measurement, and imaging**: Lasers are used to align and measure objects and distances, such as in laser leveling, laser rangefinding, laser interferometry, and laser gyroscopes. Lasers are also used to image and scan objects and surfaces, such as in laser microscopy, laser scanning, laser profilometry, and laser Doppler velocimetry. Lasers can also be used to create and manipulate light patterns, such as in laser display, laser show, laser pointer, and laser harp. 

These are some of the major laser applications, but there are many more. Lasers are versatile and powerful tools that have revolutionized many fields of science and technology.
