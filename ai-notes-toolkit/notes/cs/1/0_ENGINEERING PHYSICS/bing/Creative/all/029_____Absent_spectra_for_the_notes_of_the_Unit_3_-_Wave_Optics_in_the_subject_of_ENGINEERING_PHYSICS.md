# Absent spectra

- Absent spectra are the missing orders of principal maxima in the diffraction pattern of a plane diffraction grating.
- They occur when the angle of diffraction θ is such that the minima due to diffraction component in the intensity distribution falls at the same positions of principal maxima due to interference component.
- The condition for absent spectra is given by:

$$
n\lambda = (N-1)d\sin\theta
$$

where n is the order of the absent spectrum, λ is the wavelength of light, N is the number of slits, d is the grating element, and θ is the angle of diffraction.

- The above equation implies that the absent spectra are more likely to occur for larger values of n, N, and λ.
- Absent spectra reduce the resolving power of a diffraction grating, which is the ability to distinguish between two closely spaced wavelengths.
- Absent spectra can be avoided by using a blazed grating, which is a grating with a sawtooth-shaped profile that reflects more light in a particular direction.
