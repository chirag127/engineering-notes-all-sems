# Attenuation

- Attenuation is the reduction in intensity of the light beam (or signal) with respect to distance travelled through a transmission medium .
- Attenuation is measured in decibels (dB) and is calculated by the formula: Attenuation (dB) = 10 x Log 10 (P in/P out) = 20xLog 10 (V in/V out) , where P is the power and V is the voltage of the signal.
- Attenuation limits the distance in which the signal can travel through optical fiber and affects the performance and reliability of the fiber optic network .
- Attenuation can be caused by intrinsic and extrinsic factors  .
  - Intrinsic factors are inherent to the fiber optic material and include:
    - Absorption: the conversion of optical power into heat due to the interaction of light with the molecular structure of the fiber  . Absorption depends on the wavelength of the light and the impurities in the fiber .
    - Scattering: the deflection of light from its original direction due to the microscopic variations in the refractive index of the fiber  . Scattering depends on the wavelength of the light and the size of the variations .
    - Dispersion: the spreading of light pulses over time due to the different velocities of different wavelengths or modes in the fiber  . Dispersion causes distortion and interference of the signal and limits the bandwidth of the fiber .
  - Extrinsic factors are related to the external conditions and include:
    - Bending: the loss of light due to the curvature of the fiber that causes some light rays to escape from the core  . Bending can be macroscopic (visible) or microscopic (invisible) and depends on the radius of curvature and the numerical aperture of the fiber .
    - Splicing: the joining of two fiber segments that causes some light to reflect or refract at the interface  . Splicing can be fusion (melting) or mechanical (adhesive) and depends on the alignment and quality of the splice .
    - Connector: the coupling of two fiber ends that causes some light to reflect or refract at the interface  . Connector can be physical contact (PC) or expanded beam (EB) and depends on the cleanliness and quality of the connector .
    - Environmental: the variation of temperature, humidity, pressure, vibration, etc. that affects the physical properties of the fiber and causes changes in the attenuation  . Environmental factors can be controlled by proper installation and protection of the fiber .
