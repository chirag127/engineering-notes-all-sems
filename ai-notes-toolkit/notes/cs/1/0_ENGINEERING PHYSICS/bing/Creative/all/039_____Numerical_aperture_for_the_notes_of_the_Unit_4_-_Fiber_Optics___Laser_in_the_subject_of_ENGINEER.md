# Numerical Aperture of Optical Fiber

- Numerical aperture (NA) is a measure of the ability of an optical fiber to collect or confine the incident light ray inside it .
- It is among the most basic property of optical fiber and shows the efficiency with which light is collected inside the fiber in order to get propagated.
- Numerical aperture is defined as the sine of half of the angle of the fiber's light acceptance cone .
- The acceptance cone is the cone of light that can enter the fiber and be transmitted along it .
- The acceptance angle is the maximum angle at which the light can be incident on the fiber and still be transmitted along it .
- The numerical aperture depends on the refractive indices of the core and the cladding of the fiber, as well as the wavelength of the light .
- The numerical aperture can be calculated by the following formula :

NA = sin θ_a = √(n_1^2 - n_2^2)

where θ_a is the acceptance angle, n_1 is the refractive index of the core, and n_2 is the refractive index of the cladding.

- The numerical aperture determines the following characteristics of the optical fiber :

  - The amount of light that can be coupled into the fiber
  - The bandwidth or information-carrying capacity of the fiber
  - The modal dispersion or pulse spreading in the fiber
  - The resolution or image quality in fiber-optic imaging systems

- The numerical aperture is usually specified for a particular wavelength of light, as it varies slightly with the wavelength .
- The numerical aperture is usually higher for multimode fibers than for single-mode fibers, as multimode fibers can support more modes or paths of light propagation .
- The numerical aperture is usually lower for graded-index fibers than for step-index fibers, as graded-index fibers have a gradual change in refractive index from the core to the cladding, which reduces the acceptance angle .
