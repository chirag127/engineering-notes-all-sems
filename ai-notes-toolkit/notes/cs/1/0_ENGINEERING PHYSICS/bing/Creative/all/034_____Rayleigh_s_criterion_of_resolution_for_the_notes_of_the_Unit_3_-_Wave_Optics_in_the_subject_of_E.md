# Rayleigh’s criterion of resolution

- Rayleigh’s criterion of resolution is a rule that defines the minimum angular separation between two point sources of light that can be resolved by an optical instrument such as a telescope or a microscope.
- The criterion is based on the diffraction patterns produced by the circular aperture of the instrument, which are called Airy disks. The Airy disk is the bright central spot surrounded by concentric rings of light and dark regions.
- The criterion states that two point sources are just resolvable when the center of the Airy disk of one source coincides with the first dark ring of the Airy disk of the other source. This is illustrated in the figure below.

![Rayleigh's criterion](https://sciencedemonstrations.fas.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/images/5/5a/Rayleighs_criterion.png)

- The minimum angular separation θ that satisfies the criterion is given by the formula:

θ = 1.22 λ / D

where λ is the wavelength of light and D is the diameter of the aperture.

- The smaller the value of θ, the better the resolution of the instrument. The resolution also depends on the wavelength of light, with shorter wavelengths giving better resolution. The aperture size also affects the resolution, with larger apertures giving better resolution.
