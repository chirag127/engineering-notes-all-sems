### Spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation

- Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state decays to a lower energy state and emits a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states .
- Spontaneous emission does not require any external agency or interaction with other photons .
- Spontaneous emission is random and the emitted photons are incoherent, meaning they have different frequencies, phases, polarizations, and directions of propagation .
- Spontaneous emission is the basis of fluorescence and phosphorescence, which are types of luminescence.
- Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state is induced to emit a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states by interacting with another photon of the same energy .
- Stimulated emission requires an external agency or interaction with other photons .
- Stimulated emission is not random and the emitted photons are coherent, meaning they have the same frequency, phase, polarization, and direction of propagation as the incident photons  .
- Stimulated emission is the basis of laser, which is an acronym for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation .
