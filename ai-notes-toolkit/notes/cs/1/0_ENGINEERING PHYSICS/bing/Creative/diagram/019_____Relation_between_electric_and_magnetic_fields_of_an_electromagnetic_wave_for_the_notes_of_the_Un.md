### Relation between electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave

- An electromagnetic wave is a disturbance that propagates through space and time, carrying both electric and magnetic fields .
- The electric field and the magnetic field of an electromagnetic wave are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation  .
- The electric field and the magnetic field of an electromagnetic wave have the same magnitude and the same phase.
- The electric field and the magnetic field of an electromagnetic wave satisfy Maxwell's equations, which describe how they are generated and how they interact with each other .
- Maxwell's equations show that a changing electric field produces a changing magnetic field, and vice versa . This is the mechanism by which electromagnetic waves are created and propagated.
- The speed of an electromagnetic wave in vacuum is given by c = 1/sqrt(epsilon_0 mu_0), where epsilon_0 is the permittivity of free space and mu_0 is the permeability of free space. This speed is independent of the frequency and wavelength of the wave.
- The frequency and wavelength of an electromagnetic wave are related by c = f lambda, where f is the frequency and lambda is the wavelength.
- The energy and momentum of an electromagnetic wave are carried by its electric and magnetic fields. The energy density of an electromagnetic wave is given by u = epsilon_0 E^2 + 1/mu_0 B^2, where E is the electric field and B is the magnetic field. The momentum density of an electromagnetic wave is given by p = epsilon_0 E x B, where x denotes the cross product.
- The intensity of an electromagnetic wave is the rate of energy transfer per unit area perpendicular to the direction of propagation. It is given by I = c u, where u is the energy density.
- The polarization of an electromagnetic wave is the orientation of its electric field vector in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation. It can be linear, circular, or elliptical.
