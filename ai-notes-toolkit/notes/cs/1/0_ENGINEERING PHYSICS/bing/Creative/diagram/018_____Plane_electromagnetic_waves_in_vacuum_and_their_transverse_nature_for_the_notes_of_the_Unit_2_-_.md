### Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of an electric field (E) and a magnetic field (B) that oscillate sinusoidally in time and space, and are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation .
- In vacuum, the plane electromagnetic waves travel at a constant speed of c = 3 x 10^8 m/s, which is the speed of light.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves means that the electric and magnetic fields are always in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation, and never along it  .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can be derived from Gauss's law for electric fields and Gauss's law for magnetic fields, which state that the net electric flux and the net magnetic flux through any closed surface are zero .
- This implies that the electric and magnetic fields have no divergence, or no sources or sinks, in a plane electromagnetic wave. Therefore, the electric and magnetic fields must be perpendicular to the direction of propagation, and also to the normal vector of any closed surface enclosing the wave .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can also be verified by using the right-hand rule, which relates the direction of the electric field, the magnetic field, and the propagation direction in a plane electromagnetic wave.
- According to the right-hand rule, if the thumb of the right hand points in the direction of propagation, then the fingers curl in the direction of the electric field, and the palm faces the direction of the magnetic field.
- This shows that the electric and magnetic fields are always perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation in a plane electromagnetic wave.
