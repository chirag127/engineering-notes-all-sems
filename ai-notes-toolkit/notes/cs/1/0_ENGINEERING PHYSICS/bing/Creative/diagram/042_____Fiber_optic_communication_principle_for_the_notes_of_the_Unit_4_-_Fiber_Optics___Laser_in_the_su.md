### Fiber optic communication principle

- Fiber optic communication is a method of transmitting information from one place to another by sending pulses of infrared light through an optical fiber.
- The light is a form of carrier wave that is modulated to carry information.
- Optical fiber is a thin, transparent fiber made of glass or plastic that can guide light along its length .
- The principle of fiber optic communication is based on total internal reflection, which occurs when light travels from a denser medium to a less dense medium at an angle greater than the critical angle .
- Total internal reflection ensures that the light is confined within the fiber and does not leak out, thus minimizing the loss of signal and increasing the transmission distance .
- The main components of a fiber optic communication system are :
  - A transmitter, which converts an electrical signal to an optical signal and injects it into the fiber.
  - A receiver, which detects the optical signal at the end of the fiber and converts it back to an electrical signal.
  - A fiber optic cable, which consists of one or more optical fibers bundled together and protected by a cladding and a jacket.
  - A connector, which joins two fibers or a fiber and a device, such as a transmitter or a receiver.
  - A coupler or a splitter, which divides or combines optical signals from multiple fibers.
  - A repeater or an amplifier, which boosts the optical signal to overcome attenuation or dispersion in the fiber.
- The advantages of fiber optic communication over other forms of communication are :
  - Higher bandwidth and data rate, which means more information can be transmitted in a given time.
  - Lower attenuation and interference, which means less signal degradation and noise.
  - Smaller size and weight, which means less space and power consumption.
  - Higher security and reliability, which means less risk of hacking or damage.
