### Meissner effect

- The Meissner effect is the expulsion of a magnetic field from the interior of a material that is in the process of becoming a superconductor .
- A superconductor is a material that loses its resistance to the flow of electrical currents when cooled below a certain temperature, called the critical temperature, usually close to absolute zero .
- The Meissner effect was discovered by the German physicists W. Meissner and R. Ochsenfeld in 1933 .
- The Meissner effect is a property of all superconductors, and it is a consequence of the perfect diamagnetism of superconductors .
- Diamagnetism is the tendency of a material to oppose an applied magnetic field, and thus be repelled by it .
- The Meissner effect can be explained by the London theory, which states that the superconducting electrons form a thin layer near the surface of the material that cancels out the magnetic field inside the material .

- The Meissner effect has applications in magnetic levitation, magnetic shielding, and superconducting magnets.
