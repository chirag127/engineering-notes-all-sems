### Persistent current in superconductors

- Persistent current is the current that flows indefinitely in a superconductor without any external power source.
- Persistent current is possible in superconductors because they have zero resistance and no energy dissipation  .
- Persistent current can be induced in a superconducting ring by placing it in a magnetic field above its critical temperature, then cooling it below its critical temperature, and then removing the magnetic field .
- Persistent current can also be induced in a superconducting ring by applying a voltage across a weak link or a junction in the ring, and then removing the voltage.
- Persistent current can create a magnetic field that opposes the applied field, according to Lenz's law . This is the basis of the Meissner effect, which is the expulsion of magnetic flux from a superconductor.
- Persistent current can be affected by various factors, such as the geometry of the superconductor, the strength of the applied field, the temperature, the type of superconductor, and the presence of impurities or defects .
- Persistent current can be measured by various methods, such as magnetization, torque, flux noise, or SQUID (superconducting quantum interference device).
- Persistent current has many applications, such as superconducting magnets, MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), particle accelerators, and quantum computing .
