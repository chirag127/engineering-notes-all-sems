## Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also called physical optics, is a branch of optics that studies the behavior of light when it exhibits wave characteristics, such as interference, diffraction, polarization, and scattering  .
- Wave optics is applicable when the size of the objects or apertures that light interacts with is comparable to or smaller than the wavelength of light, which is about 400-700 nm for visible light .
- Some of the important phenomena and concepts in wave optics are:

  - **Huygens' principle**: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets  .
  - **Interference**: The superposition of two or more coherent waves, resulting in constructive or destructive interference patterns  .
  - **Diffraction**: The bending or spreading of waves around obstacles or apertures, producing fringes of varying intensity  .
  - **Polarization**: The orientation of the electric field vector of a transverse wave, which can be linear, circular, or elliptical  .
  - **Scattering**: The redirection of light by particles or molecules in a medium, which can be elastic (Rayleigh or Mie scattering) or inelastic (Raman scattering)  .
  - **Thin film interference**: The interference of light reflected or transmitted by a thin layer of material, such as oil on water or soap bubbles  .
  - **Double slit experiment**: A demonstration of the wave-particle duality of light, where a beam of light passing through two narrow slits produces an interference pattern on a screen  .
  - **Single slit experiment**: A demonstration of the diffraction of light, where a beam of light passing through a single narrow slit produces a diffraction pattern on a screen  .
  - **Diffraction grating**: A device consisting of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits, which can be used to disperse light into its constituent wavelengths or colors  .
  - **Polarizer and analyzer**: Devices that can be used to produce, measure, or manipulate the polarization of light, such as polaroid filters or calcite crystals  .
  - **Brewster's angle**: The angle of incidence at which the reflected light is completely polarized perpendicular to the plane of incidence, when light is incident on a transparent medium  .
  - **Malus' law**: The law that states that the intensity of light transmitted by a polarizer is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle between the polarization of the incident light and the axis of the polarizer  .
  - **Optical activity**: The property of some materials to rotate the plane of polarization of linearly polarized light, such as sugar solutions or quartz crystals  .
  - **Birefringence**: The property of some materials to split a beam of unpolarized light into two beams with orthogonal polarizations, such as calcite crystals or stressed plastics  .
  - **Fresnel's equations**: The equations that describe the reflection and refraction of light at the interface of two media, taking into account the polarization of light  .
  - **Fresnel's biprism**: A device that consists of two thin prisms joined at their bases, which can be used to produce interference fringes by splitting a beam of light into two virtual sources  .
