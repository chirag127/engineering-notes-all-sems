### Temperature dependence of critical field

- The critical field is the maximum magnetic field that a superconductor can withstand before losing its superconductivity.
- The critical field depends on the temperature of the superconductor, and generally decreases as the temperature increases.
- The temperature dependence of the critical field can be described approximately by the following empirical formula:

$$
B_c(T) = B_c(0) \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2 \right]
$$

- where $B_c(0)$ is the critical field at absolute zero temperature, and $T_c$ is the critical temperature of the superconductor.
- The formula is valid for low-temperature superconductors, which have a single-band s-wave pairing mechanism and a weak coupling between electrons and phonons.
- For high-temperature superconductors, which have a more complex pairing mechanism and a strong coupling between electrons and phonons, the temperature dependence of the critical field is more anomalous and may show a negative curvature at any temperature .
- The critical field may also depend on other factors, such as the dimensionality, the purity, the anisotropy, and the multiband effects of the superconductor.
