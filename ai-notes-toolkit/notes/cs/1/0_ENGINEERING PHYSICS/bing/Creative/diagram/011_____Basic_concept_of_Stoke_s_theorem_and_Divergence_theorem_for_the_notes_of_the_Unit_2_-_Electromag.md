### Basic concept of Stoke’s theorem and Divergence theorem

- Stoke's theorem and Divergence theorem are two important theorems in vector calculus that relate different types of integrals over different types of regions.
- Stoke's theorem (also known as Kelvin-Stoke's theorem) states that the line integral of a vector field around a closed curve is equal to the surface integral of the curl of the vector field over any surface bounded by the curve.
- Mathematically, Stoke's theorem can be expressed as:

$$\oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

- Where $\mathbf{F}$ is a vector field, $\partial S$ is the boundary curve of the surface $S$, and $d\mathbf{r}$ and $d\mathbf{S}$ are the differential elements of the curve and the surface respectively.
- Stoke's theorem can be seen as a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus and Green's theorem to three dimensions.
- Stoke's theorem can be used to simplify the calculation of line integrals when the curl of the vector field is easier to evaluate than the vector field itself.
- Stoke's theorem can also be used to prove some important results in physics, such as the conservation of angular momentum, the Maxwell-Faraday equation, and the Helmholtz decomposition theorem.

$$\oiint_{\partial V} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) dV$$

- Where $\mathbf{F}$ is a vector field, $\partial V$ is the boundary surface of the volume $V$, and $d\mathbf{S}$ and $dV$ are the differential elements of the surface and the volume respectively.
