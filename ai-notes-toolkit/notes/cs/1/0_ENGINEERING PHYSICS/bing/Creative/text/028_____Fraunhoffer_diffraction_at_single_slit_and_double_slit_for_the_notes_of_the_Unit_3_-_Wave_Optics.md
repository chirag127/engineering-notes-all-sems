### Fraunhofer diffraction at single slit and double slit

- Fraunhofer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when plane waves are incident on a diffracting object, and the diffraction pattern is viewed at a sufficiently long distance from the object (in the far-field region), or at the focal plane of a converging lens.
- Fraunhofer diffraction can be used to analyze the shape and size of the diffracting object, such as a slit, a grating, or an aperture.
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit is the simplest case of diffraction, where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength `λ` is incident on a slit of width `d` that is of the order of `λ` .
- According to Huygens' principle, every point of the wavefront in the plane of the slit acts as a secondary source and sends secondary waves in all directions. These waves interfere constructively or destructively at different points on a screen placed at a large distance from the slit, forming a diffraction pattern .
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern at an angle `θ` from the center of the slit is given by the formula :

    `I(θ) = I0 (sin(πd sin(θ)/λ) / (πd sin(θ)/λ))^2`

    where `I0` is the intensity at the center of the slit, and `d sin(θ)` is the path difference between the waves from the two edges of the slit.
- The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright maximum, flanked by a series of alternate bright and dark fringes of decreasing intensity. The angular width of the central maximum is given by :

    `θ0 = 2λ/d`

    where `θ0` is the angle subtended by the first dark fringe on either side of the central maximum.
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a double slit is a more complex case of diffraction, where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength `λ` is incident on two slits of width `a` separated by a distance `b` that are both of the order of `λ`  .
- According to Huygens' principle, each slit acts as a secondary source and sends secondary waves in all directions. These waves interfere constructively or destructively at different points on a screen placed at a large distance from the slits, forming a diffraction pattern  .
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern at an angle `θ` from the center of the slits is given by the formula  :

    `I(θ) = I0 (sin(πa sin(θ)/λ) / (πa sin(θ)/λ))^2 (cos(πb sin(θ)/λ))^2`

    where `I0` is the intensity at the center of the slits, `a sin(θ)` is the path difference between the waves from the two edges of each slit, and `b sin(θ)` is the path difference between the waves from the two slits.
- The diffraction pattern consists of a series of bright and dark fringes of varying intensity. The angular width of the fringes is given by  :

    `θ1 = λ/b`

    where `θ1` is the angle subtended by the first dark fringe on either side of the central bright fringe.
- The diffraction pattern also shows a modulation of intensity due to the single slit diffraction of each slit. The angular width of the central bright maximum due to the single slit diffraction is given by  :

    `θ0 = 2λ/a`

    where `θ0` is the angle subtended by the first dark fringe on either side of the central bright maximum.
