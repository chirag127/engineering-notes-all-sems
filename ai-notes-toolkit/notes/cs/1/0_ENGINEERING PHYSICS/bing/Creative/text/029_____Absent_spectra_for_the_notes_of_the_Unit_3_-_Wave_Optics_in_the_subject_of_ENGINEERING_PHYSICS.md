### Absent spectra

- Absent spectra are the missing orders of principal maxima in the diffraction pattern of a plane grating.
- They occur when the minima due to diffraction coincide with the maxima due to interference for a given order.
- The condition for absent spectra is given by the equation: **nλ = 2d sin θ**, where **n** is the order of the spectrum, **λ** is the wavelength of light, **d** is the grating element, and **θ** is the angle of diffraction .
- The order of the absent spectrum is given by the equation: **n = 2N**, where **N** is the number of lines per mm in the grating .
- Absent spectra reduce the resolving power of the grating, which is the ability to distinguish between two closely spaced wavelengths.
- Absent spectra can be avoided by using a blazed grating, which is a grating with a sawtooth-shaped profile that reflects more light in a particular direction.
