### Attenuation

- Attenuation is the reduction in intensity of the light beam (or signal) with respect to distance travelled through a transmission medium .
- Attenuation is measured in decibels (dB) and is calculated by the formula: Attenuation (dB) = 10 x Log 10 (P in/P out) = 20xLog 10 (V in/V out) where P is the power and V is the voltage of the signal.
- Attenuation limits the distance in which the signal can travel through optical fiber and affects the performance and reliability of the fiber optic network.
- Attenuation can be classified into two types: intrinsic and extrinsic .
- Intrinsic attenuation is the inherent loss of light in the fiber due to the physical properties of the fiber material and the manufacturing process. It includes absorption, scattering and bending losses .
- Absorption is the conversion of light energy into heat due to the interaction of light with the atoms and molecules of the fiber material. It depends on the wavelength of the light and the impurities in the fiber .
- Scattering is the deflection of light from its original direction due to the microscopic variations in the density and refractive index of the fiber material. It also depends on the wavelength of the light and the temperature of the fiber .
- Bending is the loss of light due to the curvature of the fiber. It can be macrobending or microbending. Macrobending is the large-scale bending of the fiber due to improper installation or handling. Microbending is the small-scale bending of the fiber due to external stresses or defects in the fiber .
- Extrinsic attenuation is the loss of light due to the external factors that affect the fiber optic network. It includes splicing, connectors, couplers, splitters and other devices that join or divide the fiber .
- Splicing is the process of joining two fibers by melting or fusing them together. It can cause loss of light due to the mismatch in the core diameter, numerical aperture, mode field diameter or refractive index of the fibers .
- Connectors are the devices that connect the fiber to the source, receiver or other fibers. They can cause loss of light due to the misalignment, air gap, dirt, dust or damage of the connector surfaces .
- Couplers and splitters are the devices that divide the light from one fiber into two or more fibers or combine the light from two or more fibers into one fiber. They can cause loss of light due to the insertion loss, reflection loss, excess loss or crosstalk of the device .
- Attenuation can be minimized by choosing the appropriate fiber type, wavelength, source, receiver and network design. It can also be compensated by using amplifiers, repeaters or regenerators that boost or regenerate the signal along the fiber .
