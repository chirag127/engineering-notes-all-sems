### Acceptance cone

- The acceptance cone is the cone-shaped region of space around the optical fiber that defines the range of angles within which light can enter the fiber and be propagated through it .
- The acceptance cone is derived by rotating the acceptance angle, which is the maximum angle with the axis of the optical fiber at which light can enter the fiber, around the fiber axis .
- The acceptance cone depends on the numerical aperture (NA) of the fiber, which is a measure of the light-gathering ability and resolution of the fiber.
- The numerical aperture is given by the formula: NA = n0 sin θa, where n0 is the refractive index of the medium surrounding the fiber, and θa is the acceptance angle .
- The numerical aperture also depends on the refractive indices of the core (nf) and the cladding (nc) of the fiber, as NA = √(nf^2 - nc^2)  .
- The larger the acceptance cone, the larger the numerical aperture, which means that the fiber can accept and propagate more light.
- However, a larger acceptance cone also means that the fiber can accept more modes of light, which can cause modal dispersion and reduce the bandwidth of the fiber.
- Therefore, there is a trade-off between the acceptance cone and the performance of the fiber.
