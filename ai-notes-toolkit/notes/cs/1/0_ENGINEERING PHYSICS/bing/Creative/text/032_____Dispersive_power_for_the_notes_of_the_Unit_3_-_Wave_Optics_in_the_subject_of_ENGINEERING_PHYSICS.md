### Dispersive Power

- Dispersive power is a measure of how much a material or a device can separate light of different wavelengths by refraction or diffraction.
- The higher the dispersive power, the more the material or device can split light into its constituent colors.
- One example of a device that has dispersive power is a prism. A prism can refract white light into a spectrum of colors, as shown in the diagram below.

![Prism dispersion](https://www.toppr.com/ask/content/concept/dispersive-power-271099/dispersive-power-271099-1.png)

- The dispersive power of a prism depends on the refractive indices of the prism material for different wavelengths of light. The refractive index of a material varies with the wavelength of light, a phenomenon known as dispersion.
- The formula for the dispersive power of a prism is given by  :

$$\omega = \frac{\theta}{\delta} = \frac{\mu - 1}{\mu_v - \mu_r}$$

- Where $\omega$ is the dispersive power, $\theta$ is the angular dispersion, $\delta$ is the mean deviation, $\mu$ is the refractive index for the mean wavelength, $\mu_v$ is the refractive index for the violet wavelength, and $\mu_r$ is the refractive index for the red wavelength.
- The angular dispersion is the difference between the angles of deviation of the violet and red rays, as shown in the diagram below.

![Angular dispersion](https://www.toppr.com/ask/content/concept/dispersive-power-271099/dispersive-power-271099-2.png)

- The mean deviation is the angle of deviation of the ray having the mean wavelength, which is usually taken as the yellow ray, as shown in the diagram below.

![Mean deviation](https://www.toppr.com/ask/content/concept/dispersive-power-271099/dispersive-power-271099-3.png)

- The refractive indices of the prism material for different wavelengths of light can be obtained from experimental data or from empirical formulas such as the Cauchy's equation or the Sellmeier's equation.
- The dispersive power of a prism can be calculated using the formula given above, if the refractive indices and the angles of deviation are known. Alternatively, the dispersive power can be measured experimentally using a spectrometer or a spectroscope.
- The dispersive power of a prism is a dimensionless quantity, as it is a ratio of two angles. It has no units.
- The dispersive power of a prism is a positive quantity, as the angular dispersion is always greater than the mean deviation.
- The dispersive power of a prism is affected by the prism material, the prism angle, and the wavelength range of the incident light. Different materials have different dispersive powers, as they have different refractive indices for different wavelengths. The prism angle determines the amount of refraction that occurs at the prism surfaces. The wavelength range of the incident light determines the extent of dispersion that occurs inside the prism.
- The dispersive power of a prism is useful for studying the spectrum of light sources, such as stars, lamps, lasers, etc. It can also be used to measure the refractive indices of different materials for different wavelengths of light. However, the dispersive power of a prism also causes chromatic aberration, which is the distortion of images due to the different focal lengths of different colors of light. Chromatic aberration can be reduced by using achromatic lenses or prisms, which are combinations of materials that have opposite dispersive powers.
