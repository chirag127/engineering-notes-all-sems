### Coherent sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Wave optics is the branch of physics that studies the nature and behavior of light as a wave phenomenon.
- One of the important aspects of wave optics is the interference of light, which occurs when two or more waves superpose and produce a new wave pattern.
- Interference of light can be observed only when the waves are coherent, meaning that they have the same frequency and a constant phase difference.
- Coherent sources are the sources that emit coherent waves, such as lasers, which produce monochromatic, parallel and unbroken wave chains.
- Coherent sources can be obtained by using a single source and splitting it into two or more beams by using devices such as mirrors, prisms, slits, etc.
- Coherent sources can also be obtained by using two or more independent sources that are synchronized in frequency and phase by using electronic or optical methods.
- Coherent sources are essential for producing sustained and stable interference patterns, which can be used for various applications such as holography, metrology, spectroscopy, etc.
- Coherent sources can be classified into two types: spatially coherent and temporally coherent.
- Spatially coherent sources produce waves that have a constant phase difference across the wavefront, meaning that they are in phase at any point on the same plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- Spatially coherent sources can produce interference patterns in any direction, and the degree of spatial coherence depends on the size and shape of the source and the distance from the source.
- Temporally coherent sources produce waves that have a constant phase difference over time, meaning that they are in phase at any point on the same wave train.
- Temporally coherent sources can produce interference patterns only in the direction of propagation, and the degree of temporal coherence depends on the duration and bandwidth of the source.
