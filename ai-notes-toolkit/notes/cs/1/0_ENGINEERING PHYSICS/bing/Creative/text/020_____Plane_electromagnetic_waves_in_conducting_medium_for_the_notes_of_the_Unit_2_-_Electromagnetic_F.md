### Plane electromagnetic waves in conducting medium

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has constant amplitude and direction of electric and magnetic fields in any plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A conducting medium is a medium that has finite conductivity (\uD835\uDF82), permittivity (\uD835\uDF3A) and permeability (\uD835\uDF41), and allows electric currents to flow through it.
- When a plane electromagnetic wave propagates in a conducting medium, it experiences attenuation (loss of energy) and phase shift (change in phase angle) due to the presence of free charges and currents in the medium.
- The wave equation for a plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium is given by:

$$\nabla^2 \vec{E} = \mu \epsilon \frac{\partial^2 \vec{E}}{\partial t^2} + \mu \sigma \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t}$$

- The general solution for the electric field of a plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium is given by:

$$\vec{E} = \vec{E}_0 e^{-\alpha x} e^{i(\omega t - \beta x)}$$

- where \uD835\uDC4E0 is the amplitude of the electric field, \uD835\uDC9C is the angular frequency of the wave, \uD835\uDC66 is the phase constant, and \uD835\uDC9E is the attenuation constant.
- The attenuation constant \uD835\uDC9E and the phase constant \uD835\uDC66 are related to the conductivity, permittivity and permeability of the medium by:

$$\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{\mu \epsilon}}{2} \sqrt{\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\sigma}{\omega \epsilon}\right)^2} - 1}$$

$$\beta = \frac{\sqrt{\mu \epsilon}}{2} \sqrt{\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\sigma}{\omega \epsilon}\right)^2} + 1}$$

- The magnetic field of the plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium can be obtained from the electric field by using the relation:

$$\vec{B} = \frac{1}{\omega \mu} \left(\vec{k} \times \vec{E}\right)$$

- where \uD835\uDC4B is the wave vector given by \uD835\uDC4B = \uD835\uDC66\uD835\uDC5F + \uD835\uDC9E\uD835\uDC60, and \uD835\uDC5F and \uD835\uDC60 are the unit vectors along the y and z axes, respectively.
- The plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium has a complex propagation constant \uD835\uDC67 given by \uD835\uDC67 = \uD835\uDC9E + i\uD835\uDC66, which determines the speed and attenuation of the wave.
- The speed of the plane electromagnetic wave in a conducting medium is given by:

$$v = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu \epsilon}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\sigma}{\omega \epsilon}\right)^2} + 1}}$$

- The speed of the wave decreases as the conductivity of the medium increases, and approaches zero as the medium becomes a perfect conductor.

$$A = 2 \alpha = \sqrt{\mu \epsilon} \sqrt{\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\sigma}{\omega \epsilon}\right)^2} - 1}$$

- The attenuation of the wave increases as the conductivity of the medium increases, and becomes infinite as the medium becomes a perfect conductor.
