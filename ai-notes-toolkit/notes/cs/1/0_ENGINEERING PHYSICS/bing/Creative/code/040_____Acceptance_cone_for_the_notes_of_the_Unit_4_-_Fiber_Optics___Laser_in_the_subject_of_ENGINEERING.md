### Acceptance cone

- The acceptance cone is the cone-shaped region of incident light that can enter an optical fiber and be propagated through it.
- The acceptance cone is derived by rotating the acceptance angle about the fiber axis.
- The acceptance angle is the maximum angle with the axis of the optical fiber at which light can enter the fiber and be guided by total internal reflection.
- The acceptance angle depends on the refractive indices of the core, the cladding, and the surrounding medium of the fiber.
- The numerical aperture (NA) is a measure of the light-gathering ability and resolution of an optical fiber.
- The NA is related to the acceptance angle by the formula: NA = n0 sin θa, where n0 is the refractive index of the surrounding medium and θa is the acceptance angle.
- The larger the acceptance cone, the larger the NA, which means that the fiber can accept and propagate more light.
- However, a larger NA also means a larger modal dispersion, which limits the bandwidth and transmission distance of the fiber.
