# Unit 3 - Wave Optics

Wave optics, also called physical optics, is a branch of optics that studies the behavior of light when it exhibits wave characteristics, such as interference, diffraction, polarization, and other phenomena that cannot be explained by the ray approximation of geometric optics  .

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- Huygens' principle: A method of analyzing the propagation of waves based on the assumption that every point on a wavefront is a source of spherical wavelets.
- Interference: The phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves resulting in a new wave pattern.
- Young's double-slit experiment: An experiment that demonstrates the interference of light waves from two coherent sources and produces a pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen.
- Coherence: The property of two or more waves having a constant phase difference and the same frequency.
- Diffraction: The bending of waves around obstacles or openings.
- Single-slit diffraction: The diffraction pattern produced by a single narrow slit illuminated by a monochromatic light source.
- Diffraction grating: A device consisting of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits that produces interference patterns when light passes through it.
- Polarization: The phenomenon of restricting the vibrations of a transverse wave, especially light, to one direction.
- Malus' law: A law that states that the intensity of polarized light transmitted through a polarizer is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle between the transmission axis of the polarizer and the polarization direction of the incident light.
- Brewster's angle: The angle of incidence at which the reflected light is completely polarized perpendicular to the plane of incidence when light is incident on a transparent medium.
