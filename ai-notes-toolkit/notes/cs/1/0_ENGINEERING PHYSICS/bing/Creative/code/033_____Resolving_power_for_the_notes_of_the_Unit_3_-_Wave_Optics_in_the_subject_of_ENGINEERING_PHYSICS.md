### Resolving power

- Resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to separate two adjacent points or objects from each other from a considerable distance.
- Resolving power depends on the wavelength of the light used and the aperture of the instrument.
- Resolving power is inversely proportional to the minimum angular separation between two points or objects that can be resolved by the instrument .
- Resolving power is measured in radians or degrees.
- There is no generalized formula for resolving power of an optical instrument. It depends from one instrument to another.
- Some common formulas for resolving power are:

  - For a telescope: RP = d / (1.22 λ), where d is the diameter of the objective lens, and λ is the wavelength of the light .
  - For a microscope: RP = 2 n sin θ / λ, where n is the refractive index of the medium, θ is the half-angle of the cone of light entering the objective lens, and λ is the wavelength of the light .
  - For a prism: RP = λ / dλ, where λ is the wavelength of the spectral line, and dλ is the difference between the wavelengths of two adjacent spectral lines .
  - For a diffraction grating: RP = λ / Δλ, where λ is the wavelength of the spectral line, and Δλ is the difference between the wavelengths of two adjacent spectral lines .

- Resolving power is important for applications such as astronomy, microscopy, spectroscopy, and photography.
