### Attenuation in Fiber Optics

- Attenuation in fiber optics, also known as transmission loss, is the reduction in intensity of the light beam (or signal) with respect to distance travelled through a transmission medium.
- Attenuation is a measure of decay of signal strength or loss of light power that occurs as light pulses propagate through the length of the optical fiber.
- Attenuation is expressed in decibels (dB), which is a logarithmic unit of the ratio of input power to output power.
- Attenuation (dB) = 10 x Log 10 (P in/P out) = 20xLog 10 (V in/V out) 
- Attenuation in optical fibers is mainly caused by two physical factors: absorption and scattering losses.
- Absorption is the process of converting optical power into heat due to the interaction of light with the molecules of the fiber material or impurities in the fiber.
- Scattering is the process of deflecting light from its original direction due to the microscopic variations in the refractive index of the fiber material.
- The attenuation coefficient of fiber is a function of wavelength and depends on the type and size of the fiber .
- The attenuation coefficient of fiber can be 0.36dB/km in wavelength of 1310nm and 0.22dB/km in wavelength of 1550nm.
- The attenuation coefficient of fiber is also affected by external factors such as bending, twisting, splicing, and connectors.
- The attenuation of fiber can be measured by using an optical power meter, an optical time domain reflectometer, or an optical spectrum analyzer.
