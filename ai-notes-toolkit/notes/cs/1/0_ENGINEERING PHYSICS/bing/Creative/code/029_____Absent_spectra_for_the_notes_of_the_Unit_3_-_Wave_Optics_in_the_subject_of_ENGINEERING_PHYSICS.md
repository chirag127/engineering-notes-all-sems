# Absent spectra for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Absent spectra are the missing orders of principal maxima in the diffraction pattern of a plane grating.
- They occur when the minima due to diffraction coincide with the maxima due to interference for a certain order of diffraction.
- The condition for absent spectra is given by the equation: nλ = 2d sin θ, where n is the order of diffraction, λ is the wavelength of light, d is the grating element, and θ is the angle of diffraction .
- The order of absent spectra can be found by dividing the grating element by the wavelength of light: n = d/λ.
- Absent spectra can be observed by using a monochromatic light source and a plane grating with a known grating element.
- Absent spectra can be used to measure the wavelength of light or the grating element by using the above equations.
- Absent spectra can also be used to verify the Rayleigh criterion for resolving two spectral lines of different wavelengths.
- Absent spectra are different from absorption spectra, which are the dark lines in the continuous spectrum of a light source due to the absorption of certain wavelengths by the intervening medium .
