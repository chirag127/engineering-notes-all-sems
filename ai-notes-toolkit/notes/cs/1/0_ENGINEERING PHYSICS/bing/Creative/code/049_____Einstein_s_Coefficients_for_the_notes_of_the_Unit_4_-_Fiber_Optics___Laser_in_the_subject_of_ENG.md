### Einstein's Coefficients

- Einstein coefficients are mathematical quantities that describe the probability of absorption or emission of a photon by an atom or molecule .
- There are three types of Einstein coefficients: A, B, and C.
- The Einstein A coefficient is related to the rate of spontaneous emission of light, which is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state emitting a photon and dropping to a lower energy state without any external influence  .
- The Einstein B coefficient is related to the absorption and stimulated emission of light, which are the processes of an atom or molecule in a lower energy state absorbing a photon and jumping to a higher energy state, or an atom or molecule in an excited state emitting a photon and dropping to a lower energy state due to the presence of an external radiation field  .
- The Einstein C coefficient is related to the collisional de-excitation of light, which is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state losing energy by colliding with another particle and dropping to a lower energy state.
- The Einstein coefficients are fixed probabilities per time associated with each atom, and do not depend on the state of the gas of which the atoms are a part. Therefore, any relationship that we can derive between the coefficients at, say, thermodynamic equilibrium will be valid universally.
- The Einstein coefficients can be used to calculate the rates of absorption, emission, and scattering of light by a gas of atoms or molecules, as well as the spectral line shapes and intensities .
- The Einstein coefficients can also be used to derive the Planck's law of blackbody radiation, which is the distribution of energy radiated by a perfect absorber and emitter of light at a given temperature .
