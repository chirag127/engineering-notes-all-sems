### Fraunhofer diffraction at single slit

- Fraunhofer diffraction is the diffraction of waves when plane waves are incident on a diffracting object, and the diffraction pattern is viewed at a sufficiently long distance from the object (in the far-field region), or at the focal plane of a converging lens.
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit is the simplest case of diffraction, where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength `λ` is incident on a slit of width `d` as shown in the figure below.

![Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit](https://gkscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Fraunhofer-Diffraction-at-a-Single-Slit.png)

- According to Huygens' principle, every point of the wavefront in the plane of the slit acts as a secondary source and sends secondary waves in all directions. These secondary waves interfere with each other and produce a diffraction pattern on a screen placed at a large distance from the slit.
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern is given by the formula :

`I = I0 (sinβ/β)^2`

where `I0` is the intensity at the center of the pattern, `β = πd sinθ/λ`, `d` is the slit width, `θ` is the angular position of the point on the screen, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light.

- The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright maximum, followed by alternate dark and bright fringes of decreasing intensity. The angular width of the central maximum is given by :

`θ = 2λ/d`

- The condition for the first minimum is `β = π`, which gives `sinθ = λ/d`. The higher order minima occur when `β = nπ`, where `n` is an integer. The angular width of the higher order maxima is given by :

`θ = λ/(nd)`

### Fraunhofer diffraction at double slit

- Fraunhofer diffraction at double slit is the diffraction of waves when plane waves are incident on two slits of width `a` separated by a distance `b` as shown in the figure below.

![Fraunhofer diffraction at double slit](https://scienceworld.wolfram.com/physics/graphics/FraunhoferDiffractionDoubleSlit.gif)

- The wavefunction of the diffracted waves is given by the formula:

`ψ = C exp(ikr) (sinα/α) (cosβ/2)`

where `C` is a constant, `k` is the wavenumber, `r` is the distance from the slits to the screen, `α = πa sinθ/λ`, `β = πb sinθ/λ`, `a` is the slit width, `b` is the slit separation, `θ` is the angular position of the point on the screen, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light.

- The intensity of the diffraction pattern is given by the formula :

`I = I0 (sinα/α)^2 (cosβ/2)^2`

where `I0` is the intensity at the center of the pattern.

- The diffraction pattern consists of a series of bright and dark fringes, modulated by a single slit envelope. The angular position of the maxima is given by :

`sinθ = nλ/b`

where `n` is an integer. The angular position of the minima is given by :

`sinθ = (n + 1/2)λ/b`

where `n` is an integer. The angular width of the fringes is given by :

`θ = λ/b`

- The intensity of the fringes decreases as the slit width `a` decreases, and the envelope becomes narrower. The intensity of the fringes increases as the slit separation `b` decreases
