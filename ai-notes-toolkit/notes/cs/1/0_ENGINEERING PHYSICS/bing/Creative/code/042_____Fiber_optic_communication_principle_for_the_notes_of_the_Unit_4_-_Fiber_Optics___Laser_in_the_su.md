### Fiber optic communication principle

- Fiber optic communication is a method of transmitting information from one place to another by sending pulses of infrared light through an optical fiber.
- The light is a form of carrier wave that is modulated to carry information. The modulation can be done by varying the intensity, phase, frequency, or polarization of the light.
- The optical fiber is a thin, flexible, transparent strand of glass or plastic that acts as a waveguide for the light. It has a core, a cladding, and a protective coating.
- The core is the central region of the fiber where the light is confined and propagated. It has a higher refractive index than the cladding, which is the outer layer of the fiber that surrounds the core and prevents the light from escaping.
- The protective coating is a material that covers the cladding and protects the fiber from physical damage and environmental factors.
- The principle of fiber optic communication is based on total internal reflection, which is the phenomenon that occurs when light traveling in a medium with a higher refractive index reaches the boundary with a medium with a lower refractive index at an angle greater than the critical angle.
- The critical angle is the minimum angle of incidence for which total internal reflection occurs. It depends on the refractive indices of the two media.
- When total internal reflection occurs, the light is reflected back into the core and continues to travel along the fiber. This way, the light can be transmitted over long distances with minimal loss of signal.
- The advantages of fiber optic communication include high bandwidth, low attenuation, immunity to electromagnetic interference, security, and flexibility  .
- The disadvantages of fiber optic communication include high cost, fragility, difficulty of splicing, and need for specialized equipment and skills  .
