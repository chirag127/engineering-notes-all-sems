### Laser applications

Laser is an acronym for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation. It is a device that produces a coherent beam of light with high intensity and low divergence. Lasers have many applications in various fields of science, engineering, medicine, industry, and entertainment. Some of the common laser applications are:

- **Transmission and processing of information**: Lasers are used to transmit and process information in optical communication systems, such as fiber optics, optical disks, barcode scanners, laser printers, and holography. Lasers can also be used to store and retrieve data in optical memory devices, such as CDs, DVDs, and Blu-ray discs. Lasers can also be used to encrypt and decrypt information using quantum cryptography.
- **Precise delivery of energy**: Lasers are used to deliver energy precisely and selectively to a target, such as in laser surgery, laser cutting, laser welding, laser drilling, laser marking, and laser engraving. Lasers can also be used to generate high temperatures and pressures, such as in laser fusion, laser heating, and laser propulsion. Lasers can also be used to induce chemical reactions, such as in laser spectroscopy, laser isotope separation, and laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy .
- **Alignment, measurement, and imaging**: Lasers are used to align and measure objects and distances, such as in laser leveling, laser rangefinding, laser interferometry, laser gyroscope, and laser Doppler velocimetry. Lasers are also used to image and scan objects and surfaces, such as in laser scanning, laser microscopy, laser profilometry, and laser-induced fluorescence .

These are some of the main laser applications, but there are many more. Lasers are versatile and powerful tools that have revolutionized many fields of science and technology.
