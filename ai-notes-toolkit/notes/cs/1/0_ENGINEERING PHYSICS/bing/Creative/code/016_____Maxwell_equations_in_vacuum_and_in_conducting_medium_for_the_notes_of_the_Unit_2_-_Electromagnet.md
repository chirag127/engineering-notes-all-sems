# Maxwell equations in vacuum and in conducting medium

- Maxwell equations are a set of four partial differential equations that describe the behavior of electric and magnetic fields in vacuum and in matter.
- They are derived from the experimental laws of Coulomb, Gauss, Ampere and Faraday, and modified by Maxwell to include the concept of displacement current.
- They are the foundation of electromagnetic theory and have many applications in physics and engineering.

## Maxwell equations in vacuum

- In vacuum, there are no charges or currents, so the electric and magnetic fields are only determined by the electric and magnetic flux densities, denoted by **E** and **B**, respectively.
- The Maxwell equations in vacuum are:

  - Gauss's law: $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = 0$$
  - Gauss's law for magnetism: $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$
  - Faraday's law of induction: $$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$
  - Ampere-Maxwell law: $$\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

- Where **∇** is the vector differential operator, **×** and **·** are the cross product and dot product, respectively, **t** is the time, **μ<sub>0</sub>** is the magnetic permeability of vacuum, and **ε<sub>0</sub>** is the electric permittivity of vacuum.
- These equations imply that electric and magnetic fields can generate each other and propagate as electromagnetic waves in vacuum at the speed of light, given by $$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}$$

## Maxwell equations in conducting medium

- In a conducting medium, there are free charges and currents that affect the electric and magnetic fields. The electric and magnetic fields are related to the electric and magnetic flux densities by the constitutive relations:

  - $$\mathbf{D} = \epsilon \mathbf{E}$$
  - $$\mathbf{H} = \frac{1}{\mu} \mathbf{B}$$

- Where **D** and **H** are the electric and magnetic flux densities, respectively, **ε** is the electric permittivity of the medium, and **μ** is the magnetic permeability of the medium.
- The Maxwell equations in conducting medium are:

  - Gauss's law: $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{D} = \rho$$
  - Gauss's law for magnetism: $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$
  - Faraday's law of induction: $$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$
  - Ampere-Maxwell law: $$\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

- Where **ρ** is the charge density and **J** is the current density in the medium.
- These equations describe how electric and magnetic fields interact with charges and currents in a conducting medium, and how electromagnetic waves propagate and attenuate in such a medium.
