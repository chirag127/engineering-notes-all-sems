### Plane Electromagnetic Waves in Conducting Medium

- Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves that consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields.
- In a conducting medium, the electric field generates a current that produces a magnetic field, and the magnetic field generates an electric field.
- The electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of wave propagation.
- The speed of the wave in a conducting medium is determined by the conductivity and permittivity of the medium.
- The wave is attenuated as it travels through the medium due to the resistance of the medium.
- The skin depth is the distance over which the amplitude of the wave decreases by a factor of 1/e. It is determined by the conductivity and frequency of the wave.
- The wave impedance is the ratio of the electric field to the magnetic field and is determined by the conductivity and permittivity of the medium.
- The reflection and transmission of the wave at the interface between two media are determined by the wave impedance of the two media.
