### Fraunhoffer Diffraction at Single Slit and Double Slit

#### Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

1. **Fraunhoffer Diffraction**: Fraunhoffer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when a wave passes through an aperture or around an obstacle, and the diffracted wavefronts are essentially parallel. This type of diffraction is also known as far-field diffraction.

2. **Single Slit Diffraction**: When a parallel beam of monochromatic light passes through a narrow slit, the light spreads out and forms a diffraction pattern on a screen placed behind the slit. This phenomenon is known as single slit diffraction.

3. **Double Slit Diffraction**: Double slit diffraction is a phenomenon where light passing through two closely spaced slits produces an interference pattern on a screen placed behind the slits. This pattern consists of a series of bright and dark fringes.

4. **Difference between Single and Double Slit Diffraction**: The main difference between single and double slit diffraction is that in single slit diffraction, the diffraction pattern is formed due to the interference of light waves diffracted from a single slit, while in double slit diffraction, the diffraction pattern is formed due to the interference of light waves diffracted from two closely spaced slits.

5. **Applications of Single and Double Slit Diffraction**: Single and double slit diffraction have many applications in various fields such as optics, physics, and engineering. Some of the common applications include the determination of the wavelength of light, the study of the properties of light, and the measurement of the size of small objects.
