### Dispersive Power

Dispersive power is a property of a material that determines the degree of separation of light of different colors (wavelengths) when it passes through the material. It is defined as the ratio of the difference in the angles of deviation of two light rays of different wavelengths to the difference in the angles of minimum deviation of the same two rays.

Mathematically, the dispersive power (ω) of a material is given by the formula:

ω = (μv - μr) / (μy - 1)

where μv, μr, and μy are the refractive indices of the material for violet, red, and yellow light, respectively.

Dispersive power is an important property of optical materials, as it determines the degree of chromatic aberration in optical instruments. Materials with high dispersive power can separate light into its constituent colors more effectively, but also produce more chromatic aberration.

In summary, dispersive power is a measure of the ability of a material to separate light into its constituent colors. It is determined by the refractive indices of the material for different wavelengths of light and is an important property to consider in the design of optical instruments.
