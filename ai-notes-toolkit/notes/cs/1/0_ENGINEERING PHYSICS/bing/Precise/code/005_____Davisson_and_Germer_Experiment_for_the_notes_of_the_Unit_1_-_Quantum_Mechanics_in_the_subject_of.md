### Davisson and Germer Experiment

- The Davisson-Germer experiment demonstrated the wave nature of the electron, confirming the earlier hypothesis of deBroglie.
- Putting wave-particle duality on a firm experimental footing, it represented a major step forward in the development of quantum mechanics.
- First conducted in 1927, the Davisson Germer Experiment was carried out by two scientists, C.J. Davisson and L.H. Germer, to explain the dual nature of electrons, i.e. Particle-wave Nature of the Electron.
- De Broglie proposed it in 1924, the dual nature of matter and the affirmation of Quantum Mechanics.
- Davisson and Germer Experiment, for the first time, proved the wave nature of electrons and verified the de Broglie equation.
- The basic thought behind the Davisson and Germer experiment was that the waves reflected from two different atomic layers of a Ni crystal will have a fixed phase difference.
- After reflection, these waves will interfere either constructively or destructively. Hence producing a diffraction pattern.
- In 1927 at Bell Labs, Clinton Davisson and Lester Germer fired slow moving electrons at a crystalline nickel target.
- The angular dependence of the reflected electron intensity was measured and was determined to have the same diffraction pattern as those predicted by Bragg for X-rays.
