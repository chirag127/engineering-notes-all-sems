### Persistent Current

- A persistent current is a steady electric current that flows indefinitely in a superconducting loop.
- This phenomenon occurs because superconductors have zero electrical resistance, allowing the current to flow without any energy loss.
- The current will continue to flow until an external force, such as a change in temperature or the application of a magnetic field, disrupts the superconducting state.
- Persistent currents are used in a variety of applications, including superconducting magnets and energy storage devices.
- In superconducting magnets, the persistent current generates a strong magnetic field that can be used for medical imaging, particle accelerators, and other applications.
- In energy storage devices, the persistent current can be used to store electrical energy with high efficiency and minimal energy loss.
