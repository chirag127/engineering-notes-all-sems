## Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory

1. Electromagnetic field theory is the study of the interaction between electric charges and magnetic fields.
2. The fundamental principles of electromagnetic field theory are based on the laws of electromagnetism, which were first formulated by James Clerk Maxwell in the 19th century.
3. These laws describe the behavior of electric and magnetic fields, and their interactions with charged particles.
4. The four Maxwell's equations are the foundation of electromagnetic field theory. They are:
    - Gauss's law for electricity: The electric flux through any closed surface is proportional to the charge enclosed within the surface.
    - Gauss's law for magnetism: The magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero.
    - Faraday's law of induction: The electromotive force induced in a closed circuit is equal to the rate of change of the magnetic flux through the circuit.
    - Ampere's law with Maxwell's correction: The magnetic field around a closed loop is proportional to the current flowing through the loop, plus the displacement current.
5. Electromagnetic waves, such as light, radio waves, and microwaves, are a consequence of the interaction between electric and magnetic fields.
6. Electromagnetic field theory has numerous applications, including the design of electrical and electronic devices, communication systems, and medical imaging technologies.
