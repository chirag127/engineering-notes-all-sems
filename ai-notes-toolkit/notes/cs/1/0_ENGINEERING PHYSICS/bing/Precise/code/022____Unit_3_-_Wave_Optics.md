## Unit 3 - Wave Optics

Wave optics, also known as physical optics, is the study of light as a wave. It is concerned with the behavior of light when it encounters obstacles or interacts with other waves. Some of the phenomena that can be explained using wave optics include interference, diffraction, and polarization.

1. **Interference**: Interference occurs when two or more waves meet and interact with each other. The resulting wave can have a larger or smaller amplitude than the individual waves, depending on how they combine. This can result in constructive or destructive interference.

2. **Diffraction**: Diffraction is the bending of light around obstacles or through small openings. It occurs because light behaves as a wave and can spread out as it passes through an opening or around an obstacle.

3. **Polarization**: Polarization is the orientation of the electric field of a light wave. Light can be polarized by passing it through a polarizing filter, which only allows light with a certain orientation of the electric field to pass through.
