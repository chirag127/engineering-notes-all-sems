### Type I and Type II superconductors

Superconductors are materials that exhibit zero electrical resistance and the expulsion of magnetic fields below a certain temperature, known as the critical temperature. There are two types of superconductors: Type I and Type II.

1. **Type I superconductors** are also known as "soft" superconductors. They are usually made of pure metals or alloys and have a relatively low critical temperature. When exposed to a magnetic field above a certain critical value, they lose their superconducting properties and revert to a normal state. This phenomenon is known as the Meissner effect.

2. **Type II superconductors** are also known as "hard" superconductors. They are usually made of compounds or alloys and have a higher critical temperature than Type I superconductors. Unlike Type I superconductors, Type II superconductors do not lose their superconducting properties when exposed to a magnetic field above a certain critical value. Instead, they allow magnetic flux to penetrate the material in the form of quantized vortices, which allows them to maintain their superconducting properties in high magnetic fields.
