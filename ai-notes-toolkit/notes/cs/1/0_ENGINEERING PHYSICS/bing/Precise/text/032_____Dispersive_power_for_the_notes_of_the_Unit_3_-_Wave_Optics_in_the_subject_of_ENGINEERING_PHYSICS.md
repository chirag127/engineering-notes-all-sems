### Dispersive Power

Dispersive power is a measure of the ability of a medium to separate light of different wavelengths by refraction. It is defined as the ratio of the difference in the angles of deviation of two light rays of different wavelengths to the difference in their angles of minimum deviation.

- The dispersive power of a medium is given by the formula: 
$$
\omega = \frac{\delta_{2} - \delta_{1}}{\Delta \delta_{m}}
$$
where $\delta_{2}$ and $\delta_{1}$ are the angles of deviation of two light rays of different wavelengths, and $\Delta \delta_{m}$ is the difference in their angles of minimum deviation.

- The dispersive power of a medium is dependent on the material of the medium and the wavelengths of the light rays being refracted.

- A medium with a high dispersive power will separate light of different wavelengths to a greater extent than a medium with a low dispersive power.

- The dispersive power of a prism is used in the construction of spectrometers, which are instruments used to measure the wavelengths of light.

- The dispersive power of a medium can also be used to correct chromatic aberration in optical instruments such as telescopes and microscopes.

- In summary, dispersive power is an important property of a medium that determines its ability to separate light of different wavelengths by refraction. It is used in the design of optical instruments and has practical applications in fields such as spectroscopy and microscopy.
