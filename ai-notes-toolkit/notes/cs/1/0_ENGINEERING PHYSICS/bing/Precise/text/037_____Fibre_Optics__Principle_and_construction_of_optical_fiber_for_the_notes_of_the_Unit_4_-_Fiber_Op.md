### Fibre Optics: Principle and construction of optical fiber

Fiber optics is the science of transmitting data, voice, and images by the passage of light through thin, transparent fibers. In telecommunications, fiber optic technology has virtually replaced copper wire in long-distance telephone lines, and it is used to link computers within local area networks.

The working principle of optical fibers is based on the total internal reflection of light. This means that no part of the signal gets refracted or transmitted to the second medium, but instead, all of the signals is retained inside the fiber.

Modern optical fibers are made of double layers of glass. The inner fiber core, which is 8 μm in diameter, is encircled by cladding, a concentric core of lower index glass that is 125 μm in diameter. A protective layer surrounds the cladding. The core-cladding interface is where total internal reflection occurs.

Optical fibers have many advantages over copper cables. They transmit information far more quickly and have a higher bandwidth.
