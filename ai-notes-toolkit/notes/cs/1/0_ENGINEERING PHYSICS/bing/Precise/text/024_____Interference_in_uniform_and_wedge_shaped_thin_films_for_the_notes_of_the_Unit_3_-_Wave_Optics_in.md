### Interference in uniform and wedge shaped thin films for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

1. Interference is the phenomenon in which two or more waves superimpose to form a resultant wave of greater, lower, or the same amplitude.
2. Interference usually refers to the interaction of waves that are correlated or coherent with each other, either because they come from the same source or because they have the same or nearly the same frequency.
3. Thin film interference occurs when light waves reflect off the top and bottom surfaces of a thin film and interfere with each other.
4. The interference can be constructive or destructive, depending on the thickness of the film, the wavelength of the light, and the angle of incidence.
5. In a uniform thin film, the thickness of the film is constant, and the interference pattern is uniform across the film.
6. In a wedge-shaped thin film, the thickness of the film varies, and the interference pattern changes across the film.
7. The interference pattern in a wedge-shaped thin film can be used to measure the thickness of the film and to determine the angle of the wedge.
8. Thin film interference has many practical applications, including anti-reflective coatings, mirrors, and optical filters.
