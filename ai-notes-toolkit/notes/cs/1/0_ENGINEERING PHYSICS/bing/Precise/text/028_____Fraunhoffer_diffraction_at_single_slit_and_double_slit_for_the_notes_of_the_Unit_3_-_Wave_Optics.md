### Fraunhoffer diffraction at single slit and double slit for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

1. Fraunhofer diffraction is the far-field diffraction pattern of light from a source that is effectively at infinity.
2. It is named after Joseph von Fraunhofer, who first studied it.
3. Fraunhofer diffraction can be observed at a single slit or a double slit.
4. In the case of a single slit, the diffraction pattern is characterized by a central bright fringe, with alternating dark and bright fringes on either side.
5. The width of the central bright fringe is inversely proportional to the width of the slit.
6. In the case of a double slit, the diffraction pattern is characterized by a series of bright fringes, with dark fringes in between.
7. The separation between the bright fringes is inversely proportional to the separation between the slits.
8. The intensity of the bright fringes decreases as one moves away from the central bright fringe.
9. The diffraction pattern can be explained using the principle of superposition and the wave nature of light.
10. The study of Fraunhofer diffraction is important in understanding the behavior of light and its interaction with matter.
