### Skin Depth

Skin depth is a measure of the distance an electromagnetic wave can penetrate into a conductor before it is attenuated to 1/e (about 37%) of its original amplitude. It is a function of the frequency of the wave, the conductivity of the conductor, and the permeability of the conductor.

1. Skin depth is defined as the distance at which the amplitude of an electromagnetic wave decreases to 1/e of its original value.
2. The skin depth is given by the formula: δ = 1/√(πfμσ), where δ is the skin depth, f is the frequency of the wave, μ is the permeability of the conductor, and σ is the conductivity of the conductor.
3. The skin depth decreases with increasing frequency, meaning that higher frequency waves penetrate less into a conductor than lower frequency waves.
4. The skin depth also decreases with increasing conductivity and permeability of the conductor, meaning that a better conductor will have a smaller skin depth.
5. Skin depth is an important concept in electromagnetic field theory, as it determines the effective thickness of a conductor for a given frequency of electromagnetic wave.
