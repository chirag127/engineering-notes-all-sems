### Rayleigh’s criterion of resolution for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Rayleigh's criterion of resolution is a standard for the minimum resolvable detail in an optical imaging system.
- It states that two point sources are just resolved when the principal diffraction maximum of one image coincides with the first minimum of the other.
- If the distance between the two sources is greater than the Rayleigh criterion, the two sources can be resolved, otherwise, they cannot.
- The Rayleigh criterion is given by the formula θ = 1.22λ/D, where θ is the angular resolution, λ is the wavelength of light, and D is the diameter of the aperture.
- This criterion is widely used in various fields such as microscopy, astronomy, and photography to determine the resolving power of optical instruments.
- It is important to note that the Rayleigh criterion is a guideline and not a hard and fast rule. In some cases, it may be possible to resolve details smaller than the Rayleigh criterion using advanced techniques such as image processing or superresolution microscopy.
