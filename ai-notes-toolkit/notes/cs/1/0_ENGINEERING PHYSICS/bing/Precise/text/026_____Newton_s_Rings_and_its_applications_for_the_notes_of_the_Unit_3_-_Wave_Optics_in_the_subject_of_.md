### Newton’s Rings and its applications

Newton's rings is a phenomenon in which an interference pattern is created by the reflection of light between two surfaces - a spherical surface and an adjacent flat surface. It is named after Sir Isaac Newton, who first studied the effect in the 17th century.

The phenomenon is observed due to the interference of light waves reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of a thin film of air between the lens and the glass plate. When viewed with monochromatic light, the pattern appears as a series of concentric, alternating bright and dark rings centered at the point of contact between the two surfaces.

Applications of Newton's Rings include:
1. Determining the wavelength of light: By measuring the diameter of the rings, the wavelength of the light used can be calculated.
2. Testing the quality of lenses: Newton's rings can be used to test the quality of lenses by observing the shape and regularity of the rings.
3. Measuring the refractive index of a liquid: By immersing the lens and glass plate in a liquid and observing the change in the diameter of the rings, the refractive index of the liquid can be determined.

This phenomenon is an important concept in the study of wave optics and is covered in Unit 3 of the subject of Engineering Physics. It is important to understand the principles behind Newton's rings in order to fully grasp the concepts of interference and thin film optics.
