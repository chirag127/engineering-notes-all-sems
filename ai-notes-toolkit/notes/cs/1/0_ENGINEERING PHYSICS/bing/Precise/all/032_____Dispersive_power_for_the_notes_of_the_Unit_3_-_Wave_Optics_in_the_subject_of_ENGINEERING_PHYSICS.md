### Dispersive Power

Dispersive power is a measure of the ability of a medium to separate light of different wavelengths by refraction. It is defined as the ratio of the difference in the angles of deviation for two rays of light of different wavelengths to the difference in the angles of minimum deviation for the same two rays.

In mathematical terms, the dispersive power (ω) of a medium is given by the formula:

ω = (δ1 - δ2) / (Dm1 - Dm2)

where δ1 and δ2 are the angles of deviation for two rays of light of different wavelengths, and Dm1 and Dm2 are the angles of minimum deviation for the same two rays.

The dispersive power of a medium is an important property in the field of optics, as it determines the degree to which a prism or lens will separate light into its constituent colors. A medium with a high dispersive power will produce a more pronounced separation of colors than a medium with a low dispersive power.

In the context of wave optics, the dispersive power of a medium is related to its refractive index and the wavelength of light. The refractive index of a medium generally decreases with increasing wavelength, which means that longer wavelengths of light are refracted less than shorter wavelengths. This difference in refraction causes the separation of colors in a prism or lens.
