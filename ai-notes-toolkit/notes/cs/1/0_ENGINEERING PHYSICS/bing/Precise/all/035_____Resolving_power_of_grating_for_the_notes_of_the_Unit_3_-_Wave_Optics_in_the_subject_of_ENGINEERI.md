# Resolving Power of Grating

The resolving power of a grating is defined as its ability to separate two closely spaced spectral lines. It is given by the formula:

R = nN

Where R is the resolving power, n is the order of the spectrum, and N is the total number of lines on the grating.

The resolving power of a grating increases with the order of the spectrum and the number of lines on the grating. A grating with a large number of lines and used in a high order of the spectrum can resolve very closely spaced spectral lines.

The resolving power of a grating can also be expressed in terms of the wavelength difference between two spectral lines that can be just resolved. This is given by the formula:

Δλ = λ / R

Where Δλ is the wavelength difference between two spectral lines that can be just resolved, λ is the average wavelength of the two spectral lines, and R is the resolving power of the grating.

In summary, the resolving power of a grating is its ability to separate closely spaced spectral lines. It is determined by the order of the spectrum and the number of lines on the grating. The resolving power can also be expressed in terms of the wavelength difference between two spectral lines that can be just resolved.
