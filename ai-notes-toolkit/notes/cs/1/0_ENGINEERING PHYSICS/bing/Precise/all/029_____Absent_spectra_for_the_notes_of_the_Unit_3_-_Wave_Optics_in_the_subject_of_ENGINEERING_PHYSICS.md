### Absent Spectra

- Absent spectra, also known as absorption spectra, are produced when light passes through a medium and certain wavelengths are absorbed by the medium.
- This results in dark lines or bands in the spectrum, where the absorbed wavelengths would have been.
- Absent spectra are the opposite of emission spectra, where light is emitted by a medium at specific wavelengths, resulting in bright lines or bands in the spectrum.
- Absent spectra can be used to identify the composition of a medium, as different elements and compounds absorb light at specific wavelengths.
- In the context of wave optics, absent spectra can be observed when white light passes through a prism and is dispersed into its component colors. If the light passes through a medium before reaching the prism, certain wavelengths may be absorbed, resulting in an absent spectrum.
- Absent spectra are important in fields such as astronomy, where they are used to determine the composition of stars and other celestial objects.
