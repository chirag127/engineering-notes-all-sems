### Fraunhofer diffraction at single slit and double slit

- Fraunhofer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when plane waves are incident on a diffracting object, and the diffraction pattern is viewed at a sufficiently long distance from the object (in the far-field region), or at the focal plane of a converging lens.
- Fraunhofer diffraction can be used to analyze the shape and size of the diffracting object, such as a slit, a grating, or an aperture, by measuring the intensity and angular distribution of the diffracted light.
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit is the simplest case of diffraction, where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength λ is incident on a slit of width d, which is of the order of λ .
- According to Huygens' principle, every point of the wavefront in the plane of the slit acts as a secondary source and sends secondary waves in all directions. These secondary waves interfere with each other and form a diffraction pattern on a screen placed at a large distance from the slit, or at the focal plane of a lens .
- The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright maximum, called the principal maximum, and a series of alternate bright and dark fringes, called the secondary maxima and minima, on either side of the central maximum .
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern at any angle θ from the center of the slit can be calculated by using the Fraunhofer diffraction equation for a single slit, which is given by :

  I(θ) = I0 (sin(πd sin θ / λ) / (πd sin θ / λ))^2

  where I0 is the intensity at the center of the slit, d is the slit width, λ is the wavelength of light, and θ is the angle from the center of the slit.
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern is maximum when the argument of the sine function is zero, which occurs when θ = 0 (the principal maximum), or when πd sin θ / λ = nπ, where n is any integer other than zero (the secondary maxima). The intensity is zero when the argument of the sine function is ±π/2, ±3π/2, ..., which occurs when πd sin θ / λ = (n + 1/2)π, where n is any integer (the secondary minima) .
- The angular width of the central maximum, or the angular distance between the first minima on either side of the central maximum, is given by :

  β = 2θ = 2 (λ / d)

  where β is the angular width, λ is the wavelength of light, and d is the slit width.
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a double slit is a more complex case of diffraction, where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength λ is incident on two slits of width a, separated by a distance d, where a and d are of the order of λ  .
- According to Huygens' principle, each slit acts as a secondary source and sends secondary waves in all directions. These secondary waves interfere with each other and form a diffraction pattern on a screen placed at a large distance from the slits, or at the focal plane of a lens  .
- The diffraction pattern consists of a series of bright and dark fringes, called the interference fringes, superimposed on a broader diffraction pattern, called the envelope, which is similar to the diffraction pattern of a single slit of width d  .
