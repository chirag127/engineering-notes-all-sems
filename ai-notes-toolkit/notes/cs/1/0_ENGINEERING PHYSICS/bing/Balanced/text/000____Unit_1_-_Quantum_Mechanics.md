## Unit 1 - Quantum Mechanics

- Quantum mechanics is a subfield of physics that describes the behavior of particles — atoms, electrons, photons and almost everything in the molecular and submolecular realm.
- Quantum mechanics is a fundamental theory in physics that provides a description of the physical properties of nature at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles.
- Quantum mechanics attempts to describe and account for the properties of molecules and atoms and their constituents— electrons, protons, neutrons, and other more esoteric particles such as quarks and gluons.
- Quantum mechanics is a mathematical machine for predicting the behaviors of microscopic particles — or, at least, of the measuring instruments we use to explore those behaviors.
- Quantum mechanics is based on a set of postulates, principles, and equations that govern the quantum phenomena, such as the uncertainty principle, the superposition principle, the Schrödinger equation, the Dirac equation, the Pauli exclusion principle, the Heisenberg picture, the Feynman path integral, the Born rule, and the Copenhagen interpretation.
- Quantum mechanics has many applications in various fields, such as quantum chemistry, quantum field theory, quantum technology, and quantum information science.
- Quantum mechanics also reveals many aspects of reality that are counterintuitive, paradoxical, and mysterious, such as the wave-particle duality, the quantum entanglement, the quantum tunneling, the quantum Zeno effect, the quantum eraser, and the quantum measurement problem .
