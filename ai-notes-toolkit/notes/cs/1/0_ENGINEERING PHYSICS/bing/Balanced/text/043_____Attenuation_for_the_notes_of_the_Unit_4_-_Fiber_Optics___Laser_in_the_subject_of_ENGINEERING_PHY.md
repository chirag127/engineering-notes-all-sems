### Attenuation

- Attenuation is the reduction in intensity of the light beam (or signal) with respect to distance travelled through a transmission medium .
- Attenuation is measured in decibels (dB) and is calculated by the formula: `Attenuation (dB) = 10 x Log10 (P in/P out) = 20xLog10 (V in/V out)` where P is power and V is voltage.
- Attenuation limits the distance in which the signal can travel through optical fiber and affects the performance and reliability of the fiber optic network.
- Attenuation can be caused by intrinsic and extrinsic factors .
  - Intrinsic factors are inherent to the fiber optic material and include:
    - Absorption: the conversion of optical power into heat due to the interaction of light with the molecules of the fiber material or impurities in the fiber .
    - Scattering: the deflection of light from its original direction due to the microscopic variations in the density and refractive index of the fiber material .
    - Dispersion: the spreading of light pulses over time due to the different velocities of different wavelengths or modes of light in the fiber .
  - Extrinsic factors are external to the fiber optic material and include:
    - Bending: the loss of light due to the curvature of the fiber beyond its minimum bend radius .
    - Splicing: the loss of light due to the imperfect alignment or connection of two fiber segments .
    - Connector: the loss of light due to the reflection, misalignment or contamination of the fiber end faces at the connector interface .
    - Environmental: the loss of light due to the changes in temperature, humidity, pressure or vibration that affect the fiber characteristics .
