### Resolving power of grating

- Resolving power of grating is the ability of a grating to separate two spectral lines of very close wavelengths into distinct maxima .
- It is measured by the ratio of the mean wavelength of the spectral line to the smallest difference in wavelengths that can be resolved by the grating, i.e. R.P. = λ/dλ .
- The resolving power of a grating depends on the number of lines per unit length of the grating, the order of diffraction, and the width of the grating .
- The expression for the resolving power of a grating is given by R.P. = Nm, where N is the total number of lines on the grating and m is the order of diffraction  .
- The resolving power of a grating is maximum when the angle of diffraction is 90°, i.e. when the diffracted rays are perpendicular to the grating plane .
- The resolving power of a grating is proportional to the dispersive power of the grating, which is the ratio of the change in angle of diffraction to the corresponding change in wavelength .
- A standard benchmark for the resolving power of a grating is the resolution of the sodium doublet, which are two spectral lines of sodium at 589.00 nm and 589.59 nm. Another example is the resolution of the hydrogen and deuterium lines, which are at 656.28 nm and 656.10 nm respectively.
