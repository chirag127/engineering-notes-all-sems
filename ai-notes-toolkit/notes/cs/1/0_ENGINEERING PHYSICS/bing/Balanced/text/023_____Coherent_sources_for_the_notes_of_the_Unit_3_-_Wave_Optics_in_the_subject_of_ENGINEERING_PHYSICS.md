### Coherent sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Wave optics is the branch of physics that studies the nature and behavior of light as a wave phenomenon.
- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves that results in a new wave pattern.
- Coherent sources are the sources that emit waves with the same frequency, wavelength and phase or having a constant phase difference.
- Coherent sources are necessary for producing a stable interference pattern, where the positions of maxima and minima are fixed.
- Examples of coherent sources are lasers, which produce monochromatic, parallel and unbroken wave chains, and two slits illuminated by a single light source, which act as secondary coherent sources.
- Incoherent sources are the sources that emit waves with different frequencies, wavelengths and phases or having a random phase difference.
- Incoherent sources produce a transient interference pattern, where the positions of maxima and minima change with time.
- Examples of incoherent sources are ordinary light sources, such as the sun, stars, lamps, etc., which produce polychromatic, divergent and broken wave chains.
- Coherent superposition of optical wave fields has applications in holography, which is a technique of creating three-dimensional images using interference of light beams.
- Coherent superposition of non-optical wave fields has applications in radio astronomy, which is a branch of astronomy that studies celestial objects using radio waves.
