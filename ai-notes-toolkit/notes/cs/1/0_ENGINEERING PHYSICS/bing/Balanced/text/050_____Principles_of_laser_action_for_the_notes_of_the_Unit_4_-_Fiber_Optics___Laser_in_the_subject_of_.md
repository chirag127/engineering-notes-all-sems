### Principles of laser action

- Laser is an acronym for **Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**.
- Laser action is the process of producing a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by stimulating atoms or molecules to emit photons of the same frequency, phase, and direction.
- The basic principles of laser action are :
  - **Spontaneous emission**: An atom or molecule in an excited state can decay to a lower energy state by emitting a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states. This process is random and isotropic, meaning that the photon can be emitted in any direction and with any phase.
  - **Stimulated emission**: An atom or molecule in an excited state can also decay to a lower energy state by emitting a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states, but only if it is stimulated by another photon of the same frequency and phase. This process is coherent and directional, meaning that the emitted photon has the same frequency, phase, and direction as the stimulating photon. The two photons are said to be in phase with each other.
  - **Stimulated absorption**: An atom or molecule in a lower energy state can absorb a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states and jump to a higher energy state. This process is the reverse of stimulated emission and reduces the intensity of the light beam.
  - **Non-radiative decay**: An atom or molecule in an excited state can also decay to a lower energy state by transferring its excess energy to other atoms or molecules through collisions or vibrations. This process is non-radiative and does not involve the emission or absorption of photons. It reduces the population of the excited state and increases the population of the lower state.
  - **Population inversion**: For laser action to occur, there must be more atoms or molecules in the excited state than in the lower state, so that the rate of stimulated emission is greater than the rate of stimulated absorption. This condition is called population inversion and can be achieved by pumping energy into the system through various methods, such as electrical discharge, optical pumping, chemical reaction, etc.
  - **Amplification of light**: When a light beam passes through a medium with population inversion, it undergoes multiple cycles of stimulated emission and absorption, resulting in an exponential increase in its intensity. This process is called amplification of light and depends on the length of the medium, the cross-section of the beam, and the degree of population inversion. The amplified light beam is then extracted from the medium by using mirrors or other optical devices.
