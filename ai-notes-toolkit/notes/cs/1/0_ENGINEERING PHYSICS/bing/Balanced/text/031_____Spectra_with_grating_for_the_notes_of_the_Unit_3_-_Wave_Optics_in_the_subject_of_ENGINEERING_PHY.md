### Spectra with grating

- A **grating** is a device that splits a beam of light into its component wavelengths, producing a **spectrum**.
- A grating consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits or grooves on a transparent or reflective surface.
- The **grating equation** relates the angles of incidence and diffraction of a monochromatic light beam to the grating spacing and the order of the diffracted beam.
- The grating equation is given by: $$d(\sin i + \sin r) = n\lambda$$ where $d$ is the grating spacing, $i$ is the angle of incidence, $r$ is the angle of diffraction, $n$ is the order of diffraction, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light.
- The **angular dispersion** of a grating is the rate of change of the angle of diffraction with respect to the wavelength of light.
- The angular dispersion is given by: $$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = \frac{n}{d\cos\theta}$$ where $\theta$ is the angle of diffraction.
- The **resolving power** of a grating is the ability to separate two adjacent spectral lines of slightly different wavelengths.
- The resolving power is given by: $$R = \frac{\lambda}{\Delta\lambda} = nN$$ where $\Delta\lambda$ is the minimum wavelength difference that can be resolved, and $N$ is the total number of slits or grooves in the grating.
- A grating can be used in a **spectrometer** to measure the wavelengths of different spectral lines in a light source.
- A spectrometer consists of a collimator, a grating, and a telescope, arranged as shown in the figure below:

- The collimator produces a parallel beam of light from the source and directs it onto the grating.
- The grating diffracts the light into different orders and angles according to the grating equation.
- The telescope is adjusted to view the diffracted beams of a particular order and wavelength.
- The angle of the telescope can be measured using a scale or a vernier.
- The wavelength of the light can be calculated using the grating equation and the known grating spacing.
- A grating can produce a **bright and clear spectrum** with **high resolving power** and **low intensity loss**.
- A grating can also be used to study the **emission spectra** of different elements or compounds, which are characteristic of their atomic or molecular structure.
- A grating can also be used to study the **absorption spectra** of different substances, which show the wavelengths of light that are absorbed by them.
- A grating can also be used to study the **Fourier optics** of coherent light sources, which show the spatial frequency components of the light wave.
