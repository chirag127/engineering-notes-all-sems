### Step index and graded index fibers

- Step index fiber and graded index fiber are two types of multimode fibers that can transmit multiple modes of light simultaneously.
- Step index fiber has a core with constant refractive index and a sharp change at the core-cladding interface. The core diameter is typically larger than 50 microns.
- Graded index fiber has a core with varying refractive index that decreases gradually from the center to the cladding. The core diameter is typically between 50 and 62.5 microns.
- The main differences between step index and graded index fibers are:

  - Graded index fiber offers superior transmission quality, higher bandwidth, and longer distance than step index fiber.
  - Step index fiber suffers from higher modal dispersion, which limits its bandwidth and data rate. Modal dispersion occurs when different modes travel at different speeds and arrive at different times at the receiver.
  - Graded index fiber reduces modal dispersion by making the higher modes travel faster in the lower refractive index regions of the core. This compensates for the longer path length of the higher modes and makes them arrive at the same time as the lower modes.
  - Step index fiber is cheaper and easier to manufacture than graded index fiber.
  - Graded index fiber is preferred for modern communication applications that require high bandwidth and long distance.
  - Step index fiber is mainly used for short distance and low bandwidth applications, such as local area networks (LANs) and sensors.
