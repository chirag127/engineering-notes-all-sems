### Acceptance cone

- The acceptance cone is the cone-shaped region of incident light that can enter an optical fiber and be propagated along its core.
- The acceptance cone is derived by rotating the acceptance angle, which is the maximum angle of incidence for which total internal reflection occurs at the core-cladding interface, about the fiber axis .
- The size of the acceptance cone depends on the numerical aperture (NA) of the fiber, which is a measure of its light-gathering ability and resolution.
- The numerical aperture is defined as the sine of the acceptance angle, multiplied by the refractive index of the medium in which the fiber is immersed .
- The numerical aperture is also related to the refractive indices of the core and the cladding of the fiber, by the formula NA = sqrt(n_core^2 - n_cladding^2), where n_core and n_cladding are the core and cladding refractive indices, respectively .
- The larger the acceptance cone, the larger the numerical aperture, which means that the fiber can accept and propagate more light.
- However, a larger acceptance cone also implies a larger divergence of the output beam, which may reduce the quality of the transmitted signal.
- Therefore, there is a trade-off between the acceptance cone and the performance of the fiber optic system.
