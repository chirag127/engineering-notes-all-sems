### Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a given plane.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of an electric field and a magnetic field that oscillate in phase and are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation of the wave.
- A plane electromagnetic wave can be represented by the following equations:

  - E = E_0 sin(kz - ωt) î
  - B = B_0 sin(kz - ωt) ĵ
  - where E_0 and B_0 are the amplitudes of the electric and magnetic fields, k is the wave number, ω is the angular frequency, z is the direction of propagation, and î and ĵ are the unit vectors along the x and y axes.

- A plane electromagnetic wave travels in a vacuum with a constant speed of c = 3 x 10^8 m/s, which is the same as the speed of light.
- A plane electromagnetic wave satisfies the following relations:

  - c = ω/k = λf
  - E_0/B_0 = c
  - where λ is the wavelength and f is the frequency of the wave.

- A plane electromagnetic wave is transverse in nature, meaning that the electric and magnetic fields vibrate in planes perpendicular to the direction of propagation of the wave.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can be explained by Gauss's law for electric and magnetic fields, which state that the net electric and magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero.
- This implies that the electric and magnetic fields cannot have any component along the direction of propagation of the wave, otherwise there would be a net flux through a cylindrical surface enclosing the wave.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can also be demonstrated by using a polarizer, which is a device that allows only the electric or magnetic field component along a certain direction to pass through.
- If a plane electromagnetic wave is incident on a polarizer, only the component of the electric or magnetic field that is parallel to the polarizer's axis will be transmitted, while the perpendicular component will be blocked.
- This shows that the electric and magnetic fields of a plane electromagnetic wave can be resolved into two orthogonal components that vibrate in different planes.
