### Interference in uniform and wedge shaped thin films

- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves of the same frequency and phase, resulting in a new wave pattern.
- Interference can occur in thin films of transparent materials, such as air, water, oil, soap, etc., when light is reflected and transmitted at the boundaries of the film.
- The interference pattern depends on the thickness of the film, the wavelength of light, and the refractive indices of the film and the surrounding media.
- There are two types of thin films: uniform and wedge shaped.

#### Uniform thin films

- A uniform thin film is a film of constant thickness, such as a soap bubble or an oil slick on water.
- When light is incident on a uniform thin film, some of it is reflected at the first boundary (air-film) and some of it is transmitted into the film. The transmitted light is then partially reflected at the second boundary (film-water) and partially transmitted into the water. The reflected light from both boundaries can interfere constructively or destructively, depending on the path difference and the phase change upon reflection.
- The path difference between the two reflected rays is given by:

$$\Delta = 2t \cos r$$

where $t$ is the thickness of the film, $r$ is the angle of refraction in the film, and $\cos r$ is the correction factor for oblique incidence.

- The phase change upon reflection depends on whether the light is reflected from a medium of higher or lower refractive index than the incident medium. If the light is reflected from a higher refractive index medium, there is a phase change of $\pi$ radians (or half a wavelength). If the light is reflected from a lower refractive index medium, there is no phase change.
- The condition for constructive interference is:

$$\Delta = m \lambda + \frac{\lambda}{2}$$

where $m$ is an integer and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light in the film. The extra term $\frac{\lambda}{2}$ accounts for the phase change at the first boundary.

- The condition for destructive interference is:

$$\Delta = m \lambda$$

where $m$ is an integer and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light in the film. There is no extra term because there is no phase change at the second boundary.

- The interference pattern of a uniform thin film is a series of concentric bright and dark rings, called Newton's rings, when viewed from above. The radius of the $m$th ring is given by:

$$R_m = \sqrt{m \lambda R}$$

where $R$ is the radius of curvature of the film.
