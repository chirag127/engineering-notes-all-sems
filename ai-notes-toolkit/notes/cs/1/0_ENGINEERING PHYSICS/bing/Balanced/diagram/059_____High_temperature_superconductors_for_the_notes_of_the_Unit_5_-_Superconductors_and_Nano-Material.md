### High temperature superconductors

- High temperature superconductors (HTS) are materials that behave as superconductors at temperatures above 77 K (-196.2 °C), the boiling point of liquid nitrogen.
- Superconductors are materials that have zero electrical resistance and can expel magnetic fields when cooled below a critical temperature (Tc).
- HTS have higher Tc than conventional superconductors, which usually have Tc below 30 K (-243.2 °C).
- HTS have potential applications in power transmission, magnetic levitation, quantum computing, and medical imaging.
- The main class of HTS is copper oxides combined with other metals, especially the rare-earth barium copper oxides (REBCOs) such as yttrium barium copper oxide (YBCO).
- The second class of HTS is the iron-based compounds, such as iron pnictides and iron chalcogenides.
- The mechanism of HTS is still not fully understood, but it involves the formation of Cooper pairs, which are pairs of electrons that are bound together by a quantum phenomenon called superexchange .
- The Cooper pairs can move through the lattice of the material without resistance, as they are in a coherent quantum state.
- HTS are usually fabricated by thin film deposition, powder-in-tube, or melt-textured growth methods.
- HTS are characterized by various techniques, such as resistivity, magnetization, specific heat, and scanning tunneling microscopy.
