### Spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation

- Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state decays to a lower energy state by emitting a photon without the aid of external agency.
- Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state is induced to emit a photon by interacting with another photon of the same frequency.
- The properties of photons generated by spontaneous and stimulated emission are quite different:
  - Spontaneous emission takes place without interaction with other photons, and the direction and phase are random. The emitted radiation is incoherent and has a broad spectrum .
  - Stimulated emission takes place when the excited electron interacts with another photon. The emitted photon has the same frequency, phase, polarization, and direction of propagation as the incident photon. The emitted radiation is coherent and has a narrow spectrum  .
- Spontaneous emission is the basis of fluorescence and phosphorescence, while stimulated emission is the basis of laser action .
