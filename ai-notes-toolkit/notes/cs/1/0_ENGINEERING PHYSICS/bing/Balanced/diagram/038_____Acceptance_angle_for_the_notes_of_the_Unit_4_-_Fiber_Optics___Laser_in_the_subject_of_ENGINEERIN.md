### Acceptance angle

- The acceptance angle of an optical fiber is defined based on a purely geometrical consideration (ray optics): it is the maximum angle of a ray (against the fiber axis) hitting the fiber core which allows the incident light to be guided by the core .
- The acceptance angle depends on the refractive indices of the core, the cladding, and the surrounding medium (usually air). It can be calculated using Snell's law and the condition for total internal reflection .
- The acceptance angle is related to the numerical aperture (NA) of the fiber by the equation NA = n0 sin θa, where n0 is the refractive index of the surrounding medium and θa is the acceptance angle .
- The acceptance angle determines the cone of light that can be coupled into the fiber. The larger the acceptance angle, the more light can be launched into the fiber .
- The acceptance angle also affects the bandwidth and dispersion of the fiber. The smaller the acceptance angle, the lower the number of modes that can propagate in the fiber, and the higher the bandwidth and lower the dispersion .
