### Diffraction grating

- A diffraction grating is an optical component with a periodic structure that diffracts light into several beams travelling in different directions (i.e., different diffraction angles).
- A diffraction grating can be a glass plate or a mirror with a large number of equidistant parallel lines or grooves on its surface. It causes diffraction of transmitted or reflected light, ultraviolet radiation, or X-rays.
- A diffraction grating can produce a spectrum by diffraction and interference of light. The emerging coloration is a form of structural coloration.
- A diffraction grating can be used to measure the wavelength of light, to analyze the spectral composition of light sources, to separate different wavelengths of light, and to create optical effects.
- A diffraction grating can be classified into two types: transmission grating and reflection grating, depending on whether the light is transmitted or reflected by the grating.
- A diffraction grating can be characterized by its grating constant, which is the distance between adjacent grooves, and its blaze angle, which is the angle of the grooves with respect to the normal to the grating surface.
- A diffraction grating can be analyzed using the grating equation, which relates the diffraction angle, the wavelength of light, the grating constant, and the order of diffraction.
