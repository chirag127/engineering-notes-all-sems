### Resolving power of grating

- Resolving power of grating is the ability of a grating to separate two spectral lines of very close wavelengths into distinct maxima.
- Resolving power of grating is measured by the ratio of the mean wavelength to the smallest difference in wavelengths that can be resolved, i.e. R.P. = λ/dλ .
- Resolving power of grating depends on the number of lines per unit length of the grating, the order of diffraction, and the width of the grating .
- Resolving power of grating can be derived from the condition of minimum angular separation between two spectral lines of wavelengths λ and λ + dλ in a given order of diffraction n.
- The condition is given by d(sinθ + sinθ') = n(λ + dλ) - nλ, where d is the grating element, θ is the angle of incidence, and θ' is the angle of diffraction.
- Simplifying the condition, we get d(cosθ + cosθ')dθ' = ndλ, or d(cosθ + cosθ')/n = dλ/λ.
- Assuming that the angular separation is small, we can approximate cosθ + cosθ' ≈ 2cosθ, and dθ' ≈ dλ/λ.
- Therefore, the resolving power of grating is given by R.P. = λ/dλ = nN, where N is the total number of lines on the grating.
- The resolving power of grating increases with the order of diffraction and the number of lines on the grating .
- A standard benchmark for the resolving power of grating is the resolution of the sodium doublet, which are two spectral lines of sodium at 589.00 nm and 589.59 nm.
- Another standard example is the resolution of the hydrogen and deuterium lines, which are at 656.28 nm and 656.10 nm respectively.
- A grating's resolving power may also be specified by its resolvance, which is the product of the resolving power and the mean wavelength, i.e. R = nNd.
- A grating's resolvance is a constant that depends only on the grating element and not on the wavelength or the order of diffraction.
- A grating's resolvance is related to the system's spectral resolution, which is the minimum difference in wavelengths that can be resolved by the system.
- A grating's spectral resolution depends on the grating's resolvance, the focal length of the system, and the size of the detector.
