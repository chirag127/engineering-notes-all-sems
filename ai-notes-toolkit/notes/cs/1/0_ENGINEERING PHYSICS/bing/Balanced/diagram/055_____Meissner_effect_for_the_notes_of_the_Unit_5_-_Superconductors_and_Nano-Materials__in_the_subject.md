### Meissner effect

- The Meissner effect is the expulsion of a magnetic field from the interior of a material that is in the process of becoming a superconductor .
- A superconductor is a material that loses its resistance to the flow of electrical currents when cooled below a certain temperature, called the critical temperature.
- The Meissner effect was discovered by the German physicists W. Meissner and R. Ochsenfeld in 1933 .
- The Meissner effect is a property of all superconductors and it is one of the characteristic features that distinguish a superconductor from a perfect conductor.
- The Meissner effect can be demonstrated by making a magnet levitate above a superconductor, as seen in the figure below.

![Figure 1: Levitating magnet above a superconductor](https://eng.libretexts.org/@api/deki/files/193911/Meissner_effect.png)

- The Meissner effect can be explained by the London theory, which states that the superconducting electrons form a thin layer near the surface of the material that cancels out the applied magnetic field inside the material.
- The Meissner effect can also be understood by the concept of magnetic flux quantization, which states that the magnetic flux through a superconducting loop is quantized and can only change by discrete units.
- The Meissner effect has important applications in superconducting devices, such as magnetic levitation trains, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs).
