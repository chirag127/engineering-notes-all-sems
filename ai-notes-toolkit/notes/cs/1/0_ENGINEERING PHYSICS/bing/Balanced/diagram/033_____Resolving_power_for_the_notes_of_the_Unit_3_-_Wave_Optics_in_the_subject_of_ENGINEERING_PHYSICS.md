### Resolving power

- Resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to separate two adjacent points or objects from each other from a considerable distance.
- Resolving power depends on the wavelength of the light used and the aperture of the instrument.
- The smaller the wavelength and the larger the aperture, the higher the resolving power.
- Resolving power is measured by the minimum angular separation between two points or objects that can be resolved by the instrument.
- Resolving power is also known as the chromatic resolution when applied to a system of grating.
- Resolving power is inversely proportional to the angular separation, so the higher the resolving power, the smaller the angular separation.

#### Resolving power formula

- There is no generalized formula for resolving power of an optical instrument. It depends from one instrument to another.
- For a telescope, the resolving power formula is:

`R = d / (1.22 λ)`

where `R` is the resolving power, `d` is the diameter of the objective lens, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light used.

- For a microscope, the resolving power formula is:

`R = 2 n sin θ / λ`

where `R` is the resolving power, `n` is the refractive index of the medium between the object and the objective lens, `θ` is the half-angle of the cone of light entering the objective lens, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light used.

- For a prism, the resolving power formula is:

`R = λ / dλ`

where `R` is the resolving power, `λ` is the wavelength of the spectral line, and `dλ` is the difference between the wavelengths of two adjacent spectral lines.

- For a system of grating, the resolving power formula is:

`R = λ / Δλ`

where `R` is the resolving power, `λ` is the wavelength of the spectral line, and `Δλ` is the difference between the wavelengths of two adjacent spectral lines.

#### Resolving power unit

- Resolving power has no specific unit, as it is a dimensionless quantity.
- Resolving power is usually expressed as a ratio or a fraction.
- For example, the resolving power of a telescope can be written as 1/0.5 arcsec, which means that the telescope can resolve two points or objects that are 0.5 arcsec apart.

#### Resolving power applications

- Resolving power is an important parameter for optical instruments, as it determines the quality and clarity of the images they produce.
- Resolving power is used to compare the performance and efficiency of different optical instruments, such as telescopes, microscopes, prisms, and gratings.
- Resolving power is also used to study the spectral lines of different elements and compounds, as it helps to identify and separate the closely spaced wavelengths.
- Resolving power is also relevant for the phenomenon of wave nature of electrons, as proposed by Louis de Broglie. The resolving power of an electron microscope is much higher than that of an optical microscope, as the wavelength of an electron is much smaller than that of visible light.
