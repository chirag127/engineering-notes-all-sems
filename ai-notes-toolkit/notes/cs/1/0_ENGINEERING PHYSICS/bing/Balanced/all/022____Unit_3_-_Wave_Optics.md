## Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also called physical optics, is a branch of optics that studies the behavior of light when it exhibits wave characteristics, such as interference, diffraction, polarization, and scattering  .
- Wave optics is applicable when the size of the objects or the openings that light interacts with is comparable to or smaller than the wavelength of light .
- Some of the important phenomena and concepts in wave optics are:

  - Huygens' principle: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets  .
  - Interference: The superposition of two or more coherent waves results in a new wave whose amplitude depends on the relative phase of the waves  .
  - Young's double-slit experiment: A demonstration of interference where a monochromatic light source passes through two narrow slits and forms a pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen  .
  - Diffraction: The bending of light around the edges of an obstacle or an opening  .
  - Single-slit diffraction: A demonstration of diffraction where a monochromatic light source passes through a narrow slit and forms a pattern of a central bright fringe and several secondary fringes on a screen  .
  - Diffraction grating: A device that consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits and produces a spectrum of colors when light passes through it  .
  - Polarization: The orientation of the electric field vector of a light wave  .
  - Malus' law: The intensity of a polarized light beam that passes through a polarizer is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle between the polarization direction of the beam and the transmission axis of the polarizer  .
  - Brewster's angle: The angle of incidence at which a light beam is completely polarized in the plane perpendicular to the plane of incidence when it reflects from a transparent medium  .
  - Scattering: The redirection of light by particles in the medium  .
  - Rayleigh scattering: The scattering of light by particles that are much smaller than the wavelength of light, which results in a blue color of the sky and a red color of the sunset  .
  - Thin-film interference: The interference of light waves that reflect from the upper and lower surfaces of a thin film, which results in iridescent colors  .

- Some of the important formulas and equations in wave optics are:

  - The wavelength of light in a medium: $\lambda = \frac{\lambda_0}{n}$, where $\lambda_0$ is the wavelength in vacuum and $n$ is the refractive index of the medium  .
  - The path difference of two waves: $\Delta x = d \sin \theta$, where $d$ is the distance between the sources and $\theta$ is the angle of observation  .
