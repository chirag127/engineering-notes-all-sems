# Persistent Current in Superconductors

- Persistent current is the electric current that flows indefinitely in a closed loop of superconducting material without any external power source.
- Persistent current is possible in superconductors because they have zero resistance and no energy dissipation  .
- Persistent current can be induced in a superconducting ring by placing it in a magnetic field above its critical temperature and then cooling it below its critical temperature. The magnetic flux through the ring is then trapped and the current circulates to maintain it .
- Persistent current can also be induced in a superconducting ring by applying an electric field across a weak link or a junction in the ring. The current flows through the weak link and creates a phase difference between the two ends of the ring. The phase difference determines the magnitude and direction of the persistent current .
- Persistent current is a quantum phenomenon that depends on the coherence and interference of the superconducting wave function .
- Persistent current has applications in superconducting magnets, quantum computing, and metrology .
