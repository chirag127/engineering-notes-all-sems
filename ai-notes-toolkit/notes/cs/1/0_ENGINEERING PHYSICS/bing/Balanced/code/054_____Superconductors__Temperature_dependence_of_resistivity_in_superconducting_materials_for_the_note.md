### Superconductors: Temperature dependence of resistivity in superconducting materials

- Superconductivity is a phenomenon in certain metals and ceramics where the resistivity of the material drops to zero below a certain critical temperature known as Tc .
- The resistivity of materials depends on the temperature. The equation ρt = ρ0 [1 + α (T – T0) shows the relation between the temperature and the resistivity for any material .
- In the equation, ρ0 is the resistivity at a standard temperature, ρt is the resistivity at t 0 C, T0 is the reference temperature and α is the temperature coefficient of resistivity.
- For normal metals, α is positive, which means that the resistivity increases with increasing temperature. This is because the thermal vibrations of the atoms in the lattice interfere with the flow of electrons and cause scattering.
- For superconductors, α is negative, which means that the resistivity decreases with increasing temperature until it reaches zero at Tc. This is because the electrons form pairs called Cooper pairs that can move through the lattice without resistance.
- The value of Tc depends on the type and structure of the superconducting material. For most industrial superconductors, Tc is below 12 K, while for some high-temperature superconductors, Tc can reach up to 135 K.
