## Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory

- Electromagnetic field theory is the study of the interaction between electric and magnetic fields, and the sources and effects of these fields.
- Electric fields are produced by stationary electric charges, and magnetic fields are produced by moving electric charges or changing electric fields.
- The basic laws of electromagnetism are described by Maxwell's equations, which relate the electric and magnetic fields to their sources and to each other.
- Maxwell's equations also imply that electromagnetic waves can propagate in free space or in a medium, carrying energy and momentum.
- Electromagnetic waves have different frequencies and wavelengths, which determine their properties and applications, such as radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.
- Electromagnetic fields can interact with matter, affecting its electric and magnetic properties, such as polarization, magnetization, permittivity, and permeability.
- Electromagnetic fields can also exert forces on electric charges and currents, such as the Lorentz force, the electric force, and the magnetic force.
- Electromagnetic field theory has many applications in physics, engineering, and technology, such as electric circuits, antennas, motors, generators, transformers, lasers, optical devices, communication systems, and medical imaging.
