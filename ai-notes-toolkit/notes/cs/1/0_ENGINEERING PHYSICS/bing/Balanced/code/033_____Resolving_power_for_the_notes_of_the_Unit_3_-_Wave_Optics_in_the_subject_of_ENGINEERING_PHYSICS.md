### Resolving power

- Resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to separate two adjacent points or objects from each other from a considerable distance.
- Resolving power depends on the wavelength of the light used and the aperture of the instrument.
- The smaller the angular separation between two points that can be resolved by an instrument, the higher is its resolving power.
- Resolving power is measured in radians or degrees.
- Resolving power can be calculated using different formulas for different optical instruments, such as telescope, microscope, prism and grating.
- For a telescope, the resolving power is given by:

    `R = d / (1.22 λ)`

    where `d` is the diameter of the objective lens, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light.

- For a microscope, the resolving power is given by:

    `R = 2 n sin θ / λ`

    where `n` is the refractive index of the medium between the objective lens and the specimen, `θ` is the half-angle of the cone of light entering the objective lens, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light.

- For a prism, the resolving power is given by:

    `R = λ / dλ`

    where `λ` is the wavelength of the spectral line, and `dλ` is the difference between the wavelengths of two adjacent spectral lines.

- For a grating, the resolving power is given by:

    `R = N m`

    where `N` is the total number of slits in the grating, and `m` is the order of diffraction.
