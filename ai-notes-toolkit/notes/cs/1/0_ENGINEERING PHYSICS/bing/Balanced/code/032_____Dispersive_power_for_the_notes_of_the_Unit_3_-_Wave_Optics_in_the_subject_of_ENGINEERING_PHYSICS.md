### Dispersive Power

- Dispersive power is a measure of how much a material can separate different wavelengths of light by refraction.
- Dispersive power of a prism is defined as the ratio of angular dispersion to mean deviation produced by the prism  .
- Angular dispersion is the difference between the angles of deviation of the two extreme colors (usually violet and red) of the spectrum.
- Mean deviation is the angle of deviation of the mean ray (the ray having the mean wavelength) of the spectrum.
- The formula for dispersive power is given as:

```math
\omega = \frac{\theta}{\delta}
```

where $\omega$ is the dispersive power, $\theta$ is the angular dispersion, and $\delta$ is the mean deviation.

- The dispersive power of a prism depends on the material of the prism and the refractive indices of the different wavelengths of light.
- The dispersive power of a prism is inversely proportional to the refractive index of the mean ray .
- The formula for dispersive power in terms of refractive indices is given as :

```math
\omega = \frac{\mu - 1}{\mu_V - \mu_R}
```

where $\mu$ is the refractive index of the mean ray, $\mu_V$ is the refractive index of the violet ray, and $\mu_R$ is the refractive index of the red ray.

- Example: Calculate the dispersive power of a prism if the refractive indices of violet, yellow, and red rays are 1.6, 1.56, and 1.52 respectively .
- Solution: The refractive index of the mean ray is the average of the refractive indices of the violet and red rays, i.e.,

```math
\mu = \frac{\mu_V + \mu_R}{2} = \frac{1.6 + 1.52}{2} = 1.56
```

The dispersive power of the prism is then

```math
\omega = \frac{\mu - 1}{\mu_V - \mu_R} = \frac{1.56 - 1}{1.6 - 1.52} = 0.14
```
