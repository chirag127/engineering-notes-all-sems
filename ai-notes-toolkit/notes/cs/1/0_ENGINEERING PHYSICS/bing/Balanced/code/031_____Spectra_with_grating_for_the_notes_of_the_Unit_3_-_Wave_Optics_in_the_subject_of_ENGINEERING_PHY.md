### Spectra with grating

- A diffraction grating is a device that splits a beam of light into its component wavelengths or colors. It consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits or grooves on a transparent or reflective surface.
- The principle of diffraction grating is based on the interference of light waves from the slits. The path difference between the waves from adjacent slits determines the constructive or destructive interference at a given angle.
- The grating equation relates the wavelength of the light, the grating spacing, the angle of incidence and the angle of diffraction. It is given by:

$$d(\sin i + \sin r) = m\lambda$$

where $d$ is the grating spacing, $i$ is the angle of incidence, $r$ is the angle of diffraction, $m$ is the order of the spectrum and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light.
- The grating spectrum is the pattern of bright and dark lines or bands formed by the diffraction grating. The spectrum can be observed in the focal plane of a lens or a telescope. The spectrum is symmetrical about the zeroth order, which corresponds to the undeviated beam. The higher orders are more dispersed and fainter than the lower orders.
- The grating spectrometer is an instrument that uses a diffraction grating to analyze the spectrum of a light source. It consists of a collimator, a grating, a telescope and a scale. The collimator produces a parallel beam of light from the source. The grating diffracts the beam into different angles according to the grating equation. The telescope focuses the diffracted beam onto the scale, where the wavelength or the frequency of the light can be measured.
- The resolving power of a grating is the ability to distinguish two closely spaced wavelengths or frequencies in the spectrum. It is given by:

$$R = \frac{\lambda}{\Delta \lambda} = mN$$

where $\lambda$ is the average wavelength, $\Delta \lambda$ is the smallest wavelength difference that can be resolved, $m$ is the order of the spectrum and $N$ is the number of slits or grooves in the grating.
- The diffraction grating can be used to study the emission and absorption spectra of various light sources, such as atoms, molecules, stars, etc. The emission spectrum consists of bright lines or bands on a dark background, corresponding to the wavelengths or frequencies emitted by the source. The absorption spectrum consists of dark lines or bands on a bright background, corresponding to the wavelengths or frequencies absorbed by the source. The spectra can reveal the chemical composition, temperature, pressure and motion of the source.
