### Population inversion for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Population inversion is a state of a system, for example a laser gain medium, where a higher-lying energy level is more strongly populated than a lower-lying level.
- Population inversion is a necessary condition for all lasers, regardless of their power or wavelength, because all lasers operate on the same basic principle of a chain reaction.
- Population inversion cannot occur under conditions of thermal equilibrium, where the level populations are described by a Boltzmann distribution.
- Population inversion can be achieved by various methods, such as optical pumping, electrical pumping, chemical pumping, or collisional pumping.
- The population inversion in laser principle simply involves the de-excitation of an atom to a lower energy state, under the influence of the electromagnetic field of a photon of frequency f. During this de-excitation, the excited electron emits a photon in addition to the incident photon having the same frequency f.
- The emitted photon and the incident photon are coherent, meaning they have the same phase, direction, and polarization. This process is called stimulated emission.
- The stimulated emission leads to a chain reaction of photon amplification, as the emitted photons can stimulate more atoms to de-excite and emit more photons. This is the basis of laser action.
- To sustain the population inversion and the laser action, the system must be continuously pumped with energy to excite more atoms to the higher energy level.
- The population inversion can be quantified by the ratio of the number of atoms in the higher energy level (N2) to the number of atoms in the lower energy level (N1). When N2 > N1, the population inversion is achieved.
- The population inversion can also be expressed by the inversion factor, which is defined as (N2 - N1) / (N2 + N1). The inversion factor ranges from -1 (no inversion) to +1 (complete inversion).
