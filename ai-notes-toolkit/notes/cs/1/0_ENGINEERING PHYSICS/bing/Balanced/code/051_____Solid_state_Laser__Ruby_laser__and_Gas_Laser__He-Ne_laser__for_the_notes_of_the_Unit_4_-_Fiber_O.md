### Solid state Laser (Ruby laser) and Gas Laser (He-Ne laser)

- A solid state laser is a laser that uses a solid material as its gain medium, where the atoms are excited by an external source of energy to emit light. 
- A ruby laser is a type of solid state laser that uses a synthetic ruby crystal as its gain medium. It was the first working laser invented by Theodore H. Maiman in 1960.  
- A ruby laser produces pulses of coherent visible light at a wavelength of 694.3 nm, which is a deep red color, with a very narrow linewidth of 0.53 nm. It is a three level laser, meaning that it has three energy levels for the electrons in the ruby atoms: ground state, metastable state, and excited state.  
- The construction of a ruby laser consists of the following components: 
  - A ruby rod, which is the active medium that provides the gain for the laser. The rod is doped with chromium ions that act as the lasing atoms. The rod is polished and coated with silver or gold on the ends to act as mirrors that reflect the light back and forth inside the rod.
  - A flash lamp, which is the pump source that provides the energy to excite the chromium ions in the ruby rod. The flash lamp is wrapped around the ruby rod and emits a bright flash of white light when triggered by an electric current.
  - A resonator, which is the optical cavity that forms the laser beam. The resonator consists of the two mirrors on the ends of the ruby rod and a partially reflecting mirror that allows some of the light to escape as the output beam.
  - A power supply, which is the electrical circuit that provides the current to the flash lamp and controls the timing and duration of the flashes.
- The working of a ruby laser is as follows: 
  - When the flash lamp is triggered, it emits a burst of white light that passes through the ruby rod and excites the chromium ions from the ground state to the excited state.
  - Some of the excited chromium ions quickly decay back to the ground state, emitting photons of various wavelengths. Some of these photons have the same wavelength as the ruby laser, 694.3 nm, and are called the spontaneous emission.
  - Some of the excited chromium ions stay in the metastable state for a longer time, creating a population inversion between the metastable state and the ground state. This means that there are more atoms in the higher energy state than in the lower energy state, which is necessary for laser action.
  - The photons of the spontaneous emission that have the same wavelength and direction as the ruby laser are reflected by the mirrors on the ends of the ruby rod, traveling back and forth inside the rod. These photons stimulate the chromium ions in the metastable state to emit more photons of the same wavelength and phase, creating a coherent beam of light. This is called the stimulated emission.
  - The stimulated emission amplifies the light intensity inside the rod, forming a laser beam. Some of the laser beam escapes through the partially reflecting mirror as the output of the ruby laser.
  - The ruby laser produces short pulses of light, typically lasting a few milliseconds, because the population inversion is quickly depleted by the stimulated emission and the flash lamp has a limited duration. The pulse repetition rate depends on the power supply and the cooling system of the laser.

- A gas laser is a laser that uses a gas as its gain medium, where the atoms or molecules are excited by an electric current or another laser to emit light. 
- A helium-neon laser (He-Ne laser) is a type of gas laser that uses a mixture of helium and neon gas as its gain medium. It was the first continuous wave laser invented by Ali Javan, William Bennett, and Donald Herriott in 1961.  
- A He-Ne laser produces a continuous beam of coherent light at a wavelength of 632.8 nm, which is a bright red color, with a very narrow linewidth of 0.0015 nm. It is a four level laser, meaning that it has four energy levels for the electrons in the neon atoms: ground state, first excited state, second excited state, and third excited state.
