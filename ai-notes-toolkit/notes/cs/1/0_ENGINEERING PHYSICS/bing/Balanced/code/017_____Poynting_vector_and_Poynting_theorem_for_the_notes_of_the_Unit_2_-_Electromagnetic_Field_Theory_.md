### Poynting vector and Poynting theorem

- The Poynting vector is a vector quantity that represents the directional energy flux of an electromagnetic field. It is defined as the cross product of the electric field and the magnetic field vectors:

  `S = E x H`

  where S is the Poynting vector, E is the electric field vector, and H is the magnetic field vector.

- The Poynting vector has the units of watts per square meter (W/m^2) and indicates the amount and direction of energy flow per unit area perpendicular to the direction of propagation of the electromagnetic wave.

- The Poynting theorem is a conservation law that relates the change in energy density of an electromagnetic field to the work done by the field on the charges and currents in the medium, and the energy flux across the boundary of the medium. It can be stated as:

  `dU/dt + div S = -W - Q`

  where U is the energy density of the electromagnetic field, S is the Poynting vector, W is the work done by the field per unit volume, Q is the heat generated by the field per unit volume, and div is the divergence operator.

- The Poynting theorem can be derived from Maxwell's equations and the Lorentz force law. It can be applied to any closed volume containing an electromagnetic field and a medium with charges and currents.

- The Poynting theorem can be used to calculate the power delivered by an electromagnetic wave to a load, such as an antenna or a circuit. It can also be used to analyze the energy balance and efficiency of various electromagnetic devices and systems.
