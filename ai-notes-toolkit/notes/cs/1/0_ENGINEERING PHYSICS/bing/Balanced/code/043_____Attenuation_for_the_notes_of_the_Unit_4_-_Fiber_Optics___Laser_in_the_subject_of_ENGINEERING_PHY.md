### Attenuation

- Attenuation is the reduction in intensity of the light beam (or signal) with respect to distance travelled through a transmission medium .
- Attenuation is measured in decibels (dB) and is calculated by the formula: `Attenuation (dB) = 10 x Log 10 (P in/P out) = 20xLog 10 (V in/V out)` where `P in` and `P out` are the input and output power, and `V in` and `V out` are the input and output voltage.
- Attenuation limits the distance in which the signal can travel through optical fiber and affects the performance and reliability of the fiber optic network.
- Attenuation can be caused by intrinsic and extrinsic factors .
  - Intrinsic factors are inherent to the fiber optic material and include:
    - Absorption: the conversion of optical power into heat due to the interaction of light with the molecular structure of the fiber . Absorption depends on the wavelength of the light and the impurities in the fiber.
    - Scattering: the deflection of light from its original direction due to the microscopic variations in the density and refractive index of the fiber . Scattering also depends on the wavelength of the light and the diameter of the fiber.
    - Dispersion: the spreading of light pulses over time due to the different velocities of different wavelengths or modes of light in the fiber . Dispersion causes distortion and interference of the signal and can be classified into modal, chromatic and polarization mode dispersion.
  - Extrinsic factors are external to the fiber optic material and include:
    - Bending: the loss of light due to the curvature of the fiber beyond its minimum bend radius . Bending can be macroscopic (visible) or microscopic (invisible) and can cause stress and damage to the fiber.
    - Splicing: the joining of two fiber ends by fusion or mechanical means . Splicing can cause loss of light due to the misalignment, gap, angle or air bubbles between the fiber ends.
    - Connectors: the devices that connect the fiber to the source, receiver or other fibers . Connectors can cause loss of light due to the reflection, dirt, damage or mismatch of the connector types.
- Attenuation can be reduced by choosing the appropriate fiber type, wavelength, source, receiver, splice, connector and installation method . Attenuation can also be compensated by using optical amplifiers or repeaters.
