### Fraunhofer diffraction at single slit

- Fraunhofer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when plane waves are incident on a diffracting object, and the diffraction pattern is viewed at a sufficiently long distance from the object (in the far-field region), or at the focal plane of a converging lens.
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit is a phenomenon where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength `λ` is incident on a slit of width `d`, and the diffracted light is observed on a screen or at the focal plane of a lens .
- According to Huygen's principle, every point on the slit acts as a secondary source of spherical waves, and the interference of these waves produces the diffraction pattern on the screen or at the focal plane .
- The intensity of the diffracted light at an angle `θ` from the center of the slit is given by the formula :

```math
I(\theta) = I_0 \left(\frac{\sin \beta}{\beta}\right)^2
```

where `I_0` is the intensity at the center of the slit, `β = (πd/λ) sin θ`, and `d` is the slit width.

- The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright maximum, followed by alternate dark and bright fringes of decreasing intensity on both sides .
- The angular width of the central maximum is given by the formula :

```math
\theta_0 = \frac{2\lambda}{d}
```

where `λ` is the wavelength of the light, and `d` is the slit width.

- The angular position of the first dark fringe on either side of the central maximum is given by the formula :

```math
\theta_1 = \frac{\lambda}{d}
```

where `λ` is the wavelength of the light, and `d` is the slit width.

- The angular position of the nth dark fringe on either side of the central maximum is given by the formula :

```math
\theta_n = \frac{n\lambda}{d}
```

where `n` is an integer, `λ` is the wavelength of the light, and `d` is the slit width.
