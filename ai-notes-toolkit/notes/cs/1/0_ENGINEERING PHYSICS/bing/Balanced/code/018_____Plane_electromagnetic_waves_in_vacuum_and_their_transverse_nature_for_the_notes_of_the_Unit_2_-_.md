### Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of an electric field and a magnetic field that oscillate in phase and are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave can be represented by the following equations:

  - E = E_0 sin(kz - wt + f) i
  - B = B_0 sin(kz - wt + f) j

  where E_0 and B_0 are the amplitudes of the electric and magnetic fields, k is the wave number, w is the angular frequency, f is the phase constant, i and j are the unit vectors along the x and y axes, and z is the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave travels in vacuum with a constant speed c, which is equal to the speed of light, c = 3 x 10^8 m/s.
- A plane electromagnetic wave satisfies the following relations:

  - c = w/k
  - E_0/B_0 = c
  - k = 2p/l
  - w = 2pf

  where l is the wavelength and f is the frequency of the wave.
- A plane electromagnetic wave is transverse in nature, meaning that the electric and magnetic fields vibrate in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation. This can be shown by applying Gauss's law for electric and magnetic fields, which state that the net flux of these fields through any closed surface is zero.
- If we consider a cylindrical surface with its axis along the direction of propagation of the wave, the net flux of the electric field through the curved surface is zero, since the electric field is parallel to the surface. Similarly, the net flux of the magnetic field through the curved surface is zero, since the magnetic field is parallel to the surface.
- The net flux of the electric field through the flat ends of the cylinder is also zero, since the electric field is perpendicular to the ends and has equal magnitude and opposite direction on both ends. Similarly, the net flux of the magnetic field through the flat ends of the cylinder is also zero, since the magnetic field is perpendicular to the ends and has equal magnitude and opposite direction on both ends.
- Therefore, the net flux of the electric and magnetic fields through any closed surface enclosing the wave is zero, which implies that the electric and magnetic fields are transverse to the direction of propagation.
