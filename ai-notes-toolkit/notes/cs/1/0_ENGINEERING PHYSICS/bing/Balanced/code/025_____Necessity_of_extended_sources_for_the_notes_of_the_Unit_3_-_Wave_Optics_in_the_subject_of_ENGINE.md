### Necessity of extended sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also known as physical optics, is the branch of physics that studies the interference, diffraction, polarization, and other phenomena of light waves.
- Wave optics is based on the principle of superposition, which states that the resultant displacement of a point due to two or more waves is equal to the vector sum of the individual displacements due to each wave.
- To observe the phenomena of wave optics, such as interference and diffraction, the light waves must be coherent, meaning that they have a constant phase difference and the same frequency and wavelength.
- Coherent light sources are usually obtained from lasers, which produce monochromatic and highly collimated beams of light. However, lasers are not always available or convenient for experimental purposes.
- Therefore, extended sources, such as light bulbs, are often used as alternatives to produce coherent light. Extended sources are sources that have a finite size and emit light in all directions.
- However, extended sources have some drawbacks, such as:
  - They produce incoherent light, meaning that the light waves emitted from different points of the source have random phase differences and may have different frequencies and wavelengths.
  - They produce divergent light, meaning that the light rays spread out as they propagate and become less intense.
  - They produce polychromatic light, meaning that the light waves have a range of frequencies and wavelengths, resulting in different colors.
- To overcome these drawbacks, extended sources are modified by using some optical devices, such as:
  - Slits, which are narrow openings that allow only a small portion of the light to pass through, creating a coherent and collimated beam of light.
  - Filters, which are transparent materials that selectively transmit or absorb certain wavelengths of light, creating a monochromatic beam of light.
  - Lenses, which are curved transparent materials that refract light rays, creating a convergent or divergent beam of light.
- By using these optical devices, extended sources can be used to produce coherent, collimated, and monochromatic light, which is suitable for observing the phenomena of wave optics.
