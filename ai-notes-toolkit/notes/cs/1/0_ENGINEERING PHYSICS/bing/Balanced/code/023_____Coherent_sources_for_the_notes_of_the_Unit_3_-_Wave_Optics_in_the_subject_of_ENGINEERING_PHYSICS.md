### Coherent sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Wave optics is the branch of physics that studies the nature and behavior of light as a wave phenomenon.
- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves that results in a new wave pattern.
- Coherent sources are the sources of waves that have the same frequency, wavelength and phase or a constant phase difference.
- Coherent sources are necessary for producing a stable interference pattern, where the positions of maxima and minima are fixed.
- Examples of coherent sources are:
  - Two slits illuminated by a single monochromatic light source, such as a laser. The light waves from the slits have the same frequency and a constant phase difference due to the path difference.
  - Two vibrating tuning forks of the same frequency and amplitude. The sound waves from the forks have the same frequency and a constant phase difference due to the distance between them.
  - Two antennas transmitting radio waves of the same frequency and amplitude. The radio waves from the antennas have the same frequency and a constant phase difference due to the orientation of the antennas.
- Coherent sources can be classified into two types:
  - Temporally coherent sources: These are the sources that have a constant phase difference over a certain time interval. The degree of temporal coherence depends on the coherence time, which is the time for which the phase difference remains constant. The coherence time is inversely proportional to the bandwidth or range of frequencies of the source. A monochromatic source has a high coherence time and a high degree of temporal coherence. A white light source has a low coherence time and a low degree of temporal coherence.
  - Spatially coherent sources: These are the sources that have a constant phase difference over a certain spatial region. The degree of spatial coherence depends on the coherence length, which is the distance over which the phase difference remains constant. The coherence length is inversely proportional to the size or aperture of the source. A point source has a high coherence length and a high degree of spatial coherence. An extended source has a low coherence length and a low degree of spatial coherence.
- Coherent sources have many applications in physics and engineering, such as:
  - Holography: This is a technique of creating three-dimensional images of objects by recording and reconstructing the interference pattern of coherent light waves reflected or transmitted by the object.
  - Laser: This is a device that produces a coherent, monochromatic and intense beam of light by stimulating the emission of photons from atoms or molecules.
  - Spectroscopy: This is a technique of analyzing the composition and structure of matter by measuring the interaction of coherent light waves with the matter.
  - Communication: This is a process of transmitting and receiving information by using coherent radio waves or optical fibers.
