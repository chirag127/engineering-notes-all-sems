### Acceptance cone

- The acceptance cone is the cone-shaped region of incident light that can enter an optical fiber and be propagated through it .
- The acceptance cone is derived by rotating the acceptance angle about the fiber axis.
- The acceptance angle is the maximum angle with the axis of the optical fiber at which light can enter the fiber and be guided by total internal reflection.
- The acceptance angle depends on the refractive indices of the core, the cladding, and the surrounding medium of the fiber.
- The larger the acceptance cone, the larger the numerical aperture (NA) of the fiber.
- The numerical aperture is a measure of the light-gathering ability and resolution of the fiber.
- The numerical aperture can be calculated by the formula: NA = n_f sin θ_a, where n_f is the refractive index of the surrounding medium, and θ_a is the acceptance angle.
- The numerical aperture can also be approximated by the formula: NA ≅ √(n_c^2 - n_f^2), where n_c and n_f are the refractive indices of the core and the cladding, respectively.
- The acceptance cone and the numerical aperture are important parameters for designing and optimizing fiber optic systems  .
