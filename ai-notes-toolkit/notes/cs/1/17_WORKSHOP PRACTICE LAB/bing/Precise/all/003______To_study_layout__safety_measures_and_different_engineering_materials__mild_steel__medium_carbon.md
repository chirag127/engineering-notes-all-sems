#### To study layout, safety measures and different engineering materials (mild steel, medium carbon steel, high carbon steel, high speed steel and cast iron etc) used in workshop.

1. **Layout**: The layout of a workshop refers to the arrangement of the equipment, machinery, and workstations in the space. A well-designed layout can improve efficiency, safety, and productivity.
2. **Safety measures**: Safety measures in a workshop include the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) such as safety glasses, gloves, and earplugs; proper ventilation and lighting; and the use of safety guards on machinery. Regular safety training and inspections are also important.
3. **Engineering materials**: Different engineering materials are used in a workshop depending on the specific application. Some common materials include:
    - **Mild steel**: A type of carbon steel with a low carbon content. It is relatively soft and ductile, making it easy to shape and weld. It is commonly used for general purpose structural and mechanical applications.
    - **Medium carbon steel**: A type of carbon steel with a higher carbon content than mild steel. It is stronger and harder, but less ductile. It is commonly used for applications that require strength and toughness, such as shafts, gears, and axles.
    - **High carbon steel**: A type of carbon steel with an even higher carbon content. It is very hard and strong, but brittle. It is commonly used for applications that require high strength and wear resistance, such as cutting tools and springs.
    - **High speed steel**: A type of tool steel that can withstand high temperatures without losing its hardness. It is commonly used for cutting tools such as drill bits and saw blades.
    - **Cast iron**: A group of iron-carbon alloys with a carbon content greater than 2%. It is hard and brittle, but has good wear resistance and can be cast into complex shapes. It is commonly used for engine blocks, pipes, and machine frames.
