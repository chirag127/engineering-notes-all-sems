### 2 Machine Shop 3 Hrs

Machine shop is a place where various machines and tools are used to perform different operations on metal workpieces, such as cutting, drilling, milling, turning, grinding, etc. Machine shop can also refer to the collection of machines and tools in such a place.

Some of the objectives of machine shop are:

- To produce accurate and precise parts and components for various applications and industries.
- To optimize the use of materials, time, energy, and cost in the manufacturing process.
- To ensure the safety and quality of the machines, tools, and workpieces.
- To develop the skills and knowledge of the machine operators and technicians.

Some of the machines and tools commonly used in machine shop are:

- Lathe: A machine that rotates a workpiece around a fixed axis and performs various operations, such as turning, facing, threading, knurling, etc., by using cutting tools that are moved against the workpiece.
- Milling machine: A machine that uses a rotating cutting tool, called a milling cutter, to remove material from a workpiece that is fixed on a table or a vise. The milling cutter can move in different directions and angles to create various shapes and features on the workpiece.
- Drilling machine: A machine that uses a rotating cutting tool, called a drill bit, to create holes in a workpiece that is clamped on a table or a vise. The drill bit can have different sizes, shapes, and types to suit different materials and purposes.
- Grinding machine: A machine that uses a rotating abrasive wheel, called a grinding wheel, to remove material from a workpiece that is held by a fixture or a chuck. The grinding wheel can have different shapes, sizes, and grains to achieve different finishes and accuracies on the workpiece.
- Cutting tools: The tools that are used to remove material from a workpiece by cutting, shearing, or abrasion. They can be classified into single-point tools, such as lathe tools, and multi-point tools, such as milling cutters and drill bits. They can be made of different materials, such as high-speed steel, carbide, ceramic, etc., and have different geometries, such as rake angle, clearance angle, cutting edge, etc., to suit different operations and workpiece materials.
- Measuring tools: The tools that are used to measure the dimensions, angles, and surface quality of a workpiece or a machine. They can be classified into direct measuring tools, such as calipers, micrometers, and gauges, and indirect measuring tools, such as comparators, dial indicators, and vernier scales. They can have different units, such as metric, imperial, or decimal, and different accuracies, such as resolution, tolerance, and error, to suit different requirements and standards.
