#### Introduction to Patterns, pattern allowances, ingredients of moulding sand and melting furnaces.

- Patterns are the replica of the desired casting shape, made of wood, metal, plastic or other materials.
- Pattern allowances are the modifications made to the pattern dimensions to account for various factors such as shrinkage, machining, distortion and draft.
- Moulding sand is the material used to make the mould cavity, which consists of four main ingredients: base sand, binder, additives and moisture.
- Melting furnaces are the devices used to melt the metal for casting, which can be classified into two types: fuel-fired and electric.
- Fuel-fired furnaces use coal, oil, gas or wood as the fuel source, and can be further divided into cupola, crucible, reverberatory and rotary furnaces.
- Electric furnaces use electricity as the heat source, and can be further divided into induction, arc, resistance and plasma furnaces.

#### Foundry tools and their purposes

- Foundry tools are the instruments used to perform various operations in the foundry, such as moulding, melting, pouring and finishing.
- Some of the common foundry tools are:

  - Moulding box: a container that holds the moulding sand and the pattern.
  - Rammer: a tool used to compact the moulding sand around the pattern.
  - Strike-off bar: a tool used to level the excess sand on the top of the moulding box.
  - Sprue pin: a tool used to make a hole in the sand for the molten metal to enter the mould cavity.
  - Gate cutter: a tool used to cut the channels for the molten metal to flow from the sprue to the mould cavity.
  - Vent wire: a tool used to make small holes in the sand for the gases to escape during casting.
  - Trowel: a tool used to smoothen the surface of the mould cavity and the cope.
  - Lifter: a tool used to lift and separate the cope and the drag after the pattern is removed.
  - Draw spike: a tool used to pull out the pattern from the mould cavity.
  - Flask tongs: a tool used to handle the hot moulding boxes.
  - Ladle: a tool used to transfer the molten metal from the furnace to the mould.
  - Skimmer: a tool used to remove the slag and impurities from the molten metal.
  - Chisel: a tool used to cut and trim the excess metal from the casting.
  - File: a tool used to smoothen and polish the surface of the casting.
  - Hammer: a tool used to break the sand and release the casting.

#### Demo of mould preparation and Aluminum casting

- The steps involved in mould preparation and aluminum casting are:

  - Select a suitable pattern and a moulding box of appropriate size and shape.
  - Place the pattern on a flat board and sprinkle some parting sand over it.
  - Place the drag (the lower part of the moulding box) over the pattern and fill it with moulding sand.
  - Ram the sand firmly around the pattern using a rammer.
  - Level the excess sand using a strike-off bar and make a sprue hole using a sprue pin.
  - Turn over the drag and the board and remove the board carefully.
  - Place the cope (the upper part of the moulding box) over the drag and align them properly.
  - Fill the cope with moulding sand and ram it firmly.
  - Level the excess sand using a strike-off bar and make a runner and a riser using a gate cutter.
  - Make some vents in the cope using a vent wire.
  - Separate the cope and the drag and remove the pattern using a draw spike and a lifter.
  - Repair any defects in the mould cavity using a trowel and a vent wire.
  - Assemble the cope and the drag and secure them with clamps or weights.
  - The mould is now ready for casting.
  - Heat the aluminum in a melting furnace until it reaches the desired temperature and fluidity.
  - Skim off the slag and impurities from the molten metal using a skimmer.
  - Pour the molten metal into the mould through the sprue using a ladle.
  - Wait until the metal solidifies and cools down.
  - Break the sand and remove the casting using a hammer and a chisel.
  - Cut off the sprue, the runner, the riser and any other excess metal using a chisel and a hammer.
