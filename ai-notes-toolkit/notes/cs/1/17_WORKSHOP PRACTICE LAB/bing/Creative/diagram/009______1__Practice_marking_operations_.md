#### 1. Practice marking operations.

Marking operations are the processes of identifying, measuring, and marking the locations of features on a workpiece that need to be machined, drilled, cut, or otherwise modified. Marking operations are important for ensuring accuracy, alignment, and quality of the final product. Some common marking operations are:

- Center punching: This is the process of making a small indentation on the surface of the workpiece using a pointed tool called a center punch. The indentation serves as a guide for drilling or machining operations. Center punching is usually done by hand or with a hammer and punch.
- Scribing: This is the process of drawing lines on the surface of the workpiece using a sharp tool called a scriber. The lines indicate the boundaries, dimensions, or locations of the features to be machined or cut. Scribing is usually done with a ruler, a protractor, a compass, or a template.
- Layout dyeing: This is the process of applying a thin layer of colored liquid or powder on the surface of the workpiece to make the scribed lines more visible. The dye can be applied by brushing, spraying, or dipping. The dye can be removed by wiping, washing, or sanding after the marking operations are done.
- Stamping: This is the process of imprinting letters, numbers, symbols, or codes on the surface of the workpiece using a metal tool called a stamp. The stamp is pressed against the workpiece with a hammer or a press. Stamping is used to identify the workpiece, indicate the specifications, or provide instructions for further processing.
