# Total 33 Hrs

- Total 33 Hrs is a time management technique that aims to help students achieve their academic goals in a balanced and efficient way.
- The technique is based on the idea that students need to spend 33 hours per week on their studies, divided into three categories: 11 hours of lectures, 11 hours of self-study, and 11 hours of revision and practice.
- The benefits of using Total 33 Hrs are:
  - It helps students plan their study schedule and prioritize their tasks.
  - It ensures that students cover all the aspects of learning: input, output, and feedback.
  - It reduces stress and procrastination by breaking down the workload into manageable chunks.
  - It improves retention and comprehension by reinforcing the concepts learned in lectures and self-study.
  - It boosts confidence and performance by preparing students for exams and assignments.

- The steps of implementing Total 33 Hrs are:
  - Step 1: Calculate the number of lectures you have per week and multiply it by 60 minutes. This is your lecture time.
  - Step 2: Subtract your lecture time from 11 hours. This is your self-study time.
  - Step 3: Divide your self-study time by the number of subjects you have. This is your self-study time per subject.
  - Step 4: Allocate your self-study time per subject to the days of the week according to your preference and availability. For example, if you have 4 subjects and 2 hours of self-study time per subject, you can assign 1 hour of self-study time per subject to Monday and Thursday, and another 1 hour of self-study time per subject to Tuesday and Friday.
  - Step 5: Plan your self-study activities for each subject. These can include reading, taking notes, summarizing, researching, doing exercises, etc.
  - Step 6: Calculate the number of weeks you have until your exams or deadlines and multiply it by 11 hours. This is your revision and practice time.
  - Step 7: Divide your revision and practice time by the number of subjects you have. This is your revision and practice time per subject.
  - Step 8: Allocate your revision and practice time per subject to the days of the week according to your preference and availability. For example, if you have 4 subjects and 22 hours of revision and practice time per subject, you can assign 2 hours of revision and practice time per subject to Saturday and Sunday, and another 2 hours of revision and practice time per subject to Wednesday.
  - Step 9: Plan your revision and practice activities for each subject. These can include reviewing, testing, quizzing, solving problems, writing essays, etc.
  - Step 10: Follow your plan and adjust it as needed. Monitor your progress and evaluate your results. Reward yourself for your achievements and learn from your mistakes.
