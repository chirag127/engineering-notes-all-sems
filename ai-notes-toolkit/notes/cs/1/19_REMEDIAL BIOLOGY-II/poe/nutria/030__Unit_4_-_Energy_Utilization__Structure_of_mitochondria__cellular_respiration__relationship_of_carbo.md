
## Unit 4 - Energy Utilization: 

1. Structure of Mitochondria: Mitochondria are organelles found in eukaryotic cells that are responsible for generating energy in the form of ATP. They contain their own genetic material and are surrounded by a double membrane. The inner membrane is highly folded and forms cristae, which increases the surface area for ATP production. 

2. Cellular Respiration: Cellular respiration is the process by which cells convert the energy stored in organic molecules into ATP. This process is divided into three stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain. 

3. Relationship of Carbohydrate Metabolism to Other Compounds: Carbohydrates are the primary source of energy for cells, and are broken down into glucose molecules. These molecules are then used in glycolysis to produce ATP. Other compounds such as fatty acids and proteins can also be used to produce ATP. 

4. Glycolysis: Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration and involves the breakdown of glucose molecules into pyruvate molecules. During this process, two molecules of ATP are produced. 

5. Formation of Acetyl Co-A: The pyruvate molecules produced in glycolysis are converted into acetyl Co-A molecules, which are then used in the Krebs cycle to generate more ATP. 

6. Krebs Cycle: The Krebs cycle is the second stage of cellular respiration and involves the oxidation of acetyl Co-A molecules to produce more ATP. During this process, two molecules of ATP are produced. 

7. Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation: The third stage of cellular respiration is the electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation. This process involves the transfer of electrons from NADH and FADH2 molecules to oxygen molecules, which produces the majority of ATP. 

8. ATP: ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) is the molecule that is used by cells to store and transfer energy. It is produced during cellular respiration and is used to power cellular processes.

9. Factors Affecting Respiration: Several factors can affect the rate of cellular respiration, including temperature, pH, and the availability of oxygen.
