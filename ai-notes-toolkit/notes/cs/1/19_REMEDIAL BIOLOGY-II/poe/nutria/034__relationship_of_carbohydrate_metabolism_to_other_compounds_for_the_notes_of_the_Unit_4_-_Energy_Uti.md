
### Relationship of Carbohydrate Metabolism to Other Compounds

- Carbohydrate metabolism refers to the biochemical processes by which carbohydrates are broken down and converted into energy. 
- Carbohydrate metabolism is closely related to other metabolic processes, such as the metabolism of proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. 
- The breakdown of carbohydrates is an important part of cellular respiration, which is the process by which cells convert the energy in food into energy that can be used by the body. 
- In cellular respiration, carbohydrates are broken down through glycolysis, which produces pyruvate. 
- Pyruvate is then converted into acetyl co-A, which enters the Kreb cycle and is then used to generate ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the energy currency of cells. 
- The ATP is then used to power the electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation, which produces more ATP. 
- Factors that can affect respiration and carbohydrate metabolism include temperature, pH, and the availability of oxygen.
