
### Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation

The Electron Transport System (ETS) and Oxidative Phosphorylation are processes that occur in mitochondria, the organelles responsible for energy production in the form of ATP in cells. 

* The ETS is a series of redox reactions that transfer electrons from electron donors (such as NADH and FADH2) to electron acceptors (such as oxygen). 
* The energy released from the transfer of electrons is used to create a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
* The proton gradient is then used to drive the synthesis of ATP molecules by the enzyme ATP synthase. This process is known as Oxidative Phosphorylation.

The ETS and Oxidative Phosphorylation are essential processes in the cellular respiration of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. They are also important in the relationship between carbohydrate metabolism and other compounds, such as glycolysis and the formation of acetyl co-A. In addition, the ETS and Oxidative Phosphorylation are involved in the Kreb cycle, which is the cycle of reactions that occurs during the breakdown of glucose.

The efficiency of the ETS and Oxidative Phosphorylation is dependent on several factors, such as the availability of oxygen, the concentration of NADH and FADH2, and the temperature.
