
## Unit 1 - Brief History of Microbiology

- Microbiology is the study of microscopic organisms, such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and algae. 
- It dates back to the 1600s when Antonie van Leeuwenhoek first observed and described microorganisms.
- In the 1800s, Louis Pasteur and Robert Koch developed the germ theory of disease, which states that certain diseases are caused by specific microorganisms.

## Types of Microorganisms

- Bacteria are single-celled microorganisms that can be found in virtually every environment on Earth. 
- Proteobacteria is a large group of bacteria that includes E. coli and Salmonella. 
- Non-proteobacteria are a diverse group of bacteria that includes cyanobacteria and mycoplasma.
- Gram-positive bacteria have a thick cell wall that retains a purple stain when exposed to a dye called crystal violet. 
- Gram-negative bacteria have a thin cell wall that does not retain the stain. 
- Lichens are a symbiotic association between a fungus and an alga. 
- Algae are a diverse group of aquatic organisms that range from single-celled diatoms to multicellular seaweeds. 
- Protozoa are single-celled organisms that can be free-living or parasitic. 
- Helminths are parasitic worms that can cause diseases in humans. 
- Viruses are non-living particles that can infect living cells and replicate inside them. 
- Viral structure consists of a core of genetic material surrounded by a protein coat. 
- Viral multiplication involves the attachment of the virus to the host cell, followed by the injection of the viral genetic material into the host cell.

## Role of Microorganisms in Industrial Chemicals and Pharmaceuticals

- Microorganisms are used to produce a variety of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals. 
- These include antibiotics, enzymes, hormones, vitamins, and organic acids. 
- Microorganisms are also used to produce biofuels, such as ethanol and biodiesel. 
- In addition, microorganisms are used to clean up hazardous waste and to produce biodegradable plastics.
