
## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells.

1. Prokaryotic cells are much smaller than eukaryotic cells. They range in size from 0.2-2.0 µm in diameter.
2. Prokaryotic cells are usually divided into two basic shapes: cocci (spherical) and bacilli (rod-shaped).
3. The arrangement of prokaryotic cells can be single, pairs, chains, clusters, or even complex arrangements.
4. Eukaryotic cells are much larger than prokaryotic cells. They range in size from 10-100 µm in diameter.
5. Eukaryotic cells are usually divided into four basic shapes: spherical, ovoid, rod-shaped, and spiral.
6. The arrangement of eukaryotic cells can be single, pairs, chains, clusters, or even complex arrangements.
7. The structure of a cell is determined by its function. For example, the cell membrane is responsible for controlling the movement of materials in and out of the cell, while the cell wall provides strength and protection to the cell.
8. The cytoplasm is the area inside the cell membrane and contains the organelles, which are responsible for the cell's metabolic activities.
9. The nucleus is the control center of the cell and contains the genetic material.
10. Other organelles such as mitochondria and chloroplasts are responsible for energy production and photosynthesis, respectively.
