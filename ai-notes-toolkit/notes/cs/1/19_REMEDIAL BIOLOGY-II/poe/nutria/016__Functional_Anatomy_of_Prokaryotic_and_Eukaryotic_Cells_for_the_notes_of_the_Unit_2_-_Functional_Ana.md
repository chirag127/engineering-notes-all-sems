
### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells 

- Prokaryotic cells are typically much smaller than eukaryotic cells, ranging from 0.2 to 2.0 micrometers in diameter. 
- Prokaryotic cells are typically circular or rod-shaped, while eukaryotic cells can be various shapes, including spherical, rod-shaped, or flattened.
- Prokaryotic cells lack a true nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotic cells contain a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. 
- The cell wall of prokaryotes is composed of peptidoglycan, while the cell wall of eukaryotes is composed of cellulose in plants, chitin in fungi, and other substances in animals. 
- Prokaryotes have a single circular chromosome, while eukaryotes can have multiple linear chromosomes. 
- Prokaryotes contain 70S ribosomes, while eukaryotes contain 80S ribosomes. 
- Prokaryotes typically reproduce through binary fission, while eukaryotes reproduce through mitosis and meiosis. 
- Prokaryotes contain plasmids, while eukaryotes do not.
