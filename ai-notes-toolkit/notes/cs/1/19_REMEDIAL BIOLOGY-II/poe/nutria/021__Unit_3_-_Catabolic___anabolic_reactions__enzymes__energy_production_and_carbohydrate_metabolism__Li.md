
## Unit 3 - Catabolic & Anabolic Reactions: Enzymes, Energy Production and Carbohydrate Metabolism

- Catabolic reactions are those that break down larger molecules into smaller ones, releasing energy in the process.
- Anabolic reactions are those that build larger molecules from smaller ones, requiring energy in the process.
- Enzymes are proteins that act as catalysts in biochemical reactions, speeding them up and allowing them to occur at lower temperatures.
- Energy production in cells involves the breakdown of glucose and other molecules to form ATP, which is the energy currency of cells.
- Carbohydrate metabolism involves the breakdown of glucose and other sugars to produce energy and other molecules.
- Lipid catabolism involves the breakdown of fats and oils to produce energy and other molecules.
- Protein catabolism involves the breakdown of proteins to produce energy and other molecules.
- The mechanism of energy production involves the breakdown of glucose molecules in a series of steps known as glycolysis, followed by the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.
- Metabolic diversity refers to the fact that different organisms use different pathways to produce energy and other molecules.
- Pathways of energy use refer to the various ways in which energy produced by cells can be used, such as for growth, repair, movement and reproduction.
- Integration of metabolism refers to the way in which different metabolic pathways interact with each other to produce the molecules and energy needed for life.
