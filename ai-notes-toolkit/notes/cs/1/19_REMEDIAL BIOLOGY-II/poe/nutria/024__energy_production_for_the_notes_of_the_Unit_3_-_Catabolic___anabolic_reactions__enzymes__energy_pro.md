

### Energy Production for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Catabolic & Anabolic Reactions: Enzymes, Energy Production and Carbohydrate Metabolism

1. Catabolic and anabolic reactions are two processes that are essential for the production and utilization of energy in organisms.

2. Catabolic reactions involve the breakdown of large molecules into smaller molecules, releasing energy in the process. Examples of catabolic reactions include the breakdown of carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins.

3. Anabolic reactions involve the synthesis of large molecules from smaller molecules, requiring energy in the process. Examples of anabolic reactions include the synthesis of carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins.

4. Enzymes are proteins that act as catalysts, speeding up the rate of biochemical reactions. Enzymes are involved in both catabolic and anabolic reactions.

5. Carbohydrate metabolism involves the breakdown of carbohydrates into smaller molecules, such as glucose, and the synthesis of carbohydrates from smaller molecules, such as glucose.

6. Lipid and protein catabolism involves the breakdown of lipids and proteins into smaller molecules, such as amino acids, and the synthesis of lipids and proteins from smaller molecules, such as amino acids.

7. Energy production mechanism involves the conversion of energy from one form to another, such as the conversion of chemical energy to mechanical energy.

8. Metabolic diversity refers to the variety of metabolic pathways that exist in different organisms, allowing them to utilize different sources of energy.

9. Pathways of energy use refer to the various pathways that energy can take as it is used in an organism, such as the conversion of energy from food to ATP.

10. Integration of metabolism in the subject of Remedial Biology-II KCS refers to the integration of the concepts of metabolism, energy production, and metabolic pathways into the subject of Remedial Biology-II KCS.
