### Arrangement of Bacterial Cells

- Bacteria are prokaryotic cells that exhibit a wide variety of shapes and arrangements. The most common shapes are:
- Spherical (cocci) - e.g. Staphylococcus
- Rod-shaped (bacilli) - e.g. Bacillus
- Spiral (spirilla) - e.g. Spirillum
- Comma-shaped (vibrios) - e.g. Vibrio cholerae

- Arrangements:
- Single cells - e.g. many cocci (Staphylococcus)
- Pairs - e.g. many cocci (Streptococcus)
- Chains - e.g. many bacilli (Bacillus)
- Cubes - e.g. Neisseria
- Palisades or packets - e.g. many bacilli (Corynebacterium)
- Filaments - e.g. many bacilli fused together (Streptomyces)

- The specific shape and arrangement of bacterial cells are determined by their cell wall and are adapted for their mode of nutrition, reproduction, and environment. The shape and arrangement provide structural strength and allow for efficient utilization of space and distribution of nutrients.
