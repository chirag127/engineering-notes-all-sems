### Cellular Respiration

- Mitochondria: Double membrane-bound organelles called the powerhouse of the cell. It is the site for cellular respiration.
- Cellular respiration: Process of oxidizing food molecules like glucose to produce energy in the form of ATP. It involves 3 stages:
	- Glycolysis: Occurs in the cytoplasm. Glucose is converted to pyruvate yielding 2 ATP and 2 NADH molecules.
	- Pyruvate oxidation: Pyruvate is converted to acetyl CoA which enters the Krebs cycle.
	- Krebs cycle: Occurs in the matrix of mitochondria. Acetyl CoA is converted to CO2, NADH and FADH2.
- Relationship to metabolism: Cellular respiration is the primary way our body obtains energy from the food we eat. It produces ATP which powers various cellular processes. It is interconnected with other metabolic pathways like glycolysis and electron transport chain.
- Electron transport chain: The high energy electrons from NADH and FADH2 are transported through a chain of electron carriers in the inner mitochondrial membrane. The energy released is used to pump protons and produce ATP.
- Oxidative phosphorylation: The movement of protons across the inner membrane powers the synthesis of ATP from ADP and phosphate.
- Factors affecting respiration:
	- Oxygen supply: Anaerobic respiration occurs in absence of oxygen producing lactic acid.
	- Substrate availability: The rate of respiration depends on the availability of glucose and other substrates.
	- Temperature: Increases with temperature as the rate of enzymatic reactions increases but optimum temperature is required.
	- pH: Optimum pH is required for optimal functioning of respiratory enzymes.
