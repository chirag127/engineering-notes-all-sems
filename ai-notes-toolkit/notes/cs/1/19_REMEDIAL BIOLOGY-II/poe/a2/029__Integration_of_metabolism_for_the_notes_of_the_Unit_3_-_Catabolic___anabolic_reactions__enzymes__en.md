### Integration of metabolism for the notes of the Unit 3 - Catabolic & anabolic reactions: enzymes, energy production and carbohydrate metabolism. Lipid & protein catabolism, Energy production mechanism, metabolic diversity & pathways of energy use. Integration of metabolism in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS.

1. Catabolic reactions: Breakdown of complex molecules into simpler ones releasing energy. Examples: Glycolysis, Krebs cycle, Electron transport chain.
2. Anabolic reactions: Synthesis of complex molecules from simpler ones utilizing energy. Examples: Gluconeogenesis, Fatty acid synthesis, Protein synthesis.
3. Enzymes: Biological catalysts which increase the rate of both catabolic and anabolic reactions. They are protein in nature.
4. Energy production: Through catabolic pathways, the high energy bonds of nutrients are broken down and energy is released. This energy is captured in the form of ATP which acts as the universal energy currency of cells.
5. Carbohydrate metabolism: Carbohydrates are broken down through glycolysis and Krebs cycle producing energy. The end products are CO2 and water.
6. Lipid catabolism: Lipids are broken down through beta-oxidation producing energy. The end products are Acetyl CoA, NADH and FADH2.
7. Protein catabolism: Proteins are broken down through various pathways producing energy. The end products are amino acids which are used for anabolism.
8. Energy production mechanism: The high energy electrons captured during catabolism are transferred through electron transport chain and the energy is used to pump protons creating a proton gradient. This gradient is utilized by ATP synthase to produce ATP.
9. Metabolic diversity: The proportion of different metabolic pathways active in a tissue depends on its energy requirements. For example, liver and muscle utilize both carbohydrate and lipid metabolism, brain utilizes carbohydrate metabolism, adipose tissue utilizes lipid metabolism.
10. Pathways of energy use: The ATP produced is utilized through various pathways to meet the energy demands of cells such as mechanical work, transport work, and biosynthesis.
