## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells:

- Bacterial cells are prokaryotic cells that have a simple structure:
- They are typically 0.5-5 μm in size. They have a circular/spiral shape with a relatively small genome.
- The key components are:
 - Cell membrane: regulates what enters and exits the cell.
 - Cytoplasm: a gel-like substance where cellular components are suspended.
 - Chromosome: a single circular DNA molecule that contains the bacterial genome.
 - Ribosomes: organelles that synthesize proteins.

- Eukaryotic cells are larger in size (10-100 μm) and have a more complex structure than prokaryotic cells. The key components include:
 - Nucleus: contains DNA and regulates cell activity.
 - Mitochondria: produce ATP via cellular respiration.
 - Chloroplasts: carry out photosynthesis in plant and algal cells.
 - Endoplasmic reticulum: transports materials within the cell.
 - Golgi apparatus: processes and packages macromolecules.

- The structures of cells are highly organized and adapted to carry out specific functions that are essential to sustaining life. The components work together in an integrated manner via chemical and molecular interactions and processes.
