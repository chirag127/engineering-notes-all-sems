### Size for the notes of the Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells:

- Bacterial cells are generally smaller in size ranging from 0.5 μm to 5 μm. They are prokaryotic cells lacking membrane-bound organelles.
- The size of a bacterial cell is limited by its surface area to volume ratio. As the size increases, the ratio decreases making it difficult to acquire nutrients and get rid of wastes. This limits the maximum size of a bacterial cell.
- The shape of a bacterial cell is determined by its cell wall. It can be spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), spiral (spirochetes), or comma-shaped (vibrios). The shape provides structural strength and helps in functions like motion.
- Arrangement of bacterial cells: Depending on their shapes, bacterial cells can arrange themselves in characteristic patterns:
    - Tetrads: Arrangement of 4 cocci
    - Sarcinae: Arrangement of 8 cocci
    - Cubic packets: Arrangement of 8 bacilli
    - Palisades: Long chains of bacilli
- Eukaryotic cells are larger in size ranging from 10 μm to 100 μm. They have membrane-bound organelles performing specialized functions. The large size is made possible due to the organelles and intracellular transport systems. A larger size also allows greater control over cellular components and more complex functions.
