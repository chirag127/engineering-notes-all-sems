### Enzymes

- Enzymes are biological molecules (proteins) that act as catalysts and help in accelerating chemical reactions.
- They have an active site where substrate binding takes place.
- The shape of the active site is complementary to the substrate molecule.
- The substrate binds to the active site and an enzyme-substrate complex is formed which lowers the activation energy of the reaction and speeds up the rate of reaction.
- After the reaction is completed, the products are released. The enzyme remains unchanged and is ready to catalyze another reaction.
- The activity of enzymes can be affected by temperature, pH, and presence of inhibitors.
- Each enzyme has an optimum temperature and pH at which its activity is maximum. Extreme temperatures or pH denature the enzyme and reduce its activity.

Lipid Catabolism

- Lipids are broken down by lipases to produce fatty acids and glycerol.
- Fatty acids are converted into acetyl CoA which enters the Krebs cycle.
- Glycerol is converted to pyruvate which enters the Krebs cycle.
- Beta-oxidation is the process of converting fatty acids into acetyl CoA. It occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.
