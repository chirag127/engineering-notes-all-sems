### Shape for the notes of the Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS.

- Prokaryotic cells: Bacteria are prokaryotic cells. They are generally small in size (0.5-5 μ). They have irregular shapes: spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), spiral (spirilla).
- Eukaryotic cells: Cells of plants and animals are eukaryotic cells. They are larger in size (10-100 μ). They have a defined shape: spherical, elongated, irregular, etc. They contain membrane-bound organelles.
- Cell shape is related to cellular functions. The shape provides mechanical strength and increases surface area for absorption, secretion, etc.
- Rod-shaped bacterial cells can move quickly in fluids. Flat and elongated eukaryotic cells provide flexibility. Circular cells are useful for packing and stacking. Branched prokaryotic filaments are useful for anchoring.
- The internal structure of cells consists of cytoplasm and cellular organelles with specialized functions. The prokaryotic cells lack membrane-bound organelles while eukaryotic cells have organelles like mitochondria, Plastids, ER, Golgi bodies, etc.
- The nucleus is the control center of eukaryotic cells. It contains DNA and regulates cell functions. Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus. Their genetic material is present in the cytoplasm.
