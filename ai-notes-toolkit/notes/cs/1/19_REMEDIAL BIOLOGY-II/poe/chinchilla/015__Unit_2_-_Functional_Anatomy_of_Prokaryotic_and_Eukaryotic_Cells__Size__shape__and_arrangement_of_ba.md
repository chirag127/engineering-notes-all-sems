

## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells.

- Cells are the basic unit of life, and they can be divided into two types: prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler in structure than eukaryotic cells, and they lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Bacteria are an example of prokaryotic cells, and they come in a variety of shapes and sizes, including spherical, rod-shaped, and spiral-shaped.
- The size of bacterial cells can range from 0.2 micrometers to several micrometers in diameter.
- Bacterial cells can be arranged in different ways, including chains, clusters, and pairs.
- Eukaryotic cells, on the other hand, are more complex and have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Eukaryotic cells can be found in plants, animals, and fungi.
- The size of eukaryotic cells can range from 10-100 micrometers in diameter.
- Eukaryotic cells can also come in different shapes, such as spherical, cuboidal, and columnar.
- Cells have various structures and functions that enable them to carry out their specific roles in living organisms.
- The cell membrane is a thin layer that surrounds the cell and regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell.
- The cytoplasm is the gel-like substance that fills the cell and contains various organelles.
- The nucleus is the control center of the cell and contains the genetic material.
- Mitochondria are organelles that produce energy for the cell through cellular respiration.
- Ribosomes are organelles that synthesize proteins.
- The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranes that transport proteins and lipids.
- The Golgi apparatus is an organelle that modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids for transport.
- Lysosomes are organelles that break down and recycle cellular waste and foreign substances.
- The cytoskeleton is a network of protein fibers that provides structure and support for the cell and helps with movement. 

Overall, understanding the size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells, as well as the structure and function of cells, is crucial for understanding the basic units of life and the processes that occur within them.
