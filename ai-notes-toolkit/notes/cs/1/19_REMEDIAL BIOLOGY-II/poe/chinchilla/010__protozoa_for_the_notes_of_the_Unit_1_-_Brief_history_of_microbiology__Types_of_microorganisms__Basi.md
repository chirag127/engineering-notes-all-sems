

### Protozoa

Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotic microorganisms that exhibit a variety of shapes and sizes. They are found in diverse habitats ranging from marine and freshwater to soil and even in the bodies of other organisms.

#### Characteristics of Protozoa

- They are unicellular organisms with a well-defined nucleus and other cell organelles.
- They exhibit a wide range of shapes, from spherical to elongated and even branched.
- Most protozoa are motile and move by means of flagella, cilia or pseudopodia.
- They reproduce asexually by binary fission or multiple fission and sexually by conjugation.
- Protozoa are heterotrophic and obtain their nutrition by engulfing other microorganisms or organic matter.

#### Classification of Protozoa

Protozoa are classified based on their mode of locomotion and other morphological features.

- Flagellates: These protozoa move by means of flagella and include organisms like Trypanosoma and Giardia.
- Ciliates: These protozoa move by means of cilia and include organisms like Paramecium and Stentor.
- Amoebae: These protozoa move by means of pseudopodia and include organisms like Amoeba and Entamoeba.
- Sporozoans: These protozoa are non-motile and include organisms like Plasmodium and Toxoplasma.

#### Role of Protozoa

Protozoa play an important role in the ecosystem by serving as primary consumers and decomposers. They are also used in the production of antibiotics and other pharmaceuticals. Some protozoa are parasitic and cause diseases like malaria, sleeping sickness and amoebic dysentery.

In conclusion, protozoa are a diverse group of microorganisms with unique morphological and physiological features. They play an important role in the ecosystem and have significant applications in biotechnology and medicine.
