

### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

Prokaryotic cells and eukaryotic cells have differences in their functional anatomy. Here are some of the points to keep in mind:

#### Prokaryotic cells

- Prokaryotic cells are smaller in size compared to eukaryotic cells.
- They lack a true nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Their genetic material is present in the form of a single, circular chromosome.
- The cell wall of prokaryotic cells is made up of peptidoglycan.
- They possess flagella for locomotion and pili for attachment and transfer of genetic material.
- Prokaryotic cells can be found in various shapes such as cocci, bacilli, and spirilla.

#### Eukaryotic cells

- Eukaryotic cells are larger in size compared to prokaryotic cells.
- They possess a true nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and lysosomes.
- Their genetic material is present in the form of multiple linear chromosomes.
- Eukaryotic cells may have a cell wall made up of cellulose or chitin, depending on the organism.
- They possess cilia or flagella for locomotion.
- Eukaryotic cells can be found in various shapes such as spherical, cuboidal, and columnar.
