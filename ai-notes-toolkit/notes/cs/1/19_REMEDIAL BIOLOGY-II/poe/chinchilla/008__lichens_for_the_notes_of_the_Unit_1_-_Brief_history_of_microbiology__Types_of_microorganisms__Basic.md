### Lichens

Lichens are composite organisms that arise from the mutualistic relationship between fungi and algae. Here are some key points to understand about lichens:

- Lichens are found in a wide range of habitats, including arctic tundra, deserts, and forests.
- The fungal partner in the lichen relationship provides structural support and protection, while the algal partner provides photosynthetic energy.
- Lichens can be classified into three general growth forms: crustose, foliose, and fruticose.
- Crustose lichens grow flat against the substrate, while foliose lichens have a leafy appearance and fruticose lichens grow upright or hang down like hair.
- Lichens are important indicators of air quality because they are sensitive to pollutants such as sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
- Some lichens have medicinal properties and have been used in traditional medicine for centuries.
- Lichens are also used as a food source for animals such as caribou and reindeer in arctic regions.

Overall, lichens are fascinating organisms that play important roles in their ecosystems and have practical applications in medicine and environmental monitoring.
