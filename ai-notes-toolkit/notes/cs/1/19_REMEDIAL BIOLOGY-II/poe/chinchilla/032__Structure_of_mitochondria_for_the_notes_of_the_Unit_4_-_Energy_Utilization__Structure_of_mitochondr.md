### Structure of Mitochondria

Mitochondria are the powerhouse of the cell, responsible for generating ATP, the energy currency of the cell. The structure of mitochondria is designed for this purpose, with various components working together to produce energy.

#### Outer Membrane

The outer membrane of mitochondria is a smooth, phospholipid bilayer that separates the interior of the mitochondria from the cytoplasm. It is permeable to small molecules and ions.

#### Inner Membrane

The inner membrane of mitochondria is highly folded, forming cristae. These folds increase the surface area of the membrane, allowing for more ATP production. The inner membrane is also impermeable to most molecules and ions, allowing for the creation of a proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis.

#### Matrix

The matrix is the interior space of the mitochondria, enclosed by the inner membrane. It contains enzymes and substrates involved in the Krebs cycle and fatty acid oxidation.

#### Intermembrane Space

The intermembrane space is the space between the outer and inner membranes of the mitochondria. It contains enzymes and proteins involved in electron transport and ATP synthesis.

### Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells generate ATP from the breakdown of organic compounds. The process can be broken down into three main stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.

#### Glycolysis

Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, occurring in the cytoplasm of the cell. Glucose is broken down into pyruvate, with the release of ATP and NADH.

#### Formation of Acetyl Co-A

Before entering the Krebs cycle, pyruvate is converted to acetyl co-A. This process generates NADH and CO2.

#### Krebs Cycle

The Krebs cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle, occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria. Acetyl co-A is broken down into CO2, generating ATP, NADH, and FADH2.

#### Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation

The electron transport system is located in the inner membrane of the mitochondria. Electrons from NADH and FADH2 are transported along a series of carriers, generating a proton gradient across the inner membrane. This gradient is used to drive ATP synthesis through oxidative phosphorylation.

#### ATP

ATP is the energy currency of the cell, generated through cellular respiration. It is used for many cellular processes, including muscle contraction, protein synthesis, and active transport.

### Factors Affecting Respiration

Several factors can affect cellular respiration, including temperature, pH, and the availability of oxygen and nutrients. Optimal conditions for respiration vary depending on the organism and the tissue type.
