## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells

In this unit, we will discuss the functional anatomy of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, including their size, shape, and arrangement. We will also explore the structure and function of cells.

### Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are typically much smaller than eukaryotic cells, ranging in size from 0.2 to 10 micrometers.
- Bacterial cells come in a variety of shapes, including spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), and spiral (spirilla).
- Bacterial cells can also exist in clusters or chains, depending on the species.

### Structure of prokaryotic cells

- Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- The genetic material in prokaryotic cells is located in a region of the cell called the nucleoid.
- Prokaryotic cells have a cell wall made of peptidoglycan, which provides structure and protection.
- Some prokaryotic cells also have a capsule, which is a protective layer outside the cell wall.

### Function of prokaryotic cells

- Prokaryotic cells are capable of a wide range of functions, including metabolism, reproduction, and communication.
- Some bacteria are capable of photosynthesis, while others are able to break down complex organic matter.
- Bacterial cells are also important in the cycling of nutrients in ecosystems.

### Structure of eukaryotic cells

- Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, including mitochondria, chloroplasts (in plants), and the endoplasmic reticulum.
- Eukaryotic cells also have a cytoskeleton, which provides structure and support.
- The cell membrane of eukaryotic cells is similar in structure to that of prokaryotic cells, but it may contain cholesterol and other lipids.

### Function of eukaryotic cells

- Eukaryotic cells are capable of a wide range of functions, including metabolism, reproduction, and communication.
- Some eukaryotic cells are specialized for specific functions, such as muscle cells, nerve cells, and blood cells.
- Eukaryotic cells are also capable of complex behaviors, such as phagocytosis (engulfing of foreign materials) and endocytosis (uptake of extracellular material).

In conclusion, understanding the functional anatomy of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells is crucial for understanding the basic biology of living organisms. By understanding the structure and function of cells, we can gain insight into the complex processes that underlie life.
