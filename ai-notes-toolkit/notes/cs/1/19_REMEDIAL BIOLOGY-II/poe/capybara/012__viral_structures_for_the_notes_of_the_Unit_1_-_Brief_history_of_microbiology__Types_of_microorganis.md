### Viral Structures

- Viruses are small, infectious agents that cannot replicate on their own and require a host cell to reproduce.
- They consist of a nucleic acid core, either RNA or DNA, surrounded by a protein coat called a capsid.
- The capsid may be surrounded by an envelope made up of lipids and proteins.
- The outer surface of the capsid or envelope may have spikes or other structures that allow the virus to attach to specific host cells.
- Some viruses also have enzymes, such as reverse transcriptase, that allow them to convert their RNA into DNA and integrate it into the host cell's genome.
- The size and shape of viruses can vary greatly, with some being as small as 20 nanometers and others reaching up to 400 nanometers in length.
- Some viruses have complex structures, such as the bacteriophages that infect bacteria, which have a head containing the nucleic acid, a tail for attaching to the host cell, and sometimes other structures such as spikes or fibers.
- Understanding viral structures is important for developing strategies to prevent and treat viral infections.
