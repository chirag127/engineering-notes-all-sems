# Structure and Function of Cells

- Cells are the basic units of life that contain the essential molecules and organelles for performing various functions.
- Cells can be classified into two major types: prokaryotic and eukaryotic.
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells. They lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are mostly unicellular organisms, such as bacteria and archaea.
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells. They have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, etc. They are mostly multicellular organisms, such as plants, animals, fungi, and protists.

## Size, Shape, and Arrangement of Bacterial Cells

- Bacterial cells are prokaryotic cells that vary in size, shape, and arrangement.
- The size of bacterial cells ranges from 0.2 to 10 micrometers in diameter.
- The shape of bacterial cells can be classified into three main types: cocci (spherical), bacilli (rod-shaped), and spirilla (spiral-shaped).
- The arrangement of bacterial cells can be influenced by their shape and mode of division. Some common arrangements are: diplococci (pairs of cocci), streptococci (chains of cocci), staphylococci (clusters of cocci), diplobacilli (pairs of bacilli), streptobacilli (chains of bacilli), and vibrios (comma-shaped bacilli).

## Structure and Function of Cell Membrane

- The cell membrane is a thin layer of phospholipids and proteins that surrounds the cytoplasm of a cell.
- The cell membrane acts as a selective barrier that regulates the entry and exit of substances into and out of the cell.
- The cell membrane also maintains the shape and integrity of the cell, and facilitates cell communication and recognition.

## Structure and Function of Cytoplasm

- The cytoplasm is a jelly-like substance that fills the interior of a cell.
- The cytoplasm contains various organelles, molecules, and ions that are involved in cellular functions.
- The cytoplasm also allows the movement of substances and organelles within the cell, which is called cyclosis or cytoplasmic streaming.

## Structure and Function of Nucleus

- The nucleus is a membrane-bound organelle that is present only in eukaryotic cells.
- The nucleus contains the genetic material (DNA) of the cell, which is organized into chromosomes.
- The nucleus controls the expression of genes and the synthesis of proteins, which are essential for cellular functions.
- The nucleus also contains a smaller structure called the nucleolus, which is responsible for the production of ribosomes.

## Structure and Function of Ribosomes

- Ribosomes are small structures that are composed of RNA and proteins.
- Ribosomes can be found either free in the cytoplasm or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum.
- Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis, where they translate the genetic information from mRNA into amino acid sequences.

## Structure and Function of Mitochondria

- Mitochondria are membrane-bound organelles that are present in most eukaryotic cells.
- Mitochondria have a double membrane structure, with an inner membrane that is folded into cristae and an outer membrane that is smooth.
- Mitochondria are the sites of cellular respiration, where they convert glucose and oxygen into ATP (energy) and carbon dioxide.
- Mitochondria also have their own DNA and ribosomes, and can replicate independently of the nucleus.

## Structure and Function of Chloroplasts

- Chloroplasts are membrane-bound organelles that are present only in plant cells and some algae.
- Chloroplasts have a double membrane structure, with an inner membrane that contains flattened sacs called thylakoids and an outer membrane that is smooth.
