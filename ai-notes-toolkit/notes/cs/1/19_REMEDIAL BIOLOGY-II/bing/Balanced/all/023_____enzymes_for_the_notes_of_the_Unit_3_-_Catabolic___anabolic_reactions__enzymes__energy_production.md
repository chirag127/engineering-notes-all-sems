# Enzymes

- Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up the rate of biochemical reactions by lowering the activation energy required for the reaction to occur.
- Enzymes are highly specific for their substrates, which are the molecules that bind to the enzyme's active site and undergo a chemical change.
- Enzymes can be classified into two main types based on the direction of the reaction they catalyze: anabolic enzymes and catabolic enzymes.

## Anabolic Enzymes

- Anabolic enzymes are involved in the synthesis of larger and more complex molecules from smaller units.
- Anabolic reactions are also called biosynthetic reactions, as they produce new biomolecules that are essential for the growth and maintenance of cells.
- Anabolic reactions consume energy, as they require input of ATP or other high-energy molecules to drive the formation of bonds.
- An example of an anabolic reaction is photosynthesis, where plants use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

## Catabolic Enzymes

- Catabolic enzymes are involved in the breakdown of larger and more complex molecules into smaller units.
- Catabolic reactions are also called degradative reactions, as they degrade biomolecules that are no longer needed or that can be used as energy sources.
- Catabolic reactions release energy, as they break bonds and generate ATP or other high-energy molecules that can be used for cellular work.
- An example of a catabolic reaction is cellular respiration, where cells use oxygen to oxidize glucose and produce carbon dioxide, water and ATP.

## Metabolic Pathways

- Metabolic pathways are sequences of enzyme-catalyzed reactions that transform one or more substrates into one or more products.
- Metabolic pathways can be either linear, where the product of one reaction is the substrate for the next, or cyclic, where the product of the last reaction is the substrate for the first.
- Metabolic pathways can be either reversible, where the reaction can proceed in both directions, or irreversible, where the reaction can only proceed in one direction.
- Metabolic pathways can be either anabolic, catabolic, or amphibolic, where they involve both anabolic and catabolic reactions.
- Metabolic pathways are regulated by various factors, such as the availability of substrates and products, the activity and expression of enzymes, and the feedback inhibition or activation of enzymes by the end products or intermediates of the pathway.
