## Unit 4 - Energy Utilization

- Structure of mitochondria
  - Mitochondria are small, rod-shaped organelles found in most eukaryotic cells, such as plants and animals  .
  - They have two membranes, an outer membrane and an inner membrane, made of phospholipids .
  - The inner membrane is folded into projections called cristae, which increase the surface area for chemical reactions .
  - The space between the two membranes is called the intermembrane space, and the space inside the inner membrane is called the matrix .
  - The matrix contains enzymes, DNA, ribosomes, and various molecules involved in energy production .
  - Mitochondria are the sites of cellular respiration, a process that converts organic molecules into ATP, the main energy currency of the cell  .
  - Mitochondria have their own DNA and can replicate independently of the cell .
  - Mitochondria are thought to have evolved from ancient bacteria that were engulfed by ancestral eukaryotic cells in a symbiotic relationship .

- Cellular respiration
  - Cellular respiration is a series of metabolic reactions that break down glucose and other organic molecules into carbon dioxide and water, releasing energy in the form of ATP .
  - Cellular respiration can be divided into four stages: glycolysis, formation of acetyl co-A, Krebs cycle, and electron transport chain .
  - Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, which occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell. It splits one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, producing two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH (a coenzyme that carries electrons) per glucose molecule .
  - Formation of acetyl co-A is the second stage of cellular respiration, which occurs in the mitochondria. It converts each pyruvate molecule into a two-carbon molecule called acetyl co-A, releasing one molecule of carbon dioxide and one molecule of NADH per pyruvate molecule .
  - Krebs cycle is the third stage of cellular respiration, which occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria. It completes the oxidation of glucose by breaking down each acetyl co-A molecule into two molecules of carbon dioxide, producing one molecule of ATP, three molecules of NADH, and one molecule of FADH2 (another coenzyme that carries electrons) per acetyl co-A molecule .
  - Electron transport chain is the fourth and final stage of cellular respiration, which occurs in the inner membrane of the mitochondria. It uses the electrons carried by NADH and FADH2 to pump protons across the inner membrane, creating a gradient of protons in the intermembrane space. The protons then flow back into the matrix through a protein complex called ATP synthase, which uses the energy to synthesize ATP from ADP and phosphate. This process is also called oxidative phosphorylation, because it involves the transfer of electrons to oxygen, the final electron acceptor, forming water .
  - Cellular respiration can produce up to 38 molecules of ATP per glucose molecule, depending on the efficiency of the process and the availability of oxygen .

- Relationship of carbohydrate metabolism to other compounds
  - Carbohydrate metabolism is the process of converting carbohydrates, such as glucose, into energy or other molecules, such as amino acids, fatty acids, and nucleotides .
