# Size, Shape, and Arrangement of Bacterial Cells

- Bacterial cells are microscopic organisms that cannot be seen by the naked eye. They are measured in micrometers (µm), which are one-thousandth of a millimeter. The average size of a bacterial cell is between 0.2 and 2.0 µm .
- Bacterial cells have different shapes and arrangements that help them adapt to different environments and functions. The most common shapes are:
  - Cocci: spherical or oval cells that can be arranged in pairs, chains, clusters, or other patterns .
  - Bacilli: rod-shaped or cylindrical cells that can be arranged in singles, pairs, chains, or palisades .
  - Spirilla: spiral or curved cells that can be rigid or flexible, and can have one or more flagella for motility .
- Some bacterial cells have more complex shapes and arrangements, such as:
  - Vibrios: comma-shaped cells that are similar to spirilla but shorter and thicker .
  - Spirochetes: long, thin, and flexible cells that have a corkscrew-like motion and are often pathogenic .
  - Actinomycetes: filamentous or branched cells that resemble fungi and produce antibiotics .
  - Mycoplasmas: very small cells that lack a cell wall and have a pleomorphic shape that can change according to the environment .
  - Nanobacteria: extremely small cells that are controversial and may not be true bacteria. They have an average size of 323 nm by 242 nm and may scavenge nutrients from other bacteria.
