- Brief history of microbiology
  - Microbiology is the study of microorganisms, which are microscopic living beings that can only be seen with a microscope.
  - The first observation of microorganisms was made by Antonie van Leeuwenhoek in the 17th century, using a simple microscope. He called them "animalcules".
  - The development of microbiology was influenced by the discoveries of Robert Hooke, who coined the term "cell", Louis Pasteur, who disproved the theory of spontaneous generation and demonstrated the role of microorganisms in fermentation and disease, Robert Koch, who established the criteria for proving the causative agent of an infectious disease, and many others.
  - Microbiology has many branches, such as bacteriology, virology, mycology, parasitology, immunology, etc., that focus on different aspects of microorganisms and their interactions with humans, animals, plants, and the environment.

- Types of microorganisms
  - Microorganisms can be classified into different groups based on their structure, function, and phylogeny. Some of the major groups are:
    - Bacteria: prokaryotic cells that lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, but have a rigid cell wall and a circular DNA molecule. They can be found in almost every habitat and can perform various metabolic processes, such as photosynthesis, respiration, fermentation, nitrogen fixation, etc. They can also cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants, such as tuberculosis, cholera, anthrax, etc.
    - Archaea: prokaryotic cells that are similar to bacteria in some aspects, but differ in their cell wall composition, membrane structure, and gene expression. They are mostly found in extreme environments, such as hot springs, salt lakes, deep sea vents, etc. They can also perform various metabolic processes, such as methanogenesis, sulfur reduction, etc. They are not known to cause diseases in humans, animals, or plants.
    - Eukarya: eukaryotic cells that have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, etc. They can be unicellular or multicellular, and can be classified into four kingdoms: Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. They can also perform various metabolic processes, such as photosynthesis, respiration, fermentation, etc. They can also cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants, such as malaria, candidiasis, ringworm, etc.

- Basic idea of domain bacteria, proteobacteria, non proteobacteria Gram –ve and Gram +ve bacteria
  - Domain bacteria is one of the three domains of life, along with archaea and eukarya. It consists of prokaryotic cells that have a rigid cell wall and a circular DNA molecule. It is divided into several phyla, such as proteobacteria, firmicutes, actinobacteria, cyanobacteria, etc., based on their genetic and phenotypic characteristics.
  - Proteobacteria is a major phylum of domain bacteria, that includes a wide variety of pathogenic and non-pathogenic genera, such as Escherichia, Salmonella, Vibrio, Yersinia, Legionella, etc. They are mostly Gram-negative, meaning that they have a thin layer of peptidoglycan in their cell wall, and a second outer membrane that contains lipopolysaccharides. They can be further classified into five classes: alpha, beta, gamma, delta, and epsilon, based on their molecular phylogeny.
  - Non proteobacteria Gram-negative bacteria are bacteria that belong to other phyla of domain bacteria, such as bacteroidetes, spirochaetes, chlamydiae, etc., that are also Gram-negative, meaning that they have a thin layer of peptidoglycan in their cell wall, and a second outer membrane that contains lipopolysaccharides. They have different genetic and phenotypic characteristics from proteobacteria, and can cause diseases such as syphilis, Lyme disease, gonorrhea, etc.
