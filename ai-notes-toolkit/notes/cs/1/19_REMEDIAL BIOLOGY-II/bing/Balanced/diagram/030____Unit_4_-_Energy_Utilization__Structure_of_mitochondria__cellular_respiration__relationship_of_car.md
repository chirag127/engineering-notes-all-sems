## Unit 4 - Energy Utilization

### Structure of mitochondria

- Mitochondria are small, rod-shaped organelles found in both plant and animal cells .
- They have two membranes, an outer one and an inner one, made of phospholipid layers .
- The outer membrane is smooth and encloses the organelle, while the inner membrane is folded into projections called cristae.
- The space between the two membranes is called the intermembrane space, and the space inside the inner membrane is called the matrix .
- The matrix contains enzymes, ribosomes, DNA, and other molecules involved in energy production .
- The cristae increase the surface area of the inner membrane, where the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation take place .
- Mitochondria are the site of cellular respiration, the process of converting organic molecules into ATP, the main energy currency of the cell .
- Mitochondria have their own DNA and ribosomes, and can replicate independently of the cell .
- Mitochondria are thought to have evolved from ancient bacteria that were engulfed by eukaryotic cells in a symbiotic relationship.

### Cellular respiration

- Cellular respiration is the process of breaking down organic molecules, such as glucose, into carbon dioxide and water, and releasing energy in the form of ATP .
- Cellular respiration can be divided into four stages: glycolysis, formation of acetyl co-A, Krebs cycle, and electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation .
- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, and it takes place in the cytoplasm of the cell .
- Glycolysis splits one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, producing two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH (a coenzyme that carries electrons) in the process .
- Formation of acetyl co-A is the second stage of cellular respiration, and it takes place in the matrix of the mitochondria .
- Formation of acetyl co-A converts each molecule of pyruvate into a molecule of acetyl co-A, releasing one molecule of carbon dioxide and one molecule of NADH in the process .
- Acetyl co-A is a two-carbon molecule that enters the Krebs cycle, the third stage of cellular respiration .
- Krebs cycle is also known as the citric acid cycle or the tricarboxylic acid cycle, and it takes place in the matrix of the mitochondria .
- Krebs cycle completes the oxidation of glucose by producing two molecules of ATP, six molecules of NADH, two molecules of FADH2 (another coenzyme that carries electrons), and four molecules of carbon dioxide for each molecule of glucose .
- Electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation is the fourth and final stage of cellular respiration, and it takes place in the inner membrane of the mitochondria .
- Electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation uses the electrons carried by NADH and FADH2 to generate a proton gradient across the inner membrane, which drives the synthesis of ATP by a protein complex called ATP synthase .
- Electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation produces about 32 molecules of ATP and six molecules of water for each molecule of glucose .
- Cellular respiration can produce up to 36 molecules of ATP per molecule of glucose, depending on the efficiency of the process .
