# Size, Shape, and Arrangement of Bacterial Cells

- Bacterial cells are microscopic organisms that cannot be seen by the naked eye. They are measured in micrometers (µm), which are one-thousandth of a millimeter. The average size of a bacterial cell varies between 0.2 and 2.0 µm .
- Bacterial cells have different shapes and arrangements that help them adapt to different environments and functions. The most common shapes are:
  - Cocci: spherical or oval cells that can be arranged in pairs, chains, clusters, or other patterns .
  - Bacilli: rod-shaped or cylindrical cells that can be arranged in singles, pairs, chains, or palisades .
  - Spirilla: spiral-shaped or curved cells that can be arranged in singles or chains .
- The shape and arrangement of bacterial cells are determined by their cell wall structure, which is composed of peptidoglycan, a polymer of sugars and amino acids. The cell wall provides rigidity and protection to the cell, and also determines its response to certain antibiotics.
- Some examples of bacterial cells with different shapes and arrangements are:
  - Staphylococcus aureus: a coccus that forms grape-like clusters and causes skin infections, food poisoning, and toxic shock syndrome.
  - Escherichia coli: a bacillus that forms single cells or pairs and is found in the intestinal tract of humans and animals. Some strains can cause diarrhea, urinary tract infections, and sepsis.
  - Helicobacter pylori: a spirillum that forms single cells or pairs and is found in the stomach of humans. It can cause gastritis, ulcers, and stomach cancer.
