### Proteobacteria

- Proteobacteria is a major phylum of gram-negative bacteria that are diverse in morphology, metabolism, and ecology .
- Proteobacteria are classified into five classes: alpha-, beta-, gamma-, delta-, and epsilonproteobacteria, based on their phylogenetic relationships .
- Alphaproteobacteria are mostly oligotrophs, organisms that can live in low-nutrient environments, and include some nitrogen-fixing bacteria and plant symbionts.
- Betaproteobacteria are mostly eutrophs, organisms that thrive in high-nutrient environments, and include some human pathogens and soil bacteria.
- Gammaproteobacteria are the largest and most diverse class of proteobacteria, and include many medically and industrially important bacteria, such as Escherichia, Salmonella, Vibrio, Pseudomonas, and Legionella .
- Deltaproteobacteria are mostly anaerobic bacteria that are involved in sulfur and nitrogen cycles, and include some predatory bacteria and sulfate-reducers.
- Epsilonproteobacteria are mostly microaerophilic or anaerobic bacteria that inhabit extreme environments, such as hot springs, deep-sea vents, and animal guts, and include some human pathogens, such as Helicobacter and Campylobacter.
- Proteobacteria play important roles in various biogeochemical cycles, such as carbon, nitrogen, sulfur, and iron cycles, and in bioremediation, biodegradation, and bioleaching.
- Proteobacteria also produce various industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals, such as antibiotics, vitamins, enzymes, amino acids, and organic acids.
