### Lipid and Protein Catabolism

- Lipids and proteins are important macromolecules that can be used as energy sources by living organisms.
- Lipids are composed of glycerol and fatty acids, while proteins are composed of amino acids.
- Lipids and proteins are broken down by specific enzymes into smaller molecules that can enter the central metabolic pathways of glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.
- Lipid catabolism involves the following steps:
  - Lipases hydrolyze lipids into glycerol and free fatty acids.
  - Glycerol is converted into dihydroxyacetone phosphate, which can enter glycolysis.
  - Fatty acids are activated by coenzyme A and transported into the mitochondria by carnitine.
  - Fatty acids undergo beta-oxidation, which produces acetyl-CoA, NADH, and FADH2.
  - Acetyl-CoA can enter the citric acid cycle, while NADH and FADH2 can donate electrons to the electron transport chain.
- Protein catabolism involves the following steps:
  - Proteases hydrolyze proteins into smaller peptides and amino acids.
  - Peptides and amino acids are transported into the cells by specific transporters.
  - Amino acids undergo deamination, which removes the amino group and produces ammonia and keto acids.
  - Ammonia is converted into urea, which is excreted by the kidneys.
  - Keto acids can be converted into pyruvate, acetyl-CoA, or intermediates of the citric acid cycle, depending on their structure.
  - Pyruvate, acetyl-CoA, and citric acid cycle intermediates can enter the central metabolic pathways and produce ATP, NADH, and FADH2.
- Lipid and protein catabolism are regulated by hormonal and nutritional factors, such as insulin, glucagon, epinephrine, and the availability of glucose and oxygen.
