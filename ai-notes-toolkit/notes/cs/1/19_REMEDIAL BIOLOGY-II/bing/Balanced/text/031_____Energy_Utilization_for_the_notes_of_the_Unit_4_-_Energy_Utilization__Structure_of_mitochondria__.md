### Energy Utilization

- Energy utilization is the process of converting chemical energy into useful work by biological systems.
- Energy utilization involves the breakdown of organic molecules, such as glucose, by cellular respiration, which releases energy that can be used to synthesize adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the primary energy currency of all cells .
- Energy utilization is influenced by various factors, such as temperature, oxygen availability, enzyme activity, substrate concentration, and hormonal regulation.

#### Structure of mitochondria

- Mitochondria are organelles that are the site of cellular respiration and energy production in eukaryotic cells.
- Mitochondria have a double membrane structure, with an outer membrane and an inner membrane that folds into cristae, which increase the surface area for energy production.
- Mitochondria have their own DNA, ribosomes, and enzymes, and can replicate independently of the cell.
- Mitochondria are involved in various cellular processes, such as apoptosis, calcium signaling, and oxidative stress.

#### Cellular respiration

- Cellular respiration is the process of oxidizing organic molecules, such as glucose, to produce carbon dioxide, water, and energy in the form of ATP .
- Cellular respiration can be divided into four stages: glycolysis, formation of acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA), Krebs cycle, and electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation .
- Cellular respiration can be aerobic or anaerobic, depending on the presence or absence of oxygen .
- Aerobic respiration is more efficient and produces more ATP than anaerobic respiration, which produces lactic acid or ethanol as byproducts .

#### Relationship of carbohydrate metabolism to other compounds

- Carbohydrate metabolism is the process of breaking down and synthesizing carbohydrates, such as glucose, glycogen, starch, and cellulose, in living organisms.
- Carbohydrate metabolism is linked to other metabolic pathways, such as lipid metabolism, protein metabolism, and nucleic acid metabolism, by the intermediates of glycolysis and the Krebs cycle.
- Carbohydrate metabolism can provide energy, store energy, or generate building blocks for other molecules, depending on the cellular needs and environmental conditions.

#### Glycolysis

- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, in which glucose is split into two molecules of pyruvate, producing two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH (reduced form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) as energy carriers .
- Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell and does not require oxygen .
- Glycolysis can be divided into two phases: the preparatory phase, in which glucose is phosphorylated and cleaved, and the payoff phase, in which pyruvate and energy carriers are formed .
- Glycolysis is regulated by several enzymes, such as hexokinase, phosphofructokinase, and pyruvate kinase, which respond to the levels of ATP, ADP, AMP, NAD+, NADH, and other metabolites .

#### Formation of acetyl-CoA

- Formation of acetyl-CoA is the second stage of cellular respiration, in which pyruvate is converted into acetyl-CoA, producing one molecule of CO2 and one molecule of NADH per pyruvate .
- Formation of acetyl-CoA occurs in the mitochondrial matrix and requires oxygen .
- Formation of acetyl-CoA is catalyzed by a complex of enzymes called the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, which is regulated by the levels of NAD+, NADH, acetyl-CoA, and coenzyme A .
- Formation of acetyl-CoA links glycolysis to the Krebs cycle, as acetyl-CoA is the main substrate for the Krebs cycle .
