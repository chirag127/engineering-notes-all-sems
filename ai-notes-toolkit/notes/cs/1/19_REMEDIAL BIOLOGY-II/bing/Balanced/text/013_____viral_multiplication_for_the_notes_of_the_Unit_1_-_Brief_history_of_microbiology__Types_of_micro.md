### Viral Multiplication

- Viral multiplication is the process of forming new viruses by using the host cell's machinery and resources.
- Viruses can only multiply inside living cells that are susceptible and permissive to their infection.
- The viral life cycle consists of six basic stages: attachment, penetration, uncoating, biosynthesis, assembly and release.
- Attachment: The virus binds to specific receptors on the surface of the host cell.
- Penetration: The virus or its genetic material enters the host cell by endocytosis or membrane fusion.
- Uncoating: The viral capsid is removed and the viral genome is released into the cytoplasm or nucleus of the host cell.
- Biosynthesis: The viral genome is replicated and the viral proteins are synthesized using the host cell's enzymes and ribosomes.
- Assembly: The newly synthesized viral components are assembled into mature virions.
- Release: The new virions are released from the host cell by budding or lysis.
- The viral multiplication can follow two different cycles: lytic cycle or lysogenic cycle.
- Lytic cycle: The virus replicates and destroys the host cell, releasing new virions. This cycle is typical of bacteriophages and some animal viruses.
- Lysogenic cycle: The virus integrates its genome into the host cell's genome and remains dormant until a stimulus triggers its activation. The virus then enters the lytic cycle and produces new virions. This cycle is typical of some bacteriophages and some animal viruses, such as herpesviruses and retroviruses.
