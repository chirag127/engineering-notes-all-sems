## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells

- Bacterial cells are microscopic organisms that cannot be seen by the naked eye. They are measured in micrometers (µm), which are one-thousandth of a millimeter. The average size of a bacterial cell varies between 0.2 and 2.0 µm  .
- Bacterial cells have different shapes and arrangements that help them adapt to different environments and perform different functions. The most common shapes are:
  - Cocci: spherical or oval cells that can be arranged in pairs (diplococci), chains (streptococci), clusters (staphylococci), tetrads (groups of four), sarcinae (cubical packets of eight), or other patterns  .
  - Bacilli: rod-shaped or cylindrical cells that can be arranged in singles, pairs (diplobacilli), chains (streptobacilli), or palisades (parallel rows of cells attached by their ends)  .
  - Spirilla: spiral-shaped or curved cells that can be rigid (spirilla) or flexible (spirochetes). They can be arranged in singles or in tufts  .
- Bacterial cells have a simple structure that consists of a cell membrane, a cell wall, a cytoplasm, and a nucleoid. Some bacterial cells also have additional structures, such as:
  - Flagella: long, thin, whip-like appendages that help the cell move by rotating .
  - Pili: short, hair-like projections that help the cell attach to surfaces or exchange genetic material with other cells .
  - Capsule: a thick, sticky layer of polysaccharides or proteins that surrounds the cell wall and protects the cell from dehydration, phagocytosis, or antibiotics .
  - Plasmid: a small, circular piece of DNA that can replicate independently of the nucleoid and carry genes for antibiotic resistance, virulence, or metabolism .
  - Ribosome: a small, spherical structure that synthesizes proteins from mRNA .
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells. They are measured in micrometers (µm) or nanometers (nm), which are one-billionth of a meter. The average size of a eukaryotic cell varies between 10 and 100 µm.
- Eukaryotic cells have different shapes and arrangements that depend on their function and location. Some examples are:
  - Animal cells: round or irregular cells that have a cell membrane, a cytoplasm, a nucleus, and various organelles, such as mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, peroxisomes, cytoskeleton, and centrioles.
  - Plant cells: rectangular or square cells that have a cell membrane, a cell wall, a cytoplasm, a nucleus, and various organelles, such as chloroplasts, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, vacuoles, cytoskeleton, and plasmodesmata.
  - Fungal cells: oval or elongated cells that have a cell membrane, a cell wall, a cytoplasm, a nucleus, and various organelles, such as mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, vacuoles, cytoskeleton, and septa.
  - Protist cells: diverse and variable cells that have a cell membrane, a cytoplasm, a nucleus, and various organelles, such as flagella, cilia, pseudopodia, mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, vacuoles, cytoskeleton, and contractile vacuoles.
