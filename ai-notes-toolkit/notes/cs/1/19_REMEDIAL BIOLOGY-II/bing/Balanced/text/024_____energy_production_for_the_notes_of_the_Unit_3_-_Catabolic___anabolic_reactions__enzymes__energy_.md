### Energy production for the notes of the Unit 3 - Catabolic & anabolic reactions: enzymes, energy production and carbohydrate metabolism. Lipid & protein catabolism, Energy production mechanism, metabolic diversity & pathways of energy use. Integration of metabolism in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS

- Energy production is the process of converting different sources of energy, such as chemical, light, or nuclear energy, into forms that can be used by living cells, such as ATP (adenosine triphosphate).
- Energy production involves two types of metabolic pathways: catabolic and anabolic.
- Catabolic pathways are those that break down large molecules into smaller ones, releasing energy in the process. Examples of catabolic pathways are glycolysis, which breaks down glucose into pyruvate, and cellular respiration, which oxidizes pyruvate into carbon dioxide and water, generating ATP and reducing power (NADH and FADH2).
- Anabolic pathways are those that synthesize large molecules from smaller ones, requiring energy in the process. Examples of anabolic pathways are photosynthesis, which converts light energy into chemical energy and builds glucose from carbon dioxide and water, and biosynthesis, which uses ATP and reducing power to make amino acids, nucleotides, lipids, and other biomolecules.
- Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up the rate of chemical reactions in metabolic pathways. Enzymes lower the activation energy of the reactions, making them more likely to occur. Enzymes are highly specific for their substrates and products, and can be regulated by various factors, such as temperature, pH, inhibitors, activators, and feedback mechanisms.
- Carbohydrate metabolism is the set of catabolic and anabolic pathways that involve the breakdown and synthesis of carbohydrates, such as sugars and starches. Carbohydrate metabolism provides energy and building blocks for other metabolic pathways, and is essential for maintaining blood glucose levels and cellular homeostasis.
- Lipid catabolism is the process of breaking down lipids, such as fats and oils, into glycerol and fatty acids. Lipid catabolism provides energy and reducing power for other metabolic pathways, and is important for storing and mobilizing energy in adipose tissue.
- Protein catabolism is the process of breaking down proteins into amino acids. Protein catabolism provides energy and nitrogen for other metabolic pathways, and is involved in regulating protein turnover and amino acid balance.
- Energy production mechanism is the way that cells convert different sources of energy into ATP, the universal energy currency of life. The main energy production mechanism in most cells is oxidative phosphorylation, which uses the electron transport chain and chemiosmosis to generate ATP from the oxidation of NADH and FADH2. Other energy production mechanisms include substrate-level phosphorylation, which directly transfers a phosphate group from a substrate to ADP, and photophosphorylation, which uses light energy to drive the electron transport chain and chemiosmosis in photosynthetic organisms.
- Metabolic diversity is the variation in the types and pathways of metabolism among different organisms, groups, or environments. Metabolic diversity reflects the adaptation of organisms to different ecological niches and evolutionary histories. Examples of metabolic diversity include aerobic and anaerobic respiration, fermentation, chemosynthesis, and different modes of carbon and nitrogen fixation.
- Pathways of energy use are the ways that cells use ATP and reducing power to perform different cellular functions, such as growth, maintenance, repair, movement, signaling, and reproduction. Pathways of energy use depend on the type and demand of the cell, and can be regulated by various factors, such as availability of nutrients, oxygen, hormones, and signals from other cells or the environment.
- Integration of metabolism is the coordination and regulation of different metabolic pathways to achieve optimal cellular performance and homeostasis. Integration of metabolism involves various mechanisms, such as allosteric regulation, covalent modification, transcriptional regulation, and hormonal regulation, that modulate the activity and expression of enzymes and genes involved in metabolism. Integration of metabolism also involves communication and cooperation among different cells, tissues, and organs, such as the liver, muscle, adipose tissue, and brain, to maintain whole-body metabolism and energy balance.
