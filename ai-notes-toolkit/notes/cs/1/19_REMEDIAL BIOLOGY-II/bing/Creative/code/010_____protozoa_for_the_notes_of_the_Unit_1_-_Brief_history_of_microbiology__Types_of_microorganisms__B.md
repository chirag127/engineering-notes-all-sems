### Protozoa

- Protozoa are **microscopic unicellular eukaryotes** that have a relatively complex internal structure and carry out complex metabolic activities  .
- Some protozoa have structures for **propulsion or other types of movement**, such as flagella, cilia, or pseudopodia  .
- Protozoa can be either **free-living or parasitic**, that feed on organic matter such as other microorganisms or organic tissues and debris .
- Protozoa are **heterotrophic**, meaning they use organic carbon as a source of energy .
- Protozoa have a **membrane-bound nucleus** and other organelles, such as mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and lysosomes .
- Protozoa can reproduce **asexually** by binary fission, multiple fission, budding, or schizogony, or **sexually** by conjugation, syngamy, or gametogony .
- Protozoa are currently classified into **six phyla** on the basis of light and electron microscopic morphology . These are:
  - **Sarcomastigophora**, which includes flagellates and amoebae.
  - **Apicomplexa**, which includes intracellular parasites such as Plasmodium, Toxoplasma, and Cryptosporidium.
  - **Ciliophora**, which includes ciliates such as Paramecium, Stentor, and Vorticella.
  - **Microspora**, which includes spore-forming parasites such as Encephalitozoon and Nosema.
  - **Ascetosporea**, which includes parasites of marine invertebrates such as Myxozoa and Haplosporidia.
  - **Myxozoa**, which includes parasites of fish and aquatic worms such as Myxobolus and Ceratomyxa.
- Protozoa are important in many ecological and biomedical aspects, such as nutrient cycling, decomposition, symbiosis, pathogenesis, and evolution  .
