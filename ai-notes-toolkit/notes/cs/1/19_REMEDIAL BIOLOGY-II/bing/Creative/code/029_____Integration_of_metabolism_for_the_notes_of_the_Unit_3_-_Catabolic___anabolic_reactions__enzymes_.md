### Integration of metabolism

- Metabolism is the sum of all the chemical reactions that take place in a living cell to maintain its structure and function.
- Metabolism can be divided into two major types: catabolism and anabolism.
- Catabolism is the breakdown of complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy in the process. Examples of catabolic reactions are glycolysis, lipid oxidation, and protein degradation.
- Anabolism is the synthesis of complex molecules from simpler ones, requiring energy in the process. Examples of anabolic reactions are gluconeogenesis, lipid synthesis, and protein synthesis.
- Metabolism is not a random process, but a highly coordinated and regulated one. Different metabolic pathways are interconnected and influenced by various factors, such as the availability of substrates, the demand for energy, the hormonal status, and the environmental conditions.
- Integration of metabolism refers to the harmonious functioning of different metabolic pathways in different tissues and organs to maintain the homeostasis of the whole organism.
- Integration of metabolism is achieved by several mechanisms, such as allosteric regulation, covalent modification, transcriptional regulation, and hormonal regulation.
- Allosteric regulation is the binding of a molecule to an enzyme at a site other than the active site, affecting its activity. Allosteric regulators can be either activators or inhibitors. For example, ATP is an allosteric inhibitor of phosphofructokinase, a key enzyme in glycolysis, while AMP is an allosteric activator of the same enzyme.
- Covalent modification is the addition or removal of a chemical group to or from an enzyme, affecting its activity. Covalent modifications are usually reversible and mediated by specific enzymes. For example, phosphorylation is a common covalent modification that can activate or inhibit enzymes, depending on the site and the enzyme. Phosphorylation is catalyzed by kinases and reversed by phosphatases.
- Transcriptional regulation is the control of gene expression by altering the rate of transcription of DNA into RNA. Transcriptional regulation is influenced by various factors, such as the binding of transcription factors, the methylation of DNA, and the acetylation of histones. For example, the expression of genes involved in lipid synthesis is increased by the transcription factor SREBP, which is activated by low cholesterol levels in the cell.
- Hormonal regulation is the control of metabolism by hormones, which are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands and transported by the blood to target tissues. Hormones can affect metabolism by binding to specific receptors on the cell membrane or inside the cell, triggering a cascade of events that alter the activity or expression of enzymes. For example, insulin is a hormone that stimulates glucose uptake and anabolic pathways, while glucagon is a hormone that stimulates glucose release and catabolic pathways.
