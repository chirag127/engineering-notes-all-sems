- Energy production is the process of converting different sources of energy, such as chemical, light, or mechanical, into a usable form, such as ATP, the universal energy currency of cells.
- Energy production involves two types of metabolic pathways: catabolic and anabolic.
- Catabolic pathways are those that break down large molecules into smaller ones, releasing energy in the process. Examples of catabolic pathways are glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation, which are involved in carbohydrate metabolism; beta-oxidation, which is involved in lipid metabolism; and proteolysis, which is involved in protein metabolism.
- Anabolic pathways are those that synthesize large molecules from smaller ones, requiring energy in the process. Examples of anabolic pathways are gluconeogenesis, the synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate sources; lipogenesis, the synthesis of fatty acids and triglycerides; and protein synthesis, the synthesis of amino acids and polypeptides.
- Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up the rate of chemical reactions in metabolic pathways. Enzymes lower the activation energy of the reactions, making them more likely to occur. Enzymes are highly specific for their substrates and products, and are regulated by various factors, such as temperature, pH, inhibitors, and activators.
- Energy production mechanism is the process of coupling the energy released from catabolic pathways to the energy required for anabolic pathways. The most common energy production mechanism in cells is the chemiosmotic coupling, which involves the generation of a proton gradient across a membrane by the electron transport chain, and the use of this gradient to drive the synthesis of ATP by the enzyme ATP synthase.
- Metabolic diversity is the variation in the types and pathways of energy production among different organisms, depending on their environmental conditions and evolutionary history. Some examples of metabolic diversity are:
  - Aerobic respiration, which uses oxygen as the final electron acceptor and produces the most ATP per glucose molecule.
  - Anaerobic respiration, which uses other molecules, such as nitrate, sulfate, or carbon dioxide, as the final electron acceptor and produces less ATP per glucose molecule.
  - Fermentation, which does not use an electron transport chain and produces the least ATP per glucose molecule, but allows the regeneration of NAD+ for glycolysis.
  - Photosynthesis, which uses light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen, and involves two stages: the light reactions and the Calvin cycle.
  - Chemolithotrophy, which uses inorganic molecules, such as hydrogen, sulfur, or iron, as the source of electrons and energy, and carbon dioxide as the source of carbon.
  - Chemoorganotrophy, which uses organic molecules, such as glucose, as the source of electrons, energy, and carbon.
- Pathways of energy use are the ways that cells use the energy produced from different sources to perform various functions, such as growth, maintenance, movement, signaling, and reproduction. Some examples of pathways of energy use are:
  - Biosynthesis, which uses energy to synthesize the building blocks of macromolecules, such as nucleotides, amino acids, and fatty acids.
  - Transport, which uses energy to move molecules and ions across membranes, such as the sodium-potassium pump, the proton pump, and the glucose transporter.
  - Mechanical work, which uses energy to generate force and movement, such as the actin-myosin interaction in muscle contraction, the flagellar rotation in bacterial motility, and the kinesin-dynein interaction in intracellular transport.
  - Electrical work, which uses energy to generate and maintain electrical potentials across membranes, such as the action potential in nerve cells, the resting potential in muscle cells, and the membrane potential in plant cells.
  - Heat production, which uses energy to maintain a constant body temperature, such as the brown fat tissue in mammals, the shivering in birds, and the thermogenesis in plants.
