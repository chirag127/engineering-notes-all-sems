### Lipid and Protein Catabolism

Lipids and proteins are important macromolecules that serve as energy sources and structural components for living cells. They can be broken down into smaller molecules through catabolic reactions that release energy and feed into the central metabolic pathways.

- Lipid catabolism involves the hydrolysis of triglycerides (fats) into glycerol and fatty acids by lipases, enzymes that are secreted by some microorganisms or released from adipose tissue in animals  .
- Glycerol can be converted into dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP), an intermediate of glycolysis, by a series of enzymatic reactions  .
- Fatty acids can be activated by coenzyme A (CoA) and transported into the mitochondrial matrix by carnitine shuttle, where they undergo beta-oxidation, a cyclic process that cleaves two-carbon units from the acyl-CoA chain and produces acetyl-CoA, NADH, and FADH2  .
- Acetyl-CoA can enter the citric acid cycle (also known as the Krebs cycle or the tricarboxylic acid cycle) and generate more NADH, FADH2, and GTP, which can be used for oxidative phosphorylation and ATP synthesis  .
- Protein catabolism involves the proteolysis of proteins into amino acids by proteases, enzymes that are secreted by some microorganisms or released from lysosomes in animal cells  .
- Amino acids can be transported into the cytoplasm and deaminated, a process that removes the amino group and produces ammonia and keto acids  .
- Ammonia can be excreted as waste or converted into urea or uric acid in some animals, or assimilated into other nitrogen-containing compounds in some microorganisms  .
- Keto acids can be converted into pyruvate, acetyl-CoA, or other intermediates of the citric acid cycle, depending on their chemical structures  .
- Pyruvate and acetyl-CoA can enter the citric acid cycle and generate more NADH, FADH2, and GTP, which can be used for oxidative phosphorylation and ATP synthesis  .
