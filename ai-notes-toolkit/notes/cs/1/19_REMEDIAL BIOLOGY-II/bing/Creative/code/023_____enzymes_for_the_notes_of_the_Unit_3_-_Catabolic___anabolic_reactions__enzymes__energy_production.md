### Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up the rate of biochemical reactions by lowering the activation energy. Enzymes are highly specific for their substrates and can be regulated by various factors such as temperature, pH, inhibitors, activators, and feedback mechanisms. Enzymes can be classified into two main types based on the direction of the reaction they catalyze: anabolic and catabolic enzymes.

#### Anabolic Enzymes

Anabolic enzymes are involved in the synthesis of larger complex molecules from smaller units. Anabolic reactions are also called biosynthetic reactions because they produce new biomolecules that are essential for the growth and maintenance of cells. Anabolic reactions consume energy, usually in the form of ATP, and are endergonic (positive ΔG). An example of an anabolic reaction is photosynthesis, where plants make glucose molecules from carbon dioxide and water using light energy. Some examples of anabolic enzymes are:

- DNA polymerase: synthesizes DNA from nucleotides
- RNA polymerase: synthesizes RNA from nucleotides
- Ribosome: synthesizes proteins from amino acids
- Hexokinase: adds a phosphate group to glucose
- RuBisCO: fixes carbon dioxide into organic molecules

#### Catabolic Enzymes

Catabolic enzymes are involved in the breakdown of larger complex molecules into smaller units. Catabolic reactions are also called degradative reactions because they degrade biomolecules that are no longer needed or that can be used as energy sources. Catabolic reactions release energy, usually in the form of ATP, and are exergonic (negative ΔG). An example of a catabolic reaction is cellular respiration, where glucose molecules are oxidized to carbon dioxide and water, releasing energy. Some examples of catabolic enzymes are:

- Amylase: breaks down starch into glucose
- Lipase: breaks down fats into glycerol and fatty acids
- Protease: breaks down proteins into amino acids
- Pyruvate kinase: removes a phosphate group from pyruvate
- Cytochrome c oxidase: transfers electrons from cytochrome c to oxygen
