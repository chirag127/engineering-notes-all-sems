### Formation of Acetyl Co-A

- Acetyl Co-A (acetyl coenzyme A) is a molecule that participates in many biochemical reactions in protein, carbohydrate and lipid metabolism.
- Its main function is to deliver the acetyl group to the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) to be oxidized for energy production.
- Acetyl Co-A formation occurs inside or outside the cell mitochondria. As a metabolite (a substance necessary for metabolism), acetyl Co-A must be freely available.
- It can be produced via the catabolism (breakdown) of carbohydrates (glucose) and lipids (fatty acids).
- Acetyl Co-A formation most commonly occurs during glucose catabolism. After carbohydrates have been broken down by digestive enzymes, the first stage of cellular glucose metabolism or glycolysis can begin. Glycolysis is the breaking down of glucose molecules.
- Glycolysis produces two molecules of pyruvate (a three-carbon compound) per molecule of glucose. Pyruvate then enters the mitochondria, where it is converted into acetyl Co-A by a multi-enzyme complex called pyruvate dehydrogenase.
- The conversion of pyruvate to acetyl Co-A involves the removal of a carboxyl group as carbon dioxide and the transfer of an acetyl group to coenzyme A. This reaction also produces one molecule of NADH (a high-energy electron carrier) per pyruvate.
- The overall formation reaction of acetyl Co-A may be represented as:

  `pyruvic acid + CoA + NAD+ → acetyl Co-A + NADH + H+ + CO2`

- This reaction may be called the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvic acid to acetyl Co-A.
- Acetyl Co-A then enters the citric acid cycle, where it combines with a four-carbon molecule and goes through a cycle of reactions, ultimately regenerating the four-carbon starting molecule.
- The citric acid cycle produces ATP (or, in some cases, GTP), NADH, and FADH2 (another high-energy electron carrier), and releases carbon dioxide.
- The NADH and FADH2 molecules then donate their electrons to the electron transport chain, where a series of redox reactions generate a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- The proton gradient drives the synthesis of ATP by a membrane-bound enzyme called ATP synthase. This process is called oxidative phosphorylation.
- The formation of acetyl Co-A is a key step in cellular respiration, as it links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle and the electron transport chain.
- The formation of acetyl Co-A is also influenced by various factors, such as the availability of oxygen, glucose, fatty acids, and other substrates, as well as the hormonal and enzymatic regulation of the metabolic pathways.
