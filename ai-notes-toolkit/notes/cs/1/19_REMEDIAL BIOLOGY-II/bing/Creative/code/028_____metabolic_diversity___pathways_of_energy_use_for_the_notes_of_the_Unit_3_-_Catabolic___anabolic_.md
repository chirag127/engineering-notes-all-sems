# Metabolic Diversity and Pathways of Energy Use

- Metabolic diversity mainly refers to the different metabolic strategies that organisms have evolved to obtain energy.
- Metabolic pathways are series of connected chemical reactions that feed one another and convert starting molecules into products.
- Metabolic pathways can be broadly divided into two categories based on their effects:
  - Catabolic pathways break down complex molecules into simpler ones and release energy. Examples are glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.
  - Anabolic pathways synthesize complex molecules from simpler ones and require energy. Examples are photosynthesis, gluconeogenesis, and protein synthesis.
- Metabolic pathways evolved among prokaryotes before eukaryotes arose as the result of their interaction and coevolution with changing physicochemical environmental conditions.
- Metabolic diversity is reflected in the different types of energy sources and electron acceptors that organisms can use:
  - Energy sources can be organic (such as glucose) or inorganic (such as hydrogen or ammonia). Organisms that use organic compounds as energy sources are called organotrophs, while those that use inorganic compounds are called lithotrophs.
  - Electron acceptors can be oxygen (aerobic respiration), nitrate (denitrification), sulfate (sulfate reduction), carbon dioxide (methanogenesis), or other compounds. Organisms that use oxygen as the electron acceptor are called aerobes, while those that use other compounds are called anaerobes.
- Metabolic diversity is also reflected in the different metabolic pathways that organisms can use to produce ATP, the universal energy currency of cells :
  - The phosphagen system (ATP-PC system) for immediate energy. This system uses creatine phosphate (CP) to regenerate ATP from ADP in a single enzymatic reaction. This system is used for short bursts of high-intensity activity, such as sprinting or lifting weights.
  - The glycolytic system (anaerobic glycolysis) for short-term energy. This system breaks down glucose into pyruvate and produces two ATP molecules per glucose molecule. This system is used for moderate-intensity activity, such as jogging or cycling.
  - The oxidative system (aerobic respiration) for long-term energy. This system involves the complete oxidation of glucose, fatty acids, or amino acids into carbon dioxide and water, and produces 36 to 38 ATP molecules per glucose molecule. This system is used for low-intensity activity, such as walking or resting.
- Metabolic diversity allows organisms to adapt to different environmental conditions and to exploit different ecological niches .
- Metabolic diversity also influences the biogeochemical cycles of elements, such as carbon, nitrogen, sulfur, and iron, by transforming their chemical forms and availability .
- Metabolic diversity is a result of evolutionary processes that have shaped the metabolic capabilities of organisms over billions of years .
