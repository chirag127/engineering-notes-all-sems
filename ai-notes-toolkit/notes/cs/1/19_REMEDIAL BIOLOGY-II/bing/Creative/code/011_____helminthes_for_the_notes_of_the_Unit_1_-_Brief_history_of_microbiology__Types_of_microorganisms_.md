# Helminthes

- Helminthes are **worm-like parasites** that can infect humans and other animals  .
- Helminthes are classified into three major groups based on their shape and habitat :
  - **Flukes (trematodes)**: flat, leaf-shaped worms that live in the liver, lungs, blood vessels, or intestines of the host.
  - **Tapeworms (cestodes)**: segmented, ribbon-like worms that live in the intestines of the host and absorb nutrients from the digested food.
  - **Roundworms (nematodes)**: cylindrical, thread-like worms that live in various tissues or organs of the host, such as the intestines, blood, lymphatic system, or subcutaneous tissues.
- Helminthes have complex life cycles that involve one or more intermediate hosts, such as snails, fish, insects, or mammals, where they undergo larval development before infecting the definitive host  .
- Helminthes can cause various diseases in humans, such as schistosomiasis, cysticercosis, filariasis, ascariasis, hookworm infection, trichinosis, and onchocerciasis  .
- Helminthes can be diagnosed by microscopic examination of stool, urine, blood, or tissue samples, or by serological tests that detect antibodies or antigens in the host's blood .
- Helminthes can be treated by anthelmintic drugs that kill or expel the worms from the host's body, such as praziquantel, albendazole, mebendazole, ivermectin, or diethylcarbamazine .
- Helminthes can be prevented by improving sanitation, hygiene, and access to clean water, as well as by avoiding contact with contaminated soil, water, food, or animals .
