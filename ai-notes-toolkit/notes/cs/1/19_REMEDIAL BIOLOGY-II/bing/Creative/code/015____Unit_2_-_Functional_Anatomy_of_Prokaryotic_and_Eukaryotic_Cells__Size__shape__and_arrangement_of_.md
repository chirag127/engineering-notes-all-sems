# Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

## Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacteria are microscopic organisms that can have various shapes and arrangements.
- The size of bacterial cells ranges from 0.2 to 10 micrometers (µm) in diameter, and from 2 to 20 µm in length. The average size of a bacterial cell is about 1 µm in diameter and 5 µm in length.
- The shape of bacterial cells can be classified into three main categories: cocci (spherical), bacilli (rod-shaped), and spirilla (spiral or curved). Some bacteria have more complex shapes, such as vibrios (comma-shaped), spirochetes (corkscrew-shaped), or filamentous (long and thin).
- The arrangement of bacterial cells depends on their pattern of division and attachment. Some bacteria remain attached after dividing, forming chains, clusters, or pairs. Some examples of bacterial arrangements are:
  - Diplococci: pairs of cocci
  - Streptococci: chains of cocci
  - Staphylococci: grape-like clusters of cocci
  - Diplobacilli: pairs of bacilli
  - Streptobacilli: chains of bacilli
  - Palisades: parallel rows of bacilli
  - Spirilla: single or loose coils of spiral cells
  - Spirochetes: tight coils of spiral cells

## Structure and function of cells

- Cells are the basic units of life that perform various functions, such as metabolism, growth, reproduction, and response to stimuli.
- Cells can be divided into two major types: prokaryotic and eukaryotic. Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, and lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells, and have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- The structure and function of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells can be compared as follows:

| Structure | Prokaryotic Cell | Eukaryotic Cell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Circular, in cytoplasm | Linear, in nucleus |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Cell wall | Present, made of peptidoglycan | Present in plants and fungi, made of cellulose or chitin |
| Plasma membrane | Present, made of phospholipids and proteins | Present, made of phospholipids and proteins |
| Ribosomes | Present, smaller (70S) | Present, larger (80S) |
| Cytoskeleton | Absent | Present |
| Flagella | Present, made of flagellin | Present in some cells, made of microtubules |
| Pili | Present, for attachment and conjugation | Absent |
| Capsule | Present in some cells, for protection and adhesion | Absent |
| Endospore | Present in some cells, for survival under harsh conditions | Absent |
| Plasmid | Present in some cells, for extra genetic information | Absent |
