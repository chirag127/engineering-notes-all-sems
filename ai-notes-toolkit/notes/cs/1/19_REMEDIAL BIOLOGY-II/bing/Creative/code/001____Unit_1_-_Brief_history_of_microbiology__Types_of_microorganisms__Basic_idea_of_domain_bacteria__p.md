### Brief history of microbiology
- Microbiology is the study of microscopic organisms, such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and protozoa.
- Microbiology essentially began with the development of the microscope in the 17th century.
- Antonie van Leeuwenhoek (1632–1723) was the first to observe and document microorganisms using a microscope of his own design  .
- Robert Hooke (1635–1703) was the first to use a microscope to observe living things, such as fungi and cells .
- Ferdinand Cohn (1828–1898) was the founder of bacteriology, a subdiscipline of microbiology, and described several bacteria, such as Bacillus and Beggiatoa.
- Louis Pasteur (1822–1895) was a pioneer of microbiology and demonstrated the role of microorganisms in fermentation, spoilage, and disease.
- Robert Koch (1843–1910) was a microbiologist who established the germ theory of disease and developed methods to isolate and identify pathogens.
- Other notable microbiologists include Alexander Fleming (1881–1955), who discovered penicillin, the first antibiotic; Martinus Beijerinck (1851–1931), who coined the term virus and discovered the first virus; and Carl Woese (1928–2012), who proposed the three-domain system of classification based on molecular phylogeny.

### Types of microorganisms
- Microorganisms are classified into different groups based on their structure, function, and evolutionary relationships.
- The three-domain system of classification divides all living organisms into three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya.
- Bacteria and Archaea are prokaryotes, which means they lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually unicellular and have diverse shapes, metabolisms, and habitats.
- Eukarya are eukaryotes, which means they have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They can be unicellular or multicellular and include animals, plants, fungi, and protists.
- Protists are a diverse group of eukaryotic microorganisms that do not fit into the other kingdoms. They include algae, protozoa, slime molds, and water molds.
- Fungi are eukaryotic microorganisms that are heterotrophic, which means they obtain organic molecules from other sources. They include yeasts, molds, and mushrooms.
- Viruses are non-living entities that consist of genetic material (DNA or RNA) enclosed in a protein coat. They can only replicate inside a host cell and cause various diseases.

### Basic idea of domain bacteria, proteobacteria, non proteobacteria Gram –ve and Gram +ve bacteria
- Bacteria are the most abundant and diverse group of prokaryotes. They can be found in almost every environment and play important roles in the biosphere.
- Bacteria are classified into different phyla based on their genetic and phenotypic characteristics. One of the major criteria for classification is the Gram stain, which differentiates bacteria based on the structure of their cell wall.
- Gram-positive bacteria have a thick layer of peptidoglycan, a polymer of sugar and amino acids, in their cell wall. They stain purple with the Gram stain and are resistant to some antibiotics, such as penicillin.
