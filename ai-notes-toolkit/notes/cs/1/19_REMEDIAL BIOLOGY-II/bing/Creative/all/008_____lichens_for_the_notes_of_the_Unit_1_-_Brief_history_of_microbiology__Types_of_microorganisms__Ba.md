# Lichens

- Lichens are **composite organisms** that consist of a **symbiotic association** of algae (usually green) or cyanobacteria and fungi (mostly ascomycetes and basidiomycetes).
- The dominant partner is the **fungus**, which gives the lichen the majority of its characteristics, from its **thallus shape** to its **fruiting bodies**. The alga can be either a green alga or a blue-green alga, otherwise known as **cyanobacteria**.
- Lichens are found **worldwide** and occur in a variety of **environmental conditions**. They are **pioneer species**, among the first living things to grow on bare rock or areas denuded of life by a disaster.
- Lichens have different **growth forms**, such as crustose (crusty), foliose (leafy), fruticose (shrubby), or squamulose (scaly). They can also have different **reproductive structures**, such as soredia, isidia, apothecia, or pycnidia.
- Lichens have various **roles** in the ecosystem, such as **nitrogen fixation**, **soil formation**, **biomonitoring**, **bioindication**, **lichenometry**, and **food and medicine** for humans and animals.
