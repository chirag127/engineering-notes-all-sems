# Nonproteobacteria Gram-Negative Bacteria

- Nonproteobacteria are a diverse group of bacteria that do not belong to the phylum Proteobacteria, which contains the majority of gram-negative bacteria.
- Nonproteobacteria can be either gram-negative or gram-positive, depending on the presence or absence of an outer membrane in their cell wall.
- Nonproteobacteria can have various metabolic modes, such as photoautotrophy, chemoheterotrophy, or chemoautotrophy.
- Some examples of nonproteobacteria are:

  - **Chlamydia**: These are obligate intracellular parasites that infect animals and humans, causing diseases such as trachoma, chlamydia, and lymphogranuloma venereum. They have a unique developmental cycle that involves two forms: elementary bodies and reticulate bodies. They lack peptidoglycan in their cell wall and depend on their host for ATP and other nutrients.
  - **Spirochetes**: These are motile, spiral-shaped bacteria with a long, narrow body. They have a unique structure called the axial filament, which is a bundle of flagella that runs between the inner and outer membranes of the cell wall. They can move by rotating their body like a corkscrew. They are difficult or impossible to culture in the laboratory. Some spirochetes are pathogens, such as Treponema pallidum (causes syphilis), Borrelia burgdorferi (causes Lyme disease), and Leptospira (causes leptospirosis) .
  - **CFB group**: This is a phylum of bacteria that includes the genera Cytophaga, Fusobacterium, and Bacteroides. They share some similarities in their DNA sequences, but they are phylogenetically diverse. They are mostly anaerobic and inhabit various environments, such as soil, water, and the human gut. Cytophaga are rod-shaped bacteria that can degrade cellulose and other complex carbohydrates. Fusobacterium are spindle-shaped bacteria that can cause infections in the mouth, throat, and lungs. Bacteroides are the most abundant bacteria in the human colon and play a role in digestion and immunity .
  - **Planctomycetes**: These are aquatic bacteria that have some unusual features, such as a lack of peptidoglycan in their cell wall, a compartmentalized cytoplasm, and the ability to produce membrane-bound vesicles. Some planctomycetes can perform anammox, which is a process of anaerobic ammonia oxidation that produces nitrogen gas. This process is important for the global nitrogen cycle.
  - **Phototrophic bacteria**: These are bacteria that can use light as an energy source to synthesize organic compounds. They can be either oxygenic or anoxygenic, depending on whether they produce oxygen or not during photosynthesis. Oxygenic phototrophic bacteria include cyanobacteria, which are the most abundant and diverse group of photosynthetic prokaryotes. They have chlorophyll a and phycobilins as pigments and can fix nitrogen in specialized cells called heterocysts. Anoxygenic phototrophic bacteria include purple bacteria, green bacteria, and heliobacteria, which have different types of pigments and electron donors for photosynthesis .
