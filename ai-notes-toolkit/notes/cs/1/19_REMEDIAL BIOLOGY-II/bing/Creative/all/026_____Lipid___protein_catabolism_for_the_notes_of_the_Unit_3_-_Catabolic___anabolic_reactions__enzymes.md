# Lipid and Protein Catabolism

- Lipid and protein catabolism are the processes of breaking down lipids and proteins, respectively, into smaller molecules that can be used for energy production or biosynthesis.
- Lipids and proteins are important macromolecules that store energy, provide structural support, and perform various functions in living organisms.
- Lipid and protein catabolism involve different enzymes, pathways, and intermediates than carbohydrate catabolism, but they ultimately converge with the central catabolic pathways of glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.

## Lipid Catabolism

- Lipids are a diverse group of organic molecules that include fats, oils, phospholipids, steroids, and waxes.
- The most common form of lipid catabolism is the breakdown of triglycerides, which are composed of glycerol and three fatty acids.
- Triglycerides are hydrolyzed by lipases, enzymes that cleave the ester bonds between glycerol and fatty acids .
- Glycerol can be converted to dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP), an intermediate of glycolysis, by a series of reactions involving glycerol kinase and glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase .
- Fatty acids are activated by coenzyme A (CoA) and transported into the mitochondrial matrix by the carnitine shuttle, a system that exchanges fatty acyl-CoA for carnitine across the inner mitochondrial membrane .
- Fatty acyl-CoA undergoes beta-oxidation, a cyclic process that removes two-carbon units from the carboxyl end of the fatty acid chain and produces acetyl-CoA, NADH, and FADH2 .
- Acetyl-CoA enters the citric acid cycle, where it is oxidized to carbon dioxide and water, generating more NADH and FADH2 .
- NADH and FADH2 donate their electrons to the electron transport chain, where they drive the synthesis of ATP by oxidative phosphorylation .
- The overall equation for the complete oxidation of a fatty acid is:

`Fatty acid + CoA + (n/2) O2 + (n-1) H2O -> (n/2) Acetyl-CoA + (n/2) FADH2 + (n/2) NADH + (n/2) H+`

where n is the number of carbon atoms in the fatty acid chain.

## Protein Catabolism

- Proteins are polymers of amino acids, which are linked by peptide bonds.
- Protein catabolism begins with the hydrolysis of proteins into amino acids by proteases, enzymes that cleave the peptide bonds  .
- Amino acids are transported into the cells by specific transporters and then deaminated, a process that removes the amino group from the amino acid and produces ammonia and an organic acid  .
- Ammonia is toxic to cells and must be eliminated or converted to a less harmful form, such as urea or uric acid, depending on the organism  .
- The organic acids derived from amino acids can enter different points of the central catabolic pathways, depending on their structure  .
- Some organic acids can be converted to pyruvate, the end product of glycolysis, by transamination or decarboxylation reactions  .
- Some organic acids can be converted to acetyl-CoA, the entry point of the citric acid cycle, by oxidative decarboxylation or thiolysis reactions  .
- Some organic acids can be converted to intermediates of the citric acid cycle, such as oxaloacetate, alpha-ketoglutarate, succinyl-CoA, fumarate, or malate, by various reactions involving transamination, dehydrogenation, or hydration  .
