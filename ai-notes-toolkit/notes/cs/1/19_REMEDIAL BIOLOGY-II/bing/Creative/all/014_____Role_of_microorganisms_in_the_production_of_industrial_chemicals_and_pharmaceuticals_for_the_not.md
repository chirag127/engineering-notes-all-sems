# Role of microorganisms in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals

- Microorganisms are used in industry to produce a variety of organic compounds including acids, growth stimulants, and enzymes.
- Microorganisms can be genetically modified or engineered to aid in large-scale production of desired products.
- Microorganisms can also use carbon dioxide and hydrogen as substrates to produce industrial chemicals and fuels.
- Some examples of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals produced by microorganisms are:

  - Amino acids: Microbes make only the L-isomer of amino acids, which are essential for protein synthesis and human nutrition. Some amino acids, such as lysine and glutamic acid, are used as food additives and flavor enhancers .
  - Citric acid: Produced by the mold Aspergillus niger, citric acid is widely used as a preservative, flavoring agent, and acidulant in food and beverage industries. It is also used in pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, and detergents .
  - Enzymes: Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions. Microbes produce various enzymes, such as amylases, proteases, lipases, and cellulases, that are used in industries such as starch, textile, leather, paper, and biofuel .
  - Antibiotics: Antibiotics are substances that inhibit the growth or kill other microorganisms. They are used to treat bacterial infections and prevent diseases. Most antibiotics are produced by fungi or bacteria, such as penicillin by Penicillium, streptomycin by Streptomyces, and tetracycline by Streptomyces aureofaciens .
  - Vaccines: Vaccines are preparations that stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies against specific pathogens. They are used to prevent or reduce the severity of infectious diseases. Some vaccines are made from attenuated or killed microorganisms, such as polio vaccine from poliovirus, or from recombinant DNA technology, such as hepatitis B vaccine from yeast cells .
  - Vitamins: Vitamins are organic compounds that are essential for normal metabolism and growth. Some vitamins, such as vitamin B12 and riboflavin, are produced by microorganisms and used as supplements or fortifiers in food and animal feed .
