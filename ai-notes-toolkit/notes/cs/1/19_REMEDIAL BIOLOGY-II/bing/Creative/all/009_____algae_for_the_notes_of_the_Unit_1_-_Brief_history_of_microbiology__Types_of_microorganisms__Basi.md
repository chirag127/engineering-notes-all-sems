# Algae

- Algae are a large and diverse group of photosynthetic eukaryotic organisms that are typically aquatic and include seaweeds and many single-celled forms .
- Algae are classified into different groups based on their pigments, cell wall composition, flagella, storage products, and other features.
- Some of the major groups of algae are:
  - Green algae: They have chlorophyll a and b, cellulose in their cell walls, and store starch. They are closely related to plants and can be unicellular or multicellular. Examples: Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas, Ulva.
  - Brown algae: They have chlorophyll a and c, fucoxanthin (a brown pigment), alginic acid in their cell walls, and store laminarin. They are mostly marine and multicellular. Examples: Fucus, Sargassum, kelps.
  - Red algae: They have chlorophyll a and d, phycobilins (red pigments), agar and carrageenan in their cell walls, and store floridean starch. They are mostly marine and multicellular. Examples: Porphyra, Polysiphonia, coralline algae.
  - Diatoms: They have chlorophyll a and c, fucoxanthin, silica in their cell walls, and store oil or chrysolaminarin. They are unicellular and have two overlapping shells. They are important producers in aquatic ecosystems. Examples: Cyclotella, Navicula, Pinnularia.
  - Dinoflagellates: They have chlorophyll a and c, peridinin (a yellow pigment), cellulose plates in their cell walls, and store starch or oil. They are unicellular and have two flagella. Some of them are bioluminescent or cause red tides. Examples: Ceratium, Gonyaulax, Noctiluca.
  - Euglenoids: They have chlorophyll a and b, paramylon in their cell walls, and store paramylon. They are unicellular and have one or two flagella. Some of them are heterotrophic or mixotrophic. Examples: Euglena, Phacus, Trachelomonas.
- Algae play an important role in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals, such as:
  - Agar: A polysaccharide extracted from red algae that is used as a solidifying agent in microbiology, biotechnology, and food industry.
  - Carrageenan: A polysaccharide extracted from red algae that is used as a thickening, stabilizing, and gelling agent in food, cosmetics, and pharmaceuticals.
  - Alginic acid: A polysaccharide extracted from brown algae that is used as an emulsifier, stabilizer, and binder in food, textiles, and pharmaceuticals.
  - Iodine: A chemical element obtained from brown algae that is used as an antiseptic, disinfectant, and thyroid hormone supplement.
  - Beta-carotene: A pigment extracted from green algae that is used as a food colorant, antioxidant, and vitamin A precursor.
  - Spirulina: A blue-green alga that is used as a dietary supplement, protein source, and immune booster.
