## Brief history of microbiology
- Microbiology is the study of microscopic organisms, such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and algae.
- Microbiology essentially began with the development of the microscope in the 17th century.
- Antonie van Leeuwenhoek (1632–1723) was one of the first people to observe microorganisms, using a microscope of his own design, and made one of the most important contributions to biology .
- Robert Hooke (1635–1703) was the first to use a microscope to observe living things, such as fungi, algae, and cork cells .
- Ferdinand Cohn (1828–1898) was a botanist who founded the field of bacteriology and described several bacteria, including Bacillus and Beggiatoa.
- Louis Pasteur (1822–1895) was a chemist who demonstrated the role of microorganisms in fermentation, spoilage, and disease, and developed the process of pasteurization and the germ theory of disease.
- Robert Koch (1843–1910) was a physician who established the criteria for proving the causal relationship between a microbe and a disease (Koch's postulates) and identified the causative agents of anthrax, tuberculosis, and cholera.
- Other notable figures in the history of microbiology include Joseph Lister, Alexander Fleming, Martinus Beijerinck, Sergei Winogradsky, Paul Ehrlich, Elie Metchnikoff, and Carl Woese.

## Types of microorganisms
- Microorganisms are classified into different domains, kingdoms, phyla, classes, orders, families, genera, and species based on their genetic and phenotypic characteristics.
- The three domains of life are Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya.
- Bacteria and Archaea are prokaryotes, which means they lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually unicellular and have diverse shapes, sizes, metabolisms, and habitats.
- Eukarya are eukaryotes, which means they have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They can be unicellular or multicellular and include animals, plants, fungi, and protists.
- Fungi are eukaryotic organisms that obtain nutrients by absorbing organic matter from their environment. They include molds, yeasts, mushrooms, and lichens.
- Algae are eukaryotic organisms that perform photosynthesis and produce oxygen. They include seaweeds, diatoms, dinoflagellates, and green algae.
- Protozoa are eukaryotic organisms that are usually motile and feed on other microorganisms or organic matter. They include amoebas, flagellates, ciliates, and sporozoans.
- Helminthes are eukaryotic organisms that are multicellular worms, such as roundworms, tapeworms, and flukes. They are parasites that infect animals and humans.
- Viruses are acellular entities that consist of a nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a protein coat. They are obligate intracellular parasites that can only replicate inside a living host cell.

## Basic idea of domain bacteria, proteobacteria, non proteobacteria Gram –ve and Gram +ve bacteria
- Bacteria are the most abundant and diverse domain of life. They are found in almost every environment on Earth and play important roles in biogeochemical cycles, symbiosis, and human health and disease.
- Bacteria are classified into different phyla based on their genetic and phenotypic characteristics, such as cell wall structure, metabolism, morphology, and ecology.
- One of the most common methods to classify bacteria is based on their reaction to the Gram stain, which differentiates bacteria into Gram-positive and Gram-negative based on the presence or absence of a thick peptidoglycan layer in their cell wall.
