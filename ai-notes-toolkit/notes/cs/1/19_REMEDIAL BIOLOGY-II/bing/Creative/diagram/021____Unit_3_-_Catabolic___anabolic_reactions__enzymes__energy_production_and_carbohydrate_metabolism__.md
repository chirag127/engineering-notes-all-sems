## Catabolic & anabolic reactions
- Catabolic reactions are those that break down complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy in the process. Examples are glycolysis, beta-oxidation, and proteolysis.
- Anabolic reactions are those that build up complex molecules from simpler ones, consuming energy in the process. Examples are gluconeogenesis, lipogenesis, and protein synthesis.
- Catabolic and anabolic reactions are regulated by enzymes, which are biological catalysts that speed up the rate of chemical reactions without being consumed or altered.
- Enzymes have specific substrates, which are the molecules that they act on, and products, which are the molecules that they produce.
- Enzymes are affected by factors such as temperature, pH, cofactors, inhibitors, and feedback mechanisms.

## Energy production and carbohydrate metabolism
- Carbohydrates are the main source of energy for most living organisms. They are composed of monosaccharides, which are simple sugars, such as glucose, fructose, and galactose.
- Monosaccharides can be linked together to form polysaccharides, which are complex carbohydrates, such as starch, glycogen, and cellulose.
- Carbohydrate metabolism involves the breakdown of carbohydrates into monosaccharides, and the synthesis of carbohydrates from monosaccharides.
- The breakdown of glucose into pyruvate is called glycolysis, which occurs in the cytoplasm of cells. Glycolysis produces 2 ATP, 2 NADH, and 2 H+ per glucose molecule.
- Pyruvate can be further oxidized into acetyl-CoA, which enters the citric acid cycle, also known as the Krebs cycle, in the mitochondrial matrix. The citric acid cycle produces 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH2, and 4 CO2 per glucose molecule.
- The electrons from NADH and FADH2 are transferred to the electron transport chain, which is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. The electron transport chain generates a proton gradient across the membrane, which drives the synthesis of ATP by the enzyme ATP synthase. The electron transport chain produces 32 or 34 ATP per glucose molecule, depending on the shuttle system used to transport NADH from the cytoplasm to the mitochondria.
- The synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate sources, such as lactate, amino acids, and glycerol, is called gluconeogenesis, which occurs mainly in the liver and the kidney. Gluconeogenesis consumes 6 ATP, 2 GTP, 2 NADH, and 4 H2O per glucose molecule.

## Lipid & protein catabolism
- Lipids are organic molecules that are insoluble in water, such as fats, oils, steroids, and phospholipids. They are composed of fatty acids and glycerol, or other non-glycerol molecules, such as cholesterol and sphingosine.
- Lipid catabolism involves the breakdown of lipids into fatty acids and glycerol, and the oxidation of fatty acids and glycerol into acetyl-CoA, which can enter the citric acid cycle.
- The breakdown of lipids into fatty acids and glycerol is called lipolysis, which occurs in the cytoplasm of cells. Lipolysis is catalyzed by the enzyme lipase, which hydrolyzes the ester bonds between fatty acids and glycerol.
- The oxidation of fatty acids into acetyl-CoA is called beta-oxidation, which occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Beta-oxidation involves the removal of two-carbon units from the end of the fatty acid chain, forming acetyl-CoA, NADH, and FADH2. Beta-oxidation produces 1 FADH2, 1 NADH, and 1 acetyl-CoA per two-carbon unit.
