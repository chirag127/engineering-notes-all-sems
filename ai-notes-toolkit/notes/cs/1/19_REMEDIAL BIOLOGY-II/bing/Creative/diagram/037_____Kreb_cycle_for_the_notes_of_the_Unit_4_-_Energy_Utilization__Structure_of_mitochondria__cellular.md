### Kreb cycle

- The Kreb cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle or the tricarboxylic acid cycle, is a series of chemical reactions that occur in the mitochondria of aerobic organisms  .
- The Kreb cycle is part of the cellular respiration process, which converts glucose and other organic molecules into energy in the form of ATP (adenosine triphosphate)  .
- The Kreb cycle is preceded by glycolysis, which breaks down glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, and by pyruvate oxidation, which converts pyruvate into acetyl-CoA, a two-carbon molecule that enters the Kreb cycle  .
- The Kreb cycle consists of eight steps, each catalyzed by a specific enzyme  :
  - Step 1: Acetyl-CoA combines with oxaloacetate, a four-carbon molecule, to form citrate, a six-carbon molecule. Coenzyme A is released and recycled  .
  - Step 2: Citrate is isomerized to isocitrate, a six-carbon molecule, by the removal and addition of a water molecule  .
  - Step 3: Isocitrate is oxidized to alpha-ketoglutarate, a five-carbon molecule, by the removal of a carbon dioxide molecule and the transfer of two electrons to NAD+, forming NADH  .
  - Step 4: Alpha-ketoglutarate is oxidized to succinyl-CoA, a four-carbon molecule, by the removal of a carbon dioxide molecule and the transfer of two electrons to NAD+, forming NADH. Coenzyme A is attached to succinyl-CoA  .
  - Step 5: Succinyl-CoA is converted to succinate, a four-carbon molecule, by the cleavage of the bond between succinate and CoA. Coenzyme A is released and recycled. The energy released by this reaction is used to phosphorylate GDP, forming GTP, which can be converted to ATP  .
  - Step 6: Succinate is oxidized to fumarate, a four-carbon molecule, by the transfer of two electrons to FAD, forming FADH2  .
  - Step 7: Fumarate is hydrated to malate, a four-carbon molecule, by the addition of a water molecule  .
  - Step 8: Malate is oxidized to oxaloacetate, a four-carbon molecule, by the transfer of two electrons to NAD+, forming NADH. The cycle is completed and ready to start again  .
- The Kreb cycle produces two molecules of carbon dioxide, one molecule of ATP (or GTP), three molecules of NADH, and one molecule of FADH2 per acetyl-CoA molecule that enters the cycle  .
- The Kreb cycle is important for many biochemical pathways, as it provides precursors of certain amino acids, as well as the reducing agents NADH and FADH2, that are used in numerous other reactions  .
- The Kreb cycle is regulated by the availability of substrates (such as acetyl-CoA, NAD+, and oxaloacetate) and by feedback inhibition from products (such as ATP, NADH, and citrate)  .
- The Kreb cycle is influenced by factors affecting respiration, such as temperature, oxygen, pH, and inhibitors .
