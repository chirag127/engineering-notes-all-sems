### ATP
- ATP stands for adenosine triphosphate, an organic compound that consists of an adenine base, a ribose sugar, and three phosphate groups.
- ATP is the main energy-carrying molecule used in cells because it can release energy very quickly by breaking the bond between the last two phosphate groups.
- ATP can be produced by different metabolic pathways, such as cellular respiration, photosynthesis, and fermentation.
- ATP can be used to power various cellular processes, such as muscle contraction, nerve transmission, biosynthesis, and transport.

### Cellular respiration
- Cellular respiration is the process by which biological fuels, such as glucose, are oxidized in the presence of an inorganic electron acceptor, such as oxygen, to produce large amounts of energy, to drive the bulk production of ATP.
- Cellular respiration can be divided into three main steps: glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation .
- Glycolysis is the first step of cellular respiration, where glucose is split into two molecules of pyruvate, producing a net of 2 ATP and 2 NADH (a reduced form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, an electron carrier) per glucose molecule .
- Pyruvate oxidation is the second step of cellular respiration, where pyruvate is converted into acetyl-CoA (a two-carbon molecule attached to coenzyme A), producing 1 CO2 and 1 NADH per pyruvate molecule .
- The citric acid cycle, also known as the Krebs cycle, is the third step of cellular respiration, where acetyl-CoA enters a cycle of reactions that produces 2 CO2, 3 NADH, 1 FADH2 (a reduced form of flavin adenine dinucleotide, another electron carrier), and 1 ATP per acetyl-CoA molecule .
- Oxidative phosphorylation is the final step of cellular respiration, where the electrons from NADH and FADH2 are transferred to a series of proteins called the electron transport chain, which pumps protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, creating a gradient of protons that drives the synthesis of ATP by a protein complex called ATP synthase  .
- Oxidative phosphorylation can produce up to 28 ATP per glucose molecule, depending on the efficiency of the electron transport chain and the proton gradient .
- Cellular respiration can produce a total of 30-32 ATP per glucose molecule, depending on the pathway of pyruvate oxidation and the shuttle system of NADH across the mitochondrial membrane .

### Factors affecting respiration
- Cellular respiration can be affected by various factors, such as temperature, pH, oxygen availability, substrate availability, and enzyme activity.
- Temperature can affect the rate of cellular respiration, as higher temperatures can increase the kinetic energy and the collision frequency of the molecules involved in the reactions, but also can denature the enzymes and disrupt the membrane integrity if the temperature is too high.
- pH can affect the rate of cellular respiration, as the optimal pH for most enzymes is around 7, and deviations from this range can alter the shape and the function of the enzymes and the proton gradient.
- Oxygen availability can affect the rate of cellular respiration, as oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain, and without oxygen, the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation cannot function, leading to a reduced ATP production and a shift to anaerobic respiration.
- Substrate availability can affect the rate of cellular respiration, as the availability of glucose and other respiratory substrates can determine the amount of ATP that can be produced by the different pathways of cellular respiration.
- Enzyme activity can affect the rate of cellular respiration, as the activity of the enzymes involved in the different steps of cellular respiration can be regulated by various factors, such as feedback inhibition, allosteric modulation, covalent modification, and gene expression.
