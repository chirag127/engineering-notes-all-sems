### Nonproteobacteria Gram –ve

- Nonproteobacteria Gram –ve are a diverse group of bacteria that do not belong to the phylum Proteobacteria, which contains the majority of Gram –ve bacteria.
- They include the classes Chlamydia, Spirochetes, CFB group, and Planctomycetes, as well as some phototrophic bacteria.
- Some of their characteristics and examples are:

  - Chlamydia
    - Obligate intracellular parasites that infect animals and humans, causing various diseases such as trachoma, chlamydia, and lymphogranuloma venereum.
    - They have a unique developmental cycle that involves two forms: elementary bodies (infectious and dormant) and reticulate bodies (replicative and metabolically active).
    - They lack peptidoglycan in their cell wall and depend on their host for ATP and other nutrients.
    - Examples: Chlamydia trachomatis, Chlamydia pneumoniae, Chlamydia psittaci.
  - Spirochetes
    - Motile, spiral-shaped bacteria with a long, narrow body and a flexible cell wall.
    - They have a unique mode of locomotion that involves axial filaments (endoflagella) that rotate within the periplasmic space, causing the cell to corkscrew through viscous environments.
    - They are difficult or impossible to culture in the laboratory and some are pathogenic to humans and animals, causing diseases such as syphilis, Lyme disease, and leptospirosis.
    - Examples: Treponema pallidum, Borrelia burgdorferi, Leptospira interrogans.
  - CFB group
    - A phylum of bacteria that includes the genera Cytophaga, Fusobacterium, and Bacteroides, as well as some other related taxa.
    - They are phylogenetically diverse, but share some similarities in the sequence of nucleotides in their DNA.
    - They are mostly anaerobic and inhabit various environments such as soil, water, and the human gut.
    - They have diverse metabolic capabilities, such as cellulose degradation, nitrogen fixation, and fermentation.
    - Examples: Cytophaga hutchinsonii, Fusobacterium nucleatum, Bacteroides fragilis.
  - Planctomycetes
    - A phylum of bacteria that are aquatic and mostly found in freshwater, marine, and brackish habitats.
    - They have a distinctive morphology that includes a stalk-like appendage called a holdfast, a pear-shaped cell body, and a membrane-bound compartment called a pirellulosome that contains the nucleoid and ribosomes.
    - They lack peptidoglycan in their cell wall and have a proteinaceous layer called a crista.
    - They have diverse metabolic capabilities, such as anaerobic ammonium oxidation (anammox), chemoautotrophy, and heterotrophy.
    - Examples: Planctomyces maris, Brocadia anammoxidans, Gemmata obscuriglobus.
  - Phototrophic bacteria
    - A group of bacteria that use light as an energy source and perform photosynthesis, either oxygenic or anoxygenic.
    - They have various pigments that absorb light, such as chlorophylls, bacteriochlorophylls, carotenoids, and phycobilins.
    - They belong to different phyla, such as Cyanobacteria, Chlorobi, Chloroflexi, and Proteobacteria.
    - They inhabit various environments, such as freshwater, marine, soil, and hot springs.
    - Examples: Anabaena sp., Chlorobium tepidum, Chloroflexus aurantiacus, Rhodobacter sphaeroides.
