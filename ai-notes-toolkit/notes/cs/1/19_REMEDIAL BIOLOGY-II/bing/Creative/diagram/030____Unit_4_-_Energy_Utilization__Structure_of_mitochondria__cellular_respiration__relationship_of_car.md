## Structure of Mitochondria

- Mitochondria are small, rod-shaped organelles found in both plant and animal cells. They are often called the "powerhouses" of the cell because they produce most of the cell's energy through cellular respiration  .
- Mitochondria have two membranes, an outer membrane and an inner membrane. These membranes are made of phospholipid layers, just like the cell's outer membrane.
- The outer membrane is smooth and covers the entire organelle. It has pores that allow the passage of small molecules and ions .
- The inner membrane is folded into numerous projections called cristae. The cristae increase the surface area of the membrane and provide the site for the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation, the final steps of cellular respiration  .
- The space between the outer and inner membranes is called the intermembrane space. It contains enzymes and proteins involved in various metabolic reactions .
- The space enclosed by the inner membrane is called the matrix. It contains the mitochondrial DNA, ribosomes, enzymes, and other molecules needed for the Krebs cycle, the second stage of cellular respiration  .
- Mitochondria have their own genetic material and can replicate independently of the cell's nucleus. They are inherited maternally in most organisms .
- Mitochondria are dynamic organelles that can change their shape, size, number, and location depending on the cell's needs. They can fuse together or split apart, move along the cytoskeleton, or interact with other organelles .
- Mitochondria are also involved in other cellular functions, such as calcium signaling, apoptosis (programmed cell death), and biosynthesis of some amino acids, lipids, and iron-sulfur clusters .
