### Algae

- Algae are a large and diverse group of photosynthetic eukaryotic organisms that are typically aquatic and include seaweeds and many single-celled forms .
- Algae are classified into several groups based on their pigments, cell wall composition, flagella, storage products, and other features. Some of the major groups are:
  - Green algae (Chlorophyta): They have chlorophyll a and b, store starch, and have cellulose in their cell walls. They are the most closely related to land plants and can be unicellular, colonial, or multicellular. Examples: Spirogyra, Ulva, Volvox.
  - Brown algae (Phaeophyta): They have chlorophyll a and c, fucoxanthin, store laminarin, and have alginic acid in their cell walls. They are mostly marine and multicellular, forming large seaweeds and kelps. Examples: Fucus, Sargassum, Macrocystis.
  - Red algae (Rhodophyta): They have chlorophyll a and d, phycobilins, store floridean starch, and have agar and carrageenan in their cell walls. They are mostly marine and multicellular, forming red or purple seaweeds. Examples: Porphyra, Corallina, Gracilaria.
  - Diatoms (Bacillariophyta): They have chlorophyll a and c, fucoxanthin, store oil or chrysolaminarin, and have silica in their cell walls. They are unicellular and have two overlapping shells that fit together like a box and a lid. They are found in both freshwater and marine habitats and are important producers of oxygen and organic matter. Examples: Cyclotella, Navicula, Pinnularia.
  - Dinoflagellates (Dinophyta): They have chlorophyll a and c, peridinin, store starch, and have cellulose plates in their cell walls. They are unicellular and have two flagella, one in a groove around the cell and one trailing behind. They are mostly marine and can be free-living or symbiotic with corals, sponges, or other organisms. Some of them can produce bioluminescence or toxins. Examples: Ceratium, Gonyaulax, Noctiluca.
  - Euglenoids (Euglenophyta): They have chlorophyll a and b, store paramylon, and have a proteinaceous pellicle instead of a cell wall. They are unicellular and have one or two flagella, an eyespot, and a contractile vacuole. They are mostly freshwater and can be photosynthetic or heterotrophic. Examples: Euglena, Phacus, Trachelomonas.
- Algae play an important role in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals, such as alginates, agar, carrageenan, iodine, biofuels, antioxidants, vitamins, and omega-3 fatty acids . Algae are also used as food, feed, fertilizer, and bioremediation agents .
