### Lichens

- Lichens are **composite organisms** that consist of a **symbiotic association** of algae (usually green) or cyanobacteria and fungi (mostly ascomycetes and basidiomycetes).
- The dominant partner is the **fungus**, which gives the lichen the majority of its characteristics, from its **thallus shape** to its **fruiting bodies**.
- The alga or cyanobacterium provides **photosynthesis** and **nitrogen fixation** for the fungus, while the fungus provides **protection**, **water**, and **minerals** for the alga or cyanobacterium.
- Lichens are classified according to the **morphology** of their thallus, which can be **crustose** (crusty), **foliose** (leafy), **fruticose** (shrubby), or **squamulose** (scaly).
- Lichens are found **worldwide** and occur in a variety of **environmental conditions**. They are **pioneer species**, among the first living things to grow on bare rock or areas denuded of life by a disaster.
- Lichens have various **roles** in the ecosystem, such as **bioindicators**, **decomposers**, **nitrogen fixers**, **soil formers**, and **food sources** for animals.
- Lichens also have various **uses** for humans, such as **dyes**, **medicines**, **perfumes**, **litmus paper**, and **lichenometry**.
