### Energy production

- Energy production is the process of converting food molecules into usable forms of energy, such as ATP, for cellular activities.
- Energy production involves two types of metabolic reactions: catabolic and anabolic .
- Catabolic reactions are those that break down complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy in the process. For example, the breakdown of glucose into pyruvate and then into carbon dioxide and water is a catabolic reaction that produces ATP.
- Anabolic reactions are those that build up larger molecules from smaller ones, using energy in the process. For example, the synthesis of proteins from amino acids is an anabolic reaction that consumes ATP .
- The balance between catabolic and anabolic reactions determines the net energy change in the body. If catabolic reactions release more energy than anabolic reactions use, the excess energy is stored as fat. If anabolic reactions use more energy than catabolic reactions release, the body uses stored fat or other molecules to provide the needed energy.
- Energy production can occur through different pathways, depending on the type of food molecule and the availability of oxygen. The main pathways are glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, and fermentation.
- Glycolysis is the first step of energy production from glucose. It occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell and converts one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, producing two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH (a carrier of electrons) in the process.
- The citric acid cycle is the second step of energy production from glucose. It occurs in the mitochondria of the cell and oxidizes the pyruvate into carbon dioxide and water, producing two molecules of ATP and eight molecules of NADH and two molecules of FADH2 (another carrier of electrons) in the process.
- Oxidative phosphorylation is the third and final step of energy production from glucose. It also occurs in the mitochondria of the cell and uses the electrons from NADH and FADH2 to generate a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which drives the synthesis of ATP by a protein complex called ATP synthase. This step produces about 28 molecules of ATP per molecule of glucose.
- Fermentation is an alternative pathway of energy production from glucose that occurs when oxygen is not available. It also occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell and converts the pyruvate into lactate or ethanol, depending on the type of organism, regenerating NAD+ from NADH in the process. This step produces only two molecules of ATP per molecule of glucose and does not involve the citric acid cycle or oxidative phosphorylation.
- Energy production can also occur from other food molecules, such as lipids and proteins. Lipids can be broken down into glycerol and fatty acids, which can enter glycolysis or the citric acid cycle, respectively. Proteins can be broken down into amino acids, which can be converted into intermediates of glycolysis or the citric acid cycle, depending on the type of amino acid.
- Energy production can vary depending on the metabolic diversity and pathways of energy use of different organisms. Some organisms can use different sources of energy, such as light, inorganic compounds, or organic compounds, to produce ATP. Some organisms can use different terminal electron acceptors, such as nitrate, sulfate, or carbon dioxide, instead of oxygen, to generate a proton gradient for ATP synthesis. Some organisms can use different pathways of energy use, such as the pentose phosphate pathway, the glyoxylate cycle, or the Calvin cycle, to produce or consume different molecules for biosynthesis or catabolism.
- Energy production is integrated with the regulation of metabolism in the body. The levels of hormones, such as insulin and glucagon, and the availability of nutrients, such as glucose and oxygen, can affect the rate and direction of metabolic reactions.
