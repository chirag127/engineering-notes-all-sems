### Protozoa

- Protozoa are **microscopic unicellular eukaryotes** that have a relatively complex internal structure and carry out complex metabolic activities    .
- Some protozoa have structures for **propulsion or other types of movement**, such as flagella, cilia, or pseudopodia    .
- Protozoa are either **free-living or parasitic**, that feed on organic matter such as other microorganisms or organic tissues and debris   .
- Protozoa are **heterotrophic**, using organic carbon as a source of energy.
- Protozoa have **one or more nuclei** and may have specialized organelles, such as contractile vacuoles, food vacuoles, or plastids  .
- Protozoa are **nonfilamentous**, in contrast to fungi and some algae.
- Protozoa are currently classified into **six phyla** on the basis of light and electron microscopic morphology :
  - **Sarcomastigophora**, which includes flagellates and amoebae.
  - **Apicomplexa**, which includes intracellular parasites such as Plasmodium and Toxoplasma.
  - **Ciliophora**, which includes ciliates such as Paramecium and Stentor.
  - **Microspora**, which includes spore-forming parasites such as Encephalitozoon and Nosema.
  - **Ascetosporea**, which includes parasites of marine invertebrates such as Myxozoa and Orthonectida.
  - **Myxozoa**, which includes parasites of fish and annelids such as Myxobolus and Ceratomyxa.
- Protozoa are **diverse and ecologically important** groups of organisms, playing roles in nutrient cycling, decomposition, symbiosis, and pathogenesis   .
- Protozoa can reproduce by **asexual or sexual methods**, depending on the species and environmental conditions  .
- Protozoa can exhibit different forms of **life cycles**, such as haploid, diploid, or alternation of generations  .
- Protozoa can be identified by their **morphological, biochemical, molecular, and immunological characteristics**, as well as by their **host range, transmission mode, and clinical manifestations**   .
