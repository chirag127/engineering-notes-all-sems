### Integration of metabolism

- Metabolism is the sum of all the chemical reactions that take place in a living cell to maintain its structure and function.
- Metabolism can be divided into two major types: catabolism and anabolism.
- Catabolism is the breakdown of complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy in the process. Examples of catabolic reactions are glycolysis, lipid oxidation, and protein degradation.
- Anabolism is the synthesis of complex molecules from simpler ones, requiring energy in the process. Examples of anabolic reactions are gluconeogenesis, lipid synthesis, and protein synthesis.
- Metabolism is a continuous and dynamic process, with thousands of reactions occurring simultaneously in different parts of the cell and in different tissues of the body.
- Metabolism is regulated by various factors, such as enzyme activity, substrate availability, allosteric modulation, hormonal signals, and feedback inhibition.
- Metabolism is integrated by the coordination of different pathways and organs to achieve a balance between energy production and consumption, and between biosynthesis and degradation of biomolecules.
- Metabolism is influenced by the physiological state of the organism, such as fasting, feeding, exercise, stress, and disease.
- Metabolism is also affected by the environmental factors, such as temperature, light, oxygen, and nutrient availability.
- Metabolism is essential for the survival and adaptation of living organisms to changing conditions.
