### Proteobacteria

- Proteobacteria is a phylum of gram-negative bacteria discovered by Carl Woese in the 1980s based on nucleotide sequence homology .
- Proteobacteria are further classified into the classes alpha-, beta-, gamma-, delta- and epsilonproteobacteria, each class having separate orders, families, genera, and species.
- Proteobacteria include a wide variety of pathogenic and free-living bacteria, such as Escherichia, Salmonella, Vibrio, Yersinia, Legionella, and many others.
- Proteobacteria are also involved in nitrogen fixation, carbon monoxide oxidation, and sulfur cycling .
- Proteobacteria have diverse shapes, metabolisms, and habitats, ranging from soil and water to animal and plant hosts .

Some examples of proteobacteria are:

- Alphaproteobacteria: oligotrophs, organisms capable of living in low-nutrient environments such as deep oceanic sediments, glacial ice, or deep undersurface soil. Some examples are Rhizobium (nitrogen-fixing symbionts of legumes), Rickettsia (intracellular parasites of animals), and Caulobacter (stalked bacteria).
- Betaproteobacteria: mostly aerobic or facultative anaerobic bacteria that can use various organic compounds as electron donors and acceptors. Some examples are Neisseria (human pathogens causing gonorrhea and meningitis), Nitrosomonas (nitrifying bacteria), and Burkholderia (plant and animal pathogens).
- Gammaproteobacteria: the largest and most diverse class of proteobacteria, comprising many medically and environmentally important bacteria. Some examples are Escherichia (intestinal bacteria and pathogens), Pseudomonas (opportunistic pathogens and bioremediators), and Vibrio (marine bacteria and pathogens).
- Deltaproteobacteria: mostly anaerobic bacteria that are involved in sulfur and iron cycling. Some examples are Desulfovibrio (sulfate-reducing bacteria), Bdellovibrio (predatory bacteria), and Myxococcus (gliding bacteria that form fruiting bodies).
- Epsilonproteobacteria: mostly microaerophilic or anaerobic bacteria that inhabit extreme environments such as hot springs, hydrothermal vents, and animal guts. Some examples are Helicobacter (human pathogens causing ulcers and gastritis), Campylobacter (animal pathogens causing diarrhea), and Wolinella (hydrogen-oxidizing bacteria).
