### Energy Utilization

- Energy utilization is the process of converting the chemical energy stored in organic molecules into a usable form of energy, such as ATP, for cellular activities.
- The main pathway of energy utilization in most cells is cellular respiration, which involves the oxidation of glucose and other organic molecules to produce carbon dioxide and water, and the reduction of oxygen to form water.
- Cellular respiration consists of four stages: glycolysis, formation of acetyl co-A, Krebs cycle, and electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation.
- Cellular respiration takes place in the cytoplasm and mitochondria of each cell of the body.

#### Structure of mitochondria

- Mitochondria are membrane-bound organelles that are found in most eukaryotic cells, except for mature red blood cells.
- Mitochondria have two distinct membranes: an outer membrane and an inner membrane. The outer membrane is smooth and permeable to small molecules, while the inner membrane is folded into numerous projections called cristae, which increase the surface area for biochemical reactions.
- The space between the two membranes is called the intermembrane space, and the space enclosed by the inner membrane is called the matrix. The matrix contains enzymes, ribosomes, DNA, and various molecules involved in the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

#### Glycolysis

- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, and it occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell.
- Glycolysis is the breakdown of glucose, a six-carbon molecule, into two molecules of pyruvate, a three-carbon molecule, with the net production of two ATP and two NADH molecules.
- Glycolysis consists of two phases: an energy investment phase, where two ATP molecules are used to activate glucose, and an energy payoff phase, where four ATP and two NADH molecules are generated.
- Glycolysis does not require oxygen, and it can occur in both aerobic and anaerobic conditions.

#### Formation of acetyl co-A

- Formation of acetyl co-A is the second stage of cellular respiration, and it occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria.
- Formation of acetyl co-A is the conversion of pyruvate into acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl co-A), a two-carbon molecule, with the release of carbon dioxide and the production of one NADH molecule per pyruvate.
- Formation of acetyl co-A is catalyzed by a complex of enzymes called the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, which requires several coenzymes, such as thiamine, lipoic acid, and coenzyme A.
- Formation of acetyl co-A is an irreversible reaction, and it links glycolysis to the Krebs cycle.

#### Krebs cycle

- Krebs cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle or the tricarboxylic acid cycle, is the third stage of cellular respiration, and it occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria.
- Krebs cycle is a series of reactions that oxidize acetyl co-A into carbon dioxide and water, and generate one ATP, three NADH, and one FADH2 molecule per acetyl co-A.
- Krebs cycle consists of eight steps, each catalyzed by a specific enzyme. The first step is the condensation of acetyl co-A with oxaloacetate, a four-carbon molecule, to form citrate, a six-carbon molecule. The next seven steps involve the rearrangement and decarboxylation of citrate, until oxaloacetate is regenerated.
- Krebs cycle is a cyclic process, and it operates twice per glucose molecule, since each glucose molecule produces two acetyl co-A molecules.

#### Electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation

- Electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation are the fourth and final stage of cellular respiration, and they occur in the inner membrane of the mitochondria.
- Electron transport system is a series of protein complexes and electron carriers that transfer the electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen, the final electron acceptor. The electron transport system creates a proton gradient across the inner membrane, by pumping protons from the matrix to the intermembrane space.
