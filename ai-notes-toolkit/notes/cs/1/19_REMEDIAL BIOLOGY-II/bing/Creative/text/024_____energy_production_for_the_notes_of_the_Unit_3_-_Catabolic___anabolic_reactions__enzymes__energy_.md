### Energy production for the notes of the Unit 3 - Catabolic & anabolic reactions: enzymes, energy production and carbohydrate metabolism. - Energy production is the process of converting different sources of energy, such as chemical, light, or mechanical energy, into a form that can be used by living cells, such as adenosine triphosphate (ATP).
- Energy production involves different types of metabolic pathways, which are sequences of enzyme-catalyzed reactions that transform one or more starting molecules into products.
- Metabolic pathways can be classified into two main types: catabolic and anabolic.
  - Catabolic pathways are those that break down large molecules into smaller ones, releasing energy in the process. Examples of catabolic pathways are glycolysis, which breaks down glucose into pyruvate, and cellular respiration, which oxidizes pyruvate into carbon dioxide and water, generating ATP.
  - Anabolic pathways are those that synthesize large molecules from smaller ones, requiring energy in the process. Examples of anabolic pathways are photosynthesis, which converts light energy into chemical energy and builds glucose from carbon dioxide and water, and biosynthesis, which uses ATP and other precursors to make macromolecules such as proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids.
- Enzymes are important for catalyzing all types of metabolic pathways, as they lower the activation energy and increase the rate of the reactions. Enzymes are specific for their substrates and products, and can be regulated by different factors, such as temperature, pH, inhibitors, and activators.
- Energy production and carbohydrate metabolism are closely related, as carbohydrates are the main source of energy for most living organisms. Carbohydrates can be stored as glycogen in animals or starch in plants, and can be mobilized when needed to produce ATP through glycolysis and cellular respiration. Carbohydrates can also be converted into other molecules, such as amino acids, fatty acids, and nucleotides, through different anabolic pathways.
- Lipid and protein catabolism are alternative ways of producing energy when carbohydrates are not available or sufficient. Lipids can be broken down into glycerol and fatty acids, which can enter glycolysis and cellular respiration, respectively. Proteins can be broken down into amino acids, which can be deaminated and converted into intermediates of glycolysis or the citric acid cycle, depending on their structure.
- Energy production mechanism is the process of coupling the energy released from catabolic pathways to the energy required for anabolic pathways, mainly through the synthesis and hydrolysis of ATP. ATP is the universal energy currency of the cell, as it can transfer its phosphate group to other molecules, making them more reactive and able to participate in different reactions. ATP is synthesized by three main mechanisms: substrate-level phosphorylation, oxidative phosphorylation, and photophosphorylation.
  - Substrate-level phosphorylation is the direct transfer of a phosphate group from a substrate to ADP, forming ATP. This occurs in some reactions of glycolysis and the citric acid cycle, as well as in the fermentation pathways that regenerate NAD+ from NADH.
  - Oxidative phosphorylation is the indirect synthesis of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate, using the energy of a proton gradient across a membrane. This occurs in the electron transport chain and the ATP synthase complex, which are located in the inner mitochondrial membrane in eukaryotes and the plasma membrane in prokaryotes. The electron transport chain transfers electrons from NADH and FADH2, which are generated in glycolysis and the citric acid cycle, to oxygen, which is the final electron acceptor. The energy released from this process is used to pump protons from the matrix to the intermembrane space, creating a proton gradient. The ATP synthase complex allows the protons to flow back to the matrix, using the energy of this process to synthesize ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate.
  - Photophosphorylation is the synthesis of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate, using the energy of light. This occurs in the photosystems and the ATP synthase complex, which are located in the thylakoid membrane of the chloroplasts in plants and some algae.
