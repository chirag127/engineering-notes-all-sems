### Structure and function of cells

- Cells are the basic units of life and the smallest living things that can perform all the functions of life.
- Cells can be classified into two major types: prokaryotic and eukaryotic.
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells. They do not have a nucleus or other membrane-bound organelles. They have a single circular chromosome, a cell wall, a plasma membrane, ribosomes, and sometimes a capsule, flagella, or pili.
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells. They have a nucleus that contains linear chromosomes, and various membrane-bound organelles that perform specific functions. They include animals, plants, fungi, and protists.
- All cells have some common features, such as a cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and genetic material. The cell membrane is a thin layer that separates the inside and the outside of the cell, and controls what goes in and comes out. The cytoplasm is a jelly-like substance that contains the cell's parts. The ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis. The genetic material is the information that directs the cell's activities.
- Some of the organelles found in eukaryotic cells are:
  - Nucleus: the control center of the cell that contains the DNA and regulates gene expression.
  - Mitochondria: the powerhouses of the cell that produce energy by breaking down glucose.
  - Chloroplasts: the sites of photosynthesis in plant cells that convert light energy into chemical energy.
  - Endoplasmic reticulum: a network of membranes that transports and modifies proteins and lipids.
  - Golgi apparatus: a stack of flattened membranes that sorts and packages proteins and lipids for export.
  - Lysosomes: the garbage disposals of the cell that digest and recycle waste materials.
  - Cytoskeleton: a framework of protein filaments that provides shape, support, and movement to the cell.
  - Vacuoles: the storage compartments of the cell that hold water, nutrients, or waste products.
  - Cell wall: a rigid layer that surrounds the plasma membrane of plant cells and provides support and protection.
  - Cilia and flagella: hair-like or whip-like structures that extend from the cell surface and enable movement or sensing.
