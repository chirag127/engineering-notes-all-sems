### Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation

The electron transport system (ETS) and oxidative phosphorylation are the final stages of cellular respiration, which occurs in the mitochondria. These processes are responsible for the majority of ATP production in the cell.

1. The ETS is a series of protein complexes and electron carriers located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons from NADH and FADH2, produced during glycolysis, the formation of acetyl CoA, and the Krebs cycle, are transferred to the ETS.
2. As electrons move through the ETS, they release energy, which is used to pump protons from the mitochondrial matrix into the intermembrane space, creating a proton gradient.
3. The flow of protons back into the matrix through ATP synthase, driven by the proton gradient, generates ATP through a process called chemiosmosis. This process is known as oxidative phosphorylation.
4. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the ETS, combining with electrons and protons to form water. Without oxygen, the ETS cannot function, and ATP production is significantly reduced.

In summary, the electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation are essential for efficient ATP production in the cell, utilizing the energy from electrons transferred from NADH and FADH2 to generate a proton gradient and drive the synthesis of ATP through chemiosmosis. Oxygen is a crucial component of this process, acting as the final electron acceptor in the ETS.
