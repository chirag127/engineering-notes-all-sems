### Role of microorganisms in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals

Microorganisms play a crucial role in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals. They are used to produce a variety of organic compounds including acids, growth stimulants, and enzymes. In some cases, the production results from an apparent accident in nature where an organism manufactures many thousands of times the amount necessary for its own metabolism.

In the industrial process, microorganisms act like chemical factories. To be effective, they should liberate a large amount of a single product that can be efficiently isolated and purified. The microorganisms should be easy to maintain and cultivate, and should have genetic stability with infrequent mutations.

Industrial microorganisms are used to produce many things, including food, cosmetics, pharmaceuticals and construction materials. Microorganisms can be genetically modified or engineered to aid in large-scale production. For example, exopolysaccharide is a type of sugar-composed polymer secreted by a microorganism into the external environment.

While microorganisms can be manipulated to produce industrial chemicals and fuels from organic substrates, there is little research on engineering them to use carbon dioxide and hydrogen. However, the potential for using microorganisms in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals is vast and continues to be explored.
