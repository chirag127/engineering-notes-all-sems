### Structure and function of cells

Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS

1. Cells are the basic unit of life and are responsible for carrying out all the functions necessary for an organism to survive.
2. There are two main types of cells: prokaryotic and eukaryotic.
3. Prokaryotic cells are smaller and simpler in structure than eukaryotic cells. They do not have a nucleus or other membrane-bound organelles.
4. Eukaryotic cells are larger and more complex than prokaryotic cells. They have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
5. The size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells can vary greatly. Some bacteria are spherical, while others are rod-shaped or spiral.
6. The structure and function of cells are closely related. The various organelles within a cell perform specific functions that are necessary for the cell to carry out its role within the organism.
7. For example, the nucleus contains the genetic material and is responsible for controlling the activities of the cell. The mitochondria are responsible for producing energy for the cell through cellular respiration.
8. The cell membrane is a selectively permeable barrier that controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
9. The cytoplasm is the fluid-like substance that fills the cell and contains the organelles. It is the site of many metabolic reactions.
10. The endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus are involved in the synthesis and transport of proteins and lipids.
11. The lysosomes contain enzymes that break down waste materials and cellular debris.
12. The cytoskeleton provides support and shape to the cell and is involved in cell movement and division.
