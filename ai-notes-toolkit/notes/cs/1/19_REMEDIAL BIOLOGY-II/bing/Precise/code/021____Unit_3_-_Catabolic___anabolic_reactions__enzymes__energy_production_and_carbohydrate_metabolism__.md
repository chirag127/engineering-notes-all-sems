## Unit 3 - Catabolic & Anabolic Reactions: Enzymes, Energy Production and Carbohydrate Metabolism. Lipid & Protein Catabolism, Energy Production Mechanism, Metabolic Diversity & Pathways of Energy Use. Integration of Metabolism

1. **Catabolic reactions** are the set of metabolic pathways that break down molecules into smaller units to release energy. These reactions involve the breakdown of complex organic molecules, such as carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins, into simpler molecules, such as carbon dioxide, water, and ammonia.

2. **Anabolic reactions**, on the other hand, are the set of metabolic pathways that construct molecules from smaller units. These reactions require energy and are the building blocks of growth and repair in the body.

3. **Enzymes** are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions in the body. They are essential for both catabolic and anabolic reactions, as they lower the activation energy required for the reaction to occur.

4. **Energy production** in the body occurs through the breakdown of carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins. Carbohydrate metabolism involves the breakdown of glucose through glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the primary energy currency of the cell.

5. **Lipid catabolism** involves the breakdown of fatty acids through beta-oxidation to produce acetyl-CoA, which can then enter the citric acid cycle to produce ATP.

6. **Protein catabolism** involves the breakdown of amino acids through deamination to produce ammonia and keto acids, which can then be further metabolized to produce ATP.

7. **Energy production mechanisms** vary among different organisms. Some organisms, such as plants, use photosynthesis to convert sunlight into chemical energy. Others, such as animals, use cellular respiration to convert organic molecules into ATP.

8. **Metabolic diversity** refers to the variety of metabolic pathways and energy production mechanisms found among different organisms. Some organisms, such as bacteria, can use a wide range of metabolic pathways to produce energy, while others, such as humans, have a more limited set of pathways.

9. **Pathways of energy use** refer to the various ways in which the energy produced by catabolic reactions is used by the body. This can include the synthesis of new molecules, the contraction of muscles, and the maintenance of body temperature.

10. **Integration of metabolism** refers to the coordination of the various metabolic pathways in the body to maintain homeostasis and meet the energy needs of the organism. This involves the regulation of enzyme activity, the transport of molecules between different tissues, and the storage and release of energy.
