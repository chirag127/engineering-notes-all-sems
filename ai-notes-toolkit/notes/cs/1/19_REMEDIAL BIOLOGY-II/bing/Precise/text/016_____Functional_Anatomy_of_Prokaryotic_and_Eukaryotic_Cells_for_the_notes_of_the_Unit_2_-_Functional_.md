### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS

1. Prokaryotic cells are generally smaller and simpler in structure than eukaryotic cells.
2. Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotic cells have these structures.
3. The size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells can vary greatly, with common shapes including spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), and spiral (spirilla).
4. The structure and function of cells are determined by the presence and organization of various cellular components, including the cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and genetic material.
5. In prokaryotic cells, the genetic material is contained in a single circular chromosome located in the nucleoid region of the cell.
6. In eukaryotic cells, the genetic material is contained in multiple linear chromosomes located within the nucleus.
7. The cell wall provides shape and protection to the cell, while the cell membrane regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell.
8. The cytoplasm contains various organelles and molecules that carry out the cell's metabolic processes.
9. Ribosomes are responsible for protein synthesis, using the genetic information contained in the cell's DNA.
10. The structure and function of cells are highly interconnected, with each component playing a crucial role in the overall functioning of the cell.
