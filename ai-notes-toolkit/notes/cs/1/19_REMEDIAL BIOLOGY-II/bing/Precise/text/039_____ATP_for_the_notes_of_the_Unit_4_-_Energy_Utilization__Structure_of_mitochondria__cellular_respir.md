### ATP

- ATP stands for Adenosine Triphosphate.
- ATP is a molecule that carries energy within cells.
- It is the main energy currency of the cell, and it is an end product of the processes of cellular respiration and fermentation.
- All living organisms utilize ATP to carry out their cellular processes.
- ATP is composed of a ribose sugar, an adenine base, and three phosphate groups.
- The energy stored in ATP is released when the bond between the second and third phosphate groups is broken, forming ADP (Adenosine Diphosphate) and an inorganic phosphate molecule.
- This process is called hydrolysis and releases energy that can be used by the cell to carry out various functions.
- ATP can be regenerated from ADP and inorganic phosphate through a process called phosphorylation, which is carried out by the enzyme ATP synthase during cellular respiration or photosynthesis.
- ATP is a crucial component in the process of cellular respiration, which is the process by which cells generate energy from the oxidation of organic molecules.
- During cellular respiration, ATP is generated through the processes of glycolysis, the citric acid cycle (also known as the Krebs cycle), and oxidative phosphorylation.
- ATP is also involved in other cellular processes such as muscle contraction, active transport, and DNA synthesis.
