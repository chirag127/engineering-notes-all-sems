### Energy Production

Energy production in living organisms occurs through metabolic pathways. These pathways can be broadly divided into two categories: anabolic and catabolic.

- **Anabolic pathways** synthesize molecules and require energy. These pathways are used to build up large molecules from smaller ones, a process known as biosynthesis. Anabolic reactions are endergonic, meaning they require an input of energy to progress and are not spontaneous.

- **Catabolic pathways** break down molecules and produce energy. These pathways are used to break down large molecules into smaller ones, a process known as degradation. The reactions governing the breakdown of food to obtain energy are called catabolic reactions.

Because almost all metabolic reactions take place non-spontaneously, proteins called enzymes help facilitate those chemical reactions. Typically, anabolic and catabolic reactions are coupled, with catabolism providing the activation energy for anabolism.

Carbohydrate, protein, and lipid metabolic pathways are interconnected. Anabolic reactions use the energy produced by catabolic reactions to synthesize larger molecules from smaller ones, such as when the body forms proteins by stringing together amino acids.
