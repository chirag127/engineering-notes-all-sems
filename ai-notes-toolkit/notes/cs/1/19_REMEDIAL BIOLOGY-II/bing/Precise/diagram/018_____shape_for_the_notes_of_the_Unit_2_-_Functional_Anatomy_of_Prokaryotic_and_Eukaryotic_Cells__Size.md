### Shape of Bacterial Cells

Bacterial cells come in a variety of shapes and sizes. The three most common shapes are:

1. **Coccus**: These are spherical or oval-shaped cells. They can exist as single cells, in pairs (diplococci), in chains (streptococci), in clusters (staphylococci), or in other arrangements.

2. **Bacillus**: These are rod-shaped cells. They can exist as single cells, in pairs (diplobacilli), in chains (streptobacilli), or in other arrangements.

3. **Spirillum**: These are spiral-shaped cells. They can exist as single cells or in chains.

The shape of a bacterial cell is determined by its cell wall. The cell wall is a rigid structure that provides support and protection to the cell. It is made up of peptidoglycan, a complex polymer of sugars and amino acids.

The shape of a bacterial cell can also be influenced by environmental factors such as temperature, pH, and nutrient availability. Some bacteria can change their shape in response to changes in their environment.

In summary, bacterial cells come in a variety of shapes, with the most common being coccus, bacillus, and spirillum. The shape of a bacterial cell is determined by its cell wall and can be influenced by environmental factors.
