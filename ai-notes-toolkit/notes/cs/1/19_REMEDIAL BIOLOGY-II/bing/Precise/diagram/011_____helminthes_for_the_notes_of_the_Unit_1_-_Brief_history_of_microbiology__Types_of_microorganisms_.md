### Helminths

- Helminths are a group of parasitic worms that belong to the phylum Platyhelminthes (flatworms) and Nematoda (roundworms).
- They are multicellular organisms that can be seen with the naked eye in their adult stages.
- Helminths are responsible for a range of diseases in humans and animals, including intestinal infections, tissue infections, and blood-borne infections.
- Common examples of helminths include tapeworms, flukes, and roundworms.
- Helminths have complex life cycles that often involve multiple hosts.
- Infection with helminths can occur through ingestion of contaminated food or water, contact with contaminated soil, or through the bite of an infected insect.
- Treatment for helminth infections typically involves the use of antiparasitic medications.
- Prevention of helminth infections involves practicing good hygiene, such as washing hands and food, and avoiding contact with contaminated soil or water.
