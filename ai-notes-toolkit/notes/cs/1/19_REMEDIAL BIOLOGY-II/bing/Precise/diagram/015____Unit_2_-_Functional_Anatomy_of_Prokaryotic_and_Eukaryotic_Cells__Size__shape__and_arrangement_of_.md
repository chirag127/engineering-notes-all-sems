## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

### Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are typically much smaller than eukaryotic cells, with an average size of about 1-5 micrometers in length.
- Bacterial cells come in a variety of shapes, including spheres (cocci), rods (bacilli), and spirals (spirilla).
- The arrangement of bacterial cells can also vary, with some bacteria forming clusters, chains, or pairs.
- The shape and arrangement of bacterial cells can be influenced by environmental factors, such as temperature and nutrient availability.

### Structure and function of cells

- Cells are the basic unit of life and are responsible for carrying out all the functions necessary for an organism to survive.
- Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, are simpler in structure than eukaryotic cells and lack a defined nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Eukaryotic cells, on the other hand, have a defined nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum, that perform specific functions within the cell.
- Both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells have a plasma membrane that regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell, as well as a cytoplasm where many cellular processes take place.
- Cells also contain genetic material in the form of DNA, which provides the instructions for the cell's functions and characteristics.
