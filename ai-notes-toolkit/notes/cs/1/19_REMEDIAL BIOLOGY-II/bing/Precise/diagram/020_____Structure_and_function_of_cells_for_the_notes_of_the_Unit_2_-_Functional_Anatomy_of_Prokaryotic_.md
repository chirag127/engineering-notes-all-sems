### Structure and function of cells

Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS

1. Cells are the basic unit of life and are responsible for carrying out all the functions necessary for an organism to survive.
2. Prokaryotic cells are simple in structure and are found in organisms such as bacteria. They lack a defined nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
3. Eukaryotic cells, on the other hand, are more complex and are found in organisms such as plants and animals. They have a defined nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
4. The size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells can vary greatly. Some bacteria are spherical, while others are rod-shaped or spiral. They can also be arranged in clusters, chains, or pairs.
5. The structure and function of cells are closely related. For example, the cell membrane is responsible for controlling what enters and exits the cell, while the mitochondria are responsible for producing energy for the cell.
6. Other important organelles in eukaryotic cells include the endoplasmic reticulum, which is involved in protein synthesis, and the Golgi apparatus, which is responsible for modifying and sorting proteins.
7. In prokaryotic cells, the cell wall provides support and protection, while the flagella and pili are used for movement and attachment.
8. Understanding the structure and function of cells is crucial for understanding how organisms function and how they interact with their environment.
