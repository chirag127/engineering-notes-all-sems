### ATP

ATP, or Adenosine Triphosphate, is a molecule that serves as the primary source of energy for cells. It is composed of an adenine base, a ribose sugar, and three phosphate groups. The energy stored in ATP is released when the bond between the second and third phosphate groups is broken, producing ADP (Adenosine Diphosphate) and an inorganic phosphate molecule.

- ATP is generated through cellular respiration, which occurs in the mitochondria.
- The structure of the mitochondria includes an outer membrane, an inner membrane, and a matrix.
- The inner membrane is highly folded, forming cristae, which increases the surface area for the electron transport chain and ATP synthesis.
- Cellular respiration involves the breakdown of glucose to produce ATP.
- The process can be divided into four stages: Glycolysis, Formation of Acetyl Co-A, Krebs Cycle, and Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation.
- Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and involves the breakdown of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate.
- The pyruvate is then transported into the mitochondria, where it is converted into acetyl Co-A.
- The acetyl Co-A then enters the Krebs Cycle, also known as the Citric Acid Cycle, which takes place in the mitochondrial matrix.
- The Krebs Cycle produces ATP, NADH, and FADH2, which are used in the final stage of cellular respiration, the Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation.
- This stage occurs on the inner mitochondrial membrane and involves the transfer of electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen, producing water and ATP.
- The amount of ATP produced can vary depending on factors such as the availability of oxygen and the type of cell.
