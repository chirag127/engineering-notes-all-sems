### Glycolysis

Glycolysis is the first step in the breakdown of glucose to extract energy for cellular metabolism. It is a series of ten chemical reactions that occur in the cytoplasm of the cell. The process can be divided into two phases: the energy investment phase and the energy payoff phase.

1. In the energy investment phase, two ATP molecules are used to phosphorylate glucose, forming fructose-1,6-bisphosphate.
2. Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate is then split into two molecules of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P).
3. In the energy payoff phase, two molecules of NADH and four molecules of ATP are produced for each molecule of glucose that enters glycolysis.
4. The net gain of ATP from glycolysis is two molecules per molecule of glucose.
5. The end product of glycolysis is pyruvate, which can be further metabolized through the citric acid cycle (also known as the Krebs cycle) in the mitochondria to produce more ATP.

Glycolysis is an important process in the metabolism of carbohydrates and other compounds. It is the first step in the breakdown of glucose, which is the primary source of energy for cells. The process is regulated by several enzymes and can be affected by various factors, including the availability of oxygen and the levels of certain hormones.
