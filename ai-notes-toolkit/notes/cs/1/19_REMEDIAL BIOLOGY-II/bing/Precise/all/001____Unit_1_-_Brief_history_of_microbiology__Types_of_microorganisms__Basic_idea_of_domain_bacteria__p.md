## Unit 1 - Brief history of microbiology, Types of microorganisms, Basic idea of domain bacteria, proteobacteria, non proteobacteria Gram –ve and Gram +ve bacteria, lichens, algae, protozoa, helminthes, viral structures, viral multiplication, Role of microorganisms in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals

1. **Brief history of microbiology:** Microbiology is the study of microorganisms, which are unicellular or cell-cluster microscopic organisms. The field of microbiology can be traced back to the 1670s when Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, known as the "father of microbiology," first observed microorganisms using a microscope.
2. **Types of microorganisms:** There are several types of microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and algae.
3. **Basic idea of domain bacteria:** Bacteria are single-celled prokaryotic organisms that belong to the domain Bacteria. They are found in almost every habitat on Earth and play important roles in various ecosystems.
4. **Proteobacteria:** Proteobacteria is a major phylum of gram-negative bacteria that includes a wide variety of pathogens, such as Escherichia coli and Salmonella.
5. **Non-proteobacteria Gram-negative bacteria:** Non-proteobacteria Gram-negative bacteria include several phyla of bacteria that are not closely related to the Proteobacteria. These include the Bacteroidetes, the Cyanobacteria, and the Spirochaetes.
6. **Gram-positive bacteria:** Gram-positive bacteria are a group of bacteria that retain the crystal violet stain used in the Gram staining method. They are characterized by a thick peptidoglycan cell wall and are generally divided into two groups: the Firmicutes and the Actinobacteria.
7. **Lichens:** Lichens are a symbiotic association between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner, usually an alga or a cyanobacterium. They are found in a wide range of habitats and play important roles in their ecosystems.
8. **Algae:** Algae are a diverse group of aquatic organisms that are capable of photosynthesis. They range in size from single-celled organisms to large multicellular forms and are found in both freshwater and marine environments.
9. **Protozoa:** Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotic organisms that are classified as protists. They are found in a wide range of habitats and play important roles in various ecosystems.
10. **Helminthes:** Helminthes are a group of parasitic worms that include roundworms, tapeworms, and flukes. They are found in a wide range of hosts and can cause various diseases in humans and animals.
11. **Viral structures:** Viruses are non-living infectious agents that are composed of a nucleic acid genome surrounded by a protein coat called a capsid. Some viruses also have an outer envelope composed of lipids and proteins.
12. **Viral multiplication:** Viruses replicate by taking over the host cell's machinery and using it to produce new virus particles. The specific steps involved in viral replication vary depending on the type of virus and the host cell.
13. **Role of microorganisms in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals:** Microorganisms play important roles in the production of various industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals. For example, bacteria are used to produce antibiotics, vaccines, and insulin, while fungi are used to produce citric acid and penicillin.
