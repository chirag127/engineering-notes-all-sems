## Unit 3 - Catabolic & Anabolic Reactions: Enzymes, Energy Production and Carbohydrate Metabolism. Lipid & Protein Catabolism, Energy Production Mechanism, Metabolic Diversity & Pathways of Energy Use. Integration of Metabolism

1. **Catabolic reactions** are the set of metabolic pathways that break down molecules into smaller units and release energy. These reactions involve the breakdown of complex organic molecules, such as carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins, into simpler molecules, such as carbon dioxide, water, and ammonia.

2. **Anabolic reactions**, on the other hand, are the set of metabolic pathways that construct molecules from smaller units and require energy. These reactions involve the synthesis of complex molecules from simpler ones, such as the synthesis of proteins from amino acids.

3. **Enzymes** are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions in the body. They are essential for both catabolic and anabolic reactions, as they lower the activation energy required for the reaction to occur.

4. **Energy production** in the body occurs mainly through the process of cellular respiration, which involves the breakdown of glucose to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the main source of energy for cells.

5. **Carbohydrate metabolism** involves the breakdown of carbohydrates, such as glucose, into simpler molecules, such as pyruvate, through the process of glycolysis. Pyruvate can then be further broken down through the citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation to produce ATP.

6. **Lipid catabolism** involves the breakdown of lipids, such as triglycerides, into simpler molecules, such as fatty acids and glycerol. Fatty acids can then be further broken down through the process of beta-oxidation to produce acetyl-CoA, which can enter the citric acid cycle to produce ATP.

7. **Protein catabolism** involves the breakdown of proteins into amino acids, which can then be further broken down to produce energy or be used in other metabolic pathways.

8. **Energy production mechanisms** vary among different organisms. Some organisms, such as plants, use photosynthesis to produce energy, while others, such as animals, use cellular respiration.

9. **Metabolic diversity** refers to the variety of metabolic pathways and processes that exist among different organisms. This diversity allows organisms to adapt to different environments and use different sources of energy.

10. **Pathways of energy use** refer to the different ways in which energy is used in the body. These pathways include the production of ATP through cellular respiration, the use of ATP to power cellular processes, and the storage of energy in the form of glycogen or fat.

11. **Integration of metabolism** refers to the coordination of different metabolic pathways to maintain homeostasis in the body. This involves the regulation of energy production, storage, and use to meet the changing needs of the body.
