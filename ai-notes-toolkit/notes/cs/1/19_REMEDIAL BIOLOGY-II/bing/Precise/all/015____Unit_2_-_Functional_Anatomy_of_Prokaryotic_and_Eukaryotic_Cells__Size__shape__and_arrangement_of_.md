## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

### Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are typically much smaller than eukaryotic cells, with an average size of about 1-5 micrometers in length.
- Bacteria come in a variety of shapes, including spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), and spiral (spirilla).
- The shape of a bacterial cell is determined by its cell wall, which provides structural support and protection.
- Bacterial cells can be arranged in various ways, including singly, in pairs, in chains, or in clusters.
- The arrangement of bacterial cells is often used as a characteristic for identification and classification.

### Structure and function of cells

- Cells are the basic unit of life and are responsible for carrying out all the functions necessary for an organism to survive.
- Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Instead, their genetic material is contained in a single, circular chromosome located in the nucleoid region of the cell.
- Prokaryotic cells also have a cell wall, which provides structural support and protection, and a plasma membrane, which regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
- Eukaryotic cells, on the other hand, have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria and chloroplasts.
- The nucleus contains the cell's genetic material and is responsible for controlling the cell's activities.
- Mitochondria are responsible for generating energy for the cell through cellular respiration, while chloroplasts are responsible for photosynthesis in plant cells.
- Eukaryotic cells also have a cytoskeleton, which provides structural support and helps with cell movement and division.
