#### CO CO Statement Bloom’s

- CO stands for Course Outcomes, which are statements that describe what learners should be able to do or demonstrate at the end of a course.
- CO statements should be clear, measurable, and aligned with the course objectives and assessment methods.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a framework that classifies different levels of cognitive skills, from the most basic to the most complex.
- Bloom's taxonomy can help teachers write effective CO statements by providing a list of action verbs that correspond to each level of cognition.
- The revised Bloom's taxonomy has six levels: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Remember: The ability to recall or recognize facts, terms, concepts, or procedures.
- Understand: The ability to explain, interpret, summarize, or paraphrase information.
- Apply: The ability to use knowledge or skills in new or different situations.
- Analyze: The ability to break down information into parts, identify relationships, or compare and contrast elements.
- Evaluate: The ability to judge the quality, value, or relevance of information, arguments, or solutions.
- Create: The ability to generate, produce, or synthesize new or original ideas, products, or solutions.
- Examples of CO statements using Bloom's taxonomy verbs are:

  - Remember: By the end of this course, learners will be able to list the main components of a computer system.
  - Understand: By the end of this course, learners will be able to explain the functions of different software applications.
  - Apply: By the end of this course, learners will be able to use a word processor to create and format documents.
  - Analyze: By the end of this course, learners will be able to compare and contrast different types of operating systems.
  - Evaluate: By the end of this course, learners will be able to assess the advantages and disadvantages of various online platforms.
  - Create: By the end of this course, learners will be able to design and develop a simple website using HTML and CSS.
