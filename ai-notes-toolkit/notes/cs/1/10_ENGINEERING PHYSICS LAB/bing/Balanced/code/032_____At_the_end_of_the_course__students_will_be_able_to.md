### At the end of the course, students will be able to

- Define the basic concepts and terminology of computer science, such as algorithms, data structures, abstraction, complexity, and programming languages.
- Apply the principles of computational thinking, such as decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and generalization, to solve problems and design algorithms.
- Implement, test, debug, and document programs using a high-level programming language, such as Python, Java, or C++.
- Analyze the efficiency and correctness of algorithms using mathematical tools, such as asymptotic notation, recurrence relations, and induction.
- Compare and contrast different data structures, such as arrays, lists, stacks, queues, trees, graphs, and hash tables, and their applications and trade-offs.
- Design and implement abstract data types, such as stacks, queues, sets, maps, and graphs, using object-oriented programming concepts, such as classes, inheritance, polymorphism, and encapsulation.
- Use common algorithms and techniques, such as sorting, searching, recursion, divide-and-conquer, greedy, dynamic programming, and backtracking, to solve problems and optimize solutions.
- Evaluate the ethical, social, and legal implications of computing, such as privacy, security, intellectual property, and digital citizenship.
