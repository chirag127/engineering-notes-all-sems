#### Graphical and Computational Analysis

- Graphical analysis is a method of presenting and interpreting data using graphs, charts, diagrams, and other visual tools.
- Computational analysis is a method of applying mathematical and logical operations to data using algorithms, software, and hardware.
- Both methods are useful for exploring, summarizing, and communicating data in different contexts and domains.
- Some examples of graphical analysis are:
  - Line graphs: show the relationship between two variables over time or along a continuum.
  - Bar graphs: show the frequency or amount of different categories or groups.
  - Pie charts: show the proportion or percentage of different parts of a whole.
  - Scatter plots: show the correlation or association between two variables.
  - Histograms: show the distribution or frequency of a single variable.
  - Box plots: show the summary statistics and outliers of a single variable or a group of variables.
- Some examples of computational analysis are:
  - Descriptive statistics: calculate and display the measures of central tendency, variability, and shape of a data set.
  - Inferential statistics: test hypotheses and draw conclusions about a population based on a sample of data.
  - Regression analysis: model the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables.
  - Classification analysis: assign data points to different groups or classes based on their features or attributes.
  - Clustering analysis: group data points based on their similarity or dissimilarity.
  - Dimensionality reduction: reduce the number of variables or features in a data set while preserving the essential information.
