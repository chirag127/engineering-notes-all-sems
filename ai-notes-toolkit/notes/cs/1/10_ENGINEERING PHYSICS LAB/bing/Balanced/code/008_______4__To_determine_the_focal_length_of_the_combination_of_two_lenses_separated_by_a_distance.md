##### 4. To determine the focal length of the combination of two lenses separated by a distance

- The focal length of a combination of two lenses separated by a distance is given by the formula:

`1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/f1f2`

where f is the focal length of the combination, f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the individual lenses, and d is the distance between them.

- To determine the focal length of the combination experimentally, you need the following apparatus:

  - A light source, such as a candle or a lamp
  - A screen, such as a white paper or a wall
  - A meter scale or a ruler
  - Two convex lenses of known focal lengths
  - A lens holder or a stand to hold the lenses

- The procedure is as follows:

  - Place the light source at a fixed distance from the screen, such that a clear image of the source is formed on the screen. This distance is the object distance, u.
  - Place one of the lenses between the source and the screen, such that the image of the source is still clear on the screen. Adjust the position of the lens until the image is at the minimum size. This position is the principal focus of the lens, and the distance from the lens to the screen is the focal length of the lens, f1.
  - Place the other lens between the first lens and the screen, such that the image of the source is still clear on the screen. Adjust the position of the second lens until the image is at the minimum size. This position is the principal focus of the second lens, and the distance from the second lens to the screen is the focal length of the second lens, f2.
  - Measure the distance between the two lenses, d, using the meter scale or the ruler.
  - Use the formula above to calculate the focal length of the combination, f, using the values of f1, f2, and d.
  - Repeat the experiment with different values of d and compare the results.

- The expected outcome is that the focal length of the combination will vary depending on the distance between the lenses. The focal length of the combination will be equal to the focal length of the first lens when the second lens is at infinity, and equal to the focal length of the second lens when the first lens is at infinity. The focal length of the combination will be shorter than the focal lengths of the individual lenses when the lenses are close to each other, and longer than the focal lengths of the individual lenses when the lenses are far apart. The focal length of the combination will be negative when the lenses have opposite signs, i.e., one is convex and the other is concave.
