#### CO-4 Determine and calculate various physical properties of a given material by using

Physical properties are the traits a material has before it is used, such as colour, hardness, density, etc. They can be observed or measured without changing the composition of the material. Physical properties are important for selecting and designing materials for different applications. Some of the physical properties of materials are:

- Density: The mass per unit volume of a material. It is a measure of how compact or heavy a material is. Density can be calculated by dividing the mass by the volume of the material. The unit of density is kg/m3 or g/cm3. Density can affect the strength, stiffness, and durability of a material. For example, metals have high density and are strong and durable, while plastics have low density and are light and flexible.
- Specific gravity: The ratio of the density of a material to the density of a reference substance, usually water. It is a dimensionless quantity that indicates how heavy or light a material is compared to water. Specific gravity can be calculated by dividing the density of the material by the density of water. Specific gravity can affect the buoyancy and corrosion resistance of a material. For example, materials with specific gravity less than 1 can float on water, while materials with specific gravity more than 1 can sink in water.
- State of matter: The physical form of a material, such as solid, liquid, or gas. It depends on the temperature and pressure of the material. State of matter can affect the shape, volume, and flow of a material. For example, solids have fixed shape and volume, liquids have fixed volume but can change shape, and gases have no fixed shape or volume and can expand or compress.
- Melting point: The temperature at which a solid material changes into a liquid state. It is a measure of how much heat energy is required to break the bonds between the atoms or molecules of the material. Melting point can be determined by heating the material until it melts and measuring the temperature. The unit of melting point is °C or K. Melting point can affect the thermal stability and conductivity of a material. For example, metals have high melting points and can withstand high temperatures, while plastics have low melting points and can melt or deform easily.
- Boiling point: The temperature at which a liquid material changes into a gas state. It is a measure of how much heat energy is required to overcome the intermolecular forces of the material. Boiling point can be determined by heating the material until it boils and measuring the temperature. The unit of boiling point is °C or K. Boiling point can affect the volatility and evaporation of a material. For example, water has a boiling point of 100°C at normal atmospheric pressure, while ethanol has a boiling point of 78°C and can evaporate faster than water.
- Electrical conductivity: The ability of a material to allow electric current to pass through it. It is a measure of how easily the electrons can move in the material. Electrical conductivity can be calculated by dividing the electric current by the potential difference and the cross-sectional area of the material. The unit of electrical conductivity is S/m or Ω-1m-1. Electrical conductivity can affect the electrical performance and efficiency of a material. For example, metals have high electrical conductivity and are good conductors of electricity, while plastics have low electrical conductivity and are good insulators of electricity.
- Thermal conductivity: The ability of a material to transfer heat energy from one point to another. It is a measure of how fast the heat can flow in the material. Thermal conductivity can be calculated by dividing the heat flux by the temperature gradient and the thickness of the material. The unit of thermal conductivity is W/mK or J/smK. Thermal conductivity can affect the thermal performance and efficiency of a material. For example, metals have high thermal conductivity and can transfer heat quickly, while plastics have low thermal conductivity and can retain heat longer.
