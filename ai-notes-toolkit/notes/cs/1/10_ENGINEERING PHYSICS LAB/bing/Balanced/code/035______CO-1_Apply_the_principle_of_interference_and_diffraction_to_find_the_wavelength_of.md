#### CO-1 Apply the principle of interference and diffraction to find the wavelength of

- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of waves from two or more sources, resulting in a new wave pattern that may have regions of constructive or destructive interference .
- Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of waves around obstacles or apertures, resulting in a new wave pattern that may have regions of spreading or narrowing .
- The principle of interference and diffraction can be used to find the wavelength of a wave source by using a device called a diffraction grating, which consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits .
- When a wave of wavelength λ passes through a diffraction grating with slit spacing d, it produces an interference pattern on a screen at a distance L from the grating. The bright fringes (maxima) occur at angles θ such that d sin θ = mλ, where m is an integer .
- By measuring the distance between the central maximum (m = 0) and the first order maximum (m = 1), the angle θ can be calculated using trigonometry. Then, by knowing the values of d and θ, the wavelength λ can be found using the equation d sin θ = mλ .
- This method can be used to find the wavelength of any wave source, such as light, sound, or microwaves, as long as the slit spacing d is comparable to the wavelength λ .
