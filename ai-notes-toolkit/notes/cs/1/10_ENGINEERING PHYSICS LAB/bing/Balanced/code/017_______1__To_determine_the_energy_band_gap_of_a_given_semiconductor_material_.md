##### 1. To determine the energy band gap of a given semiconductor material.

- The energy band gap of a semiconductor is the difference between the energy of the highest occupied state in the valence band and the lowest unoccupied state in the conduction band.
- The energy band gap determines the electrical conductivity of the semiconductor, as well as its optical and thermal properties.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor can be measured by various methods, such as absorption spectroscopy, photoluminescence spectroscopy, or electrical conductivity measurements.
- One of the common methods to determine the energy band gap of a semiconductor is to use a four-probe setup, which consists of a semiconductor sample, a constant current source, a voltmeter, and four electrodes attached to the sample.
- The four-probe method eliminates the effect of the contact resistance between the electrodes and the sample, and measures the true resistance of the sample.
- The procedure of the four-probe method is as follows:

  - Connect the four electrodes to the sample, such that the current flows through the outer electrodes and the voltage is measured across the inner electrodes.
  - Connect the current source and the voltmeter to the appropriate terminals of the four-probe setup.
  - Vary the temperature of the sample by using a heating or cooling device, and record the corresponding values of the current and the voltage.
  - Plot the graph of the natural logarithm of the current versus the reciprocal of the temperature, and obtain a straight line.
  - Calculate the slope of the straight line, which is proportional to the energy band gap of the semiconductor.

- The formula for the energy band gap of the semiconductor is given by:

  - E_g = (2k_Bs)/q
  - where E_g is the energy band gap, k_B is the Boltzmann constant, s is the slope of the ln(I) vs 1/T graph, and q is the elementary charge.

- The advantages of the four-probe method are:

  - It is simple and accurate.
  - It does not require a calibrated light source or a spectrometer.
  - It can be used for any type of semiconductor material.

- The disadvantages of the four-probe method are:

  - It requires a temperature-controlled environment and a stable current source.
  - It may not be suitable for very small or very large band gap semiconductors, as the current may be too low or too high to measure.
