##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current

- The objective of this experiment is to measure the magnetic field produced by a circular coil carrying a steady current at different distances along its axis and to verify the theoretical formula for the same.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are a circular coil of known radius and number of turns, a constant current source, an ammeter, a magnetic compass, a meter scale, a stand and a clamp.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Connect the circular coil to the constant current source and the ammeter in series.
  - Place the coil horizontally on a table and adjust the current to a suitable value. Note down the current reading from the ammeter.
  - Place the magnetic compass on the stand and clamp it at a fixed height above the center of the coil. Note down the initial deflection of the compass needle from the magnetic north.
  - Move the compass along the axis of the coil and note down the deflection of the needle at different distances from the center of the coil. Repeat this for both sides of the coil.
  - Plot a graph of the deflection of the needle versus the distance from the center of the coil. The graph should be a smooth curve that decreases as the distance increases.
  - Use the following formula to calculate the magnetic field at each distance from the center of the coil:

    `B = (μ0NI/2)(R^2/(R^2 + x^2)^(3/2))`

    where B is the magnetic field, μ0 is the permeability of free space, N is the number of turns of the coil, I is the current, R is the radius of the coil and x is the distance from the center of the coil.
  - Compare the calculated values of the magnetic field with the experimental values obtained from the graph. Calculate the percentage error and comment on the sources of error and the precautions to be taken.
- The expected result of this experiment is that the magnetic field along the axis of a current carrying coil varies inversely with the cube of the distance from the center of the coil and is proportional to the current and the number of turns of the coil. The experimental values of the magnetic field should be close to the theoretical values within a reasonable margin of error. The sources of error could be due to the non-uniformity of the coil, the fluctuations of the current, the parallax error in reading the compass needle, the external magnetic fields and the human errors. The precautions to be taken are to ensure that the coil is circular and uniform, the current is constant and steady, the compass needle is free to rotate and aligned with the axis of the coil, the external magnetic fields are minimized and the readings are taken carefully and accurately.
