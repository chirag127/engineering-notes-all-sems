##### 6. To determine the wavelength of He-Ne laser light using single slit diffraction.

- The aim of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of a He-Ne laser light by observing the diffraction pattern produced by a single slit of known width.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which states that every point on a wavefront can be considered as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the wavefront at any later time is the envelope of these wavelets.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are: a He-Ne laser, a single slit, a screen, a meter scale, and a magnifying lens.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Set up the apparatus as shown in the figure below, with the laser, the slit, and the screen aligned on a horizontal line. Adjust the distance between the slit and the screen to about 1 m.
  - Turn on the laser and observe the diffraction pattern on the screen. You should see a central bright spot (the zeroth-order maximum) and several alternating bright and dark fringes on either side (the higher-order maxima and minima).
  - Use the meter scale to measure the distance between the slit and the screen (D) and the width of the slit (a). Also, use the magnifying lens to measure the distance between the central spot and the first dark fringe on either side (y).
  - Repeat the measurements for different values of D and a, and record the data in a table.
  - Use the formula `λ = ay/D` to calculate the wavelength of the laser light (λ) for each set of data, where y is the distance between the central spot and the first dark fringe, a is the width of the slit, and D is the distance between the slit and the screen.
  - Find the average value of λ and compare it with the theoretical value of 632.8 nm for He-Ne laser light.
