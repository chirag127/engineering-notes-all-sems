# 9. To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid.

## Aim
To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid by measuring the terminal velocity of a spherical body falling through the liquid.

## Theory
Viscosity is the property of a fluid that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces of the fluid that are moving at different velocities. The coefficient of viscosity (η) is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow.

The terminal velocity (v) of a spherical body of radius (r) and density (ρ) falling through a liquid of density (σ) and viscosity (η) under the action of gravity (g) is given by Stokes' law:

v = (2/9) (ρ - σ) g r^2 / η

The coefficient of viscosity can be calculated by rearranging the equation as:

η = (2/9) (ρ - σ) g r^2 / v

## Apparatus
- A glass tube of uniform cross-section and about 1 m long
- A stand and clamp to hold the tube vertically
- A liquid (e.g. glycerine) to fill the tube
- A spherical body (e.g. a steel ball bearing) of known radius and density
- A stopwatch
- A metre scale
- A thermometer

## Procedure
- Fill the glass tube with the liquid up to a suitable height and clamp it vertically on the stand.
- Measure and note the radius and density of the spherical body.
- Measure and note the density and temperature of the liquid.
- Drop the spherical body gently into the liquid from the top of the tube and start the stopwatch.
- Observe the motion of the spherical body and stop the stopwatch when it attains a constant velocity (terminal velocity).
- Measure and note the time taken (t) by the spherical body to cover a known distance (d) in the tube at terminal velocity.
- Repeat the experiment with different spherical bodies of different radii and densities and tabulate the observations.
