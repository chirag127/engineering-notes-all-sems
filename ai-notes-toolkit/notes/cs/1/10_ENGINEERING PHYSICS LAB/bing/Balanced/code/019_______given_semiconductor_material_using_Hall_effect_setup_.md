# Given semiconductor material using Hall effect setup

The Hall effect is a phenomenon that occurs when a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field perpendicular to the direction of the current. The magnetic field exerts a force on the moving charge carriers, causing them to accumulate on one side of the conductor. This creates a potential difference across the conductor, called the Hall voltage.

The Hall effect can be used to determine the type, concentration, and mobility of charge carriers in a given semiconductor material. A Hall effect setup consists of the following components:

- A semiconductor sample of known dimensions and shape, usually a thin rectangular slab.
- A pair of electrodes attached to the opposite ends of the sample, to apply a constant current through the sample.
- A pair of electrodes attached to the opposite sides of the sample, to measure the Hall voltage across the sample.
- A magnet that can produce a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the sample.
- A voltmeter that can measure the Hall voltage accurately.
- A current source that can provide a constant current to the sample.
- A gaussmeter that can measure the magnetic field strength.

- Calculate the Hall coefficient, the charge carrier concentration, and the charge carrier mobility using the following formulas:
