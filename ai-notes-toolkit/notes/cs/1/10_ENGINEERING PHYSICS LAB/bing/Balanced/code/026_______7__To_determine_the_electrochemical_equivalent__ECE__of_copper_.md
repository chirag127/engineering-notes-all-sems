##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

- The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or liberated at an electrode during electrolysis by one coulomb of charge.
- The ECE of copper can be determined by using a copper voltameter, which consists of two copper electrodes immersed in a solution of copper sulfate.
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Clean and polish the copper electrodes and weigh them accurately.
  - Connect the electrodes to a battery or a power supply and a rheostat in series. Also connect an ammeter and a voltmeter in the circuit to measure the current and the potential difference.
  - Adjust the rheostat to obtain a steady current of about 0.5 A and note the readings of the ammeter and the voltmeter.
  - Start a stopwatch and let the electrolysis run for a fixed time, such as 10 minutes.
  - Disconnect the electrodes and wash them with distilled water. Dry them with a filter paper and weigh them again.
  - Calculate the mass of copper deposited at the cathode and the mass of copper dissolved at the anode. The difference between them is the mass of copper transferred by the current.
  - Calculate the charge passed through the circuit by multiplying the current and the time.
  - Calculate the ECE of copper by dividing the mass of copper transferred by the charge passed.
  - Repeat the experiment with different values of current and time and take the average of the ECE values obtained.
- The ECE of copper can also be calculated theoretically by using Faraday's laws of electrolysis, which state that:
  - The mass of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to the charge passed through the electrolyte.
  - The mass of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to its chemical equivalent weight, which is the ratio of its molar mass to its valency.
- The theoretical formula for the ECE of copper is:

  - ECE of copper = (Molar mass of copper / Valency of copper) / Faraday constant
  - ECE of copper = (63.5 g/mol / 2) / 96485 C/mol
  - ECE of copper = 0.000329 g/C
- The experimental and theoretical values of the ECE of copper should be close to each other, with some possible sources of error being:
  - Impurities in the electrodes or the electrolyte
  - Incomplete drying of the electrodes
  - Variation in the current or the time
  - Side reactions at the electrodes
