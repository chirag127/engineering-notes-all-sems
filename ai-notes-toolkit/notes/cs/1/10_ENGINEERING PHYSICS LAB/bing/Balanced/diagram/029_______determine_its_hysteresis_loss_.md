##### Determine the hysteresis loss

- Hysteresis loss is the energy dissipated as heat in a magnetic material when it is subjected to a cyclic magnetization process.
- Hysteresis loss is proportional to the area enclosed by the hysteresis loop of the material on the B-H plane, where B is the magnetic flux density and H is the magnetic field intensity.
- To determine the hysteresis loss of a material, we need to measure the B-H curve of the material for a given frequency of magnetization and calculate the area under the curve.
- The steps to measure the B-H curve are as follows:

  - Take a sample of the material and wind a primary coil and a secondary coil around it. Connect the primary coil to an AC voltage source and the secondary coil to a galvanometer.
  - Vary the AC voltage and measure the corresponding current in the primary coil. This gives the value of H for each voltage.
  - Measure the induced voltage in the secondary coil and divide it by the number of turns in the coil. This gives the value of B for each voltage.
  - Plot the B-H curve on a graph paper or a computer software.
  - Find the area under the curve by using a planimeter or a numerical integration method.

- The hysteresis loss per cycle is given by the formula:

  - `P_h = k * f * A`
  - Where P_h is the hysteresis loss, k is a constant that depends on the material, f is the frequency of magnetization, and A is the area under the B-H curve.
  - The hysteresis loss per unit volume is given by dividing P_h by the volume of the sample.
  - The hysteresis loss per unit mass is given by dividing P_h by the mass of the sample.

- Hysteresis loss can be reduced by using materials with narrow hysteresis loops, such as soft magnetic materials, or by using higher frequencies of magnetization, which reduce the area under the curve.
