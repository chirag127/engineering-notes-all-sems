##### Using Carey Foster's Bridge

- A Carey Foster's bridge is a bridge circuit used to measure medium resistances, or to measure small differences between two large resistances.
- It is a variant of the Wheatstone bridge, which consists of four resistances connected in a diamond shape, with a galvanometer and a battery in the two opposite corners.
- The difference between the Wheatstone bridge and the Carey Foster's bridge is that one of the four resistances in the latter is replaced by a uniform resistance wire with a sliding contact.
- The sliding contact can be moved along the wire to balance the bridge, that is, to make the galvanometer show zero deflection.
- The balanced condition of the bridge is given by the equation:

    R1/R2 = (L1 - x)/(L2 + x)

    where R1 and R2 are the known resistances, L1 and L2 are the lengths of the wire on either side of the sliding contact, and x is the distance of the sliding contact from the end of the wire.

- To use the Carey Foster's bridge to measure an unknown resistance Rx, the following steps are followed:

    - Connect Rx in series with R1, and connect a standard resistance Rs in series with R2.
    - Adjust the sliding contact until the bridge is balanced, and note the value of x.
    - Swap the positions of Rx and Rs, and balance the bridge again, and note the new value of x.
    - The unknown resistance Rx can be calculated by the formula:

        Rx = Rs + r(2x - L)

        where r is the resistance per unit length of the wire, and L is the total length of the wire.

- The advantages of the Carey Foster's bridge are:

    - It can measure resistances with high accuracy and sensitivity.
    - It can measure small differences between two large resistances.
    - It can measure the resistance per unit length of the wire.

- The applications of the Carey Foster's bridge are:

    - It can be used to calibrate ammeters and voltmeters.
    - It can be used to measure the resistivity of materials.
    - It can be used to measure the temperature coefficient of resistance.
