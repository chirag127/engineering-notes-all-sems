##### 7. To study the polarization of light using He-Ne laser light.

- Polarization of light is the phenomenon of restricting the vibrations of the electric vector of a transverse electromagnetic wave to a certain plane or direction.
- A polarizer is a device that can produce polarized light from unpolarized light by transmitting only the component of the electric vector that is parallel to its transmission axis and absorbing the rest.
- A He-Ne laser is a type of gas laser that uses a mixture of helium and neon as the active medium and produces a coherent beam of light at a wavelength of 632.8 nm in the red part of the visible spectrum.
- A He-Ne laser has a linearly polarized output, which means that the electric vector of the light oscillates in a fixed plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- The polarization of the He-Ne laser beam can be affected by the anisotropy and mode competition of the laser cavity, as well as by the external factors such as reflection, refraction, scattering, and birefringence of the optical elements in the beam path.
- To study the polarization of light using He-Ne laser, one can use a polarizer and an analyzer, which are two polarizers placed in series, and measure the intensity of the transmitted light as a function of the relative orientation of their transmission axes.
- According to Malus' law, the intensity of the transmitted light is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle between the transmission axes of the polarizer and the analyzer, i.e., I = I0 cos^2 θ, where I0 is the intensity of the incident light and θ is the angle between the transmission axes.
- By rotating the analyzer and recording the intensity of the transmitted light, one can plot a graph of I vs. θ and verify Malus' law. The graph will show a sinusoidal variation of the intensity with a period of 180°.
- The experiment can also demonstrate the concept of polarization by reflection, which is the phenomenon of producing polarized light by reflecting unpolarized light from a non-metallic surface at a certain angle called the Brewster's angle. At this angle, the reflected light is completely polarized in a plane perpendicular to the plane of incidence, while the refracted light is partially polarized in a plane parallel to the plane of incidence.
- To observe polarization by reflection, one can use a glass plate as the reflecting surface and place it at an angle of 56.6° with respect to the incident He-Ne laser beam. By placing the analyzer after the glass plate and rotating it, one can see that the intensity of the reflected light becomes zero when the transmission axis of the analyzer is parallel to the plane of incidence, indicating that the reflected light is polarized perpendicular to that plane. The intensity of the refracted light will show a maximum when the transmission axis of the analyzer is parallel to the plane of incidence, indicating that the refracted light is partially polarized parallel to that plane.
