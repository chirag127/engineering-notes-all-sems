A carrying coil is a loop of wire that carries an electric current. The current produces a magnetic field around the coil, which can be calculated using the Biot-Savart law or Ampere's law. The magnetic field at the center of the coil is given by the formula:

$$B=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2R}$$

where $\mu_0$ is the magnetic permeability of free space, $N$ is the number of turns of the coil, $I$ is the current, and $R$ is the radius of the coil.

To estimate the radius of the coil, we can use the inverse of this formula, given that we know the other parameters:

$$R=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2B}$$

Alternatively, we can measure the magnetic field at a point on the axis of the coil, and use the formula:

$$B=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2}\frac{R^2}{(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}}$$

where $x$ is the distance from the center of the coil. Then, we can solve for $R$ using a quadratic equation or a numerical method.

Some points to remember about carrying coils are:

- The magnetic field is strongest at the center of the coil and decreases as we move away from it.
- The direction of the magnetic field can be determined by the right-hand rule: if we curl the fingers of our right hand around the coil in the direction of the current, the thumb points in the direction of the magnetic field.
- The magnetic field of a carrying coil is similar to that of a bar magnet, with a north pole and a south pole.
- If we place two carrying coils near each other, they can interact with each other's magnetic fields, depending on their relative orientation and distance. If the coils have the same radius and are separated by a distance equal to their radius, they form a Helmholtz coil, which produces a nearly uniform magnetic field in the region between them.
