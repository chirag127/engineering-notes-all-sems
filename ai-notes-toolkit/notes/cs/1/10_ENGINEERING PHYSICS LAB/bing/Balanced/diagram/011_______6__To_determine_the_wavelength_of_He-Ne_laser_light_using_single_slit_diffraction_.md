##### 6. To determine the wavelength of He-Ne laser light using single slit diffraction.

- The aim of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of a He-Ne laser light source using the phenomenon of single slit diffraction.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which states that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the resulting wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are:
  - A He-Ne laser source, which emits coherent and monochromatic light of wavelength around 632.8 nm.
  - A single slit of adjustable width, which acts as a diffracting element.
  - A screen, which receives the diffraction pattern formed by the slit.
  - A meter scale, which measures the distance between the slit and the screen, and the width of the central bright fringe on the screen.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Set up the apparatus as shown in the diagram below, ensuring that the laser beam is perpendicular to the slit and the screen, and that the slit is narrow enough to produce a visible diffraction pattern.
  - Measure and record the distance D between the slit and the screen, and the width of the slit a.
  - Measure and record the width of the central bright fringe w on the screen, using the meter scale.
  - Repeat the measurements for different values of a, keeping D constant.
  - Calculate the wavelength of the laser light using the formula: `λ = wD/a`, where λ is the wavelength, w is the width of the central bright fringe, D is the distance between the slit and the screen, and a is the width of the slit.
  - Find the average value of λ and compare it with the theoretical value of 632.8 nm.

- The sources of error and uncertainty in this experiment are:

  - The measurement of the slit width and the central fringe width may have some parallax and human errors, which can introduce errors in the calculation of the wavelength.

  - The ambient light may interfere with the observation of the diffraction pattern, which can reduce the contrast and clarity of the fringes.
