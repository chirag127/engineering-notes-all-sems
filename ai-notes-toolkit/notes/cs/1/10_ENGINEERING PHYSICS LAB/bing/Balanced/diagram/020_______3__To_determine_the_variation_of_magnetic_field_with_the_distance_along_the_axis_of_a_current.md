##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current carrying circular coil using a magnetic compass.

- Aim: To study how the magnetic field varies with the distance from the center of a circular coil carrying electric current.
- Apparatus: A circular coil of wire, a battery, a rheostat, a key, an ammeter, a magnetic compass, a meter scale, a stand and a clamp.
- Theory: A circular coil carrying electric current produces a magnetic field around it. The magnetic field is strongest at the center of the coil and decreases as the distance from the center increases. The magnetic field at a point on the axis of the coil is given by the formula:

  B = (μ₀NI/2) / (R² + x²)^(3/2)

  where B is the magnetic field, μ₀ is the permeability of free space, N is the number of turns of the coil, I is the current in the coil, R is the radius of the coil and x is the distance from the center of the coil.

- Procedure:
  - Connect the battery, rheostat, key and ammeter in series with the circular coil.
  - Fix the coil on a stand and clamp it horizontally.
  - Place the magnetic compass on a meter scale at the center of the coil and note the deflection of the needle.
  - Move the compass along the meter scale on the axis of the coil and note the deflection of the needle at different distances from the center of the coil.
  - Repeat the experiment for different values of current in the coil by adjusting the rheostat.
  - Plot a graph of magnetic field (B) versus distance (x) for each value of current.
  - Observe the shape of the graph and compare it with the theoretical formula.

- Calculation: The magnetic field at a point on the axis of the coil can be calculated from the deflection of the magnetic compass using the formula:

  B = Bₑ tan θ

  where Bₑ is the horizontal component of the earth's magnetic field and θ is the deflection of the needle.
