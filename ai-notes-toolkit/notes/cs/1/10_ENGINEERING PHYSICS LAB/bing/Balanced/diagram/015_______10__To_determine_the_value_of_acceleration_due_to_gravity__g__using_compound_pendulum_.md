##### 10. To determine the value of acceleration due to gravity (g) using compound pendulum.

- A compound pendulum is a rigid body that can oscillate about a horizontal axis passing through it.
- The time period of a compound pendulum depends on its length, mass, moment of inertia, and the distance of the axis of oscillation from its center of mass.
- The acceleration due to gravity (g) can be determined by measuring the time period of a compound pendulum for different values of the distance of the axis of oscillation from its center of mass.
- The formula for the time period of a compound pendulum is:

    `T = 2π √(I/mgd)`

    where T is the time period, I is the moment of inertia, m is the mass, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and d is the distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass.

- The formula for the acceleration due to gravity (g) is:

    `g = 4π²I/md²T²`

    where g is the acceleration due to gravity, I is the moment of inertia, m is the mass, d is the distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass, and T is the time period.

- The procedure for the experiment is as follows:

    - Suspend a compound pendulum from a rigid support such that it can oscillate freely about a horizontal axis.
    - Measure the mass (m) and the length (L) of the compound pendulum.
    - Mark several points along the length of the compound pendulum at equal intervals. These points will serve as the axes of oscillation for different trials.
    - Measure the distance (d) of each point from the center of mass of the compound pendulum.
    - For each point, suspend the compound pendulum from that point and set it into small oscillations. Measure the time taken for 20 oscillations using a stopwatch. Repeat this for at least three times and take the average time. Divide the average time by 20 to get the time period (T) for that point.
    - Record the values of d and T in a table.
    - Plot a graph of T² versus d and draw the best fit line. The slope of the line will give the value of 4π²I/m.
    - Calculate the value of g using the formula:

        `g = 4π²I/md²T²`

        where g is the acceleration due to gravity, I is the moment of inertia, m is the mass, d is the distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass, and T is the time period.

    - Compare the value of g obtained from the experiment with the standard value of g at the location of the experiment.
