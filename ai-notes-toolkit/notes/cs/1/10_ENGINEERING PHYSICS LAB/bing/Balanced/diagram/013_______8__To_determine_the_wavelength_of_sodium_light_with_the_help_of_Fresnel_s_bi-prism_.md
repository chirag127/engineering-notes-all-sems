##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

- Fresnel's bi-prism is a device that produces two coherent sources of light from a single source by using a thin prism with a very small refracting angle.
- The principle of Fresnel's bi-prism is based on the interference of light waves from the two virtual sources created by the prism.
- The interference pattern consists of alternate bright and dark fringes of equal width, which can be observed on a screen placed at some distance from the prism.
- The wavelength of the light source can be determined by measuring the fringe width and the distance between the prism and the screen, using the formula:

    `λ = bD/d`

    where λ is the wavelength, b is the distance between the virtual sources, D is the distance between the prism and the screen, and d is the fringe width.

- The experimental setup consists of the following components:

    - A sodium lamp as the light source, which emits monochromatic yellow light of wavelength 589 nm.
    - A slit S to produce a narrow beam of light.
    - A convex lens L to make the beam parallel.
    - A Fresnel's bi-prism P with a small refracting angle (about 1°).
    - A micrometer eyepiece E to measure the fringe width.
    - A screen SC to observe the interference pattern.

- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:

    - Adjust the slit S, the lens L and the prism P on an optical bench, such that the slit is illuminated by the sodium lamp and the prism is placed symmetrically with respect to the slit.
    - Place the screen SC at a suitable distance from the prism and observe the interference pattern. Adjust the position of the screen to obtain clear and sharp fringes.
    - Measure the distance D between the prism and the screen using a meter scale.
    - Place the micrometer eyepiece E near the screen and focus it on the fringes. Measure the distance d between any two consecutive bright fringes or dark fringes using the micrometer scale.
    - Repeat the measurements for different positions of the screen and take the average values of D and d.
    - Calculate the distance b between the virtual sources using the formula:

        `b = αD`

        where α is the refracting angle of the prism, which can be measured using a spectrometer or a protractor.

    - Calculate the wavelength λ of the sodium light using the formula:

        `λ = bD/d`

    - Compare the experimental value of λ with the theoretical value of 589 nm and calculate the percentage error.
