Physical properties are the traits a material has before it is used, such as colour, hardness, density, etc. They can be observed or measured without changing the composition of the material . Physical properties can be classified as intensive or extensive, depending on whether they depend on the size or amount of the material. Some examples of physical properties are:

- Density: the mass per unit volume of a material. It can be calculated by dividing the mass by the volume.
- Specific gravity: the ratio of the density of a material to the density of a reference substance, usually water. It can be calculated by dividing the density of the material by the density of water.
- State: the physical form of a material, such as solid, liquid or gas. It depends on the temperature and pressure of the material.
- Melting point: the temperature at which a solid material changes to a liquid state. It can be measured by heating the material until it melts.
- Boiling point: the temperature at which a liquid material changes to a gas state. It can be measured by heating the material until it boils.
- Thermal conductivity: the ability of a material to transfer heat through itself. It can be measured by applying a temperature difference across the material and measuring the heat flow.
- Electrical conductivity: the ability of a material to allow electric current to pass through itself. It can be measured by applying a voltage difference across the material and measuring the electric current.
- Magnetic properties: the ability of a material to be attracted or repelled by a magnet, or to become a magnet itself. They can be measured by using a magnetometer or a compass.
- Optical properties: the ability of a material to interact with light, such as reflection, refraction, absorption, etc. They can be measured by using a spectrometer or a microscope.
