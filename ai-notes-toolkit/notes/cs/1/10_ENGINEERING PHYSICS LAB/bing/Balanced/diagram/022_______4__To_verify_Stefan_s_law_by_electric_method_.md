##### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

Stefan's law states that the energy radiated per second by unit area of a black body at thermodynamic temperature T is directly proportional to T<sup>4</sup>. The constant of proportionality is the Stefan constant, equal to 5.670400 × 10<sup>–8</sup> Wm<sup>–2</sup> K<sup>–4</sup>.

The electric method for verifying Stefan's law involves using a light-bulb filament as a radiating body. The power radiated by the filament can be determined from the voltage and current of the filament. The temperature of the filament can be measured by a thermocouple or a pyrometer.

The experiment setup consists of the following components :

- A light bulb with a tungsten filament
- A rheostat to vary the current through the filament
- A voltmeter and an ammeter to measure the voltage and current of the filament
- A thermocouple or a pyrometer to measure the temperature of the filament
- A blackened copper calorimeter to absorb the radiation from the filament
- A stirrer and a thermometer to measure the temperature of water in the calorimeter
- A stopwatch to measure the time interval

The procedure of the experiment is as follows :

- Connect the light bulb, the rheostat, the voltmeter and the ammeter in series to a power supply
- Fill the calorimeter with water and note the initial temperature of water
- Place the light bulb inside the calorimeter and close the lid
- Adjust the rheostat to obtain a suitable current through the filament
- Note the readings of the voltmeter, the ammeter and the thermocouple or the pyrometer
- Start the stopwatch and stir the water in the calorimeter
- After a certain time interval, stop the stopwatch and note the final temperature of water
- Repeat the experiment for different values of current and temperature

The analysis of the experiment is as follows :

- Plot a graph of power (P) versus temperature (T) and obtain the slope (m)
- Plot a graph of rate of energy gain by water (E/t) versus temperature (T) and obtain the slope (n)
- Compare the values of m and n and verify that they are equal
- Calculate the value of Stefan constant (σ) using the formula P = σAT<sup>4</sup>, where A is the surface area of the filament
- Compare the value of σ with the theoretical value of 5.670400 × 10<sup>–8</sup> Wm<sup>–2</sup> K<sup>–4</sup> and calculate the percentage error

The precautions of the experiment are as follows :

- Ensure that the connections are tight and the rheostat is working properly
- Avoid parallax errors while reading the voltmeter, the ammeter and the thermometer
- Ensure that the filament is completely inside the calorimeter and the lid is closed
- Stir the water in the calorimeter uniformly and continuously
- Avoid heat losses from the calorimeter to the surroundings
- Use a calibrated thermocouple or pyrometer to measure the temperature of the filament accurately
