##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

- Fresnel's bi-prism is a device that produces two coherent sources of light from a single source by using a thin prism with a very small refracting angle.
- The principle of the experiment is based on the interference of light waves from the two virtual sources and the measurement of the fringe width.
- The fringe width is given by the formula: β = λD/d, where λ is the wavelength of light, D is the distance between the slit and the screen, and d is the distance between the two virtual sources.
- The distance d can be calculated by using the formula: d = 2μt/α, where μ is the refractive index of the prism, t is the thickness of the prism, and α is the refracting angle of the prism.
- The refracting angle α can be measured by using a travelling microscope and a graduated scale.
- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:
  - Set up the optical bench with a sodium lamp, a slit, a bi-prism, and a screen.
  - Adjust the slit width to get a sharp and bright image of the source on the screen.
  - Place the bi-prism close to the slit and observe the interference fringes on the screen.
  - Move the screen along the bench until the fringes are clear and well-defined.
  - Measure the distance D between the slit and the screen with a meter scale.
  - Measure the fringe width β by using a travelling microscope and a vernier scale.
  - Repeat the measurements for different values of D and calculate the average value of β.
  - Measure the refracting angle α of the bi-prism by using a travelling microscope and a graduated scale.
  - Measure the thickness t of the bi-prism by using a screw gauge.
  - Calculate the distance d between the two virtual sources by using the formula: d = 2μt/α.
  - Calculate the wavelength λ of sodium light by using the formula: λ = βd/D.
  - Record the observations and results in a tabular form.
