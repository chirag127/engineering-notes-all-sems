##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

- Explain the concept of hysteresis and its significance for transformers.
- Describe the experimental setup and procedure for measuring the B-H curve of a transformer core.
- Analyze the results and compare them with the theoretical predictions.

Some possible points to cover are:

- Hysteresis is the phenomenon of magnetic memory, where the magnetic flux density (B) of a material depends not only on the applied magnetic field intensity (H), but also on its previous history of magnetization and demagnetization.
- Hysteresis causes energy loss in the core of transformers, as the magnetic domains are repeatedly aligned and reversed by the alternating current. The area of the B-H curve represents the energy dissipated per cycle of magnetization.
- The shape of the B-H curve depends on the type and quality of the core material. Soft magnetic materials, such as iron, have a narrow and steep B-H curve, which means they can be easily magnetized and demagnetized with low energy loss. Hard magnetic materials, such as steel, have a wide and flat B-H curve, which means they retain their magnetization and require more energy to reverse it.
- To measure the B-H curve of a transformer core, a specimen in the form of a toroid is used. The toroid is wound with a primary coil, which is connected to an AC voltage source, and a secondary coil, which is connected to an oscilloscope. The primary coil produces a magnetic field (H) in the core, and the secondary coil measures the induced voltage (V), which is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux (dB/dt) in the core.
- The B-H curve can be plotted by using the following relations:

  - H = N1I / 2πr, where N1 is the number of turns in the primary coil, I is the current in the primary coil, and r is the radius of the toroid.
  - B = μ0N2V / 2πrω, where μ0 is the permeability of free space, N2 is the number of turns in the secondary coil, V is the voltage in the secondary coil, and ω is the angular frequency of the AC source.
  - The B-H curve is a closed loop that repeats itself for each cycle of the AC source. The loop can be divided into four quadrants, corresponding to the positive and negative values of B and H. The loop can be characterized by its coercivity (Hc), which is the value of H required to reduce B to zero, and its remanence (Br), which is the value of B remaining when H is zero.
- The results can be compared with the theoretical predictions based on the idealized B-H curve of a linear and lossless material, which is a straight line passing through the origin with a slope equal to the relative permeability of the material. The deviations from the ideal curve can be attributed to the effects of hysteresis, saturation, and eddy currents in the core.
