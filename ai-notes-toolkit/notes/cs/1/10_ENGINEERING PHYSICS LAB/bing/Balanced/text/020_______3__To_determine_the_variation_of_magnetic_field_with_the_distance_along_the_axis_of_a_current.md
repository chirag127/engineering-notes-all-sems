##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current

- The objective of this experiment is to measure the magnetic field produced by a circular coil carrying a steady current at different distances along its axis, and to verify the theoretical formula for the variation of magnetic field with distance.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are a circular coil of known radius and number of turns, a constant current source, an ammeter, a magnetic compass, a meter scale, and a stand to hold the coil vertically.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Connect the circular coil to the constant current source and the ammeter in series, and adjust the current to a suitable value.
  - Place the coil vertically on the stand, and fix the magnetic compass at the center of the coil, such that the needle is parallel to the plane of the coil.
  - Note the deflection of the needle due to the magnetic field of the coil, and record it as θ0.
  - Move the compass along the axis of the coil, and note the deflection of the needle at different distances from the center of the coil, and record them as θ1, θ2, ..., θn.
  - Repeat the above steps for different values of current, and tabulate the results.
- The theory of this experiment is based on the Biot-Savart law, which states that the magnetic field dB at a point due to a small current element Idl is given by

  dB = (μ0/4π) * (Idl x r̂)/r^2

  where μ0 is the permeability of free space, r is the distance from the current element to the point, and r̂ is the unit vector along r.

  - For a circular coil of radius R and N turns carrying a current I, the magnetic field at a point P on its axis at a distance x from the center of the coil is given by

    B = (μ0NI/2) * (R^2/(R^2 + x^2)^(3/2))

  - The magnetic field at the center of the coil is given by

    B0 = (μ0NI/2R)

  - The magnetic field at a point P on the axis of the coil can also be expressed in terms of the deflection of the magnetic compass needle at that point, as

    B = B0 * cosθ

    where θ is the angle between the magnetic field of the coil and the horizontal component of the earth's magnetic field.

  - Therefore, the variation of magnetic field with distance along the axis of the coil can be represented by the equation

    cosθ = (R^2/(R^2 + x^2)^(3/2))

- The graph of cosθ versus x^2 is a rectangular hyperbola, which can be verified by plotting the experimental data.

- The sources of error in this experiment are the fluctuations in the current, the parallax error in reading the compass needle, the non-uniformity of the earth's magnetic field, and the presence of other magnetic materials near the coil.

- The precautions to be taken in this experiment are to ensure that the coil is placed vertically, the compass needle is parallel to the plane of the coil, the current is constant, and the readings are taken without disturbing the coil or the compass.
