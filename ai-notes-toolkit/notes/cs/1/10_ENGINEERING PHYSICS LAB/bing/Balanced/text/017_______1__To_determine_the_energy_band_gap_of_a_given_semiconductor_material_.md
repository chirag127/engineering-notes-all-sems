##### 1. To determine the energy band gap of a given semiconductor material.

- The energy band gap of a semiconductor is the difference between the energy levels of the valence band and the conduction band.
- The energy band gap determines the electrical conductivity of the semiconductor, as well as its optical and thermal properties.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor can be measured by various methods, such as absorption spectroscopy, photoluminescence spectroscopy, or electrical conductivity measurements.
- One of the common methods to determine the energy band gap of a semiconductor is to plot the variation of electrical conductivity with temperature and extrapolate the curve to zero conductivity.
- The electrical conductivity of a semiconductor depends on the number of charge carriers (electrons and holes) that are available to move under an applied electric field.
- The number of charge carriers in a semiconductor is proportional to the probability of electrons jumping from the valence band to the conduction band, which is given by the Boltzmann factor: $$P \propto e^{-\frac{E_g}{2k_BT}}$$ where $P$ is the probability, $E_g$ is the energy band gap, $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the absolute temperature.
- The electrical conductivity of a semiconductor can be expressed as: $$\sigma = n_e e \mu_e + n_h e \mu_h$$ where $\sigma$ is the conductivity, $n_e$ and $n_h$ are the concentrations of electrons and holes, $e$ is the elementary charge, and $\mu_e$ and $\mu_h$ are the mobilities of electrons and holes.
- Assuming that the mobilities of electrons and holes are constant and independent of temperature, the electrical conductivity of a semiconductor can be written as: $$\sigma = A e^{-\frac{E_g}{2k_BT}}$$ where $A$ is a constant that depends on the material properties and the doping level of the semiconductor.
- Taking the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation, we get: $$\ln \sigma = \ln A - \frac{E_g}{2k_B} \frac{1}{T}$$
- This equation shows that the plot of $\ln \sigma$ versus $\frac{1}{T}$ is a straight line with a negative slope of $\frac{E_g}{2k_B}$ and an intercept of $\ln A$.
- By measuring the electrical conductivity of a semiconductor at different temperatures and plotting the graph of $\ln \sigma$ versus $\frac{1}{T}$, we can determine the energy band gap of the semiconductor by finding the slope of the line and multiplying it by $2k_B$.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor can also be determined by measuring the absorption coefficient of the semiconductor at different wavelengths of light and plotting the graph of $(\alpha h \nu)^2$ versus $h \nu$, where $\alpha$ is the absorption coefficient, $h$ is the Planck constant, and $\nu$ is the frequency of light. The energy band gap of the semiconductor is equal to the value of $h \nu$ at which the graph intersects the $h \nu$ axis. This method is based on the fact that the absorption coefficient of a semiconductor increases sharply when the photon energy exceeds the energy band gap.
