#### Group A

- Group A is a term used to classify some elements in the periodic table that have similar properties and valence electrons.
- Group A elements are also called the main group elements or the representative elements.
- Group A elements include the alkali metals (group 1A), the alkaline earth metals (group 2A), the boron group (group 3A), the carbon group (group 4A), the nitrogen group (group 5A), the oxygen group (group 6A), the halogens (group 7A), and the noble gases (group 8A).
- Group A elements tend to form ions by losing or gaining electrons to achieve a stable octet configuration. For example, sodium (Na) in group 1A loses one electron to form Na+ ion, while chlorine (Cl) in group 7A gains one electron to form Cl- ion.
- Group A elements show trends in their physical and chemical properties across the periods and down the groups. For example, the atomic radius of group A elements increases down the group and decreases across the period, while the ionization energy of group A elements decreases down the group and increases across the period.
