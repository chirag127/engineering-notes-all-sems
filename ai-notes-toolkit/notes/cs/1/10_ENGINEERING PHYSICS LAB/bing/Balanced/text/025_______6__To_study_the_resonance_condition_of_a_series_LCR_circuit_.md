##### 6. To study the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit.

- A series LCR circuit consists of an inductor (L), a capacitor (C) and a resistor (R) connected in series to an alternating voltage source (V).
- The current (I) in the circuit is the same for all the components, but the voltages across them may differ in magnitude and phase.
- The total impedance (Z) of the circuit is given by Z = R + j(XL - XC), where XL = ωL is the inductive reactance, XC = 1/ωC is the capacitive reactance, and j is the imaginary unit.
- The phase difference (φ) between the current and the voltage is given by tan φ = (XL - XC)/R.
- The resonance condition occurs when the inductive reactance equals the capacitive reactance, i.e., XL = XC, or ω = 1/√LC. This is called the resonant frequency (f0) of the circuit.
- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is purely resistive, i.e., Z = R, and the phase difference is zero, i.e., φ = 0. The current in the circuit is maximum and equal to I0 = V/R.
- The resonance condition can be verified by measuring the current in the circuit as a function of the frequency of the voltage source, and finding the frequency at which the current is maximum. Alternatively, the resonance condition can be verified by measuring the voltage across the capacitor or the inductor as a function of the frequency, and finding the frequency at which the voltage is minimum. This is because the voltage across the capacitor or the inductor is 180° out of phase with the current, and hence is minimum when the current is maximum.
