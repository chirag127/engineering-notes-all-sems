# Focal Length of Combination of Lenses

The formula for the focal length of a combination of lenses is given by the formula:

1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/f1f2

Where:
- f is the focal length of the combination of lenses
- f1 is the focal length of the first lens
- f2 is the focal length of the second lens
- d is the distance between the two lenses

This formula can be derived using the thin lens formula and the principle of superposition. The thin lens formula states that for a thin lens, the distance of the object, image, and focal length are related by the equation:

1/f = 1/u + 1/v

Where:
- f is the focal length of the lens
- u is the distance of the object from the lens
- v is the distance of the image from the lens

When two lenses are placed close to each other, the image formed by the first lens acts as the object for the second lens. Using the principle of superposition, the total power of the combination of lenses is the sum of the powers of the individual lenses. The power of a lens is given by the reciprocal of its focal length. Therefore, the total power of the combination of lenses is given by:

P = P1 + P2 = 1/f1 + 1/f2

Substituting the value of P in the thin lens formula for the combination of lenses, we get:

1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/f1f2

This is the formula for the focal length of a combination of lenses. It can be used to calculate the focal length of a combination of two thin lenses placed close to each other. The distance between the lenses should be small compared to the focal lengths of the lenses for the formula to be valid.
