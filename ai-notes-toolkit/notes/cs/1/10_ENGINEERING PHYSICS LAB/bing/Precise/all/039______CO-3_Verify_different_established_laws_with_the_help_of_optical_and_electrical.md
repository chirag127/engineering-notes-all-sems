#### CO-3 Verify different established laws with the help of optical and electrical

There are several established laws in the field of optics and electricity that can be verified through experiments and observations. Some of these laws include:

1. **Ohm's Law**: This law states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage applied across it, provided the temperature remains constant. This law can be verified by measuring the current flowing through a conductor at different voltage levels and plotting a graph of voltage against current.

2. **Snell's Law**: This law describes the relationship between the angles of incidence and refraction of light when it passes from one medium to another. This law can be verified by measuring the angles of incidence and refraction of a light beam as it passes through different media and comparing the results with the predictions of Snell's Law.

3. **Kirchhoff's Laws**: These laws describe the conservation of charge and energy in electrical circuits. Kirchhoff's Current Law states that the sum of the currents entering a junction in a circuit must equal the sum of the currents leaving the junction. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law states that the sum of the voltage drops around any closed loop in a circuit must equal the sum of the voltage rises. These laws can be verified by measuring the currents and voltages in a circuit and checking that they satisfy the conditions of Kirchhoff's Laws.

4. **Lensmaker's Equation**: This equation describes the relationship between the focal length of a lens, the refractive indices of the media on either side of the lens, and the radii of curvature of the lens surfaces. This equation can be verified by measuring the focal length of a lens and comparing it with the value predicted by the Lensmaker's Equation.

These are just a few examples of the many established laws in the field of optics and electricity that can be verified through experiments and observations. By conducting such experiments, we can gain a deeper understanding of the fundamental principles that govern the behavior of light and electricity.
