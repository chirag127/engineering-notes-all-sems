#### CO CO Statement Bloom’s

CO (Course Outcome) and CO (Course Objective) statements are used to describe the expected learning outcomes of a course. These statements are typically written in terms of what the student should be able to do or understand by the end of the course.

Bloom’s Taxonomy is a framework for categorizing educational goals and objectives into different levels of complexity and specificity. It is often used to help develop CO and CO statements by providing a structure for defining and organizing learning outcomes.

The taxonomy consists of six levels, arranged in a hierarchy from lower-order thinking skills to higher-order thinking skills:

1. **Remembering**: recalling information from memory.
2. **Understanding**: comprehending the meaning of information.
3. **Applying**: using information in a new situation.
4. **Analyzing**: breaking down information into parts and understanding their relationships.
5. **Evaluating**: making judgments about the value of information.
6. **Creating**: combining information to form a new whole.

By using Bloom’s Taxonomy to develop CO and CO statements, educators can ensure that their courses are designed to promote higher-order thinking skills and provide a well-rounded learning experience for their students.
