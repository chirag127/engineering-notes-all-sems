##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode during electrolysis by the passage of one coulomb of electric charge. The ECE of copper can be determined by performing an experiment in which a known amount of electric charge is passed through a copper sulfate solution, and the mass of copper deposited on the cathode is measured.

The steps to determine the ECE of copper are as follows:

1. Set up the electrolysis apparatus with a copper sulfate solution and two copper electrodes.
2. Connect the electrodes to a power source and an ammeter to measure the current.
3. Record the initial mass of the cathode.
4. Pass a known amount of electric charge through the solution by running the current for a measured amount of time.
5. Record the final mass of the cathode.
6. Calculate the mass of copper deposited on the cathode by subtracting the initial mass from the final mass.
7. Divide the mass of copper deposited by the amount of electric charge passed to determine the ECE of copper.

The ECE of copper can be used to calculate the amount of copper that will be deposited during electrolysis under different conditions, such as varying the current or the time of electrolysis. It is an important parameter in electroplating and other industrial processes that involve the deposition of copper.
