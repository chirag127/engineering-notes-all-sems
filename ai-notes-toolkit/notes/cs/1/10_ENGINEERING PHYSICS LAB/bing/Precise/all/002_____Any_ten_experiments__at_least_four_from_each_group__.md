### Any ten experiments (at least four from each group)

Here are ten experiments, with at least four from each group:

#### Group 1: Physics
1. **The Double-Slit Experiment**: This experiment demonstrates the wave-particle duality of light, where light behaves as both a wave and a particle.
2. **The Michelson-Morley Experiment**: This experiment was designed to detect the presence of aether, a hypothetical medium for the propagation of light. The results showed that aether did not exist.
3. **The Millikan Oil Drop Experiment**: This experiment measured the charge of an electron by balancing the gravitational and electric forces on tiny charged droplets of oil.
4. **The Rutherford Gold Foil Experiment**: This experiment showed that atoms have a small, dense, positively charged nucleus, surrounded by a cloud of negatively charged electrons.

#### Group 2: Chemistry
1. **The Iodine Clock Reaction**: This experiment demonstrates the concept of reaction rates and how they can be affected by factors such as concentration and temperature.
2. **The Briggs-Rauscher Oscillating Reaction**: This experiment is a visually striking example of a chemical oscillator, where the reaction mixture changes color periodically.
3. **The Belousov-Zhabotinsky Reaction**: This is another example of a chemical oscillator, where the reaction mixture exhibits spatiotemporal patterns.
4. **The Thermite Reaction**: This experiment demonstrates an exothermic redox reaction, where iron oxide reacts with aluminum to produce iron and aluminum oxide.

#### Group 3: Biology
1. **The Hershey-Chase Experiment**: This experiment showed that DNA, not protein, is the genetic material of phages, viruses that infect bacteria.
2. **The Meselson-Stahl Experiment**: This experiment demonstrated that DNA replication is semi-conservative, where each strand of the original DNA molecule serves as a template for the synthesis of a new strand.
