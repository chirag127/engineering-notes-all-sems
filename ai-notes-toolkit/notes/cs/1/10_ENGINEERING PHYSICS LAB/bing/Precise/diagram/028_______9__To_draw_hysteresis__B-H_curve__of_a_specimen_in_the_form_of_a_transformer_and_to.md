##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

Hysteresis is the dependence of the state of a system on its history. In the context of magnetic materials, hysteresis refers to the lag between the magnetic field B and the magnetizing force H. The B-H curve, also known as the hysteresis loop, is a graphical representation of this phenomenon.

To draw the B-H curve of a specimen in the form of a transformer, the following steps can be followed:

1. Set up the transformer with the specimen as the core, and connect it to an AC power source.
2. Connect an ammeter in series with the primary coil to measure the current, and a voltmeter across the secondary coil to measure the induced voltage.
3. Gradually increase the current in the primary coil and record the corresponding values of the induced voltage in the secondary coil.
4. Plot the induced voltage (B) against the current (H) to obtain the B-H curve.

The shape of the B-H curve depends on the magnetic properties of the specimen. Materials with a large hysteresis loop, such as ferromagnetic materials, exhibit a large amount of energy loss when subjected to alternating magnetic fields. This energy loss is known as hysteresis loss and is an important factor to consider in the design of transformers and other magnetic devices.
