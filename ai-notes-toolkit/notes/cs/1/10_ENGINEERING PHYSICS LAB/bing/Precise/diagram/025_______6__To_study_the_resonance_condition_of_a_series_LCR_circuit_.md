##### 6. To study the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit.

A series LCR circuit consists of an inductor (L), a capacitor (C), and a resistor (R) connected in series. The resonance condition of a series LCR circuit occurs when the inductive reactance (XL) is equal to the capacitive reactance (XC). At resonance, the circuit behaves as a purely resistive circuit, and the current in the circuit is maximum.

The resonance condition can be mathematically expressed as:

XL = XC

where XL = 2πfL and XC = 1 / (2πfC)

Substituting the values of XL and XC in the above equation, we get:

2πfL = 1 / (2πfC)

Solving for f, we get the resonant frequency (fr) as:

fr = 1 / (2π√(LC))

At resonance, the impedance (Z) of the circuit is equal to the resistance (R) of the circuit, and the current (I) in the circuit is given by:

I = V / Z = V / R

where V is the applied voltage.

The resonance condition of a series LCR circuit can be studied by varying the frequency of the applied voltage and measuring the current in the circuit. At resonance, the current in the circuit will be maximum. The resonant frequency can be calculated using the above equation and compared with the experimentally obtained value.

In summary, the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit occurs when the inductive reactance is equal to the capacitive reactance, resulting in a purely resistive circuit with maximum current. The resonant frequency can be calculated using the equation fr = 1 / (2π√(LC)), and the resonance condition can be studied experimentally by varying the frequency of the applied voltage and measuring the current in the circuit.
