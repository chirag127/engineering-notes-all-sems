##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- Spectral lines are produced when an element is heated and its electrons are excited to higher energy levels.
- When the electrons return to their ground state, they emit light at specific wavelengths, producing a characteristic spectrum for each element.
- Mercury is a commonly used element in spectroscopy due to its well-defined and easily observable spectral lines.
- To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light, a plane transmission diffraction grating can be used.
- A diffraction grating is an optical component with a regular pattern of closely spaced lines that diffract light into its component wavelengths.
- When light from a mercury lamp is passed through a diffraction grating, the light is diffracted into its component wavelengths, producing a spectrum.
- By measuring the angles at which the different spectral lines are observed, the wavelengths of the spectral lines can be calculated using the grating equation.
- The grating equation relates the angle of diffraction, the grating spacing, and the wavelength of the diffracted light.
- By using this equation and the measured angles, the wavelengths of the different spectral lines of mercury light can be determined.
