##### 5. To determine resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance

- Resistance is a measure of the opposition to the flow of electric current through a conductor.
- The resistance of a conductor depends on its length, cross-sectional area, and the material it is made of.
- The resistance per unit length is calculated by dividing the total resistance of the conductor by its length.
- The specific resistance, also known as resistivity, is a property of the material the conductor is made of. It is defined as the resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area.
- To determine the resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance, the following steps can be followed:
  1. Measure the length of the conductor using a ruler or measuring tape.
  2. Measure the cross-sectional area of the conductor using a micrometer screw gauge or Vernier calipers.
  3. Connect the conductor in a circuit with a battery, an ammeter, and a voltmeter.
  4. Measure the current flowing through the conductor using the ammeter and the potential difference across the conductor using the voltmeter.
  5. Calculate the resistance of the conductor using Ohm's law, which states that the resistance is equal to the potential difference divided by the current.
  6. Divide the resistance of the conductor by its length to obtain the resistance per unit length.
  7. Divide the resistance of the conductor by its cross-sectional area to obtain the specific resistance or resistivity of the material it is made of.
