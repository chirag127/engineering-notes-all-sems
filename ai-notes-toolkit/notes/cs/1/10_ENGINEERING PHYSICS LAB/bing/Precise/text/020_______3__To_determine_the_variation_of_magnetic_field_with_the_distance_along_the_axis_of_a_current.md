##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current

The magnetic field produced by a current-carrying wire can be determined using the Biot-Savart law. This law states that the magnetic field at a point due to a current element is directly proportional to the current, the length of the current element, and the sine of the angle between the current element and the line joining the point to the current element, and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between the point and the current element.

To determine the variation of the magnetic field with distance along the axis of a current-carrying wire, the following steps can be followed:

1. Set up the apparatus, including a current-carrying wire, a magnetic field sensor, and a measuring device to measure the distance from the wire.
2. Turn on the current and measure the magnetic field at various distances along the axis of the wire.
3. Record the data and plot a graph of the magnetic field strength against the distance from the wire.
4. Analyze the graph to determine the relationship between the magnetic field strength and the distance from the wire.

The relationship between the magnetic field strength and the distance from the wire can be determined by fitting a curve to the data points. The shape of the curve will depend on the specific experimental setup and the strength of the current in the wire. In general, the magnetic field strength is expected to decrease as the distance from the wire increases. This relationship can be used to predict the magnetic field strength at other distances along the axis of the wire.
