##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

1. The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode during electrolysis by the passage of one coulomb of electric charge.
2. The ECE of copper can be determined experimentally by performing an electrolysis experiment using a copper sulfate solution and copper electrodes.
3. The mass of the copper electrode is measured before and after the experiment to determine the change in mass.
4. The electric charge passed through the solution during the experiment is measured using an ammeter and a stopwatch.
5. The ECE of copper is calculated by dividing the change in mass of the copper electrode by the electric charge passed through the solution.
6. The ECE of copper can also be calculated theoretically using Faraday's laws of electrolysis and the atomic weight of copper.
7. The experimental and theoretical values of the ECE of copper can be compared to evaluate the accuracy of the experiment.
