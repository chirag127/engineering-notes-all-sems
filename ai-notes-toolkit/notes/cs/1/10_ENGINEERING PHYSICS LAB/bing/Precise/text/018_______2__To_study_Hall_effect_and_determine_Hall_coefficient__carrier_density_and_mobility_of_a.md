##### 2. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a

- The **Hall effect** is defined as the difference in voltage generated across a current-carrying conductor, is transverse to an electrical current in the conductor and an applied magnetic field perpendicular to the current.
- The drift velocity acquired in unit applied electric field is known as the **mobility** of the carrier and is denoted by µH and is also called **Hall mobility**.
- By measuring the resistivity of the materials and knowing the Hall coefficient, density along y-axis and current density along x-axis, we can determine the **Hall angle**.
- The **Hall coefficient** (RH) and **mobility** (µH) are independent of magnetic and electric fields, however they changes with film thickness and composition of the film.
- The **carrier concentration** (nH) depends on thickness and composition of the films.
- The **Hall measurement**, carried out in the presence of a magnetic field, yields the sheet carrier density ns and the bulk carrier density n or p (for n -type or p -type material) if the conducting layer thickness of the sample is known.
- The **Hall voltage** for thick, heavily doped samples can be quite small (of the order of microvolts).
