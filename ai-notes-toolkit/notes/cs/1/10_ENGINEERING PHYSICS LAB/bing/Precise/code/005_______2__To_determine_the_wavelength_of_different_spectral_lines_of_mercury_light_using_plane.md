##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- Spectral lines are produced when an element is heated and its electrons are excited to higher energy levels.
- When the electrons return to their ground state, they emit light at specific wavelengths, producing spectral lines.
- The wavelengths of these spectral lines are characteristic of the element and can be used to identify it.
- In this experiment, a mercury lamp is used as the light source.
- The light from the mercury lamp is passed through a diffraction grating, which separates the light into its component wavelengths.
- The diffraction grating produces a series of bright lines, known as the spectrum, on a screen.
- The position of each line in the spectrum is measured and used to calculate the wavelength of the corresponding spectral line.
- This experiment can be used to determine the wavelengths of the different spectral lines of mercury light.
