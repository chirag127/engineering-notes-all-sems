#### CO-3 Verify different established laws with the help of optical and electrical

Optical and electrical experiments can be used to verify different established laws in physics. Some of these laws include:

1. **Ohm's Law**: This law states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage applied across it, provided the temperature remains constant. This law can be verified using a simple circuit consisting of a battery, a resistor, an ammeter, and a voltmeter.

2. **Snell's Law**: This law describes the relationship between the angles of incidence and refraction when light passes through the interface between two different media. This law can be verified using a simple experiment involving a light source, a transparent block of material with known refractive index, and a protractor.

3. **Kirchhoff's Laws**: These laws describe the conservation of charge and energy in electrical circuits. Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) states that the algebraic sum of currents entering a node is zero, while Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) states that the algebraic sum of voltages around a closed loop is zero. These laws can be verified using a simple circuit consisting of a battery, resistors, and a multimeter.

4. **The Law of Reflection**: This law states that the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection when light is reflected off a smooth surface. This law can be verified using a simple experiment involving a light source, a flat mirror, and a protractor.

These are just a few examples of the many established laws that can be verified using optical and electrical experiments. By conducting these experiments, students can gain a deeper understanding of the fundamental principles of physics.
