##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

Fresnel's bi-prism is an optical instrument used to determine the wavelength of light. It consists of two prisms placed base to base, forming a bi-prism. When a beam of light is passed through the bi-prism, it is split into two beams, which interfere with each other to produce an interference pattern.

To determine the wavelength of sodium light using Fresnel's bi-prism, the following steps can be followed:

1. Set up the bi-prism experiment by placing the bi-prism, a sodium lamp, and a screen in a straight line.
2. Adjust the position of the bi-prism and the screen to obtain a clear interference pattern on the screen.
3. Measure the distance between the bi-prism and the screen, as well as the distance between the central bright fringe and a fringe of known order.
4. Use the formula for the fringe width to calculate the wavelength of the sodium light.

The formula for the fringe width is given by:

`w = λD/d`

where `w` is the fringe width, `λ` is the wavelength of the light, `D` is the distance between the bi-prism and the screen, and `d` is the distance between the virtual sources.

By measuring the fringe width and the distance between the bi-prism and the screen, the wavelength of the sodium light can be calculated. This method is a simple and accurate way to determine the wavelength of light.
