##### 1. To determine the wavelength of sodium light by Newton’s ring experiment.

- Newton's ring experiment is a method of measuring the wavelength of light using the phenomenon of interference.
- The setup consists of a plano-convex lens of large radius of curvature placed on a plane glass plate. A monochromatic light source, such as a sodium lamp, is used to illuminate the lens from above. A viewing telescope is used to observe the interference pattern formed by the light reflected from the upper and lower surfaces of the air film between the lens and the plate.
- The interference pattern consists of concentric bright and dark rings, called Newton's rings, centered at the point of contact of the lens and the plate. The radius of the rings depends on the thickness of the air film, which varies with the distance from the center.
- The wavelength of the light can be determined by measuring the radius of the rings and applying the following formula:

where m is the order of the ring, λ is the wavelength of the light, R is the radius of curvature of the lens, and r_m is the radius of the mth ring.

- To measure the radius of the rings, the telescope is focused on the rings and a micrometer eyepiece is used to measure the diameter of a few rings. The radius of the rings can be calculated by dividing the diameter by 2.
- To measure the radius of curvature of the lens, the lens is removed from the plate and the telescope is focused on the image of a distant object formed by the lens. The distance between the lens and the telescope is then measured, which is equal to the focal length of the lens. The radius of curvature of the lens can be calculated by multiplying the focal length by 2.
- The wavelength of the light can be calculated by substituting the values of m, R, and r_m in the formula and solving for λ. The average value of λ can be obtained by taking the mean of the values obtained for different rings.
