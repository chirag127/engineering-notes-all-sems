#### Group A

- Group A is a category of blood types that have the A antigen on the surface of red blood cells and the anti-B antibody in the plasma.
- The A antigen is a protein that helps the immune system recognize the blood cells as self or non-self.

- Group A can be further divided into two subgroups: A1 and A2, based on the amount of A antigen expressed on the red blood cells. A1 has more A antigen than A2.
- Group A can also have the Rh factor, which is another antigen that can be positive or negative. A positive Rh factor means that the blood cells have the Rh antigen, while a negative Rh factor means that they do not.

- Group A is the second most common blood type in the world, after group O. It is more prevalent in Europe, North America, and Australia than in Asia and Africa.
- Group A is associated with certain health risks and benefits, such as a higher risk of gastric cancer, a lower risk of ovarian cancer, and a higher susceptibility to malaria and cholera.
