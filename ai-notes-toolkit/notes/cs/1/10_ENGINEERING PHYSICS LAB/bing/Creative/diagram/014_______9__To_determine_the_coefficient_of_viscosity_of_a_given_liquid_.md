# 9. To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid.

## Aim:

To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid by measuring the terminal velocity of a small spherical body falling through the liquid.

## Theory:

The coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is a measure of its resistance to flow. It is defined as the ratio of the shear stress to the rate of shear strain in the liquid.

When a small spherical body of radius r and density ρ falls through a liquid of density σ and viscosity η, it experiences three forces:

- The weight of the body, W = mg, acting downwards, where m is the mass of the body and g is the acceleration due to gravity.
- The upthrust or buoyant force, U = Vσg, acting upwards, where V is the volume of the body.
- The viscous drag force, F = 6πηrv, acting opposite to the direction of motion, where v is the velocity of the body.

At terminal velocity, the net force on the body is zero, i.e., W - U - F = 0. Therefore,

mg - Vσg - 6πηrv = 0

or

v = (2/9)(r^2)g(ρ - σ)/η

The coefficient of viscosity of the liquid can be calculated by rearranging the above equation as:

η = (2/9)(r^2)g(ρ - σ)/v

## Apparatus:

- A glass tube of uniform cross-section and about 1 m long, filled with the given liquid.
- A stand and clamp to hold the tube vertically.
- A stopwatch to measure the time of fall of the spherical body.
- A meter scale to measure the length of the tube and the diameter of the spherical body.
- A balance to measure the mass of the spherical body.
- A thermometer to measure the temperature of the liquid.
- A set of small spherical bodies of different sizes and densities, such as steel, glass, or plastic balls.

## Procedure:

- Select a spherical body of suitable size and density, such that it falls slowly through the liquid and does not touch the walls of the tube.
- Measure the mass and diameter of the spherical body using the balance and the meter scale, and calculate its radius and density.
- Fill the glass tube with the given liquid up to a certain mark, and clamp it vertically on the stand.
- Measure the temperature of the liquid using the thermometer, and note the density of the liquid from a standard table.
- Drop the spherical body gently into the tube from the top, and start the stopwatch as soon as it enters the liquid.
- Observe the motion of the spherical body through the liquid, and stop the stopwatch when it reaches the bottom of the tube.
- Note the time of fall and the length of the tube, and calculate the terminal velocity of the spherical body.
- Repeat the experiment with the same spherical body at least three times, and take the average value of the terminal velocity.
- Repeat the experiment with different spherical bodies of different sizes and densities, and calculate their terminal velocities.
- Calculate the coefficient of viscosity of the liquid for each spherical body using the formula:

η = (2/9)(r^2)g(ρ - σ)/v

- Take the average value of the coefficient of viscosity of the liquid as the final result.

## Precautions:

- The spherical body should be small enough to fall through the liquid without touching the walls of the tube, and large enough to be visible.
- The spherical body should be clean and dry before dropping it into the liquid, to avoid any air bubbles or impurities.
- The liquid should be free from any dust or dirt, and should be at a constant temperature throughout the experiment.
- The stopwatch should be started and stopped accurately, and the time of fall should be measured with minimum error.
- The experiment should be repeated with different spherical bodies of different sizes and densities, to minimize the systematic errors.
