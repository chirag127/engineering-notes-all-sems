##### Using Carey Foster's Bridge

- A Carey Foster's bridge is a bridge circuit used to measure medium resistances, or to measure small differences between two large resistances   .
- It is a modified form of the Wheatstone bridge, which consists of four arms with four resistances connected in a loop, and a galvanometer and a battery connected across the loop  .
- The main difference between the Wheatstone bridge and the Carey Foster's bridge is that one of the arms of the latter contains a uniform resistance wire with a sliding contact, which can be moved along the wire to balance the bridge  .
- The resistance wire is calibrated by measuring its resistance per unit length using a standard resistance and a known length of the wire .
- The unknown resistance can be determined by comparing it with the calibrated resistance wire, and finding the position of the sliding contact that balances the bridge   .
- The advantage of the Carey Foster's bridge is that it can measure small differences in resistance with high accuracy, as the balancing condition depends on the length of the wire rather than the ratio of the resistances  .
- The applications of the Carey Foster's bridge include measuring the resistivity of materials, verifying Ohm's law, and determining the temperature coefficient of resistance  .
