#### CO-3 Verify different established laws with the help of optical and electrical

- Optical laws are the principles that govern the behavior of light when it interacts with different media, such as reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference, polarization, etc.
- Electrical laws are the rules that describe the relationship between voltage, current, resistance, power, energy, etc. in electric circuits and devices, such as Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws, Joule's law, etc.
- Some of the optical and electrical laws that can be verified with the help of experiments are:

  - **Ohm's law**: It states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends, provided the physical conditions remain constant. It can be verified by measuring the current and voltage across a resistor using an ammeter and a voltmeter, and plotting a graph between them. The slope of the graph should be equal to the resistance of the resistor. 
  - **Kirchhoff's laws**: They are two laws that deal with the conservation of charge and energy in electric circuits. The first law, also known as the junction rule, states that the algebraic sum of currents entering and leaving a node is zero. The second law, also known as the loop rule, states that the algebraic sum of voltages around a closed loop is zero. They can be verified by applying them to a simple circuit with a battery, resistors, and a switch, and measuring the currents and voltages using an ammeter and a voltmeter. The results should satisfy the equations derived from the laws. 
  - **Joule's law**: It states that the heat produced by an electric current in a conductor is proportional to the square of the current, the resistance of the conductor, and the time for which the current flows. It can be verified by measuring the temperature rise of water in a calorimeter that contains a coil of wire connected to a battery and a rheostat. The heat produced by the coil should be equal to the product of the current, resistance, and time, and the specific heat of water. 
  - **Law of reflection**: It states that when a ray of light falls on a smooth surface, the angle of reflection is equal to the angle of incidence, and the incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal to the surface all lie in the same plane. It can be verified by using a plane mirror, a ray box, and a protractor. The angles of incidence and reflection can be measured and compared, and the rays can be traced on a paper to show that they are coplanar. 
  - **Law of refraction**: It states that when a ray of light passes from one medium to another, the angle of refraction is related to the angle of incidence by the ratio of the sines of the angles, which is constant for a given pair of media. This ratio is also known as the refractive index of the second medium with respect to the first. It can be verified by using a glass slab, a ray box, and a protractor. The angles of incidence and refraction can be measured and the ratio of their sines can be calculated. The refractive index of glass can be obtained from a table or by using a formula involving the speed of light in different media. 
  - **Snell's law**: It is another name for the law of refraction, which can also be written as n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2, where n1 and n2 are the refractive indices of the first and second medium, and θ1 and θ2 are the angles of incidence and refraction, respectively. It can be verified by using the same experiment as the law of refraction, and substituting the values of n1, n2, θ1, and θ2 in the equation. The equation should be satisfied for any pair of media and any angle of incidence.
