##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current

- The magnetic field of a current-carrying circular loop is given by the formula:

  `B = (μ₀I)/(2R) * (x/√(x² + R²))`

  where `B` is the magnetic field, `μ₀` is the permeability of free space, `I` is the current, `R` is the radius of the loop, and `x` is the distance from the center of the loop along the axis.

- To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance, we can plot the graph of `B` versus `x` for different values of `I` and `R`.

- The graph will have the following features:

  - The magnetic field is zero at the center of the loop (`x = 0`).
  - The magnetic field increases as `x` increases, reaches a maximum value at `x = R`, and then decreases as `x` increases further.
  - The maximum value of the magnetic field is `Bmax = (μ₀I)/(2R)`.
  - The magnetic field approaches zero as `x` approaches infinity.
  - The magnetic field is proportional to the current `I` and inversely proportional to the radius `R` of the loop.

- To perform the experiment, we need the following apparatus:

  - A circular loop of wire with a variable resistor and a battery to control the current.
  - A magnetic compass or a Hall probe to measure the magnetic field at different points along the axis of the loop.
  - A meter stick to measure the distance from the center of the loop.
  - A graph paper and a pencil to plot the graph.

  - Move the magnetic compass or the Hall probe along the axis of the loop and note the readings of the magnetic field at different distances from the center.
  - Repeat the steps for different values of current and radius of the loop.
  - Plot the graph of magnetic field versus distance for each set of data.
  - Compare the graphs and observe the variation of magnetic field with the distance, current, and radius of the loop.

- The expected results and observations are as follows:

  - The graph of magnetic field versus distance will have the shape of a bell curve, as predicted by the formula.
  - The magnetic field will be zero at the center of the loop and will increase as the distance increases, reach a maximum value at the distance equal to the radius of the loop, and then decrease as the distance increases further.
  - The maximum value of the magnetic field will depend on the current and the radius of the loop. It will be higher for higher current and lower radius, and lower for lower current and higher radius.
  - The magnetic field will approach zero as the distance approaches infinity.

  - The measurements of the distance and the magnetic field may have some errors due to the limitations of the instruments and the human eye.
