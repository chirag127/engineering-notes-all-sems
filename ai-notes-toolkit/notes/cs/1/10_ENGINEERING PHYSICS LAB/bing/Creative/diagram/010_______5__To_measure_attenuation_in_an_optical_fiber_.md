To measure attenuation in an optical fiber, you can follow these steps:

1. Connect a light source, such as a laser or an LED, to one end of the optical fiber. The light source should have a known wavelength and power output.
2. Connect a power meter, such as a photodetector or a spectrometer, to the other end of the optical fiber. The power meter should be able to measure the wavelength and power of the light received from the fiber.
3. Turn on the light source and record the power reading from the power meter. This is the input power, P_in, of the fiber.
4. Cut the optical fiber at a certain length, L, from the light source. You can use a cleaver or a cutter to make a clean cut.
5. Connect the cut end of the fiber to the power meter and record the power reading. This is the output power, P_out, of the fiber.
6. Calculate the attenuation, A, of the fiber using the formula:

A = 10 * log10(P_in / P_out)

The unit of attenuation is decibel (dB).

7. Repeat steps 4 to 6 for different lengths of the fiber and plot the attenuation versus the length. The slope of the plot is the attenuation coefficient, alpha, of the fiber. The unit of attenuation coefficient is dB/m.

8. Compare the attenuation coefficient of the fiber with the theoretical values for different wavelengths and types of fibers. The attenuation coefficient depends on the material, structure, and quality of the fiber, as well as the wavelength of the light. The lower the attenuation coefficient, the better the fiber performance.
