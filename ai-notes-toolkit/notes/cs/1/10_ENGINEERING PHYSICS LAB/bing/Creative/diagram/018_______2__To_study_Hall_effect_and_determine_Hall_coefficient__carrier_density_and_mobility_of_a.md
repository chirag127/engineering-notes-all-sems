##### 2. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a semiconductor

- The Hall effect is the phenomenon of a transverse electric field (Hall voltage) generated in a conductor when a current flows through it in the presence of a perpendicular magnetic field .
- The Hall effect can be used to determine the sign, density and mobility of the charge carriers in a semiconductor .
- The Hall coefficient, R_H, is defined as the ratio of the Hall voltage to the product of the current and the magnetic field: R_H = V_H / (I B).
- The carrier density, n, is the number of charge carriers per unit volume in the semiconductor. It can be obtained from the Hall coefficient as: n = 1 / (R_H e), where e is the elementary charge.
- The mobility, μ, is a measure of how easily the charge carriers can move in the semiconductor under the influence of an electric field. It can be obtained from the Hall coefficient and the resistivity, ρ, of the semiconductor as: μ = R_H / ρ.
- To perform the Hall effect experiment, a thin rectangular sample of a semiconductor is placed between two poles of a magnet, such that the magnetic field is perpendicular to the plane of the sample . A constant current is passed through the sample along its length, and the Hall voltage is measured across its width using a voltmeter. The magnetic field can be varied by changing the current in the electromagnet, and the Hall voltage can be recorded for different values of the magnetic field. The Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility can then be calculated from the slope and intercept of the plot of Hall voltage versus magnetic field.
