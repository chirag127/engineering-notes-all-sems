# Engineering Physics Lab

Engineering physics lab is a practical course that complements the theoretical concepts of engineering physics. It aims to provide students with hands-on experience in performing experiments, analyzing data, and reporting results using modern equipment and techniques. Engineering physics lab covers topics such as optics, electronics, mechanics, thermodynamics, and quantum physics.

Some of the objectives of engineering physics lab are:

- To develop experimental skills and scientific reasoning in students.
- To familiarize students with the use of various instruments and devices for measuring physical quantities and phenomena.
- To enhance students' understanding of the physical principles and laws underlying the experiments.
- To train students to communicate their findings and conclusions effectively in written and oral forms.

Some of the experiments that are typically performed in engineering physics lab are:

- Measurement of errors and uncertainties in physical quantities.
- Verification of Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's laws using electrical circuits.
- Determination of the focal length of a convex lens using a spherometer and a travelling microscope.
- Study of interference and diffraction patterns using a laser and a diffraction grating.
- Measurement of the wavelength of light using a spectrometer and a prism.
- Verification of the laws of reflection and refraction using a plane mirror and a glass slab.
- Determination of the Young's modulus of a metal wire using a Searle's apparatus.
- Study of the resonance phenomenon in a sonometer and a tuning fork.
- Measurement of the specific heat capacity of a solid using a calorimeter.
- Study of the thermoelectric effect using a thermocouple and a potentiometer.
- Measurement of the Planck's constant using a photocell and a voltmeter.
- Study of the photoelectric effect using a photodiode and an oscilloscope.
- Measurement of the e/m ratio of an electron using a cathode ray tube and a magnetic field.
- Study of the Compton effect using a gamma ray source and a scintillation counter.
- Measurement of the half-life of a radioactive substance using a Geiger-Muller tube and a counter.

Engineering physics lab is usually conducted in a well-equipped laboratory with the guidance of an instructor and a lab assistant. Students are expected to follow the safety rules and precautions while handling the apparatus and materials. Students are also required to maintain a lab notebook and write a lab report for each experiment. The lab report should include the aim, theory, procedure, observations, calculations, graphs, results, discussion, and conclusion of the experiment. The lab report should be submitted within the specified deadline and should follow the prescribed format and style. The lab report should also be free of plagiarism and errors.

Engineering physics lab is an essential part of the engineering physics curriculum and helps students to develop their practical skills and scientific attitude. It also prepares students for further studies and research in physics and engineering. Engineering physics lab is a challenging and rewarding course that requires students to apply their knowledge and creativity to solve real-world problems.
