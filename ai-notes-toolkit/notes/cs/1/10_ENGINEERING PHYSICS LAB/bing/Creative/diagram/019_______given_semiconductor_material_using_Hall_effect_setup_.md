The Hall effect is the phenomenon of generating a voltage across a conductor when it is placed in a magnetic field and a current is flowing through it. The voltage is proportional to the product of the current, the magnetic field, and the Hall coefficient of the material. The Hall coefficient depends on the type, number, and mobility of the charge carriers in the material.

To measure the Hall coefficient of a given semiconductor material using a Hall effect setup, the following steps are required:

- Connect the Hall effect setup to a power supply and a digital multimeter. The setup consists of a wire coil, a thick piece of metal, and a battery. The wire coil produces a magnetic field when current flows through it. The metal piece has four terminals: two for the current and two for the voltage. The battery provides the current for the metal piece.
- Place the metal piece inside the wire coil and adjust the current and the magnetic field to the desired values. Note the readings of the current, the magnetic field, and the voltage across the terminals of the metal piece.
- Use the formula V = R_H * I * B / t to calculate the Hall coefficient of the material, where V is the voltage, R_H is the Hall coefficient, I is the current, B is the magnetic field, and t is the thickness of the metal piece.
- Repeat the experiment for different values of current and magnetic field and calculate the average Hall coefficient of the material. Compare the result with the theoretical value and the literature value of the Hall coefficient for the given material.
