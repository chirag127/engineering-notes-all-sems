##### 4. To determine the focal length of the combination of two lenses separated by a distance

- The focal length of a combination of two lenses separated by a distance is given by the formula:

  `1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/f1f2`

  where f is the focal length of the combination, f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the individual lenses, and d is the distance between them.

- To determine the focal length of the combination experimentally, we can use the following steps:

  - Set up a light source, a screen, and a lens holder on an optical bench.
  - Place the first lens on the lens holder and adjust its position until a clear image of the light source is formed on the screen. Measure the distance between the lens and the screen, which is the focal length of the first lens, f1.
  - Place the second lens on the lens holder behind the first lens, and adjust its position until a clear image of the light source is formed on the screen again. Measure the distance between the second lens and the screen, which is the image distance for the second lens, v2.
  - Measure the distance between the two lenses, which is d.
  - Use the lens formula `1/v2 - 1/u2 = 1/f2` to calculate the focal length of the second lens, f2, where u2 is the object distance for the second lens, which is equal to d - f1.
  - Use the formula for the focal length of the combination to calculate f.
  - Repeat the experiment with different values of d and f1, and take the average of f as the final result.
