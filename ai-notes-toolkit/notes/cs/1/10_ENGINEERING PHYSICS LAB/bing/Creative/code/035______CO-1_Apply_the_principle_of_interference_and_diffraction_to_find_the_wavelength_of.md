#### CO-1 Apply the principle of interference and diffraction to find the wavelength of

- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves that results in a new wave pattern. Interference can be constructive (when the waves add up) or destructive (when the waves cancel out).
- Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of waves around obstacles or openings. Diffraction depends on the size of the obstacle or opening relative to the wavelength of the wave. The smaller the obstacle or opening, the more the wave diffracts.
- The principle of superposition states that when two or more waves overlap, the resulting wave is the sum of the individual waves at each point.
- To find the wavelength of a wave using interference and diffraction, one can use the following methods:
  - Double-slit experiment: In this experiment, a monochromatic light source is passed through two narrow slits that are close together. The light waves diffract at the slits and interfere on a screen. The interference pattern consists of bright and dark fringes that are equally spaced. The wavelength of the light can be calculated using the formula: `λ = (d sin θ)/n`, where `λ` is the wavelength, `d` is the distance between the slits, `θ` is the angle of the fringe from the central axis, and `n` is the order of the fringe (0 for the central fringe, 1 for the first fringe, and so on).
  - Single-slit experiment: In this experiment, a monochromatic light source is passed through a single narrow slit. The light waves diffract at the slit and interfere on a screen. The interference pattern consists of a central bright fringe and a series of dark and bright fringes on either side. The wavelength of the light can be calculated using the formula: `λ = (a sin θ)/n`, where `λ` is the wavelength, `a` is the width of the slit, `θ` is the angle of the fringe from the central axis, and `n` is the order of the dark fringe (1 for the first dark fringe, 2 for the second dark fringe, and so on).
  - Diffraction grating: In this experiment, a monochromatic light source is passed through a device that has a large number of parallel slits that are very close together. The light waves diffract at the slits and interfere on a screen. The interference pattern consists of a series of bright spots that are equally spaced. The wavelength of the light can be calculated using the formula: `λ = (d sin θ)/n`, where `λ` is the wavelength, `d` is the distance between the slits, `θ` is the angle of the spot from the central axis, and `n` is the order of the spot (0 for the central spot, 1 for the first spot, and so on).
