##### 1. To determine the wavelength of sodium light by Newton’s ring experiment.

- Newton's ring experiment is a method of measuring the wavelength of light using the phenomenon of interference.
- The setup consists of a plano-convex lens of large radius of curvature placed on a plane glass plate. A monochromatic light source, such as a sodium lamp, is used to illuminate the lens from above. A viewing telescope is used to observe the interference pattern formed by the light reflected from the upper and lower surfaces of the air film between the lens and the plate.
- The interference pattern consists of concentric bright and dark rings, called Newton's rings, centered at the point of contact of the lens and the plate. The radius of the nth bright ring is given by the formula:

`r_n = sqrt[(n + 1/2)Rλ]`

where r_n is the radius of the nth bright ring, R is the radius of curvature of the lens, λ is the wavelength of light, and n is the order of the ring.

- To determine the wavelength of sodium light, the following steps are followed:

  - Adjust the telescope to focus on the Newton's rings and measure the diameters of several bright rings using a micrometer eyepiece.
  - Plot a graph of D^2_n versus n, where D_n is the diameter of the nth bright ring. The slope of the graph is given by:

  `S = d(D^2_n)/dn = 4Rλ`

  - Calculate the value of λ using the formula:

  `λ = S/(4R)`

  - Compare the experimental value of λ with the theoretical value of sodium light, which is 589 nm.
