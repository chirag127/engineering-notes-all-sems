##### 10. To determine the value of acceleration due to gravity (g) using compound pendulum.

- A compound pendulum is a rigid body that can oscillate about a horizontal axis passing through it.
- The time period of a compound pendulum depends on its length, mass, and the distance of the axis of oscillation from its center of mass.
- The formula for the time period of a compound pendulum is:

`T = 2π √(I/mgd)`

where T is the time period, I is the moment of inertia of the pendulum about the axis of oscillation, m is the mass of the pendulum, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and d is the distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass.

- To determine the value of g using a compound pendulum, the following steps are followed:

  - A compound pendulum of known mass and length is suspended from a rigid support by a thin wire or a knife edge.
  - The distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass is measured using a meter scale.
  - The pendulum is displaced slightly from its equilibrium position and released. The time taken for a certain number of oscillations is measured using a stopwatch.
  - The time period of one oscillation is calculated by dividing the total time by the number of oscillations.
  - The experiment is repeated for different values of the distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass and the corresponding time periods are recorded.
  - A graph is plotted between the square of the time period (T^2) and the distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass (d).
  - The graph is a straight line passing through the origin. The slope of the graph is given by:

  `slope = 4π^2(I/mg)`

  - The value of g can be calculated by rearranging the formula as:

  `g = 4π^2(I/m*slope)`

  - The value of g obtained from the experiment can be compared with the standard value of g at the location of the experiment. The percentage error can be calculated as:

  `percentage error = |(g - g0)/g0| * 100`

  where g0 is the standard value of g at the location of the experiment.

- The sources of error in the experiment are:

  - The friction at the axis of oscillation may affect the motion of the pendulum.
  - The air resistance may dampen the oscillations of the pendulum.
  - The amplitude of the oscillations may not be small enough to ensure simple harmonic motion.
  - The measurement of the time, length, and mass may have some errors due to the limitations of the instruments.

- The precautions to minimize the errors are:

  - The axis of oscillation should be as smooth and sharp as possible to reduce friction.
  - The pendulum should be oscillated in a vacuum or in a low-pressure chamber to reduce air resistance.
  - The amplitude of the oscillations should be kept as small as possible, preferably less than 10 degrees.
  - The time, length, and mass should be measured with high precision and accuracy using appropriate instruments.
