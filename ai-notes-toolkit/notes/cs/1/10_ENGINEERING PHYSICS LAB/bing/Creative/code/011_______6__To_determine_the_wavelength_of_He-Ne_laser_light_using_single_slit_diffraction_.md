##### 6. To determine the wavelength of He-Ne laser light using single slit diffraction.

- The aim of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of a He-Ne laser light by observing the diffraction pattern produced by a single slit of known width.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which states that every point on a wavefront can be considered as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the wavefront at any later time is the envelope of these wavelets.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are: a He-Ne laser, a single slit of known width, a screen, a meter scale, and a magnifying lens.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Set up the apparatus as shown in the figure below, with the laser, the slit, and the screen aligned on a horizontal line. Adjust the distance between the slit and the screen to obtain a clear diffraction pattern.
  - Use the meter scale to measure the distance D between the slit and the screen, and the magnifying lens to measure the distance d between the central bright fringe and the first dark fringe on either side of the central fringe.
  - Repeat the measurements for different values of D and d, and record the data in a table.
  - Calculate the wavelength of the laser light using the formula: λ = 2dD/a, where a is the width of the slit, and λ is the wavelength of the laser light.
  - Find the average value of λ and the percentage error from the theoretical value of 632.8 nm.
