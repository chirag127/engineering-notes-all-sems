#### CO-2 Compute and analyze various electrical and electronic properties of a given

- Electrical and electronic properties are the characteristics of a material or a device that affect its behavior when an electric current or a voltage is applied to it.
- Some of the common electrical and electronic properties are resistance, capacitance, inductance, impedance, reactance, admittance, conductance, susceptance, power, energy, efficiency, and frequency response.
- To compute and analyze these properties, we need to use some mathematical tools and techniques, such as Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws, Thevenin's theorem, Norton's theorem, superposition principle, mesh analysis, nodal analysis, phasor analysis, complex numbers, and Laplace transform.
- Here are some examples of how to compute and analyze these properties for a given circuit or device:

  - Resistance: Resistance is the measure of how much a material or a device opposes the flow of electric current. It is calculated by dividing the voltage across the material or device by the current through it. The unit of resistance is ohm (Ω). For example, if a resistor has a voltage of 10 V and a current of 2 A, its resistance is 10/2 = 5 Ω.
  - Capacitance: Capacitance is the measure of how much electric charge a material or a device can store when a voltage is applied to it. It is calculated by dividing the charge on the material or device by the voltage across it. The unit of capacitance is farad (F). For example, if a capacitor has a charge of 0.01 C and a voltage of 5 V, its capacitance is 0.01/5 = 0.002 F.
  - Inductance: Inductance is the measure of how much a material or a device can generate an electromotive force (emf) when the current through it changes. It is calculated by dividing the emf induced in the material or device by the rate of change of current through it. The unit of inductance is henry (H). For example, if an inductor has an emf of 0.5 V and the current through it changes by 0.1 A in 0.01 s, its inductance is 0.5/(0.1/0.01) = 0.05 H.
  - Impedance: Impedance is the measure of how much a material or a device opposes the flow of alternating current (AC). It is calculated by dividing the phasor voltage across the material or device by the phasor current through it. The unit of impedance is ohm (Ω). For example, if a circuit has a phasor voltage of 10∠30° V and a phasor current of 2∠-15° A, its impedance is 10∠30°/2∠-15° = 5∠45° Ω.
  - Reactance: Reactance is the measure of how much a material or a device opposes the flow of AC due to its capacitance or inductance. It is calculated by multiplying the angular frequency of the AC by the capacitance or the inductance of the material or device. The unit of reactance is ohm (Ω). For example, if a capacitor has a capacitance of 0.002 F and the AC has a frequency of 50 Hz, its reactance is 2π×50×0.002 = 0.628 Ω.
  - Admittance: Admittance is the measure of how much a material or a device allows the flow of AC. It is calculated by dividing the phasor current through the material or device by the phasor voltage across it. The unit of admittance is siemens (S). For example, if a circuit has a phasor current of 2∠-15° A and a phasor voltage of 10∠30° V, its admittance is 2∠-15°/10∠30° = 0.2∠-45° S.
