##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

- Explain the concept of hysteresis and its significance in transformers.
- Describe the experimental setup and procedure to obtain the B-H curve.
- Analyze the shape and area of the B-H curve and relate them to the properties of the core material.

**Hysteresis and its significance in transformers**

- Hysteresis is the phenomenon of lagging of magnetic flux density (B) behind the magnetic field intensity (H) in a magnetic material.
- Hysteresis causes a loop to form when B is plotted against H, as shown in the figure below. The loop is called the B-H curve or the hysteresis loop.
- Hysteresis is significant in transformers because it causes energy loss in the core due to the repeated reversal of magnetization. The energy loss is proportional to the area of the hysteresis loop .
- Hysteresis loss can be reduced by using soft magnetic materials that have a thin hysteresis loop, such as silicon steel or ferrite.

**Experimental setup and procedure to obtain the B-H curve**

- The experimental setup consists of a transformer with a primary winding and a secondary winding, a variable AC voltage source, an ammeter, a voltmeter, and an oscilloscope .
- The primary winding is connected to the variable AC voltage source and the ammeter, while the secondary winding is connected to the voltmeter and the oscilloscope.
- The oscilloscope is set to display the voltage across the secondary winding (V) on the horizontal axis and the current through the primary winding (I) on the vertical axis.
- The variable AC voltage source is adjusted to vary the input voltage and current, which in turn vary the magnetic field intensity (H) and the magnetic flux density (B) in the core.
- The oscilloscope traces the B-H curve as the input voltage and current are changed. The B-H curve can be obtained by multiplying the voltage by the number of turns in the secondary winding and dividing the current by the number of turns in the primary winding.
- The B-H curve can be recorded and analyzed using the oscilloscope or a computer software.

**Analysis of the shape and area of the B-H curve**

- The shape of the B-H curve depends on the type of core material used in the transformer. Hard magnetic materials have a wide hysteresis loop, while soft magnetic materials have a thin hysteresis loop.
- The area of the B-H curve represents the energy loss per cycle due to hysteresis in the core. The larger the area, the higher the hysteresis loss .
- The area of the B-H curve can be calculated by integrating the product of B and H over one cycle, or by using graphical methods such as the planimeter or the Simpson's rule.
- The area of the B-H curve can be minimized by choosing a suitable core material, frequency, and flux density for the transformer.
