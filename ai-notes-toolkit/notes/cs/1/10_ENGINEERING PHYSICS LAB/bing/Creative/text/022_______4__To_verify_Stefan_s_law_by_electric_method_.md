##### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

- Stefan’s law states that the total radiant heat energy emitted per second per unit area of a black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
- Mathematically, Stefan’s law can be written as E = σT^4, where E is the emissive power, T is the absolute temperature, and σ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.
- To verify Stefan’s law by electric method, we need the following apparatus: a Leslie’s cube, a thermometer, a voltmeter, an ammeter, a rheostat, a battery, a key, and connecting wires.
- A Leslie’s cube is a hollow metal cube with four different surfaces: blackened, polished, white, and rough. It has a small hole at the top to fill it with hot water and a thermometer to measure its temperature.
- The experimental setup is as follows: the Leslie’s cube is filled with hot water and placed on a stand. The voltmeter and the ammeter are connected in parallel and in series with the battery, the rheostat, and the key, respectively. The terminals of the voltmeter are also connected to two thin wires, which are fixed on the opposite faces of the cube.
- The procedure is as follows: the key is closed and the rheostat is adjusted to get a constant current in the circuit. The voltmeter reading gives the potential difference between the two faces of the cube, which is proportional to the heat radiated by them. The thermometer reading gives the temperature of the water in the cube, which is assumed to be equal to the temperature of the faces. The experiment is repeated for different faces of the cube and for different temperatures of the water.
- The observations are recorded in a table as follows:

| Face of the cube | Temperature (T) in K | Potential difference (V) in V | Emissive power (E) in W/m^2 |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Blackened        |                      |                               |                              |
| Polished         |                      |                               |                              |
| White            |                      |                               |                              |

- The emissive power is calculated by using the formula E = V^2/R, where R is the resistance of the wires, which is assumed to be constant.
- The graph is plotted between E and T^4 for different faces of the cube. The slope of the graph gives the value of σ for each face. The value of σ for the blackened face is the highest, and it is close to the theoretical value of 5.67 x 10^-8 W/m^2 K^4. The value of σ for the other faces is lower, and it decreases with the decrease in the absorptivity of the surface.
- The result is that Stefan’s law is verified by electric method, and it is found that the emissive power of a black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
