##### Using Carey Foster's Bridge

- Carey Foster's bridge is a bridge circuit used to measure medium resistances, or to measure small differences between two large resistances.
- It is a variant of the Wheatstone bridge, which consists of four arms with four resistances connected in a loop, and a galvanometer in between two junctions .
- The main difference between the Wheatstone bridge and the Carey Foster's bridge is that one of the arms of the latter contains a uniform resistance wire with a sliding contact, which can be moved along the wire to balance the bridge .
- The resistance wire is calibrated by using a standard resistance of known value, and the resistance per unit length of the wire is determined .
- The unknown resistance can then be measured by placing it in another arm of the bridge, and adjusting the sliding contact until the galvanometer shows zero deflection .
- The unknown resistance is equal to the product of the resistance per unit length of the wire and the length of the wire between the sliding contact and the end of the wire .
- The advantages of using Carey Foster's bridge are that it can measure resistances with high accuracy and sensitivity, and it can eliminate the errors due to contact resistance and lead resistance .
- The applications of using Carey Foster's bridge are that it can be used to measure the resistivity of materials, the temperature coefficient of resistance, and the specific resistance of electrolytes .
