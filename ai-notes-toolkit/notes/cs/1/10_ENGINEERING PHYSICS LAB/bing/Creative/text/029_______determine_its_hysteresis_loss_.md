##### Hysteresis loss
- Hysteresis loss is the energy dissipated as heat in a magnetic material when it is subjected to a changing magnetic field.
- Hysteresis loss occurs because the magnetic domains in the material have to overcome a certain resistance to change their orientation and alignment with the external field.
- Hysteresis loss depends on the shape and size of the hysteresis loop of the material, which is a graphical representation of the relationship between the magnetic flux density and the magnetic field intensity.
- To determine the hysteresis loss, one can use the following formula:

$$
W_h = \oint B \cdot dH \, A \, f
$$

where $W_h$ is the hysteresis loss per cycle, $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $H$ is the magnetic field intensity, $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the material, and $f$ is the frequency of the alternating magnetic field.

- Alternatively, one can use the Steinmetz equation, which is an empirical formula that relates the hysteresis loss to the maximum flux density and the frequency:

$$
W_h = \eta B_{\text{max}}^n f
$$

where $\eta$ and $n$ are constants that depend on the material and its magnetic properties, and $B_{\text{max}}$ is the maximum flux density.
