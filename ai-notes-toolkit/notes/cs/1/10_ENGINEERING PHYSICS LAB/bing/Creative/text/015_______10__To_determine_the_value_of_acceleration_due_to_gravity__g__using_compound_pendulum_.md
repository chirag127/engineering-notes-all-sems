##### 10. To determine the value of acceleration due to gravity (g) using compound pendulum.

- A compound pendulum is a rigid body that can oscillate about a horizontal axis passing through it.
- The time period of a compound pendulum depends on its length, mass, and the distance of the axis of oscillation from its center of mass.
- The formula for the time period of a compound pendulum is:

    T = 2π √(I/mgd)

    where T is the time period, I is the moment of inertia of the pendulum about the axis of oscillation, m is the mass of the pendulum, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and d is the distance of the axis of oscillation from the center of mass.

- To determine the value of g using a compound pendulum, the following steps are followed:

    1. Measure the mass and the dimensions of the pendulum and calculate its moment of inertia using the parallel axis theorem.
    2. Suspend the pendulum from a rigid support and adjust the position of the axis of oscillation such that the pendulum is in equilibrium.
    3. Displace the pendulum slightly from its equilibrium position and release it. Start a stopwatch and count the number of oscillations (n) in a given time interval (t).
    4. Repeat the experiment for different values of the axis of oscillation and record the corresponding values of n and t.
    5. Calculate the time period (T) for each trial using the formula:

        T = t/n

    6. Plot a graph of T^2 versus d and find the slope (m) and the intercept (c) of the best fit line.
    7. Use the relation:

        m = 4π^2 I/mg

        and

        c = 4π^2 d0/mg

        where d0 is the distance of the center of mass from the axis of oscillation.

    8. Solve for g using the values of m, c, I, and d0. The value of g obtained from the graph should be close to the standard value of 9.8 m/s^2.
