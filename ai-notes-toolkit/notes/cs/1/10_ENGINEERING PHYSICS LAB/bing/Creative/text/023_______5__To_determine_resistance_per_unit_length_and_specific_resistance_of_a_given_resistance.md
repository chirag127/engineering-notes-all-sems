##### 5. To determine resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance

- Resistance is the property of a material that opposes the flow of electric current through it. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
- Resistance per unit length is the ratio of resistance to the length of a material. It is measured in ohms per meter (Ω/m).
- Specific resistance or resistivity is the resistance of a material of unit length and unit cross-sectional area. It is measured in ohm-meter (Ωm).
- To determine the resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance, we need the following apparatus:
  - A resistance wire of known length and cross-sectional area
  - A meter bridge or a Wheatstone bridge
  - A galvanometer
  - A battery
  - A jockey
  - A rheostat
  - A key
  - A ruler
  - A screw gauge
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Connect the resistance wire between points A and B of the meter bridge or the Wheatstone bridge.
  - Connect a standard resistance R between points C and D of the bridge.
  - Connect the galvanometer between points B and D of the bridge.
  - Connect the battery, the rheostat and the key in series between points A and C of the bridge.
  - Adjust the rheostat to get a suitable current in the circuit.
  - Slide the jockey along the wire AB and find the point of null deflection of the galvanometer, which indicates a balanced condition of the bridge.
  - Measure the length l of the wire AB from point A to the null point.
  - Measure the length L of the wire AB using the ruler.
  - Measure the diameter d of the wire AB using the screw gauge and calculate the cross-sectional area A of the wire as A = πd^2/4.
  - Calculate the resistance per unit length of the wire as r = Rl/(L-l).
  - Calculate the specific resistance of the wire as ρ = rA.
