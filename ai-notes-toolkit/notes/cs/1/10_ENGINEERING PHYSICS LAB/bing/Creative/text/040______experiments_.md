#### Experiments

- An experiment is a scientific procedure that tests a hypothesis or a prediction by manipulating one or more variables and measuring the outcomes.
- The purpose of an experiment is to establish a causal relationship between the independent variable (the factor that is changed by the experimenter) and the dependent variable (the factor that is measured or observed).
- A well-designed experiment should have the following features:
  - A clear research question or hypothesis that specifies the expected effect of the independent variable on the dependent variable.
  - A control group and an experimental group that are identical in all aspects except for the independent variable.
  - A random assignment of participants or units to the control and experimental groups to minimize confounding factors or biases.
  - A standardized procedure that ensures the same conditions and treatments for all participants or units in each group.
  - A reliable and valid measurement of the dependent variable that reflects the construct of interest.
  - A replication of the experiment to confirm the results and increase the generalizability of the findings.
- The advantages of experiments are that they can provide strong evidence for causality, test the effects of multiple variables, and control for extraneous variables that might affect the results.
- The limitations of experiments are that they can be costly, time-consuming, and difficult to conduct in some settings, they can have ethical issues or practical constraints that limit the manipulation of the independent variable, and they can have low ecological validity or external validity if the experimental conditions do not reflect the real-world situations.
