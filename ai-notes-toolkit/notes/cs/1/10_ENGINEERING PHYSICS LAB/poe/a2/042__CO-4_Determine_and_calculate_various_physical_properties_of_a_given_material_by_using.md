#### CO-4 Determine and calculate various physical properties of a given material by using:

1. Density: Mass/Volume
- Measure mass and volume of the material and calculate ratio of mass to volume to get density.
- Density determines how tightly packed the atoms/molecules are in a material.

2. Melting Point: Temperature at which a solid melts to become a liquid.
- Heat the material and note down the temperature at which it melts. This temperature is its melting point.
- Higher melting point indicates stronger intermolecular forces between particles making it difficult to overcome them and melt the solid.

3. Boiling Point: Temperature at which a liquid boils to become a gas.
- Heat the liquid and note down the temperature at which it starts boiling. This temperature is its boiling point.
- Higher boiling point indicates stronger intermolecular forces between particles making it difficult to overcome them and convert the liquid to gas.

4. Specific Heat: Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a material by 1°C.
- Supply known amount of heat to known mass of material and note rise in temperature. Calculate ratio of heat supplied to mass x temperature rise to get specific heat.
- Specific heat determines how difficult or easy it is to change the temperature of a material. Higher specific heat means more heat is required to change its temperature.
