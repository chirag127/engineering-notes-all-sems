#### 4. To determine the focal length of the combination of two lenses separated by a distance.

When two lenses are placed in close proximity, they can act as a single lens with a combined focal length. This is known as a lens combination. The focal length of a lens combination can be determined using the following steps:

1. Measure the focal length of each individual lens. This can be done by placing the lens in front of a distant object and adjusting the distance between the lens and the object until a sharp image is formed on a screen or piece of paper behind the lens. The distance between the lens and the screen is the focal length of the lens.

2. Calculate the effective focal length of the lens combination using the lens formula:

   - 1/f = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂ - d/f₁f₂
   
   Where f is the effective focal length of the lens combination, f₁ and f₂ are the focal lengths of the two individual lenses, and d is the distance between the two lenses.

3. Measure the distance between the two lenses. This can be done by placing a ruler or measuring tape between the two lenses and recording the distance.

4. Substitute the values of f₁, f₂, and d into the lens formula and solve for f. The resulting value of f is the effective focal length of the lens combination.

5. Verify the result by using the lens combination to form an image of a distant object on a screen or piece of paper. Adjust the distance between the lens combination and the screen until a sharp image is formed. The distance between the lens combination and the screen is the focal length of the combination.

It is important to note that the lens formula assumes that the lenses are thin and that the distance between them is small compared to their focal lengths. If the lenses are thick or the distance between them is large, additional corrections may be necessary to accurately calculate the effective focal length of the combination.
