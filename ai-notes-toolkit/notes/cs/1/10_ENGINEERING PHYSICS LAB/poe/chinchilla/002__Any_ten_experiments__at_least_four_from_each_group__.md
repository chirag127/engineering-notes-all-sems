### Any ten experiments (at least four from each group)

As a student of science, it is essential to have a sound understanding of various experiments that have been conducted in the past. These experiments have played a significant role in shaping our understanding of the world around us. In this article, we will discuss ten experiments, at least four from each group, that every science student should know.

#### Physics Experiments

1. **Young's Double Slit Experiment** - This experiment demonstrated the wave-like nature of light. It involved shining a beam of light through two slits, which produced an interference pattern on a screen. This pattern proved that light behaves like a wave.

2. **Michelson-Morley Experiment** - This experiment was conducted to measure the speed of light. The experiment involved splitting a beam of light and measuring the time it took to travel a set distance. The results showed that the speed of light is constant, regardless of the direction of the beam.

3. **Cavendish Experiment** - This experiment was conducted to measure the gravitational constant. It involved measuring the force of attraction between two masses and using this to calculate the gravitational constant.

4. **Millikan Oil Drop Experiment** - This experiment determined the charge of an electron. It involved suspending charged oil droplets in an electric field and measuring the force on them. The results showed that the charge on each droplet was a multiple of the charge of an electron.

#### Chemistry Experiments

1. **Mendeleev's Periodic Table** - This experiment established the periodic table of elements. It involved arranging elements in order of increasing atomic weight and grouping them according to their chemical properties.

2. **Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment** - This experiment demonstrated the existence of the atomic nucleus. It involved firing alpha particles at a thin gold foil and observing the pattern of deflection on a screen behind the foil. The results showed that most of the alpha particles passed through the foil, but some were deflected, indicating the presence of a small, dense nucleus.

3. **Miller-Urey Experiment** - This experiment simulated the conditions of early Earth to demonstrate how life could have originated. It involved passing an electric current through a mixture of gases that were thought to have been present in the early atmosphere. The result was the formation of amino acids, the building blocks of life.

4. **Haber Process** - This experiment was used to synthesize ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen gases. It involved passing the gases over a catalyst at high pressure and temperature. The process revolutionized the production of fertilizers and played a significant role in the growth of modern agriculture.

#### Biology Experiments

1. **Griffith Experiment** - This experiment demonstrated the concept of bacterial transformation. It involved injecting mice with a mixture of live and heat-killed bacteria. The results showed that the live bacteria had taken up and expressed the genetic material from the dead bacteria.

2. **Watson and Crick's DNA Model** - This experiment proposed the double helix structure of DNA. It involved analyzing data from X-ray crystallography to create a model of the DNA molecule.

3. **Beadle and Tatum's One Gene-One Enzyme Hypothesis** - This experiment proposed that each gene codes for a specific enzyme. It involved exposing bread mold to various mutagens and observing changes in the mold's ability to produce specific enzymes.

4. **Milgram Experiment** - This experiment demonstrated the power of obedience to authority. It involved asking participants to administer electric shocks to a person in another room, who they believed was receiving painful shocks. The results showed that many participants were willing to administer the highest level of shock, even when they believed it was causing harm.

In conclusion, these ten experiments, at least four from each group, have played a critical role in shaping our understanding of the world around us. As a science student, it is essential to have a good understanding of these experiments and their significance.
