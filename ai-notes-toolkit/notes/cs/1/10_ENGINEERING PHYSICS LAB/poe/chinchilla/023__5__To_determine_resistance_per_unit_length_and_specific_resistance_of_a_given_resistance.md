##### Introduction:

In this experiment, we will be determining the resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance wire. This experiment is important as it helps us understand the behavior of electrical conductors and their properties.

##### Materials Required:

- Resistance wire
- Meter scale
- Ammeter
- Voltmeter
- Battery/Power Supply
- Connecting wires
- Resistance box

##### Procedure:

1. First, we need to set up the circuit by connecting the battery/power supply, ammeter and voltmeter in series with the resistance wire. 

2. The resistance wire is connected to the resistance box in parallel with the ammeter and voltmeter.

3. The resistance wire is then stretched between two fixed points and its length is measured using a meter scale.

4. The resistance box is adjusted to get different values of resistance for the resistance wire.

5. The voltage across the wire is measured using the voltmeter and the current flowing through the wire is measured using the ammeter.

6. The experiment is repeated for different values of resistance and the corresponding values of current and voltage are recorded.

##### Calculations:

1. To calculate the resistance per unit length, we use the formula:

   Resistance per unit length = (Resistance x Wire length) / Cross-sectional area

2. To calculate the specific resistance, we use the formula:

   Specific resistance = (Resistance x Cross-sectional area) / Wire length

   where Cross-sectional area = πr^2, r is the radius of the wire.

##### Observations and Results:

1. The values of current, voltage and resistance for different values of resistance are recorded.

2. Using the above formulas, the resistance per unit length and specific resistance are calculated.

3. A graph is plotted between the resistance per unit length and the corresponding values of resistance.

4. The slope of the graph gives the specific resistance of the wire.

##### Precautions:

1. Ensure that the wire is stretched tautly between the fixed points and is not sagging.

2. The connections in the circuit should be tight.

3. The ammeter and voltmeter should be connected in series and should not be interchanged.

4. The resistance box should be handled carefully.

##### Conclusion:

In this experiment, we have successfully determined the resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance wire. This experiment helps us understand the properties of electrical conductors and how they behave under different conditions.
