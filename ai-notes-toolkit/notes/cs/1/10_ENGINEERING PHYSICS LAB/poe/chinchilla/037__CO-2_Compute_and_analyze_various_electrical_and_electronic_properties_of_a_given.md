#### CO-2 Compute and analyze various electrical and electronic properties of a given

In this topic, we will discuss the various electrical and electronic properties of a given material. Here are some important points to keep in mind:

1. Electrical conductivity: It is the measure of a material's ability to conduct an electric current. The SI unit of electrical conductivity is siemens per meter (S/m). Metals are good conductors of electricity due to the presence of free electrons.

2. Resistivity: It is the measure of a material's opposition to the flow of electric current. The SI unit of resistivity is ohm-meter (Ω·m). Insulators have high resistivity while conductors have low resistivity.

3. Dielectric constant: It is the measure of a material's ability to store electrical energy in an electric field. The higher the dielectric constant of a material, the more it can store electrical energy. The SI unit of dielectric constant is farad per meter (F/m).

4. Permittivity: It is the measure of a material's ability to permit the flow of electrical flux through it. The SI unit of permittivity is farad per meter (F/m). It is a measure of the ability of a material to store electric charge.

5. Magnetic permeability: It is the measure of a material's ability to conduct magnetic flux. The SI unit of magnetic permeability is henry per meter (H/m). It is a measure of the ability of a material to conduct magnetic fields.

6. Thermal conductivity: It is the measure of a material's ability to conduct heat. The SI unit of thermal conductivity is watts per meter-kelvin (W/m·K). Metals are good conductors of heat due to the presence of free electrons.

7. Thermal expansion: It is the measure of a material's tendency to expand or contract with changes in temperature. The SI unit of thermal expansion is kelvin^-1. Different materials have different coefficients of thermal expansion.

In conclusion, the study of electrical and electronic properties of materials is important in understanding their behavior in various applications such as electronics, power generation and transmission, and material science.
