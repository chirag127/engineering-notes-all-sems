##### Introduction

Fresnel's bi-prism is a device that can be used to determine the wavelength of light. It consists of a prism that splits a light beam into two parts, which then interfere with each other to create a pattern of bright and dark fringes. By measuring the distance between these fringes, we can calculate the wavelength of the light.

##### Procedure

Here are the steps to determine the wavelength of sodium light using Fresnel's bi-prism:

1. Set up the bi-prism: Place the bi-prism on a stable surface and adjust the position of the light source until the two images of the source are seen through the prism.

2. Adjust the distance: Move the screen until the interference fringes are visible. Adjust the distance between the bi-prism and the screen until the fringes are sharp and clear.

3. Measure the fringe width: Measure the distance between two adjacent bright fringes using a ruler or a micrometer. Record this distance as 'd'.

4. Calculate the wavelength: Use the formula λ = fd/D, where λ is the wavelength of the light, f is the focal length of the lens, d is the distance between two adjacent fringes, and D is the distance between the bi-prism and the screen.

5. Repeat the experiment: To ensure accuracy, repeat the above steps several times and take the average of the values obtained.

##### Results

Using the above method, we can determine the wavelength of sodium light to be approximately 589 nm. This value is consistent with the accepted value for the wavelength of sodium light, which is 589.3 nm.

##### Conclusion

Fresnel's bi-prism is a useful tool for determining the wavelength of light. By measuring the distance between interference fringes, we can calculate the wavelength of the light source. This experiment can be repeated with light of different wavelengths to determine their values.
