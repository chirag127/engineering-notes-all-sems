### Non-inverting Amplifier

- It is a op-amp circuit configuration which produces an amplified output voltage which is in-phase with the input voltage.
- The output voltage is always greater than or equal to the input voltage.
- The gain of the non-inverting amplifier is determined by the resistor values and is always greater than 1.
- The circuit diagram consists of an op-amp with resistor Rf connected between the output and inverting terminal and resistor R1 connected between non-inverting terminal and ground.
- The input voltage is applied between the non-inverting terminal and ground.
- The gain of the non-inverting amplifier is given by:
Gain = 1 + Rf/R1
- The output voltage is given by:
Output voltage = Input voltage × (1 + Rf/R1)
- The non-inverting amplifier has high input impedance, low output impedance and has a bandwidth similar to the op-amp.
- The main advantages of non-inverting amplifier are:
- The input is applied to the non-inverting terminal so input voltage range can extend to the negative power supply.
- Even order harmonics and DC offsets get cancelled.
- The closed loop gain does not get affected by the variations in the op-amp parameters.
