### Inverting Amplifier

- An inverting amplifier is a circuit that produces an output which is out of phase with the input by 180 degrees.
- The circuit uses an operational amplifier with its inverting input connected to the input voltage source and the non-inverting input connected to ground.
- The output voltage is a scaled and inverted version of the input voltage. The amount of scaling and inversion is determined by the feedback resistor RF and the input resistor RIN.
- The gain of the inverting amplifier is given by: Gain = -RF/RIN
- The negative sign indicates that the output is inverted with respect to the input.
- Inverting amplifiers are used when an inverted but amplified signal is required. They have high input impedance and can provide high voltage gain accuracy.
- The circuit has good common mode rejection and is useful in instrumentation and measurement systems.
