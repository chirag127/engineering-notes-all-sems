### Introduction to Wireless Communication

- Wireless communication is the transfer of information over a distance without the use of electrical conductors or "wires". The distances can be short (a few meters as in television remote control) or long (thousands or millions of kilometers for radio communications).
- Wireless communication is generally considered to be a branch of telecommunications. The electromagnetic waves used in wireless communications include radio waves, microwaves, infrared, and visible light.
- The major components of a wireless communication system are:
 - Transmitter: Converts information into electromagnetic waves
 - Receiver: Converts electromagnetic waves back into information
 - Modulator: Converts information into an electronic signal for transmission
 - Demodulator: Converts a received electronic signal back into information
 - Antenna: Used for the transmission and reception of the radio waves
 - Propagation channel: The medium through which the radio waves travel

Some key characteristics of wireless communication are:
- Limited available frequency spectrum: The radio spectrum is limited, so only a certain number of communication channels are available.
- Interference: Wireless communication may suffer from interference from other devices using the same frequency.
- Multipath fading: The transmitted radio signal may reach the receiver through multiple paths due to reflection, causing fading.
- Limited range: The range of wireless communication is limited by the power of the transmitter and sensitivity of the receiver.
- Security: Wireless communication may be vulnerable to eavesdropping and hacking. Encryption techniques are often used to improve security.
