### Clampers for the notes of the Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

1. A clamper is a circuit that shifts the DC level of a signal to a desired DC level without affecting the AC signal.
2. Clampers are used to remove unwanted portions of the signal, for coupling two stages and for impedance matching.
3. The two basic clamper circuits are:
- Positive clamper: It shifts the zero volt reference of the input AC signal to a positive DC voltage level.
- Negative clamper: It shifts the zero volt reference of the input AC signal to a negative DC voltage level.
4. The diode used in clamper circuits can be either silicon or germanium depending upon the amount of shift required and the peak inverse voltage requirements.
5. Time constants of clamping must be longer than the time period of the input AC signal so that the clamping action can be effective. Capacitors are used for this purpose.
