### Zener Diodes breakdown mechanism (Zener and avalanche)

- A Zener diode is a type of diode that allows current to flow in the reverse direction when the voltage is above a certain value known as the breakdown voltage or Zener voltage.
- The Zener breakdown occurs in a heavily doped p-n junction diode. At a particular reverse voltage, known as the Zener voltage (Vz), the depletion region disappears and the reverse saturation current increases rapidly. This causes the diode to conduct in the reverse direction.
- The avalanche breakdown occurs in a lightly doped p-n junction diode. When the reverse voltage is increased beyond a particular value known as the avalanche voltage (Va), the electrons gain sufficient kinetic energy to free additional electrons by collisions with the semiconductor atoms. This leads to an avalanche of carriers resulting in a rapid increase in the reverse current.
- The Zener breakdown occurs at a lower voltage than the avalanche breakdown. However, both these mechanisms cause the diode to conduct in the reverse direction, and the diode is designed to use either the Zener or avalanche breakdown depending on the doping level and required breakdown voltage.
- The Zener diode is mainly used as a voltage reference and to provide voltage regulation. It is widely used in power supply circuits because it has a predictable and stable reverse breakdown voltage.
