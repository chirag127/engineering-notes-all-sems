### Electromagnetic spectrum

- Electromagnetic spectrum refers to the range of all possible frequencies of electromagnetic radiation.
- It includes radiowaves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.
- These radiations differ from each other in terms of wavelength and frequency. Wavelength decreases and frequency increases as we move from radiowaves to gamma rays.
- Radiowaves have the longest wavelength and lowest frequency. They are used in radio and TV communication.
- Microwaves have shorter wavelength and higher frequency than radiowaves. They are used in microwave ovens and radar systems.
- Infrared has shorter wavelength than microwaves and is used in remote controls and night vision devices.
- Visible light has wavelength range of 400-700 nanometers which is detectable by human eyes.
- Ultraviolet has shorter wavelength than visible light and is used in fluorescent lamps and sterilization.
- X-rays have high frequency and short wavelength and are used in X-ray imaging and crystallography.
- Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength and highest frequency. They are produced in radioactive decay and are used in gamma ray spectroscopy.
