### Voltage-Multiplier Circuits

- Voltage multipliers are circuits that output a DC voltage that is a multiple of the peak value of the AC input voltage.
- They are commonly used to generate high DC voltages from low AC voltages.
- The basic element of a voltage multiplier is the diode-capacitor combination. The diode allows current to flow only during the positive half-cycle of the input, charging the capacitor. During the negative half-cycle, the diode is reverse-biased and the capacitor is isolated, thereby storing the charge.
- By cascading several stages of diode-capacitor combinations, a higher DC output voltage can be obtained. Each stage outputs approximately the peak voltage of the input, and by putting several stages in series, the final output voltage is the sum of the individual stage outputs.
- The two most popular types of voltage multipliers are the half-wave multiplier and the full-wave multiplier:
-- Half-wave multiplier: Consists of diode-capacitor stages with diodes connected in series, with each diode conducting current only during alternate half-cycles. Output voltage is approximately equal to the peak input voltage multiplied by the number of stages.
-- Full-wave multiplier: Consists of diode-capacitor stages with diodes connected to conduct current during both half-cycles of the input. Output voltage is approximately equal to twice the peak input voltage multiplied by the number of stages. The full-wave multiplier is more efficient than the half-wave multiplier.
