### Differential and Common-Mode Operation

- An op amp has two inputs - non-inverting (+) and inverting (-) input terminals.
- The potential difference between these two inputs is called differential input voltage (Vid).
- The average potential of the two inputs with respect to ground is called common-mode voltage (Vic).
- An ideal op amp responds only to the differential input voltage and rejects the common-mode voltage. This is known as common-mode rejection.
- The ability of an op amp to reject common-mode voltages is called common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR). A high CMRR is desirable.
- Practical op amps have some finite CMRR and respond slightly to the common-mode voltage which appears as common-mode signals in the output.
- The output of an op amp depends on the difference between the two input voltages and is independent of the average of the two inputs. This is called differential-mode operation.
- The output of an op amp also contains signals that depend on the average of the two inputs. This is called common-mode operation and is undesirable.
- To get an op amp to function properly in a circuit, it is important to understand both differential and common-mode operation.
