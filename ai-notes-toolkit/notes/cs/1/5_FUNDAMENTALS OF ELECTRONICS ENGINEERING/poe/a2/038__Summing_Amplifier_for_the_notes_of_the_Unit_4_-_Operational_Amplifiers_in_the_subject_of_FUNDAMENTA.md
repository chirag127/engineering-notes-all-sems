### Summing Amplifier

- A summing amplifier adds together multiple input signals and produces an output that is proportional to the sum of the input signals.
- It has multiple input terminals and one output terminal.
- The output voltage is the sum of the input voltages multiplied by the ratio of the relevant input resistor to the feedback resistor.
- The input resistors are identical and the feedback resistor is the sum of all the input resistors. This ensures that the output voltage is directly proportional to the sum of the input signals.
- The advantages of a summing amplifier are:
    - It can add signals of different frequencies.
    - It has high input impedance and isolation between the inputs.
    - The gain is solely determined by the external resistors and is not dependent on the characteristics of the operational amplifier.
- The applications of a summing amplifier include:
    - Mixing signals from multiple sensors
    - Combining the outputs of multiple stages of signal processing
    - Generating a reference voltage which is a weighted sum of multiple voltages
