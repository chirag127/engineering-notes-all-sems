### Photo Diodes

- A photo diode is a PN junction diode that converts light energy into electrical energy.
- When light falls on the PN junction, electron-hole pairs are generated which increase the conductivity of the diode. This results in flow of current through the circuit.
- The amount of current flowing depends on the intensity of light falling on the PN junction. Higher the intensity of light, higher is the current through the circuit.
- Photo diodes are used to detect light and convert light signals into electrical signals. They are used in various optical instruments and circuits like light meters, remote controls, optical communication systems, etc.
- The sensitivity of a photo diode depends on the material used and the area of the PN junction. Larger area and materials like silicon and germanium having lower band gap result in higher sensitivity.
- The response time of a photo diode depends on the recombination time of carriers. Faster recombination results in faster response and vice versa.
- The two modes of operation of a photo diode are:
- Photovoltaic mode: Used to produce a voltage or current in response to light. No external voltage is applied.
- Photoconductive mode: Used to change the conductivity of the diode in response to light. A voltage is applied externally and the change in current is measured.
