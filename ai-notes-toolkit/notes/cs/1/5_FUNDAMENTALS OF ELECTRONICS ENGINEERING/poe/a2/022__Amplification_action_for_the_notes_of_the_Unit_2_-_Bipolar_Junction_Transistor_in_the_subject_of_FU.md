### Amplification action for the notes of the Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

1. A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a three terminal semiconductor device that can be used for amplification of signals.
2. The three terminals of a BJT are:
- Emitter (E): Injects electrons into the base region.
- Base (B): Thin region between emitter and collector which controls the flow of electrons.
- Collector (C): Collects the electrons from the emitter.
3. The two junctions in a BJT are:
- Emitter-base junction: Forward biased junction (heavily doped)
- Base-collector junction: Reverse biased junction (moderately doped)
4. Working principle: When a voltage is applied across the emitter-base junction, it gets forward biased and electrons flow from emitter to base. These electrons then diffuse into the depletion region of base-collector junction and get collected by the collector terminal. This results in flow of collector current. The ratio of change in collector and base currents is called current gain which results in amplification.
5. The amplification can be calculated as: Amplification (β) = Change in collector current (ΔIc)/Change in base current (ΔIb)
6. The amplification factor can be varied by changing the doping concentrations and dimensions of the three regions.
