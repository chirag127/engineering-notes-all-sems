## Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers

1. An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a direct-coupled high-gain voltage amplifier with two inputs and one output.
2. An op-amp has a large voltage gain (typically very high, greater than 10,000). The output voltage depends largely on the difference between the two input voltages, which is called the differential input voltage.
3. An op-amp has an inverting input terminal, a non-inverting input terminal and an output terminal. The voltage across both inputs are kept nearly zero by feedback circuits.
4. Op-amps are used to perform mathematical operations like summing, integration, differentiation, etc. Due to very high gain, op-amps are prone to become unstable. Negative feedback is used to improve stability and accuracy.
5. Some applications of op-amps are voltage followers, integrators, differentiators, summing amplifiers, instrumentation amplifiers, active filters, etc.
6. Important characteristics to consider while selecting an op-amp are voltage gain bandwidth product, slew rate, input offset voltage, common mode rejection ratio, supply voltages, etc.
