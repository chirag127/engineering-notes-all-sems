### Fundamentals of Satellite & Radar Communication

- Satellite communication uses artificial satellites placed in the orbit around the earth to establish communication. The satellite acts as a relay station to transmit and receive radio signals.
- The satellite has a microwave transponder that receives, amplifies and retransmits the signals back to the earth.
- The key components of a satellite communication system are the ground station, the uplink, the satellite, and the downlink.
- The ground station sends an uplink signal to the satellite which then sends a downlink signal to the ground station or other terminals.
- Due to the height of the satellite from the earth, a line of sight is present between the ground stations and the satellite which allows long-distance communication.
- The properties of the transmitted signal such as frequency, power level, modulation, etc. depend on the application of satellite communication like transponders, broadcasting, etc.
- Radar or RAdio Detection And Ranging uses radio waves to detect the location and velocity of objects. Radar systems transmit radio signals and listen to their echoes to sense objects and their properties.
- The radar system consists of a transmitter to generate radio signals, an antenna to transmit and receive signals, a modulator to modify signals, a receiver to detect echoes, and a signal processor to analyze received signals.
- The radar measures the time taken by the transmitted signal to bounce off the target and return to the radar to calculate the distance of the target. The change in frequency of the received signal is used to measure the velocity of the target.
- The applications of radar include air traffic control, weather monitoring, autonomous vehicles, etc. The radar technology works effectively even in harsh environmental conditions like fog, heavy rain, etc.
