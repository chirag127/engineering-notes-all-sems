### Op-Amp Basic

Operational Amplifiers, or Op-Amps, are high-gain electronic voltage amplifiers with a differential input and a single-ended output. They are widely used in electronic circuits for signal processing, filtering, amplification, and other applications. Here are some basics of Op-Amps:

- Op-Amps have two inputs, the inverting input (-) and the non-inverting input (+), and one output.
- The differential voltage between the two inputs is amplified by the gain of the Op-Amp.
- The output voltage is the amplification of the differential voltage, multiplied by the gain of the Op-Amp.
- The gain of the Op-Amp is determined by the feedback network, which can be a resistor or a combination of resistors and capacitors.
- Op-Amps have a very high input impedance, typically in the range of mega-ohms, which means they draw very little current from the input source.
- Op-Amps have a very low output impedance, typically in the range of ohms, which means they can drive heavy loads without significant distortion.
- Op-Amps have a wide range of power supply voltages, from a few volts to a few hundred volts, depending on the type of Op-Amp.
- Op-Amps can be used in a variety of configurations, such as inverting amplifier, non-inverting amplifier, differential amplifier, summing amplifier, integrator, differentiator, and many more.

Op-Amps are important building blocks in electronic circuits, and understanding the basics of Op-Amps is essential for any electronics engineer.
