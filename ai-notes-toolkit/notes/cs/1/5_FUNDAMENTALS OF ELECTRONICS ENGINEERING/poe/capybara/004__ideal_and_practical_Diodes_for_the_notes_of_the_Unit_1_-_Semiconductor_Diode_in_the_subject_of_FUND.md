### Ideal and Practical Diodes for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode in the Subject of Fundamentals of Electronics Engineering

In the study of semiconductor diodes, it is important to understand the difference between an ideal diode and a practical diode. Here are some key points to consider:

#### Ideal Diodes

- An ideal diode is a theoretical device that acts as a perfect conductor when a voltage is applied in the forward direction, and as a perfect insulator when a voltage is applied in the reverse direction.
- In other words, an ideal diode has zero resistance when conducting current in the forward direction and infinite resistance when blocking current in the reverse direction.
- An ideal diode can be represented by a simple model that consists of a voltage source in series with a switch that is closed in the forward direction and open in the reverse direction.

#### Practical Diodes

- A practical diode is a real-world device that exhibits some degree of non-ideal behavior.
- One of the most significant non-ideal characteristics of a practical diode is its forward voltage drop. This is the voltage that must be applied across the diode in the forward direction before it begins to conduct current.
- Another non-ideal characteristic is the reverse current that flows through the diode when a reverse voltage is applied. This is known as the reverse leakage current.
- Practical diodes can be modeled using more complex circuit models that take into account these non-ideal characteristics.

#### Types of Practical Diodes

- There are several types of practical diodes that are commonly used in electronic circuits, including:

  - Silicon diodes: These are the most common type of diode, and are used in a wide variety of applications. They have a forward voltage drop of around 0.7 volts.
  - Germanium diodes: These were once more commonly used than silicon diodes, but are now less common. They have a lower forward voltage drop of around 0.3 volts.
  - Schottky diodes: These are designed to have a lower forward voltage drop than silicon diodes, making them useful in applications where low voltage drop is important.
  - Zener diodes: These are designed to operate in the reverse breakdown region, and are used as voltage regulators.

Understanding the ideal and practical characteristics of semiconductor diodes is fundamental to the study of electronics engineering. By understanding these characteristics, engineers can design electronic circuits that operate reliably and efficiently.
