
### Amplification Action for Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor 

1. A Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal semiconductor device composed of two p-n junctions. 
2. BJTs are used primarily as amplifiers or switches, and are widely used in a variety of electronic circuits. 
3. The BJT consists of three regions: the emitter, the base, and the collector. 
4. The emitter is the source of charge carriers, the base is the control region, and the collector is the sink of charge carriers. 
5. The BJT is used as an amplifier because of its ability to control current and voltage gain. 
6. The voltage gain of a BJT is determined by the ratio of the collector current to the base current, and is referred to as the current gain (β). 
7. The current gain of the BJT is determined by the ratio of the collector current to the emitter current, and is referred to as the voltage gain (α). 
8. The current gain and voltage gain of the BJT are affected by the biasing of the transistor. 
9. The biasing of the BJT is the process of setting the voltage across the base-emitter junction so that the desired current flow is achieved. 
10. The biasing of the BJT can be achieved by applying either a fixed bias or an emitter bias. 
11. The fixed bias is set by applying a voltage across the base-emitter junction, while the emitter bias is set by applying a current to the emitter. 
12. The BJT can be used as a switch by applying a voltage to the base-emitter junction. 
13. When a voltage is applied to the base-emitter junction, the BJT will turn on, allowing current to flow from the collector to the emitter. 
14. The BJT can be used as an amplifier by applying a signal to the base-emitter junction. 
15. When a signal is applied to the base-emitter junction, the BJT will amplify the signal, allowing the output signal to be larger than the input signal.
