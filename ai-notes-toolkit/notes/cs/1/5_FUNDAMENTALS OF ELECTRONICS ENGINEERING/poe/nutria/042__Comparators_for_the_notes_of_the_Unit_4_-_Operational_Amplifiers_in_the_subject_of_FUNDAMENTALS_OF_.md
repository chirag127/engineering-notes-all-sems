
### Comparators for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

1. A comparator is an electronic circuit or device that compares two input voltages or currents and outputs a digital signal indicating which of the inputs is greater.
2. Comparators are used in many applications, including analog-to-digital converters, zero-crossing detectors, and motor control.
3. The basic operation of a comparator is to compare two input signals and output a signal indicating which of the two is larger.
4. Comparators are usually designed with high gain, meaning that any small difference between the two input signals will result in a large change in the output signal.
5. Comparators can also be used to detect changes in the input signals, such as a signal crossing a certain threshold.
6. Operational amplifiers (op-amps) are commonly used as comparators.
7. Op-amps have a high gain and can be used to compare two input signals and output a signal indicating which of the two is larger.
8. Op-amps can also be used to detect changes in the input signals, such as a signal crossing a certain threshold.
9. Comparators are also used in analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) to convert an analog signal into a digital signal.
10. Comparators are also used in motor control to detect changes in the motor speed and control the motor accordingly.
