
### Depletion Layer for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode in the Subject of Fundamentals of Electronics Engineering

1. A semiconductor diode is a two-terminal electronic device made of semiconductor material that allows current to flow in only one direction. 
2. The depletion layer is a region of the semiconductor diode where the electric field is strong enough to prevent the majority carriers from crossing the region and hence, no current can flow through the diode. 
3. The depletion layer is formed when the P-type and N-type materials are brought together. 
4. When a voltage is applied across the P-type and N-type materials, the majority carriers in the P-type material are attracted to the N-type material and vice versa. 
5. This creates an electric field in the depletion layer which prevents the majority carriers from crossing the region. 
6. The width of the depletion layer increases with the increase in the applied voltage. 
7. The depletion layer also acts as an insulator and prevents the current from flowing through the diode.
