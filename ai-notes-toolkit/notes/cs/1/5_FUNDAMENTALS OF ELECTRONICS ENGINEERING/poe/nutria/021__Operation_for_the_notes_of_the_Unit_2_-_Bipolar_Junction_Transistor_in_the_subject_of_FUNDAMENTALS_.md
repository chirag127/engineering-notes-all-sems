
### Operation for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor in the Subject of Fundamentals of Electronics Engineering

1. Bipolar junction transistors (BJTs) are three-terminal semiconductor devices used for switching and amplifying electrical signals. 
2. BJTs are composed of two p-n junctions, which form the base-emitter and base-collector junctions. 
3. BJTs can be used as either an amplifier or a switch, depending on the biasing of the base-emitter junction. 
4. When the base-emitter junction is forward biased, current flows from the emitter to the base, and the BJT is in the active region. 
5. In the active region, the BJT acts as an amplifier, and the output current is proportional to the input current. 
6. When the base-emitter junction is reverse biased, the BJT is in the cutoff region, and current does not flow from the emitter to the base. 
7. In the cutoff region, the BJT acts as a switch, and the output current is zero. 
8. The base-collector junction is always reverse biased, so the BJT is always in the cutoff region when the base-emitter junction is reverse biased. 
9. BJTs can be used to build complex circuits such as oscillators, amplifiers, and logic gates.
