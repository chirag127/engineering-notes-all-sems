
### Common Emitter for the notes of the Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

1. A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal electronic device composed of two p-n junctions. 
2. The BJT is made up of three layers of semiconductor material, the base, the emitter, and the collector. 
3. The base of the BJT acts as a control element, allowing current to flow from the emitter to the collector when the base-emitter junction is forward biased.
4. In the common emitter configuration, the emitter is connected to the ground, the base is connected to the input signal, and the collector is connected to the output signal. 
5. The input signal is amplified by the BJT, and the output signal is proportional to the input signal. 
6. The common emitter configuration is the most widely used BJT configuration due to its high gain and low input impedance. 
7. The common emitter configuration also provides voltage gain, current gain, and power gain. 
8. The common emitter configuration is used in many applications, such as amplifiers, oscillators, and switching circuits.
