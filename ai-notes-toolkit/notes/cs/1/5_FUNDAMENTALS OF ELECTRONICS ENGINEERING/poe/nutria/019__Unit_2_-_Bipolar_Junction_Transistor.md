
## Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

1. A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal semiconductor device that can be used as an amplifier or switch.

2. BJTs are composed of two p-n junctions formed between a layer of n-type semiconductor material and a layer of p-type semiconductor material.

3. BJTs are current-controlled devices, meaning that the current flowing through the base-emitter junction of the transistor controls the current flowing through the collector-emitter junction.

4. BJTs can be used in two basic configurations: common-emitter and common-base.

5. Common-emitter configuration is the most popular configuration for BJTs, as it provides the highest current gain (hfe).

6. Common-base configuration provides the highest voltage gain (hfe).

7. BJTs are used in a variety of applications, including amplifiers, oscillators, switches, and voltage regulators.
