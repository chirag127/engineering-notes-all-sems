### Light-Emitting Diodes

- A light-emitting diode (LED) is a semiconductor device that emits light when an electric current flows through it.
- When current passes through an LED, the electrons recombine with holes in the semiconductor, releasing energy in the form of photons . This process is called electroluminescence.
- The wavelength of the light produced depends on the energy band gap of the semiconductors used. Different colors of LEDs can be obtained by using different materials, such as gallium arsenide (GaAs) for infrared, gallium phosphide (GaP) for green, and gallium nitride (GaN) for blue.
- LEDs have many advantages over conventional light sources, such as low power consumption, long lifetime, high efficiency, small size, fast switching, and environmental friendliness .
- LEDs have many applications in various fields, such as digital displays, indicators, remote controls, optical communication, lighting, and biomedical devices  .
