### Photo Diodes

- A photo diode is a **light-sensitive semiconductor diode** that produces current when it absorbs photons .
- A photo diode has a **PIN structure or p-n junction** that creates an electron-hole pair when a photon of sufficient energy strikes the diode. This mechanism is also known as the **inner photoelectric effect**.
- A photo diode can be used to **measure light intensity**, either for its own sake or as a measure of some other property (such as smoke density, temperature, etc.).
- A photo diode can also be used as the **receiver of data** encoded on an infrared beam, as in household remote controls, optical communication, etc..
- A photo diode has a **package** that allows light (or infrared or ultraviolet radiation, or X-rays) to reach the sensitive part of the device. The package may include **lenses or optical filters** to focus or filter the incoming light.
- A photo diode can operate in two modes: **photovoltaic mode** or **photoconductive mode**. In photovoltaic mode, the diode is connected to an open circuit and generates a voltage proportional to the light intensity. In photoconductive mode, the diode is connected to a reverse bias voltage and generates a current proportional to the light intensity.
