- A full wave rectifier is a circuit that uses two or four diodes to convert both half cycles of the AC input into DC output. The negative half cycle is inverted or flipped by the diodes and is added to the positive half cycle.

- A third difference between half wave and full wave rectifier is peak inverse voltage (PIV). PIV is the maximum voltage that a diode can withstand in the reverse direction. A higher PIV means a more robust diode. A half wave rectifier has a low PIV of Vm (the peak value of the AC input) while a full wave rectifier has a high PIV of 2Vm.
