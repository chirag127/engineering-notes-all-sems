### MOSFET (MOS) (Depletion and Enhancement) Type

- MOSFET stands for Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor.
- It is a type of FET that uses an electric field to control the conductivity of a channel between the source and drain terminals.
- The channel is formed by a thin layer of semiconductor material, usually silicon, that is sandwiched between a metal gate electrode and an insulating oxide layer.
- The gate voltage determines the amount of charge carriers (electrons or holes) that can flow through the channel.
- There are two major types of MOSFETs: depletion mode and enhancement mode.
- Depletion mode MOSFETs have a channel that is already present at zero gate voltage, and the channel conductivity decreases as the gate voltage becomes more negative (for n-channel) or positive (for p-channel) .
- Enhancement mode MOSFETs have no channel at zero gate voltage, and the channel conductivity increases as the gate voltage becomes more positive (for n-channel) or negative (for p-channel) .

- Some applications of MOSFETs are:
  - Switching circuits, such as logic gates, inverters, and amplifiers.
  - Analog circuits, such as voltage regulators, oscillators, and filters.
  - Power electronics, such as converters, inverters, and motor drivers.
  - Sensors, such as pressure, temperature, and light sensors.
  - Memory devices, such as ROM, RAM, and flash memory.
