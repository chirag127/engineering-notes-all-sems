## Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

- A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a type of semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electrical signals.
- A BJT consists of three regions: the emitter, the base, and the collector. The emitter and the collector are doped with the same type of impurities (either n-type or p-type), while the base is doped with the opposite type of impurities. The base region is very thin and lightly doped compared to the emitter and the collector.
- A BJT can operate in three modes: active, saturation, and cutoff. In the active mode, the base-emitter junction is forward biased and the base-collector junction is reverse biased. The current flowing from the emitter to the collector is proportional to the base current and is controlled by the base voltage. In the saturation mode, both the base-emitter and the base-collector junctions are forward biased. The current flowing from the emitter to the collector is limited by the supply voltage and the resistance of the circuit. In the cutoff mode, both the base-emitter and the base-collector junctions are reverse biased. The current flowing from the emitter to the collector is negligible.
- A BJT can be classified into two types: npn and pnp. In an npn transistor, the emitter and the collector are n-type regions and the base is a p-type region. The current flows from the emitter to the collector when a positive voltage is applied to the base. In a pnp transistor, the emitter and the collector are p-type regions and the base is an n-type region. The current flows from the emitter to the collector when a negative voltage is applied to the base.
- A BJT can be used as an amplifier or a switch in various circuits. Some of the common configurations are: common emitter, common base, and common collector. In a common emitter configuration, the emitter is connected to the ground and the output is taken from the collector. The common emitter configuration provides high voltage gain, high current gain, and high power gain. In a common base configuration, the base is connected to the ground and the output is taken from the collector. The common base configuration provides high voltage gain, low current gain, and high power gain. In a common collector configuration, the collector is connected to the supply voltage and the output is taken from the emitter. The common collector configuration provides low voltage gain, high current gain, and low power gain.
