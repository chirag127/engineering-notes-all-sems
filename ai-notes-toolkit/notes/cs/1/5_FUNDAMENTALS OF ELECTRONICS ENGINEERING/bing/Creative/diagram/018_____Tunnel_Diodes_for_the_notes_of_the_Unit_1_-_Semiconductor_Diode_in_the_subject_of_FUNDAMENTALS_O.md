### Tunnel Diodes

- A tunnel diode is a type of semiconductor diode that has effectively "negative resistance" due to the quantum mechanical effect called tunneling.
- Tunneling is the phenomenon where an electron can pass through a potential barrier that is higher than its kinetic energy.
- A tunnel diode is formed by a heavily doped p-n junction, where the doping concentration is about 1000 times higher than a normal diode .
- The heavy doping results in a narrow depletion region (about 10 nm wide) and a broken band gap, where the conduction band of the n-side overlaps with the valence band of the p-side .
- The current-voltage (I-V) characteristic of a tunnel diode is shown below:

![I-V characteristic of a tunnel diode](https://www.electronicshub.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Tunnel-Diode-IV-Characteristics.jpg)

- The I-V curve has three regions: forward bias, negative resistance, and reverse bias.
- In the forward bias region, the current increases rapidly as the voltage increases until it reaches a peak value (I<sub>p</sub>) at a small voltage (V<sub>p</sub>).
- This is because the electrons on the n-side can tunnel through the potential barrier and reach the empty states on the p-side.
- As the voltage increases further, the current decreases until it reaches a valley value (I<sub>v</sub>) at a higher voltage (V<sub>v</sub>).
- This is because the number of available states on the p-side decreases and the tunneling probability decreases.
- This region of decreasing current with increasing voltage is called the negative resistance region.
- In this region, the tunnel diode can act as an oscillator or an amplifier.
- As the voltage increases beyond the valley point, the current increases again in the reverse bias region.
- This is because the conventional diode behavior dominates and the reverse breakdown occurs.

- Some of the applications of tunnel diodes are :

  - High-speed switching circuits
  - Microwave oscillators and amplifiers
  - Logic circuits
  - Pulse generators
  - Waveform converters
  - Frequency multipliers
  - Mixers and detectors
