### Integrator

- An integrator is an electronic circuit that performs the mathematical operation of integration with respect to time.
- An integrator circuit is based on an operational amplifier (op-amp) with a resistor and a capacitor connected in the feedback loop .
- The output voltage of an integrator is proportional to the input voltage integrated over time, that is, Vout = -1/RC ∫ Vin dt + C where R is the resistance, C is the capacitance, and C is a constant of integration.
- An integrator can be used to generate a ramp output from a constant input, to measure the area under a waveform, to perform analog computation, and to filter out high-frequency signals .
- An ideal integrator has infinite input impedance, zero output impedance, and infinite bandwidth. However, a practical integrator has finite input and output impedances, limited bandwidth, and may suffer from saturation, drift, and noise .
- An integrator can be improved by adding a resistor in parallel with the capacitor to prevent the output from drifting to saturation, and by adding a diode or a switch to reset the capacitor voltage to zero periodically .
- An integrator can be combined with a differentiator to form an active filter, such as a low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, or band-stop filter .
- An integrator can also be implemented using other devices, such as transistors, operational transconductance amplifiers, or digital signal processors .
