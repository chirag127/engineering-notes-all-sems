- Rectification is the process of converting alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC) by using one or more diodes.
- A diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It has two terminals: anode and cathode.
- A half wave rectifier is a rectifier that uses only one diode to convert one half cycle of the AC input into DC output. The other half cycle is blocked by the diode and wasted.
- A full wave rectifier is a rectifier that uses two or four diodes to convert both half cycles of the AC input into DC output. The negative half cycle is inverted or flipped by the diodes and added to the positive half cycle.
- The main difference between half wave and full wave rectifier is efficiency. A full wave rectifier has higher efficiency than a half wave rectifier because it utilizes both half cycles of the AC input and produces a smoother DC output.
- The main disadvantage of a full wave rectifier is that it requires more diodes and a center-tapped or a bridge configuration to achieve full wave rectification.
- The output of a rectifier is not a pure DC, but a pulsating DC that has ripples or variations. To reduce the ripples, a filter circuit such as a capacitor or an inductor is used after the rectifier.
- The performance of a rectifier can be measured by parameters such as peak inverse voltage (PIV), average output voltage, ripple factor, rectification efficiency, and transformer utilization factor (TUF).
