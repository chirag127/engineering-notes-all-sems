### V-I characteristics of semiconductor diode

- The V-I characteristics of a semiconductor diode is a curve that shows the relationship between the voltage across the diode and the current through it.
- The V-I characteristics can be obtained by connecting a diode in series with a resistor and a variable voltage source, as shown in the figure below.

![Circuit diagram for V-I characteristics of diode](https://circuitglobe.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/V-I-Characteristics-of-Diode.png)

- The V-I characteristics can be divided into two regions: forward bias and reverse bias.
- Forward bias is the condition when the positive terminal of the voltage source is connected to the p-type material and the negative terminal to the n-type material of the diode.
- In forward bias, the diode offers a low resistance to the current flow and allows a large current to pass through it.
- The forward current increases exponentially with the increase in the forward voltage, as shown in the figure below.

![V-I characteristics of diode in forward bias](https://electronicspost.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/forward-bias.png)

- The forward voltage required to initiate the current flow is called the cut-in voltage or the threshold voltage. It is typically 0.7 V for silicon diodes and 0.3 V for germanium diodes.
- The forward resistance of the diode is the ratio of the forward voltage to the forward current. It is usually very small and decreases with the increase in the forward current.
- Reverse bias is the condition when the positive terminal of the voltage source is connected to the n-type material and the negative terminal to the p-type material of the diode.
- In reverse bias, the diode offers a high resistance to the current flow and allows a very small current to pass through it.
- The reverse current is due to the thermally generated minority carriers in the diode. It is almost constant and independent of the reverse voltage, as shown in the figure below.

![V-I characteristics of diode in reverse bias](https://electronicspost.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/reverse-bias.png)

- The reverse resistance of the diode is the ratio of the reverse voltage to the reverse current. It is usually very large and increases with the increase in the reverse voltage.
- The reverse voltage at which the diode breaks down and allows a large current to flow is called the breakdown voltage or the peak inverse voltage. It depends on the type and doping level of the diode.
- The breakdown region is not shown in the V-I characteristics as it can damage the diode. It is usually avoided in normal operation of the diode.
