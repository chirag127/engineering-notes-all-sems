### Op-Amp Basics

- An op-amp (operational amplifier) is a device that can amplify the difference between two input voltages and produce a single-ended output voltage.
- An op-amp has two input terminals, labeled as V+ (non-inverting input) and V- (inverting input), and one output terminal, labeled as Vout.
- An op-amp also has two power supply terminals, labeled as Vs+ and Vs-, which provide the maximum and minimum output voltage levels, respectively.
- An op-amp can be modeled as a voltage-controlled voltage source (VCVS) with a very high open-loop gain (A), a very high input impedance (Zin), and a very low output impedance (Zout).
- The basic operation of an op-amp can be described by the following equation:

  Vout = A * (V+ - V-)

- The open-loop gain (A) of an op-amp is the ratio of the output voltage to the input voltage when there is no feedback. It is typically very large, in the order of 10^5 to 10^6.
- The input impedance (Zin) of an op-amp is the resistance seen by the input voltage sources. It is typically very high, in the order of 10^6 to 10^12 ohms.
- The output impedance (Zout) of an op-amp is the resistance seen by the load connected to the output terminal. It is typically very low, in the order of 10 to 100 ohms.
- An op-amp can be used in various configurations to perform different functions, such as amplification, buffering, filtering, summing, subtracting, integrating, differentiating, etc. These configurations are called op-amp circuits and they use feedback to control the gain and the output of the op-amp.
