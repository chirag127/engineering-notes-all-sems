# Unit 3 - Field Effect Transistor

- A field effect transistor (FET) is a type of transistor that uses an electric field to control the flow of current in a semiconductor.
- FETs have three terminals: source, gate, and drain.
- The source is the terminal where the current enters the device, the drain is the terminal where the current leaves the device, and the gate is the terminal that modulates the current flow by applying a voltage.
- There are two main types of FETs: junction field effect transistors (JFETs) and metal-oxide-semiconductor field effect transistors (MOSFETs).
- JFETs are FETs that have a pn junction between the gate and the channel, which is the region between the source and the drain.
- MOSFETs are FETs that have a thin layer of insulating material (usually oxide) between the gate and the channel, which is formed by a thin layer of semiconductor (usually silicon).
- JFETs and MOSFETs can be further classified into n-channel and p-channel types, depending on the polarity of the voltage applied to the gate and the type of charge carriers in the channel.
- FETs are widely used in various electronic applications, such as amplifiers, switches, sensors, and logic circuits.
- FETs have some advantages over bipolar junction transistors (BJTs), such as higher input impedance, lower power consumption, and better temperature stability .
- FETs also have some disadvantages, such as lower gain, higher noise, and susceptibility to damage by electrostatic discharge .
