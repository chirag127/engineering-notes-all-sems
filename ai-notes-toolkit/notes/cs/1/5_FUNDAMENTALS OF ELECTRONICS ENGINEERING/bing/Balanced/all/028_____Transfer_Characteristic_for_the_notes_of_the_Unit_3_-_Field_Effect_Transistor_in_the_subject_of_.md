# Transfer Characteristic of Field Effect Transistor

- A field effect transistor (FET) is a type of transistor that uses an electric field to control the flow of current in the channel between the source and the drain terminals.
- The gate terminal is used to apply a voltage that modulates the electric field and thus the channel conductivity.
- The transfer characteristic of a FET is a curve that shows the relationship between the gate voltage and the drain current, while keeping the drain-source voltage constant .
- The transfer characteristic can be used to determine the operating point, the transconductance, the input resistance, and the cut-off voltage of a FET .
- The shape of the transfer characteristic depends on the type, mode, and biasing of the FET .
- There are two main types of FETs: junction field effect transistors (JFETs) and metal oxide semiconductor field effect transistors (MOSFETs).
- JFETs have a p-n junction between the gate and the channel, while MOSFETs have a metal oxide layer between the gate and the channel.
- JFETs can operate in depletion mode only, while MOSFETs can operate in both enhancement and depletion modes.
- Depletion mode means that the channel is normally conductive and the gate voltage reduces the channel width and the drain current.
- Enhancement mode means that the channel is normally non-conductive and the gate voltage increases the channel width and the drain current.
- The transfer characteristic of a JFET is nonlinear and has a negative slope, meaning that the drain current decreases as the gate voltage becomes more negative .
- The transfer characteristic of a MOSFET is linear in the saturation region and has a positive slope, meaning that the drain current increases as the gate voltage becomes more positive.
- The transfer characteristic of a FET can be affected by various factors, such as temperature, channel length, channel width, threshold voltage, and load resistance .
- The transfer characteristic of a FET can be measured experimentally by using a circuit that applies a variable gate voltage and a constant drain-source voltage, and records the corresponding drain current.
- The transfer characteristic of a FET can be modeled mathematically by using a square-law equation that relates the drain current to the gate voltage and the drain-source voltage, with some parameters that depend on the FET type and mode .
