# Introduction for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain, direct-coupled, differential-input amplifier that can perform various linear and nonlinear operations on input signals.
- An op-amp has two input terminals, called the inverting (-) and the non-inverting (+) inputs, and one output terminal. It also has two power supply terminals, usually labeled as Vcc and Vee, which provide the positive and negative voltages to the op-amp.
- An op-amp can be modeled as a voltage-controlled voltage source (VCVS) with a very large open-loop voltage gain (A), a very high input impedance (Zin), and a very low output impedance (Zout).
- The ideal op-amp has the following characteristics:
  - Infinite open-loop voltage gain (A = ∞)
  - Infinite input impedance (Zin = ∞)
  - Zero output impedance (Zout = 0)
  - Zero input offset voltage (Vos = 0)
  - Infinite bandwidth (BW = ∞)
  - Zero noise
- The practical op-amp deviates from the ideal op-amp in some aspects, such as finite gain, finite input impedance, non-zero output impedance, input offset voltage, finite bandwidth, and noise. These factors affect the performance and accuracy of the op-amp circuits.
- The op-amp can be used to implement various linear and nonlinear circuits, such as amplifiers, filters, oscillators, comparators, integrators, differentiators, etc. The op-amp circuits are usually designed using feedback, which is the process of feeding back a fraction of the output signal to the input. Feedback can be positive or negative, depending on the polarity of the feedback signal.
- Negative feedback reduces the overall gain of the op-amp circuit, but improves its stability, bandwidth, linearity, and input-output impedance matching. Negative feedback is commonly used in op-amp circuits to achieve a desired closed-loop gain and frequency response.
- Positive feedback increases the overall gain of the op-amp circuit, but reduces its stability, bandwidth, linearity, and input-output impedance matching. Positive feedback is used in op-amp circuits to achieve oscillation, hysteresis, or regeneration.
