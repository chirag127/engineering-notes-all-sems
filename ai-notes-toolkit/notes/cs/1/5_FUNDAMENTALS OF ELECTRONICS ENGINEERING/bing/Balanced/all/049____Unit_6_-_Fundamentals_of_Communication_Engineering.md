## Unit 6 - Fundamentals of Communication Engineering

- Communication engineering is a field that focuses on supporting systems that transfer information from one place to another.
- Information can be in the form of signals, such as audio, video, data, or text.
- Communication systems can be classified into analog or digital, depending on the nature of the signals and the devices used to process them.
- Some of the basic communication engineering concepts are:
  - Signals, spectra, and bandwidth: Signals are the physical representation of information, spectra are the frequency components of signals, and bandwidth is the range of frequencies that a signal occupies.
  - Attenuation, distortion, and noise: Attenuation is the loss of signal power due to transmission medium, distortion is the alteration of signal shape or quality due to non-linear effects, and noise is the unwanted interference that affects the signal quality.
  - Filtering, equalizing, and companding: Filtering is the process of removing unwanted frequency components from a signal, equalizing is the process of compensating for the distortion caused by the transmission medium, and companding is the process of compressing and expanding the signal amplitude to reduce the dynamic range and improve the signal-to-noise ratio.
  - Modulation and multiplexing: Modulation is the process of changing the characteristics of a carrier signal according to the information signal, and multiplexing is the process of combining multiple signals into one signal for transmission over a common medium.
  - Information measurement, source and channel coding, channel capacity and Shannon theorems: Information measurement is the quantification of the amount of information in a signal, source coding is the process of reducing the redundancy in a signal, channel coding is the process of adding redundancy to a signal to protect it from errors, channel capacity is the maximum rate of information that can be transmitted over a channel, and Shannon theorems are the fundamental limits of communication systems.
  - Baseband data transmission, digital modulation and spread-spectrum systems: Baseband data transmission is the direct transmission of digital signals over a channel, digital modulation is the process of converting digital signals into analog signals for transmission over a channel, and spread-spectrum systems are the systems that use a wide bandwidth to transmit signals with low power and high security.
  - Local area networks and architecture and performance: Local area networks are the networks that connect devices within a small geographical area, such as a building or a campus, and their architecture and performance depend on the topology, medium, protocol, and traffic characteristics of the network.
  - Layered network architecture, protocols, switching techniques, TCP/IP, traffic engineering and basic capacity analysis: Layered network architecture is the hierarchical organization of network functions into different layers, protocols are the rules and formats that govern the communication between network devices, switching techniques are the methods of transferring data from one device to another, TCP/IP is the most widely used network protocol suite, traffic engineering is the optimization of network performance and resource utilization, and basic capacity analysis is the estimation of the maximum throughput and delay of a network.
