# Need of Modulation and Typical Applications

Modulation is the process of changing the characteristics of a carrier signal (such as amplitude, frequency or phase) according to the information signal (such as voice, data or video) to be transmitted. Modulation is required for the following reasons:

- To increase the range and quality of communication: Modulation enables the transmission of low-frequency signals over long distances by using high-frequency carrier waves. This reduces the attenuation and noise interference of the signals. Modulation also improves the signal quality at the receiver by using techniques such as error correction and encryption .
- To reduce the size and cost of the antenna: Modulation allows the use of smaller and cheaper antennas for transmitting and receiving signals. The antenna size is inversely proportional to the frequency of the signal. By modulating the low-frequency signal with a high-frequency carrier wave, the antenna size can be reduced .
- To enable multiplexing and sharing of the channel: Modulation enables the transmission of multiple signals over the same channel by using different carrier frequencies or modulation schemes. This increases the efficiency and capacity of the communication system. Modulation also allows the sharing of the channel among different users by using techniques such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM) or code division multiple access (CDMA) .
- To facilitate the conversion and compatibility of signals: Modulation enables the conversion of signals from one form to another, such as analog to digital or vice versa. This allows the use of different types of devices and systems for communication. Modulation also ensures the compatibility of signals with the transmission medium and the receiver .

Some of the typical applications of modulation are:

- Radio and television broadcasting: Modulation is used to transmit audio and video signals over the airwaves by using different carrier frequencies and modulation schemes. For example, amplitude modulation (AM) and frequency modulation (FM) are used for radio broadcasting, while vestigial sideband (VSB) and quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) are used for television broadcasting .
- Mobile and wireless communication: Modulation is used to transmit voice and data signals over wireless networks by using different carrier frequencies and modulation schemes. For example, global system for mobile communication (GSM) and code division multiple access (CDMA) are used for cellular communication, while Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are used for wireless local area network (WLAN) and personal area network (PAN) respectively .
- Optical communication: Modulation is used to transmit data signals over optical fibers by using different wavelengths and modulation schemes. For example, wavelength division multiplexing (WDM) and dense wavelength division multiplexing (DWDM) are used for optical communication .
- Satellite communication: Modulation is used to transmit signals from the earth station to the satellite and vice versa by using different carrier frequencies and modulation schemes. For example, single sideband (SSB) and phase shift keying (PSK) are used for satellite communication .
