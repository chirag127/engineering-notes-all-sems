### Transistor Construction

- A transistor is a three-layer semiconductor device that can act as an amplifier or a switch.
- A semiconductor is a material that can conduct electricity under certain conditions, such as when an electric field or a light is applied to it.
- The three layers of a transistor are made of either P-type or N-type semiconductor material, which have different electrical properties.
- P-type semiconductor material has a surplus of holes, which are the absence of electrons. Holes can move through the material when an electric field is applied, creating a positive current.
- N-type semiconductor material has a surplus of electrons, which can move through the material when an electric field is applied, creating a negative current.
- The three layers of a transistor are arranged in either NPN or PNP configuration, as shown in the figure below.

- The middle layer is called the base, and the outer layers are called the emitter and the collector.
- The base is very thin and lightly doped, meaning it has fewer holes or electrons than the emitter and the collector, which are heavily doped.
- The emitter and the collector are connected to an external circuit, and the base is connected to a control voltage or current.
- The transistor works by controlling the flow of current from the emitter to the collector, depending on the voltage or current applied to the base.
- In an NPN transistor, the emitter is N-type and the collector is P-type. When a positive voltage is applied to the base, it attracts electrons from the emitter, which then flow through the base to the collector, creating a large output current. This is called the forward active mode of the transistor, and it works as an amplifier.
- When a negative voltage is applied to the base, it repels electrons from the emitter, and no current flows from the emitter to the collector. This is called the cutoff mode of the transistor, and it works as a switch.
- In a PNP transistor, the emitter is P-type and the collector is N-type. The operation is similar to the NPN transistor, but with opposite polarities. A negative voltage at the base causes a large output current from the emitter to the collector, and a positive voltage at the base causes no output current.
