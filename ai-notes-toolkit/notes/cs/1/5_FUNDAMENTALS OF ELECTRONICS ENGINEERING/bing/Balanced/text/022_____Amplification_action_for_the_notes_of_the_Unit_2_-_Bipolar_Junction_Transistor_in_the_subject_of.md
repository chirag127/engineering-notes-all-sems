### Amplification action for the notes of the Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

- A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal device that can amplify the flow of current by controlling the current in one terminal (base) with the voltage applied at another terminal (emitter) .
- A BJT has two types of terminals: N-type and P-type, which are formed by doping a semiconductor material with impurities. A BJT can have either NPN or PNP configuration, depending on the arrangement of the terminals .
- A BJT can operate in three regions: cut-off, active, and saturation. In the cut-off region, the base-emitter junction is reverse biased and the base-collector junction is either reverse or forward biased, resulting in no current flow in the collector. In the saturation region, both the base-emitter and base-collector junctions are forward biased, resulting in maximum current flow in the collector. In the active region, the base-emitter junction is forward biased and the base-collector junction is reverse biased, resulting in a controlled current flow in the collector .
- A BJT amplifier is a circuit that uses a BJT in the active region to increase the strength of the input signal and produce an amplified output. The input signal is applied to the base terminal and the output signal is taken from the collector terminal. The emitter terminal is usually connected to a common ground .
- Depending on the quantity amplified by the circuit, the BJT amplifier can be a voltage, current, or power amplifier. A voltage amplifier increases the voltage of the input signal and has a high input impedance and a low output impedance. A current amplifier increases the current of the input signal and has a low input impedance and a high output impedance. A power amplifier increases the power of the input signal and has a moderate input and output impedance .
- A BJT amplifier can also be classified based on the configuration of the terminals: common emitter, common base, or common collector. A common emitter amplifier has the emitter terminal as the common terminal for both input and output. It has a high voltage gain, a moderate current gain, and a moderate power gain. A common base amplifier has the base terminal as the common terminal for both input and output. It has a low voltage gain, a high current gain, and a high power gain. A common collector amplifier has the collector terminal as the common terminal for both input and output. It has a low voltage gain, a low current gain, and a low power gain .
- A BJT amplifier can be used for various applications, such as audio amplification, radio frequency amplification, signal processing, switching, and logic circuits .
