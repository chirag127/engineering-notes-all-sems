### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain, direct-coupled, differential-input amplifier that can perform various linear and nonlinear operations on input signals.
- The term "operational" refers to the fact that op-amps were originally designed to perform mathematical operations, such as addition, subtraction, integration, and differentiation, in analog computers.
- The basic symbol of an op-amp is shown below:

![op-amp symbol](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Op-Amp.svg/1200px-Op-Amp.svg.png)

- The op-amp has two input terminals: the inverting input (-) and the non-inverting input (+), and one output terminal. It also has two power supply terminals: the positive supply (V+) and the negative supply (V-).
- The op-amp amplifies the difference between the input voltages (V+ - V-) and produces an output voltage (Vout) that is proportional to the input difference.
- The op-amp has a very high input impedance (Zin), which means that it draws very little current from the input sources. It also has a very low output impedance (Zout), which means that it can drive a large current to the output load.
- The op-amp has a very high open-loop gain (A), which is the ratio of the output voltage to the input difference without any feedback. The open-loop gain is typically in the order of 10^5 to 10^6, and it varies with frequency and temperature.
- The op-amp is usually connected with a feedback network, which is a circuit that connects the output to the input. The feedback network can control the gain, bandwidth, stability, and linearity of the op-amp. The feedback network can be either negative or positive, depending on whether it subtracts or adds the output to the input.
- The op-amp can be used to implement various linear and nonlinear circuits, such as amplifiers, filters, oscillators, comparators, integrators, differentiators, etc. The op-amp is a versatile and widely used component in electronic systems.
