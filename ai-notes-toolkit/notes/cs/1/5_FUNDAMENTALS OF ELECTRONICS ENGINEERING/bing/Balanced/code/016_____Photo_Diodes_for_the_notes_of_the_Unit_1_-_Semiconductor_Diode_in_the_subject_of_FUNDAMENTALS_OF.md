### Photo Diodes

- A photo diode is a light-sensitive semiconductor diode that produces current when it absorbs photons.
- The package of a photo diode allows light (or infrared or ultraviolet radiation, or X-rays) to reach the sensitive part of the device. The package may include lenses or optical filters.
- A photo diode is designed to work in reverse bias condition, meaning that the positive terminal of the battery is connected to the n-type and the negative terminal to the p-type .
- When light falls on the junction, electron-hole pairs are generated. The electric field of the junction causes the electrons to move towards the n-type and the holes to move towards the p-type. This creates a photocurrent that is proportional to the light intensity .
- A photo diode can be used to measure light intensity, either for its own sake or as a measure of some other property (density of smoke, for example). It can also be used as the receiver of data encoded on an infrared beam, as in household remote controls.
- Some of the applications of photo diodes are:
  - Optical communication: Photo diodes can be used to convert optical signals into electrical signals in fiber optic networks.
  - Solar cells: Photo diodes can be used to convert solar energy into electrical energy by generating a voltage across the junction.
  - Photometry: Photo diodes can be used to measure the luminous intensity or flux of a light source or a surface.
  - Smoke detectors: Photo diodes can be used to detect the presence of smoke by measuring the change in light intensity due to scattering or absorption.
  - Barcode scanners: Photo diodes can be used to read the patterns of black and white bars on a product by detecting the reflected light.
