### Zener Diodes breakdown mechanism (Zener and avalanche)

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode that can operate in the reverse breakdown region, where the current increases rapidly with the applied reverse voltage.
- The reverse breakdown region is the region where the electric field across the depletion layer becomes so high that it can break the covalent bonds of the semiconductor atoms, creating free electrons and holes that can conduct current.
- There are two types of reverse breakdown mechanisms: Zener breakdown and avalanche breakdown.
- Zener breakdown occurs when the electric field is strong enough to pull electrons from the valence band to the conduction band, creating electron-hole pairs. This happens at low reverse voltages (typically less than 5 V) and high doping levels.
- Avalanche breakdown occurs when the free electrons and holes in the depletion layer gain enough kinetic energy from the electric field to collide with other atoms, creating more electron-hole pairs. This happens at high reverse voltages (typically more than 5 V) and low doping levels.
- The breakdown voltage of a Zener diode is the reverse voltage at which the Zener diode starts to conduct significant current. It depends on the doping level, the temperature, and the physical dimensions of the diode.
- The Zener diode can be used as a voltage regulator, as it maintains a constant voltage across its terminals regardless of the current flowing through it, as long as the reverse voltage is higher than the breakdown voltage.
- The Zener diode can also be used as a voltage reference, as it provides a stable and precise voltage that can be used for calibration or comparison purposes.
