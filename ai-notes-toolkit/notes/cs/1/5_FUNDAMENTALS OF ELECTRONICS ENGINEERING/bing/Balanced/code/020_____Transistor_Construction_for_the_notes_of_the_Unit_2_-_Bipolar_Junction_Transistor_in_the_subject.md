### Transistor Construction

- A transistor is a three-layer semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electric currents.
- A transistor consists of two types of semiconductor materials: P-type and N-type.
- P-type semiconductor has a surplus of holes (positive charge carriers) and N-type semiconductor has a surplus of electrons (negative charge carriers).
- The process of adding or removing electrons from a semiconductor is called doping.
- A transistor can be fabricated by sandwiching one type of semiconductor between two similar other types of semiconductor, or by sandwiching two types of semiconductor between one similar other type of semiconductor.
- The former configuration is called a bipolar junction transistor (BJT) and the latter configuration is called a field-effect transistor (FET).
- A BJT can be either PNP or NPN, depending on the order of the semiconductor layers.
- A FET can be either P-channel or N-channel, depending on the type of the semiconductor layer between the two similar other layers.
- The three terminals of a transistor are called the emitter, the base, and the collector for a BJT, and the source, the gate, and the drain for a FET.
- The emitter and the collector are the input and output terminals of a transistor, and the base or the gate is the control terminal that modulates the current flow between the emitter and the collector, or the source and the drain.
