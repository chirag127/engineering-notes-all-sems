### Basics of signal representation and analysis

- A signal is a physical quantity that varies with time, space, or any other independent variable. It can be used to convey information, such as speech, music, images, etc. 
- Signal representation is the process of describing a signal using mathematical functions or symbols. Signal representation can be done in different domains, such as time, frequency, or space.  
- Signal analysis is the process of extracting useful information from a signal, such as its amplitude, frequency, phase, spectrum, etc. Signal analysis can be done using various techniques, such as Fourier transform, Laplace transform, Z-transform, etc.  
- Time domain representation is the most common way of representing a signal as a function of time. Time domain representation shows the variation of the signal amplitude with respect to time. Time domain representation is useful for studying the temporal characteristics of a signal, such as its duration, rise time, fall time, etc.  
- Frequency domain representation is another way of representing a signal as a function of frequency. Frequency domain representation shows the variation of the signal amplitude or power with respect to frequency. Frequency domain representation is useful for studying the spectral characteristics of a signal, such as its bandwidth, frequency components, harmonics, etc.  
- To convert a signal from time domain to frequency domain, or vice versa, we can use the Fourier transform, which is a mathematical tool that decomposes a signal into a sum of sinusoidal waves of different frequencies, amplitudes, and phases. The Fourier transform can be applied to both continuous and discrete signals, and can be either one-dimensional or multi-dimensional.  
- Some signals, such as periodic signals, have a simple and finite frequency domain representation, which is called the Fourier series. The Fourier series expresses a periodic signal as a sum of sinusoidal waves of frequencies that are multiples of the fundamental frequency of the signal. The Fourier series can be used to analyze the harmonic content of a signal, and to synthesize new signals from existing ones.  
- Some signals, such as non-periodic signals, have a complex and infinite frequency domain representation, which is called the Fourier transform. The Fourier transform expresses a non-periodic signal as a sum of sinusoidal waves of all possible frequencies, amplitudes, and phases. The Fourier transform can be used to analyze the spectrum of a signal, and to perform filtering, modulation, and demodulation operations.  
- Some signals, such as discrete signals, have a discrete frequency domain representation, which is called the discrete Fourier transform (DFT). The DFT expresses a discrete signal as a sum of complex exponential waves of discrete frequencies, amplitudes, and phases. The DFT can be used to analyze the frequency content of a discrete signal, and to perform digital signal processing operations.  
- Some signals, such as complex signals, have a complex time domain representation, which is called the analytic signal. The analytic signal is a signal that has only positive frequency components, and is obtained by adding a 90-degree phase-shifted version of the original signal to itself. The analytic signal can be used to analyze the amplitude and phase modulation of a signal, and to perform envelope detection and demodulation operations.
