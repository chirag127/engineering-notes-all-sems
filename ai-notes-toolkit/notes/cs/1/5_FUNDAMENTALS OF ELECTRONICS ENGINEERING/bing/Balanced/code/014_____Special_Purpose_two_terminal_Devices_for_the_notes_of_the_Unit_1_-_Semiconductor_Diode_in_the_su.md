### Special Purpose Two Terminal Devices

Two terminal devices are electronic components that have only two terminals, such as anode and cathode, and allow current to flow only in one direction. The most common two terminal device is the diode, which is a semiconductor device that has a p-n junction and can be used for rectification, switching, clamping, clipping, modulation, etc. However, there are some special purpose two terminal devices that have different characteristics and applications than the ordinary diodes. Some of these devices are:

- **Tunnel diode**: A tunnel diode is a type of diode that has a very thin p-n junction and a high doping level, which allows quantum tunneling of electrons across the junction. This results in a negative resistance region in the current-voltage characteristic, where the current decreases as the voltage increases. Tunnel diodes can be used for high-speed switching, oscillation, amplification, and microwave applications.
- **Photo diode**: A photo diode is a type of diode that is sensitive to light. When light falls on the p-n junction, it generates electron-hole pairs, which increase the current flow. Photo diodes can be used for light detection, optical communication, solar cells, and photometry.
- **Varactor diode**: A varactor diode is a type of diode that has a variable capacitance depending on the applied reverse bias voltage. The p-n junction acts as a parallel-plate capacitor, whose plate area and distance vary with the voltage. Varactor diodes can be used for frequency tuning, modulation, filtering, and oscillation.
- **Schottky diode**: A Schottky diode is a type of diode that has a metal-semiconductor junction instead of a p-n junction. The metal-semiconductor junction has a lower potential barrier and a faster switching speed than the p-n junction. Schottky diodes can be used for rectification, switching, logic circuits, and voltage clamping.
- **Light emitting diode (LED)**: A light emitting diode is a type of diode that emits light when a forward bias voltage is applied. The light is produced by the recombination of electrons and holes in the p-n junction, which releases energy in the form of photons. LEDs can be used for illumination, display, signaling, and optical communication.

These are some of the special purpose two terminal devices that have different properties and functions than the ordinary diodes. They are widely used in various electronic circuits and systems for different purposes.
