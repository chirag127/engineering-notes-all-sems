### MOSFET (MOS) (Depletion and Enhancement) Type

- MOSFET stands for metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor. It is a type of FET that uses an electric field to control the current flow through a channel between the source and drain terminals.
- MOSFETs can be classified into two types based on the presence or absence of a channel at zero gate voltage: depletion type and enhancement type.
- Depletion type MOSFET (D-MOSFET) has a channel fabricated during manufacturing. It conducts current between the source and drain terminals even when the gate voltage is zero .
- Enhancement type MOSFET (E-MOSFET) has no channel during manufacturing. It does not conduct current when the gate voltage is zero. A channel is induced by applying a positive gate voltage for n-channel MOSFET or a negative gate voltage for p-channel MOSFET .
- The main difference between D-MOSFET and E-MOSFET is that the former can operate in both depletion mode and enhancement mode, while the latter can only operate in enhancement mode .
- Depletion mode means that the channel conductivity decreases as the gate voltage becomes more negative for n-channel MOSFET or more positive for p-channel MOSFET. Enhancement mode means that the channel conductivity increases as the gate voltage becomes more positive for n-channel MOSFET or more negative for p-channel MOSFET.
