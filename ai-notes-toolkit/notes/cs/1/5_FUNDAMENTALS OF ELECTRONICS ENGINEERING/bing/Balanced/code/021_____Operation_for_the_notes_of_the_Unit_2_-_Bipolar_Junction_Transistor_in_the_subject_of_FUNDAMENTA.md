### Operation of Bipolar Junction Transistor

- A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a type of transistor that uses both electron and hole charge carriers. In contrast, unipolar transistors, such as field-effect transistors, only use one kind of charge carrier.
- A BJT has three terminals: the base (B), the collector (C), and the emitter (E). The base is the control terminal, the collector is the output terminal, and the emitter is the input terminal. The base-emitter junction is forward-biased, while the base-collector junction is reverse-biased.
- A BJT can operate in three regions: active, saturation, and cutoff. In the active region, the transistor acts as an amplifier, where the collector current is proportional to the base current. In the saturation region, the transistor is fully on and acts as a switch, where the collector current is equal to the saturation current. In the cutoff region, the transistor is fully off and acts as an open circuit, where the collector current is zero.
- The operation of a BJT can be explained by the movement of charge carriers across the two junctions. In an npn transistor, the majority carriers are electrons, while in a pnp transistor, the majority carriers are holes. The following steps describe the operation of an npn transistor in the active region:
  - A small forward bias voltage is applied to the base-emitter junction, which causes electrons to flow from the emitter to the base, and holes to flow from the base to the emitter. This constitutes the emitter current (I_E).
  - Most of the electrons injected into the base are minority carriers, and they diffuse across the base region towards the reverse-biased base-collector junction. Only a small fraction of the electrons recombine with the holes in the base, and constitute the base current (I_B).
  - The electrons that reach the base-collector junction are swept across the junction by the reverse bias voltage, and enter the collector region. This constitutes the collector current (I_C).
  - The collector current is much larger than the base current, and the ratio of the two currents is called the current gain (β). The emitter current is the sum of the base and collector currents: I_E = I_B + I_C.
  - The output voltage across the collector and emitter terminals (V_CE) depends on the collector current and the load resistance. The output voltage is inversely proportional to the collector current: V_CE = V_CC - I_C * R_L, where V_CC is the supply voltage and R_L is the load resistance.
