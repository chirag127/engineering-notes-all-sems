# How to play chess

- Chess is a board game that involves two players who move pieces on a square board with 64 squares of alternating colors.
- The goal of chess is to checkmate the opponent's king, which means to trap it in a position where it cannot escape from any attack.
- Each player has 16 pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights, and eight pawns.
- The pieces have different movements and abilities:

  - The king can move one square in any direction, but cannot move into check or out of the board.
  - The queen can move any number of squares in any direction, as long as the path is not blocked by another piece.
  - The rook can move any number of squares horizontally or vertically, as long as the path is not blocked by another piece.
  - The bishop can move any number of squares diagonally, as long as the path is not blocked by another piece.
  - The knight can move in an L-shape, two squares horizontally and one vertically, or vice versa, and can jump over other pieces.
  - The pawn can move one square forward, or two squares on its first move, as long as the path is not blocked by another piece. It can also capture an enemy piece by moving one square diagonally forward.

- The game starts with the white player making the first move, and then the players alternate turns.
- A player can make a special move called castling, which involves moving the king two squares towards a rook, and then moving the rook to the square the king crossed over. This move can only be done if the king and the rook have not moved before, and if there are no pieces between them, and if the king is not in check or moving through check.
- A player can also make a special move called en passant, which involves capturing an enemy pawn that has moved two squares forward on its first move, by moving a pawn to the square behind it. This move can only be done on the next move after the enemy pawn has moved.
- A player can also promote a pawn that reaches the last rank of the board, by replacing it with a queen, rook, bishop, or knight of the same color.
- A player can win the game by checkmating the opponent's king, or by the opponent resigning or running out of time.
- A game can end in a draw if:

  - There is a stalemate, which means the player whose turn it is has no legal move and is not in check.
  - There is a threefold repetition, which means the same position has occurred three times with the same player to move.
  - There is a fifty-move rule, which means no pawn has moved and no piece has been captured in the last fifty moves.
  - There is insufficient material, which means neither player has enough pieces to checkmate the opponent.
  - There is a mutual agreement, which means both players agree to end the game in a draw.
