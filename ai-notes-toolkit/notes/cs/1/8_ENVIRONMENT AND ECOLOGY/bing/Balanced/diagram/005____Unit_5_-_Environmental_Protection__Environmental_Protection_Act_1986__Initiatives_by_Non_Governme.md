## Unit 5 - Environmental Protection

### Environmental Protection Act 1986

- The Environmental Protection Act 1986 is an Act of the Parliament of India that aims to provide for the protection and improvement of the environment .
- The Act empowers the Central Government to establish authorities, issue rules and directions, and take measures to prevent, control and abate environmental pollution  .
- The Act covers various aspects of environmental protection, such as standards for emission and discharge of pollutants, hazardous substances management, environmental impact assessment, environmental audit, and penalties for non-compliance  .

### Initiatives by Non Governmental Organizations (NGO’s)

- Non Governmental Organizations (NGOs) are non-profit and citizen-led organizations that initiate environmental awareness and action among people and act as catalysts for environmental protection.
- Some of the initiatives taken by NGOs for environmental protection are  :
  - Environmental education and awareness among people
  - Environmental (air, water, soil, noise) pollution control
  - Protection of forest wealth and biodiversity
  - Afforestation and social forestry
  - Wildlife conservation and animal welfare
  - Recycling and waste utilization
  - Rural development and eco-development
  - Climate change mitigation and adaptation
  - Advocacy and lobbying for environmental policies and laws
  - Research and innovation for sustainable solutions

### Human Population and the Environment

- The human population has experienced a period of unprecedented growth, more than tripling in size since 1950. It reached almost 7.8 billion in 2020 and is projected to grow to over 8.5 billion in 2030.
- The ways in which populations are spread across Earth and the levels of consumption and waste production have an effect on the environment.
- Some of the impacts of human population on the environment are  :
  - Consumption of resources such as land, food, water, air, fossil fuels and minerals
  - Waste products as a result of consumption such as air and water pollutants, toxic materials and greenhouse gases
  - Environmental degradation such as deforestation, soil erosion, desertification, loss of biodiversity, and climate change
  - Conflicts and displacement due to resource scarcity, environmental disasters, and population pressure
- Environmental education is a process of learning and action that aims to increase awareness, knowledge, skills, attitudes, and values related to the environment and its challenges and opportunities .
- Environmental education can help to address the population-environment relationship by :
  - Promoting responsible and sustainable consumption and production patterns
  - Enhancing critical thinking and problem-solving skills for environmental issues
  - Fostering environmental values and ethics
  - Encouraging participation and empowerment for environmental action
  - Integrating population and environmental concepts and perspectives in various disciplines and levels of education
- Women education is a key factor in influencing the population-environment relationship, as it can affect the fertility, mortality, health, and empowerment of women and their families .
- Women education can contribute to environmental protection by :
  - Reducing population growth and stabilizing population size
  - Improving maternal and child health and well-being
  - Enhancing women's access to and control over resources and decision-making
  - Increasing women's participation and leadership in environmental management and governance
  - Empowering women to advocate for their rights and interests in relation to the environment
