### Broadcast Messages for the Notes of Unit 2 - Basic Structural Modeling in Object Oriented System Design

In this unit, we will be discussing the basics of structural modeling in object-oriented system design. Here are some important points to keep in mind while taking notes:

- Structural modeling is the process of creating a visual representation of the system's structure. This can be done using various diagrams, such as class diagrams, object diagrams, and package diagrams.
- Class diagrams are the most commonly used diagrams in structural modeling. They represent the classes, attributes, and methods of the system, as well as the relationships between them.
- Object diagrams, on the other hand, represent instances of classes and their relationships with each other. They can be used to test the validity of a class diagram.
- Package diagrams are used to organize classes into packages, which can then be used to manage the complexity of the system.
- When taking notes on structural modeling, it's important to pay attention to the relationships between the classes. These can be association, aggregation, composition, or inheritance.
- Association is a relationship between two classes, where one class knows about the other. Aggregation is a relationship where one class contains the other, but the contained class can exist on its own. Composition is similar to aggregation, but the contained class cannot exist on its own. Inheritance is a relationship where one class inherits the attributes and methods of another class.
- It's also important to note the visibility of the attributes and methods in each class. This can be public, private, or protected.
- Finally, while taking notes on structural modeling, it's important to practice drawing the diagrams yourself. This will help you understand the relationships between the classes better and will also help you in your exams.

We hope these points will help you in taking effective notes on structural modeling in object-oriented system design. Good luck with your studies!
