### Sequence Diagrams for the notes of the Unit 2 - Basic Structural Modeling in the subject of Object Oriented System Design

Sequence diagrams are an important tool in object-oriented system design. They provide a visual representation of the interactions between objects in a system and help to identify potential problems and bottlenecks. Here are some key points to keep in mind when working with sequence diagrams:

- A sequence diagram is a type of interaction diagram that shows the interactions between objects in a system over time.
- The vertical axis of a sequence diagram represents time, while the horizontal axis represents the objects and actors involved in the interaction.
- The different objects and actors are represented by lifelines, which show the duration of their existence during the interaction.
- Messages between objects are represented by arrows, which show the direction of the communication and the order in which the messages are sent.
- There are two types of messages in a sequence diagram: synchronous messages, which require a response before the interaction can continue, and asynchronous messages, which do not require a response and can be sent at any time.
- Sequence diagrams can also show the activation of objects and the creation and destruction of objects during the interaction.
- Sequence diagrams can be used to identify potential problems and bottlenecks in a system, such as long response times or excessive message traffic.
- When creating a sequence diagram, it is important to focus on the interactions between objects rather than the implementation details of the system.
- Sequence diagrams can be used in conjunction with other diagrams, such as class diagrams and use case diagrams, to provide a comprehensive view of a system.

By understanding the key concepts and best practices for working with sequence diagrams, you will be better equipped to design and analyze complex object-oriented systems.
