

### Principles of Modelling for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: The Meaning of Object Orientation in the Subject of Object Oriented System Design

1. Object-oriented systems design is a process of representing the behavior and structure of a system as a set of objects and their interactions.
2. Objects are the basic building blocks of object-oriented systems design, and they represent real-world entities such as people, places, or things.
3. Objects have both data and behavior, and they interact with each other by sending and receiving messages.
4. Objects are grouped into classes, which define the data and behavior that all objects of that class share.
5. Classes are organized into hierarchies and can inherit data and behavior from their parent classes.
6. Modeling is the process of creating a representation of a system and its behavior.
7. UML (Unified Modeling Language) is a standard graphical notation for modeling object-oriented systems.
8. UML diagrams can be used to represent the structure and behavior of a system, including class diagrams, object diagrams, and sequence diagrams.
9. Modeling is an iterative process, meaning that the model is constantly refined and improved as the system is developed.
