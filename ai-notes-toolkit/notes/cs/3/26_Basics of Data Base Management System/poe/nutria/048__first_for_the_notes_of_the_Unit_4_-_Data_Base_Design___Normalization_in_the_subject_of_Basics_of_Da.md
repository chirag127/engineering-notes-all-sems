
### Unit 4 - Database Design & Normalization

1. Database Design is the process of designing a database to store and retrieve data efficiently. This process involves creating a logical structure of the data, defining the relationships between the data, and normalizing the data to reduce redundancy.

2. Normalization is the process of organizing data into tables in such a way that the data is stored in the most efficient manner possible. Normalization involves breaking down data into smaller, more manageable pieces and eliminating redundant data.

3. The first step in normalization is to identify the entities and attributes that make up the data. Entities are the objects that will be stored in the database, while attributes are the characteristics of the entities.

4. Once the entities and attributes have been identified, the data must be organized into tables. This process involves creating relationships between the entities and attributes.

5. The next step in normalization is to apply the normal forms. Normal forms are rules that help to ensure that the data is stored in an efficient manner. The most commonly used normal forms are the First Normal Form (1NF), Second Normal Form (2NF), and Third Normal Form (3NF).

6. The First Normal Form (1NF) requires that all attributes of an entity are stored in a single table. This ensures that the data is stored in an efficient manner and helps to reduce data redundancy.

7. The Second Normal Form (2NF) requires that all attributes of an entity are stored in a single table and that all attributes are related to the primary key. This ensures that the data is stored in an efficient manner and helps to reduce data redundancy.

8. The Third Normal Form (3NF) requires that all attributes of an entity are stored in a single table and that all attributes are related to the primary key. Additionally, all non-key attributes must be dependent on the primary key. This ensures that the data is stored in an efficient manner and helps to reduce data redundancy.
