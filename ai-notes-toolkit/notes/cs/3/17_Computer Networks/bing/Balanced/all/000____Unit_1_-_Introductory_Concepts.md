## Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts

- This unit introduces some basic concepts and terms that are essential for understanding the nature and scope of economics.
- Economics is the social science that studies how people make choices under conditions of scarcity and uncertainty.
- Scarcity means that human wants are unlimited, but the resources available to satisfy them are limited.
- Resources are the inputs used to produce goods and services. They can be classified into four categories: land, labor, capital, and entrepreneurship.
- Goods are tangible items that can be seen and touched, such as food, clothing, and cars. Services are intangible activities that provide satisfaction, such as education, health care, and entertainment.
- A trade-off is a situation where one has to give up something in order to get something else. An opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that is forgone as a result of making a choice.
- A production possibilities frontier (PPF) is a graph that shows the maximum combinations of two goods or services that can be produced with a given amount of resources and technology.
- The PPF illustrates the concepts of scarcity, choice, opportunity cost, efficiency, and growth.
- A point on the PPF represents an efficient allocation of resources, where there is no waste or idle capacity. A point inside the PPF represents an inefficient allocation of resources, where some resources are underutilized or unemployed. A point outside the PPF represents an unattainable combination of goods or services, given the current resources and technology.
- The shape of the PPF depends on the nature of the trade-offs between the two goods or services. If the opportunity cost of producing one good or service is constant, the PPF is a straight line. If the opportunity cost of producing one good or service increases as more of it is produced, the PPF is bowed outwards (concave).
- Economic growth is the increase in the quantity and quality of goods and services that an economy can produce over time. It can be achieved by increasing the quantity or quality of resources, or by improving the technology or methods of production.
- Economic growth shifts the PPF outward, allowing more combinations of goods and services to be produced and consumed.
