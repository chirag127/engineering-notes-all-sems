# Services for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks

- A service is a set of functions or features that a layer provides to the layer above it.
- Services are classified into two types: connection-oriented and connectionless.
- Connection-oriented services require the establishment of a connection before any data can be exchanged. They provide reliable, ordered, and error-free delivery of data.
- Connectionless services do not require a connection and allow data to be sent without any prior arrangement. They provide fast, but unreliable, unordered, and error-prone delivery of data.
- Examples of connection-oriented services are TCP, FTP, and HTTP. Examples of connectionless services are UDP, IP, and DNS.
- Services are implemented by protocols, which are rules and formats that govern the communication between entities in a network.
- Protocols are defined by standards, which are documents that specify the characteristics and behavior of a service or a protocol.
- Standards are developed by organizations, such as IEEE, ISO, IETF, and W3C.
- The Internet is a network of networks that uses a common set of protocols, called the TCP/IP protocol suite, to provide communication services across different types of networks.
- The TCP/IP protocol suite consists of four layers: application, transport, network, and link.
- The application layer provides services to user applications, such as email, web browsing, and file transfer.
- The transport layer provides services to the application layer, such as reliable or unreliable data delivery, flow control, and congestion control.
- The network layer provides services to the transport layer, such as routing, addressing, and fragmentation.
- The link layer provides services to the network layer, such as framing, error detection, and medium access control.
