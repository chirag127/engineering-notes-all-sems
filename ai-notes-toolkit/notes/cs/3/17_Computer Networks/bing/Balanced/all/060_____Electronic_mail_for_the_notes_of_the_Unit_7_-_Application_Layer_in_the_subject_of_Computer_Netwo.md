# Electronic Mail

- Electronic mail (e-mail) is a computer-based application for the exchange of messages between users.
- E-mail is the electronic equivalent of a letter, but with advantages in timeliness and flexibility.
- An e-mail address is a unique identifier for each user, typically in the format of name@domain.com.
- An e-mail message consists of two parts: a header and a body.
- The header contains information such as the sender, the recipient, the subject, the date, and other optional fields.
- The body contains the actual content of the message, which can be plain text, formatted text, images, attachments, or other types of data.
- E-mail messages are sent and received using e-mail protocols, such as Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP), Post Office Protocol (POP), and Internet Message Access Protocol (IMAP).
- SMTP is used to send e-mail messages from a client to a server, or from one server to another.
- POP and IMAP are used to retrieve e-mail messages from a server to a client.
- POP downloads the messages to the client and deletes them from the server, while IMAP synchronizes the messages between the client and the server.
- E-mail messages are stored in mailboxes, which are folders or directories that organize the messages by sender, recipient, subject, date, or other criteria.
- E-mail messages can be encrypted, signed, or verified using cryptographic techniques, such as Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity of the messages.
- E-mail messages can also be filtered, sorted, searched, or processed using various tools, such as spam filters, rules, folders, labels, or flags.
- E-mail is one of the most widely used applications of the Internet, and has many benefits, such as speed, convenience, cost-effectiveness, and global reach.
- E-mail also has some drawbacks, such as security risks, privacy issues, spam, phishing, malware, and information overload.
- E-mail is thus a method of exchanging messages (\"mail\") between people using electronic devices.
