# Signal transmission and encoding

- Signal transmission is the process of sending and receiving data over a physical medium, such as a wire, a fiber optic cable, or a wireless channel.
- Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted by a signal, such as a voltage level, a light pulse, or a radio wave.
- The physical layer of the computer network is responsible for generating, encoding, transmitting, receiving, and decoding signals.
- The physical layer must also deal with issues such as synchronization, error detection, modulation, multiplexing, and medium access control.

## Types of signals

- Signals can be classified into two types: analog and digital.
- Analog signals are continuous and vary smoothly over time, such as sound waves or light waves.
- Digital signals are discrete and have only two values, such as 0 or 1, representing bits of data.
- Analog signals can carry analog or digital data, while digital signals can only carry digital data.

## Types of encoding

- Encoding can be classified into two types: analog-to-analog and digital-to-digital.
- Analog-to-analog encoding is the process of converting an analog data signal into an analog signal that can be transmitted over an analog medium, such as a telephone line or a radio channel.
- Examples of analog-to-analog encoding are amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM), and phase modulation (PM).
- Digital-to-digital encoding is the process of converting a digital data signal into a digital signal that can be transmitted over a digital medium, such as a wire or a fiber optic cable.
- Examples of digital-to-digital encoding are unipolar, polar, bipolar, and Manchester encoding.

## Factors affecting signal transmission and encoding

- Signal transmission and encoding are affected by various factors, such as bandwidth, noise, attenuation, distortion, and interference.
- Bandwidth is the range of frequencies that a signal can occupy or a medium can support. It determines the data rate and the quality of the signal transmission.
- Noise is any unwanted signal that interferes with the original signal and reduces its quality. Noise can be caused by external sources, such as other signals or devices, or internal sources, such as thermal or electrical fluctuations.
- Attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it travels over a medium. Attenuation depends on the distance, the medium, and the frequency of the signal. Attenuation can be compensated by using amplifiers or repeaters.
- Distortion is the change in the shape or form of the signal as it travels over a medium. Distortion can be caused by the non-linear characteristics of the medium or the signal, such as harmonics or intermodulation.
- Interference is the overlapping or mixing of signals from different sources that share the same medium or frequency. Interference can be constructive or destructive, depending on the phase and amplitude of the signals. Interference can be reduced by using filters or shielding.
