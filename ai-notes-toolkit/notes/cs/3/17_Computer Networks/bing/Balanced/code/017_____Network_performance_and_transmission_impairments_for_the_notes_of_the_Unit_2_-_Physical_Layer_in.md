# Network performance and transmission impairments

- Network performance is the measure of how well a network can deliver data and services to its users.
- Network performance can be affected by various factors, such as bandwidth, latency, jitter, packet loss, throughput, availability, reliability, and security.
- Transmission impairments are the damage or harm caused to the signal during the signal transmission. They can degrade the quality and integrity of the data and affect the network performance.
- There are various kinds of transmission impairments in the network, such as noise, attenuation, distortion, and interference.

## Noise
- Noise is any unwanted or random signal that interferes with the original signal and reduces its signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).
- There are several types of noise, such as induced noise, crosstalk noise, thermal noise, and impulse noise, which may corrupt the signal.
- Induced noise comes from sources such as motors and appliances. These devices act as sending antenna and transmission medium act as receiving antenna.
- Crosstalk noise is the interference caused by signals from adjacent wires or channels in the same transmission medium.
- Thermal noise is the random fluctuation of electrons in the transmission medium due to thermal agitation. It depends on the temperature and resistance of the medium.
- Impulse noise is a sudden and short-duration spike in the signal, caused by external events such as lightning, power surges, or switching circuits.

## Attenuation
- Attenuation is the loss of signal strength or power as it travels through the transmission medium. It is measured in decibels (dB).
- Attenuation depends on the distance, frequency, and characteristics of the transmission medium. The longer the distance, the higher the frequency, and the more resistive or absorptive the medium, the more attenuation occurs.
- Attenuation can be compensated by using amplifiers or repeaters, which boost the signal strength at regular intervals along the transmission path.

## Distortion
- Distortion is the alteration or change in the shape or form of the signal as it travels through the transmission medium. It can affect the amplitude, phase, or frequency of the signal.
- Distortion can be caused by various factors, such as non-linearities in the transmission medium or devices, multipath propagation, or dispersion.
- Non-linearities can result in harmonic distortion, intermodulation distortion, or clipping of the signal. They occur when the transmission medium or devices do not respond linearly to the input signal, creating new frequencies or altering the existing ones.
- Multipath propagation is the phenomenon where the signal travels along different paths to reach the destination, due to reflection, refraction, or scattering of the signal by obstacles or atmospheric conditions. This can cause delay distortion, phase distortion, or fading of the signal.
- Dispersion is the spreading or separation of the signal components based on their frequency or wavelength. It can occur in both wired and wireless transmission media. It can cause frequency distortion or inter-symbol interference (ISI) of the signal.

## Interference
- Interference is the unwanted or undesired signal that affects the original signal and reduces its signal-to-interference ratio (SIR).
- There are two types of interference, namely co-channel interference and adjacent channel interference.
- Co-channel interference is the interference caused by signals from the same frequency or channel in the same transmission medium. It can occur due to frequency reuse, multiple access, or overlapping channels.
- Adjacent channel interference is the interference caused by signals from nearby frequencies or channels in the same transmission medium. It can occur due to imperfect filtering, channel spacing, or bandwidth allocation.
