### Categories of networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications. Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the transmission medium, the network size, the topology, and organizational intent. Based on a geographical scale, the different types of networks are:

- **Personal area network (PAN)**: A PAN is the smallest and simplest type of network. PANs connect devices within the range of an individual and are no larger than about 10 meters (m). Examples of PANs are wireless headphones, Bluetooth speakers, smartwatches, and smartphones.
- **Local area network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited area, such as a home, an office, or a building. LANs are usually owned and managed by a single organization or individual. LANs can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth. LANs enable fast and secure data transfer, resource sharing, and communication among the devices .
- **Wireless local area network (WLAN)**: A WLAN is a type of LAN that uses wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, to connect devices. WLANs provide mobility, flexibility, and scalability to the users. WLANs can be standalone or connected to a wired LAN. WLANs are subject to interference, security risks, and performance issues due to the wireless medium.
- **Campus area network (CAN)**: A CAN is a network that connects devices within a campus, such as a university, a school, or a business park. A CAN can span several buildings and can use wired or wireless technologies. A CAN is usually owned and managed by the campus administration. A CAN can provide high-speed data transfer, internet access, and network services to the campus users.
- **Metropolitan area network (MAN)**: A MAN is a network that connects devices within a metropolitan area, such as a city or a town. A MAN can span several kilometers and can use wired or wireless technologies, such as fiber-optic cables, microwave links, or cellular networks. A MAN is usually owned and operated by a public or private entity, such as a local government, a cable TV provider, or a telecom company. A MAN can provide broadband access, voice and video services, and public safety communications to the metropolitan users .
- **Wide area network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices across a large geographical area, such as a country or a continent. A WAN can span thousands of kilometers and can use wired or wireless technologies, such as copper wires, satellite links, or cellular networks. A WAN is usually owned and operated by multiple entities, such as internet service providers, telecom companies, or governments. A WAN can provide global access, data transmission, and network services to the wide area users .
- **Virtual private network (VPN)**: A VPN is a network that creates a secure and encrypted connection between devices over a public network, such as the internet. A VPN can use various protocols, such as IPsec, SSL, or PPTP, to establish the connection. A VPN can provide privacy, security, and anonymity to the users. A VPN can also enable remote access, bypass geo-restrictions, and enhance network performance .
