### LAN standards

A local area network (LAN) is a data communication network connecting various terminals or computers within a building or limited geographical area. The connection among the devices could be wired or wireless . LAN standards are the specifications that define the physical and logical characteristics of a LAN, such as the transmission medium, the data rate, the access method, the addressing scheme, the error control, and the security. Some of the most common LAN standards are:

- **Ethernet**: It is the most widely used LAN standard, based on the IEEE 802.3 specification. It uses a bus or star topology and supports data rates of 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, and 10 Gbps. It uses the carrier sense multiple access with collision detection (CSMA/CD) protocol to control the access to the shared medium  .
- **Token Ring**: It is a LAN standard based on the IEEE 802.5 specification. It uses a ring topology and supports data rates of 4 Mbps and 16 Mbps. It uses a token-passing protocol to control the access to the shared medium. A token is a special frame that circulates around the ring and grants the permission to transmit to the node that holds it  .
- **Wireless LAN**: It is a LAN standard based on the IEEE 802.11 family of specifications. It uses radio waves or infrared signals to transmit data between wireless devices. It supports data rates of up to 600 Mbps, depending on the frequency band, the modulation scheme, and the number of spatial streams. It uses the carrier sense multiple access with collision avoidance (CSMA/CA) protocol to control the access to the shared medium  .
- **Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI)**: It is a LAN standard that uses optical fiber as the transmission medium. It supports data rates of 100 Mbps and uses a dual ring topology for redundancy and reliability. It uses a token-passing protocol to control the access to the shared medium .
