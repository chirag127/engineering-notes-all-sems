### Network topology design

- Network topology design is the process of creating a physical, virtual, and logical arrangement of infrastructure in an IT network.
- Network topology design helps to optimize the performance, security, scalability, and reliability of the network.
- Network topology design involves the following steps:
  - Analyzing the business requirements and objectives of the network
  - Identifying the network devices, nodes, and connections that are needed to meet the requirements
  - Choosing the best network topology type for the network, such as ring, chain, tree, or mesh
  - Creating a network diagram or a topology map that shows the placement and interconnection of the network elements
  - Implementing, testing, and monitoring the network design
- Network topology design can be classified into two major categories: physical and logical.
  - Physical topology design refers to the actual layout of the network devices, nodes, and connections in a specific location.
  - Logical topology design refers to the way data flows and communicates across the network, regardless of the physical layout.
- Network topology design can also be classified into various types based on the shape and structure of the network, such as:
  - Bus topology: A linear network where all nodes are connected to a single cable or backbone.
  - Star topology: A network where all nodes are connected to a central hub or switch.
  - Ring topology: A circular network where each node is connected to two other nodes, forming a closed loop.
  - Tree topology: A hierarchical network where nodes are arranged in a tree-like structure, with a root node at the top and branches at the lower levels.
  - Mesh topology: A network where each node is connected to every other node, forming a complete or partial mesh.
  - Hybrid topology: A network that combines two or more of the above topology types.
