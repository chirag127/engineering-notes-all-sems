### ISP

- ISP stands for **Internet Service Provider**    .
- An ISP is a company that provides **access to the internet** to both personal and business customers   .
- An ISP gives you an **internet account**, an **e-mail address**, and **web space** for a web page for a fee.
- An ISP also provides **software packages** (such as browsers), **hosting services** for websites, and **technical support** for its customers .
- An ISP uses special **networking, telecommunications, and routing equipment** to connect its customers to the internet.
- An ISP may also offer other services such as **voice over IP (VoIP)**, **online gaming**, **streaming media**, **cloud computing**, and **security** .
- An ISP can be classified into different types based on the **technology** they use to provide internet access, such as **dial-up**, **DSL**, **cable**, **fiber-optic**, **wireless**, or **satellite**  .
- An ISP can also be classified into different levels based on the **scope** of their network and the **customers** they serve, such as **tier 1**, **tier 2**, or **tier 3** .
- A **tier 1 ISP** is a large ISP that has **global coverage** and can exchange traffic with other tier 1 ISPs without paying fees. Examples of tier 1 ISPs are **AT&T**, **Verizon**, and **Level 3** .
- A **tier 2 ISP** is a medium-sized ISP that has **regional coverage** and can exchange traffic with some tier 1 ISPs and other tier 2 ISPs, but may have to pay fees to access some networks. Examples of tier 2 ISPs are **Comcast**, **Sprint**, and **Cogent** .
- A **tier 3 ISP** is a small ISP that has **local coverage** and can only exchange traffic with other ISPs by paying fees. Examples of tier 3 ISPs are **local cable companies**, **wireless providers**, and **resellers** .
- An ISP can also be classified into different categories based on the **services** they offer, such as **access provider**, **hosting provider**, **transit provider**, or **content provider**.
- An **access provider** is an ISP that provides internet access to end users or subscribers, such as **home users**, **small businesses**, or **mobile users** .
- A **hosting provider** is an ISP that provides web hosting services to customers who want to create and maintain their own websites, such as **domain name registration**, **web design**, **web space**, and **e-mail accounts** .
- A **transit provider** is an ISP that provides internet access to other ISPs, such as **tier 1 ISPs** or **tier 2 ISPs**, by leasing or selling bandwidth and routing services.
- A **content provider** is an ISP that provides online content to end users or subscribers, such as **news**, **entertainment**, **education**, or **social media**.
