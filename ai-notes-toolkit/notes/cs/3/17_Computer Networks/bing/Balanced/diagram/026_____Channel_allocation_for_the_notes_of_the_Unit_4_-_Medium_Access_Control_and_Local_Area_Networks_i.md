### Channel allocation

- Channel allocation is a process in which a single channel is divided and allotted to multiple users in order to carry user specific tasks.
- Channel allocation techniques are used to manage the access of multiple users to a shared network and to avoid interference and collisions.
- Channel allocation can be classified into three main categories: static, dynamic and hybrid.

### Static channel allocation

- Static channel allocation is a strategy in which fixed number of channels or voice frequencies are assigned to each cell.
- Static channel allocation is also known as fixed channel allocation or frequency division multiple access (FDMA).
- Static channel allocation is suitable for networks with low or constant traffic demand, such as local area networks (LANs) and metropolitan area networks (MANs).
- Static channel allocation has some advantages and disadvantages:

  - Advantages:
    - Simple and easy to implement
    - No overhead or delay for channel assignment
    - No interference or collision between users
  - Disadvantages:
    - Inefficient use of channel resources
    - Wastage of bandwidth when some channels are idle
    - Difficulty in accommodating variable traffic demand

### Dynamic channel allocation

- Dynamic channel allocation is a strategy in which channels are not permanently assigned to any cell, but are allocated on demand according to the traffic conditions.
- Dynamic channel allocation is also known as demand assigned multiple access (DAMA) or channel borrowing.
- Dynamic channel allocation is suitable for networks with high or variable traffic demand, such as wireless networks and mobile networks.
- Dynamic channel allocation has some advantages and disadvantages:

  - Advantages:
    - Efficient use of channel resources
    - Adaptability to changing traffic demand
    - Reduction of wastage of bandwidth
  - Disadvantages:
    - Complex and costly to implement
    - Overhead and delay for channel assignment
    - Possibility of interference or collision between users

### Hybrid channel allocation

- Hybrid channel allocation is a strategy that combines the features of both static and dynamic channel allocation.
- Hybrid channel allocation is also known as frequency hopping multiple access (FHMA) or code division multiple access (CDMA).
- Hybrid channel allocation is suitable for networks that require both high efficiency and high reliability, such as military and satellite networks.
- Hybrid channel allocation has some advantages and disadvantages:

  - Advantages:
    - Flexibility and scalability of channel allocation
    - Robustness and security of communication
    - Diversity and multiplexing of signals
  - Disadvantages:
    - High complexity and cost of implementation
    - High overhead and delay for channel assignment
    - High interference and noise level
