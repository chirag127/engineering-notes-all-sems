### Transmission media

Transmission media are the physical channels that carry data from one device to another in a computer network. They can be classified into two main types: guided media and unguided media.

- Guided media: These are the media that use physical wires or cables to transmit data. The signals are confined and directed along the wires. Examples of guided media are twisted pair, coaxial cable, and optical fiber.
- Unguided media: These are the media that use wireless transmission methods such as radio waves, microwaves, infrared, and light. The signals are not confined and can spread in any direction. Examples of unguided media are radio broadcast, satellite, and cellular networks.

Some characteristics of transmission media are:

- Bandwidth: The amount of data that can be transmitted per unit time over the media. Higher bandwidth means higher data rate and faster communication.
- Attenuation: The loss of signal strength as it travels over the media. Higher attenuation means lower signal quality and more errors.
- Noise: The unwanted interference or distortion of the signal by external factors such as electromagnetic interference, thermal noise, or crosstalk. Higher noise means lower signal-to-noise ratio and more errors.
- Cost: The expense of installing, maintaining, and operating the media. Lower cost means higher affordability and feasibility.

Some factors that affect the choice of transmission media are:

- Distance: The length of the communication link between the sender and receiver. Longer distance means higher attenuation and lower bandwidth.
- Environment: The physical and electromagnetic conditions of the communication area. Harsh environment means higher noise and lower signal quality.
- Security: The degree of protection against unauthorized access or eavesdropping. Higher security means lower vulnerability and higher confidentiality.
- Performance: The efficiency and reliability of the communication process. Higher performance means lower delay, lower error rate, and higher throughput.
