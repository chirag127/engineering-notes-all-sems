### Goals and applications of networks

A network is a system of two or more devices (such as computers, printers, scanners, etc.) that are connected by some medium (such as cables, wireless, satellites, etc.) and can communicate and share resources with each other  .

Some of the main goals of networks are  :

- **Resource sharing**: This means to make all programs, data and equipment available to anyone on the network regardless of the physical location of the resource and the user. For example, a group of office workers can share a common printer, fax, modem, scanner, etc. Resource sharing also enables cost reduction, as fewer devices are needed to serve multiple users.
- **High reliability**: This means to provide alternative sources of supply in case of failure or damage of any component. For example, if one server goes down, another server can take over its tasks. High reliability also improves performance, as the load can be distributed among multiple devices.
- **Scalability**: This means to allow the network to grow and accommodate new devices and users without affecting the existing ones. For example, a network can be expanded by adding more switches, routers, cables, etc. Scalability also enhances flexibility, as the network can be reconfigured or upgraded as needed.
- **Security**: This means to protect the network and its resources from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction. For example, a network can use encryption, authentication, firewalls, etc. to secure the data and devices. Security also ensures privacy, as the network can prevent eavesdropping or snooping of the communication.

Some of the main applications of networks are :

- **Communication**: This means to enable the exchange of information and messages among users and devices on the network. For example, the internet, email, chat, voice over IP, video conferencing, etc. are all forms of communication that rely on networks. Communication also fosters collaboration, as the network can support teamwork and coordination among users.
- **Entertainment**: This means to provide access to various forms of media and content that can be enjoyed by the users on the network. For example, online gaming, streaming, social media, etc. are all forms of entertainment that depend on networks. Entertainment also stimulates creativity, as the network can enable the creation and sharing of user-generated content.
- **Research**: This means to facilitate the collection, analysis, and dissemination of data and knowledge that can be used for scientific or academic purposes. For example, online search, databases, cloud computing, etc. are all forms of research that utilize networks. Research also advances innovation, as the network can enable the discovery and development of new ideas and solutions.
