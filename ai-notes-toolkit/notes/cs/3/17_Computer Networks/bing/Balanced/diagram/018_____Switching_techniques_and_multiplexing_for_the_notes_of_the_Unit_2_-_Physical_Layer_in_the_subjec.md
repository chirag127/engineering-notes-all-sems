### Switching techniques and multiplexing

Switching techniques and multiplexing are two important concepts in computer networks that enable efficient and reliable data transmission over shared communication links.

#### Switching techniques

Switching techniques are methods of connecting multiple devices in a network and allowing them to communicate with each other by establishing and releasing connections as needed. There are three main types of switching techniques: circuit switching, message switching, and packet switching.

- Circuit switching: In circuit switching, two nodes communicate with each other over a dedicated communication path. The path is established before the data transmission begins and remains active until the communication is terminated. Circuit switching is suitable for real-time and continuous data streams, such as voice and video, but it may waste bandwidth if the path is idle or underutilized. An example of circuit switching is the traditional telephone network.
- Message switching: In message switching, the whole message is treated as a data unit and stored and forwarded by intermediate nodes until it reaches the destination. Message switching does not require a dedicated path, but it may introduce delays and require large storage capacity at the nodes. Message switching is suitable for non-real-time and bursty data, such as email and file transfer. An example of message switching is the store-and-forward network.
- Packet switching: The packet switching technique is derived from message switching where the message is broken down into smaller chunks called packets. Each packet has a header that contains the source and destination addresses and other information. The packets are routed independently by the nodes based on the network conditions and reassembled at the destination. Packet switching does not require a dedicated path, but it may introduce errors and congestion if the network is overloaded. Packet switching is suitable for both real-time and non-real-time data, such as voice over IP and web browsing. An example of packet switching is the internet.

#### Multiplexing

Multiplexing is a method by which multiple analog or digital signals are combined into one signal over a shared medium. Multiplexing allows the efficient utilization of bandwidth and reduces the cost of transmission. There are different types of multiplexing techniques, such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), and statistical multiplexing.

- Frequency division multiplexing: In FDM, the frequency spectrum of the shared medium is divided into several non-overlapping frequency bands, and each signal is modulated by a different carrier frequency. FDM allows the simultaneous transmission of multiple signals over a single channel, but it may introduce interference and crosstalk if the frequency bands are not separated properly. An example of FDM is the radio broadcast network.
- Time division multiplexing: In TDM, the time axis of the shared medium is divided into several time slots, and each signal is assigned a different time slot. TDM allows the sequential transmission of multiple signals over a single channel, but it may introduce synchronization and latency issues if the time slots are not allocated properly. An example of TDM is the digital telephone network.
- Statistical multiplexing: Statistical multiplexing is a communication link sharing technique, which is used in packet switching. The shared linking is variable in statistical multiplexing, whereas it is fixed in TDM or FDM. This is a strategic application for maximizing the utilization of bandwidth. This can increase the efficiency of network, as well. Statistical multiplexing does not assign a fixed time slot or frequency band to each signal, but rather allocates the channel resources dynamically based on the demand and availability. Statistical multiplexing allows the flexible transmission of multiple signals over a single channel, but it may introduce variability and unpredictability in the performance. An example of statistical multiplexing is the internet.
