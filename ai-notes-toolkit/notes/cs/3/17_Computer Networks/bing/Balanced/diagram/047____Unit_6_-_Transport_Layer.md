## Unit 6 - Transport Layer

The transport layer is the fourth layer of the OSI model. It is responsible for the end-to-end delivery of data between two processes (running applications) on different computers. The transport layer performs the following functions and services:

- **Segmentation and reassembly**: The transport layer divides the data received from the application layer into smaller units called segments, and adds a header to each segment with information such as source and destination port numbers, sequence numbers, and checksums. The transport layer at the destination reassembles the segments into the original data and delivers it to the application layer.
- **Connection-oriented and connectionless communication**: The transport layer can use different protocols to establish a reliable or unreliable communication between two processes. The most common protocols are TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) and UDP (User Datagram Protocol). TCP is a connection-oriented protocol that uses a three-way handshake to establish a connection, and ensures reliable and ordered delivery of data with error detection and correction, flow control, and congestion control mechanisms. UDP is a connectionless protocol that does not establish a connection, and does not guarantee reliable or ordered delivery of data, but is faster and more efficient for applications that can tolerate some data loss or delay, such as streaming media or online gaming.
- **Multiplexing and demultiplexing**: The transport layer can use port numbers to identify different processes or applications on the same computer, and to direct the data to the correct process or application. This is called multiplexing at the source and demultiplexing at the destination. Port numbers are 16-bit numbers that range from 0 to 65535, and are divided into three categories: well-known ports (0 to 1023), registered ports (1024 to 49151), and dynamic or private ports (49152 to 65535).
- **Quality of service (QoS)**: The transport layer can provide different levels of service to different applications or processes, depending on their requirements and preferences. QoS parameters include bandwidth, delay, jitter, and packet loss. Some transport layer protocols, such as DCCP (Datagram Congestion Control Protocol) and SCTP (Stream Control Transmission Protocol), can support QoS features such as congestion control, stream management, and message-oriented communication.
