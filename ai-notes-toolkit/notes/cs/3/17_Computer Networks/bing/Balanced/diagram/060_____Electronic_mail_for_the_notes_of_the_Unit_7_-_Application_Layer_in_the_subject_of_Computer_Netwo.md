### Electronic mail

- Electronic mail (e-mail) is a computer-based application for the exchange of messages between users.
- E-mail is the electronic equivalent of a letter, but with advantages in timeliness and flexibility.
- An e-mail address is a unique identifier for each user, typically in the format of name@domain.com.
- An e-mail message consists of two parts: a header and a body.
- The header contains information such as the sender, the recipient, the subject, the date, and other optional fields.
- The body contains the actual content of the message, which can be text, images, attachments, or other formats.
- E-mail messages are sent and received by using e-mail clients, which are software applications that allow users to compose, send, receive, and manage e-mail messages.
- E-mail clients communicate with e-mail servers, which are computers that store and process e-mail messages.
- E-mail servers use protocols such as Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP), Post Office Protocol (POP), and Internet Message Access Protocol (IMAP) to exchange e-mail messages between them.
- E-mail is one of the most widely used applications of the Internet, and has many benefits such as speed, convenience, cost-effectiveness, and global reach.
- E-mail also has some challenges such as security, privacy, spam, phishing, and etiquette.
- E-mail is thus a method of exchanging messages ("mail") between people using electronic devices.
