### Logical addressing for the notes of the Unit 5 - Network Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

- Logical addressing is a scheme of addressing that allows computers to communicate over a network using internet protocol (IP) addresses.
- Logical addresses are software-based addresses that are assigned to each network interface of a host.
- Logical addresses are different from physical addresses, which are hardware-based addresses that identify the actual location of a device on the network.
- Logical addresses are used to identify the source and destination of a packet at the network layer, while physical addresses are used to identify the source and destination of a frame at the data link layer.
- Logical addresses are independent of the underlying network topology and can be changed or translated by routers or other devices.
- Logical addresses are hierarchical, meaning they consist of two parts: a network identifier and a host identifier.
- Logical addresses can be classified into different versions, such as IPv4 and IPv6, depending on the format and size of the address.
- Logical addresses can be assigned to hosts statically or dynamically, using methods such as manual configuration, DHCP, or autoconfiguration.
- Logical addresses can be represented in different notations, such as binary, decimal, hexadecimal, or dotted-decimal.
- Logical addresses can be divided into different classes, such as A, B, C, D, or E, depending on the range and purpose of the address.
