### Network performance and transmission impairments

- Network performance is the measure of how well a network can deliver data and services to its users. It can be evaluated by various metrics, such as throughput, delay, jitter, packet loss, availability, reliability, etc.
- Transmission impairments are the factors that degrade the quality of a signal during its transmission over a medium. They can cause errors, distortions, or losses in the data communication. Some common types of transmission impairments are:
  - Attenuation: The gradual loss of signal strength as it travels over a distance. It depends on the frequency, distance, and medium of the signal. It can be compensated by using amplifiers or repeaters.
  - Distortion: The alteration of the shape or form of the signal due to the different propagation speeds of different frequency components. It can cause inter-symbol interference (ISI) or inter-channel interference (ICI) in the signal. It can be reduced by using equalizers or filters.
  - Noise: The unwanted or random signals that interfere with the original signal and cause errors or fluctuations in the data. There are several types of noise, such as thermal noise, induced noise, crosstalk noise, impulse noise, etc. It can be minimized by using shielding, modulation, error detection and correction, etc.
  - Delay: The amount of time it takes for a signal to travel from the sender to the receiver. It consists of two components: propagation delay and transmission delay. Propagation delay is the time required for a signal to travel over a medium, and transmission delay is the time required for a signal to be transmitted over a link. Delay can affect the performance of real-time applications, such as voice or video.
  - Jitter: The variation in the delay of the signal due to the congestion, routing, or buffering in the network. It can cause the signal to arrive out of order, or with different inter-arrival times. Jitter can degrade the quality of voice or video applications, and can be reduced by using synchronization, buffering, or smoothing techniques.
  - Packet loss: The loss or discarding of packets due to the errors, congestion, or failures in the network. It can cause the data to be incomplete, corrupted, or delayed. Packet loss can affect the throughput and reliability of the network, and can be recovered by using retransmission, acknowledgment, or error correction schemes.
