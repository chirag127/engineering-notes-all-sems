### Channel allocation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks in the subject of Computer Networks

- Channel allocation is a process in which a single channel is divided and allotted to multiple users in order to carry user specific tasks.
- Channel allocation techniques are used to manage the access of multiple users to a shared network and to avoid collisions and interference.
- There are three main types of channel allocation techniques: static, dynamic and hybrid.
- Static channel allocation is a technique in which a fixed number of channels or frequency bands are assigned to each user or station in advance, regardless of the traffic or demand .
  - Static channel allocation is suitable for LANs and MANs, where the number of users and the traffic pattern are relatively stable and predictable.
  - Static channel allocation can be implemented using frequency division multiple access (FDMA) or time division multiple access (TDMA) schemes.
  - Static channel allocation has the advantages of simplicity, low overhead and guaranteed quality of service, but it also has the disadvantages of low efficiency, poor scalability and waste of resources.
- Dynamic channel allocation is a technique in which channels or frequency bands are allocated to users or stations on demand, based on the current traffic or demand .
  - Dynamic channel allocation is suitable for wireless networks, where the number of users and the traffic pattern are highly variable and unpredictable.
  - Dynamic channel allocation can be implemented using demand assigned multiple access (DAMA) or packet radio schemes.
  - Dynamic channel allocation has the advantages of high efficiency, high scalability and optimal resource utilization, but it also has the disadvantages of complexity, high overhead and variable quality of service.
- Hybrid channel allocation is a technique that combines the features of static and dynamic channel allocation, and tries to achieve a balance between efficiency and quality of service.
  - Hybrid channel allocation is suitable for cellular networks, where the number of users and the traffic pattern are moderately variable and predictable.
  - Hybrid channel allocation can be implemented using frequency hopping multiple access (FHMA) or code division multiple access (CDMA) schemes.
  - Hybrid channel allocation has the advantages of flexibility, adaptability and robustness, but it also has the disadvantages of increased complexity and interference.
