### LAN standards

- A local area network (LAN) is a data communication network connecting various terminals or computers within a building or limited geographical area.
- The connection among the devices could be wired or wireless.
- LAN standards are defined by the IEEE 802 family of specifications, which cover different aspects of LAN technologies.
- Some of the most common LAN standards are:

  - IEEE 802.3: Ethernet, which is the dominant LAN technology that uses a bus or star topology and supports data transfer rates of 10 Mbps to 100 Gbps .
  - IEEE 802.5: Token Ring, which is a LAN technology that uses a ring topology and a token-passing mechanism to regulate access to the shared medium. It supports data transfer rates of 4 Mbps or 16 Mbps .
  - IEEE 802.11: Wireless LAN (WLAN), which is a LAN technology that uses radio waves or infrared signals to communicate among devices. It supports data transfer rates of up to 600 Mbps and has different variants such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, and 802.11ac .
  - IEEE 802.15: Wireless Personal Area Network (WPAN), which is a LAN technology that enables short-range wireless communication among devices such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, and peripherals. It includes standards such as Bluetooth, ZigBee, and NFC.
  - IEEE 802.16: Wireless Metropolitan Area Network (WMAN), which is a LAN technology that provides broadband wireless access to a large geographical area. It is also known as WiMAX.

- LAN standards are important for ensuring interoperability, compatibility, and reliability among different devices and vendors in a network.
