## Unit 2 - Physical Layer

- The physical layer is the lowest layer of the OSI model, which deals with the transmission and reception of raw bits over a physical medium.
- The physical layer is responsible for converting data into signals, encoding and modulating signals, synchronizing bits, multiplexing and demultiplexing channels, detecting and correcting errors, and regulating the flow of data.
- The physical layer can use different types of physical media, such as copper wires, optical fibers, radio waves, or infrared light, to transmit signals. Each medium has its own characteristics, such as bandwidth, attenuation, noise, and interference, that affect the quality and speed of data transmission.
- The physical layer can use different types of transmission modes, such as simplex, half-duplex, or full-duplex, to determine the direction of data flow. Simplex mode allows data to flow in one direction only, half-duplex mode allows data to flow in both directions, but not at the same time, and full-duplex mode allows data to flow in both directions simultaneously.
- The physical layer can use different types of encoding schemes, such as NRZ, RZ, Manchester, or 4B/5B, to represent bits as voltage levels or pulses. Encoding schemes can affect the efficiency, reliability, and synchronization of data transmission.
- The physical layer can use different types of modulation techniques, such as amplitude modulation, frequency modulation, or phase modulation, to vary the properties of a carrier signal according to the data signal. Modulation techniques can increase the bandwidth efficiency, security, and robustness of data transmission.
- The physical layer can use different types of multiplexing techniques, such as frequency division multiplexing, time division multiplexing, or wavelength division multiplexing, to combine multiple signals into one signal over a shared medium. Multiplexing techniques can increase the utilization, capacity, and flexibility of data transmission.
- The physical layer can use different types of error detection and correction methods, such as parity check, checksum, or cyclic redundancy check, to identify and correct errors in data transmission. Error detection and correction methods can improve the accuracy, reliability, and performance of data transmission.
- The physical layer can use different types of flow control methods, such as stop-and-wait, sliding window, or selective repeat, to regulate the rate of data transmission between the sender and the receiver. Flow control methods can prevent data loss, congestion, and deadlock in data transmission.
