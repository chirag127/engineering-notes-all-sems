### Signal transmission and encoding

- Signal transmission is the process of sending data over a physical medium, such as a cable or the airwaves, using electric or electromagnetic impulses .
- Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted and understood by the sender and the receiver .
- Encoding can be classified into two types: digital-to-digital encoding and analog-to-digital encoding.
- Digital-to-digital encoding is the representation of digital information (binary 1s and 0s) by a digital signal, such as a series of voltage pulses . This process is also known as line coding.
- Analog-to-digital encoding is the representation of analog information (continuous signals) by a digital signal, such as a sequence of bits. This process is also known as modulation.
- Some of the factors that affect the choice of encoding technique are bandwidth, bit rate, bit error rate, synchronization, and complexity .
- Some of the common encoding techniques are:
  - Non-return-to-zero (NRZ): A binary 1 is represented by a high voltage level and a binary 0 is represented by a low voltage level .
  - Return-to-zero (RZ): A binary 1 is represented by a high voltage level for half of the bit duration and a zero voltage level for the other half. A binary 0 is represented by a zero voltage level for the entire bit duration .
  - Manchester: A binary 1 is represented by a low-to-high transition in the middle of the bit duration and a binary 0 is represented by a high-to-low transition in the middle of the bit duration .
  - Differential Manchester: A binary 1 is represented by the absence of a transition in the middle of the bit duration and a binary 0 is represented by the presence of a transition in the middle of the bit duration. There is also a transition at the beginning of each bit duration .
  - Amplitude-shift keying (ASK): A binary 1 is represented by a high amplitude signal and a binary 0 is represented by a low amplitude signal.
  - Frequency-shift keying (FSK): A binary 1 is represented by a high frequency signal and a binary 0 is represented by a low frequency signal.
  - Phase-shift keying (PSK): A binary 1 is represented by a phase shift of 180 degrees and a binary 0 is represented by no phase shift.
