### Services for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks

- A service is a set of functions or capabilities that a network provides to its users or applications.
- Services can be classified into two categories: connection-oriented and connectionless.
- Connection-oriented services require the establishment of a logical connection between the sender and the receiver before any data can be transferred. An example of a connection-oriented service is the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP).
- Connectionless services do not require a prior connection and allow the sender to transmit data to the receiver without any guarantee of delivery or ordering. An example of a connectionless service is the User Datagram Protocol (UDP).
- Services can also be classified based on the level of abstraction they provide to the applications. For example, some services may provide a reliable byte-stream service, while others may provide a message-oriented service or a packet-oriented service.
- Services can be implemented at different layers of the network architecture, such as the application layer, the transport layer, the network layer, or the link layer. Each layer provides a service to the layer above it by using the services of the layer below it.
- Services can be characterized by various parameters, such as throughput, delay, jitter, reliability, security, etc. These parameters affect the quality of service (QoS) that the network can offer to the applications.
- Services can be designed to meet the specific requirements of different types of applications, such as real-time, interactive, or bulk data transfer. For example, a real-time application may need a low-delay and low-jitter service, while a bulk data transfer application may need a high-throughput and reliable service.
