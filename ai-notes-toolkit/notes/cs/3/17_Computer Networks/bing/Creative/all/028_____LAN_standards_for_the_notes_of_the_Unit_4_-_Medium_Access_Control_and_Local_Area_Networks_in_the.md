# LAN standards

A local area network (LAN) is a data communication network connecting various terminals or computers within a building or limited geographical area. The connection among the devices could be wired or wireless. LANs enable the sharing of resources, such as files, printers, applications, and internet access, among the connected devices.

The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) has defined a series of standards for LAN technologies, known as IEEE 802. These standards specify the physical and data link layer protocols for different types of LANs, such as Ethernet, Token Ring, Wireless LAN, etc .

Some of the most common LAN standards are:

- **IEEE 802.3**: This standard defines the Ethernet technology, which is the most widely used LAN technology today. Ethernet uses a bus or star topology and supports data transfer rates of 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, and 10 Gbps. Ethernet uses the carrier sense multiple access with collision detection (CSMA/CD) protocol for medium access control  .
- **IEEE 802.5**: This standard defines the Token Ring technology, which is a LAN technology that uses a ring topology and a token-passing protocol for medium access control. Token Ring supports data transfer rates of 4 Mbps and 16 Mbps. Token Ring is less prone to collisions than Ethernet, but it is more expensive and complex to implement  .
- **IEEE 802.11**: This standard defines the Wireless LAN (WLAN) technology, which is a LAN technology that uses radio waves or infrared signals to transmit data between wireless devices. WLAN supports data transfer rates of up to 600 Mbps, depending on the frequency band and modulation scheme used. WLAN uses the carrier sense multiple access with collision avoidance (CSMA/CA) protocol for medium access control  .
- **IEEE 802.1**: This standard defines the internetworking standards for LANs and metropolitan area networks (MANs). It includes standards for bridging, spanning tree, virtual LANs, security, quality of service, etc .
