# Services for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks

- A service is a set of functions or capabilities that a network provides to its users or applications.
- A service can be classified into two types: connection-oriented and connectionless.
- A connection-oriented service establishes a logical connection between the sender and the receiver before any data is transferred. The connection is maintained until the communication is completed. An example of a connection-oriented service is the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP).
- A connectionless service does not require a connection establishment or termination. The sender and the receiver are independent of each other and can send or receive data at any time. An example of a connectionless service is the User Datagram Protocol (UDP).
- A service can also be classified into two categories: reliable and unreliable.
- A reliable service guarantees that the data is delivered without any errors, loss, duplication, or reordering. A reliable service may use mechanisms such as error detection, error correction, acknowledgment, retransmission, and sequencing to ensure data integrity.
- An unreliable service does not guarantee that the data is delivered correctly or at all. An unreliable service may not use any mechanisms to ensure data integrity and may deliver data with errors, loss, duplication, or reordering.
- A service can also be characterized by its quality of service (QoS) parameters, such as bandwidth, delay, jitter, and packet loss.
- Bandwidth is the amount of data that can be transferred per unit time over a network link.
- Delay is the time it takes for a packet to travel from the sender to the receiver.
- Jitter is the variation in delay among different packets.
- Packet loss is the percentage of packets that are dropped or discarded by the network.
- Different services may have different QoS requirements depending on the type and nature of the data and the application. For example, a video streaming service may require high bandwidth, low delay, low jitter, and low packet loss, while an email service may tolerate low bandwidth, high delay, high jitter, and high packet loss.
- A network can provide different levels of QoS to different services by using techniques such as traffic classification, traffic shaping, traffic policing, and traffic engineering.
- Traffic classification is the process of identifying and labeling different types of traffic based on their QoS requirements.
- Traffic shaping is the process of controlling the rate and timing of traffic transmission to meet the QoS requirements.
- Traffic policing is the process of monitoring and enforcing the QoS requirements by dropping or marking packets that violate the QoS policies.
- Traffic engineering is the process of optimizing the network performance and resource utilization by routing traffic along the best paths based on the QoS requirements.
