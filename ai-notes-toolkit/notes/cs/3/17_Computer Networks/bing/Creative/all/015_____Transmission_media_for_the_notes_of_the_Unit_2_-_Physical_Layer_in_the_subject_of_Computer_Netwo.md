# Transmission media for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

- Transmission media is a communication channel that is used to carry the data in the form of bits from the sender to the receiver using LAN .
- Transmission media can be classified into two types: guided media and unguided media.
- Guided media, also known as wired or bounded media, uses physical links to direct and confine the signals being transmitted. Examples of guided media are twisted pair, coaxial cable, and optical fiber .
- Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded media, uses electromagnetic waves to transmit the signals through the air, water, or vacuum. Examples of unguided media are radio waves, microwaves, infrared waves, and visible light .
- The choice of transmission media depends on several factors, such as bandwidth, cost, distance, security, and interference .
- Bandwidth is the measure of the amount of data that can be transmitted in a given time over a communication channel . Higher bandwidth means higher data rate and faster transmission .
- Cost is the measure of the expense of installing and maintaining the transmission media . Lower cost means more affordability and feasibility .
- Distance is the measure of the length of the transmission media or the maximum range of the signals . Longer distance means more coverage and reachability .
- Security is the measure of the protection of the data from unauthorized access or modification . Higher security means more confidentiality and integrity .
- Interference is the measure of the noise or distortion that affects the quality of the signals . Lower interference means more reliability and accuracy .
- Generally, guided media has higher bandwidth, higher cost, shorter distance, higher security, and lower interference than unguided media . However, there are exceptions and trade-offs depending on the specific type and application of the transmission media .
