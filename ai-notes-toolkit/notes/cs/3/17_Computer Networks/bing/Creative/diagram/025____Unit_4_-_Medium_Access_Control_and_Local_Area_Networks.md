## Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

- Medium access control (MAC) is the sublayer of the data link layer that controls the hardware responsible for interaction with the transmission medium.
- MAC protocols enforce a methodology to allow multiple devices access to a shared media network.
- MAC protocols can be classified into two categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based protocols allow devices to compete for the medium, such as CSMA/CD, CSMA/CA, and ALOHA.
- Contention-free protocols allocate the medium to devices in a deterministic or random manner, such as TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and polling.
- Local area networks (LANs) are networks that connect devices within a limited geographical area, such as a building or a campus.
- LANs can be wired, optical, or wireless, depending on the transmission medium used.
- Wireless LANs (WLANs) use radio waves to transmit data between devices and access points.
- WLANs are standardized by the IEEE 802.11 series of standards, which define the MAC and physical layer (PHY) specifications for wireless connectivity   .
- WLANs support various applications with quality of service (QoS) requirements, such as voice, audio, and video.
