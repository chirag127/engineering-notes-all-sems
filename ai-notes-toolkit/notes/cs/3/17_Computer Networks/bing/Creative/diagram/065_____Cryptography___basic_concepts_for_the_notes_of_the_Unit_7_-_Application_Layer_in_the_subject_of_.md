### Cryptography – basic concepts

- Cryptography is the science and art of designing and using techniques to secure communication and data in the presence of adversaries.
- Cryptography involves the use of terms like plaintext, ciphertext, algorithm, key, encryption, and decryption.
  - Plaintext is the original message or data that needs to be protected or transmitted.
  - Ciphertext is the transformed message or data that is unreadable by unauthorized parties.
  - Algorithm is the mathematical procedure or function that performs the transformation of plaintext to ciphertext and vice versa.
  - Key is the secret parameter or input that controls the algorithm and determines the output.
  - Encryption is the process of converting plaintext to ciphertext using an algorithm and a key.
  - Decryption is the process of converting ciphertext to plaintext using an algorithm and a key.
- Cryptography has a number of goals and features, such as :
  - Confidentiality: ensuring that only authorized parties can access the information.
  - Integrity: ensuring that the information is not altered or tampered with by unauthorized parties.
  - Authentication: ensuring that the parties involved in the communication are who they claim to be.
  - Non-repudiation: ensuring that the parties cannot deny their involvement in the communication or transaction.
  - Availability: ensuring that the information is accessible and usable by authorized parties when needed.
- Cryptography can be classified into two main types: symmetric key cryptography and asymmetric key cryptography  .
  - Symmetric key cryptography: also known as secret key cryptography, it uses the same key for both encryption and decryption. The key must be shared securely between the parties before the communication. Examples of symmetric key algorithms are AES, DES, RC4, etc.
  - Asymmetric key cryptography: also known as public key cryptography, it uses different keys for encryption and decryption. The key used for encryption is called the public key, and the key used for decryption is called the private key. The public key can be shared openly, while the private key must be kept secret by the owner. Examples of asymmetric key algorithms are RSA, ECC, DSA, etc.
