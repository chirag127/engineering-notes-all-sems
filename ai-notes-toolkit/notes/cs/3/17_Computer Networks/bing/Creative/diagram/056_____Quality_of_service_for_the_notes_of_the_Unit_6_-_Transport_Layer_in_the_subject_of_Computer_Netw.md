### Quality of service for the transport layer in computer networks

- Quality of service (QoS) is the ability of a network to provide reliable service to the traffic over various technologies and applications  .
- The transport layer is responsible for providing end-to-end communication between applications and ensuring data integrity, reliability, and efficiency.
- The transport layer also determines what type of service to provide to the applications, such as error-free, in-order, or real-time delivery.
- Some techniques that can be used to improve the QoS at the transport layer are :
  - Scheduling: This is the process of deciding which packet to send or receive first based on some criteria, such as priority, deadline, or fairness. Scheduling can help reduce delay, jitter, and packet loss.
  - Traffic shaping: This is the process of controlling the rate and burstiness of the traffic sent or received by a network device. Traffic shaping can help smooth out the traffic and avoid congestion and buffer overflow.
  - Admission control: This is the process of deciding whether to accept or reject a new flow request based on the availability of resources and the QoS requirements of the flow. Admission control can help ensure that the accepted flows can meet their QoS guarantees and avoid overloading the network.
  - Resource reservation: This is the process of allocating and reserving network resources, such as bandwidth, buffer, or CPU, for a specific flow or class of flows. Resource reservation can help ensure that the reserved flows can receive the QoS they need and avoid interference from other flows.
