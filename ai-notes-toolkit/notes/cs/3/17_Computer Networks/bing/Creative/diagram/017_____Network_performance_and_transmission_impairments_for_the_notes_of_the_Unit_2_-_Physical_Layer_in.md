### Network performance and transmission impairments

- Network performance is the measure of how well a network can deliver data and services to its users. It can be evaluated by various metrics, such as throughput, delay, jitter, packet loss, availability, reliability, etc.
- Transmission impairments are the factors that degrade the quality of a signal during its transmission over a medium. They can cause errors, distortions, or losses of data in the network. They can be classified into three types: attenuation, distortion, and noise.
- Attenuation is the gradual loss of signal strength as it travels over a distance. It depends on the characteristics of the medium, the frequency of the signal, and the environmental factors. Attenuation can be compensated by using amplifiers or repeaters to boost the signal.
- Distortion is the alteration of the shape or form of the signal due to the non-uniform propagation of different frequency components. It can result from the bandwidth limitations of the medium, the multipath propagation of the signal, or the interference from other signals. Distortion can be reduced by using filters or equalizers to correct the signal.
- Noise is the unwanted or random variation of the signal due to the external sources of disturbance. It can affect the amplitude, frequency, or phase of the signal. There are various types of noise, such as thermal noise, induced noise, crosstalk noise, and impulse noise. Noise can be minimized by using shielding, modulation, or error correction techniques.
- The impact of transmission impairments on network performance depends on the type and severity of the impairment, the modulation and encoding scheme of the signal, the error detection and correction mechanism of the network, and the quality of service requirements of the application. Transmission impairments can cause lower throughput, higher delay, higher jitter, higher packet loss, lower availability, and lower reliability of the network.
