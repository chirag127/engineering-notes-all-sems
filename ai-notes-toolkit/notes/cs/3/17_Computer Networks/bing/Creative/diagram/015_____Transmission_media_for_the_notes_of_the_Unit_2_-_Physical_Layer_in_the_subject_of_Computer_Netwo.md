### Transmission media

Transmission media are the physical channels that carry data signals from one network device to another. They are also called communication media or physical media. Transmission media can be classified into two types: guided media and unguided media.

#### Guided media

Guided media are those that provide a physical path for the data to travel along. They are also called wired or bounded media. Examples of guided media are:

- Twisted pair cable: It consists of two insulated copper wires twisted around each other to reduce electromagnetic interference. It is the most common and cheapest type of guided media. It can be used for telephone, Ethernet, and DSL networks. Twisted pair cables have different categories based on their data rate and shielding. For example, Category 5 (Cat 5) cable can support up to 100 Mbps, while Category 6 (Cat 6) cable can support up to 10 Gbps. Shielded twisted pair (STP) cable has a metal foil or braided mesh around the wires to reduce interference, while unshielded twisted pair (UTP) cable does not have any shielding.
- Coaxial cable: It consists of a single copper wire surrounded by a layer of insulation, a metallic shield, and a plastic cover. It is more expensive and less flexible than twisted pair cable, but it can carry higher data rates and longer distances. It can be used for cable TV, broadband Internet, and Ethernet networks. Coaxial cables have different standards based on their impedance and frequency range. For example, RG-6 cable has an impedance of 75 ohms and a frequency range of up to 2.4 GHz, while RG-58 cable has an impedance of 50 ohms and a frequency range of up to 400 MHz.
- Fiber optic cable: It consists of a thin glass or plastic fiber that carries light pulses. It is the most expensive and fragile type of guided media, but it can carry the highest data rates and longest distances. It can be used for long-distance telecommunication, Internet backbone, and optical LANs. Fiber optic cables have different modes based on how the light travels inside the fiber. For example, single-mode fiber (SMF) has a narrow core that allows only one mode of light to propagate, while multimode fiber (MMF) has a wider core that allows multiple modes of light to propagate.

#### Unguided media

Unguided media are those that do not provide a physical path for the data to travel along. They are also called wireless or unbounded media. Examples of unguided media are:

- Radio waves: They are electromagnetic waves that can travel through air, vacuum, and some other materials. They have different frequencies and wavelengths that determine their properties and applications. They can be used for AM and FM radio, TV, cellular phones, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and satellite communication. Radio waves have different bands based on their frequency range. For example, very high frequency (VHF) band ranges from 30 MHz to 300 MHz, while ultra high frequency (UHF) band ranges from 300 MHz to 3 GHz.
- Microwaves: They are electromagnetic waves that have higher frequencies and shorter wavelengths than radio waves. They can travel in straight lines and can be focused by antennas. They can be used for point-to-point communication, radar, satellite communication, and microwave ovens. Microwaves have different bands based on their frequency range. For example, C band ranges from 4 GHz to 8 GHz, while X band ranges from 8 GHz to 12 GHz.
- Infrared waves: They are electromagnetic waves that have higher frequencies and shorter wavelengths than microwaves. They can travel only in line of sight and can be blocked by solid objects. They can be used for remote control, wireless keyboard and mouse, and infrared LANs. Infrared waves have different bands based on their wavelength range. For example, near infrared (NIR) band ranges from 0.7 micrometers to 1.4 micrometers, while far infrared (FIR) band ranges from 15 micrometers to 1000 micrometers.

#### Characteristics of transmission media

Some of the important characteristics of transmission media are:

- Bandwidth: It is the measure of the data rate that can be transmitted over a given medium. It is expressed in bits per second (bps) or Hertz (Hz). Higher bandwidth means higher data rate and higher quality of service. Bandwidth depends on the frequency range, modulation technique, and signal-to-noise ratio of the medium.
- Attenuation: It is the measure of the loss of signal strength
