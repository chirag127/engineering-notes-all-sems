### Network Performance and Transmission Impairments

#### Network Performance
Network performance refers to the level of quality of service provided by a network. It is measured by various factors such as:
- **Throughput**: The rate at which data is transmitted over the network.
- **Latency**: The time it takes for a data packet to travel from the sender to the receiver.
- **Jitter**: The variation in latency over time.
- **Packet loss**: The percentage of data packets that are lost during transmission.

#### Transmission Impairments
Transmission impairments are factors that can negatively affect the quality of data transmission over a network. Some common transmission impairments include:
- **Attenuation**: The weakening of a signal as it travels over a distance.
- **Distortion**: The alteration of a signal due to the presence of noise or other interfering signals.
- **Noise**: Unwanted signals that interfere with the transmission of data.
- **Interference**: The presence of other signals that can disrupt the transmission of data.

These impairments can affect the performance of a network by reducing the throughput, increasing the latency, and causing packet loss. To mitigate these impairments, various techniques such as error correction and signal amplification can be used.
