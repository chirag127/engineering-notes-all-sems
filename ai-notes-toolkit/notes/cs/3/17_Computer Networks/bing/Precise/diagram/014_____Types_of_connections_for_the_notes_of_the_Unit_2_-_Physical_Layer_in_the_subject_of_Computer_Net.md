### Types of connections for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

1. **Point-to-Point Connection**: This type of connection is established between two devices directly. It is the simplest type of connection and is commonly used for connecting a computer to a peripheral device such as a printer or modem.

2. **Multipoint Connection**: Also known as a multidrop connection, this type of connection is established between multiple devices. In this type of connection, one device acts as the primary device and the other devices act as secondary devices. The primary device controls the communication between all the devices.

3. **Circuit-Switched Connection**: This type of connection is established by setting up a dedicated communication path between two devices. The path is reserved for the exclusive use of the two devices for the duration of the communication.

4. **Packet-Switched Connection**: This type of connection is established by dividing the data into packets and sending them over a shared network. Each packet is sent independently and can take a different path to reach its destination.

5. **Message-Switched Connection**: This type of connection is established by sending the entire message to an intermediate device, which stores the message and then forwards it to the destination device. This type of connection is commonly used in email systems.

6. **Cell-Switched Connection**: This type of connection is similar to packet-switched connection, but the data is divided into fixed-size cells instead of variable-size packets. This type of connection is commonly used in Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM) networks.
