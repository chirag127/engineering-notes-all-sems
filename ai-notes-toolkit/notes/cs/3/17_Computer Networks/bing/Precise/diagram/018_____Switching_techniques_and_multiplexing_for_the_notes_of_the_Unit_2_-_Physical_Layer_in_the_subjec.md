### Switching techniques and multiplexing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Switching techniques and multiplexing are two important concepts in the physical layer of computer networks. These techniques are used to efficiently transmit data over a communication channel.

#### Switching Techniques
Switching techniques are used to establish a connection between two devices in a network. There are three main types of switching techniques:
1. Circuit Switching: In circuit switching, a dedicated communication path is established between two devices for the duration of their communication. This path is reserved exclusively for the use of these two devices.
2. Packet Switching: In packet switching, data is divided into small packets and transmitted over the network. Each packet is routed independently to its destination.
3. Message Switching: In message switching, the entire message is transmitted from one node to another until it reaches its destination.

#### Multiplexing
Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple signals into a single signal for transmission over a communication channel. There are several types of multiplexing techniques, including:
1. Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM): In FDM, the available bandwidth of the communication channel is divided into multiple frequency bands, each of which is used to transmit a separate signal.
2. Time Division Multiplexing (TDM): In TDM, the available time on the communication channel is divided into time slots, each of which is used to transmit a separate signal.
3. Code Division Multiplexing (CDM): In CDM, each signal is assigned a unique code, and the signals are transmitted simultaneously over the communication channel.

These techniques are used to increase the efficiency of data transmission over a communication channel and to make the best use of the available resources. They are essential for the functioning of modern computer networks.
