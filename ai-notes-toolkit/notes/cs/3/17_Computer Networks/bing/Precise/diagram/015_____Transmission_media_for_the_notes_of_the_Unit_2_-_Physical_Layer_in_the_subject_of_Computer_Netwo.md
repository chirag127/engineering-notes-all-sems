### Transmission Media

Transmission media refers to the physical pathways that connect computers, other devices, and people in a network. These pathways carry the information in the form of electromagnetic signals. There are two types of transmission media: guided and unguided.

1. **Guided Media**: Guided media, also known as wired or bounded transmission media, refers to a physical link that guides the transmission of signals from one point to another. Examples of guided media include twisted pair cable, coaxial cable, and fiber optic cable.

    - **Twisted Pair Cable**: Twisted pair cable consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together. The twisting helps to reduce interference from other devices and wires. Twisted pair cable is commonly used for telephone and Ethernet connections.

    - **Coaxial Cable**: Coaxial cable consists of a central copper conductor surrounded by an insulating layer, a braided metal shield, and an outer insulating layer. Coaxial cable is commonly used for cable television and broadband internet connections.

    - **Fiber Optic Cable**: Fiber optic cable consists of a central glass or plastic core surrounded by a cladding layer. Light signals are transmitted through the core and are reflected by the cladding to keep the light within the core. Fiber optic cable is commonly used for high-speed, long-distance data transmission.

2. **Unguided Media**: Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded transmission media, refers to the transmission of signals through the air or space. Examples of unguided media include radio waves, microwaves, and infrared waves.

    - **Radio Waves**: Radio waves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 3 kHz to 300 GHz. They are commonly used for wireless communication, such as in mobile phones, radio and television broadcasting, and satellite communication.

    - **Microwaves**: Microwaves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 300 MHz to 300 GHz. They are commonly used for wireless communication, such as in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, as well as for satellite communication and radar.

    - **Infrared Waves**: Infrared waves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 300 GHz to 400 THz. They are commonly used for short-range wireless communication, such as in remote controls and wireless keyboards and mice.
