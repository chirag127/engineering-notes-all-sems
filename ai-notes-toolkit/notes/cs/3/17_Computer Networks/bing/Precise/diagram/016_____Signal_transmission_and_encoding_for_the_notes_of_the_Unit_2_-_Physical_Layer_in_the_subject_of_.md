### Signal Transmission and Encoding

Signal transmission is the process of sending data from one point to another, usually over a communication channel. Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted over the communication channel.

1. **Analog and Digital Signals**: Signals can be either analog or digital. Analog signals are continuous and can take on any value within a range, while digital signals are discrete and can only take on a finite number of values.
2. **Modulation**: Modulation is the process of changing one or more properties of a carrier signal to encode information. There are several types of modulation, including amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM), and phase modulation (PM).
3. **Encoding Schemes**: There are several encoding schemes used to represent digital data as a signal for transmission. Some common encoding schemes include Non-Return-to-Zero (NRZ), Manchester, and 4B/5B.
4. **Transmission Media**: The transmission media is the physical path between the sender and receiver. Common transmission media include copper wire, fiber optic cable, and wireless (radio waves).
5. **Error Detection and Correction**: Errors can occur during transmission due to noise or interference. Error detection and correction techniques are used to detect and correct errors in the transmitted data.
