### Unit 6 - Transport Layer: UDP and TCP

The transport layer of the OSI model is responsible for providing end-to-end communication services for applications. Two of the main protocols used at this layer are the User Datagram Protocol (UDP) and the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP).

#### UDP
- UDP is a connectionless protocol that provides a simple, unreliable datagram service.
- It does not guarantee delivery, ordering, or error checking of data.
- It is useful for applications that require fast, low-overhead transmission, such as online gaming or streaming media.

#### TCP
- TCP is a connection-oriented protocol that provides a reliable, stream-oriented service.
- It guarantees delivery, ordering, and error checking of data.
- It is useful for applications that require reliable transmission, such as file transfers or email.

Some key differences between UDP and TCP include:
- UDP is connectionless while TCP is connection-oriented.
- UDP does not guarantee delivery, ordering, or error checking while TCP does.
- UDP is faster and has lower overhead than TCP, but is less reliable.
