### LAN Standards

Local Area Networks (LANs) are computer networks that are designed to operate over relatively small distances, such as within a single building or campus. There are several standards that define the operation of LANs, including:

1. **Ethernet (IEEE 802.3):** Ethernet is the most widely used LAN technology. It uses a bus or star topology and supports data transfer rates of 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, and 10 Gbps.

2. **Token Ring (IEEE 802.5):** Token Ring is a LAN technology that uses a ring topology. It supports data transfer rates of 4 Mbps and 16 Mbps.

3. **Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI):** FDDI is a LAN technology that uses a dual-ring topology and supports data transfer rates of 100 Mbps.

4. **Wireless LAN (IEEE 802.11):** Wireless LANs (WLANs) use radio waves to transmit data between devices. The most widely used WLAN standard is IEEE 802.11, which includes several sub-standards such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, and 802.11n.

These standards define the physical and data link layers of the OSI model, specifying how data is transmitted over the network and how devices access the network. It is important to understand these standards when designing and implementing a LAN.
