### Transmission Media

Transmission media refers to the physical pathways that connect computers, other devices, and people in a network. These pathways carry the information from one device to another in the form of electromagnetic signals. There are two types of transmission media: guided and unguided.

1. **Guided Media**: Guided media, also known as wired or bounded transmission media, refers to a physical link that guides the transmission of data from one point to another. Examples of guided media include twisted pair cable, coaxial cable, and fiber optic cable.

    - **Twisted Pair Cable**: Twisted pair cable consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together. The twisting helps to reduce crosstalk and electromagnetic interference. Twisted pair cable is commonly used in telephone and Ethernet networks.

    - **Coaxial Cable**: Coaxial cable consists of a central copper conductor surrounded by an insulating layer, a braided metal shield, and an outer insulating layer. Coaxial cable is commonly used in cable television and broadband internet networks.

    - **Fiber Optic Cable**: Fiber optic cable consists of a central glass or plastic core surrounded by a cladding layer. Data is transmitted in the form of light pulses. Fiber optic cable is commonly used in long-distance and high-bandwidth communication networks.

2. **Unguided Media**: Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded transmission media, refers to the transmission of data through the air or space using electromagnetic waves. Examples of unguided media include radio waves, microwaves, and infrared waves.

    - **Radio Waves**: Radio waves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 3 kHz to 300 GHz. Radio waves are commonly used in mobile communication, broadcast radio, and television.

    - **Microwaves**: Microwaves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 300 MHz to 300 GHz. Microwaves are commonly used in satellite communication, wireless networking, and radar.

    - **Infrared Waves**: Infrared waves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 300 GHz to 400 THz. Infrared waves are commonly used in remote controls, wireless keyboards and mice, and short-range communication.

These are the main types of transmission media used in computer networks. Each type has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of transmission media depends on factors such as distance, bandwidth, cost, and security.
