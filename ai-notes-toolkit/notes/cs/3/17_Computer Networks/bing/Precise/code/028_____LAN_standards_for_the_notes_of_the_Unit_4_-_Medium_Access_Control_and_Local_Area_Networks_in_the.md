### LAN standards for the notes of the Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks in the subject of Computer Networks

1. **Ethernet**: Ethernet is the most widely used LAN technology. It is a wired networking standard that uses a bus or star topology and supports data transfer rates of 10 Mbps.

2. **Fast Ethernet**: Fast Ethernet is an extension of the original Ethernet standard, providing data transfer rates of 100 Mbps.

3. **Gigabit Ethernet**: Gigabit Ethernet is another extension of the Ethernet standard, providing data transfer rates of 1 Gbps.

4. **Token Ring**: Token Ring is a LAN technology that uses a ring topology and token passing for media access control. It supports data transfer rates of 4 or 16 Mbps.

5. **FDDI**: Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI) is a LAN technology that uses a dual-ring topology and token passing for media access control. It supports data transfer rates of 100 Mbps.

6. **Wireless LAN (WLAN)**: Wireless LAN (WLAN) is a LAN technology that uses radio waves for communication. The most widely used WLAN standard is IEEE 802.11, also known as Wi-Fi.

7. **Bluetooth**: Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology used for communication between devices. It is commonly used for wireless communication between mobile phones, headsets, and other portable devices.

8. **HomePNA**: HomePNA is a LAN technology that uses existing telephone wiring for communication. It supports data transfer rates of up to 320 Mbps.

9. **Powerline**: Powerline is a LAN technology that uses existing electrical wiring for communication. It supports data transfer rates of up to 500 Mbps.

These are some of the most widely used LAN standards. Each standard has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of standard depends on the specific requirements of the network.
