### Electronic Mail

Electronic mail, commonly known as email, is a method of exchanging digital messages between people using electronic devices. Email operates across computer networks, primarily the Internet. Here are some key points to note about email:

1. Email is a store-and-forward service, meaning that the messages are not sent directly from the sender to the recipient, but instead are stored on a server and then forwarded to the recipient when they are available to receive them.

2. Email messages are composed of two main parts: the header and the body. The header contains information about the sender, recipient, subject, and other metadata, while the body contains the actual message content.

3. Email messages can include attachments, which are files that are sent along with the message. These can include documents, images, videos, and other types of files.

4. Email is a widely used form of communication, with billions of email messages sent and received every day. It is used for personal, business, and academic purposes.

5. Email is not a secure form of communication by default, as messages can be intercepted and read by others. However, there are methods to encrypt email messages to increase their security.

6. Email is one of the many protocols that operate at the application layer of the OSI model, along with other protocols such as HTTP, FTP, and DNS.

7. Email is an important part of the history of the Internet, with the first email message being sent in 1971 by Ray Tomlinson, a computer engineer working on the ARPANET project.
