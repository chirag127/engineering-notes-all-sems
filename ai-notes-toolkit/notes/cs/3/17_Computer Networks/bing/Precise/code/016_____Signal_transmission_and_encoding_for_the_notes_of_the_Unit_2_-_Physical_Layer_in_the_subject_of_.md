### Signal transmission and encoding for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

1. Signal transmission refers to the process of transmitting data from one point to another in the form of electromagnetic or optical signals.
2. Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted over a communication channel.
3. There are several methods of encoding, including digital and analog encoding.
4. Digital encoding involves converting data into a binary format, while analog encoding involves converting data into an analog signal.
5. Some common encoding techniques include amplitude modulation, frequency modulation, and phase modulation.
6. The choice of encoding technique depends on factors such as the type of data being transmitted, the communication channel, and the required data rate.
7. The physical layer of the OSI model is responsible for the transmission and reception of raw data over a physical medium.
8. The physical layer is responsible for defining the characteristics of the transmission medium, including the type of cable, connectors, and signal encoding techniques.
9. The physical layer also defines the electrical and mechanical characteristics of the interface between the transmission medium and the network devices.
10. The physical layer is the lowest layer of the OSI model and provides the foundation for all higher-layer functions.
