### Network Structure and Architecture

Network structure and architecture refer to the design and layout of a computer network. It includes the physical components, such as routers, switches, and cables, as well as the logical components, such as protocols, addressing schemes, and routing algorithms.

Some key points to consider when studying network structure and architecture are:

1. **Topology**: The physical and logical arrangement of the network's components. Common topologies include bus, star, ring, and mesh.
2. **Protocols**: The rules and standards that govern communication between devices on the network. Examples include TCP/IP, HTTP, and FTP.
3. **Addressing**: The method used to assign unique identifiers to devices on the network. This can include IP addresses, MAC addresses, and hostnames.
4. **Routing**: The process of determining the best path for data to travel from one device to another on the network. This can involve static or dynamic routing algorithms.
5. **Scalability**: The ability of the network to grow and accommodate more devices and traffic without a significant decrease in performance.
6. **Security**: The measures taken to protect the network and its data from unauthorized access and malicious attacks.

These are some of the key concepts to consider when studying network structure and architecture in the context of Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks. It is important to have a solid understanding of these concepts in order to design and maintain effective and efficient computer networks.
