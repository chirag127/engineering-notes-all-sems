### Multiplexing

Multiplexing is a technique used in communication systems to allow multiple signals to be transmitted over a single communication channel. This is achieved by combining the signals into a composite signal, which is then transmitted over the channel. At the receiving end, the composite signal is separated into its individual components, allowing the original signals to be recovered.

There are several types of multiplexing techniques, including:

1. **Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM):** This technique divides the available bandwidth of the communication channel into multiple frequency bands, each of which is used to transmit a separate signal. The signals are modulated onto different carrier frequencies and combined into a composite signal for transmission.

2. **Time Division Multiplexing (TDM):** This technique divides the time available for transmission into multiple time slots, with each time slot used to transmit a separate signal. The signals are transmitted one after the other in a repeating cycle, with each signal occupying a specific time slot.

3. **Code Division Multiplexing (CDM):** This technique assigns a unique code to each signal, allowing multiple signals to be transmitted simultaneously over the same frequency band. The signals are spread over a wide frequency band using the assigned codes, and can be separated at the receiving end using the same codes.

4. **Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM):** This technique is used in optical communication systems and is similar to FDM. It divides the available bandwidth of the optical fiber into multiple wavelength bands, each of which is used to transmit a separate optical signal.

Multiplexing is an important concept in the transport layer of computer networks, as it allows multiple connections to be established and maintained over a single network link. This improves the efficiency of the network and allows multiple applications to share the available bandwidth. The transport layer uses port numbers to identify different connections and to multiplex and demultiplex data transmitted over the network.
