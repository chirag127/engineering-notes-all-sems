### LAN standards for the notes of the Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks in the subject of Computer Networks

- LAN (Local Area Network) standards are a set of guidelines and specifications that define the operation of local area networks.
- These standards are developed and maintained by organizations such as the IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) and the ISO (International Organization for Standardization).
- Some of the most widely used LAN standards include Ethernet, Token Ring, and FDDI (Fiber Distributed Data Interface).
- Ethernet is the most widely used LAN technology and is defined by the IEEE 802.3 standard. It uses a bus or star topology and supports data transfer rates of up to 10 Gbps.
- Token Ring is another popular LAN technology that is defined by the IEEE 802.5 standard. It uses a ring topology and supports data transfer rates of up to 16 Mbps.
- FDDI is a high-speed LAN technology that is defined by the ANSI X3T9.5 standard. It uses a dual-ring topology and supports data transfer rates of up to 100 Mbps.
- These standards ensure that different devices and systems can communicate and interoperate with each other on a local area network. They also provide a framework for the development of new technologies and the improvement of existing ones.
