### Cryptography – basic concepts for the notes of the Unit 7 - Application Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Cryptography is the practice of secure communication in the presence of third parties. It involves techniques for secure communication, data storage, and data transmission. Here are some basic concepts in cryptography:

1. **Encryption**: The process of converting plaintext (readable data) into ciphertext (encoded data) to prevent unauthorized access.
2. **Decryption**: The reverse process of encryption, where ciphertext is converted back into plaintext.
3. **Cipher**: An algorithm used for encryption and decryption.
4. **Key**: A piece of information used by a cipher to encrypt and decrypt data.
5. **Symmetric-key cryptography**: A type of cryptography where the same key is used for both encryption and decryption.
6. **Asymmetric-key cryptography**: A type of cryptography where different keys are used for encryption and decryption. It is also known as public-key cryptography.
7. **Hash function**: A function that takes an input and produces a fixed-size output, commonly used for verifying the integrity of data.
8. **Digital signature**: A cryptographic technique used to verify the authenticity and integrity of a message or document.
9. **Certificate authority (CA)**: An entity that issues digital certificates, which are used to verify the identity of the owner of a public key.

These are some of the basic concepts in cryptography. Cryptography plays an important role in securing communication and data in computer networks. It is used in various applications, such as secure email, online banking, and virtual private networks (VPNs).
