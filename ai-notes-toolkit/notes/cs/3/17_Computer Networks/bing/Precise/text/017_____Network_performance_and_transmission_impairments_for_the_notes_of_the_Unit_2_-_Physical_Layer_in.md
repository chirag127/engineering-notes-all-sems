### Network performance and transmission impairments

- Transmission impairments refer to any damage or harm caused to the signal during transmission. Due to transmission impairments, the signal received at the receiver end may differ from the signal sent by the sender.
- Transmission media are not ideal and have some imperfections, which cause signal impairment.
- Impairments may be called noise or anything else that might cause an error in data transmission.
- There are several types of noise such as induced noise, crosstalk noise, thermal noise, and impulse noise which may corrupt the signal.
- Induced noise comes from sources such as motors and appliances. These devices act as sending antennas and the transmission medium acts as a receiving antenna.
- Transmission impairments can degrade the signal quality as it progresses towards its destination, and the received bit-error rate (BER) at the destination node might become unacceptably high.
- Previous work on lightpath routing and wavelength assignment (RWA) assumed an ideal physical layer and ignored these transmission impairments.
