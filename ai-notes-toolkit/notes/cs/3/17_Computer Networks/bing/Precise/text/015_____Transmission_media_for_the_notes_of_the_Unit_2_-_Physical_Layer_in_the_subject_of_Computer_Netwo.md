### Transmission Media

Transmission media refers to the physical pathways that carry data signals between devices in a computer network. There are two main types of transmission media: guided and unguided.

1. **Guided media:** Guided media, also known as wired or bounded media, refers to the transmission of data over a physical medium that is guided along a specific path. Examples of guided media include twisted pair cable, coaxial cable, and fiber optic cable.

    - **Twisted pair cable:** Twisted pair cable consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce interference. It is commonly used for telephone and Ethernet connections.
    - **Coaxial cable:** Coaxial cable consists of a central copper conductor surrounded by an insulating layer, a braided metal shield, and an outer insulating layer. It is commonly used for cable television and broadband internet connections.
    - **Fiber optic cable:** Fiber optic cable consists of thin strands of glass or plastic that transmit data using light. It is commonly used for high-speed, long-distance data transmission.

2. **Unguided media:** Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded media, refers to the transmission of data through the air or space without the use of a physical medium. Examples of unguided media include radio waves, microwaves, and infrared waves.

    - **Radio waves:** Radio waves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 3 kHz to 300 GHz. They are commonly used for wireless communication, such as in mobile phones and wireless networks.
    - **Microwaves:** Microwaves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 300 MHz to 300 GHz. They are commonly used for satellite communication and wireless networking.
    - **Infrared waves:** Infrared waves are electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from 300 GHz to 400 THz. They are commonly used for short-range communication, such as in remote controls and wireless keyboards.

These are the main types of transmission media used in computer networks. Each type has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of transmission media depends on factors such as the distance, data rate, and cost.
