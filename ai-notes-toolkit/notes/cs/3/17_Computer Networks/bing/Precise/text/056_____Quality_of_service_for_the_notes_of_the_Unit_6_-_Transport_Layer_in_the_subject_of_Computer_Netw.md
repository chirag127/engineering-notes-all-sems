### Quality of Service (QoS) in Transport Layer of Computer Networks

Quality of Service (QoS) refers to the ability of a network to provide improved service to certain network traffic. The primary goal of QoS is to provide priority including dedicated bandwidth, controlled jitter and latency, and improved loss characteristics to specific flows of data in a network.

In the context of the Transport Layer of Computer Networks, QoS can be achieved through several mechanisms, including:

1. **Resource Reservation:** Reserving resources such as bandwidth and buffer space for specific flows of data to ensure that they receive the required level of service.

2. **Traffic Shaping:** Controlling the rate at which data is sent into the network to prevent congestion and ensure that traffic conforms to a specified traffic profile.

3. **Packet Scheduling:** Determining the order in which packets are transmitted to ensure that high-priority traffic is transmitted before lower-priority traffic.

4. **Admission Control:** Deciding whether to admit a new flow of data into the network based on the current network conditions and the QoS requirements of the new flow.

By implementing these mechanisms, the Transport Layer can provide improved QoS to specific flows of data, ensuring that they receive the required level of service. This can be particularly important for applications such as voice and video, which require low latency and jitter, as well as for mission-critical applications that require high levels of reliability.
