# Multiplexing

Multiplexing is a technique used in communication systems to allow multiple signals to be transmitted over a single communication channel. This is achieved by combining the signals into a composite signal, which is then transmitted over the channel. At the receiving end, the composite signal is separated into its individual components, allowing the original signals to be recovered.

There are several types of multiplexing techniques, including:

1. **Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM):** This technique divides the available bandwidth of the communication channel into multiple frequency bands, with each band assigned to a different signal. The signals are modulated onto different carrier frequencies and combined into a composite signal for transmission.

2. **Time Division Multiplexing (TDM):** This technique divides the time available for transmission into multiple time slots, with each slot assigned to a different signal. The signals are transmitted one after the other in their assigned time slots, allowing multiple signals to be transmitted over the same channel.

3. **Code Division Multiplexing (CDM):** This technique assigns a unique code to each signal, allowing multiple signals to be transmitted simultaneously over the same channel. The signals are separated at the receiving end by correlating the received signal with the unique codes.

4. **Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM):** This technique is used in optical communication systems and is similar to FDM. It divides the available bandwidth of the optical fiber into multiple wavelength bands, with each band assigned to a different signal. The signals are modulated onto different wavelengths of light and combined into a composite signal for transmission.

Multiplexing is an important technique in communication systems, as it allows multiple signals to be transmitted over a single channel, increasing the efficiency of the system. It is used in a wide range of applications, including telephone systems, cable television, and computer networks.
