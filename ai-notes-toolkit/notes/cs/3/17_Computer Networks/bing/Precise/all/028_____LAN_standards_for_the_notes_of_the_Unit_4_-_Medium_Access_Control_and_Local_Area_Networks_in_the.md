### LAN Standards

Local Area Network (LAN) standards are a set of guidelines and protocols that define how devices communicate with each other within a local network. These standards are developed and maintained by organizations such as the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) and the International Organization for Standardization (ISO).

Some of the most widely used LAN standards include:

1. **Ethernet (IEEE 802.3):** Ethernet is the most widely used LAN technology. It uses a bus or star topology and supports data transfer rates of up to 10 Gbps.

2. **Token Ring (IEEE 802.5):** Token Ring is a LAN technology that uses a ring topology. It supports data transfer rates of up to 16 Mbps.

3. **Wireless LAN (IEEE 802.11):** Wireless LAN, also known as Wi-Fi, is a LAN technology that uses radio waves to transmit data. It supports data transfer rates of up to 1.3 Gbps.

4. **Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI):** FDDI is a LAN technology that uses fiber optic cables to transmit data. It supports data transfer rates of up to 100 Mbps.

These standards define the physical and data link layers of the OSI model, specifying how data is transmitted over the network and how devices access the network. They also define the format of the data packets and the protocols used for error detection and correction.
