# Network Structure and Architecture

Network structure and architecture refer to the design and layout of a computer network. It involves the physical and logical arrangement of network components, including devices, connections, and protocols. The structure and architecture of a network determine its functionality, performance, and reliability.

Some key concepts in network structure and architecture include:

1. **Topology**: This refers to the physical and logical layout of the network. Common topologies include bus, star, ring, and mesh.

2. **Protocols**: These are the rules and standards that govern communication between devices on a network. Common protocols include TCP/IP, HTTP, and FTP.

3. **Devices**: These are the hardware components that make up a network, including routers, switches, hubs, and modems.

4. **Connections**: These refer to the physical and logical links between devices on a network. Connections can be wired or wireless.

5. **Addressing**: This involves assigning unique identifiers to devices on a network to facilitate communication.

6. **Routing**: This involves determining the best path for data to travel between devices on a network.

7. **Security**: This involves protecting the network and its data from unauthorized access and attacks.

In summary, network structure and architecture are essential components of computer networks, and a well-designed network can improve performance, reliability, and security. This topic is covered in Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts of the subject of Computer Networks.
