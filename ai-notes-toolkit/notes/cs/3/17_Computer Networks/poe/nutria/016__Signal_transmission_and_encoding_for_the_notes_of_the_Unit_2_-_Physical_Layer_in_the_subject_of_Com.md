
### Signal Transmission and Encoding for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the Subject of Computer Networks

* Signal transmission is the process of sending data from one point to another through a medium. This process involves the use of a transmitter, which converts the data into a signal, and a receiver, which converts the signal back into data.
* The physical layer of a computer network is responsible for the transmission of data over physical media. It is responsible for the transmission of signals, encoding and decoding of data, and the physical connection between devices.
* Encoding is the process of converting data from one form to another. It is used to ensure that data is transmitted accurately and efficiently over a network.
* Digital signals are encoded using binary code, which is a sequence of 0s and 1s. This binary code is used to represent characters, numbers, and other data.
* Analog signals are encoded using various modulation techniques, such as amplitude modulation (AM) and frequency modulation (FM). These techniques allow analog signals to be transmitted over long distances.
* Modulation techniques are used to reduce the amount of noise in a signal and to increase the signal's bandwidth.
* Error detection and correction techniques are used to ensure that data is transmitted accurately. These techniques detect errors in the data and then use error-correction algorithms to correct them.
