
### IPv6 for the Notes of Unit 5 - Network Layer in Computer Networks

* IPv6 (Internet Protocol version 6) is the successor of IPv4 and is the most recent version of the Internet Protocol. 
* It was designed to address the scalability issues of IPv4 and to provide enhanced security, mobility, and quality of service to users.
* IPv6 is a 128-bit address protocol that is capable of providing up to 3.4 x 10^38 unique IP addresses. It uses a hexadecimal format for its addresses, which are longer and more complex than those of IPv4.
* IPv6 offers several advantages over IPv4, including improved security, better multicasting support, simpler network configuration, and better support for mobile devices.
* IPv6 also supports the concept of a host-based routing table, which allows hosts to determine the best route to a destination. This eliminates the need for a router to determine the best route.
* IPv6 also supports the concept of auto-configuration, which allows devices to automatically configure their IP address without manual intervention.
* IPv6 also supports the concept of Neighbor Discovery Protocol (NDP), which allows nodes to discover each other on the same network.
* IPv6 also supports the concept of Quality of Service (QoS), which allows nodes to prioritize traffic based on certain criteria. This allows for more efficient use of network resources.
