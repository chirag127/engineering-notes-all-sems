
### TCP Congestion Control

* Congestion control is a process which helps maintain the quality of service in a network by managing the rate of data transmission between two nodes. 
* It is used to prevent network congestion, which can lead to packet loss, long delays, and low throughput.
* TCP congestion control is an algorithm that is used to regulate the amount of data that can be sent over a network at any given time.
* It works by monitoring the amount of data that is being sent and received, and adjusting the transmission rate accordingly. 
* TCP congestion control is based on the “additive increase/multiplicative decrease” (AIMD) algorithm. 
* When a connection is first established, the transmission rate is set to a low value. 
* As the connection progresses, the transmission rate is gradually increased until the network begins to experience congestion. 
* At this point, the transmission rate is reduced to prevent further congestion.
* TCP congestion control also uses a variety of other techniques such as slow-start, fast retransmit, and fast recovery to ensure that the network is not overloaded. 
* These techniques help to ensure that the network is able to provide a reliable and consistent service.
