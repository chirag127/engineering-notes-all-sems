
### Organization of the Internet

* The Internet is a global network of interconnected computers. It is made up of millions of computers, servers, routers, and other devices that communicate with each other to exchange data.

* The Internet is organized into a hierarchy of networks, with each network connected to the next. At the top of the hierarchy is the Internet backbone, which is made up of large, high-speed networks that connect the major networks around the world.

* Below the backbone are regional and local networks, which are connected to the backbone via routers. These networks are responsible for transmitting data between computers within their region.

* At the lowest level of the hierarchy are the individual computers that are connected to the regional and local networks. These computers are responsible for sending and receiving data to and from other computers on the network.

* The Internet is also organized into domains, which are organized hierarchically. The highest level of the domain hierarchy is the top-level domain (TLD), which is followed by the second-level domain (SLD), and so on. Each domain is responsible for hosting websites and other services.

* The Internet is also organized into protocols, which are sets of rules that specify how data is transmitted over the network. The most commonly used protocols are HTTP, FTP, and SMTP.
