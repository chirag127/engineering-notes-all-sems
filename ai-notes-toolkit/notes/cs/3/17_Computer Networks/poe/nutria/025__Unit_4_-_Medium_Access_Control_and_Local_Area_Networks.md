
## Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

1. Medium Access Control (MAC) is a sublayer of the Data Link Layer in the OSI model. It is responsible for controlling how devices in a network gain access to a shared media. 
2. The two types of MAC protocols are distributed and centralized. Distributed protocols allow each device to access the medium independently, while centralized protocols require one device to control access. 
3. The most common distributed MAC protocol is Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD), which is used in Ethernet networks.
4. Local Area Networks (LANs) are networks that are limited to a small geographic area, such as a home, office, or building. 
5. LANs are typically used for sharing resources, such as files and printers, and for communication between computers. 
6. The most common type of LAN is Ethernet, which uses CSMA/CD for media access control. 
7. Other types of LANs include Token Ring, FDDI, and Wireless LANs. 
8. Each type of LAN has its own set of advantages and disadvantages, and can be used in different scenarios.
