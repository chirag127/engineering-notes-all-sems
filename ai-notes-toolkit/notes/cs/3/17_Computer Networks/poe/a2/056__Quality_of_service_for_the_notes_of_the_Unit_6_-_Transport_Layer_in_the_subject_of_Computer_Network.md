### Quality of service for the notes of the Unit 6 - Transport Layer in the subject of Computer Networks.

1. Throughput: It refers to the average rate of successful message delivery over a communication channel. This can be measured in bits per second (bps). Higher throughput leads to faster data transfer.
2. Delay: It refers to the time taken for a packet to be transmitted across a network from source to destination. It is also known as latency and is measured in seconds. Lower delay leads to faster delivery of packets.
3. Jitter: It refers to the variation in the delay of received packets. A lower jitter leads to more consistent delays which is desirable for real-time applications like video calling. High jitter can lead to packets arriving late and degradation in performance.
4. Packet loss: It refers to the failure of packets to reach their destination. Some packets may get dropped due to congestion, corruption, or errors. A lower packet loss leads to more complete data delivery which is important for applications like video streaming.

The above points cover the key quality of service metrics that are important to consider for the transport layer. By managing and optimizing these metrics, the transport layer can provide the desired level of service for various applications.
