### Network performance and transmission impairments for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks.

1. Bandwidth - The amount of data that can be transmitted through a network at a given time. It is measured in bits per second (bps). Higher bandwidth leads to faster data transfer.
2. Latency - The time taken for a packet of data to be transmitted across a network from one device to another. It is measured in milliseconds (ms) or microseconds (us). Lower latency is desired for applications like video calling or online gaming.
3. Jitter - The variation in the latency of received packets. It can lead to packets arriving out of order or delayed, thereby degrading performance. Low jitter is necessary for real-time applications.
4. Error rate - The ratio of packets received with errors to the total packets transmitted in a network. A lower error rate is needed for accurate data transmission. Errors can be corrected using techniques like retransmission of packets and error-correcting codes.
5. Interference - The unwanted obstruction of transmission of signals by external noise sources. It can lead to higher error rates and degradation in network performance. Proper shielding and frequency planning can be used to minimize interference.
6. Attenuation - The loss of signal strength as it propagates through the transmission medium. It results in weaker signals reaching the receiver, increasing the error rate. Boosting signal power and using signal repeaters can be solutions to attenuation.
