## Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

1. Medium Access Control
- Methods for coordinating transmission between multiple devices on a shared communication medium
- ALOHA: Nodes transmit whenever they have data to send, leading to collisions and retransmissions
- Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA): Nodes check for carrier signal from other transmissions before sending, reducing collisions
- CSMA/Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA): Extra steps taken to reserve the medium before transmitting to avoid collisions
- CSMA/Collision Detection (CSMA/CD): Detect collisions as they occur and handle with retransmissions (used in Ethernet)

2. Ethernet
- Most widespread LAN technology, using CSMA/CD
- Devices connect to a shared cable or wireless medium, synchronized through a clock signal
- Data transmitted in variable-length frames containing source/destination address, payload, and checksum
- Half-duplex means devices can only transmit or receive at a time, while full-duplex allows simultaneous transmission and reception
- Switched Ethernet uses a switch to direct frames between devices, increasing efficiency over a shared medium

3. Wireless LANs
- Use radio frequency signals instead of physical cables to connect devices
- More convenient but can be less reliable or secure
- Typically use spread-spectrum or frequency hopping techniques to prevent interference
- Can use the same MAC protocols as Ethernet (CSMA/CA is common) but with the added challenge of coordinating access among wireless devices and avoiding collisions
