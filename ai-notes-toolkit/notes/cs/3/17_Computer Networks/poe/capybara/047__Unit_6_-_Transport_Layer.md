

## Unit 6 - Transport Layer

The transport layer is responsible for ensuring reliable communication between two network devices. It provides end-to-end communication services for applications and performs certain functions, such as error recovery and flow control to ensure the delivery of data.

Here are the key points to remember about the transport layer:

- The transport layer is the fourth layer in the OSI model, and it is responsible for providing end-to-end communication services for applications.
- The transport layer provides two main protocols: TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) and UDP (User Datagram Protocol).
- TCP provides reliable, connection-oriented communication services that ensure the delivery of data from one device to another. It uses a three-way handshake to establish a connection, and it provides error recovery and flow control to ensure the delivery of data.
- UDP provides unreliable, connectionless communication services that do not guarantee the delivery of data. It is used for applications that require low latency and do not require reliability, such as video streaming and online gaming.
- The transport layer uses port numbers to identify applications and services on a device. There are 65,535 available port numbers, with well-known ports assigned to specific applications and services.
- The transport layer also performs segmentation and reassembly of data, breaking it into smaller segments for transmission and then reassembling it at the receiving end.
- The transport layer can also perform congestion control to prevent network congestion and ensure efficient use of network resources.
- Some common transport layer protocols include HTTP, FTP, SMTP, and Telnet.

Overall, the transport layer plays a critical role in ensuring reliable communication between network devices. Understanding its protocols and functions is essential for anyone working with network communications.
