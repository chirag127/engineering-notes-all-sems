## Unit 2 - Design process

The design process is a structured approach to creating and developing a product or solution. It involves several stages, including:

1. **Identifying the problem or need:** This involves understanding the problem or need that the design is intended to address. This can be done through research, observation, and interaction with potential users or customers.

2. **Defining the requirements:** Once the problem or need has been identified, the next step is to define the requirements for the design. This involves specifying the features and characteristics that the design must have in order to meet the needs of the users or customers.

3. **Generating ideas:** This stage involves coming up with a range of possible solutions to the problem or need. This can be done through brainstorming, sketching, and other ideation techniques.

4. **Developing and testing concepts:** Once a range of ideas has been generated, the next step is to develop and test the most promising concepts. This involves creating prototypes or models of the design and testing them to see how well they meet the requirements.

5. **Refining the design:** Based on the results of the testing, the design may need to be refined or improved. This can involve making changes to the design to improve its performance, usability, or other characteristics.

6. **Implementing the design:** Once the design has been refined and finalized, the next step is to implement it. This can involve manufacturing the product, building the solution, or otherwise putting the design into use.

7. **Evaluating the design:** After the design has been implemented, it is important to evaluate its success. This can involve gathering feedback from users or customers, measuring the design's performance, and making any necessary improvements.

The design process is an iterative process, meaning that it may involve going back and forth between stages until a satisfactory solution is reached. It is also a collaborative process, often involving input from a range of stakeholders, including designers, engineers, users, and customers.
