### Unit 1 - Introduction: The Concept of Direct Manipulation

Direct manipulation is a concept in Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) that refers to the way users interact with digital systems. It is characterized by the following features:

1. **Visibility of the object of interest:** The objects and their actions are visible and easily identifiable on the screen.
2. **Rapid, reversible, incremental actions:** The user can perform actions quickly, and if a mistake is made, it can be easily undone. The actions are also incremental, allowing the user to see the effects of their actions immediately.
3. **Physical actions or labeled button presses:** The user interacts with the system through physical actions such as dragging and dropping, or by pressing labeled buttons, rather than through complex command languages.
4. **Immediate and continuous feedback:** The system provides immediate and continuous feedback to the user, allowing them to see the effects of their actions in real-time.

Direct manipulation interfaces are considered to be more intuitive and user-friendly than other types of interfaces, as they allow users to interact with digital systems in a way that closely mimics the way they interact with physical objects in the real world. This can make it easier for users to learn and use the system, and can also increase user satisfaction and engagement.
