# Unit 2 - Design Process

The design process is a structured approach to creating and developing a product or solution. It involves several stages, including:

1. **Identifying the problem or need:** This involves understanding the problem or need that the design is intended to address. This may involve research, observation, and analysis to gather information and insights.

2. **Defining the design criteria:** This involves establishing the requirements and constraints that the design must meet. This may include functional, aesthetic, and technical criteria, as well as considerations such as cost, safety, and sustainability.

3. **Generating ideas:** This involves brainstorming and exploring different ideas and concepts for the design. This may involve sketching, modeling, and prototyping to visualize and test different options.

4. **Evaluating and selecting a solution:** This involves assessing the different ideas and concepts against the design criteria to determine the most promising solution. This may involve testing, analysis, and feedback from stakeholders to refine and improve the design.

5. **Developing and refining the design:** This involves further developing and refining the selected solution to create a detailed and finalized design. This may involve technical drawings, specifications, and documentation to communicate the design to others.

6. **Implementing the design:** This involves bringing the design to life by building, manufacturing, or constructing the final product or solution. This may involve sourcing materials, assembling components, and testing the final product to ensure it meets the design criteria.

7. **Evaluating the design:** This involves evaluating the final product or solution to determine its effectiveness and success in meeting the design criteria. This may involve feedback from users, testing, and analysis to identify areas for improvement and refinement.

The design process is an iterative and collaborative approach that involves input and feedback from a range of stakeholders, including designers, engineers, users, and others. It is a flexible and adaptable process that can be applied to a wide range of design challenges and contexts.
