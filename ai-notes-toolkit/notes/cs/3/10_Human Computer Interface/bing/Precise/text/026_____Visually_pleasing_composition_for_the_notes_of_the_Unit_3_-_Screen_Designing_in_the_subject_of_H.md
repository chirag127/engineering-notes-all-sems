### Visually pleasing composition for the notes of the Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the subject of Human Computer Interface

1. **Balance**: Balance refers to the distribution of visual weight in a design. A balanced composition feels stable and aesthetically pleasing. There are two types of balance: symmetrical and asymmetrical. Symmetrical balance is achieved when elements are arranged evenly on either side of a central axis, while asymmetrical balance is achieved through the use of contrast and scale to create visual interest.

2. **Contrast**: Contrast is the use of opposing elements to create visual interest. This can be achieved through the use of color, shape, texture, and size. Contrast can help to guide the viewer's eye and create a focal point in a design.

3. **Alignment**: Alignment refers to the arrangement of elements in relation to a common line or edge. Proper alignment can help to create a sense of order and organization in a design.

4. **Proximity**: Proximity refers to the grouping of related elements. By grouping related elements together, a designer can create a sense of unity and organization in a design.

5. **Repetition**: Repetition is the use of recurring elements to create a sense of consistency and unity in a design. This can be achieved through the use of color, shape, texture, and size.

6. **White Space**: White space, also known as negative space, refers to the areas of a design that are left unoccupied. White space can help to create a sense of balance and contrast in a design, and can also be used to guide the viewer's eye.

7. **Hierarchy**: Hierarchy refers to the arrangement of elements in order of importance. This can be achieved through the use of size, color, contrast, and placement. Hierarchy can help to guide the viewer's eye and create a sense of order in a design.

By incorporating these principles into your screen designs, you can create visually pleasing compositions that are both aesthetically pleasing and easy to use.
