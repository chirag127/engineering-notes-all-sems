# Human interaction with computers for the notes of the Unit 2 - Design process

Human interaction with computers (HCI) is a design field that focuses on the interfaces between people and computers. HCI incorporates multiple disciplines, such as computer science, psychology, human factors, and ergonomics, into one field. The goal of HCI is to create intuitive and usable interfaces that meet the needs, goals and behaviors of the users.

The design process of HCI involves the following steps:

- Conduct user research: This step involves understanding the needs, goals and behaviors of the people who will use the product. User research methods include interviews, surveys, observations, personas, scenarios, etc.
- Use familiar design patterns: This step involves using common and consistent design elements and interactions that the users are familiar with. Design patterns help users learn and use the product more easily and efficiently.
- Make interactions efficient and predictable: This step involves designing the product in a way that minimizes the user's effort and cognitive load, and provides clear feedback and guidance. Interactions should be simple, intuitive, and consistent.
- Craft aesthetically pleasing design: This step involves creating a design that is visually appealing and engaging, and that matches the user's expectations and preferences. Aesthetics can influence the user's perception, emotion, and satisfaction with the product.
- Define usability metrics that guide product development: This step involves setting measurable and testable criteria that evaluate the product's usability and user experience. Usability metrics include effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction, learnability, memorability, etc.
