# Characteristics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Human Computer Interface

- Human Computer Interface (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers and other technologies.
- HCI aims to design, evaluate, and improve the usability, accessibility, and user experience of computer systems and applications.
- HCI is an interdisciplinary field that draws from computer science, psychology, design, engineering, and social sciences.
- Some of the characteristics of HCI are:

  - User-centered: HCI focuses on the needs, preferences, and goals of the users, and involves them in the design and evaluation process.
  - Contextual: HCI considers the physical, social, and cultural context of the users and the tasks they perform with the computer system.
  - Iterative: HCI follows an iterative cycle of prototyping, testing, and refining the design based on user feedback and evaluation results.
  - Evaluative: HCI uses various methods and metrics to measure the usability, accessibility, and user experience of the computer system, such as user satisfaction, task completion, error rate, and cognitive load.
  - Creative: HCI encourages creativity and innovation in designing novel and engaging user interfaces that meet the user and task requirements.
