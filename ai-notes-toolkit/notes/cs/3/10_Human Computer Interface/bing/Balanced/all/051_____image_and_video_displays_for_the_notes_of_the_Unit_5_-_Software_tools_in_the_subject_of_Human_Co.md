# Image and Video Displays for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Software Tools in the Subject of Human Computer Interface

- Image and video displays are types of screenless displays that use different technologies to project images or videos onto various surfaces or into the air without using a physical screen.
- Image and video displays can be used for various purposes in human-computer interaction (HCI), such as enhancing the user experience, providing feedback, creating immersive environments, or facilitating communication.
- Some examples of image and video displays are:
  - Holographic display: A display that creates a three-dimensional image of an object or scene by using interference and diffraction of light waves.
  - Virtual reality goggles: A display that covers the user's eyes and provides a simulated view of a virtual environment that responds to the user's head movements and inputs.
  - Heads up display: A display that projects information or graphics onto a transparent surface, such as a windshield or a helmet visor, so that the user can see them without looking away from their primary task.
  - Smart-room scenario: A display that uses cameras, sensors, and projectors to create an interactive environment that can recognize and respond to the user's gestures, voice, or actions.
  - Augmented reality game: A display that overlays virtual elements onto the real world, such as characters, objects, or information, and allows the user to interact with them through a device, such as a smartphone or a wearable.
- Image and video processing tools are software tools that enable the creation, manipulation, analysis, and presentation of image and video data for HCI applications.
- Some examples of image and video processing tools are:
  - Image and video capture tools: Tools that allow the acquisition of image or video data from various sources, such as cameras, scanners, or sensors.
  - Image and video enhancement tools: Tools that improve the quality or appearance of image or video data, such as by adjusting the brightness, contrast, color, or resolution.
  - Image and video segmentation tools: Tools that divide image or video data into meaningful regions or objects, such as by using edge detection, thresholding, or clustering algorithms.
  - Image and video recognition tools: Tools that identify or classify image or video data based on their features, such as by using face recognition, object recognition, or optical character recognition algorithms.
  - Image and video synthesis tools: Tools that generate new image or video data from existing or novel sources, such as by using computer graphics, animation, or deep learning algorithms.
  - Image and video display tools: Tools that present image or video data on various devices or surfaces, such as by using projection, rendering, or compression algorithms.
