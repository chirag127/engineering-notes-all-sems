# Presentation information simply and meaningfully for the notes of the Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the subject of Human Computer Interface

- Screen design is the process of creating graphical user interfaces (GUIs) for computer systems and applications. Screen design includes the layout, appearance, and functionality of the interface elements, such as windows, menus, icons, buttons, and text boxes.
- Human-computer interaction (HCI) is a multidisciplinary field that studies the design, implementation, and evaluation of computer systems and related phenomena that are for human use. HCI aims to create intuitive and usable interfaces that meet the needs and preferences of the users  .
- Presentation information simply and meaningfully is one of the principles of HCI that guides the screen design process. It means that the interface should display the information in a clear, concise, and consistent manner, using appropriate visual elements, such as colors, fonts, graphics, and icons, to enhance the comprehension and retention of the information .
- Some of the benefits of presenting information simply and meaningfully are:
  - It reduces the cognitive load and mental effort of the users, as they can easily understand and remember the information.
  - It increases the efficiency and effectiveness of the users, as they can perform their tasks faster and with fewer errors.
  - It improves the user satisfaction and engagement, as they can enjoy and appreciate the interface aesthetics and functionality.
- Some of the guidelines for presenting information simply and meaningfully are:
  - Use simple and familiar words and phrases, and avoid jargon and technical terms, unless they are necessary and well-defined.
  - Use short and meaningful labels and headings, and avoid abbreviations and acronyms, unless they are common and well-known.
  - Use consistent terminology and formatting throughout the interface, and follow the conventions and standards of the domain and the platform.
  - Use appropriate fonts and font sizes, and ensure that the text is legible and readable, considering the contrast, alignment, spacing, and line length.
  - Use colors and graphics sparingly and purposefully, and ensure that they are relevant, informative, and attractive, considering the contrast, harmony, and accessibility.
  - Use icons and symbols that are recognizable and intuitive, and provide text labels or tooltips for clarification, if needed.
  - Organize and group the information logically and hierarchically, and use white space and visual cues, such as borders, lines, and boxes, to separate and emphasize the information.
  - Provide feedback and guidance to the users, and indicate the status, progress, and results of their actions, using appropriate visual elements, such as messages, indicators, and sounds.
