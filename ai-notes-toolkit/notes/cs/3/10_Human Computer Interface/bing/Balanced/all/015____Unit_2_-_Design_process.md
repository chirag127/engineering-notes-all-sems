## Unit 2 - Design process

The design process is a systematic and creative approach to solving problems and developing solutions. It involves identifying the needs and requirements of the users, generating and evaluating ideas, prototyping and testing, and implementing and communicating the final outcome.

The design process can be divided into five main stages:

- **Empathize**: This stage involves understanding the problem and the context from the perspective of the users and stakeholders. It requires conducting research, such as interviews, surveys, observations, and data analysis, to gather information about the needs, goals, challenges, and expectations of the users and stakeholders. Empathizing helps to define the problem statement and the scope of the project.

- **Define**: This stage involves synthesizing the information gathered from the empathize stage and defining the core problem and the user needs. It requires creating a clear and concise problem statement that summarizes the challenge and the desired outcome. It also requires defining the criteria and constraints for the solution, such as the budget, timeline, resources, and specifications.

- **Ideate**: This stage involves generating and exploring a wide range of possible solutions to the problem. It requires using various brainstorming techniques, such as mind mapping, sketching, SCAMPER, and 6-3-5 method, to generate as many ideas as possible without judging or filtering them. It also requires selecting and refining the most promising ideas based on the criteria and constraints.

- **Prototype**: This stage involves creating and testing low-fidelity models or representations of the selected ideas. It requires using various prototyping methods, such as paper prototyping, wireframing, mockups, and storyboarding, to create simple and inexpensive prototypes that can demonstrate the key features and functions of the solution. It also requires testing the prototypes with the users and stakeholders to gather feedback and evaluate the usability, feasibility, and desirability of the solution.

- **Test**: This stage involves creating and testing high-fidelity prototypes or final products of the selected solution. It requires using various testing methods, such as usability testing, user acceptance testing, beta testing, and A/B testing, to test the solution with the users and stakeholders in real or simulated environments. It also requires collecting and analyzing the data and feedback from the testing and making improvements and refinements to the solution based on the results.

The design process is not linear or fixed, but iterative and flexible. It allows for going back and forth between the stages, revisiting and revising the problem statement, the ideas, the prototypes, and the tests, as new information and insights emerge. The design process is also collaborative and multidisciplinary, involving the participation and contribution of various stakeholders, such as the users, the clients, the designers, the developers, the testers, and the experts. The design process aims to create solutions that are user-centered, innovative, and effective.
