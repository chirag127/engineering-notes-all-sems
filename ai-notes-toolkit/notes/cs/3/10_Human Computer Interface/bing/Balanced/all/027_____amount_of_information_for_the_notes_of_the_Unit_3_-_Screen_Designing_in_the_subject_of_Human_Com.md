# Unit 3 - Screen Designing

## Introduction

Screen designing is the process of creating the visual layout and appearance of a user interface for a software application or a website. Screen designing involves the use of various elements such as colors, fonts, icons, images, animations, and interactions to convey information and functionality to the users. Screen designing is an important aspect of human-computer interaction, as it affects the usability, accessibility, and aesthetics of a system.

## Objectives

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the principles and guidelines of screen designing
- Apply the concepts of visual hierarchy, alignment, consistency, and contrast to create effective screen layouts
- Use appropriate colors, fonts, and icons to enhance the readability and attractiveness of screen elements
- Design screen elements such as menus, buttons, forms, and dialogs to support user tasks and feedback
- Evaluate and test screen designs for usability and user satisfaction

## Content

### Principles and Guidelines of Screen Designing

Screen designing is not a random or arbitrary process, but rather a systematic and disciplined one that follows some general principles and guidelines. These principles and guidelines are based on the knowledge of human perception, cognition, and behavior, as well as the best practices and conventions of the domain and platform. Some of the common principles and guidelines of screen designing are:

- **Know your users and their goals**: Screen designing should be user-centered, meaning that it should take into account the characteristics, needs, preferences, and expectations of the target users and their tasks. Screen designing should also support the users' goals and motivations, and provide them with a clear and meaningful purpose for using the system.
- **Simplify and minimize**: Screen designing should aim to reduce the cognitive load and the visual clutter on the screen, by eliminating unnecessary or redundant information and elements, and by organizing and grouping related items. Screen designing should also use simple and familiar words and symbols, and avoid technical jargon and acronyms.
- **Provide feedback and guidance**: Screen designing should provide the users with timely and appropriate feedback and guidance, to inform them of the system status, the results of their actions, and the possible errors and how to recover from them. Screen designing should also use cues and affordances to indicate the functionality and interactivity of screen elements, and to help the users navigate and explore the system.
- **Ensure consistency and standards**: Screen designing should ensure consistency and standards across the system, as well as with the external conventions and expectations of the users. Screen designing should use consistent terminology, colors, fonts, icons, and layouts throughout the system, and follow the common rules and patterns of the domain and platform. Screen designing should also adhere to the accessibility and internationalization standards, to accommodate the diverse needs and preferences of the users.

### Visual Hierarchy, Alignment, Consistency, and Contrast

Visual hierarchy, alignment, consistency, and contrast are some of the key concepts of screen designing that affect the visual layout and appearance of the screen elements. These concepts are based on the Gestalt principles of visual perception, which describe how humans tend to organize and interpret visual information. These concepts are:

- **Visual hierarchy**: Visual hierarchy is the order of importance and attention that the screen elements receive from the users, based on their size, position, color, shape, and other visual attributes. Visual hierarchy helps the users to scan and understand the screen content, and to focus on the most relevant and important information and elements. Screen designing should use visual hierarchy to create a clear and logical structure and flow of the screen content, and to emphasize the main points and actions.
- **Alignment**: Alignment is the arrangement and alignment of the screen elements along the horizontal and vertical axes, or along a common edge or center. Alignment helps to create a sense of order, balance, and harmony among the screen elements, and to reduce the visual noise and confusion on the screen. Screen designing should use alignment to organize and group the screen elements, and to create a consistent and professional look and feel of the screen.
- **Consistency**: Consistency is the similarity and uniformity of the screen elements in terms of their appearance, behavior, and functionality. Consistency helps to create a sense of familiarity and predictability among the screen elements, and to reduce the cognitive load and the learning curve of the users. Screen designing should use consistency to reinforce the identity and the message of the system, and to enhance the usability and the user satisfaction of the system.
