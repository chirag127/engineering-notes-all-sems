# Importance of 8 good design principles for human computer interface

Human computer interface (HCI) is the field of study that focuses on the interaction between humans and computers. HCI aims to design interfaces that are easy to use, efficient, and satisfying for the users. One of the most influential sources of HCI design principles is Shneiderman's eight golden rules of interface design  , which are:

- **Strive for consistency** by utilizing familiar icons, colors, menu hierarchy, call-to-actions, and user flows when designing similar situations and sequence of actions. Consistency reduces the cognitive load and confusion for the users, and helps them learn and remember the interface faster.
- **Enable frequent users to use shortcuts** such as keyboard commands, abbreviations, function keys, hidden commands, etc. Shortcuts allow users to perform tasks more quickly and efficiently, and enhance their sense of control and mastery over the interface.
- **Offer informative feedback** for every user action, such as visual, auditory, or tactile cues, progress indicators, confirmation messages, error messages, etc. Feedback informs the users about the state and outcome of their actions, and reassures them that the system is responsive and reliable.
- **Design dialogs to yield closure** by organizing the interface into meaningful and coherent groups of actions, such as steps, stages, phases, etc. Dialogs should have a clear beginning, middle, and end, and provide feedback when a group of actions is completed. Closure helps the users understand the structure and logic of the interface, and gives them a sense of accomplishment and satisfaction.
- **Prevent errors** by designing the interface to avoid or minimize the possibility of user mistakes, such as incorrect inputs, invalid commands, unintended actions, etc. Errors can be prevented by using constraints, warnings, confirmations, undo functions, etc. Preventing errors reduces the frustration and dissatisfaction of the users, and improves the usability and reliability of the interface.
- **Permit easy reversal of actions** by allowing the users to undo or redo their actions, such as using the back button, the cancel button, the undo command, the history function, etc. Reversal of actions enables the users to recover from errors, explore different options, and change their minds without penalty. Reversal of actions increases the confidence and comfort of the users, and encourages them to experiment and learn from the interface.
- **Support internal locus of control** by designing the interface to give the users the initiative and the freedom to perform their tasks, such as using direct manipulation, providing multiple ways to accomplish a goal, allowing customization, etc. Internal locus of control empowers the users to feel that they are in charge of the interface, and not the other way around. Internal locus of control enhances the motivation and engagement of the users, and fosters a positive attitude towards the interface.
- **Reduce short-term memory load** by designing the interface to minimize the amount of information that the users have to remember and process, such as using simple and natural language, providing visual cues, chunking information, etc. Reducing short-term memory load eases the mental effort and strain for the users, and improves their performance and satisfaction.

These eight principles are not exhaustive or absolute, but they provide a useful and practical framework for designing effective and user-friendly interfaces. By following these principles, HCI designers can create interfaces that meet the needs and expectations of the users, and enhance their experience and interaction with the computers.
