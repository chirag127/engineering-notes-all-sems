### Statistical Graphics

- Statistical graphics are graphics used in the field of statistics for data visualization.
- They allow the display of numerical or tabular data in some sort of pictorial form, such as bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs, scatterplots, and maps .
- They can be used for different purposes, such as:
  - presenting data and the results of statistical analysis in a clear and concise way
  - assisting in the analysis of data by revealing patterns, trends, outliers, and relationships 
  - facilitating statistical computation by providing visual aids or shortcuts
- They can be classified into different types, such as:
  - univariate graphs, which show the distribution of a single variable, such as histograms, boxplots, and density plots
  - bivariate graphs, which show the relationship between two variables, such as scatterplots, line graphs, and bar graphs
  - multivariate graphs, which show the relationship among three or more variables, such as contour plots, bubble charts, and parallel coordinates plots
  - special-purpose graphs, which show specific types of data or analysis, such as time series plots, statistical maps, and mosaic plots
- They can be designed and evaluated according to some principles, such as:
  - clarity, which means the graph should convey the intended message without ambiguity or confusion
  - accuracy, which means the graph should represent the data faithfully and avoid distortion or deception
  - simplicity, which means the graph should use the minimum amount of ink or pixels to convey the information
  - aesthetics, which means the graph should be pleasing to the eye and use appropriate colors, shapes, and fonts
  - relevance, which means the graph should suit the purpose, audience, and context of the data analysis
