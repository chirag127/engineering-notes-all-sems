### Importance of 8 good design principles for human computer interface

Human computer interface (HCI) is the field of study that focuses on the interaction between humans and computers. HCI aims to design interfaces that are easy to use, efficient, and satisfying for the users. A good interface can enhance the user experience, productivity, and satisfaction, while a bad interface can cause frustration, errors, and dissatisfaction.

One of the most influential and widely cited sources of HCI design principles is Shneiderman's eight golden rules of interface design  . These rules provide a convenient and succinct summary of the key principles of interface design. The eight rules are:

- **Strive for consistency** by utilizing familiar icons, colors, menu hierarchy, call-to-actions, and user flows when designing similar situations and sequence of actions. Consistency reduces the cognitive load and learning curve for the users, and increases their confidence and efficiency.
- **Enable frequent users to use shortcuts** such as keyboard commands, abbreviations, function keys, hidden commands, etc. Shortcuts allow expert users to perform tasks faster and more conveniently, and enhance their sense of control and satisfaction.
- **Offer informative feedback** for every user action, such as visual, auditory, or tactile cues, progress indicators, confirmation messages, error messages, etc. Feedback informs the users about the system status, the outcome of their actions, and the possible next steps. Feedback enhances the user's understanding, engagement, and trust in the system.
- **Design dialogs to yield closure** by organizing the tasks into meaningful groups or sequences, and providing clear indications of the beginning, middle, and end of each group or sequence. Closure helps the users to keep track of their progress, goals, and expectations, and gives them a sense of accomplishment and satisfaction.
- **Prevent errors** by designing the interface to avoid or minimize the possibility of user mistakes, such as incorrect inputs, invalid commands, unintended actions, etc. Error prevention reduces the user's frustration, confusion, and dissatisfaction, and improves the system's reliability and usability.
- **Permit easy reversal of actions** by providing undo, redo, cancel, back, or other similar functions that allow the users to correct their mistakes or change their minds. Reversibility increases the user's confidence and willingness to explore the system, and reduces the negative consequences of errors.
- **Support internal locus of control** by designing the interface to give the users the initiative, flexibility, and freedom to perform their tasks, rather than forcing them to follow the system's pace, sequence, or logic. Locus of control enhances the user's sense of autonomy, empowerment, and satisfaction.
- **Reduce short-term memory load** by designing the interface to minimize the amount of information, options, or actions that the users have to remember or process at any given time, such as by using visual cues, chunking, recognition rather than recall, etc. Memory load reduction improves the user's efficiency, accuracy, and satisfaction.

These eight rules are not exhaustive or absolute, but they provide a useful framework and guidelines for designing effective and user-friendly interfaces. By following these rules, HCI designers can create interfaces that meet the user's needs, preferences, and expectations, and improve the overall quality of human-computer interaction.
