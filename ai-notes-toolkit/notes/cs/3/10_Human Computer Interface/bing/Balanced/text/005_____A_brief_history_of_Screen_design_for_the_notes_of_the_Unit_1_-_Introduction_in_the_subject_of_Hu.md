### A brief history of screen design

- Screen design is the process of creating visual interfaces for digital devices, such as computers, smartphones, and tablets.
- Screen design has evolved over time, influenced by technological developments, user needs, and aesthetic trends.
- Some of the major milestones in the history of screen design are:

  - The first screen displays were attached to computers in the 1950s, using cathode ray tubes (CRTs) that emitted beams of electrons onto phosphor-coated screens. These screens could only display monochrome text and simple graphics, and had low resolution and refresh rates.
  - In the 1970s, screen design was mainly driven by developers, who created text-based interfaces with many fields, commands, and menus. These screens were often cluttered, confusing, and inconsistent, and required users to memorize complex syntax and commands.
  - In the 1980s, graphical user interfaces (GUIs) emerged, inspired by the work of Xerox PARC and popularized by Apple and Microsoft. GUIs used icons, windows, menus, and pointers to represent objects and actions, and allowed users to interact with the screen using a mouse. GUIs made screen design more user-friendly, intuitive, and attractive, and opened up new possibilities for graphics, animation, and multimedia.
  - In the 1990s, the advent of the World Wide Web and the Internet revolutionized screen design, as web pages became the dominant form of digital content. Web design required new skills and tools, such as HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and introduced new challenges, such as cross-browser compatibility, accessibility, and usability. Web design also incorporated elements from print design, such as typography, layout, and color.
  - In the 2000s, screen design became more diverse and complex, as new devices, platforms, and applications emerged. Mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets, introduced new constraints and opportunities for screen design, such as smaller screens, touch input, and location-awareness. Screen design also became more interactive, dynamic, and personalized, as web applications, social media, and user-generated content became more prevalent.
  - In the 2010s, screen design continued to evolve and innovate, as new technologies, such as artificial intelligence, augmented reality, and virtual reality, created new possibilities and challenges for screen design. Screen design also became more responsive, adaptive, and inclusive, as designers aimed to create screen experiences that could accommodate different devices, contexts, and users. Screen design also became more collaborative, participatory, and ethical, as designers engaged with users and stakeholders, and considered the social and environmental impacts of their work.
