### Importance of 8 human characteristics for design process

Human characteristics are the qualities and traits that define humans as a species and as individuals. They include physical, cognitive, emotional, social, and cultural aspects of human behavior and experience. Understanding human characteristics is essential for designing products, services, and systems that are user-friendly, effective, and satisfying. Some of the important human characteristics in design are:

- **Perception**: Perception is the process of interpreting sensory information from the environment. Perception is influenced by factors such as attention, expectations, context, and prior knowledge. Designers need to consider how users perceive their products, and how to make them appealing, intuitive, and accessible. For example, designers can use colors, shapes, sounds, and textures to convey meaning, attract attention, and elicit emotions.
- **Memory**: Memory is the ability to store and retrieve information over time. Memory can be divided into short-term memory, which holds a limited amount of information for a few seconds or minutes, and long-term memory, which stores more information for longer periods of time. Designers need to consider how users remember their products, and how to reduce the cognitive load and enhance the recall. For example, designers can use mnemonics, chunking, repetition, and feedback to help users remember important information and actions.
- **Learning**: Learning is the process of acquiring new knowledge and skills through experience, observation, or instruction. Learning can be intentional or incidental, and can involve different types of cognitive processes, such as reasoning, problem-solving, and creativity. Designers need to consider how users learn their products, and how to support their learning goals and needs. For example, designers can use tutorials, guides, examples, and challenges to help users learn how to use their products effectively and efficiently.
- **Motivation**: Motivation is the drive that influences human behavior and actions. Motivation can be intrinsic or extrinsic, and can be affected by factors such as goals, values, interests, and rewards. Designers need to consider what motivates users to use their products, and how to enhance their motivation and engagement. For example, designers can use gamification, personalization, feedback, and social features to make their products more fun, relevant, rewarding, and social.
- **Emotion**: Emotion is the affective state that accompanies human experiences and reactions. Emotion can be positive or negative, and can influence human cognition, behavior, and well-being. Designers need to consider how users feel about their products, and how to elicit positive emotions and avoid negative ones. For example, designers can use aesthetics, humor, stories, and empathy to make their products more attractive, enjoyable, meaningful, and human-centered .
- **Communication**: Communication is the exchange of information and ideas between humans through verbal and non-verbal means. Communication can involve different modes, such as speech, writing, gestures, and images. Designers need to consider how users communicate with their products, and how to facilitate effective and efficient communication. For example, designers can use natural language, icons, symbols, and animations to make their products more understandable, expressive, and interactive.
- **Sociality**: Sociality is the tendency and ability of humans to form social relationships and groups. Sociality can involve different aspects, such as cooperation, competition, trust, and norms. Designers need to consider how users interact with other users through their products, and how to foster positive and productive social interactions. For example, designers can use social media, communities, ratings, and recommendations to make their products more collaborative, supportive, and trustworthy.
- **Culture**: Culture is the set of shared values, beliefs, practices, and artifacts that characterize a human group or society. Culture can influence human behavior, preferences, and expectations. Designers need to consider how users are influenced by their cultural backgrounds and contexts, and how to respect and accommodate their cultural diversity. For example, designers can use localization, customization, and inclusivity to make their products more adaptable, relevant, and respectful.
