### Visually pleasing composition for the notes of the Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the subject of Human Computer Interface

- Screen design is the process of creating the visual appearance and layout of a digital interface, such as a website, an app, or a game.
- Screen design involves applying the principles of visual communication, user experience, and interaction design to create a coherent and engaging interface that meets the needs and expectations of the users and the goals of the project.
- Screen design is influenced by various factors, such as the content, the context, the platform, the device, the audience, the brand, and the trends.
- Screen design requires a balance between aesthetics and functionality, as well as consistency and creativity.
- Screen design can be improved by following some basic principles, such as:

  - **Visual hierarchy**: This is the order of importance and attention that the elements of a screen receive from the viewers. Visual hierarchy can be achieved by using different sizes, colors, shapes, positions, contrasts, and alignments to emphasize the most relevant and important information and actions on a screen. Visual hierarchy helps the viewers to scan, understand, and navigate the interface easily and efficiently .
  - **Copy**: This is the text content that appears on a screen, such as headlines, labels, buttons, instructions, and messages. Copy is part of the design and should be clear, concise, and consistent with the tone and voice of the project. Copy should also be legible, readable, and accessible, using appropriate fonts, sizes, colors, and spacing .
  - **Conventions**: These are the common and expected ways of designing and interacting with a screen, such as the placement of the logo, the menu, the search bar, the back button, the scroll bar, and the icons. Conventions help the viewers to recognize and use the interface intuitively and confidently, without confusion or frustration. Conventions should be followed unless there is a good reason to break them or to innovate them .
  - **Mobile design**: This is the design of screens that are intended to be viewed and used on mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets. Mobile design requires special attention to the size, resolution, orientation, and touch input of the device, as well as the context and environment of the user. Mobile design should be responsive, adaptive, and optimized for the mobile experience, using simple and clear layouts, large and visible elements, and minimal and relevant content .
  - **Effective surface area ratio**: This is the ratio of the area of the screen that is occupied by the content and the elements that are relevant and useful for the user, versus the area that is occupied by the background, the margins, the padding, and the elements that are not essential or distracting for the user. Effective surface area ratio can be increased by reducing the clutter, the noise, and the redundancy on a screen, and by using negative or white space to create breathing room and contrast for the content and the elements .
  - **Simplicity and transparency**: This is the principle of designing screens that are easy to comprehend and use, without unnecessary or excessive elements, features, or steps. Simplicity and transparency can be achieved by following the principle of "less is more", and by focusing on the core functionality and value of the interface. Simplicity and transparency can also be enhanced by using clear and consistent labels, icons, colors, and feedback, and by avoiding ambiguity, confusion, or hidden elements .
  - **Animation and motion**: These are the effects of movement and transformation that can be applied to the elements of a screen, such as transitions, transformations, rotations, scaling, fading, sliding, bouncing, and parallax. Animation and motion can be used to create interest, engagement, and delight for the viewers, as well as to convey meaning, direction, and feedback for the users. Animation and motion should be used sparingly, purposefully, and appropriately, and should not interfere with the usability, performance, or accessibility of the interface .
