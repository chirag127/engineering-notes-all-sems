### Selection of Device-Based and Screen-Based Controls for Windows

Device-based controls, often called input devices, are the mechanisms through which people communicate their desires to the system . Screen-based controls, often called widgets, are the graphical components that appear on the screen and allow users to interact with the system.

Some of the factors that influence the selection of device-based and screen-based controls for windows are:

- The characteristics and capabilities of the device-based controls, such as pointing accuracy, speed, feedback, and compatibility .
- The characteristics and capabilities of the screen-based controls, such as size, shape, color, label, and functionality.
- The nature and complexity of the task that the user wants to perform, such as selecting, entering, or manipulating data .
- The preferences and expectations of the user, such as familiarity, consistency, and ease of use .
- The constraints and limitations of the system, such as screen size, resolution, and performance .

Some of the guidelines for selecting the proper device-based and screen-based controls for windows are:

- Use device-based controls that match the user's physical and cognitive abilities, such as a mouse, keyboard, touch screen, or voice input .
- Use screen-based controls that match the user's mental model, such as buttons, menus, sliders, or checkboxes .
- Use device-based and screen-based controls that are compatible and consistent with each other, such as using a mouse to click on a button, or using a keyboard to type in a text box .
- Use device-based and screen-based controls that are appropriate for the task, such as using a mouse to select an option from a menu, or using a keyboard to enter a password .
- Use device-based and screen-based controls that are responsive and provide feedback, such as changing the cursor shape, highlighting the selected option, or displaying a message .
- Use device-based and screen-based controls that are adaptable and customizable, such as allowing the user to resize, move, or hide windows, or to change the settings or preferences of the controls .
- Use device-based and screen-based controls that are efficient and effective, such as minimizing the number of clicks, keystrokes, or gestures, or providing shortcuts or accelerators .
- Use device-based and screen-based controls that are clear and informative, such as using meaningful labels, icons, or symbols, or providing help or tips .
- Use device-based and screen-based controls that are aesthetically pleasing and visually appealing, such as using appropriate colors, fonts, or styles, or providing animations or sounds .

If the user has multiple displays, they can use the Windows + P "Project" menu to quickly change how Windows 10 handles them, such as extending, duplicating, or switching the displays. The user can also use the Settings > System > Display > Multiple displays option to detect, identify, arrange, or scale the displays .
