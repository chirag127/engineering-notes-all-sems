### Focus and Emphasis for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the Subject of Human Computer Interface

- Focus and emphasis are important principles of screen design that help to guide the user's attention and convey the meaning and importance of the information presented on the screen.
- Focus and emphasis can be achieved by using various techniques, such as contrast, alignment, proximity, repetition, hierarchy, color, typography, whitespace, and animation.
- Contrast is the difference between two or more elements on the screen, such as size, shape, color, brightness, or texture. Contrast can be used to create focal points, highlight important information, or separate different sections of the screen.
- Alignment is the arrangement of elements on the screen in relation to each other or to a common reference point, such as the edge, center, or baseline of the screen. Alignment can be used to create order, balance, harmony, or unity among the elements, or to show the relationship or connection between them.
- Proximity is the closeness or distance of elements on the screen. Proximity can be used to group or separate elements, to show the similarity or difference between them, or to indicate the level of importance or relevance of the information.
- Repetition is the use of the same or similar elements on the screen, such as color, shape, font, or style. Repetition can be used to create consistency, coherence, or identity among the elements, or to reinforce the message or theme of the screen.
- Hierarchy is the arrangement of elements on the screen according to their relative importance or significance, such as by size, position, color, or style. Hierarchy can be used to create structure, organization, or logic among the elements, or to show the order or sequence of the information.
- Color is the hue, saturation, and brightness of the elements on the screen. Color can be used to create contrast, harmony, mood, or emotion among the elements, or to convey the meaning or function of the information.
- Typography is the choice and arrangement of fonts, sizes, styles, and spacing of the text on the screen. Typography can be used to create contrast, hierarchy, readability, or personality among the text, or to communicate the tone or voice of the message.
- Whitespace is the empty or negative space on the screen, such as the margins, gaps, or padding between the elements. Whitespace can be used to create contrast, balance, clarity, or elegance on the screen, or to emphasize the positive or active space on the screen.
- Animation is the movement or change of the elements on the screen, such as by fading, sliding, rotating, or scaling. Animation can be used to create interest, engagement, or interactivity on the screen, or to show the transition or progression of the information.
