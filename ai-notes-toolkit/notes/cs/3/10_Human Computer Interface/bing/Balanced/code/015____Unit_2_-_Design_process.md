## Unit 2 - Design process

The design process is a systematic and creative approach to solving problems and developing solutions. It involves identifying the needs and requirements of the users, generating and evaluating ideas, prototyping and testing, and implementing and communicating the results.

The design process can be divided into five main stages:

- **Empathize**: This stage involves understanding the problem and the context from the perspective of the users and stakeholders. It involves conducting research, interviews, observations, surveys, and other methods to gather information and insights about the needs, wants, challenges, and emotions of the users and stakeholders.
- **Define**: This stage involves synthesizing and analyzing the information and insights gathered from the empathize stage, and defining the problem statement and the design criteria. The problem statement is a clear and concise description of the problem and the users that need to be addressed. The design criteria are the specifications and constraints that guide the design process and the evaluation of the solutions.
- **Ideate**: This stage involves generating and exploring a wide range of possible solutions that address the problem statement and meet the design criteria. It involves using various techniques such as brainstorming, mind mapping, sketching, storyboarding, and others to stimulate creativity and divergent thinking.
- **Prototype**: This stage involves creating and testing low-fidelity models or representations of the selected ideas from the ideate stage. The prototypes can be physical, digital, or conceptual, and they are used to test the feasibility, functionality, usability, and desirability of the solutions. The prototypes are also used to gather feedback and data from the users and stakeholders, and to identify the strengths and weaknesses of the solutions.
- **Test**: This stage involves evaluating and refining the prototypes and the solutions based on the feedback and data collected from the prototype stage. It involves measuring the performance, effectiveness, and satisfaction of the solutions against the problem statement and the design criteria, and identifying the areas for improvement and iteration. The test stage can also lead to new insights and questions that can inform the next cycle of the design process.

The design process is not linear or sequential, but rather iterative and cyclical. It involves going back and forth between the stages, and repeating the stages as needed, until a satisfactory solution is reached. The design process is also flexible and adaptable, and can be modified and customized according to the nature and scope of the problem, the resources and time available, and the preferences and skills of the designers.
