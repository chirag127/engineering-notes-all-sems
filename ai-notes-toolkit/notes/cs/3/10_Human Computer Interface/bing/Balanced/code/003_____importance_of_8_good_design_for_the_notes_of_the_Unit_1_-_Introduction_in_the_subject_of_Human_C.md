### Importance of 8 good design principles for human computer interface

Human computer interface (HCI) is the field of study that focuses on the interaction between humans and computers. HCI aims to design interfaces that are easy to use, efficient, and satisfying for the users. To achieve this goal, HCI designers should follow some general principles that guide their design decisions. One of the most widely cited sets of principles is Shneiderman's eight golden rules of interface design  , which are:

- **Strive for consistency** by utilizing familiar icons, colors, menu hierarchy, call-to-actions, and user flows when designing similar situations and sequence of actions. This helps users to learn and remember the interface and reduces confusion and errors.
- **Enable frequent users to use shortcuts** that can accelerate their performance and reduce their cognitive load. Shortcuts can be keyboard commands, hidden commands, macros, or abbreviations. They should be easy to learn and remember, and should not interfere with novice users.
- **Offer informative feedback** for every user action, especially for long-running tasks. Feedback should be clear, concise, and timely, and should indicate the progress and outcome of the user's action. Feedback can be visual, auditory, or tactile, depending on the context and the user's preference.
- **Design dialogues to yield closure** by organizing the interface into meaningful and coherent sequences of actions that have a clear beginning and end. Each dialogue should have a title, a purpose, and a completion indicator. Users should be able to exit or cancel the dialogue at any time.
- **Offer simple error handling** by preventing errors from occurring in the first place, or by providing clear and constructive error messages that help users to recover from errors. Error messages should be polite, precise, and indicate the cause and solution of the error. Users should be able to undo or redo their actions if possible.
- **Permit easy reversal of actions** by allowing users to undo or redo their actions without penalty or loss of data. This feature enhances user confidence and encourages exploration and experimentation. Users should be able to undo or redo multiple actions, and the interface should indicate the availability and scope of this feature.
- **Support internal locus of control** by giving users the sense that they are in charge of the interface and the system, rather than the other way around. Users should be able to initiate and terminate actions, customize the interface to their needs and preferences, and access the information and functions they need without unnecessary restrictions or delays.
- **Reduce short-term memory load** by minimizing the amount of information and actions that users have to remember and perform at any given time. The interface should use recognition rather than recall, provide visual cues and prompts, group and chunk information, and automate or eliminate repetitive tasks.

These eight principles are important for HCI design because they help to create interfaces that are user-friendly, effective, and enjoyable. They are based on the understanding of human cognitive and emotional processes, and the characteristics and limitations of computer systems. By following these principles, HCI designers can improve the user experience and the usability of their interfaces.
