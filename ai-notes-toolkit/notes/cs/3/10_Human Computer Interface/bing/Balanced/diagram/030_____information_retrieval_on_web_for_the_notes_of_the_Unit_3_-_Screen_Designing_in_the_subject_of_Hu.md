### Information retrieval on web

- Information retrieval (IR) is the process of finding and accessing relevant information from a large collection of documents, such as the web.
- IR involves matching a user's query, which is a formal statement of information need, to the best information about that query.
- IR models are ways of integrating various sources of evidence about documents, such as the links, the structure, the content, the quality, etc., to rank them according to their relevance to the query.
- IR on the web has some unique challenges and opportunities, such as:
  - The web is dynamic, heterogeneous, and unstructured, which requires efficient and scalable methods to crawl, index, and update the documents.
  - The web is rich in multimedia, such as images, videos, audio, etc., which requires effective methods to represent, analyze, and retrieve them.
  - The web is interactive, social, and personalized, which requires adaptive and intelligent methods to understand the user's intent, context, and preferences.
  - The web is a source of direct information, such as answers to questions, or tasks, such as appointment booking, which requires advanced methods to generate and extract them.
- IR on the web can be applied to various domains and applications, such as:
  - Web search engines, which are the most popular and widely used IR systems on the web.
  - Web mining, which is the process of discovering useful patterns and knowledge from the web data, such as web structure, web content, and web usage.
  - Web recommender systems, which are systems that suggest items or services to the users based on their preferences and behavior.
  - Web information extraction, which is the process of extracting structured information from unstructured or semi-structured web documents, such as entities, relations, events, etc..
  - Web question answering, which is the process of providing direct and concise answers to natural language questions posed by the users.
