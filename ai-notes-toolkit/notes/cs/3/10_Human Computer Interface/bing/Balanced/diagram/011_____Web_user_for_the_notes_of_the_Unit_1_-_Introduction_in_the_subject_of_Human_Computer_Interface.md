# Unit 1 - Introduction

## What is HCI?

- HCI stands for Human Computer Interface, which is the study of interaction between people (users) and computers.
- HCI deals with the design, execution and assessment of computer systems and related phenomenon that are for human use.
- HCI aims to improve the interactions between users and computers by making computers more usable and receptive to the user's needs.

## Why is HCI important?

- HCI is important because it affects the performance, satisfaction, safety and well-being of the users and the society.
- HCI is important because it helps to create systems that minimize the barrier between the human's cognitive model of what they want to accomplish and the computer's understanding of the user's task.
- HCI is important because it enables the development of new and innovative applications and services that can enhance the quality of life and work of the users.

## What are the components of HCI?

- HCI consists of three main components: the human, the computer and the interaction.
- The human component refers to the characteristics and capabilities of the users, such as their input/output channels, memory, reasoning and problem solving skills.
- The computer component refers to the hardware and software of the system, such as the devices, memory, processing and networks.
- The interaction component refers to the methods and techniques of communication between the human and the computer, such as the models, frameworks, ergonomics, styles, elements and paradigms.

## What are the challenges of HCI?

- HCI faces many challenges due to the complexity, diversity and dynamism of the human and computer factors involved in the interaction.
- Some of the challenges are:

  - Understanding the needs, preferences and expectations of the users and designing systems that can meet them.
  - Evaluating the usability, effectiveness and efficiency of the systems and improving them based on the feedback and data collected from the users.
  - Adapting the systems to the changing contexts, environments and technologies that affect the interaction.
  - Balancing the trade-offs between the functionality, aesthetics, accessibility and security of the systems.
  - Addressing the ethical, social and legal issues that arise from the interaction, such as privacy, trust, responsibility and inclusion.

## What are the sources of HCI?

- HCI draws from various disciplines and fields of knowledge, such as computer science, psychology, sociology, design, engineering, education, art and media.
- HCI integrates the theories, methods and tools from these sources to create a multidisciplinary and holistic approach to the interaction.
- HCI also contributes to these sources by providing new insights, challenges and opportunities for research and innovation.
