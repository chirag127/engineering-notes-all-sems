### Importance of 8 human characteristics for the design process

- Perception: Perception is our awareness and understanding of the elements and objects of our environment through the physical sensation of our various senses, including sight, sound, smell, and so forth. Perception is influenced, in part, by experience. Perception is important for the design process because it affects how users perceive and interact with the interface, and how they interpret the information and feedback provided by the system. A good design should consider the perceptual abilities and limitations of the users, and use appropriate visual, auditory, and tactile cues to enhance the user experience.
- Memory: Memory is the process of storing and retrieving information in the brain. Memory can be divided into three types: sensory memory, short-term memory, and long-term memory. Sensory memory holds information for a very brief period of time (less than a second), short-term memory holds information for a few seconds to a few minutes, and long-term memory holds information for a longer period of time (from hours to years). Memory is important for the design process because it affects how users learn and remember how to use the interface, and how they recall the information and tasks they need to perform. A good design should minimize the memory load of the users, and provide cues and reminders to help them recall the relevant information and actions.
- Attention: Attention is the process of selecting and focusing on a specific stimulus or task among many competing ones. Attention can be divided into two types: selective attention and divided attention. Selective attention is the ability to focus on one stimulus or task while ignoring others, and divided attention is the ability to perform two or more tasks simultaneously. Attention is important for the design process because it affects how users allocate their cognitive resources and prioritize their goals and actions. A good design should avoid distracting and irrelevant stimuli, and support the users' attention management and multitasking abilities.
- Learning: Learning is the process of acquiring new knowledge and skills through experience, observation, or instruction. Learning can be divided into two types: explicit learning and implicit learning. Explicit learning is the conscious and intentional acquisition of knowledge and skills, and implicit learning is the unconscious and incidental acquisition of knowledge and skills. Learning is important for the design process because it affects how users adapt to and master the interface, and how they improve their performance and satisfaction over time. A good design should facilitate both explicit and implicit learning, and provide clear and consistent feedback and guidance to the users.
- Motivation: Motivation is the process of initiating and sustaining goal-directed behavior. Motivation can be divided into two types: intrinsic motivation and extrinsic motivation. Intrinsic motivation is the internal drive to perform a behavior for its own sake, and extrinsic motivation is the external drive to perform a behavior for some reward or punishment. Motivation is important for the design process because it affects how users engage with and persist in using the interface, and how they evaluate their outcomes and experiences. A good design should stimulate both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, and provide meaningful and rewarding goals and challenges to the users.
- Emotion: Emotion is the process of experiencing and expressing affective states, such as happiness, sadness, anger, fear, etc. Emotion can be divided into two types: discrete emotion and dimensional emotion. Discrete emotion is the specific and distinct affective state that can be labeled by a word, and dimensional emotion is the general and continuous affective state that can be described by a scale, such as valence (positive or negative) and arousal (high or low). Emotion is important for the design process because it affects how users feel and react to the interface, and how they form their attitudes and preferences. A good design should elicit positive and appropriate emotions, and provide emotional support and feedback to the users.
- Empathy: Empathy is the process of understanding and sharing the feelings and perspectives of another person or group.
