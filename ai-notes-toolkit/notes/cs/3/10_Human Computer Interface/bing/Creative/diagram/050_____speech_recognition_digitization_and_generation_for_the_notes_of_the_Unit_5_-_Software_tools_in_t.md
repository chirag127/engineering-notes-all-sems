### Speech Recognition, Digitization, and Generation

- Speech recognition is the process of converting spoken audio into text or commands that can be understood by a computer system  .
- Speech digitization is the process of converting analog speech signals into digital data that can be stored, transmitted, or manipulated by a computer system .
- Speech generation is the process of converting text or commands into synthetic or natural speech that can be output by a computer system  .

#### Speech Recognition

- Speech recognition can be classified into two types: discrete-word recognition and continuous-speech recognition .
- Discrete-word recognition requires the speaker to pause between each word and is suitable for applications that have a limited vocabulary and grammar, such as voice dialing or menu navigation .
- Continuous-speech recognition allows the speaker to talk naturally without pauses and is suitable for applications that have a large vocabulary and grammar, such as dictation or transcription .
- Speech recognition systems typically consist of the following components :
  - Speech input: a microphone or other device that captures the speech signal and converts it into an electrical signal.
  - Feature extraction: a process that transforms the speech signal into a sequence of feature vectors that represent the acoustic characteristics of the speech.
  - Decoder: a process that uses acoustic models, pronunciation dictionaries, and language models to search for the most likely word sequence that matches the feature vectors.
  - Word output: a process that displays or executes the recognized word sequence.

#### Speech Digitization

- Speech digitization can be performed using two methods: waveform coding and parametric coding .
- Waveform coding preserves the shape of the speech signal and reproduces it as closely as possible. It requires a high bit rate and provides a high quality of speech. Examples of waveform coding are pulse code modulation (PCM) and adaptive differential pulse code modulation (ADPCM) .
- Parametric coding analyzes the speech signal and extracts its parameters, such as pitch, energy, and spectral envelope. It requires a low bit rate and provides a lower quality of speech. Examples of parametric coding are linear predictive coding (LPC) and code-excited linear prediction (CELP) .

#### Speech Generation

- Speech generation can be performed using two methods: speech synthesis and speech concatenation  .
- Speech synthesis generates speech from scratch using rules or models that describe how speech sounds are produced. It can produce any text or command, but it may sound unnatural or robotic. Examples of speech synthesis are formant synthesis and articulatory synthesis  .
- Speech concatenation combines pre-recorded speech segments to form new speech. It can produce natural sounding speech, but it may be limited by the availability and quality of the speech segments. Examples of speech concatenation are unit selection and diphone synthesis  .
