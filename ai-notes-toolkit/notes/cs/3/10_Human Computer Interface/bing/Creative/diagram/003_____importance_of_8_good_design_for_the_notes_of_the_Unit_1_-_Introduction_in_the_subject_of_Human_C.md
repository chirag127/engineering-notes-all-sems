### Importance of 8 good design principles for human computer interface

Human computer interface (HCI) is the field of study that focuses on the interaction between humans and computers. HCI aims to design interfaces that are intuitive, efficient, and satisfying for the users. To achieve this goal, HCI designers should follow some general principles that guide their design decisions. One of the most widely cited sets of principles is Shneiderman's eight golden rules of interface design  , which are:

- **Strive for consistency** by utilizing familiar icons, colors, menu hierarchy, call-to-actions, and user flows when designing similar situations and sequence of actions. Consistency reduces the cognitive load for the users and helps them learn the interface faster and easier.
- **Enable frequent users to use shortcuts** such as keyboard commands, abbreviations, function keys, hidden commands, etc. Shortcuts allow the users to perform their tasks more quickly and efficiently, and also increase their sense of control and mastery over the interface.
- **Offer informative feedback** for every user action, such as visual, auditory, or tactile cues, progress indicators, confirmation messages, error messages, etc. Feedback informs the users about the state and outcome of their actions, and helps them correct their errors and monitor their progress.
- **Design dialogues to yield closure** by organizing the interface into meaningful and coherent sequences of actions, such as steps, stages, phases, etc. Each sequence should have a clear beginning and end, and provide feedback on the completion of the task. Closure gives the users a sense of accomplishment and satisfaction, and motivates them to continue using the interface.
- **Offer simple error handling** by preventing, detecting, and recovering from errors. Errors should be avoided as much as possible by designing the interface to prevent invalid inputs, warn the users of potential errors, and provide undo and redo functions. Errors should also be detected and reported clearly and politely, and provide guidance on how to fix them. Error handling should minimize the negative impact of errors on the users' performance and emotions.
- **Permit easy reversal of actions** by allowing the users to undo and redo their actions, and to cancel or abort their tasks. Reversibility reduces the users' anxiety and fear of making mistakes, and encourages them to explore and experiment with the interface.
- **Support internal locus of control** by designing the interface to be responsive, flexible, and customizable to the users' needs, preferences, and goals. The interface should allow the users to initiate and terminate actions, control the pace and sequence of the interaction, and adjust the level of difficulty and complexity. Supporting internal locus of control enhances the users' sense of autonomy and empowerment, and increases their satisfaction and engagement with the interface.
- **Reduce short-term memory load** by minimizing the amount of information and actions that the users have to remember and perform at any given time. The interface should use recognition rather than recall, provide visual cues and reminders, group and chunk information, and automate or eliminate unnecessary tasks. Reducing short-term memory load improves the users' efficiency and accuracy, and reduces their mental fatigue and frustration.

These eight principles are important for designing effective and user-friendly interfaces, as they are based on the understanding of the human cognitive and emotional processes, and the characteristics and limitations of the human-computer interaction. Following these principles can help the HCI designers to create interfaces that are consistent, efficient, informative, satisfying, error-free, reversible, controllable, and memorable for the users.
