### Human interaction speeds

- Human interaction speeds are the rates at which people can perform tasks using different input and output methods, such as reading, listening, speaking, typing, etc.
- Human interaction speeds are important to consider in the design process of human computer interface, because they affect the usability, efficiency, and satisfaction of the users.
- Some factors that influence human interaction speeds are the complexity of the task, the familiarity of the user with the system, the environmental conditions, and the individual differences among users.
- Some typical human interaction speeds for common tasks are :

  - Reading prose text: 250-300 words per minute (wpm)
  - Proofreading text on paper: 200 wpm
  - Proofreading text on a monitor: 180 wpm
  - Listening to spoken words: 150-160 wpm
  - Speaking: 100-150 wpm
  - Typing on a keyboard: 40-60 wpm
  - Handwriting: 20-30 wpm
  - Pointing and clicking with a mouse: 2-4 seconds per target
  - Dragging and dropping with a mouse: 4-6 seconds per target
  - Touching a screen: 1-2 seconds per target
  - Swiping a screen: 0.5-1 second per gesture
  - Pinching a screen: 0.5-1 second per gesture

- Some general guidelines for designing human computer interfaces based on human interaction speeds are:

  - A response time of 100 milliseconds (ms) or less is perceived as instantaneous by the user, and should be the goal for most interactions.
  - A response time of 1 second or less is fast enough for the user to feel they are interacting freely with the information, and should be the norm for most interactions.
  - A response time of 10 seconds or more is too slow for the user to maintain their attention, and should be avoided or accompanied by feedback or progress indicators.
