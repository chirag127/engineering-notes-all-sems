### Colors

- Colors are important elements of visual design that can affect the user's perception, attention, emotion, and behavior.
- Colors can be used to convey meaning, create contrast, highlight important information, group related elements, and enhance aesthetics.
- Colors can also influence the user's mood, preference, and satisfaction with the interface.
- Colors can be described by three attributes: hue, saturation, and brightness.
  - Hue is the name of the color, such as red, green, or blue.
  - Saturation is the intensity or purity of the color, ranging from gray to vivid.
  - Brightness is the amount of light or darkness in the color, ranging from black to white.
- Colors can be classified into three categories: primary, secondary, and tertiary.
  - Primary colors are the basic colors that cannot be created by mixing other colors, such as red, green, and blue.
  - Secondary colors are the colors that are created by mixing two primary colors, such as yellow, cyan, and magenta.
  - Tertiary colors are the colors that are created by mixing a primary color and a secondary color, such as orange, green-yellow, and blue-violet.
- Colors can be arranged into different color models or systems that define how colors are represented and manipulated, such as RGB, CMYK, HSB, and CIE.
  - RGB (Red, Green, Blue) is a color model that uses additive color mixing to create colors by combining different amounts of red, green, and blue light. RGB is commonly used for digital displays, such as monitors and TVs.
  - CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black) is a color model that uses subtractive color mixing to create colors by removing different amounts of cyan, magenta, yellow, and black ink or pigment. CMYK is commonly used for printing, such as books and magazines.
  - HSB (Hue, Saturation, Brightness) is a color model that uses polar coordinates to represent colors by their hue, saturation, and brightness values. HSB is commonly used for color selection and manipulation, such as in graphic design software.
  - CIE (Commission Internationale de l'Eclairage) is a color model that uses a color space to represent colors by their chromaticity and luminance values. CIE is commonly used for color measurement and standardization, such as in colorimetry and color management.
- Colors can be combined into different color schemes or harmonies that create pleasing and balanced color combinations, such as monochromatic, complementary, analogous, triadic, and tetradic.
  - Monochromatic color scheme is a color scheme that uses only one hue and its variations of saturation and brightness, such as shades, tints, and tones. Monochromatic color scheme creates a simple and harmonious effect, but can also be boring and dull.
  - Complementary color scheme is a color scheme that uses two hues that are opposite to each other on the color wheel, such as red and green, or blue and orange. Complementary color scheme creates a strong and dynamic contrast, but can also be jarring and irritating.
  - Analogous color scheme is a color scheme that uses three or more hues that are adjacent to each other on the color wheel, such as yellow, green, and blue, or red, orange, and yellow. Analogous color scheme creates a smooth and natural transition, but can also be monotonous and lack variety.
  - Triadic color scheme is a color scheme that uses three hues that are equally spaced on the color wheel, such as red, green, and blue, or yellow, magenta, and cyan. Triadic color scheme creates a balanced and vibrant effect, but can also be overwhelming and chaotic.
  - Tetradic color scheme is a color scheme that uses four hues that are arranged into two complementary pairs on the color wheel, such as red and green, and blue and orange, or yellow and purple, and cyan and magenta. Tetradic color scheme creates a rich and complex effect, but can also be confusing and conflicting.
