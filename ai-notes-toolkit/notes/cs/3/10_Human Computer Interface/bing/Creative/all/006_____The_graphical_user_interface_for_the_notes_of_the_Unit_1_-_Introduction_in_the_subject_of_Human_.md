# Unit 1 - Introduction

## What is Human Computer Interface (HCI)?

- HCI is the study of how humans interact with computers and other technologies.
- HCI aims to design, evaluate, and improve the usability, accessibility, and user experience of computer systems and applications.
- HCI involves multiple disciplines, such as computer science, psychology, design, engineering, and social sciences.

## Why is HCI important?

- HCI is important because it affects the quality of life, productivity, and satisfaction of users and society.
- HCI can enhance the functionality, efficiency, and effectiveness of computer systems and applications.
- HCI can prevent or reduce the negative impacts of computer systems and applications, such as errors, frustration, fatigue, stress, and isolation.

## What are the goals of HCI?

- The goals of HCI are to:
  - Understand the needs, preferences, and characteristics of users and contexts of use.
  - Design and develop user interfaces that are easy to learn, use, and remember.
  - Evaluate and test the usability, accessibility, and user experience of user interfaces.
  - Improve and innovate the user interfaces based on the feedback and data collected from users and stakeholders.

## What are the components of HCI?

- The components of HCI are:
  - Users: the people who use or are affected by the computer systems and applications.
  - Tasks: the activities or goals that users want to achieve with the computer systems and applications.
  - Interfaces: the elements that enable the communication and interaction between users and computer systems and applications, such as screens, keyboards, mice, touchscreens, voice, gestures, etc.
  - Contexts: the physical, social, and organizational environments and situations in which users and computer systems and applications operate.

## What are the challenges of HCI?

- The challenges of HCI are to:
  - Balance the trade-offs between different design criteria, such as functionality, usability, aesthetics, security, privacy, etc.
  - Adapt to the diversity and variability of users, tasks, interfaces, and contexts.
  - Keep up with the rapid changes and developments of technology and society.
  - Address the ethical, social, and environmental implications of computer systems and applications.
