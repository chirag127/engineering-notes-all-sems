# Information Retrieval on Web

- Information retrieval (IR) is the process of finding and accessing relevant information from a large collection of documents, such as the web.
- IR involves matching a user's query, which is a formal statement of information need, to the best information about that query.
- IR models are ways of integrating various sources of evidence about documents, such as the content, the structure, the links, the quality, etc., to rank them according to their relevance to the query.
- IR on the web has some unique challenges and opportunities, such as:
  - The web is very large, dynamic, heterogeneous, and unstructured, which makes it difficult to crawl, index, and query efficiently and effectively.
  - The web contains rich representations of information, such as images, videos, audio, etc., which require different techniques to process and retrieve.
  - The web users have diverse and complex information needs, such as direct answers, task completion, personalization, etc., which require advanced natural language processing and understanding.
  - The web provides various signals and feedback, such as click-through data, user behavior, social media, etc., which can be used to improve the quality and diversity of the search results.
- IR on the web can be applied to various domains and applications, such as e-commerce, education, health, entertainment, etc., to provide useful and relevant information to the users.
