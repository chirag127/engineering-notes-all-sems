# A brief history of screen design

- Screen design is the process of creating visual interfaces for digital devices, such as computers, smartphones, and tablets.
- Screen design has evolved over time, influenced by technological innovations, user needs, and aesthetic trends.
- Some of the major milestones in the history of screen design are:

  - The first screens were cathode ray tube (CRT) displays, which were attached to computers in the 1950s and 1960s. They could only display monochrome text and simple graphics, and required users to enter commands using keyboards or punch cards.
  - In the 1970s, graphical user interfaces (GUIs) emerged, which allowed users to interact with icons, menus, and windows using a mouse or a touchpad. GUIs were popularized by the Xerox Alto, the Apple Macintosh, and the Microsoft Windows operating systems .
  - In the 1980s and 1990s, screen design became more diverse and sophisticated, as different types of devices and applications emerged, such as personal computers, laptops, mobile phones, video games, and multimedia. Screen design also incorporated more colors, fonts, images, animations, and sounds .
  - In the 2000s and 2010s, screen design was influenced by the rise of the internet, social media, and mobile devices, which enabled users to access and share information anytime and anywhere. Screen design also adopted more minimalist, flat, and responsive styles, to enhance usability, accessibility, and adaptability .
  - In the 2020s and beyond, screen design is expected to face new challenges and opportunities, such as incorporating artificial intelligence, virtual reality, augmented reality, and voice and gesture recognition, to create more immersive, personalized, and interactive experiences.
