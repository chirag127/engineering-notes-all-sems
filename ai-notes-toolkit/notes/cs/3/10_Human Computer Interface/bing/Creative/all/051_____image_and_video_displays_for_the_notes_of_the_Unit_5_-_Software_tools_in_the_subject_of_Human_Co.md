# Image and Video Displays for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Software Tools in the Subject of Human Computer Interface

- Image and video displays are types of screenless displays that use different technologies to project images or videos onto various surfaces or into the air without using a physical screen.
- Image and video displays can be used for various purposes in human-computer interaction (HCI), such as enhancing the user experience, providing feedback, creating immersive environments, or facilitating communication.
- Some examples of image and video displays are:
  - Holographic display: A display that creates a three-dimensional image of an object or scene by using interference and diffraction of light waves.
  - Virtual reality goggles: A display that covers the user's eyes and provides a simulated view of a virtual environment that responds to the user's head movements and inputs.
  - Heads up display: A display that projects information or graphics onto a transparent surface, such as a windshield or a helmet visor, so that the user can see them without looking away from the main view.
  - Video-based interface: A display that uses a camera to capture the user's movements and gestures and maps them to commands or actions on a computer system.
  - Graphics, visualization and human-computer interaction: A display that uses graphical, visual, informational and interactive representations to convey complex or abstract data or concepts to the user in an effective and engaging way.
- Image and video displays have some advantages and disadvantages in HCI, such as:
  - Advantages: They can provide a more natural, intuitive, and immersive way of interacting with computers, enhance the user's perception and cognition, and support various tasks and domains.
  - Disadvantages: They can be costly, complex, or unreliable, require more processing power and bandwidth, and pose some ethical, social, or health issues, such as privacy, security, or motion sickness.
