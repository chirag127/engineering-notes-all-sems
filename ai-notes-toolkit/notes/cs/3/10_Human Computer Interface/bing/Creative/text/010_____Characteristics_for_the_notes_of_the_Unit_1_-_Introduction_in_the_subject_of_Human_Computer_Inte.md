### Characteristics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Human Computer Interface

- Human Computer Interface (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers and other technologies.
- HCI aims to design, evaluate, and improve the usability, accessibility, and user experience of computer systems and applications.
- HCI is an interdisciplinary field that draws from computer science, psychology, design, engineering, and social sciences.
- HCI has many applications in various domains, such as education, entertainment, health, business, and social media.
- HCI can be divided into three main components: the user, the computer, and the interaction.
- The user is the human who uses the computer system or application for a specific goal or task.
- The computer is the hardware and software that provides the functionality and interface for the user.
- The interaction is the communication and feedback between the user and the computer, which can be through various modalities, such as visual, auditory, tactile, or gesture.
- HCI can be characterized by the following aspects:
  - User-centered: HCI focuses on the needs, preferences, and abilities of the user, and involves the user in the design and evaluation process.
  - Contextual: HCI considers the physical, social, and cultural context of the user and the computer system, and how they affect the interaction.
  - Iterative: HCI follows an iterative cycle of design, implementation, testing, and refinement, based on user feedback and evaluation methods.
  - Evaluative: HCI uses various methods and metrics to measure and improve the usability, accessibility, and user experience of the computer system or application.
  - Innovative: HCI explores new and creative ways of designing and interacting with computer systems and applications, using emerging technologies and paradigms.
