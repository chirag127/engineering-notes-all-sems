### Importance of 8 human characteristics for design process

Human characteristics are the qualities and traits that define humans as a species and as individuals. They include physical, cognitive, emotional, social, and cultural aspects of human behavior and experience. Understanding human characteristics is essential for designing products, services, and systems that are user-friendly, effective, and satisfying. Some of the important human characteristics that designers should consider are:

- **Perception**: Perception is the process of interpreting sensory information from the environment. Perception is influenced by factors such as attention, expectations, context, and prior knowledge. Designers should design for different perceptual modalities, such as vision, hearing, touch, smell, and taste, and ensure that the design elements are clear, consistent, and meaningful for the users.
- **Memory**: Memory is the ability to store and retrieve information over time. Memory can be divided into sensory, short-term, and long-term memory, each with different capacities and functions. Designers should design for memory limitations, such as using cues, reminders, feedback, and chunking to help users remember important information and actions.
- **Learning**: Learning is the process of acquiring new knowledge and skills through experience, observation, or instruction. Learning can be explicit or implicit, and can involve different types of cognitive processes, such as induction, deduction, analogy, and problem-solving. Designers should design for learning, by providing clear instructions, examples, practice, and feedback, and by supporting different learning styles and preferences.
- **Problem-solving**: Problem-solving is the ability to find solutions to complex or novel situations or challenges. Problem-solving can involve different types of reasoning, such as deductive, inductive, abductive, and analogical, and can require different types of skills, such as creativity, logic, intuition, and collaboration. Designers should design for problem-solving, by providing clear goals, constraints, and resources, and by facilitating exploration, experimentation, and evaluation.
- **Emotion**: Emotion is the affective state that arises from the appraisal of a situation or an event. Emotion can influence cognition, motivation, behavior, and well-being. Emotion can be expressed through facial expressions, body language, voice, and words. Designers should design for emotion, by understanding the emotional needs and expectations of the users, and by creating positive, engaging, and empathetic user experiences.
- **Motivation**: Motivation is the drive that initiates, directs, and sustains behavior towards a goal. Motivation can be intrinsic or extrinsic, and can be influenced by factors such as interest, curiosity, challenge, reward, feedback, and autonomy. Designers should design for motivation, by providing clear and meaningful goals, challenges, and rewards, and by supporting user autonomy, competence, and relatedness.
- **Social interaction**: Social interaction is the exchange of information, feelings, and actions between two or more individuals or groups. Social interaction can involve different modes of communication, such as verbal, non-verbal, and written, and can occur in different contexts, such as face-to-face, online, or mediated. Designers should design for social interaction, by understanding the social norms and expectations of the users, and by facilitating communication, collaboration, and community.
- **Culture**: Culture is the set of shared values, beliefs, practices, and artifacts that characterize a group of people. Culture can influence perception, cognition, emotion, motivation, behavior, and communication. Culture can vary across regions, countries, organizations, and communities. Designers should design for culture, by respecting the diversity and uniqueness of the users, and by adapting the design to the cultural preferences and sensitivities of the users.
