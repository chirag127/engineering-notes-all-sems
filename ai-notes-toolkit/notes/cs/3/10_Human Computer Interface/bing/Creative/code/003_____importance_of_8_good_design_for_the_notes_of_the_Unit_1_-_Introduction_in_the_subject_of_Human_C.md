### Importance of 8 good design principles for human computer interface

Human computer interface (HCI) is the field of study that focuses on the interaction between humans and computers. HCI aims to design interfaces that are easy to use, efficient, and satisfying for the users. A good interface can enhance the user experience, increase productivity, reduce errors, and improve accessibility.

One of the most influential sources of HCI design principles is Shneiderman's eight golden rules of interface design  . These rules provide a convenient and succinct summary of the key principles of interface design. The eight rules are:

- **Strive for consistency** by utilizing familiar icons, colors, menu hierarchy, call-to-actions, and user flows when designing similar situations and sequence of actions. Consistency can help users learn the interface faster, reduce confusion, and increase confidence.
- **Enable frequent users to use shortcuts** by providing keyboard commands, hidden commands, abbreviations, and macros. Shortcuts can help users perform tasks more quickly, efficiently, and accurately.
- **Offer informative feedback** by providing clear and timely messages, sounds, graphics, or other indicators of the system status, user actions, and errors. Feedback can help users understand the consequences of their actions, monitor the progress of their tasks, and recover from mistakes.
- **Design dialogs to yield closure** by organizing the interface into meaningful and coherent sequences of actions, each with a clear beginning and end. Dialogs can help users maintain a sense of control, orientation, and accomplishment.
- **Prevent errors** by designing the interface to avoid ambiguous, confusing, or misleading situations, and by providing error prevention and correction mechanisms. Errors can cause frustration, delay, and loss of data or work.
- **Permit easy reversal of actions** by providing undo, redo, cancel, or back functions. Reversal of actions can help users correct their errors, explore different options, and reduce anxiety.
- **Support internal locus of control** by designing the interface to allow users to initiate and terminate actions, rather than being forced by the system. Locus of control can help users feel empowered, autonomous, and responsible.
- **Reduce short-term memory load** by designing the interface to minimize the amount of information, options, or steps that users have to remember or process at a time. Memory load can affect the user's cognitive performance, attention, and satisfaction.

These eight rules can help you design better interfaces that are consistent, efficient, informative, clear, error-free, reversible, controllable, and simple. By following these rules, you can improve the usability, effectiveness, and satisfaction of your interface. However, these rules are not absolute or exhaustive, and you should always consider the context, goals, and needs of your users and tasks when applying them. You should also evaluate your interface design using appropriate methods, such as user testing, heuristic evaluation, or cognitive walkthrough, to ensure that it meets the desired standards and expectations.
