# Unit 1 - Introduction

## What is Human Computer Interface (HCI)?

- HCI is the study of how humans interact with computers and other technologies.
- HCI aims to design, evaluate, and improve the usability, accessibility, and user experience of computer systems and applications.
- HCI involves multiple disciplines, such as computer science, psychology, design, engineering, and social sciences.

## Why is HCI important?

- HCI is important because it affects the efficiency, effectiveness, and satisfaction of users and organizations that use computer systems and applications.
- HCI is important because it can enhance the quality of life, education, health, entertainment, and social interaction of users and society.
- HCI is important because it can address the ethical, social, and environmental issues and challenges posed by the rapid development and adoption of computer technologies.

## What are the goals of HCI?

- The goals of HCI are to:
  - Understand the needs, preferences, and characteristics of users and contexts of use.
  - Design and develop user interfaces that are easy to learn, use, and remember, and that match the users' tasks, goals, and expectations.
  - Evaluate and test the usability, accessibility, and user experience of user interfaces using various methods and metrics.
  - Improve and innovate the user interfaces based on the feedback and data collected from the evaluation and testing.

## What are the components of HCI?

- The components of HCI are:
  - Users: the people who use the computer systems and applications, or who are affected by them.
  - Tasks: the activities and goals that the users want to accomplish with the computer systems and applications.
  - Interfaces: the elements and features that enable the communication and interaction between the users and the computer systems and applications, such as menus, buttons, icons, windows, etc.
  - Devices: the hardware and software that support the user interfaces, such as keyboards, mice, touchscreens, monitors, speakers, etc.
  - Contexts: the physical, social, and organizational environments and situations in which the users and the computer systems and applications are situated.

## What are the principles of HCI?

- The principles of HCI are the general guidelines and best practices that inform and guide the design, evaluation, and improvement of user interfaces. Some of the common principles of HCI are:
  - Know your users: understand and empathize with the users' needs, preferences, and characteristics, and involve them in the design and evaluation process.
  - Match the interface to the task: design the interface to support the users' tasks, goals, and expectations, and to minimize the cognitive, physical, and emotional load on the users.
  - Provide feedback: provide clear, timely, and appropriate feedback to the users about the status, progress, and outcome of their actions and interactions.
  - Ensure consistency: maintain the same appearance, behavior, and terminology across the interface and across different interfaces, and follow the established standards and conventions.
  - Prevent errors: design the interface to prevent, detect, and recover from errors, and to provide helpful and constructive error messages and guidance to the users.
  - Support user control: design the interface to give the users the freedom and flexibility to customize, personalize, and adapt the interface to their preferences and needs, and to provide undo and redo functions.
  - Enhance aesthetics: design the interface to be visually appealing, engaging, and pleasing to the users, and to convey the intended message and mood.
