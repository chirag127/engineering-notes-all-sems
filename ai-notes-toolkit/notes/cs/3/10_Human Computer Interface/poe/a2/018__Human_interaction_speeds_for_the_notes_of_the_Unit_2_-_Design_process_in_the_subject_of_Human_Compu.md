### Human interaction speeds

1. Human perceptual speed: The speed at which humans can perceive and comprehend information. This limits how fast information can be presented to the user. Example: Reading speed, visual search speed.
2. Human motor speed: The speed at which humans can physically interact with a system. This limits how fast a user can input information or manipulate controls. Example: Typing speed, movement speed.
3. Cognitive processing speed: The speed at which humans can interpret information and make decisions. This limits how fast a user can mentally process information and determine appropriate responses or actions.

These human speeds must be considered when designing interfaces and interactions to ensure the system response time stays within human limits and does not result in overloading the user. Response times that are too fast can be confusing or frustrating, while response times that are too slow can lead to boredom or the perception that the system is unresponsive.
