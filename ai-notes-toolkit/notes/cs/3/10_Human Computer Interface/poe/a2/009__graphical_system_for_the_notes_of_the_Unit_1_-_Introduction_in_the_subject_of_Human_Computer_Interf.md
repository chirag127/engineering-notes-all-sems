1. Introduction to HCI
- Human Computer Interface (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers and to what extent computers are or are not developed for successful interaction with human beings.
- The goal of HCI is to improve the interaction between users and computers by making systems that are safe, easy to use and effective.
- HCI involves studying and designing interfaces to make them easy to use. It incorporates findings and methods from psychology, anthropology and other social sciences as well as computer science and other technical fields.

2. The user and task analysis
- Understanding the user is critical to the success of any user interface design. The designer needs to understand the user's capabilities, limitations, preferences and work practices or tasks.
- Task analysis is the process of learning about ordinary users' goals and the steps required to achieve these goals when interacting with a system. The resulting information can then be used to make decisions about the design of interfaces and interactions.

3. Design principles in HCI
- Some important principles in HCI design are:
-- Visibility: The system should always keep users informed about what is going on, through appropriate feedback.
-- Feedback: The system should provide timely and appropriate feedback to the user about the results of the interactions and the user's actions.
-- Simplicity: The system should be as simple as possible to avoid confusion and errors.
-- Flexibility: The system should be flexible enough to accommodate different users and changing user needs.
-- Consistency: similar parts of the interface should look, operate and respond in the same way.
-- Error prevention: The system should be designed to prevent problems arising or avoid users making errors. If errors do occur, the system should be designed to minimize their impact.
