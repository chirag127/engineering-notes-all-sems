### The concept of direct manipulation

- Direct manipulation is a style of human-computer interaction where users interact with digital objects on a computer screen in a direct, physical way, using actions that correspond intuitively to the physical world.
- The user manipulates objects on the screen directly, instead of typing commands to instruct the computer to perform actions on the objects.
- Examples of direct manipulation include:
-- Dragging and dropping objects.
-- Resizing windows.
-- Highlighting text.
-- Pinching to zoom in and out.
- The key characteristics of direct manipulation are:
-- Visibility of the object of interest. The user can see the objects they are manipulating.
-- Physical actions. The user's physical actions correspond to the effects, such as drag and drop.
-- Immediate feedback. The system provides rapid feedback to the user about the effects of their actions.
-- Reversibility. The user can reverse actions if desired.
- The advantages of direct manipulation include:
-- Easy to learn. The interface is intuitive and easy to understand.
-- Increases user engagement and satisfaction. The interactive and hands-on style is engaging.
-- Faster to use once learned. Actions are faster than typing commands.
-- Errors are easy to recover from. Users can undo or redo actions.
