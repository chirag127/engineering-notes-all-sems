### Model of Real Time Communication

Real-time communication is a critical aspect of modern-day systems, and it is essential to ensure that information is transmitted accurately and reliably. The Model of Real-Time Communication provides a framework for understanding how real-time communication works and how it can be optimized to achieve the desired results. Here are the key points of the Model of Real-Time Communication:

1. Sender: The sender initiates the communication process by creating a message and transmitting it to the receiver. The sender is responsible for ensuring that the message is accurate and understandable.

2. Medium: The medium is the channel through which the message is transmitted. The medium can be a physical layer or a network layer, depending on the system's requirements. The medium must be selected appropriately to ensure that the message is transmitted accurately and efficiently.

3. Receiver: The receiver is responsible for receiving the message and decoding it. The receiver must ensure that the message is understood correctly and that any errors or discrepancies are corrected.

4. Feedback: Feedback is a crucial component of real-time communication. It enables the sender to monitor the communication process and make adjustments as necessary. Feedback can be either explicit or implicit, depending on the system's requirements.

5. Control: Control is an essential aspect of real-time communication. It enables the system to manage the communication process and ensure that messages are transmitted accurately and efficiently. Control can be either centralized or distributed, depending on the system's requirements.

6. Timing: Timing is critical in real-time communication. It ensures that messages are transmitted and received in a timely manner. Timing can be either synchronous or asynchronous, depending on the system's requirements.

7. Quality of Service: Quality of Service (QoS) is a critical aspect of real-time communication. It ensures that messages are transmitted accurately and efficiently, and that any errors or discrepancies are corrected. QoS can be either guaranteed or best-effort, depending on the system's requirements.

In conclusion, the Model of Real-Time Communication provides a framework for understanding how real-time communication works and how it can be optimized to achieve the desired results. By considering the key points of this model, system designers can ensure that their systems are designed to meet the requirements of real-time communication effectively.
