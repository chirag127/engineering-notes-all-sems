### Basic Concepts in Real Time Communication

Real time communication (RTC) is a type of communication that happens in real time, meaning that there is no significant delay or storage between the sender and the receiver of the information. RTC is important for applications that require immediate feedback, interaction, or coordination, such as video conferencing, online gaming, telemedicine, or remote control. Some of the basic concepts in RTC are:

- **Latency**: Latency is the time it takes for a message or a signal to travel from the sender to the receiver. Latency can be affected by various factors, such as the distance, the bandwidth, the network congestion, or the processing speed of the devices involved. Low latency is desirable for RTC, as it improves the quality and the responsiveness of the communication. Latency can be measured in milliseconds (ms) or microseconds (µs).
- **Bandwidth**: Bandwidth is the amount of data that can be transmitted or received per unit of time over a communication channel. Bandwidth can be expressed in bits per second (bps) or megabits per second (Mbps). Bandwidth can limit the quality and the quantity of the data that can be exchanged in RTC, such as the resolution, the frame rate, or the number of participants in a video call. Bandwidth can be affected by the type of the channel, such as wired or wireless, or the availability of the network resources, such as routers or servers.
- **Jitter**: Jitter is the variation in the latency of the packets or the signals that are sent or received in RTC. Jitter can cause the packets or the signals to arrive out of order, or to be delayed or lost. Jitter can degrade the quality and the reliability of the RTC, such as causing glitches, artifacts, or interruptions in the audio or the video. Jitter can be caused by the network congestion, the interference, or the routing changes. Jitter can be measured in milliseconds (ms) or microseconds (µs).
- **Packet loss**: Packet loss is the percentage of the packets that are sent or received in RTC that are corrupted, dropped, or discarded. Packet loss can reduce the quality and the completeness of the data that is exchanged in RTC, such as causing distortion, noise, or gaps in the audio or the video. Packet loss can be caused by the network congestion, the errors, or the collisions. Packet loss can be measured in percentage (%) or in packets per second (pps).
- **Quality of service (QoS)**: QoS is the ability of a network to provide different levels of priority, performance, or reliability to different types of traffic or applications in RTC. QoS can help to improve the quality and the efficiency of the RTC, by allocating more bandwidth, reducing latency, jitter, or packet loss, or reserving network resources for the RTC. QoS can be implemented by using various techniques, such as traffic classification, traffic shaping, or traffic engineering. QoS can be measured by using various metrics, such as mean opinion score (MOS), peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), or subjective quality assessment (SQA).
