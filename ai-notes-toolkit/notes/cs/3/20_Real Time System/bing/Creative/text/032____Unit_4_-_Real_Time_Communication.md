## Unit 4 - Real Time Communication

- Real time communication (RTC) is the exchange of information between two or more parties without significant delay.
- RTC can be synchronous or asynchronous, depending on whether the parties are communicating at the same time or not.
- RTC can be text-based, voice-based, video-based, or a combination of these modalities.
- RTC can be one-to-one, one-to-many, or many-to-many, depending on the number of participants and the direction of communication.
- RTC can be implemented using various technologies, such as:
  - Internet Protocol (IP) networks, which use protocols such as Session Initiation Protocol (SIP), Real-time Transport Protocol (RTP), and Web Real-Time Communication (WebRTC) to establish and manage RTC sessions over the internet.
  - Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN), which uses circuit-switched technology to provide voice and fax communication over analog or digital phone lines.
  - Cellular networks, which use radio frequency (RF) technology to provide voice and data communication over wireless networks.
  - Satellite networks, which use orbiting satellites to relay signals between ground stations and provide global coverage for voice and data communication.
- RTC can be used for various purposes, such as:
  - Personal communication, such as chatting, calling, or video conferencing with friends and family.
  - Business communication, such as collaborating, presenting, or negotiating with colleagues and clients.
  - Education communication, such as teaching, learning, or tutoring online.
  - Entertainment communication, such as streaming, gaming, or socializing online.
  - Emergency communication, such as contacting, alerting, or assisting authorities and responders.
