
### Recurrent Networks

* Recurrent networks are a type of artificial neural network that are well-suited to processing sequences of data, such as text, audio, or video.
* They are composed of a series of interconnected neurons, each of which has a memory state which is updated at each step.
* This allows the network to remember information from previous steps and use it to inform its decisions in the current step.
* Recurrent networks can be used for a variety of tasks, including language modeling, speech recognition, and machine translation.
* They have been used to great success in natural language processing and computer vision applications.
* Recurrent networks have several advantages over traditional feed-forward networks, including their ability to handle variable-length sequences, and their ability to capture long-term dependencies.
* However, they also have several drawbacks, such as the difficulty of training them and their susceptibility to vanishing gradients.
