### Operators in JS

- Operators are special symbols that perform operations on operands (values and variables) .
- There are different types of operators in JavaScript, such as:
  - Arithmetic operators: perform mathematical calculations on operands, such as `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `%`, `**`, `++`, `--`  .
  - Assignment operators: assign values to variables, such as `=`, `+=`, `-=`, `*=`, `/=`, `%=`, `**=`  .
  - Comparison operators: compare operands and return a boolean value, such as `==`, `!=`, `===`, `!==`, `>`, `<`, `>=`, `<=`  .
  - Logical operators: perform logical operations on operands and return a boolean value, such as `&&`, `||`, `!`  .
  - Bitwise operators: perform bitwise operations on operands and return a numeric value, such as `&`, `|`, `^`, `~`, `<<`, `>>`, `>>>`  .
  - String operators: concatenate strings or assign strings to variables, such as `+`, `+=`  .
  - Conditional (ternary) operator: evaluate a condition and return one of two values, such as `condition ? value1 : value2`  .
  - Comma operator: evaluate two expressions and return the value of the second one, such as `x = (1, 2)`  .
  - Unary operators: operate on a single operand and return a value, such as `delete`, `typeof`, `void`, `+`, `-`  .
  - Relational operators: check the relationship between operands, such as `in`, `instanceof`  .
- Operators have different precedence and associativity, which determine the order of evaluation and grouping of operands  .
- Operators can be used in expressions, which are combinations of values, variables, and operators that produce a value  .
