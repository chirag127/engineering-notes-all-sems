### Types of Websites

A website is a collection of web pages that are linked together and hosted on a server. A website can be accessed by anyone who has an internet connection and a web browser. Websites can have different purposes, functions, and designs, depending on the goals and needs of the owner or creator.

There are many types of websites, but some of the most common ones are:

- **E-commerce website**: An e-commerce website allows the owner to sell products or services online. Customers can browse, select, and pay for the items they want through the website. Examples of e-commerce websites are Amazon, eBay, and Shopify.
- **Business website**: A business website provides information about a company, its products, services, mission, vision, values, and contact details. A business website can also have features such as testimonials, reviews, FAQs, and newsletters. Examples of business websites are Apple, Microsoft, and Coca-Cola.
- **Portfolio website**: A portfolio website showcases the work and skills of an individual, group, or organization. A portfolio website can be used to display projects, achievements, awards, and credentials. Examples of portfolio websites are Behance, Dribbble, and Squarespace.
- **Blogging website**: A blogging website allows the owner to share their thoughts, opinions, experiences, and knowledge on a specific topic or niche. A blogging website can also have features such as comments, social media integration, and RSS feeds. Examples of blogging websites are Medium, WordPress, and Blogger.
- **Media or news website**: A media or news website provides the latest information and updates on current events, politics, sports, entertainment, and more. A media or news website can also have features such as videos, podcasts, polls, and forums. Examples of media or news websites are CNN, BBC, and The New York Times.
- **Educational website**: An educational website provides learning resources and materials for students, teachers, and learners of all ages and levels. An educational website can also have features such as quizzes, courses, certificates, and badges. Examples of educational websites are Khan Academy, Coursera, and Udemy.
- **Social media website**: A social media website allows the users to create profiles, connect with other users, and share content such as text, images, videos, and audio. A social media website can also have features such as groups, pages, stories, and live streams. Examples of social media websites are Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram.
- **Entertainment website**: An entertainment website provides fun and engaging content for the users, such as games, jokes, memes, quizzes, and puzzles. An entertainment website can also have features such as leaderboards, achievements, and chat rooms. Examples of entertainment websites are BuzzFeed, 9GAG, and Miniclip.
- **Personal website**: A personal website is a website that belongs to an individual and reflects their personality, interests, hobbies, and goals. A personal website can also have features such as a resume, a blog, a photo gallery, and a contact form. Examples of personal websites are Tim Ferriss, Neil Patel, and Marie Forleo.
- **Non-profit website**: A non-profit website is a website that belongs to an organization that works for a social or environmental cause. A non-profit website provides information about the organization, its mission, vision, values, and impact. A non-profit website can also have features such as donations, volunteers, events, and campaigns. Examples of non-profit websites are UNICEF, Greenpeace, and Amnesty International.
