### Elements of Visual Perception

- Visual perception is the process of interpreting and understanding the information received by the eyes from the light rays reflected or emitted by objects in the environment.
- The elements of visual perception include:
  - Light and the electromagnetic spectrum
  - The human eye and its structure and function
  - The image formation and the visual pathway
  - The brightness adaptation and discrimination
  - The color perception and representation
- Light and the electromagnetic spectrum
  - Light is a form of electromagnetic radiation that has a wavelength range of about 400 to 700 nanometers (nm) in the visible spectrum.
  - The electromagnetic spectrum is the range of all possible frequencies of electromagnetic radiation, from gamma rays to radio waves.
  - The visible spectrum is a small part of the electromagnetic spectrum that can be detected by the human eye. It consists of different colors that correspond to different wavelengths of light, from violet (shortest wavelength) to red (longest wavelength).
  - The wavelength of light determines its hue, or color. The intensity of light determines its brightness, or luminance. The purity of light determines its saturation, or chromaticity.
- The human eye and its structure and function
  - The human eye is a complex optical system that converts light rays into electrical signals that are transmitted to the brain for visual processing.
  - The main parts of the human eye are:
    - The cornea: a transparent dome-shaped layer that covers the front of the eye and refracts (bends) light rays entering the eye.
    - The iris: a circular muscle that controls the size of the pupil, the opening in the center of the iris that regulates the amount of light entering the eye.
    - The lens: a flexible biconvex structure that changes its shape to focus light rays onto the retina, the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye.
    - The retina: a thin layer of photoreceptor cells (rods and cones) that convert light into electrical signals. Rods are more sensitive to low light levels and detect shades of gray, while cones are more sensitive to high light levels and detect colors. There are three types of cones: red, green, and blue, that respond to different wavelengths of light.
    - The optic nerve: a bundle of nerve fibers that carries the electrical signals from the retina to the brain.
- The image formation and the visual pathway
  - The image formation is the process of creating a two-dimensional representation of a three-dimensional scene on the retina.
  - The image formation involves two steps: projection and perspective.
    - Projection is the mapping of a point in the scene to a point on the retina, according to the geometry of the eye and the light rays. Projection is affected by the distance and the angle of the object from the eye, and the focal length of the lens.
    - Perspective is the distortion of the image due to the projection. Perspective causes objects that are farther away to appear smaller and closer together, and parallel lines to converge at a vanishing point.
