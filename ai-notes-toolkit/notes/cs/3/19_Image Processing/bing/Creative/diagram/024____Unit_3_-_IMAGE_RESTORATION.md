## Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION

- Image restoration is the process of improving the quality of an image that has been degraded by noise, blur, or other distortions.
- Image restoration aims to recover the original image from the degraded image, or to estimate the degradation model and the original image simultaneously.
- Image restoration differs from image enhancement, which modifies the image to make it more visually pleasing or suitable for a specific task, without necessarily recovering the original image.
- Image restoration can be classified into two categories: spatial domain methods and frequency domain methods.
- Spatial domain methods operate directly on the pixel values of the image, and apply filters or operators to remove or reduce the degradation effects.
- Frequency domain methods transform the image into a different domain, such as the Fourier domain, where the degradation effects can be more easily modeled and removed, and then transform the image back to the spatial domain.
- Some common types of degradation that affect images are:
  - Noise: random or systematic variations in pixel values that reduce the image contrast and detail. Noise can be caused by various sources, such as sensor defects, transmission errors, or environmental factors.
  - Blur: loss of sharpness or focus in the image, which reduces the image resolution and edges. Blur can be caused by various factors, such as camera motion, object motion, defocus, or atmospheric turbulence.
  - Geometric distortion: changes in the shape or size of the image, which affect the image alignment and registration. Geometric distortion can be caused by various factors, such as lens distortion, perspective projection, or warping.
- Some common techniques for image restoration are:
  - Noise reduction: removing or reducing the noise from the image, while preserving the image details and edges. Noise reduction can be achieved by various methods, such as averaging, median filtering, Gaussian filtering, bilateral filtering, or wavelet denoising.
  - Deblurring: removing or reducing the blur from the image, while preserving the image resolution and edges. Deblurring can be achieved by various methods, such as inverse filtering, Wiener filtering, Richardson-Lucy algorithm, blind deconvolution, or deep learning.
  - Geometric correction: correcting or compensating the geometric distortion in the image, while preserving the image shape and size. Geometric correction can be achieved by various methods, such as homography, affine transformation, projective transformation, or thin-plate spline.
