### Steps in Digital Image Processing

Digital image processing is the process of manipulating digital images using computer algorithms. It can be used for various purposes, such as enhancing, restoring, compressing, segmenting, and recognizing images. The following are the basic steps involved in digital image processing, according to  and :

- **Image acquisition**: This involves capturing an image using a digital camera or scanner, or importing an existing image into a computer. The image is then converted into a digital form, using analog-to-digital converters, if necessary. The image can be represented as a matrix of pixel values, where each pixel has a certain intensity or color value.
- **Image enhancement**: This involves improving the visual quality of an image, such as increasing contrast, reducing noise, and removing artifacts. The goal of image enhancement is to make the image more suitable for a specific application or task, such as human perception, analysis, or printing. Image enhancement can be done in the spatial domain, where the pixel values are directly modified, or in the frequency domain, where the image is transformed into a different representation, such as Fourier or wavelet, and then modified.
- **Image restoration**: This involves removing degradation from an image, such as blurring, noise, and distortion. The goal of image restoration is to recover the original image as much as possible, using some prior knowledge or model of the degradation process. Image restoration can also be done in the spatial or frequency domain, using various techniques, such as inverse filtering, deconvolution, or regularization.
- **Image compression**: This involves reducing the size of an image, while preserving its essential information and quality. The goal of image compression is to save storage space and transmission bandwidth, while minimizing the loss of information and quality. Image compression can be lossless, where the original image can be exactly reconstructed from the compressed image, or lossy, where some information and quality are sacrificed for higher compression ratios. Image compression can use various methods, such as run-length encoding, Huffman coding, arithmetic coding, or transform coding, such as JPEG or MPEG.
- **Image segmentation**: This involves dividing an image into meaningful regions or objects, based on some criteria, such as pixel intensity, color, texture, or shape. The goal of image segmentation is to simplify or change the representation of an image, so that it is easier to analyze or understand. Image segmentation can use various techniques, such as thresholding, edge detection, region growing, clustering, or watershed.
- **Image recognition**: This involves identifying or classifying the objects or regions in an image, based on some features, such as shape, color, texture, or pattern. The goal of image recognition is to assign a label or category to the image or its parts, such as face, car, flower, or digit. Image recognition can use various methods, such as template matching, feature extraction, or machine learning, such as neural networks, support vector machines, or deep learning.
