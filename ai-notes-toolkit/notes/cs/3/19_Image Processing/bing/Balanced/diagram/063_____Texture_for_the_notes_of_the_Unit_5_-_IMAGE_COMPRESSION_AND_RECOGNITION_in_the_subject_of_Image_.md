### Texture

- Texture is a property of an image that describes the spatial arrangement of intensity or color variations in a local neighborhood.
- Texture can be used to segment images into regions of interest, to classify images based on their content, or to enhance images by filtering out noise or unwanted details.
- Texture analysis methods can be divided into three categories: statistical, structural, and spectral.
- Statistical methods use numerical measures to characterize the distribution and relationships of pixel values in a texture region. Examples of statistical methods are co-occurrence matrices, run-length encoding, and fractal dimension.
- Structural methods use rules or grammar to describe the composition and arrangement of primitive patterns or elements in a texture region. Examples of structural methods are textons, Markov random fields, and syntactic methods.
- Spectral methods use frequency domain techniques to capture the periodicity or orientation of texture patterns. Examples of spectral methods are Fourier transform, Gabor filters, and wavelet transform.
