### Need for data compression

Data compression is the process of reducing the amount of data needed for the storage or transmission of a given piece of information, typically by the use of encoding techniques. Data compression is important for the following reasons:

- It saves disk space and reduces the cost of storage .
- It improves transmission speed and reduces the bandwidth and latency of communication networks  .
- It optimizes backup storage and facilitates data recovery.
- It improves file integrity and reduces the risk of data corruption.
- It enables sending files via email over the Internet more easily and efficiently.
- It secures files by encrypting them or adding passwords.
- It saves money by reducing the expenses of data storage and transmission.

Data compression is especially useful for image processing, as images tend to have large file sizes and high resolution. Image compression can reduce the number of bits required to represent an image, while preserving the quality and information of the image as much as possible. Image compression can be classified into two types: lossless and lossy. Lossless compression preserves the exact original data, while lossy compression discards some data that is deemed less important or perceptible. Lossless compression is suitable for images that require high accuracy and fidelity, such as medical images or text documents. Lossy compression is suitable for images that can tolerate some degradation and distortion, such as natural images or photographs. Image compression can be achieved by various methods, such as run-length encoding, Huffman coding, arithmetic coding, dictionary coding, transform coding, predictive coding, fractal coding, and wavelet coding. Image compression can improve the performance and efficiency of image processing applications, such as image recognition, image analysis, image enhancement, image restoration, image segmentation, image encryption, and image watermarking.
