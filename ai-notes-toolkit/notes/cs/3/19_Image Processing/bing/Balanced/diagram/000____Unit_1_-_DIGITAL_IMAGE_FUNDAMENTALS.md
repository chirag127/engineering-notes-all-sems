## Unit 1 - DIGITAL IMAGE FUNDAMENTALS

- A digital image is a representation of a two-dimensional image as a finite set of digital values, called pixels or picture elements.
- Each pixel has a numeric value that corresponds to its brightness, color, or intensity.
- A digital image can be stored, processed, and displayed by a computer system or a digital device.
- The quality of a digital image depends on several factors, such as the resolution, bit depth, compression, and noise level.

### Resolution
- Resolution is the measure of how much detail a digital image can capture or display.
- It is usually expressed in terms of pixels per inch (ppi) or dots per inch (dpi) for printing, or pixels per unit length (such as cm or mm) for display devices.
- Higher resolution means more pixels and more detail, but also more storage space and processing time.

### Bit Depth
- Bit depth is the measure of how many bits are used to represent each pixel in a digital image.
- It determines the number of possible values or colors that a pixel can have.
- Higher bit depth means more colors and more dynamic range, but also more storage space and processing time.
- Common bit depths are 1-bit (black and white), 8-bit (256 colors or grayscale), 16-bit (65,536 colors or grayscale), and 24-bit (16.7 million colors or true color).

### Compression
- Compression is the process of reducing the size of a digital image by removing some of the information or data from it.
- It can be lossless or lossy, depending on whether the original image can be exactly reconstructed from the compressed image or not.
- Lossless compression preserves all the information and quality of the original image, but achieves less compression ratio.
- Lossy compression discards some of the information and quality of the original image, but achieves higher compression ratio.
- Common compression formats are JPEG, PNG, GIF, TIFF, and BMP.

### Noise
- Noise is the unwanted variation or distortion in a digital image that affects its quality and appearance.
- It can be caused by various factors, such as sensor imperfections, transmission errors, environmental conditions, or processing operations.
- Noise can be random or structured, depending on whether it follows a predictable pattern or not.
- Noise can be reduced or removed by various techniques, such as filtering, smoothing, or denoising.
