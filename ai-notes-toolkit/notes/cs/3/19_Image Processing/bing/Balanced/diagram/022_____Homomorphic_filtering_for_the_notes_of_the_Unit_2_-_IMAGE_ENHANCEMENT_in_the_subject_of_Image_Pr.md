### Homomorphic filtering

- Homomorphic filtering is a generalized technique for signal and image processing, involving a nonlinear mapping to a different domain in which linear filter techniques are applied, followed by mapping back to the original domain .
- Homomorphic filtering can be used to enhance an input image by reducing its dynamic range and increasing its contrast. It can also be used to split low-frequency illumination and high-frequency reflectance from an image, which can be useful for change detection.
- Homomorphic filtering is based on the image formation model that represents an input image as the product of the illumination and the reflectance. The illumination component is related to the amount and direction of light sources, while the reflectance component is related to the properties of the objects in the scene.
- Homomorphic filtering assumes that the illumination and reflectance components can be separated in the logarithmic domain, where the product becomes a sum. Then, a high-pass filter can be applied to attenuate the illumination component and enhance the reflectance component. Finally, the inverse logarithm can be applied to map the image back to the original domain.
- Homomorphic filtering can be implemented using a frequency domain approach, where the input image is transformed to the Fourier domain using the discrete Fourier transform (DFT), then multiplied by a filter function, and then transformed back to the spatial domain using the inverse DFT (IDFT).
- The filter function can be designed according to the desired characteristics of the output image, such as the amount of attenuation or enhancement, the cutoff frequency, and the shape of the transition band. A common choice of filter function is the Butterworth filter, which has a smooth frequency response and can be easily parameterized.
- Homomorphic filtering can improve the appearance and quality of images that suffer from poor lighting conditions, such as shadows, glare, or uneven illumination. It can also enhance the details and edges of the objects in the image, making them more visible and distinct.
