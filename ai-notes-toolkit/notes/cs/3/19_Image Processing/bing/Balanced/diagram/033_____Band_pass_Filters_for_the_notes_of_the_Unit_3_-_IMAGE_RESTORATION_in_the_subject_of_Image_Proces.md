### Band pass Filters

- Band pass filters are filters that allow only a certain range of frequencies to pass through, while attenuating the frequencies below and above that range.
- Band pass filters can be used to denoise images, reduce low-frequency artifacts, and enhance image features such as blobs and edges.
- Band pass filters can be implemented in the spatial domain or the frequency domain, depending on the application and the desired effect.
- In the spatial domain, band pass filters can be obtained by multiplying a low pass filter with a high pass filter, where the low pass filter has a higher cut-off frequency than the high pass filter.
- In the frequency domain, band pass filters can be obtained by applying a circular mask to the Fourier transform of the image, where the radius of the mask determines the bandwidth of the filter.
- Band pass filters can be designed with different shapes and characteristics, such as Gaussian, Butterworth, Chebyshev, etc.
- Band pass filters can be adjusted to suit the local signal conditions in an image, without prior knowledge of the signal statistics, by using a system of multiple band pass filters modelled on the early mechanisms of human vision.
- Band pass filters have applications in various fields, such as astronomy, microscopy, medical imaging, remote sensing, etc.
