### Color image fundamentals

- A color image is a digital representation of a scene that captures the visual appearance of the objects and the light sources in the scene.
- A color image can be modeled as a function of two spatial variables (x and y) and one spectral variable (λ), which represents the wavelength of the light.
- A color image can be stored and processed in different color models, which are mathematical frameworks that define how colors are represented and manipulated.
- Some common color models are:
  - RGB (red, green, blue): This model is based on the additive color mixing of three primary colors of light. Each color is represented by a component value ranging from 0 to 255. The RGB model is widely used for color display devices, such as monitors and TVs.
  - CMY (cyan, magenta, yellow): This model is based on the subtractive color mixing of three primary colors of ink or paint. Each color is represented by a component value ranging from 0 to 255. The CMY model is widely used for color printing devices, such as printers and scanners.
  - CMYK (cyan, magenta, yellow, black): This model is an extension of the CMY model, with an additional component for black. The black component is used to reduce the amount of ink or toner needed and to improve the contrast and quality of the printed image. The CMYK model is also widely used for color printing devices.
  - HSI (hue, saturation, intensity): This model is based on the perceptual attributes of color. Hue is the dominant color or the color name, such as red, green, or blue. Saturation is the purity or vividness of the color, ranging from 0 (gray) to 1 (pure color). Intensity is the brightness or lightness of the color, ranging from 0 (black) to 1 (white). The HSI model is widely used for color image processing and analysis, such as segmentation, enhancement, and feature extraction.
- Color image processing is the application of digital image processing techniques to color images. Some examples of color image processing are:
  - Color image enhancement: This is the process of improving the visual quality or the perceptual information of a color image, such as contrast, brightness, sharpness, or colorfulness.
  - Color image segmentation: This is the process of dividing a color image into meaningful regions or objects based on some criteria, such as color similarity, color difference, or color distribution.
  - Color image compression: This is the process of reducing the amount of data needed to store or transmit a color image, while preserving the essential information and minimizing the distortion or loss of quality.
  - Color image restoration: This is the process of recovering a color image from a degraded or noisy version, such as removing blur, noise, or artifacts.
  - Color image recognition: This is the process of identifying or classifying a color image or its contents, such as faces, objects, scenes, or actions.
