## Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION

- Image restoration is the process of recovering an image that has been degraded by some factors, such as noise, blur, or distortion.
- The goal of image restoration is to restore the original image as closely as possible, or to improve the quality of the image according to some criteria.
- Image restoration can be classified into two categories: spatial domain methods and frequency domain methods.
- Spatial domain methods operate directly on the pixels of the image, and apply some filters or transformations to enhance or restore the image.
- Frequency domain methods operate on the Fourier transform of the image, and manipulate the frequency components of the image to remove or reduce the degradation effects.
- Some common types of image degradation are:
  - Noise: random or systematic variations in pixel values that affect the image quality and visibility.
  - Blur: loss of sharpness or detail in the image due to motion, defocus, or atmospheric effects.
  - Distortion: geometric or radiometric changes in the image due to perspective, lens, or sensor errors.
- Some common types of image restoration techniques are:
  - Noise reduction: removing or reducing the noise from the image using filters, such as mean, median, or Gaussian filters.
  - Deblurring: restoring the sharpness or detail of the image using filters, such as inverse, Wiener, or Richardson-Lucy filters.
  - Undistortion: correcting the geometric or radiometric errors in the image using transformations, such as homography, calibration, or histogram equalization.
