## Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION

Image restoration is the process of enhancing the quality of digital images that have been degraded due to various factors such as noise, blur, and compression. It is an important area of image processing that has applications in several domains such as medical imaging, surveillance, and photography.    - Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR)
   - Structural Similarity Index (SSIM)

### Image Degradation

Image degradation is the process of reducing the quality of digital images due to various factors. The following are the types of image degradation:

- Noise: It is the random variation of brightness or color in an image. Noise can be caused by the sensor of a camera or during the transmission of an image.
- Blur: It is the loss of details in an image due to the out-of-focus lens or the motion of the camera.
- Compression: It is the reduction of the size of an image to save storage space or to transmit it over a network.

### Image Restoration Techniques

Image restoration techniques are used to enhance the quality of degraded images. The following are the commonly used techniques:

- Image filtering: It is a process of removing noise from an image by applying a filter. There are several types of filters such as median filter, Gaussian filter, and Wiener filter.
- Deconvolution: It is a process of restoring the blurred image by estimating the blur kernel and applying its inverse.
- Inpainting: It is a process of filling the missing or damaged parts of an image by estimating the missing information from the surrounding pixels.
- Super-resolution: It is a process of increasing the resolution of an image by estimating the high-frequency details from the low-resolution image.

### Evaluation Metrics

Evaluation metrics are used to measure the quality of restored images. The following are the commonly used metrics:

- Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR): It is a measure of the ratio of the maximum signal power to the noise power in an image. The higher the PSNR value, the better the quality of the restored image.
- Structural Similarity Index (SSIM): It is a measure of the similarity between the restored image and the original image. The higher the SSIM value, the better the quality of the restored image.
- Mean Square Error (MSE): It is a measure of the difference between the restored image and the original image. The lower the MSE value, the better the quality of the restored image.

### Applications of Image Restoration

Image restoration has several applications in various domains. The following are the commonly used applications:

- Medical imaging: It is used to enhance the quality of medical images such as X-rays, CT scans, and MRI scans.
- Surveillance: It is used to enhance the quality of surveillance images for better identification of objects and people.
- Photography: It is used to enhance the quality of photographs by removing noise and blur.
- Forensic analysis: It is used to enhance the quality of images for forensic analysis such as fingerprint identification and face recognition.
