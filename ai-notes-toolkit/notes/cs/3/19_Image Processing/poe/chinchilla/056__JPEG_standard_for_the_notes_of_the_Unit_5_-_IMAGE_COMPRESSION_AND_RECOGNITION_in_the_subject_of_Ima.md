### JPEG Standard

JPEG stands for Joint Photographic Experts Group, which is a group of experts who developed a standard for compressing digital images. This standard is widely used for the compression and storage of digital images.

JPEG is a lossy compression algorithm, which means that some information is lost during compression. However, the amount of information loss can be controlled by adjusting the compression ratio.

The JPEG standard uses a process called Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to compress images. The DCT converts image data from the spatial domain to the frequency domain, where it can be compressed more efficiently.

The JPEG standard also uses a process called quantization to further reduce the amount of data in an image. During quantization, the DCT coefficients are divided by a set of quantization values, which removes information that is less important to the overall image.

The JPEG standard supports different color spaces, including RGB, YCbCr, and grayscale. It also supports progressive encoding, which allows images to be displayed gradually as they are downloaded.

The JPEG standard has several different compression modes, including baseline, extended, progressive, and lossless. The baseline mode is the most commonly used and provides a good balance between compression ratio and image quality.

The JPEG standard has some limitations, including a lack of support for transparency and limited support for alpha channels. It is also not well-suited for images with sharp edges or high contrast, as these can result in artifacts and distortion.

Despite its limitations, the JPEG standard remains one of the most widely used image compression algorithms and is supported by virtually all image editing software and web browsers.
