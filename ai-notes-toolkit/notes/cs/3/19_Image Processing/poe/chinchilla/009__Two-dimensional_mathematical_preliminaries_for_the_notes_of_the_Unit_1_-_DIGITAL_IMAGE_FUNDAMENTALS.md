### Two-dimensional mathematical preliminaries for the notes of the Unit 1 - DIGITAL IMAGE FUNDAMENTALS in the subject of Image Processing.

In order to understand digital image fundamentals, it is important to have a solid grasp of two-dimensional mathematics. The following points will cover the key mathematical concepts that form the foundation of digital image processing:

1. Cartesian Coordinate System: A two-dimensional coordinate system that uses x and y axes to represent points in a plane. The origin is the point (0,0) at the intersection of the axes.

2. Vectors: A vector is a quantity that has both magnitude and direction. In two dimensions, a vector can be represented as an ordered pair of numbers (x,y) or as an arrow pointing from the origin to the point (x,y).

3. Matrices: A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers arranged in rows and columns. Matrices are used to represent images in digital image processing. Each element in the matrix represents the intensity of a pixel in the image.

4. Transformations: Transformations are mathematical operations that can be applied to an image to change its appearance. Some common transformations include rotation, scaling, translation, and shearing.

5. Convolution: Convolution is a mathematical operation that is used for filtering an image. It involves sliding a small matrix called a kernel over the image and performing a mathematical operation at each location.

6. Fourier Transform: The Fourier Transform is a mathematical technique used to analyze the frequency content of an image. It decomposes an image into its frequency components, which can be used for filtering and compression.

7. Sampling: Sampling is the process of converting a continuous image into a discrete image by selecting a finite number of points. The sampling rate determines the resolution of the resulting image.

8. Interpolation: Interpolation is the process of estimating the values of pixels in between the sampled points. This is necessary when scaling an image up or down.

By understanding these two-dimensional mathematical concepts, you will have a solid foundation for digital image processing. These concepts are used throughout the field, and are essential for understanding more advanced topics in image processing.
