### Noise models for the notes of the Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION in the subject of Image Processing

In the field of image processing, noise is an unwanted disturbance that corrupts the quality of an image. There are various types of noise models that are used to represent the noise in an image. In this unit, we will study the following noise models:

1. Additive Gaussian Noise Model: 
   - This model assumes that the noise is added to the image as a Gaussian distribution.
   - The noise is independent of the image and has zero mean and a standard deviation of sigma.
   - This model is widely used in image processing due to its simplicity and the fact that many real-world noise sources can be approximated by a Gaussian distribution.

2. Poisson Noise Model:
   - This model is used to represent the noise in images that are acquired using sensors such as cameras or microscopes.
   - It assumes that the noise is generated by a Poisson distribution, which models the randomness of photon arrivals.
   - The variance of the noise is equal to the mean, and the noise is proportional to the signal.

3. Salt-and-Pepper Noise Model:
   - This model is used to represent the noise in images that have random black and white pixels.
   - The noise is generated by replacing some pixels in the image with black or white pixels.
   - This noise model is commonly found in images that are acquired in low light conditions or when the image is transmitted over a noisy channel.

4. Multiplicative Noise Model:
   - This model is used to represent the noise in images that are acquired using sensors such as radar or sonar.
   - The noise is proportional to the signal, and the noise is multiplied by the original image.
   - This noise model is challenging to remove because the noise is correlated with the signal.

5. Impulse Noise Model:
   - This model is used to represent the noise in images that have random bright or dark pixels.
   - The noise is generated by replacing some pixels in the image with bright or dark pixels.
   - This noise model is commonly found in images that are acquired in low light conditions or when the image is transmitted over a noisy channel.

In summary, we have studied various noise models that can be used to represent the noise in an image. Each noise model has its own characteristics, and the choice of the noise model depends on the image acquisition process and the type of noise present in the image. Understanding the noise model is essential for image restoration techniques, which aim to remove the noise and improve the quality of the image.
