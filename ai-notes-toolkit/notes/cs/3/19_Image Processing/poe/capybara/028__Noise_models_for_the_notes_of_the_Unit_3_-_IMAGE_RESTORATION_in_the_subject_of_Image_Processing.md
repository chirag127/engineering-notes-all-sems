### Noise Models for the Notes of the Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION in the Subject of Image Processing

Image restoration is a significant step in image processing that aims to restore the degraded images to their original quality. Noise models play a vital role in image restoration, and understanding them is crucial for image processing professionals. Here are some essential noise models that you need to know:

1. Additive Gaussian noise: This type of noise model is widely used in image processing and is often referred to as white noise. It is characterized by its Gaussian probability density function (PDF) and independent pixel values. The noise is added to the original image, and the objective is to restore the image by removing the noise.

2. Poisson noise: Poisson noise is another common noise model that occurs due to the random nature of photon arrivals in an image acquisition process. This noise model is characterized by its Poisson probability density function, and its mean is proportional to the intensity of the image. The objective is to restore the image by estimating the original intensity values.

3. Salt and pepper noise: This noise model is characterized by the presence of black and white pixels in the image. The black pixels represent the salt and the white pixels represent the pepper. This noise model occurs due to the errors in the image acquisition process, and the objective is to restore the image by removing the salt and pepper noise.

4. Speckle noise: Speckle noise is a multiplicative noise model that occurs in images due to the interference of coherent waves. This noise model is commonly found in ultrasound and synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images. The objective of image restoration in this case is to estimate the original image by removing the speckle noise.

5. Gaussian mixture noise: Gaussian mixture noise is a complex noise model that involves a mixture of multiple Gaussian distributions. It is commonly found in real-world scenarios and is more challenging to remove compared to other noise models. The objective of image restoration in this case is to estimate the original image by removing the noise.

Understanding noise models is crucial for image restoration in image processing. By understanding the different noise models and their characteristics, image processing professionals can choose the appropriate restoration technique to remove the noise and restore the image to its original quality.
