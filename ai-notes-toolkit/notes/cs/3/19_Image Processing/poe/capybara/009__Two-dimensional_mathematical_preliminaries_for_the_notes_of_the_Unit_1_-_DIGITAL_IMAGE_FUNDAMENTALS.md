### Two-Dimensional Mathematical Preliminaries for the Notes of Unit 1 - DIGITAL IMAGE FUNDAMENTALS in the Subject of Image Processing

In this section, we will cover the mathematical preliminaries necessary for understanding digital image processing. These preliminaries are essential for image analysis and manipulation. Here are some important points to keep in mind:

- **Pixel:** A pixel, short for picture element, is the smallest unit of a digital image. It is a square or rectangle that contains a color or grayscale value. In digital image processing, we manipulate pixels to achieve desired results.

- **Image Resolution:** Image resolution is the number of pixels per unit area in an image. It is usually measured in pixels per inch (ppi) or dots per inch (dpi). A higher resolution image has more pixels, which means it can contain more detail.

- **Spatial Domain:** The spatial domain refers to the arrangement of pixels in an image. In digital image processing, we can manipulate the spatial domain to enhance or filter an image.

- **Intensity:** Intensity is the brightness or darkness of a pixel. It is usually represented by a grayscale value, with 0 being black and 255 being white. In digital image processing, we can manipulate intensity to enhance or filter an image.

- **Histogram:** A histogram is a graph that shows the distribution of grayscale values in an image. It can be used to analyze the brightness or contrast of an image.

- **Convolution:** Convolution is a mathematical operation that applies a filter to an image. The filter is a small matrix of numbers that is multiplied with each pixel in the image. Convolution is used for blurring, sharpening, edge detection, and other image processing operations.

- **Fourier Transform:** The Fourier Transform is a mathematical operation that converts an image from the spatial domain to the frequency domain. In the frequency domain, we can analyze the high and low frequencies of an image. The Fourier Transform is used for noise reduction, compression, and other image processing operations.

- **Sampling:** Sampling is the process of converting a continuous signal, such as an analog image, into a discrete signal, such as a digital image. Sampling is necessary for digital image processing because digital images are made up of discrete pixels.

- **Aliasing:** Aliasing occurs when a signal is sampled at too low a frequency. It causes distortion and loss of information in digital images. To avoid aliasing, we must sample at a frequency that is at least twice the highest frequency in the original signal.

These are the essential mathematical concepts that are necessary for understanding digital image processing. By mastering these concepts, you will be able to analyze and manipulate digital images with precision and accuracy.
