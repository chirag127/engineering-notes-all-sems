
## Unit 4 - Image Segmentation

Image segmentation is the process of dividing an image into distinct regions or segments. It is used to identify objects or features in an image, such as lines, curves, and shapes. It can also be used to label regions of interest, such as buildings, trees, and roads.

1. Image segmentation techniques: Image segmentation techniques can be divided into two main categories: supervised and unsupervised. 
    * Supervised techniques use labeled data to train a model to identify objects or features in an image.
    * Unsupervised techniques use clustering methods to identify objects or features in an image without the use of labels.

2. Applications of image segmentation: Image segmentation is used in many applications, including medical imaging, satellite imagery analysis, and autonomous vehicle navigation.
    * Medical imaging: Image segmentation is used to identify and label different organs or tissues in medical images, such as MRI or CT scans.
    * Satellite imagery analysis: Image segmentation is used to identify and classify different land cover types, such as water, vegetation, and buildings.
    * Autonomous vehicle navigation: Image segmentation is used to identify and classify objects in the environment, such as roads, cars, and pedestrians.

3. Challenges of image segmentation: Image segmentation is a challenging problem due to the difficulty of accurately identifying objects or features in an image. Some of the main challenges include:
    * Low resolution: Images with low resolution can make it difficult to accurately identify objects or features.
    * Occlusion: Occlusion occurs when an object is partially or completely hidden from view. This can make it difficult to accurately identify objects or features.
    * Variability: Images can vary in terms of lighting, color, and texture, which can make it difficult to accurately identify objects or features.
