
### Degradation Model for Notes of Unit 3 - Image Restoration in Image Processing

1. Image degradation is the process by which an image loses its information content due to factors such as noise, blur, or compression.
2. The most common types of image degradation are blur, noise, and compression artifacts.
3. Blur is caused by a number of factors, including camera shake, out-of-focus shots, and long exposures.
4. Noise is caused by the presence of random fluctuations in the image, such as electrical noise from the camera sensor or from the environment.
5. Compression artifacts are caused by the lossy compression of an image, which results in the loss of image detail.
6. Image restoration is the process of correcting or improving the quality of degraded images.
7. Image restoration techniques can be divided into two categories: point-based and model-based.
8. Point-based techniques involve making local adjustments to the image, such as sharpening, noise reduction, and color correction.
9. Model-based techniques involve using a mathematical model to reconstruct the original image from the degraded image.
10. Common model-based techniques include inverse filtering, Wiener filtering, and blind deconvolution.
