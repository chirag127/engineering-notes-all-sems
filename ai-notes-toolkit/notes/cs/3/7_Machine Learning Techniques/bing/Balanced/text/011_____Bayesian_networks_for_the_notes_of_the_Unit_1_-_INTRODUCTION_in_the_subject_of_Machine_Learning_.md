### Bayesian networks

- Bayesian networks are a type of **probabilistic graphical model** that can be used to build models from data and/or expert opinion .
- They represent a set of **variables** and their **conditional dependencies** via a **directed acyclic graph (DAG)** .
- Each node in the graph corresponds to a **random variable**, and each edge represents the **conditional probability** for the corresponding random variables .
- Bayesian networks can be used for a wide range of tasks, such as:
  - **Diagnostics**: inferring the causes of observed symptoms or events.
  - **Reasoning**: updating beliefs based on new evidence or information.
  - **Causal modeling**: discovering or testing causal relationships among variables.
  - **Decision making under uncertainty**: choosing the best action given the available information and preferences.
  - **Anomaly detection**: identifying outliers or abnormal cases.
  - **Automated insight**: generating explanations or hypotheses from data.
  - **Prediction**: estimating the future values or outcomes of variables.
- Bayesian networks are based on the **Bayes' theorem**, which relates the **posterior probability** of a hypothesis given some evidence to the **prior probability** of the hypothesis and the **likelihood** of the evidence.
- Bayesian networks can handle **incomplete**, **noisy**, or **uncertain** data, and can incorporate **domain knowledge** or **expert opinion** into the model .
- Bayesian networks can be learned from data using **structure learning** and **parameter learning** algorithms, or can be specified manually by experts.
- Bayesian networks can be **inferred** using exact or approximate methods, such as **variable elimination**, **belief propagation**, **Markov chain Monte Carlo**, or **variational inference**.
- Bayesian networks can be **evaluated** using various metrics, such as **log-likelihood**, **Bayesian information criterion**, **Akaike information criterion**, **Kullback-Leibler divergence**, or **cross-validation**.
