### Genetic Programming for Reinforcement Learning

- Genetic programming (GP) is a method of evolving computer programs that can perform a specific task, such as classification, regression, or control .
- Reinforcement learning (RL) is a paradigm of learning from trial and error, where an agent interacts with an environment and receives rewards or penalties for its actions.
- Genetic programming for reinforcement learning (GPRL) is an approach that combines GP and RL to learn interpretable policies for dynamic decision-making and control problems  .
- GPRL can be applied to model-based or model-free RL settings, depending on whether the agent has access to a model of the environment or not .
- GPRL can learn policy equations from pre-existing state-action trajectory samples, or from online interactions with the environment   .
- GPRL can generate policies that are represented by basic algebraic equations of low complexity, which can be easily understood and analyzed by humans  .
- GPRL can be useful for industrial applications, such as wind or gas turbines, where interpretable and robust policies are desired .
- GPRL can also be used for symbolic regression, where the goal is to find a mathematical expression that fits a given data set .
- GPRL can be implemented using various GP techniques, such as tree-based, linear, or graph-based GP .
- GPRL can be evaluated using various performance metrics, such as policy quality, policy complexity, policy diversity, or policy interpretability .
