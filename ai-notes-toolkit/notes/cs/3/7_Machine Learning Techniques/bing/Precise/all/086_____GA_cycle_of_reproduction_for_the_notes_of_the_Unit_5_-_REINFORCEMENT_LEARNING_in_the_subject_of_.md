# GA Cycle of Reproduction

The GA cycle of reproduction is a process used in genetic algorithms to generate new solutions to a problem. It involves the following steps:

1. **Selection:** In this step, individuals from the current population are selected to be parents for the next generation. The selection process is typically based on the fitness of the individuals, with fitter individuals having a higher chance of being selected.

2. **Crossover:** In this step, two parent individuals are combined to create one or more offspring. This is done by exchanging genetic material between the parents to create new, unique individuals.

3. **Mutation:** In this step, small changes are made to the genetic material of the offspring. This introduces variation into the population and helps to prevent the algorithm from getting stuck in a local optimum.

4. **Evaluation:** In this step, the fitness of the new individuals is evaluated. This is done by testing how well they solve the problem at hand.

5. **Replacement:** In this step, the new individuals are added to the population, replacing some of the less fit individuals from the previous generation.

This cycle is repeated until a satisfactory solution is found or a stopping criterion is met.
