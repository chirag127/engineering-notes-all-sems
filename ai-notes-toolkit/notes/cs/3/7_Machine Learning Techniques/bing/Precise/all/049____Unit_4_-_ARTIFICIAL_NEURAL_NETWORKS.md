## Unit 4 - ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS

1. **Introduction:** Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) are computational models inspired by the structure and function of the human brain. They are designed to recognize patterns and make predictions based on data.

2. **Structure:** ANNs are composed of interconnected nodes or neurons, organized into layers. The input layer receives data, and the output layer produces the network's prediction. Hidden layers between the input and output layers process the data.

3. **Function:** Each neuron receives input from other neurons, applies a weight to each input, and sums the weighted inputs. The sum is then passed through an activation function, which determines the neuron's output.

4. **Training:** ANNs are trained using a dataset, where the network's predictions are compared to the known outcomes. The weights of the connections between neurons are adjusted to improve the network's accuracy.

5. **Applications:** ANNs have a wide range of applications, including image and speech recognition, natural language processing, and predictive analytics.

6. **Limitations:** ANNs require large amounts of data to train effectively and can be computationally expensive. They can also be difficult to interpret, as the relationships between inputs and outputs are not always clear.

7. **Future developments:** Research in the field of ANNs is ongoing, with a focus on improving their accuracy, efficiency, and interpretability. New architectures and training methods are being developed to address these challenges.
