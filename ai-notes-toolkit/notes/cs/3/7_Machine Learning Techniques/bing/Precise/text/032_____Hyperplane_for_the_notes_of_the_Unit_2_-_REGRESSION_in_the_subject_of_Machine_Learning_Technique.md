### Hyperplane

A hyperplane is a subspace of one dimension less than its ambient space. In the context of regression analysis, a hyperplane is a line or a plane that separates the data into two classes. In other words, it is a decision boundary that helps to classify the data points.

Some key points to remember about hyperplanes are:

- A hyperplane is defined by a normal vector and a bias term.
- The normal vector is perpendicular to the hyperplane.
- The bias term determines the position of the hyperplane relative to the origin.
- The equation of a hyperplane is given by the dot product of the normal vector and the data point, plus the bias term.
- In a two-dimensional space, a hyperplane is a line, while in a three-dimensional space, it is a plane.
- In higher dimensions, a hyperplane is a subspace of dimension n-1, where n is the dimension of the ambient space.
- In the context of support vector machines, the hyperplane is chosen to maximize the margin between the two classes.
