### Multilayer networks for the notes of the Unit 4 - ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS in the subject of Machine Learning Techniques

- Multilayer networks, also known as multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), are a type of artificial neural network that consists of multiple layers of interconnected nodes or neurons.
- These networks are typically composed of an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer.
- The input layer receives the data and passes it through the network to the output layer, where the final result is produced.
- The hidden layers in between the input and output layers are responsible for extracting and processing the relevant features from the input data.
- Each node in a layer is connected to every node in the next layer, and the strength of these connections, known as weights, determines the influence of one node on another.
- The weights are adjusted during the training process to improve the accuracy of the network's predictions.
- Multilayer networks are commonly used for tasks such as classification, regression, and prediction, and are widely used in fields such as computer vision, natural language processing, and speech recognition.
- They are capable of learning complex, non-linear relationships between the input and output data, making them a powerful tool for machine learning.
- Some common architectures for multilayer networks include feedforward neural networks, recurrent neural networks, and convolutional neural networks.
- The choice of architecture and the number of layers and nodes in each layer depends on the specific problem being addressed and the nature of the input data.
