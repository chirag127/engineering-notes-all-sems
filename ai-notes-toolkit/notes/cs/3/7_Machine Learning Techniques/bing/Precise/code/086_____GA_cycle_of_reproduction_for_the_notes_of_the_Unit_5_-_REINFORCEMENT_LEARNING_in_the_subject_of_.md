### GA Cycle of Reproduction

The GA cycle of reproduction is a process used in genetic algorithms to generate new solutions to a problem. It involves the following steps:

1. **Selection:** In this step, individuals from the current population are selected to be parents for the next generation. The selection process is typically based on the fitness of the individuals, with fitter individuals having a higher chance of being selected.

2. **Crossover:** In this step, two parent individuals are combined to create one or more offspring. This is done by exchanging genetic material between the parents to create new, unique individuals.

3. **Mutation:** In this step, the genetic material of the offspring is altered in some way. This can involve changing the value of a gene or swapping the positions of two genes. Mutation introduces diversity into the population and helps to prevent the algorithm from getting stuck in a local optimum.

4. **Evaluation:** In this step, the fitness of the new individuals is evaluated. This is done by assessing how well they solve the problem at hand.

5. **Replacement:** In this step, the new individuals are added to the population, replacing some of the less fit individuals from the previous generation. This ensures that the population is constantly improving and moving towards an optimal solution.

This cycle is repeated until a satisfactory solution is found or a predetermined stopping criterion is met.
