## Unit 4 - ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS

- Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are **computational models** inspired by the **biological neural networks** that constitute animal brains.
- ANNs are used to **approximate functions** that are generally unknown, such as recognizing patterns, classifying data, or making predictions.
- ANNs are composed of **layers** of **nodes** or **artificial neurons**, which are connected by **weights** and have **thresholds** or **activation functions**.
- Each node receives **inputs** from other nodes or external sources, and produces an **output** based on its weight, threshold, and activation function.
- The output of one node can be the input of another node, forming a **network** of nodes.
- The network can be **trained** by adjusting the weights and thresholds based on the **error** between the **desired output** and the **actual output**.
- The network can be **tested** by applying new inputs and comparing the outputs with the expected results.
- There are different **types** of ANNs, such as **feedforward neural networks**, **recurrent neural networks**, **convolutional neural networks**, **deep neural networks**, etc., each with different architectures, learning algorithms, and applications.
- ANNs are a subset of **machine learning** and are at the heart of **deep learning** algorithms.
- ANNs have many **applications** in various fields, such as image and voice recognition, natural language processing, robotics, data mining, medical diagnosis, etc.
