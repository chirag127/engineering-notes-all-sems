### History of ML

Machine learning (ML) is a branch of artificial intelligence (AI) that deals with the creation and study of systems that can learn from data and improve their performance without explicit programming. ML has a long and rich history that spans several decades and disciplines. Here are some of the key milestones and developments in the history of ML:

- **1943**: Walter Pitts and Warren McCulloch published a paper on the mathematical modeling of neural networks, which are biologically inspired computing systems that can learn from data and perform complex tasks. They proposed a simple model of a neuron that could fire or not fire based on the weighted sum of its inputs. This model laid the foundation for the field of artificial neural networks (ANNs) .
- **1950**: Alan Turing proposed the Turing test, a method to evaluate the intelligence of a machine by comparing its responses to those of a human in a natural language conversation. He also suggested that machines could learn from experience and improve their behavior .
- **1952**: Arthur Samuel, an IBM employee and pioneer in the field of computer gaming and AI, developed a program for playing checkers that could learn from its own mistakes and improve its performance over time. He coined the term machine learning to describe this process  .
- **1957**: Frank Rosenblatt, a psychologist and engineer at Cornell University, invented the perceptron, a simple type of ANN that could learn to classify patterns of inputs using a learning rule based on the Hebbian theory of synaptic plasticity. The perceptron was one of the first models of supervised learning, where the system learns from labeled data  .
- **1967**: Peter Hart, Nils Nilsson, and Bertram Raphael introduced the k-nearest neighbor (k-NN) algorithm, a simple and intuitive method of instance-based learning, where the system learns from specific examples rather than general rules. The k-NN algorithm classifies a new input based on the similarity to its k closest neighbors in the training data .
- **1974**: John Holland, a computer scientist and geneticist at the University of Michigan, developed the genetic algorithm, a method of evolutionary computation that mimics the process of natural selection to optimize a population of candidate solutions to a given problem. The genetic algorithm is an example of unsupervised learning, where the system learns from unlabeled data .
- **1979**: David Rumelhart, James McClelland, and Geoffrey Hinton, three cognitive psychologists and neural network researchers, proposed the backpropagation algorithm, a method of training ANNs by adjusting the weights of the connections based on the error between the desired and actual outputs. The backpropagation algorithm enabled the development of more complex and deeper ANNs that could learn from large amounts of data .
- **1986**: John Quinlan, a computer scientist and AI researcher at the University of Sydney, invented the ID3 algorithm, a method of decision tree learning that recursively splits the data into subsets based on the values of the features that best discriminate the classes. The ID3 algorithm is a widely used technique of inductive learning, where the system learns from specific examples to generate general rules .
- **1995**: Vladimir Vapnik, a mathematician and computer scientist at AT&T Bell Labs, introduced the support vector machine (SVM), a powerful and elegant method of classification and regression that uses a kernel function to map the data into a high-dimensional feature space where a linear decision boundary can be found. The SVM is based on the principle of structural risk minimization, which seeks to balance the complexity and accuracy of the model .
- **1997**: Tom Mitchell, a computer scientist and AI professor at Carnegie Mellon University, published his influential textbook "Machine Learning", which provided a formal and rigorous definition of the field: "A computer program is said to learn from experience E with respect to some class of tasks T and performance measure P, if its performance at tasks in T, as measured by P, improves with experience E." 
- **2006**: Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun, and Yoshua Bengio, three leading figures in the field of deep learning, coined the term deep learning to refer to a new class of ANNs that have multiple hidden layers and can learn from complex and high-dimensional data.
