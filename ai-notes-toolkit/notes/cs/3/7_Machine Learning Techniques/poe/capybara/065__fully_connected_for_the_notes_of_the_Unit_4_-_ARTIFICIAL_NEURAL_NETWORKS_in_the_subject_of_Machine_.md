### Fully Connected Networks

Artificial Neural Networks are models that can learn from data and improve their performance over time. One of the most common types of neural networks is the fully connected network, also known as a feedforward neural network.

Here are some key points to understand about fully connected networks:

- A fully connected network is composed of multiple layers of interconnected neurons. The first layer is the input layer, and the last layer is the output layer. The layers in between are known as hidden layers.
- Each neuron in a layer is connected to every neuron in the previous layer, and every neuron in the next layer is connected to every neuron in the current layer. This means that the network is fully connected, hence the name.
- Each connection between neurons has a weight associated with it. During training, the network adjusts these weights to minimize the error between the predicted output and the actual output.
- The activation function of each neuron determines its output based on the weighted sum of its inputs. Common activation functions include sigmoid, ReLU, and tanh.
- Fully connected networks can be used for a variety of tasks, including classification, regression, and even image and speech recognition.
- One major challenge with fully connected networks is the risk of overfitting. Overfitting occurs when the network becomes too complex and starts memorizing the training data instead of learning general patterns. Regularization techniques such as dropout and weight decay can help prevent overfitting.

Overall, fully connected networks are a powerful tool in the field of machine learning. By understanding their structure and training process, you can use them to solve a wide range of problems.
