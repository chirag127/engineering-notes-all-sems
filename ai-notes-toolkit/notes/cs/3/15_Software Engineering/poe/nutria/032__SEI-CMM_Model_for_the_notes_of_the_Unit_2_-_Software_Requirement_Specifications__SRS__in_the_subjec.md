
### SEI-CMM Model for the Notes of Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS)

1. The Software Engineering Institute's (SEI) Capability Maturity Model (CMM) is a process improvement model used to assess and improve the development process of software engineering projects.

2. The CMM consists of five levels of maturity, with each level representing an increasingly sophisticated and disciplined approach to software engineering.

3. The first level of the CMM, Initial, is the most basic level of maturity and is characterized by ad hoc processes, low levels of quality assurance, and little or no documentation.

4. The second level of the CMM, Repeatable, is characterized by the development of processes that are documented and repeatable.

5. The third level of the CMM, Defined, is characterized by the development of more formalized processes and the implementation of quality assurance procedures.

6. The fourth level of the CMM, Managed, is characterized by the development of measurable processes and the implementation of process improvement initiatives.

7. The fifth and final level of the CMM, Optimizing, is characterized by the development of processes that are continually improved based on the results of data collection and analysis.

8. The CMM is an important tool for assessing the maturity of an organization's software engineering processes and for developing a plan for process improvement. It is also an important tool for understanding the software requirements specification (SRS) process and its importance in software engineering.
