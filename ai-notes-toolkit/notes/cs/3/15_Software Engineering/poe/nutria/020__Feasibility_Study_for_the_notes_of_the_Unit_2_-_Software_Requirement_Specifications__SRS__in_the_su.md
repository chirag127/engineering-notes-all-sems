
### Feasibility Study for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

1. A feasibility study is an important step in the software development process. It helps to assess the viability of a project and determine if it should be pursued or not.

2. The purpose of a feasibility study is to assess the viability of a project by evaluating its potential benefits, risks, and costs. It helps to identify any potential issues that may arise during the development process and assess the likelihood of success.

3. A feasibility study should include an analysis of the project’s objectives, scope, timeline, technology, resources, and costs. It should also consider any potential risks or constraints that may arise.

4. The software requirements specification (SRS) is a document that outlines the functional and non-functional requirements of a software project. It should be used as the basis for the development of the software.

5. The SRS should include a detailed description of the software’s functions and features, as well as any constraints or assumptions that the developer must take into account.

6. The SRS should also include a description of the user interface, any hardware or software requirements, and a timeline for completion.

7. The feasibility study should include an analysis of the SRS to determine if the project is feasible and determine the best approach for development.

8. The feasibility study should also include an evaluation of the project’s potential risks, costs, and benefits. This will help to identify any potential issues that may arise during the development process and determine if the project is viable.
