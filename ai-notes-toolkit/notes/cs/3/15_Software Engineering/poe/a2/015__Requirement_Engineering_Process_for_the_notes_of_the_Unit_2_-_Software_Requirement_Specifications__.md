### Requirement Engineering Process for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering.

1. Elicitation: The process of gathering requirements from clients, users, and other stakeholders. This is typically an iterative process involving interviews, focus groups, questionnaires, and observation.
2. Analysis: The process of reviewing and refining the elicited requirements. Requirements are prioritized, conflicts are resolved, and requirements are decomposed into smaller requirements.
3. Specification: The requirements are documented in a formal requirements document (for example, using a standard template). Requirements are written in an unambiguous, measurable, and testable manner.
4. Validation: The requirements are checked to ensure that they accurately reflect the needs of the stakeholders. This is typically done through review meetings, prototyping, and scenarios/use cases.
5. Management: Requirements are managed and controlled throughout the development process. Changes to requirements are tracked and their impact is assessed.

The above points cover the key steps involved in the Requirement Engineering Process which results in the Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document that captures the requirements for the software to be developed. The SRS document is a formal statement of what the software product must do. It serves as a contract between the client and the developer.
