### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS)

1. Elicitation is the process of gathering requirements from various stakeholders including users, customers and domain experts.
2. Some common elicitation techniques are:
- Interviews: Meeting with stakeholders and asking them open-ended questions to understand their needs.
- Questionnaires: Asking stakeholders to fill out a written set of questions.
- Observations: Observing stakeholders performing their tasks and identifying areas for improvement.
- Focus Groups: Facilitating a group discussion among stakeholders to gather diverse perspectives.
- Document Analysis: Analyzing existing documents or processes to identify requirements.
3. Effective elicitation involves:
- Preparing a list of stakeholders to elicit requirements from.
- Carefully selecting appropriate elicitation techniques based on stakeholders and project context.
- Conducting elicitation sessions professionally while building rapport with stakeholders.
- Documenting requirements accurately without missing critical details.
- Prioritizing and resolving conflicts between different stakeholder needs.

The above points cover the key highlights of elicitation for software requirement specifications. The elicitation process helps in gathering crucial requirements from stakeholders which forms the foundation for SRS documentation. Conducting elicitation professionally and documenting requirements accurately are important to develop a comprehensive SRS.
