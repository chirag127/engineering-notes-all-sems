### Top-Down and Bottom-Up Design

1. Top-Down Design:
- In top-down design, the system is designed from the top-level modules towards lower-level modules.
- The main functions and highest-level structure of the system are defined first followed by further decomposition into lower-level modules.
- The main advantages are???
    - The overall structure is clear and visible upfront.
    - The critical parts of the system can be designed first.
    - Easy to estimate time and cost.

2. Bottom-Up Design:
- In bottom-up design, the lowest-level modules are designed first and then integrated to form higher-level modules.
- The basic components and functions are designed and implemented first followed by integration.
- The main advantages are???
    - Lower-level components can be designed and implemented independently.
    - The existing components can be reused.
    - Progressive development and testing is possible.

3. Combination of Both:
- In practice, a combination of top-down and bottom-up approaches is used.
- The overall structure is defined using the top-down approach.
- The lower-level components are designed and implemented using the bottom-up approach.
- This combines the advantages of both the approaches and avoids the disadvantages.
