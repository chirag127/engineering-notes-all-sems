### Data Flow Diagrams for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

1. Data flow diagrams (DFDs) are graphical representations of the flow of data through an information system. They model the process aspects of a system and can be developed independently of implementation details.
2. DFDs consist of four basic elements:
- Data flows: Arrows that show the flow of data between processes, data stores, and terminators.
- Processes: Rounded rectangles that represent transformations or manipulations of data.
- Data stores: Rectangles that represent the storage of data.
- Terminators: Symbols that represent the source or destination of a data flow.
3. There are two types of DFDs:
- Context diagrams: The top level DFD that shows the overall system and its interactions with the outside.
- Levelled DFDs: Successive decompositions of the major processes shown in the context diagram. They show a more detailed view of the system and processes.
4. Guidelines for developing DFDs:
- Choose meaningful names for the processes, data flows, data stores, and terminators.
- Keep the diagrams simple and avoid too much detail.
- Show data flows in only one direction.
- Ensure proper connectivity between the elements.
- Check for errors like dangling data flows or processes with no inputs or outputs.
