### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

Elicitation is the process of gathering information from stakeholders in order to define and document the requirements for a software system. It is a critical step in the development of a Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document. Here are some key points to consider when conducting elicitation:

1. Identify stakeholders: It is important to identify all the stakeholders who will be involved in the development of the software system. This includes end-users, customers, developers, and managers.

2. Choose elicitation techniques: There are several techniques that can be used to elicit requirements from stakeholders. These include interviews, questionnaires, workshops, and observation. The choice of technique will depend on the nature of the project and the stakeholders involved.

3. Prepare for elicitation: Before conducting elicitation, it is important to prepare by gathering background information, defining the scope of the project, and setting goals for the elicitation process.

4. Conduct elicitation: During the elicitation process, it is important to actively listen to stakeholders, ask clarifying questions, and document the information gathered.

5. Validate requirements: After the elicitation process is complete, it is important to validate the requirements gathered to ensure that they are complete, consistent, and accurate.

6. Manage requirements: Requirements management is an ongoing process that involves tracking changes to requirements, managing conflicts, and ensuring that requirements are met throughout the development process.

Elicitation is a critical step in the development of a Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document. By following these key points, you can ensure that the requirements gathered are accurate and complete, and that the resulting SRS document is a valuable tool for guiding the development of the software system.
