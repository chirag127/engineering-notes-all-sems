### Iterative Enhancement Models

Iterative Enhancement Models are a type of software development model that involves the development of software through repeated cycles of development and refinement. This approach is based on the idea that software development is an iterative process, where each iteration builds upon the previous one to improve the overall quality of the software.

Some key points to note about Iterative Enhancement Models are:

1. The development process is divided into smaller, more manageable iterations.
2. Each iteration involves the development of a working version of the software, which is then tested and refined.
3. Feedback from testing is used to improve the software in the next iteration.
4. The process continues until the software meets the desired level of quality and functionality.
5. This approach allows for flexibility and adaptability, as changes can be made during the development process.

Iterative Enhancement Models are commonly used in software development, as they allow for a more flexible and adaptable approach to development. They are particularly useful when the requirements of the software are not well-defined or are subject to change. By breaking the development process into smaller iterations, developers can more easily respond to changes in requirements and incorporate feedback from testing into the development process. This can result in higher quality software that better meets the needs of its users.
