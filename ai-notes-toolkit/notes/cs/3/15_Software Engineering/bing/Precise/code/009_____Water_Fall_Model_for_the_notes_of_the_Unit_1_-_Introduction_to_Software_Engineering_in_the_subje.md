### Waterfall Model

The Waterfall Model is a linear sequential approach to software development. It is also known as the classic life cycle model, as it suggests a systematic, sequential approach to software development. The model is divided into phases, with the output of one phase becoming the input for the next.

1. **Requirements**: In this phase, the requirements for the software are gathered and documented.
2. **Design**: In this phase, the system and software design is prepared based on the requirements gathered in the previous phase.
3. **Implementation**: In this phase, the software is developed according to the design.
4. **Verification**: In this phase, the software is tested to ensure that it meets the requirements and is free of defects.
5. **Maintenance**: In this phase, the software is maintained to ensure that it continues to meet the requirements and remains free of defects.

The Waterfall Model is easy to understand and use, and it provides a structured approach to software development. However, it has some limitations, such as the difficulty of accommodating changes once the development process has started, and the lack of working software until late in the development process. It is best suited for projects with well-defined requirements and a clear understanding of the final product.
