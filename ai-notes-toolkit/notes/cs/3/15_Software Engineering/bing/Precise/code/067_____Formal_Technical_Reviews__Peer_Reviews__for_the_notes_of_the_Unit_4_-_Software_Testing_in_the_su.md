### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews)

Formal Technical Reviews (FTRs), also known as Peer Reviews, are a structured and organized approach to reviewing software artifacts such as requirements, design, code, and test cases. They are an important part of the software development process and can help to improve the quality of the software being developed. Some key points to consider when conducting FTRs include:

1. **Preparation**: Before the review, the participants should be provided with the necessary materials and information to effectively review the artifact. This may include the artifact itself, any relevant documentation, and any specific review criteria.

2. **Roles**: There are several roles involved in an FTR, including the moderator, the author, the reviewers, and the recorder. Each role has specific responsibilities and it is important to clearly define these roles before the review.

3. **Conducting the review**: The review should be conducted in a structured and organized manner, with the moderator leading the discussion and ensuring that all participants have the opportunity to provide feedback. The feedback should be constructive and focused on improving the quality of the artifact.

4. **Follow-up**: After the review, the author should address any issues or concerns raised during the review and make any necessary changes to the artifact. The moderator should ensure that all action items are completed and that the review is properly documented.

FTRs can be a valuable tool for improving the quality of software artifacts and for promoting collaboration and communication within the development team. By following a structured and organized approach, teams can effectively conduct FTRs and benefit from the insights and feedback provided by their peers.
