### Software Measurement and Metrics

Software measurement is a process of quantifying the characteristics of software products or processes. Metrics are the units of measurement used to quantify these characteristics. In the context of software design, software measurement and metrics are used to evaluate the quality of the design and to identify areas for improvement.

Some common software design metrics include:

1. **Cohesion:** A measure of how closely the responsibilities of a module are related to each other. High cohesion is desirable as it indicates that the module has a single, well-defined responsibility.
2. **Coupling:** A measure of the degree to which a module depends on other modules. Low coupling is desirable as it indicates that the module is independent and can be modified without affecting other modules.
3. **Complexity:** A measure of the difficulty of understanding, designing, or modifying a module. Low complexity is desirable as it makes the module easier to work with.
4. **Size:** A measure of the amount of code or functionality in a module. Smaller modules are generally easier to understand and maintain than larger ones.

These metrics can be used to evaluate the quality of a software design and to identify areas for improvement. By measuring and tracking these metrics over time, it is possible to identify trends and make informed decisions about how to improve the design.
