### Low Level Design for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Low Level Design (LLD) is the process of creating a detailed design for a software system or component. It is also known as detailed design or micro design. The main purpose of LLD is to translate the High Level Design (HLD) into a more detailed and specific design that can be implemented by the development team.

Some key points to consider when creating a Low Level Design are:

1. LLD should provide a detailed description of the software system or component, including its functionality, interfaces, and data structures.
2. LLD should be consistent with the HLD and should not introduce any new requirements or functionality.
3. LLD should provide enough detail for the development team to implement the software system or component without the need for further clarification.
4. LLD should be modular and should promote code reuse and maintainability.
5. LLD should consider non-functional requirements such as performance, security, and scalability.

In summary, Low Level Design is an important step in the software development process that helps to ensure that the final product meets the requirements and is of high quality. It is essential to have a well-defined and detailed LLD to facilitate the development process and to minimize the risk of errors and defects.
