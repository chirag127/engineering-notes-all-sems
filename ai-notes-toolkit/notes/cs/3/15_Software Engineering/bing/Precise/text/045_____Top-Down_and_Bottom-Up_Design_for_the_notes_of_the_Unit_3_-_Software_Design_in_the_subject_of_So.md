### Top-Down and Bottom-Up Design

Top-down and bottom-up are two approaches to the design of software systems. These approaches are used to decompose a complex system into smaller, more manageable components.

1. **Top-Down Design:** This approach starts with a high-level view of the system and breaks it down into smaller, more detailed components. The process continues until the lowest level of detail is reached. This approach is also known as stepwise refinement or functional decomposition.

2. **Bottom-Up Design:** This approach starts with the lowest level of detail and builds up the system by combining components into larger, more complex components. This approach is also known as synthesis or composition.

Both approaches have their advantages and disadvantages. Top-down design allows for a clear understanding of the overall system and its components, while bottom-up design allows for the reuse of existing components and can result in more efficient code. The choice of approach depends on the specific requirements of the system being designed.

In practice, a combination of both approaches is often used. This is known as hybrid design or middle-out design. In this approach, some components are designed top-down, while others are designed bottom-up. This allows for the advantages of both approaches to be utilized.
