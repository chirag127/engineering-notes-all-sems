### Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Models

The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a framework that defines the steps involved in the development of software. The SDLC process aims to produce high-quality software that meets customer expectations, reaches completion within time and cost estimates, and is maintainable. There are several SDLC models, including:

1. **Waterfall Model**: This is a linear-sequential life cycle model, where each phase must be completed before the next one can begin. The phases are: Requirements, Design, Implementation, Verification, and Maintenance.

2. **V-Model**: This is an extension of the Waterfall Model, where testing is emphasized and integrated into each phase of the development process.

3. **Incremental Model**: This model involves developing the software in increments, where each increment builds upon the previous one until the final product is complete.

4. **Spiral Model**: This model combines elements of the Waterfall and Incremental models, with a focus on risk management. The development process is divided into a series of iterations, where each iteration involves identifying and mitigating risks.

5. **Agile Model**: This model emphasizes flexibility and adaptability, with a focus on customer satisfaction and collaboration. The development process is divided into a series of iterations, where each iteration involves developing and delivering a working product increment.

These are some of the most commonly used SDLC models. Each model has its strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of model depends on the specific needs and constraints of the project.
