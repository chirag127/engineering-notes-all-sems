### Documentation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

1. **Introduction:** Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) is a document that describes the requirements of a software system. It is a comprehensive description of the intended purpose and environment for the software under development.

2. **Purpose:** The purpose of the SRS is to establish a clear understanding between the customer and the developer of what the software is expected to do. It serves as a basis for the development of the software and as a reference for future maintenance.

3. **Scope:** The SRS should define the scope of the software system, including its functionality, performance, and constraints. It should also identify any interfaces with other systems and any assumptions or dependencies.

4. **Functional Requirements:** The functional requirements describe the features and capabilities of the software system. They specify what the system should do, in terms of the tasks and services it provides to the user.

5. **Non-Functional Requirements:** The non-functional requirements describe the characteristics of the software system, such as its performance, reliability, and usability. They specify how well the system should do what it does.

6. **Constraints:** The SRS should identify any constraints on the development of the software system, such as technical, legal, or regulatory constraints.

7. **Assumptions and Dependencies:** The SRS should identify any assumptions or dependencies that may affect the development of the software system. These may include assumptions about the operating environment, dependencies on other systems, or assumptions about the availability of resources.

8. **Acceptance Criteria:** The SRS should define the acceptance criteria for the software system, specifying the conditions under which the system will be considered acceptable by the customer.

9. **Conclusion:** The SRS is an important document in the software development process. It provides a clear and comprehensive description of the requirements of the software system, serving as a basis for development and as a reference for future maintenance. It is important to ensure that the SRS is accurate, complete, and unambiguous, to avoid misunderstandings and to ensure the successful development of the software system.
