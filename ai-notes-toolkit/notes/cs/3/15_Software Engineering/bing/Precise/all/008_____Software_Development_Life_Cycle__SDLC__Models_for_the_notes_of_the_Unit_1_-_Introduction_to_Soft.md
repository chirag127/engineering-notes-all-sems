# Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Models

The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a framework that defines the process used by organizations to build an application from its inception to its decommission. There are several SDLC models that can be used to guide the development process. Here are some of the most commonly used models:

1. **Waterfall Model**: This is a linear sequential approach where each phase of the development process must be completed before the next one can begin. The phases are typically requirements gathering, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance.

2. **V-Model**: This is an extension of the Waterfall model where the testing phase is integrated into each phase of the development process. The V shape represents the relationship between the development and testing phases.

3. **Iterative Model**: This model involves building the software incrementally, with each iteration adding more functionality. The development process is divided into smaller, more manageable chunks, allowing for more flexibility and the ability to incorporate feedback from users.

4. **Spiral Model**: This model combines elements of the Waterfall and Iterative models. It involves a series of iterations, with each one consisting of four phases: planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation.

5. **Agile Model**: This is a flexible, iterative approach that emphasizes collaboration between the development team and the customer. It involves breaking the development process into small, manageable chunks called sprints, with each sprint resulting in a potentially shippable product increment.

Each of these models has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of model will depend on the specific needs and constraints of the project. It is important to carefully evaluate the requirements and characteristics of the project before selecting an SDLC model to follow.
