# Entity Relationship Diagrams

Entity Relationship Diagrams (ERD) are a graphical representation of the relationships between entities in a database. They are used in the design of database systems to illustrate the relationships between tables and to help ensure that the database is properly normalized.

Some key points to remember when creating an ERD include:

1. Entities are represented as rectangles and are usually named using singular nouns.
2. Relationships between entities are represented as lines connecting the entities. The type of relationship is indicated by the line's label.
3. Attributes of entities are represented as ovals connected to the entity rectangle by a line.
4. Cardinality, or the number of instances of one entity that can be associated with instances of another entity, is indicated by placing numbers or symbols near the relationship line.

ERDs are an important tool in the design of database systems and can help ensure that the database is properly normalized and that relationships between entities are accurately represented. They are commonly used in the development of Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) as part of the software engineering process.
