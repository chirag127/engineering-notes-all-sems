### Waterfall Model

The Waterfall Model is a linear sequential approach to software development. It is also known as the classic life cycle model or the traditional model. The model is based on the idea that the development process is divided into distinct phases, with each phase focusing on a specific activity.

The phases of the Waterfall Model are as follows:

1. **Requirements Analysis:** In this phase, the requirements of the system are gathered and analyzed. The goal is to understand the needs of the customer and to define the scope of the project.

2. **Design:** In this phase, the system is designed based on the requirements gathered in the previous phase. The design phase includes both high-level design and detailed design.

3. **Implementation:** In this phase, the system is coded and tested. The goal is to implement the design and to ensure that the system meets the requirements.

4. **Verification:** In this phase, the system is tested to ensure that it meets the requirements. The goal is to verify that the system is working as intended.

5. **Maintenance:** In this phase, the system is maintained to ensure that it continues to meet the requirements. The goal is to fix any issues that arise and to make any necessary changes to the system.

The Waterfall Model is a simple and straightforward approach to software development. However, it has some limitations. For example, it assumes that the requirements are well-defined and that they will not change during the development process. This is not always the case in real-world projects. Additionally, the model does not provide a mechanism for dealing with changes in requirements or for incorporating feedback from the customer.

Despite its limitations, the Waterfall Model is still widely used in software development. It provides a structured approach to the development process and can be effective for projects with well-defined requirements. However, it is important to be aware of its limitations and to use it appropriately.
