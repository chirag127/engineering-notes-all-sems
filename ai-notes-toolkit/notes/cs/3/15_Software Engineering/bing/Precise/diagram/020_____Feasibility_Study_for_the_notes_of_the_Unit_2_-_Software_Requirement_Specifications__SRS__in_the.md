### Feasibility Study

A feasibility study is an evaluation and analysis of the potential of a proposed project to support the process of decision making. It is conducted to determine whether a project is viable and worth pursuing. In the context of software engineering, a feasibility study is conducted during the software requirement specification (SRS) phase of the software development life cycle.

The main objectives of a feasibility study are to:

1. Determine whether the proposed project is technically, economically, and operationally feasible.
2. Identify the risks and uncertainties associated with the project.
3. Provide information to support decision making.

There are several types of feasibility studies, including:

1. **Technical feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed system can be developed using the available technology and resources.
2. **Economic feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed system is financially viable and whether the expected benefits outweigh the costs.
3. **Operational feasibility:** This assesses whether the proposed system can be integrated into the existing business processes and whether it will be accepted by the users.

A feasibility study is an important step in the software development process as it helps to identify potential problems and risks early on, allowing for better planning and decision making. It is essential to conduct a thorough feasibility study before committing to a project to ensure its success.
