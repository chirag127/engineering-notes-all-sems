### Flow Charts

Flow charts are a type of diagram that represents an algorithm, workflow or process. They are commonly used in software design to visualize the steps and logic of a program or system. Flow charts use a set of standard symbols to represent different types of actions or steps, such as:

- **Start/End**: represented by an oval or rounded rectangle, indicates the beginning or end of a process.
- **Process**: represented by a rectangle, indicates a step or action to be performed.
- **Decision**: represented by a diamond, indicates a point where a decision must be made, usually with two or more possible outcomes.
- **Input/Output**: represented by a parallelogram, indicates a step where data is input or output.
- **Flow lines**: represented by arrows, indicate the direction of flow and the sequence of steps.

Flow charts are useful for designing and documenting complex processes, as they provide a clear and concise visual representation of the steps involved. They can also be used to identify potential problems or inefficiencies in a process, and to communicate the design to others.

Here is an example of a simple flow chart that represents the process of logging in to a website:

```
Start
|
v
Enter username and password
|
v
Is the username and password correct?
|
| Yes
v
Access granted
|
v
End
|
No
v
Access denied
|
v
End
```

Flow charts can be created using specialized software, or by hand using a pen and paper. They are an important tool in software design and can help to ensure that the final product is well-designed and easy to use.
