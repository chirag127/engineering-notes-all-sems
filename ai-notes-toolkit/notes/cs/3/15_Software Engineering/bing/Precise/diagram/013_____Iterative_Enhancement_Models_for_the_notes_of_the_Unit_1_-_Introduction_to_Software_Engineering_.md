### Iterative Enhancement Models

Iterative Enhancement Models are a type of software development process model that involves the development of software in incremental stages. This model is based on the idea that software development is an iterative process, where each iteration involves the development of a small part of the software, followed by testing and feedback, and then further development based on the feedback received.

Some key points to note about Iterative Enhancement Models are:

1. The software is developed in small increments, with each increment building upon the previous one.
2. Each increment involves the development of a small part of the software, followed by testing and feedback.
3. The feedback received is used to guide further development in the next iteration.
4. This process is repeated until the software is complete and meets the requirements of the user.
5. The iterative approach allows for flexibility in the development process, as changes can be made based on feedback received.
6. This model is suitable for projects where the requirements are not well-defined or are likely to change during the development process.

Overall, the Iterative Enhancement Model is a flexible and adaptable approach to software development that allows for changes to be made during the development process based on feedback received. It is particularly useful for projects where the requirements are not well-defined or are likely to change.
