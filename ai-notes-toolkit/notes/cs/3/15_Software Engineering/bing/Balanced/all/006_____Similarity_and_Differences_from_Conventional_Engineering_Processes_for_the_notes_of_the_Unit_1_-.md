# Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes for Software Engineering

Software engineering is the application of engineering principles and practices to the development and maintenance of software systems. Conventional engineering is the branch of engineering that deals with the design, construction, and operation of physical systems, such as buildings, machines, bridges, etc. 

## Similarities

- Both software engineering and conventional engineering require **in-depth knowledge** of their field and the use of **mathematical and logical** tools to solve problems.
- Both software engineering and conventional engineering involve **planning, designing, testing, and improving** the products or systems they create .
- Both software engineering and conventional engineering follow **standards and guidelines** to ensure the quality, reliability, and safety of their products or systems .
- Both software engineering and conventional engineering are **getting automated** slowly with the help of advanced technologies and tools.

## Differences

- Software engineering is mainly concerned with the **creation of intangible** products or systems, such as software applications, websites, databases, etc., whereas conventional engineering is mainly concerned with the **creation of tangible** products or systems, such as buildings, machines, bridges, etc .
- Software engineering is more **dynamic and adaptable** to the changing requirements and needs of the customers and the market, whereas conventional engineering is more **static and rigid** to the predefined specifications and constraints of the physical world .
- Software engineering has a **higher degree of complexity and abstraction** than conventional engineering, as software systems often involve multiple layers, components, interfaces, and dependencies that are not visible or easily understandable .
- Software engineering has more **opportunities of foreign settlement** and a **typical office job**, whereas conventional engineering has more **opportunities of government sector** and a **more physically active role**.
