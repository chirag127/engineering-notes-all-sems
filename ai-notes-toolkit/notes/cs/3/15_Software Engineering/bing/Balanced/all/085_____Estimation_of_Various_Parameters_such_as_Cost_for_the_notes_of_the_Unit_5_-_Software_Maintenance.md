# Estimation of Various Parameters such as Cost for the notes of the Unit 5 - Software Maintenance and Software Project Management in the subject of Software Engineering

Estimation is the process of predicting the amount of time, effort, resources, and cost required to complete a software project. Estimation is essential for planning, budgeting, and controlling the software project. Estimation also helps to assess the feasibility, risk, and quality of the software project.

There are various techniques for estimating software projects, each with its own advantages and disadvantages. Some of the common techniques are:

- **Expert Judgment**: In this technique, a group of experts in the relevant field estimates the project size based on their experience and expertise. This technique is often used when there is limited information available about the project. The advantages of this technique are that it is quick, easy, and flexible. The disadvantages are that it is subjective, inconsistent, and prone to bias and error.

- **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)**: A big project is made manageable by first breaking it down into individual components in a hierarchical structure, known as the Work breakdown structure, or the WBS. Each component is then estimated separately and the total estimate is obtained by aggregating the estimates of all the components . The advantages of this technique are that it is systematic, detailed, and accurate. The disadvantages are that it is time-consuming, complex, and requires a lot of information.

- **Three Point Estimation**: Three-point estimation is a technique that makes use of three types of estimates for each component of the project: optimistic, pessimistic, and most likely. The optimistic estimate is the best-case scenario, the pessimistic estimate is the worst-case scenario, and the most likely estimate is the most realistic scenario. The final estimate is calculated by using a weighted average of the three estimates, usually with more weight given to the most likely estimate . The advantages of this technique are that it accounts for uncertainty, variability, and risk. The disadvantages are that it requires more data, assumptions, and calculations.

- **Functional Point Method**: Functional point method is a technique that measures the size of the software project based on the functionality and complexity of the software requirements. The functionality is expressed in terms of inputs, outputs, inquiries, files, and interfaces. The complexity is determined by using a table that assigns a weight to each type of functionality based on its difficulty. The total functional points are obtained by multiplying the number of each type of functionality by its weight. The total functional points are then converted to effort, time, and cost by using productivity factors and historical data . The advantages of this technique are that it is independent of technology, language, and development method. The disadvantages are that it is subjective, tedious, and requires a lot of expertise.

- **Story Points**: Story points are units of measure for expressing an estimate of the overall effort required to fully implement a product backlog item or any other piece of work. Teams assign story points relative to work complexity, the amount of work, and risk or uncertainty. Story points are usually estimated by using a technique called planning poker, where each team member gives their estimate using a card with a number on it. The team then discusses the estimates and reaches a consensus. The advantages of this technique are that it is collaborative, simple, and agile. The disadvantages are that it is not precise, consistent, or comparable across projects.
