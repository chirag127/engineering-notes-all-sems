# Architectural Design

Architectural design is the process of defining the structure, behavior, and interactions of the software components that form a system. It is an abstraction of the system that provides a high-level view of its functionality, quality attributes, and constraints. Architectural design is one of the important phases of software engineering, as it lays the foundation for the subsequent development activities.

Some of the objectives of architectural design are:

- To satisfy the requirements of the system, such as functionality, performance, reliability, security, etc.
- To facilitate communication and collaboration among the stakeholders, such as developers, testers, customers, users, etc.
- To enable reuse and evolution of the system, by providing modularity, flexibility, extensibility, etc.
- To reduce the complexity and risk of the system, by applying appropriate design principles, patterns, and standards.

Some of the steps involved in architectural design are:

- Identify and analyze the system requirements, such as functional, non-functional, and domain-specific requirements.
- Define and document the architectural views, such as logical, physical, process, deployment, etc., that describe the system from different perspectives.
- Select and apply the architectural styles, such as client-server, peer-to-peer, layered, pipe-and-filter, etc., that define the overall structure and organization of the system.
- Choose and integrate the architectural components, such as modules, classes, objects, services, etc., that implement the functionality and behavior of the system.
- Specify and verify the architectural connectors, such as interfaces, protocols, messages, events, etc., that enable the communication and interaction among the components.
- Evaluate and validate the architectural design, by using various methods and techniques, such as reviews, inspections, simulations, testing, etc., to ensure its quality and suitability for the system.
