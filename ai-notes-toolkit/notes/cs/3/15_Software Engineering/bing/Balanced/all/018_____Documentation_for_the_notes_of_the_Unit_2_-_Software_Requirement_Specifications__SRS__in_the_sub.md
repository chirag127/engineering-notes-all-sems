# Documentation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

## Introduction
- Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) is a document that describes the requirements of a software system.
- SRS is also known as Software Requirements Document (SRD), Software Requirements Specification (SRS), or Functional Specification (FS).
- SRS is a part of the software development process that establishes a common understanding between the stakeholders and the developers of the system.
- SRS helps to ensure that the software system meets the needs and expectations of the users and the clients.
- SRS also serves as a basis for software design, testing, validation, verification, maintenance, and evolution.

## Purpose of SRS
- The main purpose of SRS is to define the scope and the boundaries of the software system.
- SRS also specifies the functional and non-functional requirements of the system, such as the features, the performance, the reliability, the security, the usability, the portability, etc.
- SRS also provides the criteria for evaluating the quality and the completeness of the system.
- SRS also helps to manage the changes and the risks associated with the system development.
- SRS also facilitates the communication and the collaboration among the stakeholders and the developers of the system.

## Characteristics of a Good SRS
- A good SRS should be clear, concise, consistent, complete, correct, verifiable, modifiable, and traceable.
- Clear: The SRS should use simple and unambiguous language that can be easily understood by the stakeholders and the developers of the system.
- Concise: The SRS should avoid unnecessary details and repetitions that can confuse or distract the readers.
- Consistent: The SRS should maintain the same terminology, notation, format, and style throughout the document. The SRS should also avoid any conflicts or contradictions among the requirements.
- Complete: The SRS should cover all the requirements of the system, including the functional and non-functional aspects. The SRS should also specify any assumptions, dependencies, constraints, or limitations that affect the system.
- Correct: The SRS should reflect the actual needs and expectations of the users and the clients. The SRS should also be free of any errors or mistakes that can compromise the quality of the system.
- Verifiable: The SRS should state the requirements in a way that can be checked or tested against the system. The SRS should also provide the methods or the criteria for verifying the requirements.
- Modifiable: The SRS should be organized and structured in a way that can accommodate any changes or updates to the requirements. The SRS should also provide the procedures or the mechanisms for managing the changes to the requirements.
- Traceable: The SRS should be able to trace the origin and the rationale of each requirement. The SRS should also be able to track the relationship and the impact of each requirement on the other requirements, the design, the implementation, the testing, and the maintenance of the system.
