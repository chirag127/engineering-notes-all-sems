# Flow Charts for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

- A flow chart is a graphical representation of an algorithm or a process that uses different shapes and symbols to show the steps and the flow of control.
- Flow charts are useful for planning, designing, testing, debugging, and documenting software programs.
- Flow charts can also help to communicate the logic and functionality of a program to other developers, users, or stakeholders.
- Flow charts can be drawn using various tools, such as Lucidchart, Microsoft Visio, Textografo, diagrams.net, SmartDraw, etc.
- Flow charts follow some common conventions and standards, such as:
  - The start and end of a flow chart are marked by a circle or an oval with the words "Start" and "End" or "Stop".
  - The steps or actions in a flow chart are represented by rectangles with the description of the task inside.
  - The decisions or branching points in a flow chart are represented by diamonds with the condition or question inside and the possible outcomes labeled on the outgoing arrows.
  - The flow of control or direction of execution in a flow chart is indicated by arrows connecting the shapes.
  - The input and output of data in a flow chart are represented by parallelograms with the source or destination of the data inside.
  - The subroutines or functions in a flow chart are represented by rectangles with rounded corners and the name of the subroutine or function inside.
  - The predefined processes or modules in a flow chart are represented by rectangles with double vertical lines and the name of the process or module inside.
  - The comments or annotations in a flow chart are represented by rectangles with dashed lines and the text inside.

- Flow charts have some advantages and disadvantages, such as:
  - Advantages:
    - They are easy to understand and follow, even for non-programmers.
    - They can help to identify errors, gaps, or redundancies in the logic of a program.
    - They can facilitate the testing and debugging of a program by tracing the expected and actual outcomes of each step.
    - They can serve as a documentation or a specification of a program for future reference or modification.
  - Disadvantages:
    - They can be time-consuming and tedious to create and maintain, especially for complex or large programs.
    - They can become cluttered and unreadable if there are too many shapes, symbols, or arrows.
    - They can be difficult to modify or update if the program logic changes or evolves.
    - They can be inconsistent or ambiguous if different tools or standards are used to create them.
