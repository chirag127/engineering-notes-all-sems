### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Elicitation is the process of gathering, understanding, and documenting the needs and expectations of the stakeholders for a software system.
- Elicitation is also known as requirements engineering, requirements analysis, or requirements discovery.
- Elicitation is a crucial activity in software engineering, as it helps to define the scope, quality, and feasibility of the software project.
- Elicitation involves various techniques, such as interviews, questionnaires, surveys, observation, brainstorming, prototyping, scenarios, use cases, and user stories.
- Elicitation has several challenges, such as ambiguity, inconsistency, incompleteness, volatility, and conflict of requirements.
- Elicitation follows a general process, which consists of the following steps:
  - Identify the stakeholders: the people who have an interest or influence in the software project, such as customers, users, developers, managers, regulators, etc.
  - Plan the elicitation: the strategy, schedule, resources, and methods for conducting the elicitation activities.
  - Prepare for elicitation: the preparation of the elicitation materials, such as questions, documents, prototypes, etc.
  - Conduct the elicitation: the execution of the elicitation techniques, such as asking questions, listening, observing, etc.
  - Document the elicitation: the recording of the elicitation results, such as notes, transcripts, diagrams, models, etc.
  - Validate the elicitation: the verification and validation of the elicitation results, such as checking for accuracy, completeness, consistency, and clarity of the requirements.
  - Manage the elicitation: the management of the elicitation process, such as monitoring, controlling, and reporting the elicitation activities and outcomes.
