## Unit 4 - Software Testing

Software testing is the process of verifying and validating that a software product meets the requirements and expectations of the stakeholders. Software testing can be performed at different levels of the software development life cycle, such as unit testing, integration testing, system testing, and acceptance testing. Software testing can also be classified into different types, such as functional testing, non-functional testing, white-box testing, black-box testing, and grey-box testing.

The main objectives of software testing are:

- To ensure that the software product conforms to the specified requirements and design.
- To detect and correct any defects or errors in the software product before it is delivered to the end-users or customers.
- To measure and improve the quality, reliability, usability, performance, and security of the software product.
- To provide feedback and information to the developers and other stakeholders about the software product and its development process.

The main principles of software testing are:

- Testing shows the presence of defects, not the absence of defects. Testing can only reveal the existence of defects in the software product, but it cannot prove that the software product is defect-free.
- Exhaustive testing is impossible. Testing all possible inputs, outputs, paths, scenarios, and combinations of the software product is impractical and infeasible. Therefore, testing should be based on risk analysis, prioritization, and coverage criteria.
- Early testing saves time and cost. Testing should start as early as possible in the software development life cycle, and should be performed iteratively and incrementally throughout the development process. This can help to identify and fix defects at an early stage, and reduce the cost and effort of rework and maintenance.
- Defect clustering follows the Pareto principle. A small number of modules or components of the software product tend to contain most of the defects. Therefore, testing should focus on the critical and high-risk areas of the software product, and use techniques such as defect analysis and defect prevention to improve the quality of the software product.
- Pesticide paradox states that if the same tests are repeated over and over again, they will eventually lose their effectiveness in finding new defects. Therefore, testing should be regularly reviewed and updated to include new test cases, scenarios, and techniques to cope with the changes and evolution of the software product.
- Testing is context-dependent. Testing should be tailored and adapted to the specific characteristics, requirements, and objectives of the software product and its intended users, customers, and environment. There is no one-size-fits-all approach or standard for software testing.
- Absence of errors fallacy states that finding and fixing defects does not necessarily imply that the software product is useful or usable. Testing should not only focus on the technical aspects of the software product, but also on the business and user needs and expectations of the software product.
