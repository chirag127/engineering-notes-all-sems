### Testing Objectives

- Software testing is the process of verifying and validating that a software product meets the requirements and specifications that guided its development and design.
- The main objectives of software testing are:
  - To ensure that the software product is free of defects and errors that could affect its functionality, performance, reliability, security, and usability.
  - To identify and eliminate any discrepancies between the expected and actual behavior of the software product under different conditions and scenarios.
  - To provide confidence and satisfaction to the stakeholders, customers, and users that the software product meets their needs and expectations and fulfills its intended purpose.
  - To comply with the standards, regulations, and quality criteria that are applicable to the software product and its domain.
  - To reduce the risks and costs associated with software failures, malfunctions, and maintenance in the operational phase.
- The objectives of software testing can be achieved by applying various testing techniques, methods, levels, and types throughout the software development life cycle.
- Some of the common testing techniques are:
  - Static testing: It involves the analysis and inspection of the software artifacts, such as requirements, design, code, and documentation, without executing them.
  - Dynamic testing: It involves the execution of the software product or its components with different inputs, outputs, and environments to observe its behavior and results.
- Some of the common testing methods are:
  - White-box testing: It involves the testing of the internal structure, logic, and implementation of the software product or its components, using the knowledge of the source code and design.
  - Black-box testing: It involves the testing of the external functionality and interface of the software product or its components, using the knowledge of the requirements and specifications, without considering the internal structure and implementation.
  - Grey-box testing: It involves the testing of the software product or its components, using a combination of the knowledge of the internal structure and implementation and the external functionality and interface.
- Some of the common testing levels are:
  - Unit testing: It involves the testing of the smallest and independent units or modules of the software product, such as functions, classes, methods, and procedures, to verify their correctness and functionality.
  - Integration testing: It involves the testing of the interactions and interfaces between the units or modules of the software product, to verify their compatibility and coordination.
  - System testing: It involves the testing of the whole software product as a single entity, to verify its compliance with the requirements and specifications, and its suitability for the intended environment and users.
  - Acceptance testing: It involves the testing of the software product by the stakeholders, customers, and users, to verify its acceptance and satisfaction of their needs and expectations, and its readiness for deployment and operation.
- Some of the common testing types are:
  - Functional testing: It involves the testing of the functional requirements and specifications of the software product, such as inputs, outputs, processes, data, and features, to verify its functionality and correctness.
  - Non-functional testing: It involves the testing of the non-functional requirements and specifications of the software product, such as performance, reliability, security, usability, and maintainability, to verify its quality and efficiency.
  - Regression testing: It involves the testing of the software product or its components after any changes, modifications, or updates, to verify that the existing functionality and quality are not affected or degraded.
  - Smoke testing: It involves the testing of the basic and critical functionality and features of the software product, to verify its stability and readiness for further testing.
  - Sanity testing: It involves the testing of the specific and essential functionality and features of the software product, to verify its rationality and validity for the intended purpose.
