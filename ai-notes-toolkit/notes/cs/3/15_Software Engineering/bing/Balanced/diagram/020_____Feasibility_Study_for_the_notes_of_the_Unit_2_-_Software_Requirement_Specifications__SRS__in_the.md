### Feasibility Study

- A feasibility study is an analysis of the viability of a project, system, or product before it is developed or implemented.
- It helps to identify the potential benefits, costs, risks, and constraints of the proposed solution and compare them with alternative options.
- A feasibility study can be conducted at different stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC), such as before initiating the project, after defining the requirements, or before finalizing the design.
- A feasibility study typically consists of the following steps:
  - Define the problem or opportunity that the project aims to address.
  - Identify the objectives and scope of the project.
  - Identify and evaluate the possible solutions or alternatives.
  - Analyze the technical, operational, economic, legal, and social aspects of each solution or alternative.
  - Select the best solution or alternative based on the analysis and criteria.
  - Prepare and present the feasibility report that summarizes the findings and recommendations of the study.
- A feasibility study can help to:
  - Determine the feasibility of the project in terms of technical, operational, economic, legal, and social factors.
  - Assess the risks and uncertainties associated with the project and plan for mitigation strategies.
  - Estimate the resources, time, and budget required for the project and justify the investment.
  - Communicate the project goals, scope, and benefits to the stakeholders and gain their approval and support.
  - Provide a basis for developing the project plan, schedule, and budget.
