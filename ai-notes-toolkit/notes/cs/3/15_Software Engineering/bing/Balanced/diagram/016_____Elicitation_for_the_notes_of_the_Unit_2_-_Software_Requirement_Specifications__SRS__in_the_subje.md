### Elicitation

- Elicitation is the process of gathering, understanding, and documenting the needs and expectations of the stakeholders for a software system.
- Elicitation is also known as requirements engineering, requirements analysis, or requirements discovery.
- Elicitation is a crucial step in software development, as it helps to define the scope, objectives, and constraints of the system, and to avoid ambiguity, inconsistency, and incompleteness in the requirements.
- Elicitation involves various activities, such as:

  - Identifying the stakeholders: the people or groups who have an interest or influence in the system, such as customers, users, developers, managers, regulators, etc.
  - Planning the elicitation: deciding the methods, techniques, tools, and resources to be used for elicitation, such as interviews, questionnaires, workshops, observation, prototyping, etc.
  - Conducting the elicitation: applying the selected methods and techniques to collect the information from the stakeholders, such as their needs, expectations, preferences, assumptions, constraints, etc.
  - Documenting the elicitation: recording the information obtained from the elicitation in a structured and consistent way, such as using natural language, diagrams, models, etc.
  - Validating the elicitation: checking the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and clarity of the information, and resolving any conflicts, gaps, or ambiguities among the stakeholders or the requirements.

- Elicitation is an iterative and collaborative process, as it requires constant communication and feedback among the stakeholders and the elicitors, and may involve revising or refining the information as the understanding of the system evolves.
