### Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Models

- Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a workflow process that defines the core stages and activities of a software development project.
- SDLC models are frameworks that describe the operations performed at each phase of the project, from an initial feasibility study to the maintenance of the completed application.
- Different SDLC models have different approaches to the software development process, depending on the project requirements, scope, budget, timeline, and risks.
- Some of the common SDLC models are:

  - **Waterfall model**: The most traditional and widely accepted model, where the project is divided into sequential and linear phases, such as requirement analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance. Each phase must be completed and verified before moving to the next phase. This model is simple, easy to follow, and suitable for well-defined and stable projects. However, it is rigid, inflexible, and does not accommodate changes or feedback easily.
  - **Rapid Application Development (RAD) model**: A variation of the waterfall model, where the project is divided into smaller and faster iterations, and prototypes are developed and tested at each iteration. This model is more adaptive, flexible, and responsive to changes and feedback. However, it requires more user involvement, skilled developers, and may compromise on quality and scalability.
  - **Spiral model**: A risk-based approach to software development, where the project is divided into four quadrants: planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation. The project passes through these quadrants in a spiral manner, with each loop representing an iteration. This model is more comprehensive, systematic, and effective in managing risks and complexities. However, it is costly, time-consuming, and may require more documentation and coordination.
  - **V-model**: A variation of the waterfall model, where the testing phase is parallel to the development phase, and each development activity has a corresponding testing activity. This model is more rigorous, reliable, and ensures high-quality software. However, it is also rigid, inflexible, and does not accommodate changes or feedback easily.
  - **Incremental model**: A model where the project is divided into smaller and independent modules, and each module is developed and delivered incrementally. This model is more flexible, efficient, and customer-oriented, as it allows early delivery of working software and feedback incorporation. However, it may require more planning, testing, and integration, and may not have a clear vision of the final product.
  - **Agile model**: A model where the project is divided into short and iterative cycles, called sprints, and each sprint delivers a potentially shippable product increment. This model is more adaptive, collaborative, and customer-focused, as it involves frequent communication, feedback, and changes. However, it may require more user involvement, skilled developers, and may lack documentation and structure.
  - **Iterative model**: A model where the project is divided into smaller and repeated cycles, and each cycle delivers a partial but improved version of the software. This model is more evolutionary, experimental, and responsive to changes and feedback. However, it may require more resources, testing, and management, and may not have a clear vision of the final product.
  - **Big bang model**: A model where the project is developed with minimal or no planning, and the software is built based on the developer's intuition and creativity. This model is more exploratory, spontaneous, and suitable for small and simple projects. However, it is also risky, unpredictable, and may not meet the customer's expectations or requirements.
