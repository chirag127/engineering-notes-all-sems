### Software Quality Assurance (SQA) for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Software Quality Assurance (SQA) is the process of ensuring that the software products or services meet the quality standards and expectations of the customers and stakeholders.
- SQA involves planning, implementing, monitoring, and improving the activities and processes that affect the quality of the software throughout its life cycle.
- SQA aims to prevent defects, errors, and failures in the software, and to improve its functionality, performance, reliability, usability, security, and maintainability.
- SQA covers all aspects of software development, including requirements analysis, design, coding, testing, deployment, maintenance, and evolution.
- SQA requires the involvement and collaboration of all the roles and stakeholders in the software project, such as developers, testers, managers, customers, users, and regulators.

- Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) is a document that describes the features, functions, and constraints of a software system or product that the customer or user wants or needs.
- SRS defines the scope, objectives, and expectations of the software project, and serves as a contract between the customer and the developer.
- SRS should include both functional and non-functional requirements, as well as any assumptions, dependencies, risks, and quality attributes that affect the software.
- SRS should be clear, complete, consistent, concise, verifiable, traceable, modifiable, and prioritized.
- SRS should be written in a structured, standardized, and unambiguous language, using diagrams, tables, and models to illustrate the requirements.
- SRS should be reviewed, validated, and approved by the customer and the developer, and updated as the project progresses and the requirements change.

- The relationship between SQA and SRS is that SQA relies on SRS to ensure that the software meets the requirements and satisfies the customer or user.
- SQA uses SRS as a basis for planning, designing, implementing, testing, and evaluating the software quality activities and processes.
- SQA uses SRS as a reference for verifying and validating that the software conforms to the requirements and fulfills the expectations of the customer or user.
- SQA uses SRS as a source for identifying and resolving any discrepancies, ambiguities, or conflicts in the requirements and the software.
- SQA uses SRS as a measure for assessing and improving the quality of the software and the software development process.
