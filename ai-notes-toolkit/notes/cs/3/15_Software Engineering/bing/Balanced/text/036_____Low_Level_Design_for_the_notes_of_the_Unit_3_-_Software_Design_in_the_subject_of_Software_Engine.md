### Low Level Design

Low level design (LLD) is a component-level design process that follows a step-by-step refinement process. It is a detailed description of every module of software, including data structures, algorithms, interfaces, and inputs/outputs . It involves specifying the individual modules, data structures, algorithms, interfaces, and inputs/outputs of a system.

Some of the steps involved in low level design are:

- Applying object-oriented principles to the user requirements and processing them using concepts of OOPS programming.
- Analyzing the real-world problems and identifying the classes, objects, attributes, methods, and relationships among them.
- Applying design patterns to solve common design problems and improve the quality, reusability, and maintainability of the code.
- Choosing appropriate data structures and algorithms to optimize the performance, memory, and scalability of the system.
- Defining the interfaces and contracts between the modules and ensuring the consistency and compatibility of the data types, formats, and protocols.
- Documenting the low level design using diagrams, such as class diagrams, sequence diagrams, state diagrams, etc. to illustrate the structure and behavior of the system .

Low level design is an important phase in the software development life cycle, as it bridges the gap between the high level design and the implementation. It helps the developers to understand the logic and functionality of each module and write the code accordingly. It also helps the testers to verify the correctness and completeness of the system and identify any defects or errors. Low level design also facilitates the maintenance and modification of the system, as it provides a clear and modular view of the system.
