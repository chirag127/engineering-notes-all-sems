### SQA Plans for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- A software quality assurance (SQA) plan is a document that describes the standards, processes, procedures, and tools that will be used to ensure the quality of a software product or service.
- A SQA plan is usually prepared by the SQA team or the project manager, and it is reviewed and approved by the stakeholders, such as the customer, the developer, and the tester.
- A SQA plan should include the following elements:
  - The scope and objectives of the SQA activities, such as the quality goals, the quality criteria, the quality metrics, and the quality risks.
  - The roles and responsibilities of the SQA team and other participants, such as the SQA manager, the SQA engineer, the SQA auditor, and the SQA reviewer.
  - The SQA tasks and deliverables, such as the SQA plan itself, the SQA audit reports, the SQA review reports, and the SQA defect reports.
  - The SQA schedule and resources, such as the SQA milestones, the SQA budget, and the SQA tools and techniques.
  - The SQA standards and procedures, such as the SQA policies, the SQA guidelines, the SQA checklists, and the SQA templates.
  - The SQA verification and validation methods, such as the SQA audits, the SQA reviews, the SQA inspections, and the SQA testing.
  - The SQA reporting and communication mechanisms, such as the SQA meetings, the SQA reports, the SQA feedback, and the SQA escalation.
  - The SQA evaluation and improvement measures, such as the SQA measurement, the SQA analysis, the SQA improvement, and the SQA lessons learned.
- A SQA plan should be aligned with the software development life cycle (SDLC) phases, such as the planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance phases.
- A SQA plan should be updated and maintained throughout the software project, and it should be audited and reviewed periodically to ensure its effectiveness and compliance.
