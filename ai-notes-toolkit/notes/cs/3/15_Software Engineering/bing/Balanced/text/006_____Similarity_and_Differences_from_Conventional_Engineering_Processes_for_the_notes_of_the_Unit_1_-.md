### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes for Software Engineering

- Conventional engineering processes are the methods and practices used to design, build, and maintain physical systems or products, such as bridges, buildings, machines, or vehicles.
- Software engineering processes are the methods and practices used to design, build, and maintain software systems or products, such as applications, websites, or databases.
- Similarities:
  - Both are getting automated slowly. Both require in-depth knowledge of their field.
  - Both involve problem-solving, creativity, and teamwork skills.
  - Both follow a systematic and disciplined approach to ensure quality and reliability of the outcomes.
  - Both use models, diagrams, and specifications to represent and communicate the design and functionality of the system or product.
  - Both apply engineering principles, such as abstraction, modularity, decomposition, and testing, to manage complexity and reduce errors.
- Differences:
  - Software engineering is only related to programming, but conventional engineering is related to thinking, designing, and building tasks.
  - Software engineering deals with intangible and abstract entities, whereas conventional engineering deals with tangible and concrete entities.
  - Software engineering is more dynamic and adaptive to changing requirements and technologies, whereas conventional engineering is more stable and predictable.
  - Software engineering has a higher degree of reuse and customization of existing components, whereas conventional engineering has a higher degree of originality and innovation of new components.
  - Software engineering has more opportunities of foreign settlement and a typical office job, whereas conventional engineering has higher government sector opportunity and a more physically active role.
