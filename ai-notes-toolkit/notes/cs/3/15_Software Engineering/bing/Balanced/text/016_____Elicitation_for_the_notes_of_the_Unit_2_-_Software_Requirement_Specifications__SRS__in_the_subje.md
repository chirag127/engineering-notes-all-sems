### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Elicitation is the process of gathering, understanding, and documenting the software requirements from various stakeholders, such as customers, users, domain experts, etc.
- Elicitation is also known as requirements engineering, requirements analysis, or requirements discovery.
- Elicitation is a crucial activity in software engineering, as it helps to define the scope, objectives, and constraints of the software project, and to avoid ambiguity, inconsistency, incompleteness, and errors in the requirements.
- Elicitation involves various techniques, such as interviews, questionnaires, surveys, observation, prototyping, brainstorming, use cases, scenarios, etc.
- Elicitation also requires effective communication, negotiation, and validation skills, as well as the use of appropriate tools and models, such as UML diagrams, data flow diagrams, entity-relationship diagrams, etc.
- Elicitation results in the creation of a Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document, which is a formal and detailed description of the software requirements, including functional, non-functional, and quality attributes, as well as assumptions, dependencies, and risks.
- Elicitation is an iterative and incremental process, which means that the requirements are refined and revised throughout the software development life cycle, based on feedback, changes, and new information.
