### Test Drivers and Test Stubs

- Test drivers and test stubs are two types of test harnesses, which are collections of software and test data that are configured together in order to test a unit of a program by simulating various conditions while constantly monitoring its outputs and behavior.
- Test drivers and test stubs are used in integration testing, which is a type of testing that aims to verify the functionality, performance, and reliability of different modules or components of a software system when they are combined together.
- Test drivers and test stubs are mainly used in two different approaches of integration testing: top-down and bottom-up.
- Top-down integration testing is a method of testing where the higher-level modules or components are tested first, and then the lower-level modules or components are gradually integrated and tested. In this approach, test stubs are used to simulate the behavior of the lower-level modules or components that are not yet available or developed  .
- Bottom-up integration testing is a method of testing where the lower-level modules or components are tested first, and then the higher-level modules or components are gradually integrated and tested. In this approach, test drivers are used to simulate the behavior of the higher-level modules or components that are not yet available or developed  .
- Test drivers and test stubs are both computer programs that act as a replacement for some other modules or components that are not accessible for testing. They have the ability to serve the features and functionalities that a module or component can provide, and to accept the inputs and generate the outputs that are expected from the original module or component  .
- Test drivers and test stubs are both temporary and simplified substitutes for the actual modules or components. They are not meant to replicate the exact behavior or logic of the original module or component, but only to provide enough functionality to enable the testing process  .
- Test drivers and test stubs are both useful for reducing the delay and dependency in testing, and for increasing the efficiency and coverage of testing. They also help to isolate the errors and faults in the modules or components that are being tested, and to verify the interfaces and interactions between them  .
