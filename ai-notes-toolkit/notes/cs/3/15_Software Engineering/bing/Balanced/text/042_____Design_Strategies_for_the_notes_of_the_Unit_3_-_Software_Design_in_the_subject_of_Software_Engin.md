### Design Strategies for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

- Design strategies are the approaches that are taken to design a software system that meets the requirements and specifications.
- Design strategies involve making decisions about the architecture, components, modules, interfaces, and data for a software system.
- There are three major system design strategies in software engineering: structured design, function-oriented design, and object-oriented design .
  - Structured design is a design strategy that focuses on the decomposition of the system into modules that have well-defined inputs and outputs. Structured design aims to reduce the complexity and coupling of the system and increase the cohesion and modularity of the modules.
  - Function-oriented design is a design strategy that focuses on the identification and grouping of the functions that the system performs. Function-oriented design aims to capture the functional behavior and data flow of the system and organize the functions into a hierarchy of abstraction levels.
  - Object-oriented design is a design strategy that focuses on the identification and grouping of the objects that the system manipulates. Object-oriented design aims to capture the state and behavior of the objects and organize them into classes and hierarchies of inheritance and composition.
- There are two common design strategies in software engineering that can be applied to any of the above system design strategies: top-down design and bottom-up design .
  - Top-down design is a design strategy that starts with a high-level view of the system and gradually breaks it down into smaller, more manageable components. Top-down design aims to define the overall structure and functionality of the system and refine the details of the components later.
  - Bottom-up design is a design strategy that starts with the low-level components of the system and gradually integrates them into larger, more complex components. Bottom-up design aims to reuse existing components and build the system from the bottom up.
- Design strategies are not mutually exclusive and can be combined or adapted to suit the needs and characteristics of the system and the project. For example, a hybrid design strategy can use both top-down and bottom-up approaches in different phases or aspects of the system design.
- Design strategies are influenced by various factors, such as the type, size, scope, complexity, quality, and constraints of the system, the availability of resources and tools, the experience and skills of the developers, and the preferences and expectations of the stakeholders.
- Design strategies are important for enabling the best possible product development, as they help to achieve the following goals :
  - Reduce the complexity and ambiguity of the system design
  - Increase the clarity and consistency of the system design
  - Enhance the modularity and reusability of the system components
  - Improve the testability and maintainability of the system
  - Facilitate the communication and collaboration among the developers and the stakeholders
  - Ensure the alignment of the system design with the system requirements and specifications
