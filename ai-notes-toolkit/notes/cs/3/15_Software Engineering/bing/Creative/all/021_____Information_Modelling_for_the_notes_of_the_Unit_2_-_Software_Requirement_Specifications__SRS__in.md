# Information Modelling

- Information modelling is a process of creating a representation of concepts and the relationships, constraints, rules, and operations to specify data semantics for a chosen domain of discourse .
- Information modelling is an important part of software engineering, as it helps to define the requirements and design of a system, and to communicate them to the stakeholders and developers.
- Information modelling can be done at different levels of abstraction, such as conceptual, logical, and physical, depending on the purpose and scope of the model.
- Information modelling can use various techniques and notations, such as entity-relationship diagrams, UML class diagrams, XML schemas, and ontologies, to represent the entities, attributes, relationships, and operations in a domain.
- Information modelling can also include the specification of constraints, rules, and operations that govern the data and its manipulation, such as integrity constraints, business rules, and queries.
- Information modelling can be used for various purposes, such as data analysis, data integration, data quality, data governance, data security, and data visualization.

## Steps of Information Modelling

- The process of information modelling can be divided into the following steps:

  - Identify the entities: The first step is to identify the things, events, or concepts that are represented in the data set that is to be modelled. Each entity should be cohesive and logically discrete from all others. Identify key properties of each entity.
  - Define the relationships: The next step is to define the relationships between the entities, such as one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many, or hierarchical. Identify the cardinality and optionality of each relationship, and the roles and responsibilities of each entity in the relationship.
  - Add the attributes: The third step is to add the attributes or properties of each entity and relationship, such as name, type, value, and description. Identify the primary key and foreign key attributes that uniquely identify each entity and relationship, and the derived attributes that can be calculated from other attributes.
  - Specify the constraints and rules: The fourth step is to specify the constraints and rules that apply to the data and its manipulation, such as integrity constraints, business rules, and queries. Constraints and rules can be expressed in natural language, formal logic, or programming languages, depending on the level of abstraction and the intended audience of the model.
  - Validate and refine the model: The final step is to validate and refine the model by checking its completeness, consistency, correctness, and clarity. Validation can be done by reviewing the model with the stakeholders and developers, testing the model with sample data, and comparing the model with existing models or standards. Refinement can be done by adding, deleting, or modifying the entities, relationships, attributes, constraints, and rules, as needed.
