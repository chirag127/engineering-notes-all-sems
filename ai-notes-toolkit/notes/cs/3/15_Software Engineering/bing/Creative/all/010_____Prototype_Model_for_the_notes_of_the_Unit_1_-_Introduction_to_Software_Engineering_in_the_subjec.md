# Prototype Model

The prototype model is a software development model in which a prototype of the software or system is built, tested, and refined as per customer feedback until an acceptable result is obtained. A prototype is a first or preliminary version of the final software that has limited functionality, reliability, and performance. The prototype model helps to elicit the user requirements and expectations more clearly and accurately, especially when the user is not sure about them beforehand.

## Characteristics of Prototype Model

- It is an iterative and incremental model that involves building a series of prototypes and improving them based on user feedback.
- It is a user-centered model that focuses on satisfying the user needs and expectations.
- It is a flexible and adaptable model that can accommodate changes and modifications easily.
- It is a risk-reducing model that helps to identify and resolve potential issues and errors early in the development process.
- It is a cost-effective model that saves time and resources by avoiding rework and wastage.

## Advantages of Prototype Model

- It improves user involvement and satisfaction by allowing them to test and evaluate the prototype and provide feedback.
- It enhances communication and collaboration between the developers and the users by facilitating a common understanding of the system requirements and design.
- It reduces ambiguity and uncertainty by providing a concrete and tangible representation of the system.
- It increases the quality and reliability of the system by detecting and eliminating defects and errors at an early stage.
- It accelerates the development process by reducing the time and effort required for analysis, design, and documentation.

## Disadvantages of Prototype Model

- It may lead to unrealistic expectations and demands from the users who may want more features and functionality than originally planned.
- It may cause confusion and inconsistency between the prototype and the final system if they are not aligned and synchronized properly.
- It may compromise the system architecture and design by focusing more on the user interface and appearance rather than the internal structure and logic.
- It may increase the complexity and maintenance of the system by adding more code and functionality that may not be necessary or optimal.
- It may create a dependency and attachment to the prototype that may hinder the acceptance and adoption of the final system.
