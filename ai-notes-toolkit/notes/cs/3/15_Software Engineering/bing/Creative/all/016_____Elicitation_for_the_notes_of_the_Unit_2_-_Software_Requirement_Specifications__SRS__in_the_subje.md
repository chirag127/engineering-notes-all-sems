# Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Elicitation is the process of gathering, understanding, and documenting the needs and expectations of the stakeholders for a software project.
- Elicitation is the first stage of the software requirement engineering process, which also includes analysis, documentation, review, and management.
- Elicitation is important because it helps to define the scope, objectives, and constraints of the project, as well as to identify the risks, assumptions, and dependencies.
- Elicitation techniques include interviews, questionnaires, workshops, observation, prototyping, brainstorming, and document analysis.
- Elicitation challenges include communication barriers, conflicting or incomplete requirements, changing requirements, and stakeholder availability.
- Elicitation outcomes include a list of requirements, a prioritization of requirements, a traceability matrix, and a requirement specification document .
- A requirement specification document (SRS) is a formal document that describes the functional and non-functional requirements, design, and standards for a software project.
- A SRS document helps to communicate the expectations and needs of the stakeholders to the developers, testers, and managers, as well as to provide a basis for verification, validation, and maintenance.
- A SRS document should be clear, concise, consistent, complete, correct, verifiable, modifiable, and traceable.
- A SRS document should include the following sections: introduction, overall description, specific requirements, appendices, and index.
