# Low Level Design for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Low level design (LLD) is a component-level design process that follows a step-by-step refinement process. This process can be used for designing data structures, required software architecture, source code and ultimately, performance algorithms.

The main objectives of low level design are:

- To define the detailed, functional design of a software system or component.
- To specify the individual modules, data structures, algorithms, interfaces, and inputs/outputs of a system.
- To provide a micro-level design that delves deep into every specification of every system.
- To ensure the consistency, completeness, correctness, and quality of the software design.

The main steps of low level design are:

- Analyzing the user requirements and the high level design of the system.
- Applying the object-oriented principles to model the system using classes, objects, methods, attributes, inheritance, polymorphism, etc.
- Choosing the appropriate design patterns to solve the common design problems and improve the reusability, maintainability, and extensibility of the system.
- Documenting the low level design using diagrams, such as class diagrams, sequence diagrams, state diagrams, etc.
- Reviewing and testing the low level design to ensure its validity and feasibility.

Some of the benefits of low level design are:

- It helps to break down the complex system into smaller and manageable components.
- It helps to reduce the ambiguity and uncertainty in the software design.
- It helps to facilitate the communication and collaboration among the developers, testers, and stakeholders.
- It helps to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of the software development process.
- It helps to ensure the alignment of the software design with the user requirements and the high level design.

Some of the challenges of low level design are:

- It requires a high level of technical knowledge and expertise.
- It requires a lot of time and effort to create and document the low level design.
- It requires a constant update and revision to cope with the changing user requirements and system specifications.
- It requires a careful trade-off between the performance, complexity, and maintainability of the system.
- It requires a close coordination and integration with the high level design and the implementation of the system.
