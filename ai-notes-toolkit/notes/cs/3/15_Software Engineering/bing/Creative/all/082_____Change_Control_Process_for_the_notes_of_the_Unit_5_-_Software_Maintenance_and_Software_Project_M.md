# Change Control Process

Change control is the process that a company uses to document, identify and authorize changes to an IT environment. It reduces the chances of unauthorized alterations, disruption and errors in the system. Change control is a major aspect of change management, which is a broader section of change control that deals with the human and organizational aspects of change.

The change control process in software engineering usually involves the following steps :

1. **Change request initiation**: In this step, a change is requested by a stakeholder, such as a customer, a developer, a tester, or a manager. The change request should describe the type, scope, reason, and priority of the change. The change request should also be documented and assigned a unique identifier for tracking purposes.
2. **Change request assessment**: In this step, the change request is reviewed by a change control board (CCB), which is a group of experts who have the authority to approve or reject changes. The CCB evaluates the feasibility, impact, and risk of the change, as well as the resources and time required to implement it. The CCB may also consult with other stakeholders or experts for more information or feedback.
3. **Change request analysis**: In this step, the change request is analyzed in detail by the CCB or a designated team. The analysis may involve creating a change plan, which outlines the tasks, dependencies, costs, benefits, and schedule of the change. The analysis may also involve testing the change in a simulated or isolated environment to ensure its functionality and compatibility.
4. **Change request implementation**: In this step, the change request is implemented according to the change plan. The implementation may involve modifying the software code, configuration, documentation, or other artifacts. The implementation may also involve communicating the change to the affected stakeholders and providing training or support if needed.
5. **Change request verification**: In this step, the change request is verified by the CCB or a designated team. The verification may involve checking the quality, performance, and functionality of the change, as well as its compliance with the requirements and standards. The verification may also involve updating the change log, which is a record of all the changes made to the system.
