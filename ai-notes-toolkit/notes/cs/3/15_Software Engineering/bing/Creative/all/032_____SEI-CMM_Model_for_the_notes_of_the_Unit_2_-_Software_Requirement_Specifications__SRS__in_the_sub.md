# SEI-CMM Model

- SEI stands for Software Engineering Institute, a research and development center at Carnegie Mellon University that provides guidance and best practices for software engineering.
- CMM stands for Capability Maturity Model, a framework that describes the key elements of an effective software process and the levels of maturity of an organization's software development capability.
- The SEI-CMM Model was developed in 1986 based on a study of data collected from organizations that contracted with the U.S. Department of Defense.
- The SEI-CMM Model defines five levels of maturity for software development organizations, from level 1 (Initial) to level 5 (Optimizing).
- The SEI-CMM Model helps organizations to assess their current software process, identify areas for improvement, and implement best practices to achieve higher levels of maturity and quality.
- The SEI-CMM Model is not a specific software process model, but a general framework that can be applied to any software process model.
- The SEI-CMM Model is also not a prescriptive standard, but a descriptive model that provides guidelines and goals for software process improvement.

## The Five Levels of SEI-CMM Model

- Level 1: Initial
  - The software process is ad hoc, chaotic, and unpredictable.
  - The organization has no stable software process and relies on the skills and efforts of individual developers.
  - The organization has no clear understanding of the requirements, costs, schedules, and quality of the software products.
  - The organization faces frequent crises, delays, defects, and customer dissatisfaction.
- Level 2: Repeatable
  - The software process is disciplined, managed, and consistent.
  - The organization has established basic project management practices and controls, such as project planning, tracking, configuration management, and quality assurance.
  - The organization can repeat the successful practices from previous projects and ensure that the requirements, costs, schedules, and quality of the software products are met.
  - The organization can handle multiple projects and cope with changing customer demands.
- Level 3: Defined
  - The software process is standardized, documented, and integrated.
  - The organization has defined a common software process for the entire organization, based on a set of software engineering standards and guidelines.
  - The organization has implemented software engineering methods and tools to support the software process, such as requirements analysis, design, testing, and maintenance.
  - The organization can tailor the software process to suit the specific needs and characteristics of each project and customer.
  - The organization can measure and evaluate the performance and quality of the software process and products.
- Level 4: Managed
  - The software process is quantitatively controlled, measured, and improved.
  - The organization has established quantitative goals and metrics for the software process and products, based on the needs and expectations of the customers and stakeholders.
  - The organization has collected and analyzed data on the software process and products, and used statistical techniques to identify and eliminate the sources of variation and defects.
  - The organization can predict and manage the risks, costs, schedules, and quality of the software products with high accuracy and confidence.
- Level 5: Optimizing
  - The software process is continuously optimized, innovated, and aligned with the business goals.
  - The organization has a culture of continuous learning and improvement, and seeks to identify and implement best practices and new technologies to enhance the software process and products.
  - The organization has a proactive approach to change management, and can adapt and respond to the changing needs and opportunities of the market and customers.
  - The organization can achieve and sustain the highest levels of customer satisfaction and business performance.
