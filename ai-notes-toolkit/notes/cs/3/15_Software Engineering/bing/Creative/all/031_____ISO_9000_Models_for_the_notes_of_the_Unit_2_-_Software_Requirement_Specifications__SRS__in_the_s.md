# ISO 9000 Models

- ISO 9000 is a family of quality management system (QMS) standards that help organizations ensure they meet customer and other stakeholder needs within statutory and regulatory requirements related to a product or service.
- ISO 9000 deals with the fundamentals of QMS, including the seven quality management principles that underlie the family of standards.
- ISO 9001 deals with the requirements that organizations wishing to meet the standard must fulfill.
- ISO 9000:1987 had the same structure as the UK Standard BS 5750, with three "models" for quality management systems, the selection of which was based on the scope of activities of the organization:
  - ISO 9001:1987 Model for quality assurance in design, development, production, installation, and servicing was for companies and organizations whose activities included the creation of new products.
  - ISO 9002:1987 Model for quality assurance in production, installation, and servicing had basically the same material as ISO 9001 but without covering the creation of new products.
  - ISO 9003:1987 Model for quality assurance in final inspection and test covered only the final inspection of finished product, with no concern for how the product was produced.
- ISO 9000:1994 emphasized quality assurance via preventive actions, instead of just checking final product, and continued to require evidence of compliance with documented procedures.
- ISO 9000:2000 combined the three standards—ISO 9001, ISO 9002, and ISO 9003—into ISO 9001:2000, which had a simpler structure and a stronger focus on the role of top management, the process approach, and continual improvement.
- ISO 9001:2008 introduced clarifications to the existing requirements of ISO 9001:2000 and some changes intended to improve consistency with ISO 14001:2004.
- ISO 9001:2015 is the current version of the standard, which provides a set of requirements that can be used to develop a QMS that is integrated with the context and strategic direction of the organization. It also introduces the concept of risk-based thinking and the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle.
- ISO 9004:2018 provides guidance to achieve sustained success for any organization in a complex, demanding, and ever-changing environment, by enhancing the ability of the organization to satisfy its customers and other interested parties.
- ISO 9000 is not a product standard, but a process standard. It does not define the quality of the product or service, but the quality of the processes that produce it.
- ISO 9000 does not guarantee the quality of the product or service, but it helps to achieve it by providing a framework for managing and improving the QMS.
- ISO 9000 does not require a specific way of doing things, but it allows for flexibility and adaptation to the specific needs and context of each organization.
- ISO 9000 does not ensure customer satisfaction, but it helps to measure and improve it by establishing and monitoring quality objectives and indicators.
- ISO 9000 does not guarantee the success of the organization, but it helps to achieve it by fostering a culture of quality and continual improvement.
