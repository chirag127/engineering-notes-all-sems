# Cost of Maintenance

- Software maintenance is the process of modifying and updating software after delivery to correct faults, improve performance, or adapt to changing environments.
- Software maintenance is an important and costly activity in the software development life cycle. According to some studies, the cost of maintenance can range from 40% to 90% of the total project cost.
- The cost of maintenance depends on various factors, such as:

  - The quality of the software product: A software product that is well-designed, well-documented, and well-tested will require less maintenance effort than a software product that is poorly designed, poorly documented, and poorly tested.
  - The complexity of the software product: A software product that is complex, large, or has many dependencies will require more maintenance effort than a software product that is simple, small, or has few dependencies.
  - The type of maintenance: There are four types of maintenance, namely corrective, adaptive, perfective, and preventive. Corrective maintenance is the process of fixing errors or bugs in the software product. Adaptive maintenance is the process of modifying the software product to cope with changes in the environment, such as new hardware, new operating systems, or new standards. Perfective maintenance is the process of enhancing the software product to improve its functionality, performance, usability, or reliability. Preventive maintenance is the process of modifying the software product to prevent potential problems or errors in the future. The cost of maintenance varies according to the type of maintenance, with corrective maintenance being the most expensive and preventive maintenance being the least expensive.
  - The availability of resources: The cost of maintenance also depends on the availability of resources, such as skilled personnel, tools, documentation, and data. The more resources are available, the less maintenance effort is required.

- The cost of maintenance can be estimated using various models, such as:

  - The COCOMO model: The COCOMO (Constructive Cost Model) model is a widely used model for estimating the cost of software development and maintenance. The COCOMO model considers three modes of software development, namely organic, semi-detached, and embedded. The COCOMO model estimates the maintenance effort (in person-months) as a function of the software size (in thousands of source lines of code), the mode of software development, and the annual change traffic (the percentage of source lines of code that are added, deleted, or modified during a year). The COCOMO model also provides adjustment factors for various attributes of the software product, such as reliability, complexity, documentation, and reuse.
  - The Boehm model: The Boehm model is another widely used model for estimating the cost of software maintenance. The Boehm model considers three types of maintenance, namely corrective, adaptive, and perfective. The Boehm model estimates the maintenance effort (in person-years) as a function of the software size (in thousands of delivered source instructions), the type of maintenance, and the maintenance adjustment factor (a value between 0.75 and 1.25 that reflects the characteristics of the software product and the maintenance environment).
  - The SLIM model: The SLIM (Software Life Cycle Management) model is a model for estimating the cost of software development and maintenance based on the software life cycle. The SLIM model considers four phases of the software life cycle, namely inception, development, maintenance, and retirement. The SLIM model estimates the maintenance effort (in person-months) as a function of the software size (in function points), the software life cycle phase, and the productivity index (a value that reflects the efficiency of the software development and maintenance process).

- The cost of maintenance can be reduced by applying various techniques, such as:

  - Software quality assurance: Software quality assurance is the process of ensuring that the software product meets the specified requirements and standards. Software quality assurance involves activities such as planning, reviews, inspections, testing, audits, and metrics. Software quality assurance can reduce the cost of maintenance by preventing or detecting errors or defects in the software product before delivery.
  - Software configuration management: Software configuration management is the process of controlling and managing the changes and versions of the software product and its components. Software configuration management involves activities such as identification, baseline, change control, status accounting, and audit. Software configuration management can reduce the cost of maintenance by facilitating the tracking, tracing, and updating of the software product and its components.
