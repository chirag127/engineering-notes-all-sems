# Requirement Engineering Process

The requirement engineering process is a critical step in the software development life cycle as it helps to ensure that the software system being developed meets the needs and expectations of stakeholders, and that it is developed on time, within budget, and to the required quality.

The requirement engineering process is an iterative process that involves several steps, including :

- **Requirements inception or elicitation** – Developers and stakeholders meet; the latter are inquired about the objectives, expectations, constraints, and risks of the software system. Various techniques can be used to elicit requirements, such as interviews, questionnaires, workshops, observation, prototyping, etc.
- **Requirements analysis and negotiation** – Requirements are identified (including new ones if the development is incremental), classified, prioritized, and validated. Conflicts, inconsistencies, ambiguities, and gaps are resolved through negotiation and trade-offs among stakeholders.
- **System modeling** – Some engineering fields (or specific projects) require a graphical representation of the system and its behavior, structure, and properties. This can be done using various modeling languages and tools, such as UML, SysML, etc.
- **Requirements specification** – Requirements are documented in a formal or informal way, depending on the project context and standards. A common format for requirements specification is the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document, which contains functional and non-functional requirements, as well as quality attributes, design constraints, and assumptions.
- **Requirements validation** – Requirements are checked for completeness, correctness, consistency, clarity, feasibility, testability, and traceability. Various techniques can be used to validate requirements, such as reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, simulations, testing, etc.
- **Requirements management** – Requirements are monitored and controlled throughout the project life cycle. Changes to requirements are analyzed for their impact, communicated to stakeholders, and implemented accordingly. Requirements traceability is maintained to link requirements to their sources, dependencies, and artifacts.

The main characteristics of good requirements are:

- **Consistent** – No two requirements should conflict with each other.
- **Complete** – Requirements should cover all the aspects of the system and its environment.
- **Achievable** – Requirements should be realistic and feasible to implement with the available resources and technology.
- **Unambiguous** – Requirements should be clear and precise, and avoid vague or subjective terms.
- **Verifiable** – Requirements should be testable and measurable, and have clear acceptance criteria.
- **Modifiable** – Requirements should be flexible and adaptable to changing needs and circumstances.
- **Traceable** – Requirements should be linked to their sources, dependencies, and artifacts, and have a unique identifier.
