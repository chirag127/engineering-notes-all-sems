### Change Control Process

Change control is the process that a company uses to document, identify and authorize changes to an IT environment. It reduces the chances of unauthorized alterations, disruption and errors in the system.

The change control process consists of the following steps :

1. **Change request initiation**: In this step, a change is requested by a stakeholder, a user, a developer, or a manager. The change request should include the reason, the scope, the impact, and the priority of the change. The change request is then submitted to a change control board (CCB) or a change manager for review and assessment.
2. **Change request assessment**: In this step, the CCB or the change manager evaluates the change request based on the criteria such as feasibility, cost, benefit, risk, and alignment with the project objectives and the business needs. The CCB or the change manager can approve, reject, defer, or request more information about the change request.
3. **Change request analysis**: In this step, the CCB or the change manager performs a detailed analysis of the change request, including the technical, operational, and organizational aspects. The analysis should identify the resources, the tasks, the dependencies, the schedule, the budget, and the quality implications of the change. The analysis should also consider the impact of the change on the stakeholders, the users, the system, and the environment. The analysis should result in a change proposal that outlines the plan and the actions for implementing the change.
4. **Change request implementation**: In this step, the change proposal is executed according to the plan and the actions defined in the analysis. The change is implemented by the developers, the testers, the operators, or the users, depending on the nature and the scope of the change. The change is monitored and controlled by the CCB or the change manager to ensure that the change is completed on time, within budget, and with the expected quality. The change is also documented and communicated to the relevant parties.
5. **Change request review**: In this step, the change is reviewed and evaluated by the CCB or the change manager to verify that the change has met the requirements and the objectives of the change request. The change is also reviewed and evaluated by the stakeholders, the users, the system, and the environment to measure the effectiveness and the satisfaction of the change. The change is then approved or rejected based on the review and evaluation results. The change is also closed and archived in the change log or the change history.
