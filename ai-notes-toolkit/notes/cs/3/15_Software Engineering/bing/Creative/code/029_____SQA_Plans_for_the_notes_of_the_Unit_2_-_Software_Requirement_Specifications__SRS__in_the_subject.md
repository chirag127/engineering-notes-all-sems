# SQA Plans for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Software quality assurance (SQA) is a method of ensuring that all software engineering processes, methods, activities and work items are monitored and conform to established standards .
- Standards may include ISO 9000, CMMI model, ISO15504, or a combination of these standards along with others .
- A software quality assurance plan (SQAP) is a document that describes the objectives, scope, approach, roles, responsibilities, tasks, resources, and deliverables of the SQA activities for a software project .
- The purpose of an SQAP is to provide a systematic and consistent way of ensuring the quality of the software product or service, and to prevent, detect, and correct defects in the software development life cycle (SDLC) .
- The SQAP should be aligned with the software requirement specifications (SRS), which define the functional and non-functional requirements of the software product or service .
- The SQAP should cover the following aspects of SQA :
  - SQA objectives: the expected outcomes and benefits of the SQA activities, such as meeting customer expectations, complying with standards, reducing rework, improving productivity, etc.
  - SQA scope: the boundaries and limitations of the SQA activities, such as the software components, processes, methods, tools, techniques, metrics, etc. that are subject to SQA.
  - SQA approach: the strategy and methodology of the SQA activities, such as the SQA models, frameworks, standards, best practices, guidelines, etc. that are adopted and followed.
  - SQA roles and responsibilities: the assignment and accountability of the SQA tasks and activities to the relevant stakeholders, such as the SQA team, project manager, developers, testers, customers, etc.
  - SQA tasks and activities: the specific actions and steps that are performed to achieve the SQA objectives, such as planning, reviewing, testing, auditing, reporting, etc.
  - SQA resources: the human, material, and financial resources that are required and allocated for the SQA activities, such as the SQA staff, equipment, software, budget, etc.
  - SQA deliverables: the tangible and intangible outputs and outcomes of the SQA activities, such as the SQA plan, SQA reports, SQA metrics, SQA feedback, etc.
  - SQA schedule: the timeline and milestones of the SQA activities, such as the start and end dates, durations, dependencies, etc. of the SQA tasks and activities.
  - SQA risks and issues: the potential and actual problems and challenges that may affect the SQA activities, such as the SQA risks, issues, assumptions, constraints, dependencies, etc. and their mitigation and resolution strategies.
  - SQA quality control: the methods and measures that are used to monitor and evaluate the effectiveness and efficiency of the SQA activities, such as the SQA audits, reviews, inspections, tests, metrics, etc.
  - SQA quality improvement: the processes and practices that are used to identify and implement the opportunities and actions for enhancing the quality of the software product or service, such as the SQA feedback, lessons learned, best practices, etc.
