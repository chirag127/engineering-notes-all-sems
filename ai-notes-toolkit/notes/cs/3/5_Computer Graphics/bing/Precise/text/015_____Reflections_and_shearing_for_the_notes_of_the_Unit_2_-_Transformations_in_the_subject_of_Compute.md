### Reflections and Shearing

Reflections and shearing are two types of transformations in computer graphics. These transformations are used to manipulate the position, orientation, and shape of objects in a two-dimensional or three-dimensional space.

#### Reflections

Reflection is a transformation that produces a mirror image of an object. This transformation is achieved by flipping the object over an imaginary line called the axis of reflection. The axis of reflection can be horizontal, vertical, or diagonal.

In two-dimensional space, the reflection of a point (x, y) over the x-axis is given by the point (x, -y). Similarly, the reflection of a point over the y-axis is given by the point (-x, y).

In three-dimensional space, the reflection of a point (x, y, z) over the xy-plane is given by the point (x, y, -z). Similarly, reflections over the xz-plane and yz-plane are given by the points (-x, y, z) and (x, -y, z), respectively.

#### Shearing

Shearing is a transformation that distorts the shape of an object by sliding its points along a fixed direction. This transformation is achieved by shifting the points of the object along one coordinate axis while keeping the other coordinates fixed.

In two-dimensional space, the shearing of a point (x, y) along the x-axis is given by the point (x + ky, y), where k is the shearing factor. Similarly, the shearing of a point along the y-axis is given by the point (x, y + kx).

In three-dimensional space, shearing can be performed along any of the three coordinate axes. For example, the shearing of a point (x, y, z) along the x-axis is given by the point (x + ky + lz, y, z), where k and l are the shearing factors along the y and z axes, respectively.

These are some of the basic concepts of reflections and shearing in computer graphics. These transformations can be used to create a wide range of visual effects and can be combined with other transformations such as scaling and rotation to achieve more complex results.
