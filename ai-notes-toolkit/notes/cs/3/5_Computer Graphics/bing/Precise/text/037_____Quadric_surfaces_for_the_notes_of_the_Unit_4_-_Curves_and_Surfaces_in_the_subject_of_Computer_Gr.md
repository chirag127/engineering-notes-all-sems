### Quadric Surfaces

Quadric surfaces are a type of surface that can be defined by a second-degree polynomial equation in three variables. They are commonly used in computer graphics to represent smooth, curved surfaces.

Some common types of quadric surfaces include:

1. Ellipsoid: An ellipsoid is a surface that can be obtained by rotating an ellipse around one of its principal axes. It is defined by the equation (x^2/a^2) + (y^2/b^2) + (z^2/c^2) = 1, where a, b, and c are the lengths of the semi-major axes of the ellipse.

2. Hyperboloid of one sheet: A hyperboloid of one sheet is a surface that can be obtained by rotating a hyperbola around one of its principal axes. It is defined by the equation (x^2/a^2) + (y^2/b^2) - (z^2/c^2) = 1, where a, b, and c are the lengths of the semi-major axes of the hyperbola.

3. Hyperboloid of two sheets: A hyperboloid of two sheets is a surface that can be obtained by rotating a hyperbola around one of its principal axes. It is defined by the equation (x^2/a^2) - (y^2/b^2) - (z^2/c^2) = 1, where a, b, and c are the lengths of the semi-major axes of the hyperbola.

4. Elliptic paraboloid: An elliptic paraboloid is a surface that can be obtained by rotating a parabola around its axis of symmetry. It is defined by the equation z = (x^2/a^2) + (y^2/b^2), where a and b are the lengths of the semi-major axes of the parabola.

5. Hyperbolic paraboloid: A hyperbolic paraboloid is a surface that can be obtained by rotating a parabola around its axis of symmetry. It is defined by the equation z = (x^2/a^2) - (y^2/b^2), where a and b are the lengths of the semi-major axes of the parabola.

These are some of the common types of quadric surfaces that are used in computer graphics to represent smooth, curved surfaces. They can be easily manipulated and transformed to create complex shapes and models.
