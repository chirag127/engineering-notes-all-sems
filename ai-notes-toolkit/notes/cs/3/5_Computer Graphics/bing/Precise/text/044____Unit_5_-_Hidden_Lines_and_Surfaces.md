## Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces

1. Hidden lines and surfaces are used in technical drawings to represent the edges and surfaces of an object that are not visible from the current viewpoint.
2. Hidden lines are typically represented by dashed or dotted lines, while hidden surfaces are usually left blank or shaded.
3. The use of hidden lines and surfaces can help to clarify the overall shape and structure of an object, and can also be used to indicate the presence of internal features or components.
4. In some cases, hidden lines and surfaces may be omitted from a drawing for the sake of simplicity or clarity, particularly if the object is relatively simple or if the hidden features are not considered important.
5. There are several techniques for determining which lines and surfaces should be represented as hidden, including the use of projection and section views.
6. The specific conventions for representing hidden lines and surfaces can vary depending on the standards and practices of the particular field or industry.
