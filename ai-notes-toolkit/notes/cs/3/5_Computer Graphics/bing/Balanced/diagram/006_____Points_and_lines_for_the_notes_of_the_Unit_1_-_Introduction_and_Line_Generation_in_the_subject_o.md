### Points and lines for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction and Line Generation in the subject of Computer Graphics

- A point is the simplest graphical element that can be displayed on a screen. It is represented by a pair of coordinates (x, y) that specify its position on a two-dimensional plane.
- A line is a sequence of points that are connected by straight or curved segments. It is represented by two endpoints (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) that specify the starting and ending points of the line, or by a slope-intercept equation y = mx + b that specifies the direction and position of the line.
- There are different algorithms for generating lines on a raster display, such as the Digital Differential Analyzer (DDA) algorithm, the Bresenham's algorithm, and the midpoint algorithm. These algorithms use different techniques to approximate the ideal line with discrete pixels, and have different trade-offs in terms of accuracy, speed, and complexity.
- The DDA algorithm uses simple arithmetic operations to incrementally calculate the x and y coordinates of each pixel along the line. It rounds the coordinates to the nearest integer values and plots them on the screen. The DDA algorithm is simple and easy to implement, but it is slow and prone to rounding errors.
- The Bresenham's algorithm uses integer arithmetic and decision variables to determine the next pixel along the line. It compares the accumulated error with a threshold value and decides whether to increment the x or y coordinate, or both. The Bresenham's algorithm is faster and more accurate than the DDA algorithm, but it is more complex and requires more memory.
- The midpoint algorithm uses a similar approach as the Bresenham's algorithm, but it uses the midpoint of the two possible pixels as the decision variable. It compares the midpoint with the ideal line and decides which pixel to plot. The midpoint algorithm is faster and more accurate than the DDA algorithm, and simpler and more efficient than the Bresenham's algorithm. It is the preferred algorithm for line generation.
