### Raster scan displays

- Raster scan displays are the most common type of graphics monitor that use a cathode ray tube (CRT) to display images on the screen  .
- A raster scan display works by scanning an electron beam across the screen, from top to bottom, one row at a time  .
- The electron beam is modulated by turning its intensity on and off as it moves across each row, creating a pattern of illuminated spots called pixels  .
- The pixels are arranged in a rectangular grid, called the raster, that covers the entire screen  .
- The resolution of a raster scan display is determined by the number of pixels in the raster, and the color depth is determined by the number of bits used to represent each pixel .
- A raster scan display refreshes the screen periodically, by repeating the scanning process at a fixed rate, called the refresh rate  .
- A raster scan display can display graphics by storing the pixel values in a memory buffer, called the frame buffer, and reading them out during the scanning process .
- A raster scan display can also display text by using a predefined set of pixel patterns, called fonts, to represent each character .
