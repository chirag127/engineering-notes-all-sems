### 3-D viewing

- 3-D viewing is the process of displaying and interacting with 3-D computer graphics on a 2-D or 3-D display device, such as a monitor, a projector, or a virtual reality headset.
- 3-D viewing involves two main steps: 3-D modeling and 3-D projection.
- 3-D modeling is the creation of 3-D objects and scenes using 3-D modeling software, such as Blender, Maya, or SketchUp. 3-D modeling software allows the user to define the shape, color, texture, and lighting of 3-D objects and scenes using various tools and techniques, such as drawing points, lines, triangles, and other polygonal patches.
- 3-D projection is the transformation of 3-D objects and scenes into 2-D or 3-D images that can be displayed on a screen or a surface. 3-D projection involves two sub-steps: modeling transformation and viewing transformation.
- Modeling transformation is the manipulation of 3-D objects and scenes in the 3-D world coordinate system, such as translation, rotation, scaling, and shearing. Modeling transformation allows the user to change the position, orientation, size, and shape of 3-D objects and scenes according to their needs and preferences.
- Viewing transformation is the specification of the observer's position, orientation, and field of view in the 3-D world coordinate system, as well as the position and shape of the projection plane or surface. Viewing transformation defines the viewing-coordinate system, which is used as a reference for projecting 3-D objects and scenes onto the projection plane or surface.
- There are two main types of 3-D projection: parallel projection and perspective projection. Parallel projection preserves the relative sizes and shapes of 3-D objects and scenes, but does not account for the effects of distance and depth. Perspective projection simulates the effects of distance and depth, but distorts the relative sizes and shapes of 3-D objects and scenes. Parallel projection is often used for technical drawings and diagrams, while perspective projection is often used for realistic and artistic renderings.
- 3-D viewing requires a 3-D viewer, which is a software or hardware device that can display and interact with 3-D computer graphics. Some examples of 3-D viewers are 3D Viewer, a 3-D computer graphics viewer and augmented reality application that is included in Windows 10, and CyberFox, a software that can process three-dimensional scanned data and reverse engineering. 3-D viewers can also use artificial intelligence techniques to create detailed and realistic 3-D scenes from 2-D images.
