### Basic transformation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Transformations in the subject of Computer Graphics

- Transformations are operations that change the position, size, orientation, or shape of an object on the screen.
- There are three basic rigid transformations that preserve the distance and angle between the points of the object: translation, rotation, and reflection.
- There is a fourth common transformation that changes the size of the object: scaling.
- There is also a fifth common transformation that changes the shape of the object: shearing.
- Transformations can be represented by matrices that operate on the coordinates of the object.
- The following diagram shows the basic transformations and their matrices for a 2D object:

![Basic transformations and their matrices](https://i.imgur.com/5w0QZQf.png)

- The matrices can be multiplied together to form a composite transformation that applies multiple transformations in a single step.
- The order of the matrix multiplication matters, as it affects the final result of the transformation.
- The following diagram shows an example of a composite transformation that translates, rotates, and scales an object:

![Composite transformation example](https://i.imgur.com/8n7cU6O.png)

- Transformations can be applied to 3D objects as well, by using 3x3 or 4x4 matrices that operate on the x, y, and z coordinates of the object.
- The following diagram shows the basic transformations and their matrices for a 3D object:

![Basic transformations and their matrices for 3D](https://i.imgur.com/4fZj4YJ.png)

- The following diagram shows an example of a composite transformation that translates, rotates, and scales a 3D object:

![Composite transformation example for 3D](https://i.imgur.com/4oQxZ5N.png)

- Transformations are essential for computer graphics, as they allow the manipulation of objects on the screen and the creation of animations and effects.
