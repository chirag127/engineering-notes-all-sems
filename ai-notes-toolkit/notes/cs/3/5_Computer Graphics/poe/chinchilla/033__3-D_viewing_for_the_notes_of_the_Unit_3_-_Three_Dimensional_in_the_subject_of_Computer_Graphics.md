### 3-D Viewing

In computer graphics, 3-D viewing refers to the process of transforming a 3-D scene into a 2-D image that can be displayed on a computer screen. This process involves several steps, which are discussed below:

1. Projection: The first step in 3-D viewing is projection, which involves mapping points in the 3-D scene onto a 2-D plane. There are several projection techniques, including perspective and orthographic projection.

2. View Transformation: Once the 3-D scene has been projected onto a 2-D plane, it must be transformed into the desired viewpoint. This typically involves applying a series of rotations and translations to the scene.

3. Clipping: After the view transformation, the scene may contain objects that are outside the view frustum. These objects must be clipped to ensure that only the visible portions of the scene are displayed.

4. Rasterization: Once the scene has been clipped, it is rasterized into pixels that can be displayed on a computer screen. This involves determining the color of each pixel based on the objects in the scene and the lighting conditions.

5. Depth Sorting: Finally, the rasterized pixels must be sorted based on their depth in the scene. This ensures that objects that are closer to the viewer are displayed on top of objects that are farther away.

In summary, 3-D viewing is a complex process that involves several steps, including projection, view transformation, clipping, rasterization, and depth sorting. By understanding these steps, computer graphics professionals can create realistic 3-D scenes that can be displayed on a computer screen.
