### Scan line method for the notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

The scan line method is a technique used in computer graphics to render images of three-dimensional objects. It is particularly useful in identifying hidden lines and surfaces in a scene. Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying the scan line method:

- The scan line method works by dividing the viewing area into a number of horizontal lines, called scan lines.
- Starting from the topmost scan line, the method determines which parts of the scene intersect with the scan line.
- The method then determines which parts of the intersecting objects are visible from the camera position, and renders those parts of the image.
- The process is repeated for each scan line, working from top to bottom, until the entire image has been rendered.
- The scan line method is particularly useful in identifying hidden lines and surfaces in a scene, as it processes each scan line in turn, and can therefore identify which parts of the scene are not visible from the camera position.
- The scan line method can also be used in conjunction with other techniques, such as the z-buffer algorithm, to further refine the image and improve its visual quality.
- One disadvantage of the scan line method is that it can be computationally expensive, particularly when dealing with complex scenes or large numbers of objects.
- However, advancements in computer hardware and software have made the scan line method more practical and efficient, and it remains a popular technique for rendering three-dimensional images in computer graphics.

Overall, the scan line method is an important technique for rendering images of three-dimensional objects in computer graphics. By dividing the viewing area into a number of horizontal scan lines, it can identify hidden lines and surfaces in a scene, and render high-quality images that accurately represent the three-dimensional space.
