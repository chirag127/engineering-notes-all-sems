### 3-D Geometric Primitives

In Computer Graphics, 3-D Geometric Primitives are the basic building blocks used to create complex 3-D objects. These primitives are simple shapes that can be combined and transformed to form more complex objects. Here are some of the most commonly used 3-D geometric primitives:

1. **Points:** A point is the simplest 3-D geometric primitive. It is a location in space, defined by three coordinates (x, y, z). Points are often used to represent the vertices of more complex objects.

2. **Lines:** A line is defined by two points. It is a one-dimensional geometric primitive that has length but no width or depth. Lines are often used to define the boundaries of 3-D objects.

3. **Planes:** A plane is a two-dimensional geometric primitive that extends infinitely in all directions. It is defined by a point and a normal vector that is perpendicular to the plane. Planes are often used to define the surfaces of 3-D objects.

4. **Polygons:** A polygon is a two-dimensional geometric primitive with straight sides. It is defined by a set of vertices and edges that connect them. Polygons can be used to represent flat surfaces of 3-D objects.

5. **Spheres:** A sphere is a three-dimensional geometric primitive that is defined by a center point and a radius. It is a round object with no flat surfaces. Spheres are often used to represent objects like planets, balls, and bubbles.

6. **Cylinders:** A cylinder is a three-dimensional geometric primitive that is defined by a center axis, a radius, and a height. It has a circular base and a flat top or bottom surface. Cylinders are often used to represent objects like cans, tubes, and pillars.

7. **Boxes:** A box (also known as a cube) is a three-dimensional geometric primitive with six rectangular faces. It is defined by a center point and the length, width, and height of each face. Boxes are often used to represent objects like buildings, rooms, and containers.

In conclusion, understanding the basic 3-D geometric primitives is essential for creating complex 3-D objects in Computer Graphics. By combining and transforming these primitives, we can create a wide range of objects with varying shapes and sizes.
