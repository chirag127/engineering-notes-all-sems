### Combined Approach for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the Subject of Computer Graphics

In computer graphics, hidden lines and surfaces refer to the parts of an object that are not visible to the viewer. The study of hidden lines and surfaces is essential to create realistic and accurate computer graphics. In this unit, we will learn about various techniques used to handle hidden lines and surfaces. Here are some important points to consider:

1. Hidden Line Removal:
   - Hidden line removal is a technique used to remove the lines that are not visible to the viewer.
   - There are two approaches to hidden line removal: the depth-buffer algorithm and the scan-line algorithm.
   - The depth-buffer algorithm uses a buffer to store the depth of each pixel, while the scan-line algorithm scans each line to determine which lines are visible.

2. Hidden Surface Removal:
   - Hidden surface removal is a technique used to remove the surfaces that are not visible to the viewer.
   - There are two approaches to hidden surface removal: the back-face culling algorithm and the z-buffer algorithm.
   - The back-face culling algorithm removes the surfaces that are facing away from the viewer, while the z-buffer algorithm uses a buffer to store the depth of each pixel to determine which surfaces are visible.

3. Combined Approach:
   - The combined approach uses both hidden line removal and hidden surface removal techniques to create accurate and realistic computer graphics.
   - By using both techniques, we can remove the hidden lines and surfaces, providing a clear view of the object to the viewer.
   - The combined approach is widely used in various applications such as video games, simulations, and virtual reality.

4. Advantages of Combined Approach:
   - The combined approach provides a more accurate and realistic view of the object to the viewer.
   - It reduces the load on the system by removing unnecessary lines and surfaces, resulting in faster rendering times.
   - The combined approach can handle complex scenes with multiple objects more efficiently.

In conclusion, the combined approach is an essential technique in computer graphics to handle hidden lines and surfaces. It provides a more accurate and realistic view of the object to the viewer while reducing the load on the system.
