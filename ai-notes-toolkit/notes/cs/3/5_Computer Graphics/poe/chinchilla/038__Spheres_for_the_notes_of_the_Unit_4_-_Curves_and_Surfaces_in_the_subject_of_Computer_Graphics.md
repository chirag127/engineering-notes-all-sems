### Spheres

In computer graphics, a sphere is a three-dimensional geometric shape that is defined as the set of all points that are a fixed distance away from a given point in space. Spheres are commonly used in computer graphics to represent a variety of objects, including planets, balls, and other round shapes. Here are some key points about spheres in computer graphics:

- A sphere is defined by its center point and its radius.
- The equation of a sphere can be expressed as (x - cx)^2 + (y - cy)^2 + (z - cz)^2 = r^2, where (cx, cy, cz) is the center point of the sphere and r is its radius.
- Spheres can be rendered using various techniques, including ray tracing and polygonal meshing.
- Ray tracing involves tracing the path of light rays as they interact with the surface of the sphere, while polygonal meshing involves approximating the surface of the sphere using a mesh of polygons.
- Spheres can be textured using various mapping techniques, such as UV mapping and spherical mapping.
- UV mapping involves mapping a 2D texture onto the surface of the sphere using a 2D coordinate system, while spherical mapping involves mapping a 2D texture onto the surface of the sphere using a 3D coordinate system.
- Spheres can be transformed using various transformation techniques, such as translation, rotation, and scaling.
- Translation involves moving the center point of the sphere to a new location, while rotation involves rotating the sphere around its center point, and scaling involves changing the size of the sphere.
- Spheres can be combined with other geometric shapes and objects to create more complex scenes and models.

Overall, spheres are a fundamental geometric shape in computer graphics that are used extensively in many different applications. Understanding the properties and characteristics of spheres is important for anyone interested in computer graphics and related fields.
