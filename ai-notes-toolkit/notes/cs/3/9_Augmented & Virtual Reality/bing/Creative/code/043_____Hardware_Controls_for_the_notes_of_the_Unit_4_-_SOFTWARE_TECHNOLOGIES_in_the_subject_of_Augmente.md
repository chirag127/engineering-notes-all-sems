### Hardware Controls for Augmented and Virtual Reality

Hardware controls are the devices and components that enable users to interact with augmented and virtual reality applications. They can be classified into three main categories: input, output, and tracking.

- Input devices are the ones that allow users to provide commands, gestures, or actions to the system. They can be physical, such as keyboards, mice, gamepads, or controllers, or virtual, such as voice, eye gaze, or brain-computer interfaces. Some examples of input devices for augmented and virtual reality are:

  - The Leap Motion Controller, which is a motion-sensing device that allows users to see and manipulate their hands in virtual and augmented reality. It can be plugged into the USB ports of any Mac or PC, or attached to a head-mounted display (HMD).
  - The Oculus Touch, which are wireless controllers that provide haptic feedback, gesture recognition, and finger tracking for the Oculus Rift HMD. They enable users to interact with virtual objects and environments in a natural and intuitive way.
  - The Google Glass, which is a wearable device that projects a small screen in front of the user's eye, allowing them to access information and applications in an augmented reality mode. It can be controlled by voice, touch, or head movements.

- Output devices are the ones that display or render the virtual or augmented reality content to the users. They can be visual, such as screens, projectors, or HMDs, or auditory, such as speakers, headphones, or earbuds. Some examples of output devices for augmented and virtual reality are:

  - The HTC Vive, which is a HMD that provides a high-resolution and immersive virtual reality experience. It has a 110-degree field of view, a refresh rate of 90 Hz, and a resolution of 2160 x 1200 pixels. It also comes with two base stations that track the user's position and orientation in a 15 x 15 feet area.
  - The Microsoft HoloLens, which is a HMD that creates a mixed reality experience, blending virtual and real elements in the user's field of view. It has a 34-degree field of view, a refresh rate of 60 Hz, and a resolution of 1268 x 720 pixels. It also has an array of sensors, cameras, and microphones that enable spatial mapping, gesture recognition, and voice control.
  - The Bose AR, which are sunglasses that provide an augmented reality experience through sound. They have built-in speakers, microphones, and sensors that deliver audio content and information based on the user's location, orientation, and movements. They can be paired with a smartphone app that offers various AR applications.

- Tracking devices are the ones that measure and record the user's position, orientation, and movements in the physical or virtual space. They can be based on different technologies, such as optical, magnetic, inertial, or ultrasonic. Some examples of tracking devices for augmented and virtual reality are:

  - The Lighthouse, which is a system that uses two base stations that emit infrared light to track the user's HMD and controllers in a 3D space. It has a sub-millimeter accuracy and a low latency, and it can support multiple users in the same area.
  - The Kinect, which is a device that uses a depth camera and a microphone array to capture the user's body movements and voice commands. It can be used for gesture recognition, facial recognition, and skeletal tracking in virtual and augmented reality applications.
  - The Vicon, which is a system that uses multiple cameras and markers to track the user's body and objects in a 3D space. It has a high accuracy and a high frame rate, and it can support large-scale and complex environments.
