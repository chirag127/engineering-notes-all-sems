# Augmented reality methods

Augmented reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the perception of reality by superimposing digital content on the real-world environment. AR can be used for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, training, navigation, gaming, and marketing. AR can also create immersive and interactive experiences that engage the users and provide them with useful information.

There are different methods of implementing AR, depending on the type of device, the type of content, and the type of interaction. Some of the common methods are:

- **Marker-based AR**: This method uses a special visual object, such as a QR code, a logo, or a symbol, as a marker to trigger the AR content. The device's camera scans the marker and recognizes its position and orientation. Then, the AR software overlays the digital content on top of the marker, creating a virtual scene. Marker-based AR can be used for various applications, such as product packaging, advertising, education, and gaming. For example, a marker-based AR app can show a 3D model of a furniture item when the user scans its catalog.

- **Marker-less AR**: This method does not require any predefined markers to initiate the AR content. Instead, it uses the device's sensors, such as GPS, accelerometer, gyroscope, and compass, to determine the location and orientation of the device. Then, the AR software overlays the digital content on the real-world environment, based on the device's position and movement. Marker-less AR can be used for various applications, such as navigation, tourism, and gaming. For example, a marker-less AR app can show directions, landmarks, and points of interest when the user points the device at a certain location.

- **Projection-based AR**: This method uses a projector to display the digital content on a physical surface, such as a wall, a floor, or an object. The projector can be either external or embedded in the device. The projection-based AR content can be either static or dynamic, depending on the interaction. Projection-based AR can be used for various applications, such as art, education, and gaming. For example, a projection-based AR app can show a virtual keyboard on a table when the user places the device on it.

- **Superimposition-based AR**: This method replaces the original view of an object with a new view, either partially or fully. The device's camera captures the object and recognizes its shape and features. Then, the AR software overlays the new view on top of the object, creating a modified scene. Superimposition-based AR can be used for various applications, such as medical, industrial, and entertainment. For example, a superimposition-based AR app can show how a person would look with different hairstyles, glasses, or makeup.

- **Head-mounted display (HMD)**: This is a device that is worn on the head, such as a helmet, a pair of glasses, or a headset, that displays the digital content in front of the user's eyes. The HMD can be either monocular or binocular, depending on the number of displays. The HMD can also be either video-see-through or optical-see-through, depending on the type of display. Video-see-through HMDs use a camera to capture the real-world environment and display it on a screen, along with the digital content. Optical-see-through HMDs use a transparent display that allows the user to see the real-world environment directly, along with the digital content. HMDs can provide a more immersive and realistic AR experience, as they can cover the user's entire field of view and track the user's head movement. HMDs can be used for various applications, such as gaming, training, and education. For example, a HMD can show a virtual environment that simulates a real-world scenario, such as a battlefield, a classroom, or a museum.
