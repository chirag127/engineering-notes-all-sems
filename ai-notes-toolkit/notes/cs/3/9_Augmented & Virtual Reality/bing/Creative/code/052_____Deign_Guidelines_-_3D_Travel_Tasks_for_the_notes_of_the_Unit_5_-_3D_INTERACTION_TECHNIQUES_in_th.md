### Design Guidelines - 3D Travel Tasks

- 3D travel tasks are tasks that require the user to move through a 3D virtual environment, such as exploring, navigating, or searching for a target.
- 3D travel tasks can be classified into four types based on the distance and precision of the movement: directed, extended, search, and maneuver.
- Directed tasks are short-distance and high-precision tasks, such as reaching for an object or selecting a menu item.
- Extended tasks are long-distance and low-precision tasks, such as flying over a terrain or walking through a building.
- Search tasks are tasks that involve finding a target in an unknown or partially known environment, such as looking for a landmark or a hidden object.
- Maneuver tasks are tasks that require complex and coordinated movements, such as avoiding obstacles, steering a vehicle, or docking a spacecraft.
- The design of 3D travel tasks should consider the following factors: the user's goals, the characteristics of the virtual environment, the available input and output devices, and the potential challenges and risks.
- Some general design guidelines for 3D travel tasks are:

  - Provide clear and consistent feedback to the user about their position, orientation, speed, and direction of movement.
  - Support multiple levels of control and granularity for the user to adjust their movement according to the task requirements and preferences.
  - Use natural and intuitive mappings and metaphors for the input and output devices, such as steering wheels, joysticks, or hand gestures.
  - Avoid unnecessary or excessive motion, rotation, or acceleration that may cause disorientation, nausea, or fatigue for the user.
  - Provide cues and aids to help the user navigate and orient themselves in the virtual environment, such as landmarks, maps, compasses, or trails.
  - Consider the trade-offs between realism and usability, such as the level of detail, the physics model, and the collision detection.
  - Evaluate the performance and satisfaction of the user with different 3D travel techniques and methods, such as teleportation, flying, or walking.
