# Training for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality (VR) is the use of computer modeling and simulation that enables a person to interact with an artificial three-dimensional (3-D) visual or other sensory environment.
- VR applications immerse the user in a computer-generated environment that simulates reality through the use of interactive devices, such as goggles, headsets, gloves, or body suits.
- VR applications can be classified into two main categories: immersive and non-immersive.
  - Immersive VR applications provide a high degree of realism and user presence, such as in a head-mounted display (HMD) or a CAVE system.
  - Non-immersive VR applications use a conventional monitor or projector to display a 3-D environment, such as in a desktop VR system or a flight simulator.
- VR applications can be used for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, training, health care, engineering, and military.
  - Entertainment: VR applications can create immersive and engaging experiences for gaming, movies, sports, and social media.
  - Education: VR applications can enhance learning and teaching by providing interactive and realistic simulations of various subjects, such as history, geography, science, and art.
  - Training: VR applications can provide safe and cost-effective environments for skill development and practice, such as in medical procedures, driving, flying, and firefighting.
  - Health care: VR applications can assist in diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation of various medical conditions, such as phobias, chronic pain, post-traumatic stress disorder, and stroke.
  - Engineering: VR applications can facilitate design, prototyping, testing, and visualization of complex systems, such as buildings, vehicles, and robots.
  - Military: VR applications can provide realistic and challenging scenarios for combat training, mission rehearsal, and situational awareness.
- VR applications require various components and technologies, such as hardware, software, and human factors.
  - Hardware: VR applications need devices that can generate and display 3-D graphics, capture and track user movements and inputs, and provide feedback to the user's senses, such as vision, hearing, touch, and smell.
  - Software: VR applications need software that can create and manipulate 3-D models, render and animate 3-D scenes, handle user interactions and events, and synchronize and communicate with other VR systems.
  - Human factors: VR applications need to consider the psychological and physiological aspects of the user, such as perception, cognition, emotion, and comfort, to ensure a positive and effective VR experience.
