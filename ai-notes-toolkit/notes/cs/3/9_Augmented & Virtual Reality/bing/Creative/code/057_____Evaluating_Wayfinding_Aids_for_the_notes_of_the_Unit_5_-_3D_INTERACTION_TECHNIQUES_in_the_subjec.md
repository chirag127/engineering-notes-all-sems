### Evaluating Wayfinding Aids for 3D Interaction Techniques

- Wayfinding aids (WATs) are tools or techniques that help users to navigate and orient themselves in 3D virtual environments (VEs).
- WATs can be classified into four categories: maps, compasses, landmarks and signs.
- Maps provide an overview of the VE from a top-down perspective, showing the user's location and orientation, as well as the layout and features of the environment.
- Compasses indicate the direction of the user's movement or the direction of a target or goal, using arrows, pointers or icons.
- Landmarks are distinctive objects or features in the VE that serve as reference points for the user, such as buildings, statues or natural elements.
- Signs are textual or graphical labels that provide information or guidance to the user, such as names, distances or directions.
- Evaluating WATs involves measuring their effectiveness, efficiency and user satisfaction in supporting navigation tasks in VEs.
- Effectiveness refers to the accuracy and completeness of the user's performance, such as reaching the destination, following the correct path or avoiding errors.
- Efficiency refers to the speed and effort of the user's performance, such as the time spent, the distance traveled or the cognitive load required.
- User satisfaction refers to the subjective feelings and preferences of the user, such as enjoyment, comfort or confidence.
- Different methods and metrics can be used to evaluate WATs, such as questionnaires, interviews, observations, logs, eye tracking, physiological measures or performance measures.
- Some factors that can influence the evaluation of WATs are the characteristics of the VE, such as the size, complexity, realism or level of immersion, the characteristics of the user, such as the experience, spatial ability or preference, and the characteristics of the task, such as the difficulty, duration or goal.
- Some examples of studies that evaluated WATs in VEs are:

  - A study that compared a view-in-view map, an animation guide and a human-system collaboration technique in terms of effectiveness, efficiency and user satisfaction.
  - A study that evaluated four WATs (maps, compasses, landmarks and signs) in multi-level VEs, and found that compasses required less cognitive effort and that experience with games affected the user's performance positively.
  - A study that compared different types of maps (static, dynamic, egocentric and exocentric) and found that dynamic maps were more effective and efficient than static maps, and that egocentric maps were more efficient than exocentric maps.
  - A study that evaluated different types of landmarks (global, local, semantic and geometric) and found that global and semantic landmarks were more effective and efficient than local and geometric landmarks.

- Evaluating WATs is important for designing and developing 3D interaction techniques that can enhance the user's navigation experience and performance in VEs.
