### Mobile Projection Interfaces for Augmented Reality

- Mobile projection interfaces are a type of augmented reality interfaces that use a mobile device with a built-in or attached projector to display digital content on physical surfaces.
- Mobile projection interfaces can overcome some of the limitations of handheld display devices, such as small screen size, limited field of view, and occlusion by the user's hand.
- Mobile projection interfaces can also enable new forms of interaction and collaboration, such as projecting on objects, walls, floors, or other people, and using gestures, touch, or voice to control the projection.
- Mobile projection interfaces can be classified into different application classes, depending on how they adapt to the object, context, or user input. Some examples are:

  - Object-adaptive applications: These applications use the mobile projector to augment physical objects with digital information or functionality. For example, a projector phone can project a keyboard on a table, a menu on a plate, or a game on a toy .
  - Context-adaptive applications: These applications use the mobile projector to adapt to the environmental context, such as the location, orientation, or lighting conditions. For example, a projector phone can project a map on the ground, a compass on the wall, or a flashlight on the dark .
  - Camera-controlled applications: These applications use the mobile camera to capture the user's input or feedback, and use the mobile projector to display the output. For example, a projector phone can use the camera to recognize gestures, faces, or barcodes, and project the corresponding information or actions .

- Mobile projection interfaces face some challenges and limitations, such as:

  - Projector brightness and resolution: The quality of the projection depends on the brightness and resolution of the projector, which are often limited by the size and power consumption of the mobile device.
  - Projector alignment and calibration: The alignment and calibration of the projector with the physical surface or object can be affected by the movement of the user or the environment, and may require manual or automatic adjustment.
  - Projector occlusion and interference: The projection can be occluded or interfered by the user's hand, body, or other objects, and may cause unwanted shadows or reflections.
  - User interface design and evaluation: The design and evaluation of mobile projection interfaces require considering the physical and social aspects of the projection, such as the size, shape, texture, and ownership of the surface, and the privacy, security, and etiquette of the projection  .
