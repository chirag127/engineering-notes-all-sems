## Unit 4 - SOFTWARE TECHNOLOGIES

- Software technologies are the tools and methods used to create, maintain, and improve software systems.
- Software technologies can be classified into three main categories: programming languages, software development methodologies, and software engineering tools.
- Programming languages are the formal languages used to specify the instructions and data for a software system. They can be divided into low-level and high-level languages, depending on the level of abstraction and closeness to the hardware.
- Low-level languages are more difficult to write and understand, but they offer more control and efficiency over the hardware. Examples of low-level languages are assembly and machine code.
- High-level languages are easier to write and understand, but they require more processing and memory resources. Examples of high-level languages are Python, Java, and C#.
- Software development methodologies are the frameworks and processes used to organize, plan, and manage software projects. They can be divided into traditional and agile methodologies, depending on the degree of flexibility and adaptability to changing requirements.
- Traditional methodologies are more rigid and structured, but they offer more stability and predictability. Examples of traditional methodologies are waterfall and spiral models.
- Agile methodologies are more flexible and iterative, but they require more collaboration and communication. Examples of agile methodologies are scrum and extreme programming (XP).
- Software engineering tools are the applications and software components that support the software development activities. They can be divided into general-purpose and specific-purpose tools, depending on the scope and functionality they provide.
- General-purpose tools are more versatile and applicable to different domains and tasks. Examples of general-purpose tools are text editors, compilers, and debuggers.
- Specific-purpose tools are more specialized and tailored to a particular domain or task. Examples of specific-purpose tools are integrated development environments (IDEs), testing tools, and version control systems.
