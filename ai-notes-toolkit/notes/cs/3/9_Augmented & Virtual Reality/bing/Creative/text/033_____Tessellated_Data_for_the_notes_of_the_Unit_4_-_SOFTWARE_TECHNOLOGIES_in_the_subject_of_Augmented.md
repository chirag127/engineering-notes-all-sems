### Tessellated Data

- Tessellation is the process of dividing a dataset of polygons (or vertex sets) into smaller and simpler shapes for rendering.
- Tessellation can improve the visual quality and performance of graphics applications, such as augmented and virtual reality, by creating smoother and more detailed surfaces.
- Tessellation can also be used to create spatial grids or bins for analyzing and visualizing point-based data, such as population density, crime rates, or environmental variables .
- Tessellation can be done in different ways, such as using triangles, squares, diamonds, hexagons, or other regular or irregular shapes.
- Tessellation can be done in different stages of the graphics pipeline, such as at the vertex level, the geometry level, or the pixel level.
- Tessellation can be done using different algorithms, such as subdivision, displacement mapping, or adaptive tessellation.
- Tessellation can be influenced by different factors, such as the input geometry, the viewing angle, the distance, the lighting, or the user-defined parameters.
- Tessellation can have different advantages and disadvantages, such as increasing the realism, the complexity, the memory usage, or the computational cost of the graphics application.
