### Gestrual Commands for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Gestrual commands are a type of 3D interaction technique that use hand gestures to manipulate virtual objects or control the system.
- Gestrual commands can be classified into two categories: discrete and continuous.
- Discrete gestrual commands are those that have a clear start and end point, and are usually associated with a specific action or command. For example, pointing at an object to select it, or making a fist to grab it.
- Continuous gestrual commands are those that have no clear start and end point, and are usually associated with a continuous manipulation or control. For example, moving the hand to translate or rotate an object, or pinching the fingers to scale it.
- Gestrual commands can be designed using different criteria, such as:
  - The naturalness of the gesture, which refers to how intuitive and easy it is for the user to perform the gesture and understand its meaning.
  - The expressiveness of the gesture, which refers to how many different actions or commands can be performed with the gesture, and how well they can be distinguished by the system.
  - The efficiency of the gesture, which refers to how fast and accurate the gesture can be performed, and how much fatigue or discomfort it causes to the user.
  - The compatibility of the gesture, which refers to how well the gesture matches the context and the task, and how consistent it is with other gestures or interaction techniques.
- Gestrual commands can be implemented using different technologies, such as:
  - Optical tracking, which uses cameras or sensors to capture the position and orientation of the hand or the fingers, and recognize the gesture based on the shape or the motion of the hand.
  - Inertial tracking, which uses accelerometers or gyroscopes to measure the acceleration and rotation of the hand or the wrist, and recognize the gesture based on the direction or the speed of the hand.
  - Haptic feedback, which uses actuators or devices to provide tactile or force feedback to the hand or the fingers, and enhance the realism or the usability of the gesture.
