### Design Guidelines - 3D Travel Tasks

- 3D travel tasks are the tasks that involve moving the user's viewpoint or body in a 3D virtual environment .
- 3D travel tasks can be classified into four types based on the user's goal and the environment's structure :
  - Goal-oriented travel: the user has a specific destination in mind and wants to reach it as quickly and accurately as possible.
  - Exploration and search: the user does not have a specific destination, but wants to explore the environment and find interesting or relevant objects or locations.
  - Physical locomotion: the user wants to move in a natural and realistic way, using their own body or a device that mimics physical movement.
  - Exertion and naturalism: the user wants to experience the physical and emotional effects of moving in a 3D environment, such as fatigue, excitement, or immersion.
- The design of 3D travel techniques should consider the following guidelines :
  - Provide multiple travel techniques to support different travel tasks in the same application.
  - Make simple travel tasks easier by using target-based techniques for goal-oriented travel and steering techniques for exploration and search.
  - Use a physical locomotion technique if user exertion or naturalism is required.
  - Design high-speed transitional motions, not instant teleportation, if overall environment context is important.
  - Minimize the number of degrees of freedom required for the movement and the accuracy of the movement.
  - Avoid or reduce the negative effects of 3D travel, such as motion sickness, disorientation, or fatigue.
  - Consider the trade-offs between user performance, preference, and comfort when choosing a travel technique.
