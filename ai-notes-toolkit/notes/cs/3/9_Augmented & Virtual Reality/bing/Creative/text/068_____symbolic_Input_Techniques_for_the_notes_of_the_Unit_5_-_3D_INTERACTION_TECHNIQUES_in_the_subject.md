### Symbolic Input Techniques

Symbolic input is the task in which users communicate symbolic information (text, numbers, and other symbols or marks) to the system . Symbolic input is often used for tasks such as entering commands, passwords, annotations, labels, queries, or messages. Symbolic input can be challenging in 3D interaction, especially in immersive virtual environments (VEs), because of the lack of conventional input devices such as keyboards or mice, and the need to accommodate different modalities and representations of symbols.

Some of the common symbolic input techniques for 3D interaction are:

- **Keyboard-based techniques**: These techniques use physical or virtual keyboards to enter symbols. Physical keyboards can be attached to the user's body or placed in a fixed location in the real or virtual world. Virtual keyboards can be displayed on a 2D or 3D surface, such as a screen, a tablet, a wall, or a curved panel, and can be interacted with using a pointer, a touch, or a gesture. Keyboard-based techniques are familiar and efficient for text entry, but they may require additional devices or visual feedback, and they may not be suitable for immersive or mobile scenarios.

- **Pen-based techniques**: These techniques use a stylus or a finger to write or draw symbols on a 2D or 3D surface, such as a tablet, a paper, a glove, or a hand. Pen-based techniques can leverage natural handwriting or sketching skills, and can support a variety of symbols and languages. However, they may also require additional devices or visual feedback, and they may suffer from recognition errors or ambiguity.

- **Gesture-based techniques**: These techniques use hand or body movements to perform gestures that represent symbols. Gestures can be performed in free space or on a surface, and can be recognized by vision, motion, or touch sensors. Gesture-based techniques can be intuitive and expressive, and can support spatial and temporal symbols. However, they may also require a large gesture vocabulary, a high level of user skill and memory, and a robust recognition system.

- **Speech-based techniques**: These techniques use voice input to speak symbols or commands. Speech input can be captured by a microphone or a headset, and can be recognized by natural language processing or speech recognition systems. Speech-based techniques can be natural and convenient, and can support a large vocabulary and complex syntax. However, they may also require a quiet environment, a high quality of audio input and output, and a reliable recognition system.
