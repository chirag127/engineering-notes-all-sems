### Interaction Techniques for 3D Manipulation

- Interaction techniques for 3D manipulation are methods that allow users to control and modify 3D objects in virtual or augmented environments.
- 3D manipulation techniques can be classified into three main categories: device-based, mid-air gestures, and volume-based.
- Device-based techniques use the physical attributes of a handheld device, such as rotation, tilt, skew, or movement, to manipulate 3D objects. Examples of device-based techniques are:
  - Virtual Hand: The device acts as a virtual hand that can grab, move, rotate, or scale 3D objects by direct contact.
  - Virtual Pointer: The device acts as a pointer that can select and manipulate 3D objects by ray casting or fishing reel.
  - Virtual Plane: The device acts as a plane that can cut through 3D objects and manipulate them by translation or rotation.
- Mid-air gestures techniques use the tracking of bare hands or finger gestures to manipulate 3D objects. Examples of mid-air gestures techniques are:
  - Pinch: The user can pinch two fingers to select and manipulate 3D objects by translation, rotation, or scaling.
  - Swipe: The user can swipe the hand or fingers to move or rotate 3D objects along a predefined axis or plane.
  - Grab: The user can grab 3D objects with the whole hand and manipulate them by translation, rotation, or scaling.
- Volume-based techniques use the intersection of a volume defined by the device or the user's hand with 3D objects to manipulate them. Examples of volume-based techniques are:
  - Flashlight: The device acts as a flashlight that can select and manipulate 3D objects by shining a beam of light on them.
  - Aperture: The device acts as an aperture that can select and manipulate 3D objects by enclosing them within a circular or rectangular area.
  - Hand Volume: The user's hand acts as a volume that can select and manipulate 3D objects by intersecting them with the hand shape.
