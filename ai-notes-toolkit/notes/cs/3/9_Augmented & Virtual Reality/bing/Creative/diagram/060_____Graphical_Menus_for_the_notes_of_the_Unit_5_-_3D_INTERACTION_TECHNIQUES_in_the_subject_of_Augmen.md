### Graphical Menus

- Graphical menus are an integral and essential component of user interfaces and have been widely used for 2D desktop applications .
- Graphical menus for 3D UIs are the 3D equivalent of the 2D menus that have proven to be a successful system control technique in desktop UIs .
- Graphical menus in 3D UIs can be subdivided into three categories: adapted 2D menus, 1-DOF menus, and 3D widgets .
- Adapted 2D menus are the most common type of graphical menus in 3D UIs. They are basically 2D menus that are embedded in a 3D environment, either as a fixed or a movable panel .
- 1-DOF menus are graphical menus that use one degree of freedom (DOF) for navigation, such as a linear or a circular menu. They can be either attached to the user's hand or body, or placed in the environment .
- 3D widgets are graphical menus that take advantage of the extra DOF available in a 3D environment to enable more complex menu structures or better visual affordances for menu entries. They can be either 3D objects that represent menu options, or 3D structures that organize menu options .
- Some of the design issues and challenges for designing and implementing graphical menus as system control techniques in 3D UIs are: positioning of graphical user interface elements in space, choosing an appropriate selection technique, representation of the menu and its overall structure, and providing feedback and feedforward  .
