### Gestural Commands

- Gestural commands are a type of 3D interaction technique that use the hand or body movements to control the system or manipulate the virtual environment  .
- Gestural commands can be classified as either postures or gestures :
  - Posture: a static configuration of the hand, such as pointing, pinching, or making a fist.
  - Gesture: a movement of the hand, perhaps while it is in a certain posture, such as waving, swiping, or drawing a shape in the air.
- Gestural commands can be either direct or indirect :
  - Direct: the hand movement is mapped to the corresponding movement of the virtual object or cursor, such as grabbing and dragging an object.
  - Indirect: the hand movement is interpreted as a symbolic command that triggers a specific action or function, such as making a circle to select multiple objects.
- Gestural commands can be either visible or invisible :
  - Visible: the hand movement is performed on or near a visible object or widget, such as tapping a button or rotating a knob.
  - Invisible: the hand movement is performed in the empty space, without any visual feedback or guidance, such as making a pinch gesture to zoom in or out.
- Gestural commands have significant appeal for 3D UIs because of their naturalness, intuitiveness, and expressiveness  .
- Gestural commands also have some challenges and limitations for 3D UIs, such as recognition accuracy, user fatigue, lack of standards, and social acceptability  .
