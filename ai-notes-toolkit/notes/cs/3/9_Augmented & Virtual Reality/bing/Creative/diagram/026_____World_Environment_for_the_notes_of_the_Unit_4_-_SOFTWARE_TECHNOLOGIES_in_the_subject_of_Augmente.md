### World Environment

The world environment in augmented and virtual reality refers to the physical and digital spaces that are used to create immersive and interactive experiences for users. The world environment can be classified into three types:

- **Augmented reality (AR)**: AR uses the existing real-world environment and puts virtual information or objects on top of it to enhance the experience. For example, think of Pokémon Go, where users are searching in their real-life neighborhoods for animated characters that pop up on their phone or tablet.
- **Virtual reality (VR)**: VR involves putting on a headset to enter a fully immersive, 3D digital environment, where users can interact with the virtual world using controllers, gestures, or voice. For example, think of Oculus Quest, where users can play games, watch movies, or socialize with others in VR.
- **Mixed reality (MR)**: MR combines aspects of both AR and VR, where virtual objects are integrated into the real-world environment and can interact with physical objects. For example, think of Microsoft HoloLens, where users can see holograms of people, animals, or buildings in their surroundings.

Some of the benefits of using AR, VR, and MR technologies are:

- They can provide new ways of learning, working, and entertainment, by creating immersive and engaging experiences that are not possible in the real world.
- They can enhance the accessibility and inclusivity of information and services, by allowing users to access them from anywhere and in any language.
- They can support the sustainable development goals, by enabling users to experience the impacts of climate change, poverty, or health issues, and to take action to address them.

Some of the challenges of using AR, VR, and MR technologies are:

- They require high-quality hardware, software, and network connectivity, which can be expensive and inaccessible for many users, especially in developing countries.
- They pose ethical, legal, and social issues, such as privacy, security, ownership, and regulation of the digital content and data that are generated and shared by users.
- They can have negative effects on the physical and mental health of users, such as motion sickness, eye strain, addiction, or isolation.
