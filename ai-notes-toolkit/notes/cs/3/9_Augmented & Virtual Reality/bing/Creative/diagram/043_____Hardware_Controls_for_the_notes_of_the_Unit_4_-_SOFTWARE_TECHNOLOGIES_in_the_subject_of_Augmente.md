### Hardware Controls for Augmented and Virtual Reality

Hardware controls are the devices and components that enable users to interact with augmented and virtual reality applications. They can be classified into three main categories:

- **Input devices**: These are the devices that allow users to provide commands, gestures, movements, or voice to the AR or VR system. Examples of input devices are controllers, keyboards, mice, touchscreens, cameras, microphones, sensors, trackers, and haptic devices.
- **Output devices**: These are the devices that display the AR or VR content to the users, such as images, sounds, or tactile feedback. Examples of output devices are head-mounted displays (HMDs), smart glasses, projectors, speakers, and haptic devices.
- **Processing devices**: These are the devices that process the AR or VR content, such as rendering, tracking, recognition, or simulation. Examples of processing devices are computers, smartphones, consoles, or dedicated hardware.

Some hardware controls can serve multiple functions, such as HMDs that can also track the user's head orientation, or controllers that can also provide haptic feedback. Some hardware controls are specific to either AR or VR, such as smart glasses that only work for AR, or HMDs that only work for VR. Some hardware controls are compatible with both AR and VR, such as cameras, microphones, or sensors.

Some examples of hardware controls for AR and VR are:

- **Leap Motion Controller**: A motion-sensing device that allows users to see and interact with their hands in VR and AR. It can be plugged into the USB ports of any Mac or PC, or attached to an HMD.
- **Oculus Rift**: A VR HMD that provides a 110-degree field of view and a resolution of 1080 x 1200 pixels per eye. It is wired to the PC and requires a VR-ready PC to run. The user can interact with the VR world with the included Xbox gamepad, mouse, keyboard, a one-button clicker, or proprietary wireless controllers.
- **Microsoft HoloLens**: An AR smart glass that projects holograms onto the user's real environment. It is a standalone device that does not require a PC or a smartphone to run. It has a 34-degree field of view and a resolution of 1268 x 720 pixels per eye. The user can interact with the AR content with gestures, voice, or a clicker.
- **Google Glass**: An AR smart glass that displays information and notifications on a small screen in the corner of the user's eye. It is connected to the user's smartphone via Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. It has a 640 x 360 pixel display and a 5-megapixel camera. The user can interact with the AR content with voice, touch, or head movements.
