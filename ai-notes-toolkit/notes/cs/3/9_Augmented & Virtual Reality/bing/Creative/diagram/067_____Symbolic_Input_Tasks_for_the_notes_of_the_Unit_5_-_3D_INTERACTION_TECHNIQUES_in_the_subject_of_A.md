### Symbolic Input Tasks

- Symbolic input is the task in which users communicate symbolic information (text, numbers, and other symbols or marks) to the system  .
- Symbolic input is common in 2D user interfaces, such as writing email messages, entering numbers into a spreadsheet, composing documents, and so on .
- Symbolic input is challenging in 3D user interfaces, because of the lack of standard input devices, the difficulty of text entry and recognition, the spatial and temporal constraints, and the trade-off between immersion and usability  .
- Symbolic input can be classified into two types: discrete and continuous .
  - Discrete symbolic input is the input of individual symbols or characters, such as typing on a keyboard, clicking on a virtual keyboard, or speaking a word .
  - Continuous symbolic input is the input of a sequence of symbols or characters, such as writing with a pen, drawing with a stylus, or gesturing with a hand .
- Symbolic input can be performed using various techniques, such as   :
  - Physical keyboards or keypads, which are familiar and efficient, but may break the immersion and require extra hardware .
  - Virtual keyboards or keypads, which are integrated into the 3D environment and can be manipulated with various input devices, but may be slow, inaccurate, and occlude the scene .
  - Speech input, which is natural and hands-free, but may suffer from recognition errors, noise, latency, and privacy issues .
  - Handwriting or sketching input, which are intuitive and expressive, but may require special hardware, recognition algorithms, and feedback mechanisms .
  - Gesture input, which is flexible and immersive, but may be ambiguous, tiring, and hard to learn .
- Symbolic input can be evaluated using various criteria, such as  :
  - Speed, which measures how fast the user can enter the symbols or characters .
  - Accuracy, which measures how correctly the user can enter the symbols or characters .
  - Ease of use, which measures how comfortable and satisfying the user feels when using the technique .
  - Immersion, which measures how well the technique integrates with the 3D environment and maintains the user's sense of presence .
  - Compatibility, which measures how well the technique matches the input device, the application domain, and the user's expectations .
