### Choosing Input Devices for 3D Interfaces

- Input devices are hardware devices that capture and interpret the actions performed by the user in a 3D environment.
- The degrees of freedom (DOF) are one of the main features of these devices. They refer to the number and type of independent movements that the device can sense or produce.
- For example, a mouse has two DOF (x and y coordinates), a joystick has three DOF (x, y and rotation), and a 6DOF tracker has six DOF (x, y, z, pitch, yaw and roll).
- The choice of input devices depends on several factors, such as:
  - The task requirements: what kind of actions and interactions are needed to perform the task? How complex and precise are they? How much feedback is required?
  - The user preferences: what kind of devices are the user comfortable and familiar with? How much physical and mental effort are they willing to exert?
  - The system constraints: what kind of devices are compatible and available for the system? How much space and cost are involved?
- Some examples of input devices for 3D interfaces are:
  - Hand or body gestures: these devices use sensors or cameras to track the movements of the user's hand or body and translate them into commands or actions in the 3D environment. They are natural and intuitive, but may suffer from occlusion, noise or fatigue issues .
  - Physical walking: this device uses the user's actual walking motion to navigate the 3D environment. It is immersive and realistic, but may require a large space and a tracking system.
  - Tools, sensors or devices in 3D space: these devices use physical objects that the user can manipulate or attach to their body to provide input in the 3D environment. They are tangible and expressive, but may require calibration, registration or coordination issues .
