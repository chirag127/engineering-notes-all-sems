### Interaction Techniques for 3D Manipulation

- Interaction techniques for 3D manipulation are methods that allow users to control and modify 3D objects in virtual or augmented environments.
- 3D manipulation techniques can be classified into three main categories: device-based, ray-based, and volume-based .
- Device-based techniques use the physical attributes of a handheld device, such as rotation, tilt, skew, or movement, to manipulate 3D objects. For example, users can rotate a device to rotate an object, or move a device closer or farther to scale an object.
- Ray-based techniques use a ray or a vector that extends from the user's hand or device to select and manipulate 3D objects. For example, users can point at an object with a ray and drag it along the ray, or use a fishing reel metaphor to reel in or out an object .
- Volume-based techniques use a volume or a region of space that encloses 3D objects to select and manipulate them. For example, users can use a flashlight metaphor to illuminate and select objects within a cone-shaped volume, or use an aperture metaphor to adjust the size of a circular volume and select objects within it .
- 3D manipulation techniques can also be combined or enhanced with other modalities, such as gestures, voice, or haptics, to provide more natural and intuitive interaction.
- 3D manipulation techniques can be evaluated based on various criteria, such as accuracy, efficiency, ease of use, learnability, and user satisfaction.
