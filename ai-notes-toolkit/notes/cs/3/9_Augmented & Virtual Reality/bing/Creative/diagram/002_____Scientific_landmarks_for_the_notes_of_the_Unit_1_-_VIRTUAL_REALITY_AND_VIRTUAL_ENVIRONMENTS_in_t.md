### Scientific landmarks for the notes of the Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality (VR) is the use of computer-generated images and sounds to create an immersive and interactive simulation of a real or imagined environment.
- VR has many applications in various fields, such as education, entertainment, medicine, engineering, and science.
- Some of the scientific landmarks of VR are:

  - In 1968, Ivan Sutherland and Bob Sproull developed the first head-mounted display (HMD) that could display stereoscopic images and track the user's head movements. This device, called the Sword of Damocles, is considered the precursor of modern VR systems.
  - In 1985, Jaron Lanier coined the term "virtual reality" and founded VPL Research, the first company to sell VR products, such as the EyePhone HMD and the DataGlove.
  - In 1991, Carolina Cruz-Neira, Daniel Sandin, and Thomas DeFanti created the CAVE (Cave Automatic Virtual Environment), a room-sized VR system that projected images on the walls, floor, and ceiling, and used head and hand tracking to create a more immersive experience.
  - In 1995, the Virtual Reality Modeling Language (VRML) was developed as a standard for creating and sharing 3D content on the web.
  - In 2012, Palmer Luckey launched a Kickstarter campaign for the Oculus Rift, a low-cost and high-performance HMD that sparked a new wave of interest and innovation in VR.
  - In 2016, several major VR platforms were released, such as the HTC Vive, the PlayStation VR, and the Google Daydream, making VR more accessible and affordable for consumers.
  - In 2019, researchers at Caltech used VR to visualize complex scientific data, such as the structure of a tumor or the connectivity of the human genome, in a more intuitive and interactive way .
  - In 2020, astronomers at NASA used VR to explore the motion and distribution of 4 million stars in the Milky Way galaxy, and discovered new patterns and structures that were not visible in 2D representations.

- VR is a powerful tool for scientific discovery, as it can help scientists to:

  - Explore and manipulate large and complex data sets in a more natural and engaging way  .
  - Test hypotheses and perform experiments in a controlled and realistic environment.
  - Communicate and collaborate with other scientists and the public in a more effective and immersive way .
  - Enhance learning and understanding of scientific concepts and phenomena .
