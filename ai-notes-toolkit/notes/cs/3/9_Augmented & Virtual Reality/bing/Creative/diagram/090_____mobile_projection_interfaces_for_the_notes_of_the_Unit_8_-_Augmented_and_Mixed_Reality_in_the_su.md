### Mobile Projection Interfaces for Augmented Reality

- Mobile projection interfaces are devices that combine a camera and a projector to create interactive and immersive augmented reality (AR) applications.
- Mobile projection interfaces can be classified into three types :
  - Object-adaptive applications: These applications use the camera to detect and track objects in the environment and project relevant information or graphics onto them. For example, a projector phone can project a virtual keyboard onto a flat surface or a product review onto a product package.
  - Context-adaptive applications: These applications use the camera to sense the context of the user and the environment and project appropriate content or interfaces. For example, a projector phone can project a map or a navigation system onto the ground or a wall depending on the user's location and orientation.
  - Camera-controlled applications: These applications use the camera as an input device to control the projected content or interface. For example, a projector phone can project a game or a menu onto a surface and the user can interact with it by moving the phone or using gestures.
- Mobile projection interfaces have several advantages over other types of AR displays  :
  - They can create large-scale and high-resolution AR experiences without the need for head-mounted displays or special glasses.
  - They can adapt to different surfaces and environments and create context-aware and object-aware AR applications.
  - They can enable natural and intuitive interaction with the projected content or interface using the camera or gestures.
  - They can support collaborative and social AR applications by allowing multiple users to share the same projected content or interface.
- Mobile projection interfaces also have some challenges and limitations :
  - They require sufficient ambient light and contrast to project clear and visible images.
  - They may cause occlusion or distortion of the projected images due to the movement of the user, the projector, or the objects in the environment.
  - They may consume more power and generate more heat than other types of AR displays.
  - They may raise privacy and security issues as they can project sensitive or personal information onto public or private surfaces.
