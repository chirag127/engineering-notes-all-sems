# Tessellated Data

- Tessellated data is the result of dividing a dataset of polygons (or vertex sets) into smaller and simpler structures for rendering.
- Tessellation techniques are often used in computer graphics, especially for real-time rendering, to manage the complexity and detail of polygonal models .
- Tessellation can be done in different ways, such as:
  - Triangulation: splitting polygons into triangles, which are the simplest and most efficient primitive for rendering.
  - Subdivision: recursively subdividing polygons into smaller and smoother ones, which can create more realistic and organic shapes.
  - Binning: creating a regular grid of polygons (such as squares, diamonds, or hexagons) that cover the entire study area, which can be useful for spatial analysis and visualization .
- Tessellation can have various benefits, such as:
  - Reducing the number of polygons and vertices, which can improve the performance and memory usage of rendering.
  - Increasing the level of detail and resolution, which can enhance the visual quality and realism of rendering.
  - Creating uniform and consistent structures, which can facilitate the processing and analysis of spatial data .
- Tessellation can also have some challenges, such as:
  - Introducing artifacts and errors, such as cracks, gaps, or overlaps, which can degrade the accuracy and appearance of rendering.
  - Requiring additional computation and storage, such as tessellation shaders, control points, and patch primitives, which can increase the complexity and cost of rendering.
  - Depending on the shape and size of the polygons, which can affect the suitability and efficiency of tessellation .
- Tessellation is widely used in various domains and applications, such as:
  - Textiles, quilts, and origami, where tessellation patterns are used to design interlocking motifs of patch shapes.
  - Geospatial analysis and mapping, where tessellation grids are used to aggregate and visualize spatial data .
  - Augmented and virtual reality, where tessellation techniques are used to create immersive and realistic 3D environments.
