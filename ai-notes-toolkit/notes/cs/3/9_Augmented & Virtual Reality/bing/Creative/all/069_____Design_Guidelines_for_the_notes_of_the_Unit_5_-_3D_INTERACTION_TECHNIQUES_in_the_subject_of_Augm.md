# Design Guidelines for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- 3D interaction techniques are methods for manipulating and interacting with 3D objects and environments in virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) applications.
- 3D interaction techniques can be classified into four categories: selection, manipulation, navigation, and system control.
- Selection techniques are methods for indicating or acquiring a target object or location in 3D space. Examples of selection techniques are ray-casting, cone-casting, image plane selection, and hand gesture recognition .
- Manipulation techniques are methods for changing the position, orientation, size, shape, or appearance of a selected object or group of objects. Examples of manipulation techniques are direct manipulation, handle-based manipulation, gesture-based manipulation, and voice-based manipulation .
- Navigation techniques are methods for changing the viewpoint or location of the user or the virtual camera in the 3D environment. Examples of navigation techniques are walking, flying, steering, teleporting, and path following .
- System control techniques are methods for accessing menus, tools, commands, or options in the 3D application. Examples of system control techniques are 2D widgets, 3D widgets, speech commands, and gaze-based selection .
- 3D interaction techniques should be designed according to the following principles :
  - Affordance: the technique should suggest its functionality and usage by its appearance or feedback.
  - Compatibility: the technique should match the user's expectations, the task requirements, and the input and output devices.
  - Consistency: the technique should behave in a predictable and coherent manner across different contexts and applications.
  - Efficiency: the technique should enable the user to perform the task with minimal effort, time, and errors.
  - Expressiveness: the technique should support a rich and diverse range of actions and interactions in the 3D environment.
  - Feedback: the technique should provide appropriate and timely information about the state and outcome of the interaction.
  - Flexibility: the technique should accommodate different user preferences, skills, and styles of interaction.
  - Learnability: the technique should be easy to learn and remember how to use.
  - Naturalness: the technique should leverage the user's existing knowledge and skills from the real world or other domains.
  - Presence: the technique should enhance the user's sense of being in the 3D environment and being engaged with the task.
