# Beyond Text and Number Entry for 3D Interaction Techniques

- 3D interaction techniques are methods for manipulating and navigating in 3D virtual environments, such as augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR).
- Text and number entry are common tasks in 3D interaction, but they pose challenges due to the lack of physical keyboards, the limited field of view, and the need for accuracy and efficiency.
- Some of the techniques for text and number entry in 3D interaction are:

  - Surface-based interaction: using physical or virtual surfaces, such as tables, walls, or screens, to display and select characters or numbers.
  - Tangible interaction: using physical objects, such as cubes, cards, or tokens, to represent and manipulate characters or numbers.
  - Gesture-based interaction: using hand or body gestures, such as pointing, swiping, or drawing, to input characters or numbers.
  - Head-gaze interaction: using the direction of the user's gaze, tracked by a head-mounted display, to select characters or numbers on a virtual keyboard.
  - Natural object interaction: using natural objects, such as fruits, flowers, or animals, to represent and select characters or numbers in a VR environment.
  - Voice-based interaction: using speech recognition to input characters or numbers by voice commands.

- The advantages and disadvantages of these techniques depend on various factors, such as the context, the task, the user, and the system.
- Some of the criteria for evaluating these techniques are:

  - Accuracy: the rate of errors or corrections made by the user or the system.
  - Efficiency: the time and effort required to complete the task.
  - Usability: the ease of use and satisfaction of the user.
  - Immersion: the degree of presence and engagement of the user in the 3D environment.
  - Learnability: the ease of learning and remembering how to use the technique.
  - Adaptability: the ability of the technique to adjust to different situations and preferences.
