# Design Guidelines - 3D Travel Tasks

- 3D travel tasks are the tasks that involve moving the user's viewpoint or virtual body in a 3D environment.
- 3D travel tasks can be classified by their purpose, such as goal-oriented, exploration, search, or physical locomotion.
- 3D travel tasks can also be characterized by their distance, curvature, visibility, degrees of freedom, accuracy, and interference with other primary tasks.
- 3D travel tasks can be supported by different types of travel techniques, such as target-based, steering, or physical locomotion techniques.
- The design guidelines for 3D travel tasks are:

  - The most common travel tasks should require a minimum amount of effort from the user.
  - Use high-speed transitional motions, not instant teleportation, if overall environment context is important.
  - Provide multiple travel techniques to support different travel tasks in the same application.
  - Make simple travel tasks easier by using target-based techniques for goal-oriented travel and steering techniques for exploration and search.
  - Use a physical locomotion technique if user exertion or naturalism is required.
  - Consider the trade-offs between speed, accuracy, and comfort when designing travel techniques.
  - Provide feedback and guidance to the user during travel, such as highlighting the target, showing the path, or indicating the direction.
  - Avoid causing motion sickness or disorientation by minimizing the discrepancy between visual and vestibular cues, reducing the latency and jitter, and limiting the acceleration and rotation.
  - Evaluate the usability and effectiveness of the travel techniques using appropriate metrics and methods, such as task completion time, error rate, subjective ratings, or physiological measures.
