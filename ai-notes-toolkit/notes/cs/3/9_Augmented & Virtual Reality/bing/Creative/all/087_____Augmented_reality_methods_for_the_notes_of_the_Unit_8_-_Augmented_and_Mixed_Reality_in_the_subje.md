# Augmented Reality Methods

Augmented reality (AR) is a method by which we can alter our real-world environment by adding some digital elements to it. This is done by superimposing a digital image on the person’s current view, thus enhancing the experience of reality.

There are different types of AR technology that are suitable for different types of tasks. The two main types of AR are:

- **Marker-based AR**: This type of AR uses a marker, which is a special visual object like a sign or a symbol, and a camera to initiate the 3D digital animations. The system will calculate the orientation and position of the marker to position the content effectively. Marker-based AR example: A marker-based mobile-based AR furnishing app.
- **Marker-less AR**: This type of AR does not require any marker, but instead uses the location and orientation data from the device's sensors, such as GPS, accelerometer, and compass, to position the digital content in the real world. Marker-less AR example: A marker-less AR navigation app.

There are also other types of AR technology that are less common, such as:

- **Projection-based AR**: This type of AR projects a digital image onto a physical surface, such as a wall or a table, and allows the user to interact with it by touching or gesturing. Projection-based AR example: A projection-based AR keyboard.
- **Superimposition-based AR**: This type of AR replaces a part or the whole of the real-world view with a digital image, such as an object or a scene. The system needs to recognize the object or the scene to be replaced and to align the digital image with it. Superimposition-based AR example: A superimposition-based AR app that shows how a furniture item would look in a room.

There are various technologies that are used in AR rendering, such as:

- **Optical projection systems**: These are devices that project a digital image onto a transparent medium, such as a glass or a film, that can be seen by the user. Optical projection systems example: A head-up display (HUD) in a car.
- **Monitors**: These are devices that display a digital image on a screen, such as a computer monitor or a TV, that can be seen by the user. Monitors example: A monitor-based AR game.
- **Handheld devices**: These are devices that have a camera and a screen, such as a smartphone or a tablet, that can capture the real-world view and overlay the digital content on it. Handheld devices example: A handheld device-based AR app that shows information about a product.
- **Display systems worn on the human body**: These are devices that are worn on the head or the body, such as a helmet or a vest, that have a camera and a display, such as a head-mounted display (HMD) or a smart glasses, that can capture the real-world view and overlay the digital content on it. Display systems worn on the human body example: A HMD-based AR app that shows instructions for a task.

These are some of the methods of augmented reality that are used for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, navigation, marketing, and training. AR is a rapidly evolving technology that has many potential applications and benefits for the users.
