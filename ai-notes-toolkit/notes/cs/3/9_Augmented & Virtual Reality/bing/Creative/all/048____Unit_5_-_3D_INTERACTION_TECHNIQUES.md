## Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES

- 3D interaction techniques are methods for manipulating, navigating, and selecting objects in 3D virtual environments.
- 3D interaction techniques can be classified into three categories: direct manipulation, indirect manipulation, and abstract manipulation.
- Direct manipulation techniques allow the user to directly manipulate objects in 3D space using natural gestures, such as grabbing, rotating, scaling, and throwing. Examples of direct manipulation techniques are ray-casting, virtual hand, and go-go arm.
- Indirect manipulation techniques use intermediate devices or representations to manipulate objects in 3D space, such as mice, keyboards, joysticks, menus, and widgets. Examples of indirect manipulation techniques are point-and-click, drag-and-drop, and 3D widgets.
- Abstract manipulation techniques use high-level commands or gestures to manipulate objects in 3D space, such as voice, gaze, or body movements. Examples of abstract manipulation techniques are speech commands, eye tracking, and gesture recognition.
- 3D interaction techniques can also be classified into two types: discrete and continuous. Discrete techniques perform a single action or operation, such as selecting, activating, or deleting an object. Continuous techniques perform a series of actions or operations, such as moving, rotating, or scaling an object.
- 3D interaction techniques can be evaluated based on several criteria, such as usability, efficiency, effectiveness, learnability, satisfaction, and presence. Usability refers to how easy and intuitive the technique is to use. Efficiency refers to how fast and accurate the technique is to perform a task. Effectiveness refers to how well the technique achieves the desired goal or outcome. Learnability refers to how easy and quick the technique is to learn and remember. Satisfaction refers to how enjoyable and satisfying the technique is to use. Presence refers to how immersive and realistic the technique is to create a sense of being in the virtual environment.
