# Evaluating Wayfinding Aids for 3D Interaction Techniques

- Wayfinding is the process of finding one's way in an unfamiliar environment, such as a virtual reality (VR) or a 3D user interface (3D UI).
- Wayfinding aids (WAs) are tools or techniques that help users navigate and orient themselves in 3D spaces, such as maps, compasses, landmarks, signs, etc.
- Evaluating WAs is important to understand their effectiveness, efficiency, and user satisfaction in different 3D scenarios and tasks.
- Some of the evaluation methods and metrics for WAs are:

  - **Effectiveness**: the degree to which users can successfully reach their destinations or complete their tasks using WAs. It can be measured by the number of errors, the number of times users get lost, the accuracy of spatial knowledge, etc.
  - **Efficiency**: the amount of time, effort, and resources required to use WAs. It can be measured by the task completion time, the number of interactions, the cognitive load, the mental workload, etc.
  - **Satisfaction**: the subjective feelings and preferences of users regarding WAs. It can be measured by questionnaires, interviews, ratings, etc.

- Some of the factors that influence the evaluation of WAs are:

  - **The type of WA**: different WAs have different strengths and weaknesses, and may suit different users, tasks, and environments. For example, a map may provide a global overview of the space, but may require more attention and mental rotation than a compass. A landmark may be easy to recognize and remember, but may not provide enough information for navigation. A sign may be helpful for local guidance, but may not be visible or consistent in the environment.
  - **The type of task**: different tasks may require different levels of spatial knowledge and navigation skills, and may affect the use and performance of WAs. For example, a search task may require more exploration and memory than a follow task. A complex task may require more planning and decision making than a simple task.
  - **The type of environment**: different environments may have different characteristics and challenges for wayfinding, and may influence the design and evaluation of WAs. For example, a large and open environment may require more landmarks and signs than a small and closed environment. A multi-level environment may require more vertical navigation and orientation than a single-level environment.
  - **The type of user**: different users may have different preferences, abilities, and experiences with WAs, and may affect their performance and satisfaction. For example, a novice user may need more guidance and feedback than an expert user. A user with low spatial ability may benefit more from a map than a user with high spatial ability. A user with prior knowledge of the environment may rely less on WAs than a user with no prior knowledge.
