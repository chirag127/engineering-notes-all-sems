# World Environment for the notes of the Unit 4 - SOFTWARE TECHNOLOGIES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Augmented reality (AR) is an enhanced, interactive version of a real-world environment achieved through digital visual elements, sounds, and other sensory stimuli via holographic technology.
- Virtual reality (VR) is an immersive experience that isolates users from the real world, usually via a headset device and headphones designed for such activities.
- Mixed reality (MR) is a combination of AR and VR that allows users to interact with both digital and physical objects in a shared environment.
- The world environment refers to the physical, social, and cultural context in which AR, VR, and MR applications are developed and deployed.
- The world environment influences the design, development, and evaluation of AR, VR, and MR applications in various ways, such as:
  - The availability and accessibility of the technology and infrastructure required for AR, VR, and MR applications, such as internet connectivity, devices, sensors, and platforms.
  - The ethical, legal, and social implications of AR, VR, and MR applications, such as privacy, security, ownership, consent, and regulation.
  - The environmental, economic, and social impact of AR, VR, and MR applications, such as energy consumption, waste generation, resource allocation, and social inclusion.
  - The user needs, preferences, and expectations of AR, VR, and MR applications, such as usability, functionality, aesthetics, and engagement.
- The world environment also provides opportunities and challenges for AR, VR, and MR applications, such as:
  - The potential of AR, VR, and MR applications to enhance various domains and sectors, such as education, healthcare, entertainment, tourism, and mining.
  - The innovation and creativity of AR, VR, and MR applications to create new experiences, interactions, and solutions for various problems and scenarios.
  - The collaboration and communication of AR, VR, and MR applications to enable social and spatial presence, co-creation, and knowledge sharing among users and stakeholders.
  - The limitations and risks of AR, VR, and MR applications to cause technical, psychological, and physiological issues, such as glitches, cyberattacks, motion sickness, and addiction.
