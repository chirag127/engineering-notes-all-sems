# Direct Human Input for 3D User Interface Input Hardware

- Direct human input refers to the use of the human body or parts of it as input devices for 3D user interfaces (3D UIs).
- Direct human input can provide natural and intuitive interaction with 3D environments, as well as high degrees of freedom (DOF) and accuracy.
- Direct human input can be classified into two categories: gesture-based and touch-based.
- Gesture-based input involves using hand or body gestures to manipulate 3D objects or navigate 3D spaces. Examples of gesture-based input devices are:
  - 3D mice: devices that allow the user to move and rotate a 3D cursor in 6 DOF. They can be either tracked or untracked, and can have buttons or other controls for additional input.   
  - Data gloves: devices that measure the orientation and flexion of the fingers and the hand, and can also provide haptic feedback. They can be used to perform natural gestures, such as pointing, grabbing, or pinching, in 3D UIs.  
  - Motion trackers: devices that measure the position and orientation of the user's head, torso, limbs, or other body parts, and can also provide haptic feedback. They can be used to track the user's movement and posture in 3D UIs, such as walking, jumping, or leaning.  
- Touch-based input involves using the user's fingers or hands to directly touch or manipulate 3D objects or surfaces. Examples of touch-based input devices are:
  - Pen- and touch-based tablets: devices that allow the user to draw or write on a 2D surface, and can also provide haptic feedback. They can be used to create or edit 3D models, or to perform gestures or commands on a 2D interface.   
  - Touchscreens: devices that allow the user to touch a 2D surface with one or more fingers, and can also provide haptic feedback. They can be used to select or manipulate 3D objects, or to perform gestures or commands on a 2D interface.   
  - Haptic devices: devices that allow the user to feel the shape, texture, or force of 3D objects or surfaces, and can also provide input by applying force or motion. They can be used to enhance the realism and immersion of 3D UIs, or to perform tasks that require tactile feedback.   
- The choice of direct human input devices for 3D UIs depends on several factors, such as:
  - The application domain and task: different devices may be more suitable for different types of 3D applications and tasks, such as modeling, gaming, or education. 
  - The device attributes: different devices may have different characteristics, such as DOF, accuracy, resolution, latency, cost, or usability. 
  - The user preferences and abilities: different users may have different preferences and abilities, such as comfort, familiarity, or accessibility. 
- The evaluation of direct human input devices for 3D UIs can be done by using various methods, such as:
  - Taxonomies: frameworks that classify and compare different devices based on their features, functions, or performance. 
  - Empirical studies: experiments that measure and analyze the user's behavior, performance, or satisfaction with different devices in 3D UIs.
