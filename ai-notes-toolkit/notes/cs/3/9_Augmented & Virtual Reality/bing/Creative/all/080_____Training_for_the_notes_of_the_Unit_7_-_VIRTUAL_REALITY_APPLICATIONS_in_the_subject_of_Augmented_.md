# Training for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality applications are applications that make use of virtual reality (VR), an immersive sensory experience that digitally simulates a virtual environment.
- Virtual reality uses software and hardware to immerse a user, and potentially other viewers, in a digital environment. They get to explore and interact with virtual surroundings, and in some cases, also their physical environment.
- Virtual reality applications require interactive devices, such as goggles, headsets, gloves, or body suits, that send and receive information and create a sense of presence and agency for the user.
- Virtual reality applications can be classified into different categories, such as:
  - Entertainment: VR applications that provide fun and engaging experiences, such as video games, movies, concerts, and art. Examples include Allumette, Colosse, Kingspray Graffiti VR, and Tilt Brush.
  - Education: VR applications that facilitate learning and training, such as medical or military simulations, virtual field trips, and interactive lessons. Examples include Google Expeditions, Titans of Space Plus, Ocean Rift, and The Foo Show.
  - Business: VR applications that enhance productivity and collaboration, such as virtual meetings, data visualization, and remote work. Examples include Virtual Desktop, Littlstar, and Fulldive VR.
  - Social: VR applications that enable social interaction and communication, such as chat rooms, avatars, and online communities. Examples include VRChat, Rec Room, and Facebook Horizon.
  - Health: VR applications that support physical and mental well-being, such as fitness, therapy, and meditation. Examples include Supernatural, Beat Saber, and Guided Meditation VR.
- Virtual reality applications have various benefits and challenges, such as:
  - Benefits: VR applications can provide immersive, realistic, and interactive experiences that enhance engagement, motivation, and learning outcomes. They can also offer accessibility, convenience, and safety for users who cannot or prefer not to access real environments .
  - Challenges: VR applications can also pose technical, ethical, and social issues, such as high costs, hardware limitations, cyberattacks, privacy breaches, motion sickness, and isolation .
