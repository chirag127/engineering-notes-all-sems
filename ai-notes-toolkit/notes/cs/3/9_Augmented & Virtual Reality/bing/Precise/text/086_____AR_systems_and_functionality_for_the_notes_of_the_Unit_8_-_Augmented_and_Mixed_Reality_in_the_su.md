### AR systems and functionality

Augmented Reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the real world by overlaying digital information on top of it. AR systems can be categorized into two types: marker-based and markerless.

1. **Marker-based AR**: This type of AR system uses a physical marker, such as a QR code or image, to trigger the display of digital information. The AR device scans the marker and overlays the digital content on top of it.

2. **Markerless AR**: This type of AR system does not require a physical marker. Instead, it uses sensors and algorithms to detect and track the user's environment and overlay digital information on top of it.

AR systems can have various functionalities, including but not limited to:

- **Object recognition and tracking**: AR systems can recognize and track objects in the real world, allowing digital information to be overlaid on top of them.

- **Image recognition and tracking**: AR systems can recognize and track images, allowing digital information to be overlaid on top of them.

- **3D modeling and rendering**: AR systems can create and render 3D models, allowing them to be overlaid on top of the real world.

- **Gesture recognition**: AR systems can recognize and track user gestures, allowing users to interact with digital content using hand movements.

- **Spatial mapping**: AR systems can map the user's environment, allowing digital content to be placed and anchored in the real world.

These are some of the key functionalities of AR systems. They can be used in various applications, including gaming, education, marketing, and more.
