### Environment Centered Wayfinding Support

Environment-centered wayfinding support refers to the use of the virtual environment itself to aid in navigation and wayfinding. This can be achieved through various techniques, including:

1. **Landmarks:** Landmarks are distinctive features in the environment that can be used as reference points for navigation. In virtual environments, landmarks can be designed to be easily recognizable and memorable, helping users orient themselves and navigate more effectively.

2. **Paths and routes:** Paths and routes can be designed to guide users through the virtual environment, helping them reach their destination more easily. This can be achieved through the use of visual cues, such as arrows or signposts, or through the design of the environment itself, such as creating clear paths or corridors.

3. **Maps:** Maps can provide an overview of the virtual environment, helping users understand the layout and plan their route. Maps can be presented in various forms, such as 2D or 3D, and can be interactive, allowing users to zoom in or out, or to select specific locations.

4. **Spatial audio:** Spatial audio can be used to provide directional cues, helping users orient themselves and navigate more effectively. For example, sounds can be placed in the virtual environment to indicate the direction of a specific location or to guide users along a path.

These techniques can be used individually or in combination to provide effective wayfinding support in virtual environments. By leveraging the features of the virtual environment itself, environment-centered wayfinding support can help users navigate more confidently and effectively.
