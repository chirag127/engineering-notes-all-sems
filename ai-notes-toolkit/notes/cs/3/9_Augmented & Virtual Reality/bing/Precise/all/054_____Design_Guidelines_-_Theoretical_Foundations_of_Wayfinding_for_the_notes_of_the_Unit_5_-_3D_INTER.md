### Design Guidelines - Theoretical Foundations of Wayfinding

Wayfinding refers to the process of navigating through an environment to reach a desired destination. It is an essential aspect of 3D interaction techniques, particularly in the context of augmented and virtual reality. The following are some of the theoretical foundations of wayfinding that can inform the design of effective wayfinding systems:

1. **Cognitive mapping:** This refers to the mental representation of the spatial environment that individuals create to aid in navigation. Effective wayfinding systems should support the development of accurate cognitive maps by providing clear and consistent environmental cues.

2. **Landmarks:** Landmarks are distinctive features in the environment that serve as reference points for navigation. Wayfinding systems should incorporate prominent landmarks to help users orient themselves and navigate more effectively.

3. **Route knowledge:** This refers to the knowledge of the specific paths or routes that lead to a desired destination. Wayfinding systems should provide clear and unambiguous route information to help users navigate efficiently.

4. **Survey knowledge:** This refers to the understanding of the overall spatial layout of the environment. Wayfinding systems should support the development of survey knowledge by providing clear and consistent environmental information.

5. **Procedural knowledge:** This refers to the knowledge of the specific actions or procedures required to navigate through the environment. Wayfinding systems should provide clear and concise instructions to help users navigate effectively.

In summary, effective wayfinding systems should support the development of accurate cognitive maps, incorporate prominent landmarks, provide clear route and survey information, and offer concise procedural instructions to help users navigate through the environment. These theoretical foundations can inform the design of wayfinding systems in augmented and virtual reality environments.
