### User Centered Wayfinding Support

1. User-centered wayfinding support refers to the design and implementation of wayfinding systems that are tailored to the needs and preferences of the user.
2. In the context of 3D interaction techniques, this can involve the use of virtual and augmented reality technologies to enhance the user's ability to navigate and interact with their environment.
3. One example of this is the use of augmented reality (AR) multimodal interfaces that combine speech and paddle gestures for interaction with virtual objects in the real world. This has been shown to improve the efficiency of user interaction.
4. Another example is the use of cognition-driven, context-dependent, and personalized AR user interfaces and interaction methods, such as the SearchAR project, which aims to support key functions in emergency search and rescue.
5. Wayfinding and spatial knowledge can also be enhanced through the use of landmark knowledge, which involves the visual characteristics of the environment, such as shape, size, and texture, as well as procedural knowledge, which involves the sequence of actions required to follow a path.
6. The use of virtual reality in wayfinding studies has also been explored, with a focus on ergonomics and user-centered design, as well as the understanding of human behavior during interaction with the environment.
