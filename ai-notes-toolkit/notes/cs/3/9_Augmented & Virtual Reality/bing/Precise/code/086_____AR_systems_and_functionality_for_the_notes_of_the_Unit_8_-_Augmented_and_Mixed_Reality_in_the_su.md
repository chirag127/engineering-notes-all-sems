### Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality: AR systems and functionality

Augmented Reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the user's perception of the real world by overlaying digital information onto the physical environment. AR systems can be categorized into two main types: marker-based and markerless.

1. **Marker-based AR:** This type of AR system uses a physical marker, such as a QR code or image, to trigger the display of digital content. The AR device's camera scans the marker and the system uses it as a reference point to overlay digital information onto the real world.

2. **Markerless AR:** This type of AR system does not require a physical marker. Instead, it uses sensors, such as GPS, accelerometer, and gyroscope, to determine the position and orientation of the device. The system then overlays digital information onto the real world based on this data.

AR systems can also be classified based on the type of device used to display the digital content. These include:

1. **Handheld AR:** This type of AR system uses a handheld device, such as a smartphone or tablet, to display digital content. The device's camera captures the real world and the system overlays digital information onto the camera feed.

2. **Head-mounted AR:** This type of AR system uses a head-mounted display, such as a pair of smart glasses or a virtual reality headset, to display digital content. The display is positioned in front of the user's eyes, allowing them to see the digital content overlaid onto the real world.

3. **Projection-based AR:** This type of AR system uses a projector to display digital content onto a physical surface. The system tracks the position and orientation of the projector and adjusts the projection accordingly to ensure that the digital content appears correctly on the physical surface.

AR systems can provide a wide range of functionality, including:

1. **Information overlay:** AR systems can overlay digital information, such as text, images, and videos, onto the real world. This can be used to provide additional information about the physical environment, such as the name of a building or the history of a landmark.

2. **Object recognition:** AR systems can use computer vision techniques to recognize and track objects in the real world. This can be used to provide additional information about the object, such as its name or specifications.

3. **Virtual object placement:** AR systems can place virtual objects into the real world. These objects can be interacted with and manipulated by the user, providing a more immersive experience.

4. **Real-time translation:** AR systems can use natural language processing techniques to provide real-time translation of text and speech. This can be used to translate signs or spoken language in real-time, making it easier for users to navigate and communicate in foreign environments.

Overall, AR systems provide a wide range of functionality that can enhance the user's perception of the real world by overlaying digital information onto the physical environment. These systems can be used in a variety of applications, including education, entertainment, and navigation.
