### Scientific landmarks for the notes of the Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS

- Scientific landmarks are significant events or achievements in the history or development of a scientific field or discipline.
- Virtual reality (VR) is a technology that creates immersive and interactive simulations of real or imagined environments, using computer graphics, sound, and other sensory inputs.
- VR has been used for various scientific purposes, such as visualization, education, research, and therapy. Some examples of scientific landmarks of VR are:

  - In 1968, Ivan Sutherland and Bob Sproull developed the first head-mounted display (HMD) that could display computer-generated graphics, called the Sword of Damocles.
  - In 1985, Jaron Lanier coined the term "virtual reality" and founded VPL Research, the first company to sell VR products, such as the EyePhone HMD and the DataGlove.
  - In 1991, Carolina Cruz-Neira, Daniel Sandin, and Thomas DeFanti created the CAVE, a room-sized VR system that projected stereoscopic images on the walls and floor, creating a 3D immersive environment.
  - In 1995, the Virtual Reality Modeling Language (VRML) was developed as a standard format for creating and sharing 3D web content.
  - In 2012, Palmer Luckey launched a Kickstarter campaign for the Oculus Rift, a low-cost and high-performance HMD that sparked a new wave of interest and innovation in VR.
  - In 2016, several consumer VR devices were released, such as the HTC Vive, the PlayStation VR, and the Samsung Gear VR, making VR more accessible and affordable to the public.
  - In 2019, a team of scientists from Caltech used VR to visualize the connectivity of the human genome as a 3D landscape, revealing new insights into gene regulation and expression.
  - In 2020, a team of scientists from NASA used VR to simulate the motion of 4 million stars in the Milky Way galaxy, discovering new patterns and structures in the stellar distribution.

- VR is a powerful tool for scientific discovery and communication, as it can help scientists to explore, manipulate, and understand complex data and phenomena, as well as to share their findings and knowledge with others in an engaging and intuitive way.
