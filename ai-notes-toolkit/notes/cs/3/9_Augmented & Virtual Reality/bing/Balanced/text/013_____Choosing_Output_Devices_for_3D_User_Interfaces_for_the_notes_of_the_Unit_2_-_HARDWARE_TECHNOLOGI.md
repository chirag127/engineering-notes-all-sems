### Choosing Output Devices for 3D User Interfaces

- Output devices, also called display devices, allow the machine to provide information or feedback to one or more users through the human perceptual system.
- Most output devices are focused on stimulating the visual, auditory, or haptic senses, but other modalities such as olfactory or gustatory can also be used.
- The choice of output devices depends on several factors, such as the application domain, the user's tasks, the available budget, the physical space, and the desired level of immersion.
- Immersion is the degree to which the user feels surrounded by and involved in the virtual environment, and it can be influenced by the field of view, the resolution, the stereoscopy, the refresh rate, and the latency of the output devices.
- Some of the common output devices for 3D user interfaces are:

  - Visual displays: These devices present graphical information to the user's eyes, and they can be classified into three categories: monitor-based displays, projection-based displays, and head-mounted displays .
    - Monitor-based displays: These are the most common and affordable visual displays, and they include desktop monitors, laptops, tablets, and smartphones. They have the advantage of being familiar, portable, and easy to use, but they have the disadvantage of providing a limited field of view, a low level of immersion, and a lack of depth perception .
    - Projection-based displays: These are devices that project images onto a surface, such as a wall, a screen, or a dome. They have the advantage of providing a large field of view, a high level of immersion, and a possibility of stereoscopy, but they have the disadvantage of being expensive, bulky, and requiring a dedicated space .
    - Head-mounted displays: These are devices that are worn on the user's head, and they consist of two small screens that present images to each eye, creating a stereoscopic effect. They have the advantage of providing a high field of view, a high level of immersion, and a natural head tracking, but they have the disadvantage of being heavy, uncomfortable, and causing motion sickness .
  - Audio output: These devices present sound information to the user's ears, and they can be classified into two categories: headphones and speakers .
    - Headphones: These are devices that are worn on the user's ears, and they can provide either monaural or binaural sound. Monaural sound is a single channel of sound that does not convey any spatial information, while binaural sound is a two-channel sound that simulates the interaural differences that humans perceive in the real world. Binaural sound can create a realistic and immersive auditory experience, but it requires a head tracking system to adjust the sound according to the user's orientation .
    - Speakers: These are devices that emit sound into the environment, and they can provide either monophonic, stereophonic, or surround sound. Monophonic sound is a single channel of sound that does not convey any spatial information, while stereophonic sound is a two-channel sound that creates a sense of directionality by using the left and right speakers. Surround sound is a multi-channel sound that creates a sense of envelopment by using multiple speakers around the user. Surround sound can create a realistic and immersive auditory experience, but it requires a dedicated space and a calibration system to adjust the sound according to the user's position .
  - Haptic output: These devices present tactile or force information to the user's skin or muscles, and they can be classified into two categories: tactile feedback devices and force feedback devices .

They can provide either static or dynamic feedback, depending on whether the stimulation is constant or changing. Static feedback can be used to convey the shape, texture, or temperature of an object, while dynamic feedback can be used to convey the motion, vibration, or collision of an object .
