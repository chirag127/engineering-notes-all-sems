### Cullers and Occluders

- Cullers and occluders are techniques for improving the performance and realism of augmented and virtual reality applications by eliminating unnecessary rendering of objects or parts of objects that are not visible to the user.
- Culling is the process of discarding objects or polygons that are outside the view frustum, which is the region of space defined by the camera's position, orientation, and field of view. Culling can reduce the number of draw calls and the amount of geometry that needs to be processed by the graphics pipeline.
- Occlusion is the phenomenon of objects being hidden or partially hidden by other objects in the scene. Occlusion culling is the process of discarding objects or polygons that are occluded by other objects, such as walls, furniture, or people. Occlusion culling can further reduce the number of draw calls and the amount of geometry that needs to be processed by the graphics pipeline.
- Cullers and occluders can be classified into two categories: static and dynamic. Static cullers and occluders are based on precomputed information about the scene, such as spatial partitioning, visibility maps, or occlusion maps. Dynamic cullers and occluders are based on runtime information about the scene, such as depth buffers, occlusion queries, or depth sensors.
- Static cullers and occluders are more suitable for virtual reality applications, where the scene is mostly fixed and known in advance. Dynamic cullers and occluders are more suitable for augmented reality applications, where the scene is dynamic and unknown, and the virtual objects need to interact with the real environment.
- Cullers and occluders can improve the performance and realism of augmented and virtual reality applications by reducing the rendering workload and enhancing the visual coherence between the virtual and real worlds. However, cullers and occluders also introduce some challenges and limitations, such as accuracy, latency, complexity, and compatibility.
