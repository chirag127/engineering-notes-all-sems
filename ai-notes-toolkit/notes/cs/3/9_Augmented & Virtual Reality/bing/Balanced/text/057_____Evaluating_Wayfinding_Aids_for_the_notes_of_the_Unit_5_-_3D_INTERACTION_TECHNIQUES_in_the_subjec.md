### Evaluating Wayfinding Aids for 3D Interaction Techniques

- Wayfinding aids (WATs) are tools or techniques that help users navigate in 3D virtual environments (VEs) by providing spatial information and guidance.
- WATs can be classified into four categories: maps, compasses, landmarks and signs.
- Maps show a graphical representation of the VE from a top-down or bird's-eye view, allowing users to see the layout and structure of the environment.
- Compasses indicate the direction of a target or a goal, usually using an arrow or a pointer, and can be integrated with maps or displayed separately.
- Landmarks are distinctive or salient features of the VE that help users orient themselves and remember locations.
- Signs are textual or symbolic cues that provide directions or instructions for navigation, such as arrows, labels or icons.
- Evaluating WATs is important to understand their effectiveness, efficiency and user satisfaction in different types of VEs and tasks.
- Effectiveness measures how well the WATs help users achieve their navigation goals, such as finding a target, completing a route or exploring an environment.
- Efficiency measures how fast and how accurately the users can perform their navigation tasks with the WATs, such as the time, distance or errors involved.
- User satisfaction measures how the users perceive and enjoy the WATs, such as their preferences, attitudes or emotions.
- Evaluating WATs can be done using different methods, such as experiments, surveys, interviews or observations.
- Experiments are controlled studies that compare different WATs or conditions using quantitative metrics, such as task performance, error rates or questionnaires.
- Surveys are self-reported measures that collect users' opinions or feedback on the WATs, such as ratings, rankings or comments.
- Interviews are qualitative methods that elicit users' thoughts or experiences on the WATs, such as their motivations, strategies or difficulties.
- Observations are behavioral methods that record users' actions or interactions with the WATs, such as eye movements, gestures or verbalizations.
- Evaluating WATs can provide useful insights and guidelines for designing and implementing 3D interaction techniques that support navigation in VEs.
