### 3D Manipulation tasks

- 3D manipulation tasks are the actions that users perform on 3D objects in a virtual or augmented environment, such as moving, rotating, scaling, or deforming them.
- 3D manipulation tasks can be classified into two categories: direct and indirect manipulation.
  - Direct manipulation is when the user interacts with the 3D object directly, using a natural or physical input device, such as a hand, a finger, a stylus, or a glove. The user can feel a sense of presence and immersion in the virtual or augmented environment, as well as a high degree of control and feedback.
  - Indirect manipulation is when the user interacts with the 3D object indirectly, using a symbolic or abstract input device, such as a mouse, a keyboard, a joystick, or a menu. The user can perform complex and precise operations on the 3D object, but may lose some sense of presence and immersion in the virtual or augmented environment, as well as some degree of control and feedback.
- 3D manipulation tasks can also be classified into two types: transformation and deformation.
  - Transformation is when the user changes the position, orientation, or size of the 3D object, without altering its shape or topology. Transformation can be further divided into three subtypes: translation, rotation, and scaling.
    - Translation is when the user moves the 3D object along a certain direction or axis, or to a certain location or point.
    - Rotation is when the user rotates the 3D object around a certain axis or point, or to a certain angle or orientation.
    - Scaling is when the user changes the size of the 3D object uniformly or non-uniformly, or to a certain ratio or dimension.
  - Deformation is when the user changes the shape or topology of the 3D object, without altering its position, orientation, or size. Deformation can be further divided into two subtypes: free-form and constrained deformation.
    - Free-form deformation is when the user modifies the shape of the 3D object arbitrarily, using a variety of tools or techniques, such as sculpting, painting, or sketching.
    - Constrained deformation is when the user modifies the shape of the 3D object according to some predefined rules or constraints, such as bending, twisting, or extruding.
