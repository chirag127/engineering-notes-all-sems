### Travel Techniques

Travel techniques are methods for moving the user's viewpoint in a 3D virtual environment. Travel is the motor component of navigation, which also involves wayfinding (the cognitive process of determining and following a route). Travel is one of the most basic and common 3D interaction techniques, used in almost any large-scale virtual environment.

Travel techniques can be classified by the metaphor they use, such as:

- Walking: The user moves the viewpoint by simulating the natural motion of walking, either by using physical input devices (such as treadmills, pedals, or gesture sensors) or by using non-physical input devices (such as keyboards, mice, or joysticks). Walking techniques aim to provide a high level of immersion and naturalness, but they may also cause fatigue, motion sickness, or spatial disorientation.

- Steering: The user moves the viewpoint by controlling the direction and speed of a virtual vehicle, such as a car, a plane, or a spaceship. Steering techniques use non-physical input devices, such as keyboards, mice, joysticks, or head trackers. Steering techniques offer a high level of control and flexibility, but they may also require a high level of skill and attention, and they may not match the user's expectations of natural motion.

- Selection-based travel: The user moves the viewpoint by selecting a target location or direction in the virtual environment, either by pointing, clicking, or gazing. Selection-based travel techniques use non-physical input devices, such as mice, touchscreens, or eye trackers. Selection-based travel techniques are simple and efficient, but they may also cause a loss of spatial awareness, a lack of continuity, or a sense of teleportation.

- Manipulation-based travel: The user moves the viewpoint by manipulating a virtual object that represents the virtual environment, such as a map, a miniature model, or a handheld device. Manipulation-based travel techniques use physical or non-physical input devices, such as gloves, mice, or touchscreens. Manipulation-based travel techniques are intuitive and flexible, but they may also cause a loss of immersion, a mismatch of scale, or a lack of realism.

Some travel techniques may also incorporate different perspectives of multiple cameras, such as through-the-lens, transitioning to remote camera feeds, or snapshots of augmented scenes. These techniques may enhance the user's perception and understanding of the virtual environment, but they may also cause confusion, inconsistency, or distraction.
