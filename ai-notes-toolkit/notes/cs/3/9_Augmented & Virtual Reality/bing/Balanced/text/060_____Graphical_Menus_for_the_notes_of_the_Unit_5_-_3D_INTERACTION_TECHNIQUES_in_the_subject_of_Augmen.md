### Graphical Menus

- Graphical menus are an integral and essential component of user interfaces and have been widely used for 2D desktop applications.
- Graphical menus for 3D UIs are the 3D equivalent of the 2D menus that have proven to be a successful system control technique in desktop UIs.
- Graphical menus in 3D user interfaces are the adapted 2D menus that have been integrated into virtual environments.
- Graphical menus used in 3D UIs can be subdivided into three categories: Adapted 2D menus, 1-DOF menus, and 3D widgets.

#### Adapted 2D Menus

- Adapted 2D menus are the simplest form of graphical menus for 3D UIs. They are essentially 2D menus that are embedded in a 3D environment, either as a part of the virtual world or as a separate window.
- Adapted 2D menus can be positioned in space, either relative to the user's viewpoint or to a fixed location in the virtual world. They can also be attached to a hand-held device, such as a tablet or a wand, that can be moved and oriented by the user.
- Adapted 2D menus can use the same selection techniques as 2D menus, such as pointing, clicking, or gesturing. However, they may suffer from occlusion, distortion, or legibility issues due to the perspective projection and the depth cues in the 3D environment.
- Adapted 2D menus are familiar to users who have used graphical menus for desktop applications, and they can provide a consistent and efficient way of accessing system functions. However, they may not take full advantage of the 3D capabilities of the system, and they may break the immersion and presence of the user in the virtual environment.

#### 1-DOF Menus

- 1-DOF menus are graphical menus that use one degree of freedom (DOF) for navigation and selection. They are typically circular or radial menus that are arranged around a central point or a cursor.
- 1-DOF menus can be activated by a button press, a gesture, or a voice command. The user can then select an option by rotating the cursor or the menu around the central point, using a device such as a mouse, a joystick, or a wand. The selection can be confirmed by another button press, a gesture, or a voice command.
- 1-DOF menus can be positioned in space, either relative to the user's viewpoint, to a fixed location in the virtual world, or to a hand-held device. They can also be scaled and oriented to fit the user's preference and the available space.
- 1-DOF menus can reduce the occlusion, distortion, and legibility issues of adapted 2D menus, as they can be easily adjusted and aligned with the user's view. They can also provide a fast and intuitive way of accessing system functions, as they require only one DOF for navigation and selection. However, they may have a limited number of options, as they may become cluttered or confusing if there are too many options on the menu.

#### 3D Widgets

- 3D widgets are graphical menus that use more than one DOF for navigation and selection. They are 3D objects that have visual affordances and behaviors that indicate their functionality and interactivity.
- 3D widgets can be activated by a button press, a gesture, a voice command, or a direct manipulation. The user can then select an option by manipulating the 3D widget, using a device such as a mouse, a joystick, a wand, or a glove. The selection can be confirmed by another button press, a gesture, a voice command, or a direct manipulation.
- 3D widgets can be positioned in space, either relative to the user's viewpoint, to a fixed location in the virtual world, or to a hand-held device. They can also be scaled and oriented to fit the user's preference and the available space.
- 3D widgets can take advantage of the extra DOF available in a 3D environment to enable more complex menu structures or better visual affordances for menu entries.
