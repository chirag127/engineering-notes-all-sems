### Objects - Geometry

- Objects are the basic building blocks of 3D graphics. They are composed of vertices, edges, and faces that define their shape and appearance.
- Vertices are the points in 3D space that mark the corners or endpoints of an object. They have coordinates (x, y, z) that specify their position relative to the origin (0, 0, 0).
- Edges are the lines that connect two vertices. They define the boundaries of an object and can have attributes such as color, thickness, and style.
- Faces are the flat or curved surfaces that fill the space between three or more edges. They define the visible parts of an object and can have attributes such as color, texture, and normal vector.
- Geometry is the branch of mathematics that studies the properties and relationships of shapes, angles, and distances in 3D space. It is essential for creating and manipulating objects in 3D graphics.
- Some common geometric concepts and operations that are used in 3D graphics are:
  - Coordinate systems: A coordinate system is a way of defining the position and orientation of an object in 3D space. There are different types of coordinate systems, such as Cartesian, polar, spherical, and cylindrical. Each coordinate system has a set of axes, a unit of measurement, and an origin point.
  - Transformations: A transformation is a way of changing the position, orientation, size, or shape of an object in 3D space. There are different types of transformations, such as translation, rotation, scaling, and shearing. Each transformation can be represented by a matrix, a vector, or a quaternion.
  - Projections: A projection is a way of mapping a 3D object onto a 2D plane, such as a screen or a camera. There are different types of projections, such as orthographic, perspective, and oblique. Each projection has a set of parameters, such as the field of view, the aspect ratio, and the clipping planes.
  - Intersections: An intersection is a point or a region where two or more objects overlap or touch in 3D space. There are different types of intersections, such as point-line, line-line, line-plane, and plane-plane. Each intersection can be computed by using algebraic or geometric methods.
