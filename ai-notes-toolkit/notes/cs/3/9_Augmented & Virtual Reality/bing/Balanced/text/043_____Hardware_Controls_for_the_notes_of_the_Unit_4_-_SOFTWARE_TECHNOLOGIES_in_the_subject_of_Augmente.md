### Hardware Controls for Augmented and Virtual Reality

- Hardware controls are devices or components that enable users to interact with augmented and virtual reality applications.
- Hardware controls can be classified into three categories: input, output, and hybrid.
- Input controls are devices that capture user actions, such as gestures, movements, voice, or eye tracking, and send them to the application. Examples of input controls are:
  - Controllers: handheld devices that have buttons, triggers, joysticks, or touchpads to control the virtual environment. Examples are the Oculus Touch, the HTC Vive controllers, and the Xbox gamepad.
  - Motion sensors: devices that track the position and orientation of the user's head, body, or limbs in the real world and translate them to the virtual world. Examples are the Oculus Rift, the HTC Vive, and the PlayStation VR headsets, which use external cameras or base stations to track the user's head and controllers. Other examples are the Leap Motion Controller, which tracks the user's hand movements, and the Kinect, which tracks the user's body movements.
  - Voice and eye tracking: devices that capture the user's voice commands or eye movements and use them to interact with the virtual environment. Examples are the Google Glass, which uses voice recognition to control the augmented reality display, and the Fove, which uses eye tracking to control the virtual reality display.
- Output controls are devices that display the augmented or virtual reality content to the user, such as images, sounds, or haptic feedback. Examples of output controls are:
  - Displays: devices that project the virtual images onto the user's field of view, either by using a screen or a lens. Examples are the Oculus Rift, the HTC Vive, and the PlayStation VR headsets, which use screens to display the virtual reality content, and the Google Glass, the Microsoft HoloLens, and the Magic Leap, which use lenses to display the augmented reality content.
  - Audio: devices that produce sounds that match the virtual environment, either by using speakers or headphones. Examples are the Oculus Rift, the HTC Vive, and the PlayStation VR headsets, which use headphones to provide 3D audio, and the Google Glass, which uses a bone conduction speaker to provide audio without blocking the user's ears.
  - Haptic: devices that provide tactile sensations that simulate the virtual environment, such as vibrations, forces, or temperatures. Examples are the Oculus Touch and the HTC Vive controllers, which use vibrations to provide haptic feedback, and the Teslasuit, which uses electric impulses to provide full-body haptic feedback.
- Hybrid controls are devices that combine input and output functions, such as displaying and capturing information at the same time. Examples of hybrid controls are:
  - Smartphones: devices that have a screen, a camera, a microphone, a speaker, and various sensors that can be used to display and capture augmented reality content. Examples are the iPhone and the Android phones, which can run various augmented reality applications using the ARKit and the ARCore frameworks.
  - Mixed reality headsets: devices that have a display, a camera, a microphone, a speaker, and various sensors that can display and capture both augmented and virtual reality content. Examples are the Microsoft HoloLens and the Magic Leap, which use spatial mapping and gesture recognition to blend the virtual and real worlds .
