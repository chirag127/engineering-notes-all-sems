### Architecture for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality (VR) is a technology that creates immersive and interactive simulations of real or imagined environments, using computer-generated graphics, sounds, and other sensory inputs.
- VR applications for architecture are software tools that allow architects, designers, engineers, and clients to visualize, explore, and modify architectural projects in VR, using devices such as headsets, controllers, and trackers.
- VR applications for architecture can have various benefits, such as:
  - Enhancing the design process by enabling rapid prototyping, testing, and iteration of ideas in a realistic and immersive way.
  - Improving the communication and collaboration among stakeholders by allowing them to share and review the same virtual model, regardless of their physical location.
  - Increasing the engagement and satisfaction of clients by allowing them to experience and interact with the design before it is built, and to provide feedback and suggestions in real time.
  - Reducing the cost and time of construction by minimizing errors, rework, and waste, and by optimizing the use of resources and materials.
- VR applications for architecture can have different levels of detail (LOD), ranging from low-fidelity sketches and wireframes to high-fidelity photorealistic renderings, depending on the purpose and stage of the design process.
- VR applications for architecture can also have different modes of interaction, such as:
  - Passive mode, where the user can only observe and navigate the virtual environment, without changing or affecting it.
  - Active mode, where the user can manipulate and modify the virtual environment, such as changing the lighting, materials, furniture, etc.
  - Collaborative mode, where multiple users can interact and cooperate with each other in the same virtual environment, using voice chat, gestures, annotations, etc.
- Some examples of VR applications for architecture are:
  - ARki, a mixed reality application that allows users to superimpose their 3D models on the real world, using their smartphone or tablet camera.
  - Storyboard VR, a free prototyping and visualization tool that allows users to create and edit VR scenes using 2D images, sketches, and text, and to export them to VR headsets.
  - Pair, an augmented reality application that allows users to place and arrange 3D models of furniture and objects in their real space, using their smartphone or tablet camera.
  - SmartReality, an augmented reality application that allows users to view and interact with 3D models of buildings and structures, using their smartphone or tablet camera and a printed marker.
  - Fuzor, a VR application that allows users to import and synchronize their 3D models from various software platforms, such as Revit, SketchUp, Rhino, etc., and to explore and modify them in VR, using various devices and controllers.
