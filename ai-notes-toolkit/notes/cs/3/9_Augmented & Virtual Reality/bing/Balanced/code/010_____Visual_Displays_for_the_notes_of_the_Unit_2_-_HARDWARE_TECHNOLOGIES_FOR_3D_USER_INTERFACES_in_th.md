### Visual Displays for 3D User Interfaces

Visual displays are output devices that present visual information to the user in 3D user interfaces. They can vary in size, shape, resolution, field of view, field of regard, and degree of immersion. Visual displays can be classified into two main categories: fully-immersive and semi-immersive.

- Fully-immersive displays are devices that completely occlude the real world and provide a 360-degree view of the virtual environment. Examples of fully-immersive displays are head-mounted displays (HMDs), arm-mounted displays, and virtual retinal displays. These displays have the advantage of providing a high sense of presence and realism, but they also have some drawbacks, such as limited resolution, weight, cost, and motion sickness.

- Semi-immersive displays are devices that partially occlude the real world and provide a limited view of the virtual environment. Examples of semi-immersive displays are monitors, surround-screen displays, workbenches, hemispherical displays, and autostereoscopic displays. These displays have the advantage of being more affordable, comfortable, and compatible with existing hardware, but they also have some drawbacks, such as limited field of view, field of regard, and immersion.

Some of the common characteristics of visual displays that affect the quality and performance of 3D user interfaces are:

- Spatial resolution: the number of pixels per unit area on the display. Higher resolution means more details and sharpness, but also more computational cost and power consumption.

- Field of view (FOV): the angular extent of the visible area on the display. Higher FOV means more coverage and immersion, but also more distortion and peripheral vision.

- Field of regard (FOR): the angular extent of the possible viewing area on the display. Higher FOR means more flexibility and exploration, but also more head or body movement and tracking.

- Brightness: the intensity of light emitted by the display. Higher brightness means more visibility and contrast, but also more glare and eye strain.

- Color: the range and quality of colors displayed by the display. Higher color means more realism and aesthetics, but also more complexity and calibration.

- Gamma correction: the adjustment of the brightness and contrast of the display to match the human perception of light. Higher gamma correction means more natural and balanced colors, but also more processing and calibration.
