### Real-time computer graphics

- Real-time computer graphics are images that are generated and displayed by a computer at a high enough speed to interact with a user or a dynamic environment.
- Real-time computer graphics are used for various applications, such as video games, simulations, virtual reality, augmented reality, animation, and visualization.
- Real-time computer graphics require a trade-off between quality and performance, as the images have to be rendered within a limited time budget, usually measured in frames per second (FPS).
- Real-time computer graphics involve several steps, such as modeling, texturing, lighting, shading, animation, and rendering.
- Modeling is the process of creating the geometric shapes of the objects in the scene, using vertices, edges, and faces.
- Texturing is the process of applying images or patterns to the surfaces of the objects, to give them more detail and realism.
- Lighting is the process of simulating the effects of light sources and shadows on the objects and the environment, to create a sense of depth and mood.
- Shading is the process of calculating the color and brightness of each pixel on the screen, based on the lighting, the texture, and the viewing angle.
- Animation is the process of changing the position, orientation, or shape of the objects over time, to create the illusion of movement and life.
- Rendering is the process of generating the final image from the scene, using a graphics pipeline that consists of several stages, such as vertex processing, rasterization, fragment processing, and output merging.
