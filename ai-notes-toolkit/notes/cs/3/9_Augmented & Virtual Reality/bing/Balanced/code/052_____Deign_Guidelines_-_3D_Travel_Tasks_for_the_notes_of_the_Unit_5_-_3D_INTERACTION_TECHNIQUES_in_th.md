### Design Guidelines - 3D Travel Tasks

- 3D travel tasks are tasks that require the user to move through a 3D virtual environment, such as exploring, navigating, or searching for a target.
- 3D travel tasks can be classified into three types: point-to-point, path following, and free exploration.
- Point-to-point tasks involve moving from one location to another, such as teleporting or flying.
- Path following tasks involve moving along a predefined or user-defined path, such as walking or driving.
- Free exploration tasks involve moving freely in any direction, such as roaming or wandering.
- 3D travel tasks can be influenced by various factors, such as the size and complexity of the environment, the speed and accuracy of the movement, the level of immersion and presence, and the user's preferences and skills.
- 3D travel tasks can be supported by various interaction techniques, such as steering, pointing, gesturing, or voice commands.
- 3D travel tasks can also be enhanced by various feedback modalities, such as visual, auditory, haptic, or olfactory.
- 3D travel tasks can pose various challenges and issues, such as motion sickness, disorientation, fatigue, or boredom.
- Some design guidelines for 3D travel tasks are:

  - Match the interaction technique to the task type and the user's needs and abilities.
  - Provide clear and consistent feedback to the user about their position, orientation, and movement in the environment.
  - Avoid abrupt or unnatural changes in the user's viewpoint or motion.
  - Minimize the cognitive and physical workload of the user.
  - Maximize the user's sense of immersion and presence in the environment.
  - Ensure the user's safety and comfort in the real and virtual worlds.
