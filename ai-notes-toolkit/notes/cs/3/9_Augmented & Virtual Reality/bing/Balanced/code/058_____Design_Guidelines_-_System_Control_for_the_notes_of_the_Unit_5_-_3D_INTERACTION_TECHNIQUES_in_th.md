### Design Guidelines - System Control

- System control refers to the ability of the user to manipulate the state and behavior of the virtual environment and the application.
- System control includes tasks such as navigation, selection, manipulation, configuration, and command input.
- Designing effective system control techniques requires considering the following aspects:

  - The characteristics of the input and output devices, such as their degrees of freedom, resolution, accuracy, latency, and feedback modalities.
  - The characteristics of the virtual environment, such as its size, complexity, realism, and dynamics.
  - The characteristics of the user, such as their preferences, skills, expectations, and goals.
  - The characteristics of the task, such as its frequency, duration, difficulty, and importance.

- Some general design guidelines for system control are:

  - Provide multiple and consistent ways of performing the same task, such as using menus, buttons, gestures, voice commands, etc.
  - Provide clear and immediate feedback for every user action, such as highlighting, sound, vibration, etc.
  - Provide undo and redo functions for reversible actions, such as moving, rotating, scaling, etc.
  - Provide context-sensitive and adaptive techniques that adjust to the current situation, such as changing the cursor shape, the interaction mode, the level of detail, etc.
  - Provide intuitive and natural mappings between the input and output devices, such as using direct manipulation, spatial metaphors, bimanual interaction, etc.
  - Provide appropriate constraints and guides that facilitate the user's performance, such as snapping, snapping, alignment, grids, etc.
  - Provide help and guidance functions that assist the user in learning and using the system, such as tooltips, tutorials, manuals, etc.
