# Haptic Displays

Haptic displays are interfaces that provide users with haptic feedback, which is the sensation of touch, in a virtual or augmented reality environment. Haptic feedback can enhance the realism and interactivity of the virtual experience, as well as provide information about the properties and actions of the virtual objects. Haptic displays can be classified into two main types: cutaneous and kinesthetic.

- Cutaneous haptic displays stimulate the skin receptors, such as mechanoreceptors, thermoreceptors, and nociceptors, to create sensations of pressure, texture, temperature, and pain. Cutaneous haptic displays can be implemented using devices such as gloves, rings, patches, or belts that apply tactile stimuli to the user's fingers, palms, arms, or torso. Cutaneous haptic displays can also use electrostatic, ultrasonic, or magnetic forces to create tactile sensations without direct contact with the skin .

- Kinesthetic haptic displays stimulate the muscle and joint receptors, such as proprioceptors and vestibular receptors, to create sensations of force, position, movement, and balance. Kinesthetic haptic displays can be implemented using devices such as exoskeletons, joysticks, or haptic arms that apply force feedback to the user's limbs, head, or body. Kinesthetic haptic displays can also use inertial, pneumatic, or hydraulic actuators to create force feedback without direct contact with the user's body .

Haptic displays can be used for various applications, such as education, training, entertainment, gaming, art, medicine, and rehabilitation. Haptic displays can also be combined with other modalities, such as visual, auditory, or olfactory, to create multimodal interfaces that provide a richer and more immersive experience for the user. Haptic displays can also be integrated with other technologies, such as wireless communication, artificial intelligence, or brain-computer interfaces, to enable novel and advanced forms of interaction and feedback .
