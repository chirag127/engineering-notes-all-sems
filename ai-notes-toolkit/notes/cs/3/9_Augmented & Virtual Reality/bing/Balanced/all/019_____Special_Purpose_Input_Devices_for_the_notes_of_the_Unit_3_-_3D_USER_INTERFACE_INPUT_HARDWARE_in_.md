# Special Purpose Input Devices for 3D User Interface

- Special purpose input devices are designed to capture user input in physical 3D space or manipulate tools, sensors, or devices in 3D space for 3D user interface (3D UI) applications .
- Some examples of special purpose input devices are:
  - 3D trackers: These devices measure the position and orientation of an object or a user in 3D space, such as a head-mounted display, a hand-held controller, or a stylus. They can use different technologies, such as magnetic, optical, inertial, or ultrasonic .
  - 3D mice and spaceballs: These devices allow the user to control the movement and rotation of a virtual object or a viewpoint in 3D space, using a ball, a joystick, or a knob. They can have multiple degrees of freedom and buttons .
  - Data gloves: These devices measure the finger and hand movements of the user, such as bending, twisting, or pointing. They can use different technologies, such as resistive, optical, or magnetic. They can also provide haptic feedback to the user .
  - Gesture recognition devices: These devices recognize the gestures of the user, such as waving, pointing, or drawing, using cameras, depth sensors, or microphones. They can use different techniques, such as computer vision, machine learning, or speech recognition .
  - Biosignal devices: These devices measure the physiological signals of the user, such as brain activity, eye movement, or heart rate. They can use different technologies, such as electroencephalography, electrooculography, or electrocardiography. They can be used to control 3D UIs by detecting the user's intention, attention, or emotion .
- Special purpose input devices can provide more natural, intuitive, and immersive interaction for 3D UIs, compared to traditional input devices, such as keyboards, mice, or touchscreens .
- However, special purpose input devices also have some challenges, such as:
  - Cost: Some devices are expensive or require special hardware or software .
  - Accuracy: Some devices are prone to errors or noise, or have limited resolution or range .
  - Usability: Some devices are uncomfortable, cumbersome, or require training or calibration .
  - Compatibility: Some devices are not compatible with existing standards or platforms .
  - Ethical: Some devices may raise privacy or security concerns, or have social or cultural implications .
