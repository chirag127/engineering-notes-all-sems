# Augmented Reality Methods

Augmented reality (AR) is a method by which we can alter our real world by adding some digital elements to it. This is done by superimposing a digital image on the person’s current view thus it enhances the experience of reality.

There are different types of augmented reality technology that are proper for different types of tasks. The two main types of AR are:

- **Marker-based AR**: A marker, which is a special visual object like a special sign or anything, and a camera are used to initiate the 3D digital animations. The system will calculate the orientation and position of the market to position the content effectively. Marker-based AR example: A marker-based mobile-based AR furnishing app.
- **Marker-less AR**: This type of AR does not require any marker or image to trigger the digital content. Instead, it uses the location and orientation of the device to display relevant information or graphics. Marker-less AR example: A marker-less AR navigation app that shows directions on the screen.

There are also other types of AR that are less common but still useful for some applications. These are:

- **Projection-based AR**: This type of AR projects a digital image onto a physical surface, such as a wall or a table. The projected image can be interactive and respond to touch or gesture. Projection-based AR example: A projection-based AR keyboard that allows typing on any surface.
- **Superimposition-based AR**: This type of AR replaces a part or the whole of the original view with a new image. The system needs to recognize the object and its shape to overlay the new image accurately. Superimposition-based AR example: A superimposition-based AR app that shows how a furniture item would look in a room.

Various technologies are used in augmented reality rendering, including optical projection systems, monitors, handheld devices, and display systems, which are worn on the human body. A head-mounted display (HMD) is a display device worn on the forehead, such as a harness or helmet-mounted. HMDs can provide immersive and realistic AR experiences, but they can also be bulky and expensive. Other types of display systems include:

- **Smart glasses**: These are glasses that have a small display in front of one or both eyes. They can show information or graphics that are relevant to the user's context or task. Smart glasses example: Google Glass, which can show notifications, directions, or web pages.
- **Smart lenses**: These are contact lenses that have a micro-display embedded in them. They can project images directly onto the retina of the eye. Smart lenses example: Mojo Vision, which can show information or graphics that are invisible to others.
- **Smartphones and tablets**: These are devices that have a camera and a screen. They can use the camera to capture the real-world view and the screen to display the digital content on top of it. Smartphones and tablets example: Pokemon Go, which is a game that shows virtual creatures in the real environment.

Augmented reality can be used for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, tourism, health care, retail, and industrial. Some of the benefits of AR are:

- It can enhance the user's perception and interaction with the real world.
- It can provide contextual and personalized information or guidance.
- It can improve the user's learning and memory retention.
- It can create engaging and immersive experiences.
- It can reduce the cost and complexity of some tasks or processes.

Some of the challenges of AR are:

- It can cause technical issues, such as latency, battery drain, or compatibility problems.
- It can cause ethical issues, such as privacy, security, or social norms violations.
- It can cause psychological issues, such as distraction, addiction, or confusion.
- It can cause physical issues, such as eye strain, nausea, or accidents.
