# Requirements for VR

- VR stands for Virtual Reality, which is a computer-generated simulation of a three-dimensional environment that can be interacted with by a user using special equipment, such as a headset and controllers.
- VR aims to create a sense of presence, immersion, and realism for the user, by providing sensory feedback, such as visual, auditory, haptic, and sometimes olfactory.
- To achieve a convincing VR experience, there are some requirements that need to be met, such as:

  - Hardware: The VR system needs to have adequate computing power, display resolution, refresh rate, field of view, tracking accuracy, and latency to render the virtual environment and the user's actions in real time, without causing motion sickness or discomfort.
  - Software: The VR system needs to have a suitable software platform, such as a game engine, a VR framework, or a VR application, that can create and manage the virtual environment, the user interface, the interaction modes, and the content.
  - Content: The VR system needs to have engaging and realistic content, such as 3D models, textures, animations, sounds, and narratives, that can stimulate the user's senses and emotions, and provide meaningful goals and feedback.
  - User: The VR system needs to consider the user's preferences, expectations, abilities, and limitations, such as their age, gender, culture, background, physical and mental health, and accessibility needs, and provide a comfortable and enjoyable VR experience that suits their needs and goals.
