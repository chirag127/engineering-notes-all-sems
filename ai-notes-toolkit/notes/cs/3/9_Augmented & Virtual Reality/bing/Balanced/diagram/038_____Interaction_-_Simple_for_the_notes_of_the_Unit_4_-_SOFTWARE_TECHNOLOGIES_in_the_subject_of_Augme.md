### Interaction - Simple

- Interaction is the process of communicating and exchanging information between a user and a system, such as augmented reality (AR) or virtual reality (VR).
- Interaction can be classified into two types: input and output.
- Input is the way the user provides information or commands to the system, such as gestures, voice, touch, or eye tracking.
- Output is the way the system provides feedback or information to the user, such as graphics, sound, haptics, or smell.
- Interaction can also be classified into three levels: direct, indirect, and abstract.
- Direct interaction is when the user manipulates the virtual objects directly, such as grabbing, moving, or resizing them.
- Indirect interaction is when the user uses an intermediary device or tool to manipulate the virtual objects, such as a controller, a mouse, or a menu.
- Abstract interaction is when the user uses a symbolic or metaphorical representation to manipulate the virtual objects, such as a gesture, a voice command, or a button.
- Interaction can also be classified into three modes: immersive, non-immersive, and semi-immersive.
- Immersive interaction is when the user is fully surrounded by the virtual environment and has a high sense of presence and realism.
- Non-immersive interaction is when the user is aware of the real environment and has a low sense of presence and realism.
- Semi-immersive interaction is when the user is partially surrounded by the virtual environment and has a moderate sense of presence and realism.
