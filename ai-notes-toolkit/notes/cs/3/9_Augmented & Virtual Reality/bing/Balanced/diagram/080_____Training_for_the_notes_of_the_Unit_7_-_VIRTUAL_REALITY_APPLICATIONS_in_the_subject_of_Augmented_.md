### Training for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality applications are applications that make use of virtual reality (VR), an immersive sensory experience that digitally simulates a virtual environment.
- Virtual reality uses software and hardware to immerse a user, and potentially other viewers, in a digital environment. They get to explore and interact with virtual surroundings, and in some cases, also their physical environment.
- Virtual reality applications require interactive devices, such as goggles, headsets, gloves, or body suits, that send and receive information and create a sense of presence and agency for the user.
- Virtual reality applications can be classified into different categories based on their purpose, content, and level of immersion. Some of the common categories are:
  - Education and training: VR applications can be used to teach and train students and professionals in various fields, such as medical, military, engineering, and science. VR applications can provide realistic and safe simulations of complex scenarios, such as surgery, combat, or space exploration, that enhance learning outcomes and skills.
  - Entertainment and gaming: VR applications can be used to create immersive and engaging experiences for entertainment and gaming purposes, such as movies, concerts, sports, and video games. VR applications can provide a sense of realism, interactivity, and social presence for the users, as well as novel and creative forms of expression and storytelling .
  - Business and communication: VR applications can be used to facilitate business and communication activities, such as virtual meetings, presentations, collaborations, and marketing. VR applications can provide a sense of co-presence, efficiency, and innovation for the users, as well as new opportunities and challenges for the organizations.
  - Health and wellness: VR applications can be used to improve health and wellness outcomes for the users, such as physical and mental health, fitness, and rehabilitation. VR applications can provide a sense of motivation, feedback, and empowerment for the users, as well as therapeutic and diagnostic benefits for the health professionals.
- Virtual reality applications face some challenges and limitations, such as technical issues, ethical concerns, and social implications. Some of the common challenges are:
  - Technical issues: VR applications require high-performance hardware and software, as well as reliable and fast internet connectivity, to provide a smooth and realistic experience for the users. VR applications also need to address the issues of latency, resolution, tracking, and compatibility, to avoid causing discomfort, nausea, or motion sickness for the users .
  - Ethical concerns: VR applications raise some ethical questions and dilemmas, such as privacy, security, consent, and responsibility, for the users and the developers. VR applications also need to consider the potential risks and harms of creating and consuming virtual content, such as violence, addiction, deception, or manipulation, that may affect the users' behavior, emotions, or values .
  - Social implications: VR applications have some social impacts and consequences, such as social isolation, inequality, and diversity, for the users and the society. VR applications also need to balance the benefits and drawbacks of creating and accessing virtual worlds, such as escapism, empowerment, or alienation, that may affect the users' identity, relationships, or culture .
