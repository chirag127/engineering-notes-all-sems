### Direct Human Input for 3D User Interface Input Hardware

- Direct human input refers to the use of the human body or parts of it as input devices for 3D user interfaces (3D UIs).
- Direct human input can provide natural and intuitive interaction with 3D environments, as well as high degrees of freedom (DOF) and accuracy.
- Degrees of freedom (DOF) are the number of independent ways that a body can move in space. For example, a point can move in three DOF (x, y, z), a rigid body can move in six DOF (x, y, z, pitch, yaw, roll), and a human hand can move in more than 20 DOF.
- Direct human input can be classified into two types: gesture-based and touch-based.
  - Gesture-based input uses the motion or posture of the hands, fingers, arms, head, or body to control 3D UIs. Examples of gesture-based input devices are 3D mice, gloves, trackers, cameras, and depth sensors.
  - Touch-based input uses the contact or proximity of the fingers, hands, or other body parts to interact with 3D UIs. Examples of touch-based input devices are touchscreens, touchpads, touch tables, and tangible objects.
- Direct human input has some advantages and disadvantages compared to other types of input devices for 3D UIs.
  - Advantages include:
    - Natural and intuitive interaction, as the user can use familiar and expressive gestures or touch actions to manipulate 3D objects or navigate 3D spaces.
    - High DOF and accuracy, as the user can control multiple parameters or dimensions of the 3D UI with fine-grained movements or adjustments.
    - Low cost and availability, as some direct human input devices are widely used or integrated in consumer products, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, and game consoles.
  - Disadvantages include:
    - Fatigue and discomfort, as the user may have to perform repetitive or strenuous gestures or touch actions for prolonged periods of time, which can cause muscle strain, joint pain, or injury.
    - Occlusion and interference, as the user's body or parts of it may block or obscure the view of the 3D UI or the output device, or interfere with other users or objects in the 3D environment.
    - Lack of feedback and affordance, as the user may not receive adequate or appropriate haptic, auditory, or visual feedback from the 3D UI or the output device, or may not perceive the possible or intended actions or functions of the 3D UI or the input device.
