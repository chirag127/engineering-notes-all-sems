### Travel Techniques

Travel techniques are methods for moving the user's viewpoint in a 3D virtual environment. Travel is the motor component of navigation, which also involves wayfinding (the cognitive process of determining and following a route). Travel is one of the most basic and common 3D interaction techniques, used in almost any large-scale virtual environment.

Travel techniques can be classified by the metaphor they use, such as:

- Walking: The user moves the viewpoint by simulating the natural motion of walking, either by using physical input devices (such as treadmills, pedals, or gesture sensors) or by using non-physical input devices (such as keyboards, mice, or joysticks). Walking techniques aim to provide a high sense of presence and spatial awareness, but they may also cause fatigue, motion sickness, or spatial disorientation.
- Steering: The user moves the viewpoint by controlling the direction and speed of a virtual vehicle, such as a car, a plane, or a spaceship. Steering techniques use non-physical input devices, such as keyboards, mice, joysticks, or head trackers. Steering techniques are suitable for fast and smooth travel over large distances, but they may also cause loss of spatial awareness or motion sickness.
- Selection-based travel: The user moves the viewpoint by selecting a target location or direction in the virtual environment, either by pointing, clicking, or gazing. Selection-based travel techniques use non-physical input devices, such as mice, touchscreens, or eye trackers. Selection-based travel techniques are suitable for precise and discrete travel over short or medium distances, but they may also cause loss of spatial continuity or orientation.
- Manipulation-based travel: The user moves the viewpoint by manipulating the virtual environment, such as grabbing, dragging, or scaling. Manipulation-based travel techniques use physical or non-physical input devices, such as gloves, mice, or touchscreens. Manipulation-based travel techniques are suitable for intuitive and flexible travel over any distance, but they may also cause loss of realism or consistency.

Some other aspects of travel techniques are:

- Transition: The user moves the viewpoint by switching between different perspectives or cameras, such as through-the-lens, remote camera feeds, or snapshots of augmented scenes. Transition techniques use non-physical input devices, such as keyboards, mice, or buttons. Transition techniques are suitable for rapid and discontinuous travel over any distance, but they may also cause loss of spatial continuity or orientation.
- Nonphysical input: The user moves the viewpoint by using input devices that do not correspond to the physical motion of travel, such as keyboards, mice, or joysticks. Nonphysical input techniques are suitable for easy and convenient travel over any distance, but they may also cause loss of presence or realism.
