# Wireless Displays in Educational Augmented Reality Applications

- Wireless displays are output devices that can receive and display wirelessly streamed AR content in real time.
- Wireless displays can be stereoscopic head mounted displays (HMDs) or TFT display modules that have been adapted to act as wireless receivers.
- Wireless displays have advantages for educational AR applications, such as:
  - Enabling collaboration and interaction among multiple users in a virtual environment, without the hindrance of cables or wires.
  - Providing user-specific views or context-sensitive information to each user privately.
  - Supporting mobility and flexibility of users in different learning scenarios and environments.
- Two examples of educational AR applications that use wireless displays are:
  - Construct3D: a collaborative, constructive geometry application that allows users to create and manipulate 3D geometric objects in a shared virtual space.
  - ARMath: a math learning application that uses AR to visualize and explain mathematical concepts and problems in a tangible and interactive way.
- Some teaching scenarios where wireless displays can be beneficial are:
  - Students can use HMDs to explore and manipulate 3D geometric objects in Construct3D, while the teacher can use a TFT display module to monitor and guide their progress.
  - Students can use HMDs to solve math problems in ARMath, while the teacher can use a TFT display module to provide feedback and hints.
  - Students can use HMDs to work on individual or group projects in different AR applications, while the teacher can use a TFT display module to switch between different views and applications.
- The results of developing and evaluating wireless displays for educational AR applications show that:
  - Wireless displays can improve the usability and user experience of AR applications, by reducing the complexity and clutter of the hardware setup and increasing the freedom and comfort of the users.
  - Wireless displays can enhance the learning outcomes and motivation of students, by providing personalized and immersive learning environments and supporting collaborative and interactive learning activities.
  - Wireless displays can facilitate the teaching process and strategies of teachers, by allowing them to observe and control the AR content and activities of the students and to provide individualized and timely feedback and guidance.
