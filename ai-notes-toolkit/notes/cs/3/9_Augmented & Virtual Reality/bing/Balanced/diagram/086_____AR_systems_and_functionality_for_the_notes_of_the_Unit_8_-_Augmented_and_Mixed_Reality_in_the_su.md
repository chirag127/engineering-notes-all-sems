### AR systems and functionality

- AR systems are systems that combine real and virtual worlds, real-time interaction, and accurate 3D registration of virtual and real objects.
- AR systems can be classified into three types based on the display technology: head-mounted displays (HMDs), handheld displays, and spatial displays.
- AR systems can also be classified into four types based on the tracking technology: marker-based, markerless, location-based, and hybrid.
- AR systems can provide various functionalities for different applications, such as:
  - Education and training: AR can enhance learning outcomes by providing immersive and interactive experiences, such as augmented reality books, simulations, and games.
  - Entertainment and gaming: AR can create engaging and fun experiences, such as augmented reality filters, animations, and characters.
  - Healthcare and medicine: AR can improve diagnosis, treatment, and surgery, such as augmented reality glasses, guides, and overlays.
  - Industry and commerce: AR can optimize productivity, efficiency, and quality, such as augmented reality manuals, instructions, and visualizations.
  - Architecture and design: AR can facilitate collaboration, planning, and evaluation, such as augmented reality maps, models, and data feeds.
- AR systems can face various challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Technical issues: AR systems require high performance, accuracy, and robustness, which can be affected by factors such as lighting, occlusion, calibration, and latency.
  - User issues: AR systems need to consider user comfort, safety, and privacy, which can be compromised by factors such as eye strain, motion sickness, distraction, and data leakage.
  - Social issues: AR systems need to balance user expectations, acceptance, and ethics, which can be influenced by factors such as realism, usefulness, and appropriateness.
