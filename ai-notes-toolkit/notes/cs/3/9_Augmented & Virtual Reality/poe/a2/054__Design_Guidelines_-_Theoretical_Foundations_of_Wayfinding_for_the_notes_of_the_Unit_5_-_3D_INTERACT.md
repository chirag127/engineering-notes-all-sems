### Design Guidelines - Theoretical Foundations of Wayfinding

1. Provide a structurally understandable environment:
- Use consistent and logical layouts for spaces and objects.
- Use distinguishable landmarks and reference points.
- Minimize clutter and unnecessary objects.
- Use intuitive categorization and grouping of objects.

2. Offer cues for orientation and navigation:
- Use directional signs and labels for spaces/paths.
- Use highlighting/lighting to indicate important areas or routes.
- Use consistent theming/color-coding for different sections/paths.

3. Enable the formation of a cognitive map:
- Provide an overview/map of the entire space.
- Use linearity and visual continuity between spaces/paths.
- Use gradual exposure to spaces to avoid cognitive overload.

4. Choose appropriate representations:
- Use familiar representations/metaphors for interactions and objects.
- Keep the level of abstraction appropriate for the user and task.
- Ensure accuracy and realism of representations match user expectations.

5. Provide feedback and assistance:
- Give feedback on user actions and interactions.
- Offer guidance/assistance if users appear lost/confused.
- Provide options to re-orient or reset the environment if needed.
