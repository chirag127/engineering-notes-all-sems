### Augmented reality methods for the notes of the Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality

1. Marker-based AR: Marker-based AR uses visual markers or images to trigger the display of AR content. The AR software recognizes the marker and replaces it with 3D models, animations or other AR elements. Marker-based AR is easy to develop but the markers need to be present for the AR to function.

2. Markerless AR: Markerless AR does not require any special markers to trigger AR content. Instead, it uses technologies like GPS, RFID, MEMS sensors as well as object and facial recognition to determine the location and orientation of a device. The AR content is then overlaid based on the device position and orientation. Markerless AR offers more flexibility but can be more difficult to develop and may not be as accurate as marker-based AR.

3. Projection-based AR: In projection-based AR, a projector is used to project images onto real-world objects while a camera tracks the movements and orientation of those objects. This creates the illusion of AR content being attached to the objects. Projection mapping is a form of projection-based AR where 3D surfaces are mapped to physical structures. Projection-based AR does not require head-mounted displays but physical objects need to be present to project onto.

4. Superimposition-based AR: In superimposition-based AR, synthetic imagery is superimposed over a real-world view, either through optical combiners or video combining. AR-HMDs typically use optical combiners where semi-transparent mirrors overlay the AR imagery over the real world. Video combining is used to combine real world and AR imagery into a single output. Superimposition-based AR provides an immersive AR experience but can be more complex and expensive to implement.
