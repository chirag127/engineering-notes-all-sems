### Wireless displays in educational augmented reality applications

1. Wireless displays eliminate the need for cables between the AR device and the display, allowing for greater mobility and flexibility. This is important for educational AR applications where students may be moving around to view 3D models or interact with AR content from different angles.
2. Common wireless display technologies used for AR include:
- Wi-Fi: Allows AR devices to connect to displays over local Wi-Fi networks. Provides more range but can be subject to network interference and latency.
- Miracast: A peer-to-peer wireless screencasting standard that can connect AR devices directly to displays without a Wi-Fi network. Limited range but low latency.
- Wireless HDMI: Uses a wireless HDMI signal to transmit audio/video from the AR device to the display. Also provides low latency but limited range.
3. The specific wireless technology used will depend on requirements and constraints of the educational AR application. Wi-Fi may be preferable if students need to access AR content from anywhere in a classroom, while Miracast or wireless HDMI could be better for applications where students stay within close proximity of a display.
4. As wireless displays enable more mobility, they introduce the need for stronger tracking systems to accurately track the position and orientation of students as they move around. Strong tracking is crucial for registering virtual content with the real world and enabling believable and useful AR experiences. More advanced tracking systems may be required if students will be highly mobile or moving out of close proximity to reference tracking points.
