### Interaction Techniques for 3D Manipulation

- Ray casting: Using a ray (line) to interact with objects in the scene. The ray can be used to select or activate objects.
- Collision detection: Detecting when a virtual object collides with another object or surface. This can enable object manipulation through pushing, grasping, or other physical interactions.
- Widgets: Using 2D graphics to provide on-screen controls for 3D interaction, such as buttons, sliders, and dials.
- 6 degrees of freedom (6DoF) input devices: Allowing direct control of 3D motion in all degrees of freedom: X, Y, Z, pitch, yaw, roll. Examples include 3D mice, motion controllers, and tracked wands.
- Gesture recognition: Recognizing specific movements or poses of the body or input devices that map to interface actions. This can enable natural interaction without hand-held controls.
- Spatial input: Using sensors to track the movement and orientation of interactive devices in 3D space. This enables interacting with virtual objects as if they have a physical presence.
- Constraint-based techniques: Limiting interaction to align with certain constraints to simplify tasks or enable more precise control. Examples include drawing lines/shapes on surfaces or moving objects along prescribed paths.
