### Interaction - Simple

- Direct manipulation: Users interact with virtual objects directly using gestures, motions or voice commands. For example, users can resize or move objects with their hands in AR/VR.
- Menu-based: Users interact with menus, buttons or other standard interface elements to control the system. For example, users can select objects or choose actions via menus in AR/VR.
- Voice: Users speak voice commands or phrases to control the system. Voice interaction allows hands-free control but can be limited by accuracy and the need to learn specific commands.
- Gaze: The system tracks where the user is looking (their gaze) to determine their focus of attention or intent. Gaze interaction is very natural but can be limited in precision and prone to errors from glancing at objects unintentionally.

The key advantages of simple interaction techniques are that they are intuitive and easy to learn as they map closely to the real world or standard interfaces. However, the types of interactions possible may be limited compared to more complex techniques. The appropriate interaction technique depends on the requirements and context of use for an AR/VR application.
