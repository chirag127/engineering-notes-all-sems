### Choosing Output Devices for 3D User Interfaces

- Stereoscopic displays: Provide separate images to the left and right eyes to create an illusion of depth. Require users to wear stereoscopic glasses.
- Head-mounted displays: Place a small display directly in front of the user's eyes. Allow for immersive experiences but can be uncomfortable to wear for long periods.
- Cave automatic virtual environments: Project 3D graphics onto three walls and the floor of a room-sized cube. Allow for immersive experiences and natural interactions using the user's real-world movements. Require specialized and expensive hardware.
- Volumetric displays: Create light fields that occupy a true volume of space. Allow 3D content to be viewed without aids but have limited resolution and are currently very expensive.

The choice of output device for a 3D user interface depends on the specific application and experience desired:

- Stereoscopic displays and head-mounted displays are suitable for single-user experiences. Stereoscopic displays do not occlude the real world but head-mounted displays provide more immersion.
- CAVE systems and volumetric displays enable collaborative experiences but require specialized hardware and space. They provide the most immersive and naturalistic 3D experiences but are often impractical and expensive.
- The trade-off is between immersion and practicality. The appropriate technology depends on how important immersion is for the particular use case.
