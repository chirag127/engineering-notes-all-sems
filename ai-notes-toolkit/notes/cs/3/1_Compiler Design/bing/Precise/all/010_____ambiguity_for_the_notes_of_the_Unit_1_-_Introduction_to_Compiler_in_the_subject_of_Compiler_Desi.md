### Ambiguity in Compiler Design

- Ambiguity is a property of a grammar in which a single string can be derived in more than one way.
- Ambiguity can arise in the context of programming languages and compilers when the grammar of the language allows for multiple interpretations of a given program.
- Ambiguity can lead to confusion and errors in the compilation process, as the compiler may not be able to determine the intended meaning of the program.
- To avoid ambiguity, programming languages often have strict rules and conventions for syntax and grammar, which must be followed by the programmer.
- In the process of designing a compiler, it is important to ensure that the grammar of the language being compiled is unambiguous, to avoid errors and confusion during the compilation process.
- Techniques such as operator precedence and associativity can be used to resolve ambiguity in expressions.
- In some cases, ambiguity can be resolved by the use of parentheses to explicitly specify the intended order of operations.
- If ambiguity cannot be resolved, the compiler may generate an error message, indicating that the program is ambiguous and cannot be compiled.
