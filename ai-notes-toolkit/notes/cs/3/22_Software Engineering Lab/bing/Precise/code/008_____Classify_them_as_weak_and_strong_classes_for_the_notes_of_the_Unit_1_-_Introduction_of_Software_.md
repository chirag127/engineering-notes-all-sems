### Classification of Weak and Strong Classes in Software Engineering Lab

In the context of object-oriented programming, classes can be classified as weak or strong based on their design and implementation. Here are some characteristics that can help differentiate between weak and strong classes:

1. **Cohesion:** Strong classes have high cohesion, meaning that their methods and properties are closely related and work together to achieve a single, well-defined purpose. Weak classes, on the other hand, may have low cohesion, with methods and properties that are loosely related or unrelated, making the class less focused and harder to understand and maintain.

2. **Coupling:** Strong classes have low coupling, meaning that they have minimal dependencies on other classes and can be easily reused or modified without affecting other parts of the system. Weak classes may have high coupling, with many dependencies on other classes, making them harder to reuse or modify without affecting other parts of the system.

3. **Encapsulation:** Strong classes have good encapsulation, meaning that they hide their internal implementation details and only expose a well-defined interface for interacting with the class. This makes it easier to change the internal implementation without affecting other parts of the system. Weak classes may have poor encapsulation, with internal implementation details exposed, making it harder to change the internal implementation without affecting other parts of the system.

4. **Responsibility:** Strong classes have a clear and well-defined responsibility, meaning that they have a specific job or role within the system. This makes it easier to understand and maintain the class. Weak classes may have unclear or poorly defined responsibilities, making it harder to understand and maintain the class.

In summary, strong classes are well-designed, with high cohesion, low coupling, good encapsulation, and a clear responsibility. Weak classes, on the other hand, may have low cohesion, high coupling, poor encapsulation, and unclear responsibilities, making them harder to understand, maintain, and reuse. These characteristics can be used to classify classes as weak or strong in the context of software engineering.
