### Various Philosophical Doctrines

Here are some of the key philosophical doctrines in Indian religion and philosophy:

1. Samkhya - This doctrine states that the universe is composed of two fundamental entities: purusha (spirit) and prakriti (matter). It holds that liberation can be achieved by realizing the difference between the two.

2. Yoga - Yoga is a method of spiritual practice that aims to unite the individual self with the universal self. It involves various physical and mental disciplines, including meditation, breathing exercises, and ethical practices.

3. Vedanta - This doctrine holds that the ultimate reality is Brahman, an impersonal, all-pervading consciousness. It posits that the individual self (atman) is identical with Brahman and that liberation consists of realizing this identity.

4. Nyaya - Nyaya is a system of logic and epistemology that aims to provide a method for determining the truth of propositions. It posits that knowledge can be acquired through perception, inference, comparison, and testimony.

5. Mimamsa - Mimamsa is a system of interpretation of the Vedas that emphasizes the importance of ritual action (karma) in achieving liberation. It holds that the Vedas are authoritative and that their proper interpretation is essential for spiritual progress.

6. Buddhism - Buddhism is a non-theistic religion and philosophy that originated in India. It teaches the Four Noble Truths - that suffering exists, that suffering arises from craving and attachment, that suffering can be overcome, and that the path to the end of suffering is the Eightfold Path.

7. Jainism - Jainism is a non-theistic religion and philosophy that emphasizes non-violence, asceticism, and the principle of ahimsa (non-harm). It holds that liberation can be achieved through the practice of right conduct, right faith, and right knowledge.

These are just a few of the many philosophical doctrines that have emerged from the rich cultural and spiritual traditions of India. Each of these systems offers a unique perspective on the nature of reality and the path to spiritual liberation, and they continue to inspire and inform spiritual seekers around the world.
