### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy, also known as the Shad Darshanas, are the foundational schools of thought that have shaped the history of Indian philosophy. They are:

1. Samkhya: This system focuses on the dualistic nature of reality, distinguishing between the eternal, unchanging Purusha (consciousness) and the ever-changing Prakriti (matter).
2. Yoga: This system emphasizes the practice of mental and physical discipline to achieve union with the ultimate reality.
3. Nyaya: This system is concerned with the nature of knowledge and logic, with the aim of providing a method for determining the truth.
4. Vaisheshika: This system is a realism-based system, which identifies the smallest elements of matter and their properties.
5. Mimamsa: This system is concerned with the interpretation of the Vedas, and is focused on the rituals and practices of Hinduism.
6. Vedanta: This system is focused on the study and interpretation of the Upanishads, and emphasizes the ultimate reality of Brahman, which is the all-pervading, eternal consciousness.

Each of these systems offers a unique perspective on the nature of reality, the self, and the ultimate goal of human life. By studying these systems, we can gain a deeper understanding of the rich philosophical tradition of India and the various ways in which it has influenced Indian culture and society.
