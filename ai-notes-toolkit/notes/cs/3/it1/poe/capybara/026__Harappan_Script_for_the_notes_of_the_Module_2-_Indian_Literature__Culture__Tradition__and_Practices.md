### Harappan Script

The Harappan script is an ancient writing system that was used in the Indus Valley Civilization. Here are some key points about the Harappan script:

- The Harappan script is one of the oldest writing systems in the world, dating back to around 2600 BCE.
- It was used by the people of the Indus Valley Civilization, which was located in what is now modern-day Pakistan and northwest India.
- The Harappan script is still largely undeciphered, which means that we don't know what most of the symbols mean.
- The script was written from right to left, and was used to write a variety of languages.
- The script is made up of around 400 symbols, known as "signs."
- Some of the signs are pictographic, meaning that they represent objects or ideas, while others are abstract.
- The Harappan script was written on a variety of materials, including pottery, seals, and copper tablets.
- The Harappan script was used for a variety of purposes, including trade, administration, and religion.
- The decline of the Indus Valley Civilization around 1900 BCE marked the end of the use of the Harappan script.
