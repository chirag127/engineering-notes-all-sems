### Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

The religion of ancient India can be divided into two major periods: Pre-Vedic and Vedic. Here are some key points about each period:

#### Pre-Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people of the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished from around 2500 BCE to 1700 BCE.
- The religion of the Indus Valley Civilization is not well understood, as their script has not yet been deciphered, but it is believed to have been polytheistic, with a focus on fertility and mother goddesses.
- Archaeological evidence suggests that the Indus Valley Civilization had a highly developed system of water management, as well as sophisticated urban planning and construction techniques.
- The people of the Indus Valley Civilization also had a tradition of yoga and meditation, as evidenced by the discovery of seals depicting figures in yogic postures.

#### Vedic Religion

- The Vedic period began around 1500 BCE and lasted until around 500 BCE.
- The Vedic religion was based on the Vedas, a collection of sacred texts written in Sanskrit.
- The Vedas contain hymns, prayers, and rituals that were used in the worship of various gods, including Indra, Agni, and Soma.
- The Vedic religion was a polytheistic religion that included the worship of nature deities, such as the sun and the moon, as well as gods associated with specific occupations, such as the god of war and the god of agriculture.
- The caste system, which divided society into four main groups based on occupation, was an integral part of Vedic society and religion.
- The Vedas also contain philosophical and ethical teachings, such as the concept of dharma, or duty, and the idea that all beings are interconnected.

Overall, the Pre-Vedic and Vedic periods played an important role in shaping the religious and philosophical traditions of India, and continue to influence Indian culture and society to this day.
