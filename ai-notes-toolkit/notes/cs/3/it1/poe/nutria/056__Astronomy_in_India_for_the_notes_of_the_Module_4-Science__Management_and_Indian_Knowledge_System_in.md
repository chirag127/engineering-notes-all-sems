
### Astronomy in India

* Astronomy has been an integral part of Indian culture since ancient times. The Vedas, the earliest scriptures of Hinduism, contain references to astronomical phenomena such as the equinoxes, solstices, and eclipses. 
* Ancient Indian astronomers developed a sophisticated system of timekeeping, using various instruments such as the gnomon, water clock, and sundial. 
* In the 5th century BCE, Aryabhata, one of the most famous Indian astronomers, developed a model of the solar system in which the Earth rotated on its axis and around the Sun. 
* The Surya Siddhanta, a 5th-century astronomical text, contains a description of the planets, their motions, and the calculation of eclipses. 
* In the 12th century, the astronomer and mathematician Bhaskara II wrote the Siddhanta Shiromani, which contains a sophisticated description of planetary motion and the calculation of eclipses. 
* The 16th century saw the emergence of the Jyotish Shastra, a system of astrology based on astronomical calculations. 
* In the 18th century, the astronomer and mathematician Jagannatha Rao developed a mathematical model of the solar system and wrote the Surya Siddhanta Maha-Kosha, a major commentary on the Surya Siddhanta. 
* In the 19th century, the mathematician-astronomer Ramchandra Rao wrote the Surya Siddhanta Kaumudi, a major commentary on the Surya Siddhanta.
* In modern times, India has made significant contributions to the field of astronomy, including the development of the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope, the largest radio telescope in the world, and the Aryabhata satellite, the first Indian satellite. 
* Today, India has a number of observatories, research institutions, and universities dedicated to the study of astronomy.
