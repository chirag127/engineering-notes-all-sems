
### Harappan Script

The Harappan script is an ancient script used by the Indus Valley Civilization, also known as the Harappan Civilization. It is believed to have been used for writing a variety of languages, including Sanskrit, Prakrit, and Dravidian.

The script is known to have been used from around 2600 BCE to 1900 BCE. It is believed to have been written on a variety of materials, including pottery, seals, and copper tablets.

The script is composed of around 400 symbols, which are thought to represent syllables, words, or phrases. Many of the symbols are similar to those found in other early writing systems, such as Sumerian Cuneiform.

The script has not yet been deciphered, and its exact meaning remains a mystery. However, some scholars have suggested that it may be related to the Brahmi script, which is used by modern-day languages such as Hindi and Tamil.

The script is an important part of Indian culture and history, and its study is essential for understanding the history and culture of the region.
