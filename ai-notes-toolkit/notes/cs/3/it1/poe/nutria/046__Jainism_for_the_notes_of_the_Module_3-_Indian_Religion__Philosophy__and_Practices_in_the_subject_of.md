

### Jainism

* Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that dates back to the 6th century BCE. It emphasizes non-violence and respect for all living beings.
* Jainism is based on the teachings of 24 Tirthankaras or "ford-makers" who show the path to liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
* Jains believe in the doctrine of ahimsa or non-violence and follow a strict vegetarian diet.
* Jains practice asceticism, meditation, and self-control in order to attain moksha or liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
* Jains believe in karma, which is the law of cause and effect. They believe that one's actions have consequences in this life and in future lives.
* Jains are divided into two major sects: the Digambaras and the Shvetambaras. The Digambaras believe that monks should not wear any clothing, while the Shvetambaras believe that monks should wear white robes.
* Jainism is a minority religion in India, with about 4 million followers. It has also spread to other parts of the world, such as Europe, North America, and Australia.
