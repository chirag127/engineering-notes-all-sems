
### Various Philosophical Doctrines for the notes of the Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

* The Vedic tradition is the oldest and most prominent religion in India. It is based on the Vedas, ancient Hindu scriptures, and is the foundation of Hinduism. Its main belief is that the world is an illusion, and one must strive to achieve moksha, or liberation from the cycle of birth and death.

* Buddhism is another major religion in India. It was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, also known as the Buddha, and is based on his teachings of the four noble truths and the eightfold path. Buddhism emphasizes the importance of meditation and mindfulness in achieving enlightenment.

* Jainism is an Indian religion that was founded by Mahavira in the 6th century BCE. Its main belief is that all living things, including humans, should be treated with respect and kindness. Jainism also emphasizes the importance of non-violence and non-attachment.

* Sikhism is a monotheistic religion that was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century. Its main belief is that there is one God, and that all people are equal in the eyes of God. Sikhism also promotes service to others and respect for all religions.

* Advaita Vedanta is a Hindu philosophical tradition that was founded by Adi Shankara in the 8th century CE. It is based on the Upanishads, ancient Hindu scriptures, and emphasizes the unity of all things. Advaita Vedanta also teaches that the individual soul is part of the universal soul and must strive to achieve moksha, or liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
