
### Malayalam Literature 

Malayalam literature is the literature written in the Malayalam language, a South Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala and the union territories of Lakshadweep and Puducherry. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India and was designated a Classical Language in 2013. Malayalam literature has a rich and varied tradition spanning centuries. 

The earliest known literary work in Malayalam is Ramacharitam, a 12th-century poem written by the poet Cheeramakavi. This was followed by the works of Thunchaththu Ramanujan Ezhuthachan, widely regarded as the father of Malayalam literature. His works, such as Adhyatma Ramayanam and Mahabharata, are considered classics of Malayalam literature.

Other notable works include the 17th century Ezhuthachan's Mahabharata, the 18th century Unnayi Warrier's Nalacharitham, the 19th century Kunchan Nambiar's Thullal, and the 20th century Vaikom Muhammad Basheer's Balyakalasakhi and O. V. Vijayan's Khasakkinte Itihasam.

Malayalam literature has been enriched by translations from other languages, such as the Tamil classic Manimekalai, the Sanskrit Mahabharata, the Hindi Ramcharitmanas and the Persian Gulistan. In recent years, Malayalam literature has seen a resurgence, with authors such as M. Mukundan, T. D. Ramakrishnan, M. T. Vasudevan Nair, and Anand winning major literary awards.
