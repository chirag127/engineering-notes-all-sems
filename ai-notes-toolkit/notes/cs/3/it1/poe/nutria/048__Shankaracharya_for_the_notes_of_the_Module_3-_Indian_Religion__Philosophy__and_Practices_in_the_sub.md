
### Shankaracharya

Shankaracharya was an Indian philosopher and theologian who founded the Advaita Vedanta school of Hindu philosophy. He is credited with unifying and establishing the main currents of thought in Hinduism. He is also known for his works in the fields of logic, metaphysics, and epistemology.

- Shankaracharya was born in the 8th century CE in what is now modern day India.
- He is considered to be one of the most influential figures in Hinduism and his teachings are still studied and followed today.
- He is known for his commentaries on the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Brahma Sutras.
- He believed in the concept of Advaita, or non-dualism, which states that the individual soul and the Supreme Being are one and the same.
- He was a proponent of theistic monism, which states that the individual soul and the Supreme Being are both part of the same divine reality.
- He believed in the power of meditation as a means of attaining spiritual enlightenment.
- Shankaracharya's teachings had a major influence on the development of Hinduism, particularly in regards to its philosophical and spiritual aspects.
