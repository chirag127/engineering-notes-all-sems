### Persian And Urdu for the notes of the Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

Persian and Urdu are two important languages that have contributed significantly to Indian literature, culture, tradition, and practices. Here are some key points to keep in mind about these languages:

- Persian was the language of the Mughal court and was widely used in India during the Mughal period. It was also the language of literature, science, and administration during this time.
- Urdu is a language that developed from a mix of Persian, Arabic, and Hindi. It became the language of the common people in North India, especially in the cities of Delhi, Lucknow, and Hyderabad.
- Both Persian and Urdu have had a significant impact on Indian literature, especially poetry. Many famous Indian poets, such as Mirza Ghalib, Allama Iqbal, and Faiz Ahmed Faiz, wrote in Urdu. Persian poetry has also had a major influence on Indian literature, with poets such as Amir Khusro and Mir Taqi Mir being widely read and celebrated.
- Persian and Urdu have also had a major impact on Indian culture and tradition. Persian art and architecture, for example, can be seen in many of India's most famous monuments, such as the Taj Mahal and the Red Fort. Urdu, on the other hand, has influenced Indian music, with many Indian classical songs being written in Urdu.
- Today, Persian and Urdu are still important languages in India, with many people still speaking and writing in these languages. In fact, both languages are recognized as official languages in some Indian states, such as Jammu and Kashmir and Telangana.

In conclusion, Persian and Urdu have played a significant role in Indian literature, culture, tradition, and practices. Understanding these languages is therefore essential for anyone interested in Indian culture and society.
