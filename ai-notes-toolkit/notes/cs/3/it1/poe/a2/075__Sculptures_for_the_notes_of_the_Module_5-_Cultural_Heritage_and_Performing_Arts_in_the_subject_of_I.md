### Sculptures

1. Sculptures are three-dimensional art forms that are shaped by carving, modeling, casting, or assembling.
2. The Indus Valley civilization produced bronze sculptures, terracotta figures of animals and women.
3. The Mauryan period saw sculptural additions to pillars, railings and capitals exhibiting Persian, Greek and Indian styles.
4. The Gupta period produced sculptures of deities, Buddha, and Tirthankaras in stone and metal casting.
5. The Pallava dynasty constructed rock-cut and monolithic mandapas, rathas and sculptures. The sculptures primarily included deities and heroes of the Hindu mythologies.
6. The Chola dynasty built grand temples and sculpted large bronze idols of deities and kings depicting grace, simplicity, and grandeur.
7. The Vijayanagara and the Western Chalukya period witnessed ornate pillars, ornamental motifs, deities and their attendants in stone and metal sculptures.
8. The temple art and architecture of various dynasties represent a fusion of artistic traditions and regional styles with religious iconography. The sculptures played an important role in decorating the temples and giving an insight into the artistic skills and cultural histories of their times.
9. The traditional Indian sculptures pay meticulous attention to details to symbolize religious and cultural beliefs. They represent an important cultural heritage of India.
