### Varnāshrama System

- Varnāshrama refers to the ancient Indian social system based on Varna (class) and Āshrama (stages of life). It consists of the following:

Varna:
1. Brahmins: Priests, teachers and intellectuals.
2. Kshatriyas: Warriors, rulers and administrators.
3. Vaishyas: Traders and merchants.
4. Shudras: Laborers and service providers.

Āshrama:
1. Brahmacharya: Student life - focused on education.
2. Grihastha: Householder - focused on family and profession.
3. Vanaprastha: Retired life - relinquishing worldly duties and preparing for renunciation.
4. Sannyasa: Renounced life - renouncing all attachments and dedicating oneself to achieving Moksha.

- The Varnāshrama system aimed to create a society where people's vocations were dictated by their Varna and life stages, with each Varna and Āshrama having its prescribed duties or Dharma. However, the system was criticized as it led to social stratification and loss of freedom to choose one's profession. The system has declined but the four Varnas and four Āshramas still hold cultural significance in Hinduism.
