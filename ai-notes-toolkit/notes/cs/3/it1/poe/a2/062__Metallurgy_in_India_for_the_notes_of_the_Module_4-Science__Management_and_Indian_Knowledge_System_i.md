### Metallurgy in India

- India has a long history of metallurgy dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization. The people of the Indus Valley Civilization were experts in making tools and ornaments using metals like copper, bronze, and tin.
- The Iron Pillar of Delhi is a remarkable example of metallurgy in ancient India. It is made of wrought iron and has not rusted in over 1600 years. This shows the advanced metallurgical techniques used in ancient India.
- The cultivation of metals like zinc and mercury and the making of alloys like bronze and brass flourished in India. Texts like Rasarnava, Rasashaastra, and Rasaprakasa detail the metallurgical procedures followed in ancient India for extracting and purifying metals.
- Wootz steel was a special type of steel developed in India around 300 BCE. It had high amounts of carbon making it more durable than other steels of that time. It was exported to the Middle East and Europe and coveted for making swords and knives. Its production method was a closely guarded secret.
- Metallurgy in India declined after the decline of the Vijayanagara Empire in the 16th century CE and the relative lack of need for advanced metallurgy with the reduced production of arms and armaments. However, traditional Indian metallurgical practices like making bidriware, engraved brass and copperware, etc. continue to flourish even today.
