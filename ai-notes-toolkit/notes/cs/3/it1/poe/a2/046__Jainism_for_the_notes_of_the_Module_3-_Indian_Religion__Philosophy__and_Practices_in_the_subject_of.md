### Jainism

- Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes complete non-violence and non-attachment to material possessions.
- Followers of Jainism are called Jains, and they believe that liberation from suffering is possible by following a path of purification.
- Jains reject the idea of a creator God or supreme being and believe that the universe is eternal. The goal of Jain life is to reach enlightenment and liberation (moksha) from the cycle of birth and death (samsara).
- The Jains follow the teachings of the 24 Tirthankaras (masters) and the last two, Parsvanatha and Mahavira, are of particular importance. Mahavira (599 BCE–527 BCE) is considered the founder of Jainism.
- The main principles of Jainism are ahimsa (non-violence), anekantavada (non-absolutism), and aparigraha (non-attachment). Jains practice meditation, yoga, and follow a strict vegetarian diet. They consider all life sacred and many Jains are involved in protecting animals and their habitats.
- Jainism has two main sects - the Digambara and the Svetambara. They have different practices regarding nudity of monks and interpretation of the Jain scriptures. Jainism has about 4 to 5 million followers worldwide, with most Jains living in India.
