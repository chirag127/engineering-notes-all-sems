### Telugu Literature

- Telugu literature refers to the corpus of written works in the Telugu language. It is one of the four literary traditions in India, along with Sanskrit, Tamil and Kannada. Telugu literature is predominantly composed in Telugu language.
- Telugu is the third most spoken language in India after Hindi and Bengali and the second most spoken Dravidian language after Tamil.
- The earliest extant Telugu literature, a Telugu mahakavya called Andhra-shabda-chintamani, was composed by Nannayya of Rajahmundry in 905-925 CE. The most predominant literary forms in Telugu are poetry and prose.
- Poetry forms include peddanaama which glorifies patrons, prabandha known for exquisite expressions of worldly enjoyment or moksha, and padyam folk poetry. Dwipada and Shataka are two other prominent poetic forms.
- Telugu prose literature flourished with the Yoga Vasishtha, Panchatantra, Hitopadesha, and others. Devotional literature includes the 12th century Basavaraju Katha, and compositions of Bhakti poets such as Annamayya, Kshetrayya, and Tyagaraju.
- Mahaprasthanam is the most famous Telugu version of the Mahabharata, while the Telugu Ramayana was written by ten poets including Pothana. Pothana's Bhagavata Purana is considered a masterpiece and jewel of Telugu literature.
- Post-15th century literature includes Nannayya's Rajasekhara Charita of 950 CE, Srinatha's Marutratcharitamu of 1552 CE, Pingali Suranna's Kalapurnodayamu, and Ramarajabhushanudu of the 17th century. Literary styles like Prabandha, Dwipada, and Shataka declined, while prose literature flourished.
