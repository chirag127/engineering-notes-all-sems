## Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

1. Indian society is diverse and stratified along caste, class, ethnic and religious lines. The caste system is a unique feature of the traditional Indian society. The concept of purity and pollution along with hierarchies and divisions are integral to the caste system.

2. The nature of the Indian state has been contested and evolved over time. The British colonial rule led to the formation of a centralised state. After independence, India adopted a federal structure. The constitution defines the structure, functions and powers of the government. The preamble outlines the main objectives or ideals of the Indian state.

3. The constitution provides for a parliamentary system of government. The President is the head of the state, while the Prime Minister head is the head of the government. The parliament is bicameral, comprising the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha. The Judiciary is independent of the executive. The Supreme Court stands at the apex of the judicial system.

4. There is a functional separation of powers between the legislature, executive and the judiciary, but a degree of overlap exists in the working of the three organs. For example, the executive (council of ministers) is drawn from the legislature (Parliament). Similarly, the judiciary can review the working of the other two organs. Such overlaps underline the difficulties in strictly separating the functions of the three organs.

5. The making of the constitution was a elaborate exercise where several committees were involved. The Constituent Assembly took almost 3 years to complete its historic task of drafting the constitution for independent India. The finished document included not only the frame of government, but also a set of directives on building a just society. A range of strategies were adopted in resolving conflicting perspectives on the kind of political and social order to be created.
