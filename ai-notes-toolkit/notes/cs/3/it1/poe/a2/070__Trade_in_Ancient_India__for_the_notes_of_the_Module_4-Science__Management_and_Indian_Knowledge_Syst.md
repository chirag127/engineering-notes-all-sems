### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade has been an important activity in India since ancient times. India had trade relations with several countries of Asia and Europe.
- The Indus Valley civilization (3300 BCE-1300 BCE) had a flourishing trade with West Asia and Africa. The Harappans exported cotton textiles, jewellery, rice, timber, etc. and importedgold, silver, copper, ivory, etc.
- During the Gupta period (320 CE-550 CE), there was internal and external trade. Cotton and silk textiles, iron and steel, spices, etc. were traded.
- The Chola dynasty (300 BCE-1279 CE) controlled the lucrative seafaring trade with China, Arab countries, and eastern Africa. Pepper, spices, textiles, etc. were exported and gold, silver, gems, glass, etc. were imported.
- Trading communities like Banias and Chettis played an important role as merchants, bankers, and financiers of trade.
- A good infrastructure with roads, rivers, and ports facilitated the movement of goods. Guilds of traders and artisans managed the production and trade of several goods like textiles and metalwares.
- Coins made of silver and copper were used as currency for exchange. The barter system was also prevalent where goods were exchanged for other goods.
- Many towns and trading centers emerged as important trade hubs, e.g. Varanasi, Kanchipuram, Mamallapuram, etc. Fairs were also held periodically at certain places to facilitate trade.
