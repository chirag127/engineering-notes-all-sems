### Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion: The Indus Valley civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE) worshipped many gods and goddesses, including a horned deity, which is considered as proto-Shiva. They also worshipped a mother goddess, trees and animals.
- Vedic religion (1500 BCE - 500 BCE): The Vedas formed the basis of Vedic religion. They worshipped many deities like Indra, Agni, Varuna, Mitra, etc. through performance of sacrificial rituals like Yajnas. Concepts of Brahman (ultimate reality) and Atman (individual self) emerged. The four Varnas (castes) - Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras were created. The Upanishads (800 BCE onwards) marked the transition from ritualism to philosophy. They formulated the concepts of Samsara, Karma, and Moksha.
- Major beliefs: The major beliefs of the Vedic religion were - presence of many deities who control the forces of nature; performance of rituals and sacrifices to please the deities; existence of heaven and hell; immortality of soul or atman; karma and rebirth; the Vedas as sacred texts; varna system based on birth and profession; ashrama system consisting of four stages of life - brahmacharya, grihastha, vanaprastha and sannyasa.
