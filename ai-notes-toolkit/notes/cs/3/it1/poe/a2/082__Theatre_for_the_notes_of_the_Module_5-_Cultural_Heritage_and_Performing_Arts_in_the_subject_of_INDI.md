### Theatre

- Theatre is a form of performing art that uses live performers to present the experience of a real or imagined event before a live audience in a specific place.
- Indian theatre has a long and rich tradition and history dating back to ancient times. The major forms of Indian traditional theatre are:
-- Natya Shastra or the science of drama: Composed by Sage Bharata Muni around 2nd century CE and is considered as the foundation of all Indian performing arts including theatre.
-- Sanskrit theatre: Originated from Natya Shastra and flourished between the 2nd century BCE and the 10th century CE. Famous plays were Mrcchakatika by Shudraka, Shakuntala by Kalidasa, etc.
-- Classical Indian dance-drama: Performed as solo performances like Bharatanatyam and Odissi or group performances. Depict themes from Indian mythology and legends.
-- Folk theatre: Originated from local traditions and performed in regional languages. Eg. Bhand Pather from Kashmir, Jatra from Bengal, Nautanki from Uttar Pradesh, etc. Use music, dance, and dialogue to convey moral and social messages.
-- Modern Indian theatre: Influenced by Western theatre and realism. Started in 19th century and highlights contemporary social issues. Famous playwrights include Girish Karnad, Badal Sircar, etc.

The major characteristics of traditional Indian theatre are the use of elaborate costumes and make-up, inclusion of music and dance, absence of scenic effects, and use of gestures and mime to convey dialogue and emotion. Indian theatre continues to thrive and evolve while retaining its unique performance traditions and artistic elements.
