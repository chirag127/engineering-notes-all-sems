### Six System Indian Philosophy

- Samkhya: Dualistic philosophy which emphasizes on the distinction between Purush (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter). It proposes that liberation can be attained by distinguishing Purush from Prakriti.
- Yoga: Aims at attaining liberation through meditation and physical discipline. It follows the Samkhya philosophy but proposes that liberation can be attained by stilling the modifications of mind.
- Nyaya: System of logic which establishes the sources of knowledge and proves the existence of God. It uses logic and epistemology to establish the means of attaining liberation.
- Vaisheshika: An atomistic philosophy which proposes that all objects in the universe are reducible to a finite number of atoms. It enumerates the categories of existence and also establishes the existence of God and the means to attain liberation.
- Mimamsa: A philosophy which lays emphasis on the interpretation of the Vedas as the means to attain liberation. It proposes that liberation can be attained by performing actions as prescribed by the Vedas.
- Vedanta: Considered the culmination of Indian philosophy. The Advaita Vedanta proposes that liberation can be attained by attaining the knowledge of the identity of Atman (soul) and Brahman (ultimate reality). The Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita Vedanta have differing views on the relationship between Atman, Brahman, and Jagat (world).
