### Water Management in India

- Water management has been an important aspect of Indian culture since ancient times. Many traditional water harvesting systems were developed in India which were simple, sustainable and community-managed.
- Some of the traditional Indian water harvesting systems are:
- Tanks: Large water bodies with embankments built to store water. They collected rainwater and helped in groundwater recharge.
- Wells: Wells were dug and sometimes lined with stones to tap groundwater. The water was drawn using pulleys and buckets.
- Canals: Canals were built to transport water from rivers or lakes to agricultural land and settlements.
- Ponds: Ponds were small water bodies built to store rainwater for drinking and irrigation purposes.
- These systems were managed by local communities. However, many of these systems declined due to neglect and modernization.
- In modern India, dams are a major source of irrigation and water supply. However, large dams have also led to displacement of communities and ecological damage. Sustainable traditional systems and rainwater harvesting can complement modern solutions to address India's water challenges.
