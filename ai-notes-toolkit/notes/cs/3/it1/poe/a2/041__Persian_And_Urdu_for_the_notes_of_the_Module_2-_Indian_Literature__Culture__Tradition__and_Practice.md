### Persian And Urdu

- Persian was the court language of the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire. It had a profound influence on the vocabulary of many South Asian languages, including Urdu.
- Urdu developed in northern India as a result of the intermingling of the local Indic languages and Persian. It incorporated a large number of words from Persian and Arabic, with a grammar based on local spoken dialects of Hindi.
- Urdu poetry flourished in the 17th and 18th centuries. Some of the most famous poets wrote in Urdu, including Mirza Ghalib and Muhammad Iqbal.
- Urdu writers, poets and intellectuals played an important role in the Pakistan movement. After the creation of Pakistan in 1947, Urdu became the national language of Pakistan, while Hindi became the official language of India.
- Although Urdu and Hindi are closely related and largely mutually intelligible, they are considered distinct languages. They differ in their script, with Urdu using a modified Arabic script and Hindi using Devanagari. They also have somewhat different vocabularies, with Urdu incorporating more words of Persian and Arabic origin.
- Today, Urdu is the official language of Pakistan and is also spoken widely in northern India. It is an important part of the cultural heritage of South Asia, with a rich literature spanning many genres including poetry, fiction, history and philosophy.
