### Chemistry in India

- Ancient Indian texts like Vedas, Upanishads and Ayurvedic texts had details on metals, non-metals and their properties. Rasashastra deals with processing of metals and minerals.
- Nagarjuna, an ancient Indian chemist, wrote Rasaratnakaram detailing extraction and purification methods for metals and minerals. He is considered as one of the pioneers of chemistry and metallurgy.
- Knowledge of distillation, sublimation, extraction and other methods were known as revealed in ancient texts. Various acids, salts and alkalis were produced and used.
- Works of scientists like Brahmagupta, Bhaskaracharya reveal knowledge of chemical processes and use in industries like textile printing, dyeing, tanning, etc.
- The famous Iron Pillar of Delhi which does not rust, is an example of advanced metallurgical skills as revealed by composition analysis. It is often cited as a testament to the high level of skill achieved by ancient Indian metallurgists.
- Various chemical processes and compounds were used in Ayurvedic system of medicine. Rasashastra developed as an intersection of chemistry and Ayurveda. Metals like gold, silver, copper, tin, lead, etc. were used after processing in various medicinal preparations.
- After scientific revolution in Europe, modern chemistry developed. However contribution of ancient Indian sciences including chemistry is noteworthy. Revival of interest in Indian traditional chemistry can lead to development of green chemical processes and medicines.
