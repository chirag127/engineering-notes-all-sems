### Indian Architect

- Indian architecture has a long history and is rooted in tradition and culture. It utilizes local materials and is designed to suit the local climate.
- Some of the distinctive styles of Indian architecture are:
-- Indus Valley Civilization: The ancient cities of Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro had planned settlements with baked brick houses, a grid layout, and underground drainage systems.
-- Buddhist: The stupa, a hemispherical structure containing relics, and the vihara, a monastery, were developed under the patronage of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka. The Sanchi Stupa and the caves at Ajanta and Ellora are examples.
-- Hindu temple architecture: Evolved from the stupa and is characterized by towering gopurams or gateways, mandapas or halls, and the garbhagriha or inner sanctuary. The temples are highly decorated with sculptures and carvings. Examples are the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur and the Meenakshi Amman Temple at Madurai.
-- Mughal: This style combines elements from Persian, Islamic and Indian architectural styles. Red sandstone and white marble are widely used. The Taj Mahal is a famous example. The forts, palaces and tombs built by the Mughals have grand courtyards, gardens and vaulted gateways.
- The architecture aims to symbolize and elicit a sense of awe about the universe and human's place within it. Mathematical principles and proportions underlie the designs. Regional influences and locally available materials provide uniqueness while embracing universal principles.
