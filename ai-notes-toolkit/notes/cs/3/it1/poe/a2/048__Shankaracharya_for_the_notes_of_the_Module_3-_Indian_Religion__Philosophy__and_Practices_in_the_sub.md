### Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya, also known as Adi Shankara, was an 8th century Indian philosopher and theologian. He is regarded as one of the most influential figures in the development of Hindu thought.
- He was instrumental in the revival of Hinduism through his advaita philosophy. According to advaita, the ultimate reality is non-dual (a-dvaita) and there is no distinction between the self (atman) and the ultimate Brahman.
- Shankara traveled across India and established several mathas (monasteries), which helped in the historical development and spread of advaita Vedanta, one of the classical interpretations of the dominant schools of Hindu philosophy.
- His main teachings can be summarized in the following points:

- Brahman, the eternal, infinite consciousness, is the absolute truth. The phenomenal world is an illusion (maya) and there is no duality between Brahman and Atman, the eternal Self or soul.
- Moksha or liberation can be attained by penetrating the veil of illusion and realizing the identity of Atman and Brahman through knowledge and wisdom. Devotion and actions are means to achieve knowledge.
- The Vedas are the eternal truths and the Upanishads are the final parts containing the highest wisdom. The Brahma Sutras systematize the philosophy of the Upanishads.
- He composed many important texts including commentaries on the Brahma Sutras, principal Upanishads and the Bhagavad Gita.
- His teachings and philosophies contributed to the development of Hindu thought in the medieval period and remain influential in Hinduism till today. He is revered as one of the greatest saints and sages in the Indian tradition.
