### Sufi Movement

- Sufism emerged as a mystical movement within Islam that emphasised a personal, emotional connection with the divine.
- Sufis sought unity with the divine through ascetic practices, meditation and other spiritual pursuits.
- The Chishti, Naqshbandi, Qadiri and Suhrawardi orders were the most prominent Sufi silsilahs or lineages in India.
- Prominent Sufi saints like Khwaja Muinuddin Chisti, Nizamuddin Auliya and Shah Jahan's teacher Mullah Shah influenced religious practices in India.
- Sufi ideas and practices influenced devotional movements like the Bhakti movement as they emphasised personal devotion, mysticism and equality beyond caste and religious distinctions.
- Sufi dargahs or shrines became important cultural and religious sites cutting across religious boundaries as people from different faiths visited them.
- The syncretic culture of Sufism declined with the increasing Islamic orthodoxy from the 18th century and the subsequent emphasis on a separate Islamic identity in colonial and post-colonial India.
