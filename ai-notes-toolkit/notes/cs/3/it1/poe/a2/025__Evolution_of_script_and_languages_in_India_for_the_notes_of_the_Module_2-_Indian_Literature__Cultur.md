### Evolution of script and languages in India

- Indus script: The Indus Valley civilization had a script with around 400-600 signs. However, the script remains undeciphered till date.
- Brahmi script: The Brahmi script was the first Indian script which was developed in the 6th century BCE. It was developed by the speakers of Prakrit languages. The Brahmi script led to the development of many other scripts such as the Kharoshthi script, Gupta script, etc.
- Prakrit languages: The Prakrit languages such as Shauraseni, Maharashtri, and Magadhi flourished after the decline of Sanskrit. Mahavira and Buddha used the Prakrit languages to spread their teachings.
- Sanskrit: Sanskrit is one of the oldest languages in the world. It is a highly refined and standardized language with a vast corpus of literature. It was widely used for religious and philosophical discourse.
- Pali: Pali was primarily used as a sacred language of Theravada Buddhism. The earliest Buddhist scriptures were written in Pali.
- Tamil: Tamil is a Dravidian language with a rich literary tradition. The earliest extant works of Tamil literature date back to the Sangam period. The Tirukkural by Tiruvalluvar is one of the most famous works in Tamil.
- Kannada: Kannada is another major Dravidian language with a literary history of over a thousand years. The Kavirajamarga from the 9th century CE is the earliest available literary work in Kannada.
- Telugu: Telugu is also a major Dravidian language. The earliest Telugu literature can be traced to the 5th-6th centuries CE. The Telugu Mahabharata and Ramayana are some of the finest representatives of early Telugu literature.
