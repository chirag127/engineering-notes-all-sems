### Society in Ancient India

- Ancient Indian society was divided into four varnas: Brahmins (priests & teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors & rulers), Vaishyas (traders & merchants), and Shudras (laborers).
- The varna system was based on occupation and contributed to a hierarchical social order with the Brahmins at the top and the Shudras at the bottom.
- The concept of purity and pollution as well as restrictions on inter-dining and inter-marriage were an important part of the varna system.
- Outside the varna system were groups called untouchables who performed tasks considered impure like disposal of waste and corpses. They faced segregation and marginalization.
- Family and kinship ties were given a lot of importance in ancient Indian society. Joint families were common where several generations lived together.
- Women faced many restrictions and were expected to be devoted to their husbands and household duties. Though, some women like Gargi and Maitreyi are mentioned as taking part in philosophical debates.
- There were rites of passage marking significant transitions in life such as birth, marriage, and death. Many of these continue to be observed in parts of India today.
- The caste system became rigid over time and lacked mobility. It contributed to social inequality that still persists in parts of India today. There have been many social reform movements aimed at abolishing the caste system.
