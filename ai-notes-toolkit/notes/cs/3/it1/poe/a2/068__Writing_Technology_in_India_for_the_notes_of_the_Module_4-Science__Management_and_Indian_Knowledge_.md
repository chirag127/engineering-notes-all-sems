### Writing Technology in India

- The Indus script is one of the earliest writing systems developed in ancient India. The mature Harappan writing system dates back to around 3000 BCE.
- The Brahmi script was developed in the 6th century BCE and was the first script to be phonetically regularized, where one symbol represents one sound. It was the first script used to write several Indian languages.
- The Gupta script evolved from Brahmi and was used from around the 4th century CE during the Gupta Empire. It led to the development of several descendant scripts such as Nagari, Bengali, Assamese, Oriya, Tamil, etc. which are used to write modern Indian languages.
- Kharosthi was a script used in ancient India and Central Asia from the 4th century BCE to the 4th century CE. It was invented in Gandhara and was used to record Gandhari and Sanskrit. It led to the evolution of the Gurmukhi, Kashmiri, and Tibetan scripts.
- Indian mathematicians and astronomers made significant advances and discoveries in mathematics, especially in arithmetic, algebra, geometry, and trigonometry. They also developed concepts like fractions, decimal number system and the concept of zero. This enabled advancements in computing and the creation of scripts like Devanagari.
- The Kerala School of Astronomy and Mathematics discovered special functions and the infinite series for trigonometric functions in the 14th-16th centuries CE. This led to developments in geometry and computations that enabled advancements in numerical analysis and approximation theory.
