### Brahmi Script

- Brahmi script is the earliest writing system developed in India around the mid-1st millennium BCE.
- It was an abugida or alphasyllabary writing system in which each letter stands for a consonant, which carries an inherent vowel.
- Brahmi script was used in major languages and dialects of ancient India including Prakrit, Sanskrit, and Dravidian languages.
- The Brahmi script evolved into a number of other scripts including Gupta script, Nagari script, and Southern Brahmi or Tamili scripts.
- The Brahmi script was deciphered in 1837 by James Prinsep, an archaeologist, philologist, and official of the East India Company.
- The edicts of Ashoka were the key to deciphering the Brahmi script as they contained the Brahmi script along with the Prakrit language, and were located in a very large region where the script was used.
- Brahmi is very important in the study of ancient Indian history as it was used to write some of the earliest extant literature such as the Edicts of Ashoka and other ancient inscriptions and manuscripts including Buddhist and Jain canonical literature.
