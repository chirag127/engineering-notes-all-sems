### Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century

- The socio-religious reform movement of 19th century was a wave of reforming activities that aimed to modernize and rationalize the Indian society and culture.
- The main causes of the movement were:
  - The spread of western education and liberal ideas that exposed the Indians to the values of democracy, humanism, equality and nationalism.
  - The contact with the British rule and the Christian missionaries that challenged the traditional authority of the Hindu priests and scriptures.
  - The social evils and practices that oppressed the women, the lower castes and the poor, such as sati, child marriage, female infanticide, polygamy, untouchability, etc.
  - The revival of the ancient Indian heritage and the pride in the glorious past that inspired the reformers to reform the Hindu religion and society according to the Vedas and the Upanishads.
- The main types of the movement were:
  - The Brahmo Samaj, founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828, that rejected idolatry, caste system, rituals, priesthood and advocated monotheism, social equality, women's education and widow remarriage.
  - The Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867, that followed the Brahmo Samaj's principles and also worked for the upliftment of the depressed classes, the abolition of child marriage and the promotion of inter-caste marriages.
  - The Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875, that denounced polytheism, idolatry, rituals, priesthood, animal sacrifice and advocated the authority of the Vedas, the doctrine of karma and reincarnation, the equality of all castes and the emancipation of women.
  - The Ramakrishna Mission, founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897, that propagated the philosophy of Vedanta, the harmony of all religions, the service of humanity as the service of God and the ideal of renunciation and self-realization.
  - The Theosophical Society, founded by Madame Blavatsky and Colonel Olcott in 1875, that promoted the study of the ancient Indian scriptures, the occult sciences, the universal brotherhood of mankind and the revival of the Hindu religion and culture.
  - The Aligarh Movement, founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1866, that aimed to reform the Muslim society and culture by promoting western education, scientific outlook, social reforms and political awareness among the Muslims.
- The main impacts of the movement were:
  - The movement stimulated the growth of Indian nationalism and the awakening of the masses to their rights and duties.
  - The movement contributed to the social and religious reforms that improved the condition of women, the lower castes and the poor.
  - The movement fostered the development of literature, art, education, science and philosophy in India.
  - The movement created a new class of intellectuals, leaders and reformers who shaped the modern India.
