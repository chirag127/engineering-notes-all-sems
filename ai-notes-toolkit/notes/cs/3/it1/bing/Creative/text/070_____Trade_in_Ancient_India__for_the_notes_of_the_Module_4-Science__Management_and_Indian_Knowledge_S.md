### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange between different regions and civilizations.
- India has a long history of trade since the Harappan civilization (c. 2600-1900 BCE), which had extensive contacts with Mesopotamia, Egypt, and Central Asia. The Harappans traded mainly gold, copper, ivory, beads, textiles, and pottery. 
- After the decline of the Harappan civilization, trade continued in the post-Vedic period (c. 1500-600 BCE) with the rise of new urban centers such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura. These cities became hubs of political, religious, and economic activities, and facilitated trade within India and with foreign lands.
- Trade in the post-Vedic period involved various commodities such as grains, spices, gems, metals, pearls, perfumes, silk, cotton, wool, and leather. Trade was also influenced by the emergence of new religious movements such as Buddhism and Jainism, which promoted non-violence, charity, and ethical conduct among merchants and traders.
- Trade in ancient India reached its peak during the Mauryan Empire (c. 321-185 BCE), which unified most of the Indian subcontinent under a centralized administration. The Mauryan emperor Ashoka (r. c. 268-232 BCE) expanded trade networks and diplomatic relations with neighboring kingdoms and distant regions such as Greece, Egypt, Persia, and Southeast Asia. He also patronized Buddhism and sent missionaries to spread his message of peace and dharma (righteousness) across the world.
- Trade in ancient India declined after the fall of the Mauryan Empire, but revived during the Kushan Empire (c. 30-375 CE), which controlled parts of northern India, Central Asia, and China. The Kushans were instrumental in promoting trade along the Silk Road, a network of land and sea routes that connected China, India, Persia, and the Mediterranean. The Kushans traded silk, spices, textiles, gold, silver, and Buddhist art and literature with various cultures and civilizations.
- Trade in ancient India flourished again during the Gupta Empire (c. 320-550 CE), which is considered the golden age of Indian culture and science. The Guptas maintained a stable and prosperous economy, and encouraged trade and commerce within India and with foreign lands. The Guptas traded cotton, silk, spices, ivory, pearls, and metals with China, Southeast Asia, Sri Lanka, and the Roman Empire. They also supported arts, literature, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and architecture, and patronized Hinduism and Buddhism.
- Trade in ancient India declined after the collapse of the Gupta Empire, but continued in the medieval period (c. 600-1200 CE) with the rise of new regional kingdoms and dynasties. Trade in this period was influenced by the spread of Islam, which brought new cultural and religious influences to India. Trade also involved new commodities such as horses, slaves, sugar, and paper. Trade in this period was mainly conducted by sea, as India became a major maritime power and established contacts with Arabia, Persia, Africa, and Southeast Asia.
