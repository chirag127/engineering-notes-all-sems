### Slavery in India

- Slavery in India is a historical and contemporary phenomenon that has existed in various forms and periods, from ancient times to the present day.
- Slavery in India can be broadly categorized into two types: domestic slavery and bonded labor. Domestic slavery refers to the practice of owning and exploiting household servants, often for sexual purposes. Bonded labor refers to the practice of forcing workers to repay debts through labor, often in harsh and hazardous conditions.
- Slavery in India was prevalent in the ancient and medieval periods, especially under the caste system, which divided society into hierarchical groups based on birth, occupation, and ritual purity. The lowest castes, such as the Dalits or the untouchables, were often subjected to slavery and discrimination by the higher castes.
- Slavery in India intensified during the Muslim invasions and rule from the 11th century onwards, when millions of Hindus were captured and enslaved by the Muslim rulers and traders. The slaves were used for various purposes, such as soldiers, concubines, artisans, and laborers. The Muslim law, known as the Fatawa-i Alamgiri, codified the rights and duties of the slave owners and the slaves, and denied the slaves any legal or social status.
- Slavery in India continued during the colonial era, when the British and other European powers exploited the Indian population for their economic and political interests. The British enacted various laws and policies that facilitated the extraction of resources and labor from India, such as the Permanent Settlement, the Zamindari system, the Ryotwari system, the Mahalwari system, and the indentured labor system. The British also participated in the Indian Ocean slave trade, which involved the transportation of Indian slaves to various parts of the world, such as Africa, the Middle East, and the Americas.
- Slavery in India was officially abolished by the British in 1843, following the efforts of the abolitionist movement and the Indian reformers. However, slavery in India did not end completely, as many forms of exploitation and oppression persisted in the post-colonial period. The Indian Constitution of 1950 outlawed slavery and discrimination, and granted equal rights and freedoms to all citizens. However, the implementation and enforcement of these laws have been weak and ineffective, due to various factors, such as poverty, illiteracy, corruption, casteism, and patriarchy.
- Slavery in India is still a widespread and serious problem in the 21st century, with an estimated 8 million people living in modern slavery in India, according to the Global Slavery Index of 2018. Modern slavery in India takes various forms, such as forced labor, bonded labor, child labor, sex trafficking, domestic servitude, and forced marriage. Modern slavery in India affects various sectors and industries, such as agriculture, mining, construction, manufacturing, domestic work, and entertainment. Modern slavery in India also affects various groups and communities, such as women, children, Dalits, Adivasis, and religious minorities.
