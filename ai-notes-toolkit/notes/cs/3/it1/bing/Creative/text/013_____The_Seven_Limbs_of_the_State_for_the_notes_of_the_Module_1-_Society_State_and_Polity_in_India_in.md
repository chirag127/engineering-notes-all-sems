### The Seven Limbs of the State

- The seven limbs of the state (saptanga) are a political concept given by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra, which deals with the art of governance and statecraft .
- The seven limbs are: 
  - King (swami): The king is the supreme authority and the head of the state. He is responsible for the peace, justice and stability of the state. He is independent and has the final say in all matters .
  - Amatya (minister or bureaucrat): The amatya is the king's adviser and assistant. He helps the king in administration, policy-making and diplomacy. He is loyal, efficient and skilled in various fields .
  - Janapada (territory or population): The janapada is the land and the people of the state. It is the source of revenue and manpower for the state. It should be fertile, prosperous and well-protected .
  - Durga (fort or capital): The durga is the fortified city or the capital of the state. It is the center of political, economic and cultural activities. It should be strong, secure and well-equipped .
  - Kosa (treasure or wealth): The kosa is the financial resource of the state. It is the income and expenditure of the state. It should be sufficient, well-managed and wisely spent .
  - Danda (coercive authority or army): The danda is the military force of the state. It is the instrument of defense and offense. It should be powerful, disciplined and loyal  .
  - Mitra (ally or friend): The mitra is the external support of the state. It is the friendly or neutral state that helps the state in times of need. It should be reliable, trustworthy and mutually beneficial .
- According to Kautilya, the seven limbs of the state are interdependent and interrelated. The success or failure of the state depends on the proper functioning and coordination of the seven limbs  .
