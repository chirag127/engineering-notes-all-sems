### Martial Arts Traditions

- Martial arts are systems of codified practices and traditions of training for combat. They are used for self-defense, physical fitness, mental and spiritual development, entertainment, and cultural preservation.
- India has a rich and diverse history of martial arts, dating back to the Vedic period. Some of the oldest references to martial arts can be found in the Dhanurveda, a segment of the Vedas that deals with archery and warfare.
- India's martial arts traditions have been influenced by various factors, such as geography, climate, culture, religion, and foreign invasions. Some of the martial arts have evolved from indigenous forms, while others have borrowed elements from neighboring or distant regions.
- India's martial arts can be broadly classified into two categories: armed and unarmed. Armed martial arts involve the use of weapons, such as swords, spears, daggers, shields, bows, and axes. Unarmed martial arts rely on the use of the body, such as punches, kicks, locks, throws, and grappling.
- Some of the most prominent and ancient martial arts forms of India are:

  - Kalaripayattu: Originating from Kerala, it is considered to be one of the oldest and most comprehensive martial arts in the world. It involves the use of various weapons, such as the urumi (a flexible sword), the katar (a dagger), the paricha (a shield), and the otta (a curved stick). It also incorporates unarmed techniques, such as strikes, blocks, jumps, and flips. Kalaripayattu is based on the concept of vital points (marmas) on the human body, which can be targeted to inflict pain or death. Kalaripayattu is also known for its healing and therapeutic aspects, such as massage and herbal medicine.
  - Silambam: Originating from Tamil Nadu, it is a martial art that focuses on the use of the bamboo staff as the primary weapon. It also employs other weapons, such as the aruval (a sickle), the surul vaal (a spring sword), and the kuttu katai (a knuckleduster). Silambam is known for its fluid and graceful movements, as well as its speed and agility. Silambam is influenced by the principles of yoga and the Tamil philosophy of the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, and ether).
  - Gatka: Originating from Punjab, it is a martial art that developed during the Sikh wars against the Mughals and the British. It involves the use of various weapons, such as the kirpan (a sword), the chakram (a circular throwing weapon), the barcha (a spear), and the lathi (a stick). It also incorporates unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, and locks. Gatka is based on the concept of shakti (divine energy) and bhakti (devotion), and is often performed as a form of spiritual exercise or ritual.
  - Thang Ta: Originating from Manipur, it is a martial art that derives its name from the words thang (sword) and ta (spear). It involves the use of various weapons, such as the thang (a single-edged sword), the ta (a spear), the khonglon (a shield), and the pao (a dagger). It also incorporates unarmed techniques, such as strikes, kicks, and blocks. Thang Ta is based on the concept of yek salai (one lineage), which refers to the transmission of the art from master to disciple. Thang Ta is also known for its ritual and ceremonial aspects, such as the sankirtana (a dance-drama) and the leela (a martial dance).
  - Kushti: Originating from various parts of India, it is a form of traditional wrestling that involves grappling, throwing, and pinning the opponent. It is also known as pehlwani, malla-yuddha, or vajra-mushti, depending on the region and style. Kushti is practiced on a circular earthen pit, called the akhara, which is sprinkled with oil, water, and clay. Kushti is based on the concept of guru-shishya (teacher-student) relationship, which involves strict discipline, respect, and loyalty. Kushti is also known for its physical and mental benefits, such as strength, stamina, and concentration.
