### Astronomy in India

- Astronomy has a long history in the Indian subcontinent, stretching from pre-historic to modern times.
- Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilization or earlier, when ancient Indians observed the movements of the sun, moon, planets and stars, and developed calendars and festivals based on them .
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, the sacred texts of Hinduism. The Vedanga texts, such as the Jyotisha Vedanga, contain astronomical knowledge related to the timing of rituals and the calculation of the lunar and solar cycles.
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, Sanskrit translations of Greek texts disseminated from the 2nd century. These texts introduced concepts such as the zodiac, the ecliptic, the epicycles and the equinoxes to Indian astronomy.
- The classical period of Indian astronomy, from the 5th to the 15th centuries CE, saw the development of various schools of astronomical thought, such as the Siddhanta, the Aryabhatiya, the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, the Suryasiddhanta and the Panchasiddhantika . These texts contain sophisticated mathematical and astronomical theories, such as the heliocentric model, the elliptical orbits, the calculation of pi, the sine and cosine functions, the precession of the equinoxes, the lunar and solar eclipses, and the estimation of the size and distance of the earth and the moon .
- Indian astronomers also made significant contributions to observational astronomy, such as the construction of astronomical instruments, the measurement of celestial phenomena, and the recording of astronomical data . Some of the notable Indian astronomers of this period include Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Lalla, Sripati, Bhaskaracharya and Madhava .
- The medieval period of Indian astronomy, from the 16th to the 18th centuries CE, witnessed the interaction of Indian astronomy with Islamic and European astronomy, as well as the decline of the indigenous astronomical tradition due to the political and social changes in India. Some of the prominent Indian astronomers of this period include Nilakantha Somayaji, Jyeshtadeva, Munisvara, Sawai Jai Singh and Samanta Chandra Sekhara.
- The modern period of Indian astronomy, from the 19th century onwards, saw the revival of interest in Indian astronomy, as well as the development of modern scientific astronomy in India. Some of the notable Indian astronomers of this period include Mahendra Lal Sircar, C.V. Raman, Meghnad Saha, Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, Homi Bhabha, Vikram Sarabhai, Jayant Narlikar and U.R. Rao.
- Today, India has a vibrant astronomical community, with several research institutes, observatories, telescopes and satellites dedicated to the study of the cosmos. Some of the major achievements of Indian astronomy in recent times include the discovery of the Chandrasekhar limit, the participation in the International Solar Alliance, the launch of the Aryabhata satellite, the Chandrayaan and Mangalyaan missions, and the construction of the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope and the Thirty Meter Telescope .
