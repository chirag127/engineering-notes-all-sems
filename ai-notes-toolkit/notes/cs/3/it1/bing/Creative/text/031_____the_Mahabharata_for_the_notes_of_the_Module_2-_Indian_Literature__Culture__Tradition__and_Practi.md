### The Mahabharata

- The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, along with the Ramayana. It is valued for its literary merit and its religious inspiration. It is an important source of information on the development of Hinduism between 400 BCE and 200 CE. It is regarded by Hindus as both a text about dharma (Hindu moral law) and a history of their ancestors .
- The Mahabharata consists of a mass of mythological and didactic material arranged around a central heroic narrative that tells of the struggle for sovereignty between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, who belong to the Bharata dynasty .
- The main story of the Mahabharata revolves around the Kurukshetra War, a great war that lasted for 18 days and involved many kings and warriors from different regions of India. The war was fought on the plain of Kurukshetra, near the city of Hastinapura, the capital of the Kauravas. The war was triggered by the rivalry and jealousy between the Kauravas, led by Duryodhana, and the Pandavas, led by Yudhishthira, over the inheritance of the throne of Hastinapura .
- The Mahabharata also contains several smaller stories and subplots that are interwoven into the main narrative. Some of these stories are about the origins and adventures of various gods, goddesses, sages, demons, and heroes. Some of these stories are also about the moral and spiritual teachings of Hinduism, such as the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between the god Krishna and the Pandava prince Arjuna on the eve of the war .
- The Mahabharata is a very long and complex epic, with over 74,000 verses, divided into 18 books, or parvas. It is said to have been composed by the sage Vyasa, who dictated it to the god Ganesha, who wrote it down. However, the Mahabharata also acknowledges that it has been revised and interpolated by various authors and editors over time. The Mahabharata is also accompanied by the Harivamsa, an appendix that narrates the life of Krishna and his descendants .
