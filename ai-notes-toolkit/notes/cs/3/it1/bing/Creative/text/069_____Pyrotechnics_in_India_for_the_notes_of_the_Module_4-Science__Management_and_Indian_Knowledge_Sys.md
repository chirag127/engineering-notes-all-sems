### Pyrotechnics in India

- Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating and using fireworks and other explosive devices for entertainment, military, or industrial purposes.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows existed as a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events and special occasions like weddings.
- Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of the Suryavamsi Gajapati dynasty of Odisha.
- Fireworks were also used in warfare by Indian armies, such as rockets, mines, grenades, and cannons. The Mysore rockets, developed by Tipu Sultan and his father Hyder Ali, were the first iron-cased rockets used in combat.
- Fireworks are an integral part of many Indian festivals, especially Diwali, the festival of lights. They are also used in weddings, birthdays, anniversaries, and other celebrations.
- Fireworks are a major source of air pollution in India, especially during Diwali. They emit harmful gases, particulate matter, heavy metals, and noise, which affect the health and well-being of humans, animals, and plants.
- In 2018, the Supreme Court of India banned the sale and use of most fireworks, except for a few "green crackers" that have reduced emission levels and noise. The court also imposed time limits and designated areas for bursting crackers.
- The government and the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) have developed and approved several types of green crackers, such as Safe Water Releaser (SWAS), Safe Thermite Cracker (STAR), and Safe Minimal Aluminium (SAFAL).
- The green crackers are made of eco-friendly materials and chemicals, and have a unique identification number and a QR code to ensure their authenticity and traceability.
- The green crackers are expected to reduce the emission of harmful pollutants by 30-35%, and the noise level by 5-10 decibels.
- The green crackers are also cheaper and safer than conventional crackers, and have a longer shelf life.
- The green crackers are still in the process of mass production and distribution, and face challenges such as lack of awareness, availability, and enforcement.
- Pyrotechnics in India is a dynamic and evolving field that reflects the cultural, historical, and scientific aspects of the country. It also poses environmental and social challenges that require innovation and regulation.
