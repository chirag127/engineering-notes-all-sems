### Āshrama or the Stages of Life

- Āshrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras.
- The four āshramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Gṛhastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate)  .
- The Āshrama system is one facet of the Dharma concept in Hinduism  .
- The Āshrama system is believed to have been prevalent since the 5th century B.C.E. in Hindu society.
- The Āshrama system aims to guide the individual through four stages of spiritual development, each with its own duties, responsibilities, and goals  .
- The four āshramas are briefly described as follows:

  - Brahmacharya: The celibate student stage, marked by chastity, devotion, and obedience to one’s teacher. It lasts until around age 25, during which the student learns the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, and other subjects .
  - Gṛhastha: The householder stage, requiring marriage, the begetting of children, sustaining one’s family and society, and performing rituals. It is the most important and longest stage, lasting until around age 50 .
  - Vanaprastha: The retired or forest dweller stage, marked by withdrawal from worldly attachments, renunciation of material possessions, and pursuit of spiritual knowledge. It lasts until around age 75, during which the individual may live in a forest hut or a pilgrimage site with or without the spouse .
  - Sannyasa: The renunciate or ascetic stage, marked by complete detachment from worldly affairs, abandonment of family and social ties, and dedication to the quest for liberation (moksha). It is the final and highest stage, during which the individual may wander as a mendicant or live in a monastery .

- The Āshrama system is not rigid or compulsory, and individuals may choose to follow or skip any stage according to their personal inclination, circumstances, and spiritual progress .
- The Āshrama system is also not exclusive or uniform, and there are variations and alternatives in different Hindu traditions, sects, and regions .
- The Āshrama system is a way of organizing and understanding the human life cycle in Hinduism, and it reflects the values, ideals, and goals of Hindu culture and religion .
