### Evolutionary Theory

- Evolutionary theory is a sociological perspective that explains how societies change from simple to complex forms over time.
- Evolutionary theory is based on the assumption that societies gradually change from simple beginnings into even more complex forms.
- Early sociologists, beginning with Auguste Comte, believed that human societies evolve in a unilinear way - that is, in one line of development.
- According to them, social change meant progress toward something better.
- This evolutionary view of social change was highly influenced by Charles Darwin's theory of organic evolution.
- Those who were fascinated by this theory applied it to the human society and argued that societies must have evolved from the simple and primitive to that of too complex and advanced, such as the western society.
- Some of the main proponents of evolutionary theory were Herbert Spencer, Lewis Henry Morgan, Edward Burnett Tylor, and Leslie White.
- Spencer coined the phrase "survival of the fittest" and applied it to social phenomena, such as competition, cooperation, and conflict .
- Morgan classified societies into three stages: savagery, barbarism, and civilization, based on their level of technology, family structure, and political organization.
- Tylor defined culture as a complex whole that includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society.
- He also proposed a unilinear scheme of cultural evolution, from animism to polytheism to monotheism.
- White emphasized the role of energy and technology in social evolution, and argued that the amount of energy harnessed per capita per year is a measure of cultural advancement.
- Evolutionary theory has been criticized for being ethnocentric, deterministic, and teleological .
- Ethnocentric means that it judges other cultures by the standards of one's own culture, and assumes that the western culture is the most advanced and superior .
- Deterministic means that it implies that social change is inevitable and beyond human control .
- Teleological means that it implies that social change has a predetermined goal or direction .
- Evolutionary theory has also been challenged by historical and empirical evidence that shows that social change is not always linear, progressive, or uniform .
- Despite its limitations, evolutionary theory has contributed to the understanding of social change and development, and has influenced other sociological perspectives, such as functionalism, conflict theory, and modernization theory .
