### Modern religious practices in India

- India is a secular country with a diverse and pluralistic religious landscape. According to the 2011 census, the major religions practiced in India are Hinduism (79.8%), Islam (14.2%), Christianity (2.3%), Sikhism (1.7%), Buddhism (0.7%) and Jainism (0.4%). 
- Religion has historically influenced Indian society on a political, cultural and economic level. There is a sense of pride associated with the country’s rich religious history as the traditions of Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism and Jainism all emerged out of India.
- Religion also plays a significant role in the lives of most Indians, as they often express their faith through various rituals, festivals, pilgrimages, ceremonies, and charitable activities. Indians overwhelmingly say that religion is very important in their life (80%), and that they regularly visit their houses of worship (77%).
- Some of the common religious practices and beliefs that are shared by different religious groups in India are:
  - Belief in karma, or the idea that one’s actions have consequences in this life and the next.
  - Belief in reincarnation, or the cycle of birth and rebirth that is determined by one’s karma.
  - Belief in moksha, or liberation from the cycle of reincarnation, which can be achieved through various paths such as devotion, knowledge, or ethical action.
  - Belief in the existence of multiple gods and goddesses, who are often manifestations of a supreme deity or principle.
  - Belief in the sacredness of the cow, which is revered as a symbol of life, fertility, and nonviolence.
  - Belief in the importance of the guru, or the spiritual teacher, who guides the disciple on the path to enlightenment.
  - Belief in the value of ahimsa, or nonviolence, which is a core principle of Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism.
  - Belief in the practice of yoga, or the physical, mental, and spiritual discipline that aims to harmonize the body, mind, and soul.
  - Belief in the practice of meditation, or the technique of focusing the mind on a single object, thought, or mantra, to achieve a state of awareness and calmness.
  - Belief in the practice of puja, or the act of worshiping a deity or a sacred object, by offering prayers, flowers, incense, food, and other items.
  - Belief in the practice of fasting, or abstaining from food or drink for a certain period of time, as a way of expressing devotion, penance, or gratitude.
  - Belief in the practice of charity, or giving alms to the poor, the needy, or the religious institutions, as a way of expressing compassion, generosity, or merit.
  - Belief in the practice of pilgrimage, or visiting a holy place, such as a temple, a shrine, a river, or a mountain, as a way of expressing devotion, seeking blessings, or fulfilling a vow.
  - Belief in the practice of festival, or celebrating a religious or cultural event, such as a birthday, a new year, a harvest, or a victory, by performing rituals, exchanging gifts, or enjoying food and entertainment.
