### Buddhism

- Buddhism is an Indian religion or philosophy that developed from the teachings of the Buddha (Sanskrit: "Awakened One"), a teacher who lived in northern India between the mid-6th and mid-4th centuries BCE .
- Buddhism is also known as Buddha Dharma and Dharmavinaya, which means "doctrines and disciplines".
- Followers of Buddhism, called Buddhists, referred to themselves as Sakyan-s or Sakyabhiksu in ancient India.
- Buddhism is based on the Four Noble Truths, which are: the truth of suffering, the truth of the origin of suffering, the truth of the cessation of suffering, and the truth of the path to the cessation of suffering .
- The path to the cessation of suffering is the Noble Eightfold Path, which consists of right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration .
- Buddhism also teaches the concept of karma, which is the law of cause and effect that determines the quality of one's rebirth .
- Buddhism also teaches the concept of anatman, which means "no-self" or "no-soul", and implies that there is no permanent or unchanging essence in any phenomenon .
- Buddhism also teaches the concept of nirvana, which is the ultimate goal of liberation from the cycle of birth and death (samsara) .
- Buddhism has many branches and schools, such as Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana, Zen, Pure Land, and Tibetan Buddhism, each with its own doctrines, practices, and traditions .
- Buddhism has spread to many countries and regions, such as China, Japan, Korea, Southeast Asia, Tibet, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, and the West, and has influenced many aspects of culture, art, literature, ethics, and philosophy .
