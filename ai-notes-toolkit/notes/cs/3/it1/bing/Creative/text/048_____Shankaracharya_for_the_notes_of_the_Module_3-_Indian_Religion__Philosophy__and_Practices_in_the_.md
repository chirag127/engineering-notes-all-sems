### Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya is a title given to the heads of monastic institutions (mathas) in the Advaita Vedanta tradition of Hinduism, which follows the teachings of the 8th-century philosopher and theologian Shankara.
- Shankara, also known as Adi Shankara or Shankaracharya, is considered the most influential exponent of the Advaita Vedanta school, which asserts the non-dual nature of reality and the identity of the individual self (atman) with the supreme self (brahman) .
- Shankara is credited with reviving the Vedic tradition and establishing the authority of the Upanishads, the ancient scriptures that expound the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta .
- Shankara wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, as well as original works such as the Vivekachudamani and the Upadesasahasri, which explain the core principles of Advaita Vedanta .
- Shankara also founded four mathas in the four corners of India, namely Sringeri in the south, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east, and Badrinath in the north, to propagate his teachings and preserve the Vedic culture .
- Shankara is revered as a saint and a teacher by Hindus of various sects and traditions, and his works have influenced the development of various schools of Indian philosophy, religion, and culture .
