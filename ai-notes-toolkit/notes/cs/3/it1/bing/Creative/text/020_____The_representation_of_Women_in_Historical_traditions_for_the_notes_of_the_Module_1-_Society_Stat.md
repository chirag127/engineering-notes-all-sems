### The representation of Women in Historical traditions for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Women in India have played various roles in different historical periods, from ancient times to the modern era.
- Women during the **Vedic period** enjoyed high status with men in all aspects of life. They were educated, participated in religious ceremonies, and had the freedom to choose their husbands.
- Women in the **epic period** were depicted as ideal wives and mothers, such as Sita, Draupadi, and Kunti. They also influenced men on religious and social matters. However, they also faced challenges such as polygamy, child marriage, and widowhood.
- Women in the **medieval period** witnessed a decline in their status due to the invasion of foreign rulers, the rise of patriarchy, and the imposition of purdah. They were confined to the domestic sphere, denied education, and subjected to sati, jauhar, and devadasi practices.
- Women in the **colonial period** experienced both oppression and emancipation. On one hand, they suffered from social evils such as child marriage, dowry, and female infanticide. On the other hand, they also benefited from the social reforms initiated by the British and the Indian nationalists, such as the abolition of sati, the widow remarriage act, and the education of women.
- Women in the **modern period** have made significant contributions to various fields such as politics, science, arts, sports, and social work. They have also fought for their rights and equality, such as the suffrage movement, the women's reservation bill, and the anti-dowry law. Some of the notable women in modern India are Ahilyabai Holkar, Abala Bose, Amrita Sher-Gil, Anandi Gopal Joshi, Arati Saha, and many more.
- Women in India have also expressed their identity and culture through their attire, such as the sari, which has a long and diverse history. The sari is a draped garment that can be worn in various styles, depending on the region, community, and occasion. The sari reflects the richness and diversity of Indian women's traditions and heritage.
