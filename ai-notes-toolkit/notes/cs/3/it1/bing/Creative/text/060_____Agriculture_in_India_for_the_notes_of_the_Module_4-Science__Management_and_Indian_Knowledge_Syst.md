### Agriculture in India

- Agriculture is one of the major sectors of the Indian economy, contributing about 16% of the gross domestic product (GDP) and employing about 58% of the population.
- India has a diverse agro-climatic zone, ranging from tropical to temperate, and produces a variety of crops, such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugarcane, cotton, jute, tea, coffee, rubber, etc.
- India is the world's largest producer of many fresh fruits, milk, pulses, and spices, and the second-largest producer of rice, wheat, sugarcane, cotton, and vegetables .
- India is also the world's largest consumer of food, with a population of about 1.4 billion and a per capita food consumption of about 2,400 kcal per day.
- Agriculture in India is largely dependent on the monsoon rainfall, which accounts for about 70% of the annual precipitation. However, the monsoon is erratic and uneven, causing droughts and floods in different regions.
- Agriculture in India faces many challenges, such as low productivity, soil degradation, water scarcity, climate change, pests and diseases, market fluctuations, lack of infrastructure, credit, insurance, and extension services, etc.
- Agriculture in India also has many opportunities, such as increasing demand, diversification, value addition, organic farming, agro-forestry, agro-tourism, biotechnology, etc.
- Agriculture in India is influenced by the policies and programs of the central and state governments, such as the minimum support price (MSP), the public distribution system (PDS), the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY), the National Food Security Mission (NFSM), etc.
- Agriculture in India is also affected by the global and regional trade agreements, such as the World Trade Organization (WTO), the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), the South Asian Free Trade Area (SAFTA), etc.
- Agriculture in India is a dynamic and evolving sector, which requires constant innovation, adaptation, and transformation to meet the changing needs and challenges of the society, economy, and environment.
