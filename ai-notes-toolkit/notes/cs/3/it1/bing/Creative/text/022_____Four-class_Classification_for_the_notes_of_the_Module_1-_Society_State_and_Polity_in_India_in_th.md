### Four-class Classification

- The four-class classification, also known as the varna system, is a social hierarchy that divides the society into four main categories: Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers).
- The four-class classification is based on the concept of dharma, or the moral duty and obligation of each individual according to their varna. Each varna has its own set of rules, norms, and expectations that govern their conduct, occupation, and interactions with other varnas.
- The four-class classification is also influenced by the concept of karma, or the law of cause and effect that determines the quality of one's life and rebirth. According to this belief, one's actions in the present life will affect their future lives, and one's varna is determined by their past deeds and merits.
- The four-class classification is not a rigid or fixed system, but rather a dynamic and flexible one that allows for mobility and change. There are various factors that can affect one's varna, such as education, occupation, wealth, lineage, region, and personal choice. There are also sub-categories and mixed categories within each varna, such as jatis (castes), gotras (clans), and sampradayas (sects).
- The four-class classification is not a universal or homogeneous system, but rather a diverse and heterogeneous one that varies across time and space. There are different interpretations and implementations of the varna system in different regions, periods, and contexts of Indian history and culture. There are also alternative and oppositional views and movements that challenge and critique the varna system, such as Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Dalitism.
