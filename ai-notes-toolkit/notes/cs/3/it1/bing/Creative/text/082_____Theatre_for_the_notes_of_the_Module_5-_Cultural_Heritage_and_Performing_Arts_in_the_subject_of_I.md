### Theatre

- Theatre is one of the most ancient forms of art and expression in India, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Theatre in India began as a narrative form, in which recitation, singing and dancing were integral elements. The encompassing of all forms of literature and fine arts into its physical presentation and the emphasis on narrative elements made the classical Indian drama theatrical right from the beginning.
- The earliest evidence of theatre in India is found in the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, which contain hymns, rituals and dialogues that were performed by priests and sages. The Natya Shastra, a treatise on dramaturgy attributed to Bharata Muni, is the most comprehensive and authoritative source of classical Indian theatre. It covers various aspects of theatre, such as the origin, purpose, structure, genres, styles, techniques, costumes, makeup, music, dance, acting, directing and criticism of theatre .
- The classical Indian theatre was mainly divided into two types: Sanskrit theatre and folk theatre. Sanskrit theatre was based on the literary works of Sanskrit poets and playwrights, such as Kalidasa, Bhasa, Shudraka, Vishakhadatta and Harsha. Sanskrit theatre followed the rules and conventions of the Natya Shastra and was performed in royal courts and temples. Sanskrit theatre was known for its elaborate and sophisticated language, plot, characters, dialogue, poetry, music and dance. Some of the famous Sanskrit plays are Abhijnana Shakuntalam, Malavikagnimitram, Mrichchhakatika, Mudrarakshasa and Ratnavali .
- Folk theatre was based on the local traditions, myths, legends, stories and customs of different regions and communities of India. Folk theatre was more flexible and spontaneous than Sanskrit theatre and was performed in open spaces, such as streets, fields, courtyards and village squares. Folk theatre was known for its lively and colorful presentation, humor, satire, social commentary, improvisation, audience participation and use of masks, puppets and props. Some of the famous folk theatre forms are Nautanki, Tamasha, Jatra, Bhavai, Yakshagana, Koodiyattam, Kathakali and Chhau .
- The modern theatre in India emerged during the colonial period, under the influence of the Western theatre and the socio-political movements of the time. The modern theatre in India comprises mainly of English, Hindi and Hinglish (comprising of a mix of Hindi and English dialogues) plays. English theatre was brought to India during the British rule and was watched mostly by art connoisseurs of the rich, upper class. English theatre was influenced by the works of Shakespeare, Ibsen, Shaw, Wilde, Brecht and Beckett. Some of the famous English playwrights and theatre groups in India are Rabindranath Tagore, Vijay Tendulkar, Girish Karnad, Mahesh Dattani, Naseeruddin Shah, Shyam Benegal, Alyque Padamsee and Prithvi Theatre.
- Hindi and Hinglish theatre emerged as a response to the social and political issues of the post-independence era, such as nationalism, partition, communalism, corruption, poverty, gender, caste and class. Hindi and Hinglish theatre was influenced by the works of Premchand, Mohan Rakesh, Dharamvir Bharati, Badal Sircar, Habib Tanvir, Utpal Dutt and Satyajit Ray. Some of the famous Hindi and Hinglish playwrights and theatre groups in India are Rajinder Singh Bedi, Saadat Hasan Manto, Ismat Chughtai, Manjula Padmanabhan, Mahesh Bhatt, Rajkumar Hirani, Anurag Kashyap, Dibakar Banerjee and Nukkad Natak.
- Theatre in India is a rich and diverse form of cultural heritage and performing arts, that reflects the history, society, values and beliefs of the Indian people. Theatre in India is also a medium of education, entertainment, communication and social change, that challenges, inspires and engages the audience. Theatre in India is a living and evolving art form, that adapts to the changing times and needs of the people.
