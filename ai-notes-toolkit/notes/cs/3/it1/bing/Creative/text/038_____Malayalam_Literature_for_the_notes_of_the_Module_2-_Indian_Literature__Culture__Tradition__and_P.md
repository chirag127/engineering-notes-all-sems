### Malayalam Literature

- Malayalam literature is the body of writing in the Malayalam language, which is spoken in the Indian state of Kerala and some parts of Tamil Nadu and Karnataka.
- Malayalam literature has a rich and diverse history, spanning from the 12th century to the present day. It reflects the cultural, social, religious, and political aspects of Kerala and its people.
- Some of the major genres of Malayalam literature are poetry, prose, drama, biography, travelogue, essay, and criticism. Some of the prominent literary forms are pattu (song), manipravalam (mixed language), kathakali (dance drama), thullal (satirical verse), and novel.
- Some of the notable writers and works of Malayalam literature are:

  - Ramacharitam (late 12th or early 13th century), the earliest extant literary work in Malayalam, which narrates the story of Rama in a mix of Malayalam and Sanskrit.
  - Thunchaththu Ramanujan Ezhuthachan (16th century), considered the father of Malayalam literature, who wrote Adhyathmaramayanam (a devotional retelling of the Ramayana) and Mahabharatham (a condensation of the Mahabharata) in a simplified Malayalam.
  - Kunchan Nambiar (18th century), the creator of the thullal genre, who composed humorous and satirical poems on various topics, such as social evils, religious hypocrisy, and political corruption.
  - Kumaran Asan (1873-1924), one of the modernist poets of Malayalam, who wrote lyrical and philosophical poems, such as Nalini, Leela, Karuna, and Chinthavishtayaya Sita, that explored themes of love, suffering, freedom, and social reform.
  - Vallathol Narayana Menon (1878-1958), another modernist poet of Malayalam, who founded the Kerala Kalamandalam, a cultural institution for the promotion of classical arts, and wrote patriotic and epic poems, such as Ente Gurunathan, Chitrayogam, and Magdalana Mariyam.
  - Ulloor S. Parameswara Iyer (1877-1949), a contemporary of Asan and Vallathol, who wrote poems that blended the classical and the modern, such as Umakeralam, Chithrasala, and Bhakthideepika.
  - O. N. V. Kurup (1931-2016), a Jnanpith award-winning poet of Malayalam, who wrote poems that expressed his humanist and progressive views, such as Ujjayini, Aksharam, and Bhumikku Oru Charamagitam.
  - M. T. Vasudevan Nair (born 1933), a Jnanpith award-winning novelist and screenwriter of Malayalam, who wrote realistic and psychological novels, such as Naalukettu, Asuravithu, Manju, and Randamoozham, that depicted the life and culture of Kerala.
  - Vaikom Muhammad Basheer (1908-1994), a pioneer of Malayalam fiction, who wrote short stories and novels that portrayed the common people and their struggles, such as Balyakalasakhi, Pathummayude Aadu, Mathilukal, and Shabdangal.
  - Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai (1912-1999), a Jnanpith award-winning novelist and short story writer of Malayalam, who wrote social and historical novels, such as Chemmeen, Kayar, Randidangazhi, and Thottiyude Makan, that exposed the exploitation and oppression of the marginalized classes.
  - Kamala Surayya (1934-2009), a controversial and influential poet and writer of Malayalam, who wrote poems and stories that explored her personal and sexual identity, such as Summer in Calcutta, The Sirens, My Story, and Ente Katha.
