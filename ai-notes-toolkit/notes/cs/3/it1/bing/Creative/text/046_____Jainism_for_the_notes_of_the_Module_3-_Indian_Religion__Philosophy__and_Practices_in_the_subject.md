### Jainism

Jainism is one of the oldest religions of India, with a history of more than 2500 years. It is based on the teachings of 24 spiritual leaders called tirthankaras, who attained enlightenment and liberation from the cycle of rebirth. The last and most famous of these tirthankaras was Mahavira, who lived in the 6th century BCE. Jainism is a religion of non-violence, non-absolutism, and asceticism, which aims to purify the soul and attain liberation from the bondage of karma.

Some of the main features of Jainism are:

- **Ahimsa**: This is the principle of non-violence, which means to avoid harming any living being, either by thought, word, or deed. Ahimsa is the highest virtue in Jainism, and it extends to all forms of life, including animals, plants, and even microorganisms. Jains practice vegetarianism, avoid killing insects, and wear masks to prevent inhaling microbes. They also avoid occupations that involve violence, such as hunting, fishing, or warfare.
- **Anekantavada**: This is the principle of non-absolutism, which means to acknowledge the multiplicity of perspectives and the relativity of truth. Anekantavada teaches that no single viewpoint can capture the whole reality, and that different viewpoints can be valid in different contexts. Jains respect the diversity of opinions and beliefs, and avoid dogmatism and fanaticism. They also practice a form of dialectical reasoning called syadvada, which uses seven modes of expression to qualify any statement with a degree of uncertainty or conditionality.
- **Aparigraha**: This is the principle of asceticism, which means to renounce attachment to worldly possessions and pleasures. Aparigraha teaches that materialism and sensual indulgence are the sources of suffering and bondage, and that detachment and simplicity are the means of liberation and happiness. Jains practice various forms of austerity, such as fasting, celibacy, meditation, and self-restraint. They also avoid accumulating wealth, property, or power, and donate generously to charity and social causes.

Jainism has two main sects: Digambara and Svetambara, which differ in some aspects of doctrine, practice, and tradition. For example, Digambaras believe that women cannot attain liberation unless they are reborn as men, and that monks should be naked to symbolize their detachment from the world. Svetambaras, on the other hand, believe that women can attain liberation in their own right, and that monks should wear white clothes to signify their purity and renunciation.

Jainism has a rich and diverse literature, which includes scriptures, commentaries, biographies, stories, poems, and art. Some of the most important texts of Jainism are:

- **Agamas**: These are the canonical scriptures of Jainism, which contain the teachings of the tirthankaras and their disciples. The Agamas are divided into two categories: Angas (limbs) and Angabahyas (external limbs). The Angas are the primary texts, which consist of 12 books that deal with various topics such as ethics, cosmology, karma, rebirth, liberation, and conduct. The Angabahyas are the secondary texts, which consist of 34 books that supplement and elaborate on the Angas. The Digambaras and the Svetambaras have different versions of the Agamas, and do not accept each other's texts as authentic.
- **Tattvartha Sutra**: This is the most authoritative and comprehensive text of Jain philosophy, written by a scholar named Umasvati in the 2nd century CE. The Tattvartha Sutra summarizes the essential doctrines of Jainism in 350 aphorisms, covering topics such as the nature of reality, the categories of existence, the theory of knowledge, the path of liberation, and the code of conduct. The Tattvartha Sutra is accepted by both the Digambaras and the Svetambaras as a valid and authoritative text.
