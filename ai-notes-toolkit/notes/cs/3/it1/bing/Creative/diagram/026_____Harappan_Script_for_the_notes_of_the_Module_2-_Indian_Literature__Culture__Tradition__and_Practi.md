### Harappan Script

- Harappan script is the name given to the symbols used by the Indus Valley Civilisation, which flourished in the northwest of India and Pakistan from about 2500 to 1900 BCE.
- Harappan script is also known as Indus script, because most of the inscriptions containing these symbols have been found in the Indus river valley.
- Harappan script is not yet deciphered, meaning that the meaning and the language behind the symbols are still unknown  .
- Harappan script is composed of about 400 signs, which vary in shape, size and orientation . Some signs may represent words or concepts, while others may represent sounds or syllables. This type of writing system is called logo-syllabic.
- Harappan script was mainly used for short inscriptions on seals, pottery, copper tablets and other objects  . The longest inscription has only 26 signs. The inscriptions are usually written from right to left, but sometimes also from left to right or boustrophedon (alternating direction) .
- Harappan script reflects the culture and society of the Indus Valley Civilisation, which was highly urbanised, organised and sophisticated  . The script may have been used for trade, administration, religion, communication or education  .
- Harappan script is one of the oldest known scripts of India and the world, and it is a source of mystery and fascination for scholars and enthusiasts   . Many attempts have been made to decipher it, but none have been widely accepted or proven . Some of the proposed languages behind the script are Dravidian, Indo-Aryan, Sumerian, Munda and Elamite  .
