### Malayalam Literature

- Malayalam is a South-Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala and some parts of Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Lakshadweep.
- Malayalam literature comprises those literary texts written in Malayalam, ranging from ancient to modern times.
- The earliest extant literary work in Malayalam is **Ramacharitam** (late 12th or early 13th century), an epic poem based on the Sanskrit Ramayana.
- In the subsequent period, besides a popular **pattu** (song) literature, there flourished a literature of mainly erotic poetry composed in the **Manipravalam** style, an admixture of Malayalam and Sanskrit.
- The first travelogue in any Indian language is the Malayalam **Varthamanappusthakam** (1785), written by Paremmakkal Thoma Kathanar, a Syrian Christian priest who visited Rome and Lisbon.
- The modern period of Malayalam literature began in the late 19th century with the rise of the **Navodaya** (New Awakening) movement, which was influenced by Western education, journalism, and social reform.
- Some of the prominent writers of this period include **Kumaran Asan**, **Vallathol Narayana Menon**, **Ulloor S. Parameswara Iyer**, **Kesari Balakrishna Pillai**, **Changampuzha Krishna Pillai**, and **G. Sankara Kurup**.
- The post-independence period witnessed the emergence of various literary trends and genres, such as **progressive**, **modernist**, **postmodernist**, **Dalit**, **feminist**, and **ecological** literature.
- Some of the notable writers of this period include **Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai**, **Vaikom Muhammad Basheer**, **M. T. Vasudevan Nair**, **O. V. Vijayan**, **Kamala Surayya**, **M. Mukundan**, **O. N. V. Kurup**, **Akkitham Achuthan Namboothiri**, and **M. Leelavathy**.
- Malayalam literature has also produced many acclaimed works in the fields of drama, novel, short story, essay, biography, autobiography, memoir, travelogue, criticism, translation, children's literature, and cinema.
