### Bhakti Movement

- The Bhakti movement was a significant religious movement in medieval Hinduism that sought to bring religious reforms to all strata of society by adopting the method of devotion to achieve salvation.
- Bhakti means "devotion" or "love" in Sanskrit. It is the intense emotional attachment and love of a devotee toward a personal god and of the god for the devotee.
- The Bhakti movement preached against the caste system, ritualism, idolatry, and social inequalities. It used the local languages, such as Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, and Bengali, to spread its message to the masses.
- The Bhakti movement originated in South India in the 7th-8th centuries CE with the Alvars (Vaishnavite saints) and the Nayanars (Shaivite saints). They composed devotional hymns and songs in praise of Vishnu and Shiva, respectively.
- The Bhakti movement reached its peak in North India in the 15th-16th centuries CE with the emergence of various sects and schools, such as the Ramanandis, the Kabirpanthis, the Radha-Krishna cult, the Vallabhacharya sect, the Chaitanya movement, the Sikhism, and the Nirguna school.
- The Bhakti movement influenced the development of literature, music, art, and culture in India. It also contributed to the social and political changes, such as the rise of regional languages, the decline of feudalism, the emergence of new classes, and the integration of diverse communities.
