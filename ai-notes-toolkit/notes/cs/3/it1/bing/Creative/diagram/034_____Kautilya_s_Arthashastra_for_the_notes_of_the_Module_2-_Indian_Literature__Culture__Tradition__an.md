### Kautilya’s Arthashastra

- Kautilya’s Arthashastra is an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economics, military strategy, and social organization.
- It is attributed to Kautilya, also known as Chanakya, Vishnugupta, or the Machiavelli of India, who was a scholar at Taxila University and a prime minister of the Mauryan Empire under Chandragupta Maurya (c. 321-c. 297 BCE).
- The Arthashastra consists of 15 books, 150 chapters, and 180 topics, covering various aspects of governance, administration, diplomacy, law, security, welfare, trade, agriculture, industry, and education.
- The Arthashastra is considered a realistic and pragmatic manual for rulers, advising them on how to acquire, maintain, and expand their power and wealth, without much regard for ethical or moral principles.
- The Arthashastra also reflects the cultural, social, and political conditions of ancient India, such as the caste system, the role of women, the religious diversity, and the regional conflicts.
- The Arthashastra was lost for centuries and was rediscovered by Rudrapatna Shamasastry in 1905 CE. It has been translated into various languages and has influenced many modern thinkers and leaders.
