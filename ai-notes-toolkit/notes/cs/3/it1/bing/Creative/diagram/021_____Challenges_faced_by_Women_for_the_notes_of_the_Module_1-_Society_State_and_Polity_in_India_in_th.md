### Challenges faced by Women in India

- Women in India face various socio-cultural, economic, and political challenges that limit their opportunities and rights in different spheres of life.
- Some of the major challenges are:

  - **Violence against women**: Women are subjected to various forms of violence, such as domestic violence, dowry-related harassment, marital rape, genital mutilation, sexual abuse, and harassment, both in public and private spaces. According to the most recent government report on crimes in the country, India logged 31,677 cases of rape in 2021, or 86 cases per day on average, while there were approximately 49 incidences of violence against women reported every single hour .
  - **Gender discrimination**: Women face discrimination and inequality in various aspects of life, such as education, health, employment, political participation, and decision-making. Women have lower literacy rates, higher maternal mortality rates, lower labor force participation rates, and lower representation in parliament and other institutions than men. Women also face gender stereotypes, biases, and prejudices that affect their self-esteem, aspirations, and choices.
  - **Lack of empowerment**: Women lack access to and control over resources, opportunities, and services that are essential for their well-being and development. Women have limited access to land, property, credit, education, health care, information, and technology. Women also face barriers in exercising their legal and human rights, such as the right to vote, the right to inheritance, the right to divorce, and the right to protection from violence.
  - **Social and cultural norms**: Women are expected to conform to certain social and cultural norms that restrict their freedom and autonomy. Women are often seen as subordinate to men, and their roles and responsibilities are defined by patriarchal and traditional values. Women are expected to perform domestic and care work, to obey and serve their husbands and in-laws, to bear and raise children, and to uphold the honor and reputation of their families and communities. Women who deviate from these norms face social stigma, ostracism, and backlash.
  - **Lack of sanitation facilities**: Women suffer as a result of a lack of sufficient sanitation facilities in the workplace and in public places. According to Indian Census figures from 2011, there are 587 million women in India, accounting for 48 percent of the population. Despite this, only 29% of women over the age of fifteen participate in the labor force. One of the reasons for this low participation is the lack of toilets and hygiene facilities for women workers, especially in the informal sector, where they constitute the majority. Women also face difficulties in accessing safe and clean toilets and water sources when they travel, study, or visit public places, which affects their health, dignity, and mobility.
