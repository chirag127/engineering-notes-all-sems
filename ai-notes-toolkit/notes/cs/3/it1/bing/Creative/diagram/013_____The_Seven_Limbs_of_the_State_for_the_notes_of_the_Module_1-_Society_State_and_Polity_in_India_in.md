### The Seven Limbs of the State

- The seven limbs of the state (saptanga) is a theory of statecraft given by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra  .
- The theory explains the essential components of a state and their interrelations.
- The seven limbs of the state are:

  - King: The king is the supreme authority and the head of the state. He is responsible for the peace, justice and stability of the state. He is independent and has the final say in all matters .
  - Amatya: The amatya are the bureaucrats or ministers who assist the king in administration and policy-making. They are selected based on their merit, loyalty and competence. They are accountable to the king and the people .
  - Janapada: The janapada is the territory or the land of the state. It includes the natural resources, the population, the culture and the economy of the state. It is the source of revenue and security for the state .
  - Durga: The durga is the fort or the capital of the state. It is the center of power and prestige of the state. It is the place where the king resides and conducts his affairs. It is also the place where the treasury, the army and the allies are stationed. It is well-protected and well-maintained .
  - Kosa: The kosa is the treasury or the wealth of the state. It is the result of the taxation, trade and commerce of the state. It is used to finance the administration, the defense and the welfare of the state. It is also used to bribe or reward the enemies or the friends of the state .
  - Danda: The danda is the coercive authority or the force of the state. It is the army, the police and the judiciary of the state. It is used to maintain law and order, to protect the state from external and internal threats, and to enforce the king's commands  .
  - Mitra: The mitra is the ally or the friend of the state. It is another state or a group of states that share common interests and goals with the state. It is used to support the state in times of war or peace, to expand the influence and the power of the state, and to balance the enemies or the rivals of the state .

- The theory suggests that the state should maintain a balance and harmony among the seven limbs of the state. Any weakness or defect in any of the limbs can affect the stability and the prosperity of the state .
- The theory also provides guidelines and strategies for the king and the amatya to deal with various situations and challenges related to the seven limbs of the state .
