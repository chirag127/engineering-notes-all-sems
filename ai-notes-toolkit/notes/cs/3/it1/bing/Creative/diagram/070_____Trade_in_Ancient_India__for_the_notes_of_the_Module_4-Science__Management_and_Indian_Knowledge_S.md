### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange between different regions and civilizations.
- India has a long history of trade since the Harappan civilization (c. 2600-1900 BCE), which had extensive contacts with Mesopotamia, Egypt, and Central Asia. The Harappans traded mainly gold, copper, ivory, textiles, and beads.
- After the decline of the Harappan civilization, trade continued in the post-Vedic period (c. 1500-600 BCE) with the rise of new urban centers such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura. These cities were connected by roads and rivers and facilitated the exchange of goods and ideas. Trade in this period involved textiles, spices, precious metals, gems, pearls, and horses.
- Trade in ancient India reached its peak during the Mauryan Empire (c. 321-185 BCE) and the subsequent period of the Satavahanas (c. 200 BCE-200 CE) and the Kushanas (c. 30-375 CE). These empires expanded their political and economic influence over large parts of India and beyond, and established diplomatic and commercial relations with other regions such as China, Southeast Asia, Rome, Persia, and Central Asia. Trade in this period involved silk, cotton, spices, perfumes, ivory, coral, glass, wine, and coins. 
- Trade in ancient India was not only conducted by land, but also by sea. India had a long maritime tradition and developed advanced shipbuilding and navigation techniques. India's coastal regions were engaged in seaborne trade with the Arabian Peninsula, East Africa, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, and China. India's ports such as Bharuch, Broach, Kalyan, Muziris, and Arikamedu were important hubs of maritime trade and attracted merchants and travelers from various countries. 
- Trade in ancient India was regulated and promoted by various institutions and authorities. The state played a significant role in providing security, infrastructure, taxation, and legal framework for trade. The state also issued standardized weights and measures, and minted coins of various metals and denominations. The state also appointed officials such as superintendents, inspectors, and customs officers to oversee and facilitate trade. The state also encouraged trade by granting concessions, subsidies, and privileges to merchants and traders.
- Trade in ancient India was also influenced and supported by various social and religious factors. Trade was considered a respectable and profitable occupation by many castes and communities. Trade was also patronized and endorsed by various religious sects such as Buddhism, Jainism, and Hinduism. Trade also fostered cultural and intellectual exchange and diffusion among different regions and peoples. Trade also contributed to the development of art, literature, science, technology, and philosophy in ancient India.
