### Water Management in India

Water management in India is the process of planning, developing, distributing and managing the optimum use of water resources. It is a key determinant of health security and economic growth in India. India has just 4% of the world’s water resource, supporting 17.1% of its population. Over 50% of agriculture was still rain-fed.

Some of the major aspects of water management in India are:

- Providing drinking water to rural areas: Historically, the government has been responsible for providing drinking water to 833 million rural people in some 640,000 villages across the country. However, due to poor operation and maintenance, many of these systems have become dysfunctional. The World Bank has supported the government in shifting from a supply-driven approach to a demand-driven one, where communities are empowered to plan, implement, operate and maintain their own water supply systems .
- Groundwater management: Over the past few decades, groundwater extraction has risen exponentially across India. India is now the world’s largest user of groundwater, accounting for about 25% of the global total. Groundwater is a vital source of irrigation and drinking water, but it is also depleting rapidly and becoming contaminated. The World Bank has supported the government in promoting sustainable groundwater management through community participation, regulation, monitoring, and conjunctive use of surface and groundwater . The Atal Bhujal Yojana, India’s largest community-led groundwater management program, is helping improve rural livelihoods and build resilience in 7 Indian states which have the highest rates of groundwater depletion.
- Improving access to water on dry rainfed lands: About 60% of India’s net sown area is rainfed, where rainfall is erratic and droughts are frequent. These areas are home to the poorest and most marginalized sections of society, who depend on agriculture for their livelihoods. The World Bank has supported the government in enhancing water availability and productivity on these lands through watershed development, soil and water conservation, rainwater harvesting, and micro-irrigation .
- Cleaning the Ganga: The Ganga is India’s most sacred and iconic river, but it is also one of the most polluted. The World Bank has supported the government in implementing the National Ganga River Basin Project, which aims to improve the quality and quantity of water in the Ganga and its tributaries. The project focuses on strengthening institutions, building sewage infrastructure, promoting industrial pollution control, and enhancing public awareness and participation .
- Managing floods and droughts: India is highly vulnerable to floods and droughts, which cause huge losses of lives, livelihoods, and assets. The World Bank has supported the government in strengthening its disaster risk management capacity, by providing technical assistance, financing, and knowledge products. The World Bank has also helped the government in developing and implementing the National Hydrology Project, which aims to improve the availability and quality of water data and information for better planning and decision making .
- Enhancing dam safety in India: India has over 5,200 large dams, which provide irrigation, hydropower, drinking water, and flood control benefits. However, many of these dams are old and need urgent rehabilitation and maintenance. The World Bank has supported the government in improving the safety and performance of selected dams and associated appurtenances through the Dam Rehabilitation and Improvement Project, which covers 223 dams in 7 states .

Water management in India is a complex and challenging task, which requires holistic, participatory, and adaptive approaches. The World Bank has been a long-term partner of the government in addressing its water needs and priorities, by providing financial, technical, and knowledge support. The World Bank has also been a catalyst for innovation and learning, by bringing global best practices and experiences to India, and sharing India’s lessons and achievements with the world .
