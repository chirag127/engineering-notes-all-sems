### Dance

Dance is an ancient and celebrated cultural tradition in India. It is a form of expression, communication, and devotion. Dance can be classified into two broad categories: classical and folk.

- Classical dances are those that have a codified set of rules, techniques, and movements, and are performed on stage or in temples. They are based on the ancient texts of Natya Shastra and Abhinaya Darpana, which describe the theory and practice of dance, drama, and music. Classical dances are usually accompanied by classical music and have a spiritual or religious theme. There are six recognized forms of classical dances in India: Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Manipuri, Kuchipudi, and Odissi. Each of these dances has a distinct style, origin, and repertoire.

- Folk dances are those that are performed by the common people for social, cultural, or recreational purposes. They are influenced by the local traditions, customs, and environment of different regions and communities. Folk dances are usually performed in groups, with simple costumes and props, and have a festive or celebratory mood. They reflect the diversity and richness of Indian culture and heritage. Some examples of folk dances are Bhangra, Garba, Dandiya, Bihu, Lavani, and Chhau.

Dance in India is not only a form of art, but also a way of life. It is a medium to express one's emotions, beliefs, and values. It is a source of joy, beauty, and harmony. It is a part of India's cultural heritage and performing arts.
