### Slavery in India

- Slavery in India is a historical and contemporary phenomenon that has existed in various forms and periods, from ancient times to the present day.
- Slavery in India can be broadly categorized into two types: domestic slavery and bonded labor.
- Domestic slavery refers to the practice of owning and exploiting household servants, who are often kidnapped, sold, or inherited by their masters. Domestic slaves may perform various tasks such as cooking, cleaning, childcare, or sexual services.
- Bonded labor refers to the practice of forcing workers to repay a debt or a loan by working for little or no wages, often in harsh and hazardous conditions. Bonded laborers may work in various sectors such as agriculture, mining, construction, or manufacturing.
- Slavery in India has been influenced by various factors such as caste, religion, colonialism, and globalization. Some of the key historical and contemporary aspects of slavery in India are:

  - Slavery in ancient India was prevalent among the lower castes and the indigenous tribes, who were often enslaved by the upper castes and the foreign invaders. Slavery was also sanctioned by some of the religious texts and laws, such as the Manusmriti and the Arthashastra.
  - Slavery in medieval India escalated during the Muslim domination of northern India after the 11th century, when Muslim rulers re-introduced slavery to the Indian subcontinent. They captured and enslaved millions of Hindus, Buddhists, and Jains, and exported them to various parts of the Islamic world. They also imposed various restrictions and taxes on the non-Muslims, such as the jizya and the kharaj, which increased their vulnerability to slavery.
  - Slavery in colonial India continued through the 18th and 19th centuries, when Indians were taken into different parts of the world as slaves by various European merchant companies as part of the Indian Ocean slave trade. They were also exploited by the British colonial administration, which imposed various policies and laws that undermined the rights and livelihoods of the Indians, such as the Permanent Settlement, the Ryotwari System, and the Criminal Tribes Act.
  - Slavery in modern India is a result of various factors such as poverty, illiteracy, discrimination, corruption, and globalization. Despite the legal abolition of slavery in India in 1843 and the constitutional prohibition of forced labor in 1950, slavery persists in various forms and sectors, such as domestic servitude, bonded labor, sex trafficking, child labor, and organ trafficking. Slavery in India is also linked to the global demand for cheap and exploitable labor and products, such as garments, carpets, bricks, and electronics.
