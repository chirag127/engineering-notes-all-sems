### Seals

- Seals are small objects that have a carved or engraved design on one side and a flat or convex back on the other side.
- Seals are used to make impressions on clay, wax, metal, or paper to authenticate documents, mark ownership, or indicate authority.
- Seals are found in many ancient cultures, such as Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and India.
- Seals can reveal information about the culture, religion, trade, art, and writing system of the people who used them.

#### Seals in Indian Culture

- Seals are an important source of evidence for the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished in the northwest of India and Pakistan from about 2500 to 1900 BCE.
- Seals from the Indus Valley Civilization are usually square or rectangular, made of steatite (a type of soft stone), and measure about 2-3 cm on a side.
- Seals from the Indus Valley Civilization have a carved animal motif on one side and a few symbols on the other side, which are believed to be part of an undeciphered script.
- Seals from the Indus Valley Civilization depict a variety of animals, such as buffaloes, rhinoceroses, elephants, tigers, bulls, and unicorns. Some seals also show trees, plants, or composite creatures with multiple heads or limbs.
- Seals from the Indus Valley Civilization were used for trade, administration, and ritual purposes. They were carried by merchants, officials, and priests, and were stamped on clay tags, tablets, or sealings to seal containers, mark goods, or identify owners.
- Seals from the Indus Valley Civilization reflect the artistic skill, cultural diversity, and economic activity of the people who created and used them. They also indicate the possible existence of a centralized authority, a complex social hierarchy, and a widespread trade network.

#### Seals in Other Indian Cultures

- Seals continued to be used in India after the decline of the Indus Valley Civilization, by various dynasties, kingdoms, and empires that ruled over different regions and periods of Indian history.
- Seals from later Indian cultures are made of different materials, such as copper, bronze, silver, gold, ivory, or glass, and have different shapes, such as circular, oval, or triangular.
- Seals from later Indian cultures have different designs, such as human figures, deities, symbols, inscriptions, or floral patterns. Some seals also have holes, loops, or rings for attaching them to cords, chains, or rings.
- Seals from later Indian cultures were used for similar purposes as the Indus Valley seals, such as authentication, ownership, or authority. They were also used for personal adornment, religious devotion, or artistic expression.
- Seals from later Indian cultures reveal information about the political, religious, social, and cultural aspects of the people who used them. They also indicate the influence of foreign cultures, such as Persia, Greece, Rome, China, or Islam, on Indian art and society.
