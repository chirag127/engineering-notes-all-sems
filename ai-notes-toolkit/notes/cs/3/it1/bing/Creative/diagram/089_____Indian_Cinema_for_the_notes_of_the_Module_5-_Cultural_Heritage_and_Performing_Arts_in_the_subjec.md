### Indian Cinema

Indian cinema is one of the most diverse and vibrant forms of artistic expression in the world. It encompasses a variety of genres, languages, styles, and themes, reflecting the rich and complex cultural heritage of India. Here are some points to note about the history and features of Indian cinema:

- Indian cinema began in the late 19th century, when the first public screening of a movie by the Lumiere brothers took place in Bombay in 1896. The first Indian filmmaker was Dadasaheb Phalke, who is considered the father of Indian cinema. He made the first full-length feature film, Raja Harishchandra, in 1913. It was a silent film based on a Hindu mythological story.
- The advent of sound in cinema revolutionized the industry and gave birth to the talkies. The first Indian talkie was Alam Ara, directed by Ardeshir Irani, in 1931. It was a musical drama that introduced songs and dialogues to the Indian audience. The talkies also led to the growth of regional cinema, as filmmakers started to make films in various languages, such as Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi, and Kannada, to cater to the diverse linguistic and cultural preferences of the Indian people .
- The post-independence period saw the emergence of a new wave of Indian cinema, influenced by the social and political changes in the country. Filmmakers such as Satyajit Ray, Ritwik Ghatak, Mrinal Sen, Guru Dutt, Bimal Roy, and Raj Kapoor explored realistic and humanistic themes, such as poverty, oppression, corruption, and alienation, in their films. They also experimented with cinematic techniques, such as montage, flashbacks, and symbolism, to create a distinctive aesthetic and narrative style .
- The 1950s and 1960s also witnessed the rise of Bollywood, the Hindi film industry based in Bombay (now Mumbai). Bollywood is the largest and most popular sector of Indian cinema, producing films that appeal to a mass audience with a mix of genres, such as romance, comedy, action, drama, and musical. Bollywood films are known for their lavish sets, costumes, songs, dances, and star power. Some of the iconic actors and actresses of Bollywood are Dilip Kumar, Dev Anand, Rajesh Khanna, Amitabh Bachchan, Nargis, Madhubala, Waheeda Rehman, and Hema Malini .
- Indian cinema has also evolved with the changing times and tastes of the audience. The 1970s and 1980s saw the emergence of parallel cinema, which was a movement of independent and alternative filmmakers who challenged the mainstream conventions and addressed social issues, such as gender, caste, and class, in their films. Some of the notable directors of parallel cinema are Shyam Benegal, Govind Nihalani, Adoor Gopalakrishnan, and Mani Kaul. The 1990s and 2000s saw the globalization and diversification of Indian cinema, as filmmakers explored new genres, such as thriller, horror, sci-fi, and animation, and reached out to international markets and audiences. Some of the successful films of this period are Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge, Lagaan, 3 Idiots, Dangal, and Baahubali .
- Indian cinema is a global enterprise and its films have wide viewership and fanbase throughout South Asia and have spread internationally. Modern film releases are dubbed into many languages, forming a Pan-India films movement. Indians overseas account for 12% of revenue for the industry. Indian cinema has also influenced and been influenced by other film industries, such as Hollywood, Chinese cinema, and Iranian cinema. Indian cinema has also received recognition and awards at various international film festivals, such as Cannes, Berlin, Venice, and Oscar .
