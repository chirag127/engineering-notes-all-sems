### The Vedas

- The Vedas are a large body of religious texts originating in ancient India.
- The Vedas are composed in Vedic Sanskrit, the oldest layer of Sanskrit literature and the oldest scriptures of Hinduism.
- The word Veda means "knowledge" in Sanskrit, and the Vedas are thought to contain the fundamental knowledge relating to the underlying cause of, function of, and personal response to existence.
- The Vedas are divided into four collections: the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda and the Atharvaveda.
- Each Veda consists of four parts: the Samhitas (hymns), the Brahmanas (rituals), the Aranyakas (forest texts) and the Upanishads (philosophy).
- The Vedas are considered to be revealed by the gods to the sages (rishis), who transmitted them orally to their disciples for generations.
- The Vedas are the source of authority for the Hindu dharma (duty, law, ethics) and regulate the social, legal, domestic and religious customs of Hindus.
- The Vedas are also the basis of various schools of Hindu philosophy, such as Vedanta, Mimamsa, Nyaya, Samkhya and Yoga.
- The Vedas are regarded as eternal, infallible and sacred by most Hindus, and are revered as shruti (that which is heard) in contrast to smriti (that which is remembered).
- The Vedas have influenced many aspects of Indian culture, such as literature, art, music, science, law and politics.
