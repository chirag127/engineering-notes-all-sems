## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions, philosophies, and practices that have influenced its culture and history.
- The majority of Indians practice Hinduism, which is a major religious and cultural tradition of South Asia, which developed from Vedic religion.
- Hinduism is not a monolithic religion, but a collection of various schools, sects, movements, and traditions that share some common features, such as belief in reincarnation, karma, dharma, and the authority of the Vedas (sacred scriptures).
- Hinduism has many gods and goddesses, who are manifestations of the supreme reality, Brahman, which is the source and essence of everything. Some of the most popular deities are Vishnu, Shiva, Ganesha, Lakshmi, and Durga.
- Hinduism also has many philosophical texts, such as the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Brahma Sutras, which explore the nature of reality, the self, and the ultimate goal of life, which is moksha (liberation from the cycle of birth and death).
- Hinduism has various paths or ways of achieving moksha, such as karma yoga (the path of action), bhakti yoga (the path of devotion), jnana yoga (the path of knowledge), and raja yoga (the path of meditation).
- Hinduism also has many rituals, festivals, and practices, such as puja (worship), prasada (food offering), aarti (flame offering), darshan (seeing the deity), mantra (sacred sound), yoga (physical and mental discipline), and pilgrimage (visiting holy places).
- Other major religions in India are Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam, and Christianity, which have their own beliefs, doctrines, scriptures, and practices.
- Sikhism is a monotheistic religion founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century, which teaches the equality of all human beings, the rejection of caste and ritual, and the service of God and humanity.
- Buddhism is a non-theistic religion founded by Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, in the 6th century BCE, which teaches the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, and the concept of nirvana (the cessation of suffering).
- Jainism is an ancient religion that emphasizes non-violence, non-attachment, and self-control, and follows the teachings of the Tirthankaras (ford-makers), who are enlightened beings who have attained liberation.
- Islam is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of the Quran, the holy book revealed to Prophet Muhammad in the 7th century CE, and the Sunnah, the example of his life and actions.
- Christianity is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of the Bible, the holy book that contains the Old and New Testaments, and the life and teachings of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who died and rose again for the salvation of humanity.
- All these religions have contributed to the rich and diverse religious, philosophical, and cultural heritage of India, and have coexisted peacefully for centuries, despite some conflicts and challenges.
