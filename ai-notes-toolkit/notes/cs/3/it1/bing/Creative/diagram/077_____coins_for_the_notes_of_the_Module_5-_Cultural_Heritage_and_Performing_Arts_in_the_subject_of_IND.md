### Coins

Coins are a form of money that have been used in India since ancient times. Coins reflect the culture, history, art, and politics of the people who issued them. Coins can also provide valuable information about the trade, economy, religion, and society of different periods of Indian history.

Some of the main points to know about coins in Indian culture are:

- The earliest coins in India were made of copper and silver and were called **karshapanas** or **panas**. They were issued by the Mahajanapadas, the kingdoms that emerged in northern India in the 6th century BCE .
- The first gold coins in India were issued by the **Indo-Greek** kings, who ruled parts of northwestern India from the 2nd century BCE to the 1st century CE. They introduced the **drachma**, a coin that had the portrait of the king on one side and a deity or a symbol on the other.
- The most famous gold coins in Indian history were the **dinaras**, issued by the **Gupta Empire** from the 4th to the 6th century CE. The Gupta dinaras were based on the Roman coins and had the image of the emperor and a goddess on the obverse and reverse sides respectively. The Gupta coins are considered to be the finest examples of Indian art and numismatics .
- The **Indo-Sasanian** coins were a category of coins that were influenced by the Sasanian Empire of Persia, which ruled parts of western and central Asia from the 3rd to the 7th century CE. The Indo-Sasanian coins had a bust of the king on one side and a fire altar on the other. They were issued by various dynasties and kingdoms in northern and western India, such as the **Kushans**, the **Hunas**, the **Gurjara-Pratiharas**, and the **Chalukyas** .
- The **Chola Empire**, which ruled over southern India and parts of Southeast Asia from the 9th to the 13th century CE, issued a variety of coins in gold, silver, copper, and lead. The Chola coins had the image of the king, a deity, or a symbol on one side and a legend in Tamil or Sanskrit on the other. The Chola coins also depicted the naval and military achievements of the empire .
- The **Delhi Sultanate**, which ruled over most of northern India from the 13th to the 16th century CE, introduced the **tanka** and the **jital**, two types of silver coins that became the standard currency of medieval India. The Delhi Sultanate also issued gold coins called **dinars** and copper coins called **paisa**. The coins of the Delhi Sultanate had the name and title of the ruler and the mint name in Arabic or Persian on both sides .
- The **Mughal Empire**, which ruled over most of India from the 16th to the 18th century CE, issued coins in gold, silver, and copper that were known for their artistic excellence and uniformity. The Mughal coins had the name and title of the emperor and the mint name in Persian on one side and the date and a religious motto in Arabic on the other. The Mughal coins also had various symbols and motifs, such as the lion, the sun, the lotus, and the kalima .
- The **British Raj**, which ruled over India from the mid-18th to the mid-20th century CE, issued coins in gold, silver, and copper that had the portrait of the British monarch on one side and the value and the name of India in English and other languages on the other. The British coins also had various symbols and motifs, such as the crown, the lion, the star, and the trident .
- The **Republic of India**, which became independent in 1947, issued coins in various metals and denominations that had the emblem of India on one side and the value and the name of India in Hindi and English on the other. The Indian coins also had various symbols and motifs, such as the lotus, the lion capital, the Ashoka chakra, and the map of India .
