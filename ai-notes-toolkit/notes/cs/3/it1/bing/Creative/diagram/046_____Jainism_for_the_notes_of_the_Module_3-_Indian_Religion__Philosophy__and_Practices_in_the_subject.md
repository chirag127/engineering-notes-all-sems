### Jainism

- Jainism is an ancient religion from India that teaches that the way to liberation and bliss is to live a life of harmlessness and renunciation.
- The name Jainism derives from the Sanskrit verb ji, “to conquer.” It refers to the ascetic battle that, it is believed, Jain renunciants (monks and nuns) must fight against the passions and bodily senses to gain enlightenment, or omniscience and purity of soul.
- Jainism is one of the three most ancient Indian religious traditions still in existence and an integral part of South Asian culture. Along with Hinduism and Buddhism, Jainism is one of the three major heterodox systems (darshans) of Indian philosophy.
- Jainism is a transtheistic religion, holding that the universe was not created, and will exist forever. It is independent, having no creator, governor, judge, or destroyer. In this, it is unlike the Abrahamic religions and the theistic strands of Hinduism, but similar to Buddhism.
- Jainism teaches that the path to enlightenment is through nonviolence and reducing harm to living things (including plants and animals) as much as possible. This principle of ahimsa, literally “non-injury”, is the core of Jain ethics and practice.
- Jainism has two main sects: Digambara (sky-clad) and Svetambara (white-clad). The main difference between them is the degree of asceticism and the role of women in the religious community. Digambaras are more austere and require their monks to be naked, while Svetambaras allow their monks to wear white clothes. Digambaras also deny that women can attain liberation without being reborn as men, while Svetambaras affirm that women can achieve the same spiritual status as men.
- Jainism has a rich and diverse literature, consisting of scriptures, commentaries, stories, biographies, and philosophical treatises. The most sacred texts of Jainism are the Agamas, which are divided into two groups: the Angas (limbs) and the Angabahyas (those outside the limbs). The Angas are the oldest and most authoritative texts, while the Angabahyas are later and supplementary works. The Agamas contain the teachings of the Tirthankaras, the enlightened beings who founded the Jain tradition.
- Jainism has a cosmology that describes the structure and history of the universe, as well as the cycle of birth and death of all living beings. The universe is divided into three realms: the upper, the middle, and the lower. The upper realm is the abode of the liberated souls, the middle realm is the world of humans and animals, and the lower realm is the hell of the sinful souls. The universe is also divided into six regions, each with its own time cycle. The time cycles are divided into two phases: the ascending (utsarpini) and the descending (avasarpini). Each phase has six periods of varying lengths and qualities, from the golden age (sukhama-sukham) to the iron age (duhkha-duhkham).
- Jainism has a doctrine of karma that explains the cause and effect of actions and their consequences for the soul. Karma is a subtle matter that attaches to the soul and obscures its true nature. Karma is classified into eight types, according to its effect on the soul: knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring, feeling-producing, deluding, lifespan-determining, physique-determining, status-determining, and obstructive. The soul can free itself from karma by following the three jewels of Jainism: right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct.
