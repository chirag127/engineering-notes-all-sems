### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times

- Pre-colonial India refers to the period before the British occupation of India, which started in 1757 with the Battle of Plassey and ended in 1947 with the Indian independence.
- Pre-colonial India was characterized by a rich cultural and religious diversity, with various kingdoms, empires, and dynasties ruling over different regions and peoples.
- Some of the major political entities that dominated pre-colonial India were:
  - The Mauryan Empire (321-185 BCE), which was the first pan-Indian empire and one of the largest and most powerful in ancient history. It was founded by Chandragupta Maurya and reached its peak under Ashoka, who promoted Buddhism and a policy of non-violence. 
  - The Gupta Empire (320-550 CE), which is considered the golden age of Indian culture, science, mathematics, literature, and art. It was founded by Chandragupta I and expanded by his successors, especially Samudragupta and Chandragupta II. It maintained a large network of trade and diplomacy with other regions, such as China, Southeast Asia, and the Roman Empire. 
  - The Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526 CE), which was a Muslim dynasty that ruled over most of northern India and parts of southern India. It was founded by Qutb al-Din Aibak, a former slave of the Ghurid Empire, and reached its zenith under Alauddin Khalji and Muhammad bin Tughluq, who implemented various administrative, economic, and military reforms. It faced several challenges from the Mongols, the Vijayanagara Empire, and the regional kingdoms. 
  - The Mughal Empire (1526-1857 CE), which was one of the largest and most prosperous empires in world history. It was founded by Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, and consolidated by his grandson Akbar, who established a policy of religious tolerance and cultural synthesis. It reached its peak under Aurangzeb, who expanded the empire to its greatest extent, but also faced rebellions, wars, and decline. It was gradually weakened by the Marathas, the Sikhs, the British, and the regional powers. 
- Pre-colonial India was also a self-sufficient and flourishing economy, with a high level of agricultural productivity, industrial output, and trade. India was known as the golden eagle, as it was a major exporter of textiles, spices, gems, and other commodities to the world. It also had a sophisticated banking, taxation, and monetary system, as well as a vibrant intellectual and scientific culture.
