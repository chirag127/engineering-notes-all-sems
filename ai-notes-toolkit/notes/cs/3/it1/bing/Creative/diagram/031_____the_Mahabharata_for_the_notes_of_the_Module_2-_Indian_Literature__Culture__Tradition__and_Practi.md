### The Mahabharata

- The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, the other being the Ramayana .
- The Mahabharata is composed of about 74,000 verses, divided into 18 books, or parvas.
- The Mahabharata is an important source of information on the development of Hinduism between 400 BCE and 200 CE and is regarded by Hindus as both a text about dharma (Hindu moral law) and a history of their ancestors.
- The main story of the Mahabharata revolves around the struggle for the throne of Hastinapura between two branches of a family, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, who are cousins and descendants of the legendary king Bharata   .
- The Kauravas are the sons of Dhritarashtra, who is blind and cannot inherit the throne, and the Pandavas are the sons of Pandu, who becomes king but renounces the throne due to a curse that prevents him from having children .
- The Pandavas are born from the gods through the intervention of Kunti, Pandu's first wife, and Madri, his second wife, who both invoke divine boons to conceive children.
- The Kauravas are led by Duryodhana, the eldest son of Dhritarashtra, who is jealous and hateful of the Pandavas and plots to usurp their share of the kingdom .
- The Pandavas are led by Yudhishthira, the eldest son of Pandu, who is virtuous and righteous, but also prone to gambling and losing his wealth and kingdom .
- The Mahabharata narrates the events that lead to the Kurukshetra War, a great battle that lasts for 18 days and involves many kings and warriors from different regions of India, who align themselves with either the Kauravas or the Pandavas   .
- The Mahabharata also contains many sub-stories and digressions that explore various aspects of Hindu mythology, philosophy, ethics, law, cosmology, art, and culture  .
- The most famous and influential part of the Mahabharata is the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between Krishna, an avatar of the god Vishnu, and Arjuna, the third son of Pandu and the greatest archer of his time, who faces a moral dilemma before the war  .
- The Bhagavad Gita teaches the concepts of karma (action), dharma (duty), moksha (liberation), and bhakti (devotion), and presents different paths to attain the supreme reality, such as jnana (knowledge), karma (action), and bhakti (devotion)  .
- The Mahabharata ends with the victory of the Pandavas, but also with the death of most of the characters and the destruction of the Kuru dynasty  .
- The Mahabharata is considered to be a reflection of the human condition, with its characters displaying a range of emotions, motivations, virtues, and flaws  .
- The Mahabharata is also considered to be a guide for living, with its teachings on dharma, karma, and moksha, and its examples of moral dilemmas and ethical choices  .
