### Marriage

Marriage is a social and legal institution that binds two or more people together in a relationship of mutual rights and obligations. Marriage is considered to be one of the most important rites of passage in India, as it marks the transition from one stage of life to another. Marriage also has religious, cultural, economic and political implications in India, as it affects the status, identity and roles of the individuals and their families.

Some of the main points to note about marriage in India are:

- **Types of marriage**: There are different types of marriage in India, depending on the region, religion, community and personal preferences of the bride and groom. Some of the common types are:

  - Arranged marriage: This is the most prevalent type of marriage in India, where the parents or elders of the bride and groom find a suitable match for them, based on factors such as caste, class, education, horoscope, etc. The bride and groom may or may not have a say in the decision, and may or may not meet each other before the wedding.
  - Love marriage: This is the type of marriage where the bride and groom choose their own partners, based on their mutual attraction, compatibility and consent. This type of marriage is often opposed by the families or the society, as it may violate the norms of caste, religion, etc. The bride and groom may elope or seek legal protection to get married.
  - Court marriage: This is the type of marriage where the bride and groom register their marriage in a court of law, without any religious or social ceremonies. This type of marriage is usually done by those who want to avoid the hassles or expenses of a traditional wedding, or by those who face opposition from their families or the society.
  - Live-in relationship: This is the type of relationship where the couple cohabits without getting married legally or socially. This type of relationship is not recognized by the law or the society, and may face legal or social challenges. However, some couples prefer this type of relationship for various reasons, such as personal freedom, convenience, etc.

- **Age of marriage**: The legal age of marriage for women in India is 18 years, and for men is 21 years. However, child marriages are still prevalent in some parts of India, especially among the rural and poor sections of the society. Child marriages are illegal and harmful, as they violate the rights and health of the children, and increase the risks of domestic violence, early pregnancy, maternal mortality, etc. The government and various NGOs are working to prevent and eradicate child marriages in India.
- **Caste and religion of marriage**: Marrying within one's caste and religion is an essential feature of marriage in India, as it reflects the social and cultural values and norms of the society. Inter-caste and inter-faith marriages are rare and often face resistance or violence from the families or the society, as they are seen as a threat to the social order and harmony. However, some couples choose to marry outside their caste or religion, either out of love or out of rebellion, and seek legal or social support to do so. The Constitution of India grants the right to marry a person of one's choice, regardless of caste or religion, under Article 21 (right to life and liberty).
- **Documents and procedures of marriage**: In order to get married legally in India, both the bride and groom must complete certain requirements that are laid down by the laws. These include:

  - Proof of residency and address in India for at least 30 days prior to the application, for at least one of the parties
  - A valid passport for both parties
  - Original birth certificate for both parties
  - Visa or Ration Card (if applicable)
  - Affidavit stating the date of birth, marital status, nationality and consent of the parties
  - Certificate of no impediment or no objection from the respective embassy or consulate (if applicable)
  - Divorce decree or death certificate of the previous spouse (if applicable)
  - Two passport-sized photographs for each party
  - Two witnesses with their identity proofs
