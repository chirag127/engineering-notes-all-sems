### Sufi movement in India

- Sufism is a mystical and spiritual form of Islam that emphasizes the direct experience of God through love, devotion, and self-purification .
- Sufis are followers of Sufism who seek to attain union with God through various practices such as meditation, chanting, music, poetry, and service to humanity .
- The word Sufi is derived from the Arabic word "suf", which means wool, referring to the simple and coarse clothing worn by the early Sufis as a sign of renunciation of worldly pleasures .
- The origin of Sufism is not clear, but some scholars trace it back to the teachings of Prophet Muhammad and his companions, while others link it to the influences of Persian, Greek, Indian, and Christian mysticism  .
- Sufism spread to India from the 12th century onwards through the invasions of Muslim rulers and the migrations of Sufi saints and preachers   .
- Sufism in India developed into various orders or silsilas, each with its own founder, doctrine, and method of spiritual training . Some of the prominent silsilas in India were:
  - Chishti silsila: Founded by Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, who came to India from Persia in the 12th century. It was the most popular and influential silsila in India, especially in northern and central India. It emphasized love, tolerance, service, and music as the means of reaching God   .
  - Suhrawardi silsila: Founded by Sheikh Shihabuddin Suhrawardi, who came to India from Iraq in the 12th century. It was mainly based in Bihar and Bengal, and focused on strict adherence to Islamic law and rational philosophy   .
  - Qadiri silsila: Founded by Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani, who was born in Iran and lived in Baghdad in the 12th century. It was introduced in India by his followers in the 15th century. It stressed on the importance of miracles and charismatic leadership   .
  - Naqshbandi silsila: Founded by Khwaja Bahauddin Naqshband, who was born in Bukhara (now Uzbekistan) in the 14th century. It was brought to India by his disciples in the 16th century. It advocated silent meditation and avoidance of music and rituals   .
- Sufism in India had a significant impact on the religious, social, and cultural life of the people. Some of the effects of Sufism were:
  - It promoted a more tolerant and inclusive form of Islam that respected the diversity of beliefs and practices of different communities    .
  - It fostered a spirit of harmony and dialogue between Muslims and non-Muslims, especially Hindus, and influenced the development of the Bhakti movement and the Sikh religion    .
  - It enriched the literature, art, and music of India with its mystical poetry, stories, symbols, and songs, and created a distinct genre of Sufi music known as Qawwali    .
  - It inspired social reform and welfare activities among the masses, and provided spiritual guidance and moral support to the rulers and the nobility    .
