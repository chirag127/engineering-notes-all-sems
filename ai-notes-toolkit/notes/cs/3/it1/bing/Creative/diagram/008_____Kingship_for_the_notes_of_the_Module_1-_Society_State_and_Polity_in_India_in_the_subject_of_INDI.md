### Kingship

- Kingship is the concept and practice of sovereignty over a territory by a king who functions as its protector and ruler, both in secular and religious terms.
- Kingship in India has a long and complex history, spanning from the ancient Vedic period to the modern constitutional monarchy.
- Some of the main aspects of kingship in India are:

  - The sources and legitimacy of kingship: Kingship in India was derived from various sources, such as divine right, lineage, conquest, election, or merit. Kingship was also legitimized by various means, such as rituals, genealogies, inscriptions, coins, or patronage of arts and literature.
  - The ideology and symbolism of kingship: Kingship in India was influenced by various religious and philosophical traditions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Islam. Kingship was also expressed through various symbols, such as the throne, the crown, the scepter, the umbrella, the wheel, the lion, or the lotus.
  - The functions and duties of kingship: Kingship in India involved various functions and duties, such as maintaining law and order, protecting the subjects, administering justice, collecting taxes, waging wars, expanding the territory, promoting trade and commerce, or supporting religious and cultural activities.
  - The challenges and limitations of kingship: Kingship in India faced various challenges and limitations, such as rebellions, invasions, succession disputes, factionalism, corruption, or foreign interference. Kingship also had to balance the interests and demands of various groups, such as nobles, priests, merchants, peasants, or women.
