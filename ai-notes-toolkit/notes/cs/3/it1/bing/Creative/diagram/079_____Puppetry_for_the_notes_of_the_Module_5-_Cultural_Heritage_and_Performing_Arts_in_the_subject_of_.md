### Puppetry in India

- Puppetry is an ancient art form of storytelling that involves manipulating puppets to perform stories, myths, legends, folk songs, etc.   
- Puppetry in India is a family business that is passed on from generation to generation. The puppeteers make, operate, and memorize the texts for the puppets. The puppets are considered as family treasures and sometimes given as dowry. 
- Puppetry in India has philosophical significance as well. In the Bhagavad Gita, God is depicted as a puppeteer who controls the cosmos with three strings: Satta (goodness), Raja (passion), and Tama (ignorance). In Indian theatre, the storyteller is called Sutradhar, which means "string bearer". 
- Puppetry in India has many types and forms, depending on the region, culture, and material of the puppets. Some of the major types are:
  - String puppets: These are puppets that are controlled by strings attached to their limbs and head. The most popular form of string puppetry in India is Kathputli, which is native to Rajasthan. Kathputli means "wooden doll" and the puppets are made of wood and dressed in colorful costumes. The puppeteers use a wooden platform as a stage and perform stories of folk heroes, kings, and gods.  
  - Shadow puppets: These are flat puppets that are made of leather, paper, or cloth and are held against a screen that is lit from behind. The puppeteers manipulate the puppets to create shadows that depict the characters and scenes of the story. The most famous form of shadow puppetry in India is Tholu Bommalata, which is practiced in Andhra Pradesh. Tholu Bommalata means "leather puppet dance" and the puppets are painted with vibrant colors and have movable joints. The puppeteers use musical instruments and songs to accompany the performance.  
  - Rod puppets: These are puppets that are controlled by rods attached to their limbs and head. The puppeteers hold the rods and move the puppets on a stage. The most well-known form of rod puppetry in India is Putul Nautch, which is prevalent in West Bengal. Putul Nautch means "doll dance" and the puppets are made of wood and cloth and have elaborate costumes and jewelry. The puppeteers use their own voices to narrate the stories and sing the songs.  
  - Glove puppets: These are puppets that are worn on the hands of the puppeteers and are controlled by the fingers. The puppeteers use their other hand to hold props or accessories for the puppets. The most common form of glove puppetry in India is Pavakoothu, which is found in Kerala. Pavakoothu means "puppet play" and the puppets are made of cloth and wood and have painted faces and hair. The puppeteers perform stories from the Ramayana and the Mahabharata in a special temple theatre.
