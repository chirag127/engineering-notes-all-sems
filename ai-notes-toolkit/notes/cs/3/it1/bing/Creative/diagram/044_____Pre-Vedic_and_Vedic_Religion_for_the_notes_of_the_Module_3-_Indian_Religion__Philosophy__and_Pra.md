### Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC) that flourished in the northwest of India from 2500 to 1500 BCE.
- Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indo-Aryan peoples who entered India from Persia around 1500 BCE and composed the four Vedas, the oldest sacred texts of Hinduism.

#### Pre-Vedic Religion

- The IVC was a highly urbanized and sophisticated civilization that had a complex social and economic organization.
- The IVC religion was polytheistic and worshipped various deities, some of which were associated with natural phenomena, animals, plants and human activities.
- The most prominent deity of the IVC was a horned male figure, often depicted in a yogic posture, surrounded by animals. He is sometimes identified as a proto-Shiva, the later Hindu god of destruction and regeneration.
- Another important deity of the IVC was a mother goddess, who represented fertility and nature. She is sometimes seen as a precursor of the later Hindu goddesses like Parvati, Durga and Kali.
- The IVC religion also had a strong cult of the bull, which was a symbol of strength and virility. The bull was often depicted on seals, coins and pottery.
- The IVC religion also practiced ritual bathing, fire worship, animal sacrifice and burial of the dead. Some of these practices may have influenced the later Vedic religion.

#### Vedic Religion

- The Vedic religion was based on the four Vedas, namely the Rigveda, the Samaveda, the Yajurveda and the Atharvaveda. The Vedas contain hymns, rituals, myths and philosophy of the Indo-Aryan peoples.
- The Vedic religion was also polytheistic and worshipped many gods, some of whom were derived from the Indo-European pantheon, such as Indra, the god of thunder and war, Varuna, the god of water and cosmic order, Agni, the god of fire and sacrifice, and Surya, the god of the sun.
- The Vedic religion also had some gods that were unique to India, such as Rudra, the god of storms and wildness, who later evolved into Shiva, and Vishnu, the god of preservation and protection, who later became the supreme deity of Hinduism.
- The Vedic religion also had some goddesses, such as Ushas, the goddess of dawn, Saraswati, the goddess of learning and speech, and Aditi, the mother of the gods.
- The Vedic religion also had a concept of a universal soul or spirit, called Brahman, which was the source and essence of all existence. The individual soul or self, called Atman, was seen as a part of Brahman.
- The Vedic religion also had a belief in reincarnation and karma, the law of cause and effect, which determined the fate of the soul after death. The goal of the Vedic religion was to achieve liberation or moksha, which was the release from the cycle of birth and death.
- The Vedic religion also had a complex system of rituals and sacrifices, called yajna, which were performed by priests, called brahmins, to please the gods and maintain the cosmic order. The rituals involved the use of fire, soma (a sacred plant), animals, grains and other offerings.
- The Vedic religion also had a social division of four classes, called varnas, which were based on occupation and birth. The four varnas were the brahmins (priests), the kshatriyas (warriors), the vaishyas (merchants and farmers) and the shudras (servants and laborers). The varnas later became the basis of the caste system in Hinduism.
