### Music

Music is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of performing arts in India. It has a long and rich history that spans across various regions, languages, religions, and genres. Music in India can be broadly classified into two main categories: classical and folk.

#### Classical Music

Classical music is the art music that follows the rules and principles of ancient texts and traditions. It is based on the concept of raga, which is a melodic framework that defines the notes, intervals, ornaments, and mood of a musical piece. Classical music is also influenced by the concept of tala, which is a rhythmic cycle that governs the tempo, accent, and structure of a musical piece.

There are two main traditions of classical music in India: Hindustani and Carnatic. Hindustani music is the classical music of North India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Nepal. It is influenced by Persian, Arabic, and Central Asian musical elements, as well as by the bhakti (devotional) movement of medieval India. Carnatic music is the classical music of South India, Sri Lanka, and parts of Southeast Asia. It is influenced by the ancient Tamil music, as well as by the Vedas, Upanishads, and other Hindu scriptures.

Some of the prominent composers and performers of classical music in India are:

- Bharata Muni, who wrote the Natyashastra, the oldest surviving text on music, dance, and drama, in the 2nd century BCE.
- Matanga, who wrote the Brihaddesi, a treatise on music theory and classification of ragas, in the 8th or 9th century CE.
- Sarangadeva, who wrote the Sangeet Ratnakara, a comprehensive encyclopedia of music, in the 13th century CE.
- Jayadeva, who composed the Gita Govinda, a lyrical poem on the love of Radha and Krishna, set to music in various ragas, in the 12th century CE.
- Amir Khusrau, who is considered the father of Hindustani music, and who introduced many Persian and Arabic musical elements, such as the qawwali, the ghazal, and the sitar, in the 13th and 14th centuries CE.
- Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, and Syama Sastri, who are known as the Trinity of Carnatic music, and who composed thousands of kritis (devotional songs) in various ragas and talas, in the 18th and 19th centuries CE.
- Pandit Ravi Shankar, Ustad Bismillah Khan, Ustad Ali Akbar Khan, Ustad Vilayat Khan, Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, Ustad Amir Khan, Ustad Bade Ghulam Ali Khan, M.S. Subbulakshmi, M.L. Vasanthakumari, and many others, who are some of the most renowned and influential classical musicians of the 20th and 21st centuries.

#### Folk Music

Folk music is the music of the people, that reflects their culture, traditions, emotions, and aspirations. It is usually transmitted orally, and varies from region to region, community to community, and occasion to occasion. Folk music is often accompanied by folk dances, folk instruments, and folk costumes.

Some of the popular forms of folk music in India are:

- Baul, a mystical and ecstatic music of the wandering minstrels of Bengal, that expresses the philosophy of love, devotion, and harmony.
- Bhangra, a lively and energetic music of the farmers of Punjab, that celebrates the harvest season, and is accompanied by vigorous dance moves and colorful attire.
- Dandiya, a festive and joyous music of the people of Gujarat, that is performed during the Navratri festival, and is accompanied by the clapping of sticks and the swirling of skirts.
- Ghazal, a poetic and romantic music of the Urdu-speaking regions, that expresses the themes of love, longing, and separation, and is influenced by Persian and Arabic musical elements.
- Lavani, a sensual and seductive music of the women of Maharashtra, that is performed in the tamasha (theatre) shows, and is accompanied by erotic dance moves and suggestive gestures.
- Qawwali, a devotional and ecstatic music of the Sufi saints and their followers, that praises Allah, the Prophet, and the saints, and is influenced by Persian and Arabic musical elements.
