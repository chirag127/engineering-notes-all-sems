# Āshrama or the Stages of Life

- Āshrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras.
- The four āshramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Gṛhastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate).
- The Āshrama system is one facet of the Dharma concept in Hinduism.
- The Āshrama system is meant to guide a person's moral and spiritual development throughout life.
- The four āshramas are briefly described below:

  - Brahmacharya: This is the stage of the student, marked by chastity, devotion, and obedience to one’s teacher. It lasts until around age 25, during which the student learns the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, and other subjects.
  - Gṛhastha: This is the stage of the householder, requiring marriage, the begetting of children, sustaining one’s family and society, and performing the rituals prescribed by the Vedas. It lasts until around age 50, during which the householder fulfills his or her worldly duties and pleasures.
  - Vanaprastha: This is the stage of the forest walker/forest dweller, marked by withdrawal from worldly attachments, and preparation for spiritual liberation. It lasts until around age 75, during which the person renounces material possessions, social obligations, and family ties, and lives a simple and austere life in the forest or a holy place.
  - Sannyasa: This is the stage of the renunciate, marked by complete detachment from worldly affairs, and pursuit of the highest knowledge and bliss. It lasts until death, during which the person renounces all identity, rituals, and social conventions, and lives as a wandering ascetic or a recluse, seeking the ultimate reality.

- The Āshrama system is not mandatory or universal in Hinduism. It is a flexible and idealistic model that varies according to individual, social, and historical circumstances.
- The Āshrama system is also not exclusive or sequential. A person may skip or reverse the order of the stages, or combine aspects of different stages, depending on his or her inclination and situation.
- The Āshrama system is a way of organizing and understanding the human life cycle in Hinduism, and it reflects the values and goals of Hindu culture and spirituality.
