# Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions, philosophies, and practices that have influenced its culture and history.
- The majority of Indians practice Hinduism, which is a major religious and cultural tradition of South Asia, which developed from Vedic religion.
- Hinduism is not a monolithic religion, but a collection of various schools, sects, movements, and traditions that share some common features, such as belief in reincarnation, karma, dharma, and the authority of the Vedas and other sacred texts.
- Hinduism has many philosophical texts that explore various aspects of reality, ethics, metaphysics, epistemology, and logic. Some of the prominent schools of Hindu philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, Vedanta, and Tantra.
- Hinduism also has many practices that aim to achieve liberation (moksha) from the cycle of birth and death, such as rituals, worship, meditation, yoga, ethics, and asceticism. Hinduism also celebrates many festivals and holidays that mark important events and seasons in the Hindu calendar.
- The other major religions in India are Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, Christianity, and Islam. Each of these religions has its own history, beliefs, doctrines, scriptures, and practices that have contributed to the diversity and richness of Indian culture.
- Sikhism is a monotheistic religion that originated in the 15th century in Punjab. It was founded by Guru Nanak and his successors, who are known as the ten Gurus. Sikhism teaches the unity of God, the equality of all people, the rejection of caste and ritualism, and the importance of service and social justice.
- Buddhism is a non-theistic religion that originated in the 6th century BCE in India. It was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, who is known as the Buddha or the Enlightened One. Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, and the concept of dependent origination, which explain the nature of suffering and the way to end it by attaining nirvana.
- Jainism is an ancient religion that originated in the 6th century BCE in India. It was founded by Mahavira, who is known as the 24th and last Tirthankara or spiritual teacher. Jainism teaches the principles of non-violence, non-attachment, and non-absolutism, and the practice of asceticism, vegetarianism, and environmentalism.
- Christianity is a monotheistic religion that originated in the 1st century CE in the Middle East. It is based on the teachings and life of Jesus Christ, who is regarded as the Son of God and the Savior of humanity. Christianity has many branches, such as Catholicism, Orthodoxy, Protestantism, and Anglicanism, that differ in doctrine, ritual, and organization.
- Islam is a monotheistic religion that originated in the 7th century CE in Arabia. It is based on the teachings and revelations of Muhammad, who is regarded as the final prophet of God. Islam has five pillars, which are the declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage, and two main sects, which are Sunni and Shia, that differ in leadership and jurisprudence.
- India also has a small percentage of population that practice other religions, such as Judaism, Zoroastrianism, and Baha'i Faith, which have their own histories and traditions.
- Religion and philosophy in India are not separate domains, but rather interrelated and intertwined aspects of human life and culture. They reflect the diversity, complexity, and creativity of the Indian civilization and its quest for meaning and transcendence.
