# Harappan Script

- Harappan script, also known as Indus script, is a corpus of symbols produced by the Indus Valley Civilisation.
- Harappan script was discovered during the excavation of Harappan city and other sites in the Indus Valley.
- Harappan script consists of very few signs and symbols, ranging from 400 to 600, which lack bilingual inscriptions and underlying language.
- Harappan script is mostly found on seals, pottery, copper tablets, and other objects, and most inscriptions are extremely short, making it difficult to judge whether or not they constituted a writing system .
- Harappan script has long defied attempts to read it, and therefore the language remains unknown. Several scholars have proposed different theories about the origin, nature, and meaning of the script, but none have been widely accepted.
- Harappan script is believed to be a logo-syllabic system, where some symbols express ideas or words while others represent sounds. This view is based on the fact that some signs are repeated or modified in patterns that suggest phonetic value.
- Harappan script is one of the oldest known scripts of India and a major source of information about the Indus Valley Civilisation. It reflects the culture, trade, religion, and administration of the Harappan people .
