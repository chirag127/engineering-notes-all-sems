# The Seven Limbs of the State

- The seven limbs of the state (saptanga) is a theory of statecraft given by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra .
- According to this theory, a state consists of seven essential elements that support its existence and functioning  .
- The seven limbs of the state are:
  - King: The king is the supreme authority and the head of the state. He is responsible for the peace, justice and stability of the state. He is independent and has the power to appoint and dismiss his ministers and officials .
  - Amatya: The amatya are the bureaucrats or the ministers who assist the king in administration and policy-making. They are selected on the basis of merit and loyalty. They are accountable to the king and the people for their actions .
  - Janapada: The janapada is the territory or the land of the state. It includes the natural resources, the population, the agriculture and the trade of the state. It is the source of revenue and security for the state .
  - Durga: The durga is the fort or the capital of the state. It is the center of political, economic and cultural activities of the state. It is the place where the king and his court reside. It is also the place where the state stores its treasure and weapons. It is the symbol of the state's strength and prestige .
  - Kosa: The kosa is the treasure or the wealth of the state. It includes the taxes, the fines, the gifts and the spoils of war collected by the state. It is used to finance the state's expenditure on administration, defense, welfare and development .
  - Danda: The danda is the coercive authority or the military force of the state. It is used to maintain law and order, to protect the state from external and internal enemies, and to enforce the king's commands. It is also used to expand the state's territory and influence  .
  - Mitra: The mitra is the ally or the friend of the state. It is another state that shares common interests and goals with the state. It is used to support the state in times of war and peace, to balance the power of the enemies, and to promote the state's welfare and prosperity .
- Kautilya argued that a state should have all the seven limbs in a balanced and harmonious manner. He also suggested various ways to strengthen and protect each limb from internal and external threats .
