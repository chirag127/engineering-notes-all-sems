# Marriage

Marriage is a social and legal institution that regulates the relationship between two individuals and their families. Marriage is considered as one of the most important rites of passage in India, and it has various cultural, religious and legal aspects.

Some of the main points to note about marriage in India are:

- Marriage in India varies according to the region, the religion, the community and the personal preferences of the bride and groom.
- The average age of marriage for women in India has increased to 21 years, according to the 2011 Census of India. In 2009, about 7% of women got married before the age of 18.
- Arranged marriages have long been the norm in Indian society, where the parents or elders of the families select the prospective partners for their children. However, in recent times, there has been a rise in love marriages, where the individuals choose their own partners based on mutual attraction and compatibility.
- In order to be married legally, both the bride and groom must complete certain requirements that are laid down by the laws. These include proof of residency, address, age, identity, consent, and absence of any pre-existing marriage or prohibited relationship.
- There are different laws governing marriage in India, depending on the religion of the parties. For example, the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 applies to Hindus, Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs; the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937 applies to Muslims; the Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872 applies to Christians; the Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936 applies to Parsis; and the Special Marriage Act, 1954 applies to inter-religious or inter-caste marriages.
- Marrying within one's caste remains an essential feature of marriage in India. In a 2014 survey of more than 70,000 people, fewer than 10% of urban Indians said that anyone in their family had married outside their caste and not many more outside their jati or sub-caste.
- Inter-faith marriages are also rare in India, and they often face social and legal challenges. According to the 2011 Census of India, only 0.7% of the total married population was in an inter-religious marriage.
- Marriage in India is not only a union of two individuals, but also of two families. The wedding ceremonies and rituals involve the participation and contribution of both the families, and they reflect the cultural and religious diversity of India.
- Marriage in India is also influenced by various factors such as education, income, urbanization, globalization, media, and changing values and aspirations. These factors have an impact on the choice of partner, the age of marriage, the type of marriage, the role of spouse, the decision to have children, and the marital satisfaction and stability.
