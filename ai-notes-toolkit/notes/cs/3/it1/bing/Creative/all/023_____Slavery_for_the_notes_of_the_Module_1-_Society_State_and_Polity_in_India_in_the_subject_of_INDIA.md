# Slavery in India

- Slavery in India is a historical and contemporary phenomenon that has existed in various forms and degrees across different regions, religions, and communities.
- Slavery in India can be traced back to the ancient times, when the caste system, debt bondage, and domestic servitude were prevalent. Some of the earliest references to slavery in India are found in the Vedas, the Dharmashastras, and the Arthashastra.
- Slavery in India intensified during the medieval period, especially under the Muslim rule, when millions of Indians were enslaved and transported to other parts of Asia and Africa as part of the Indian Ocean slave trade. The Muslim rulers also imposed various restrictions and taxes on the non-Muslim population, such as the jizya and the kharaj, which often forced them into slavery or servitude.
- Slavery in India continued during the colonial era, when the British East India Company and other European powers exploited the Indian resources and labor. Many Indians were coerced or deceived into working as indentured laborers in plantations, mines, and factories in various parts of the world, such as the Caribbean, Mauritius, Fiji, and South Africa.
- Slavery in India was officially abolished by the British government in 1843, but it did not end the practice of bonded labor, child labor, and human trafficking, which still persist in the country. According to the Global Slavery Index, India has the highest number of people living in modern slavery, with an estimated 8 million victims in 2016. Some of the sectors where modern slavery is prevalent in India are brick kilns, rice mills, embroidery factories, quarries, farms, brothels, and domestic work. 
- Slavery in India is a complex and multifaceted issue that is influenced by various factors, such as poverty, illiteracy, social discrimination, gender inequality, corruption, and lack of law enforcement. The Indian government and various civil society organizations have taken several measures to combat slavery, such as enacting laws, implementing policies, raising awareness, providing rehabilitation, and empowering the vulnerable groups. However, much more needs to be done to eradicate slavery from India and ensure the dignity and freedom of all its citizens.
