# Telugu Literature

- Telugu literature is the body of works written in the Telugu language, a Dravidian language spoken mainly in the states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in India.
- Telugu literature consists of various genres, such as poems, short stories, novels, plays, and song lyrics, among others.
- Telugu literature dates back to at least the middle of the first millennium CE, but the first extant works are from the 11th century CE, when the Mahabharata was first translated into Telugu by Nannaya.
- Telugu literature can be broadly divided into four periods: Ancient (11th to 14th century), Medieval (15th to 18th century), Modern (19th to 20th century), and Contemporary (21st century).
- Some of the prominent features and themes of Telugu literature are:

  - The influence of Sanskrit literature, especially the epics, Puranas, and classical poetry.
  - The development of various forms of poetry, such as padya (metrical verse), gadya (prose), champu (mixed prose and verse), and dwipada (couplet).
  - The use of various meters, such as anustubh, vritta, and champakamala.
  - The expression of various emotions, such as love, devotion, heroism, and humor.
  - The depiction of various aspects of culture, such as religion, philosophy, history, society, and art.
  - The contribution of various literary movements, such as bhakti (devotional), prabandha (narrative), navala (novel), and dalita (oppressed).

- Some of the notable authors and works of Telugu literature are:

  - Nannaya, Tikkana, and Errana, who translated the Mahabharata into Telugu and are known as the Kavitraya (trio of poets).
  - Srinatha, who wrote the Haravilasa, a courtly epic, and the Sringara Naishadha, a romantic epic.
  - Bammera Potana, who wrote the Andhra Maha Bhagavatamu, a Telugu version of the Bhagavata Purana.
  - Annamacharya, who composed thousands of devotional songs in praise of Lord Venkateswara.
  - Vemana, who wrote simple and profound verses on various topics, such as morality, spirituality, and social justice.
  - Kshetrayya, who wrote erotic padams (songs) on the theme of madhura bhakti (love for God as a lover).
  - Gurajada Apparao, who wrote the first Telugu play, Kanyasulkam, a social satire.
  - Viswanatha Satyanarayana, who wrote the Ramayana Kalpavrikshamu, a modern epic, and won the Jnanpith Award, the highest literary honor in India.
  - Sri Sri, who wrote the Mahaprasthanam, a collection of revolutionary poems.
  - C. Narayana Reddy, who wrote the Viswambhara, a poetic novel, and also won the Jnanpith Award.
  - Gunturu Seshendra Sarma, who wrote the Naa Desam, Naa Prajalu, a patriotic epic, and the Kaala Rekha, a literary criticism.
  - Volga, who wrote the Vimukta, a feminist novel, and the Sahitya Akademi Award, a prestigious literary award.
