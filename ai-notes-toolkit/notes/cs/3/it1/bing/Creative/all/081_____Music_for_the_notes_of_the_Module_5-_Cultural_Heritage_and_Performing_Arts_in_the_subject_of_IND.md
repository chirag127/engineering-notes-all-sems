# Music for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Music is one of the most ancient and diverse forms of intangible cultural heritage in India, reflecting the rich and complex history, geography, and diversity of the country.
- There are two major traditions of Indian classical music: Carnatic music, a style associated with South India, and Hindustani music, a style associated with North India. Both traditions have their roots in the ancient Vedic hymns and the Natyashastra, a treatise on performing arts attributed to Bharata Muni.
- Carnatic music is based on a system of 72 melakartas or parent scales, each of which has a set of seven notes or swaras. Carnatic music also uses a variety of ragas or modes, which are derived from the melakartas by altering one or more swaras. Carnatic music is mainly vocal, with the singer accompanied by instruments such as the veena, violin, flute, mridangam, and ghatam.
- Hindustani music is based on a system of 10 thaats or parent scales, each of which has a set of seven notes or swaras. Hindustani music also uses a variety of ragas or modes, which are derived from the thaats by adding or omitting notes, or by using microtones or shrutis. Hindustani music can be vocal or instrumental, with instruments such as the sitar, sarod, sarangi, bansuri, shehnai, tabla, and harmonium.
- Both Carnatic and Hindustani music follow a structure of alapana, a slow and improvised introduction of the raga, followed by a composition or kriti, which consists of a pallavi or refrain, an anupallavi or second section, and one or more charanams or verses. The composition may also include a niraval, a melodic variation of a line of the pallavi, and a swaraprastara, a rhythmic variation of the swaras of the raga.
- Apart from classical music, India also has a rich and diverse tradition of folk music, which varies from region to region and reflects the local culture, language, and lifestyle of the people. Folk music is often associated with rituals, festive events, oral traditions, or social movements. Some examples of folk music are bhangra, dandiya, lavani, baul, bhajan, qawwali, and ghazal.
- India also has a vibrant and popular tradition of film music, which emerged in the early 20th century with the advent of cinema. Film music is influenced by various genres of Indian and Western music, and often incorporates elements of classical, folk, and modern music. Film music is composed by music directors, who work with lyricists, singers, and musicians to create songs that suit the theme, mood, and genre of the film. Some of the most famous music directors in Indian cinema are Naushad, S.D. Burman, R.D. Burman, A.R. Rahman, and Shankar-Ehsaan-Loy.
- Music is an integral part of the Indian cultural heritage and performing arts, and has contributed to the enrichment and diversity of the global musical landscape. Music is also a medium of expression, communication, and education, and has a positive impact on the social, emotional, and cognitive development of the people. Music is a source of joy, inspiration, and creativity for millions of Indians and lovers of Indian music around the world.
