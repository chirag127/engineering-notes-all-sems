# Sculptures

Sculptures are three-dimensional art forms that depict the human or animal forms, or abstract shapes, using materials such as stone, metal, wood, clay, or ivory. Sculptures are one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in the Indian subcontinent, dating back to the Indus Valley civilization (3300–1700 BCE) and continuing to the present day. Sculptures have been used to represent the religious, cultural, and aesthetic values of the Indian people, as well as to commemorate historical events, rulers, and heroes.

Some of the main features and characteristics of Indian sculptures are:

- They are often integrated with the architecture of temples, monuments, caves, and other structures, creating a harmonious and dynamic relationship between the art and the space.
- They are influenced by various religious traditions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam, and Christianity, and reflect the diversity and syncretism of the Indian culture.
- They are rich in symbolism, iconography, and motifs, conveying complex and profound meanings and messages to the viewers.
- They are expressive of the emotions, moods, and gestures of the human and divine figures, as well as the natural elements, such as animals, plants, and water.
- They are stylistically diverse, varying according to the regions, periods, dynasties, and schools of art, such as the Mauryan, Kushan, Gupta, Pallava, Chola, Hoysala, Khajuraho, Pala, Mughal, and Rajput styles.

Some of the famous examples of Indian sculptures are:

- The Dancing Girl, Mohen Jo Daro, c. 2500 BCE: A small bronze figurine of a young girl in a dancing pose, wearing bangles and a necklace, found in the Indus Valley civilization site of Mohen Jo Daro. It is one of the earliest examples of metal casting and realistic representation of the human body in Indian art .
- The Ashoka Pillar, Sarnath, 3rd century BCE: A stone pillar erected by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka at the site where Buddha gave his first sermon. It has a four-lion capital on top, which is the emblem of India, and a wheel with 24 spokes, which is the symbol of Buddhism. It also has inscriptions in Brahmi script, which are the edicts of Ashoka, promoting peace, justice, and dharma.
- The Buddha Statue, Sarnath, 5th century CE: A sandstone statue of Buddha in the Dharmachakra Mudra, or the gesture of turning the wheel of law, found in the Gupta period site of Sarnath. It is one of the finest examples of the idealized and serene depiction of Buddha in Indian art, with a halo, a monastic robe, and a lotus pedestal.
- The Nataraja, Chola Bronze Sculpture, 11th century CE: A bronze statue of Shiva as the lord of dance, or Nataraja, found in the Chola period site of Thanjavur. It is one of the most iconic and dynamic representations of Shiva in Indian art, showing him dancing in a ring of fire, with four arms, holding a drum, a flame, a mudra, and a snake, and standing on a dwarf, symbolizing the cosmic cycle of creation and destruction .
- The Khajuraho Temples, 10th-12th century CE: A group of Hindu and Jain temples in Madhya Pradesh, famous for their erotic sculptures, depicting various aspects of human sexuality, love, and desire, as well as scenes from mythology, history, and daily life. They are examples of the Nagara style of architecture, with elaborate carvings, spires, and mandapas.
