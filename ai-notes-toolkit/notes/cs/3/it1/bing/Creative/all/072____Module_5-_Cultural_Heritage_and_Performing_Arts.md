# Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- Cultural heritage is the legacy of physical and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations.
- Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art, and artefacts), intangible culture (such as folklore, traditions, language, and knowledge), and natural heritage (such as culturally significant landscapes, and biodiversity).
- Performing arts are forms of art that use the body, voice, or objects to convey artistic expression, such as music, dance, theatre, opera, mime, puppetry, circus arts, and performance art.
- Performing arts are often performed in front of a live audience, but may also be recorded or broadcasted.
- Performing arts are part of the intangible cultural heritage of many cultures and play a vital role in preserving and transmitting history, values, beliefs, and identity.
- Performing arts can also be a source of creativity, innovation, and social cohesion, as well as a means of education, communication, and entertainment.
- Some examples of performing arts that are recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage are:

  - Urtiin Duu, traditional folk long song from Mongolia, which is sung in a specific vocal technique that requires great skill and endurance.
  - Wayang puppet theatre from Indonesia, which is a form of storytelling that uses flat leather puppets manipulated by rods, accompanied by a gamelan orchestra.
  - Flamenco from Spain, which is a musical and dance expression that combines singing, guitar playing, hand clapping, finger snapping, and dancing, influenced by various cultures and regions.
  - Kabuki from Japan, which is a classical theatre form that combines drama, music, dance, and elaborate costumes and make-up, characterized by stylized movements and speech.
  - Tango from Argentina and Uruguay, which is a social dance and musical genre that originated in the late 19th century among the lower classes of Buenos Aires and Montevideo, and reflects the influences of African, European, and indigenous cultures.

- To protect and promote the diversity and vitality of the performing arts, UNESCO has established several conventions and programs, such as:

  - The Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage (2003), which aims to safeguard the practices, representations, expressions, knowledge, and skills that communities, groups, and individuals recognize as part of their cultural heritage.
  - The Convention on the Protection and Promotion of the Diversity of Cultural Expressions (2005), which seeks to foster the creation, production, distribution, and enjoyment of cultural goods and services, as well as the mobility of artists and cultural professionals.
  - The International Fund for Cultural Diversity (IFCD), which is a voluntary fund that supports projects that strengthen the governance and capacities of the cultural sector, as well as the emergence of dynamic cultural industries in developing countries.
  - The UNESCO Creative Cities Network (UCCN), which is a network of cities that have identified creativity as a strategic factor for sustainable urban development, and that cooperate in the fields of literature, film, music, crafts and folk art, design, media arts, and gastronomy.
