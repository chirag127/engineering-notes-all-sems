# Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya is a title given to the heads of monastic institutions (mathas) in the Advaita Vedanta tradition of Hinduism. The title means "teacher of the way of Shankara", referring to the 8th-century philosopher and theologian Adi Shankara, who is considered the founder of the Advaita Vedanta school.
- Adi Shankara was born in a Brahmin family in Kaladi, Kerala, around the 8th century CE. He was a prodigious scholar and a prolific writer, who composed commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, as well as original works such as the Vivekachudamani and the Upadesasahasri.
- Adi Shankara's main contribution was to revive and systematize the Advaita Vedanta philosophy, which teaches that the ultimate reality is Brahman, the impersonal and transcendent essence of all existence, and that the individual self (atman) is identical with Brahman. He also refuted the doctrines of other schools of Hindu philosophy, such as the Nyaya, the Vaisheshika, the Samkhya, the Yoga, the Mimamsa, and the various forms of theism.
- Adi Shankara also established four mathas in the four corners of India, namely the Sringeri Sharada Peetham in the south, the Dwarka Sharada Peetham in the west, the Jyotirmath in the north, and the Govardhana Matha in the east. These mathas are responsible for preserving and propagating the Advaita Vedanta tradition, and each matha is headed by a Shankaracharya, who is appointed by his predecessor.
- Adi Shankara is widely revered and respected by Hindus of various sects and traditions, and is regarded as one of the greatest philosophers and teachers of India. His teachings have influenced many thinkers and movements, both within and outside Hinduism, such as the Neo-Vedanta, the Ramakrishna Mission, the Hindu reform movements, and the modern Hindu revivalism.
