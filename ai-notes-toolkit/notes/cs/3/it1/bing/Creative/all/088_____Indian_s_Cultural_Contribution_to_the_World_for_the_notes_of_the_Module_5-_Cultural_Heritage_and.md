# Indian's Cultural Contribution to the World

India is a country of diverse and rich cultural heritage, with a history of more than 5000 years. India has influenced and been influenced by various civilizations and cultures across the world, through trade, migration, religion, art, literature, science, and technology. Some of the notable contributions of India to the world are:

- India gave the world its first university, Takshashila University, which was established around the 6th century BCE and attracted scholars from various regions and disciplines .
- India gave the world the numeral system, including the concept and symbol of zero, which was developed by Indian mathematicians around the 5th century CE and later transmitted to the Arab world and Europe .
- India gave the world the game of chess, which originated from an ancient Indian game called chaturanga, meaning "four divisions of the military". The game evolved over time and spread to Persia, China, Europe, and other regions .
- India gave the world the use and invention of buttons, which were first used in India as early as 2000 BCE. Buttons were made of seashells, metal, ivory, wood, and other materials, and were used to fasten clothes and ornaments .
- India gave the world the shampoo, which derived from the Sanskrit word champu, meaning "to massage". Shampoo was a herbal mixture of various ingredients, such as gooseberry, soapberries, hibiscus, and other plants, that was used to cleanse and condition the hair .
- India gave the world the cure for leprosy, which was a chronic and infectious disease that affected the skin, nerves, and other organs. The ancient Indian physician Sushruta described the symptoms and treatment of leprosy in his medical treatise Sushruta Samhita, which was written around the 6th century BCE. He prescribed various remedies, such as oil, resin, mercury, and surgical procedures, to treat the disease .
- India gave the world the game of snakes and ladders, which was inspired from an Indian game called mokshapat, meaning "the ladder to salvation". The game was a moral lesson that taught the players about the effects of good and bad deeds on their journey to liberation. The game was later modified and popularized by the British in the 19th century .
- India gave the world the art of yoga, which is a system of physical, mental, and spiritual practices that originated in India around the 5th century BCE. Yoga aims to achieve harmony and balance between the body, mind, and soul, and to attain a state of enlightenment. Yoga has various branches, such as hatha, raja, karma, bhakti, jnana, and kundalini, and has influenced various religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism .
- India gave the world the concept of non-violence, which is a principle of peace and harmony that rejects the use of violence and aggression as a means of resolving conflicts. The concept of non-violence was advocated by various Indian leaders and thinkers, such as Mahatma Gandhi, who led the Indian independence movement against the British colonial rule, and inspired many other movements and activists around the world, such as Martin Luther King Jr., Nelson Mandela, Dalai Lama, and Aung San Suu Kyi .
- India gave the world the literature of epics, such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, which are among the longest and oldest poems in the world. The epics narrate the stories of gods, kings, heroes, and villains, and explore various themes, such as duty, morality, love, war, and spirituality. The epics have influenced various forms of art, such as drama, dance, music, painting, sculpture, and cinema, and have been translated and adapted into various languages and cultures .
