# Theatre

Theatre is a form of performing art that involves actors, stage, audience, and script. Theatre is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of cultural expression in India. It has a rich and varied history that spans from ancient times to the present day. Theatre in India reflects the social, religious, and political aspects of the Indian society and culture.

## Origins of Theatre in India

- The earliest evidence of theatre in India can be traced back to the **Vedas**, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism, which contain hymns, rituals, and dialogues that were recited or sung by priests and sages.
- The **Natya Shastra**, a treatise on theatre written by **Bharata Muni** in the **2nd century BCE**, is considered the foundational text of Indian theatre. It describes the theory and practice of various aspects of theatre, such as drama, music, dance, costumes, makeup, stage design, and audience response .
- The **Sanskrit drama**, which flourished from the **1st century BCE** to the **10th century CE**, is the most prominent form of classical theatre in India. It was influenced by the Natya Shastra and the epics of **Ramayana** and **Mahabharata**. It was characterized by elaborate plots, poetic language, stylized gestures, and musical accompaniment. Some of the famous playwrights of Sanskrit drama were **Kalidasa**, **Bhasa**, **Shudraka**, and **Vishakhadatta** .
- The **regional theatre**, which developed from the **11th century CE** onwards, is the diverse and vibrant form of theatre that emerged in different parts of India. It was influenced by the local languages, cultures, folk traditions, and religious movements. It included various genres, such as comedy, tragedy, romance, satire, and historical drama. Some of the prominent forms of regional theatre are **Yakshagana** (Karnataka), **Kathakali** (Kerala), **Koodiyattam** (Kerala), **Bhavai** (Gujarat), **Nautanki** (Uttar Pradesh), **Jatra** (West Bengal), **Tamasha** (Maharashtra), **Bhaona** (Assam), and **Rasleela** (Manipur) .

## Characteristics of Theatre in India

- Theatre in India is a **narrative form**, in which recitation, singing, and dancing are integral elements. It often involves a **sutradhara** or a narrator who introduces the characters, sets the scene, and comments on the action.
- Theatre in India is a **synthesis of various art forms**, such as literature, music, dance, painting, sculpture, and architecture. It encompasses a wide range of styles, techniques, and themes, from realistic to symbolic, from classical to folk, from religious to secular.
- Theatre in India is a **reflection of the society and culture**, in which it is performed. It depicts the values, beliefs, customs, and conflicts of the Indian people. It also responds to the historical, political, and social changes that occur in the country.
- Theatre in India is a **medium of communication and education**, in which the performers and the audience interact and exchange ideas and emotions. It also serves as a tool for social awareness, criticism, and reform.

## Modern Theatre in India

- The modern theatre in India emerged in the **19th century**, under the influence of the **British colonial rule** and the **Western theatre**. It was initially a form of entertainment for the elite and educated classes, who preferred English or European languages and themes.
- The modern theatre in India also witnessed the rise of the **nationalist movement**, which used theatre as a means of expressing the aspirations and struggles of the Indian people. It also incorporated the elements of the classical and regional theatre, and experimented with new forms and techniques.
- The modern theatre in India also saw the emergence of the **progressive and radical theatre**, which challenged the social and political inequalities and injustices in the country. It also explored the issues of identity, gender, caste, class, and religion.
