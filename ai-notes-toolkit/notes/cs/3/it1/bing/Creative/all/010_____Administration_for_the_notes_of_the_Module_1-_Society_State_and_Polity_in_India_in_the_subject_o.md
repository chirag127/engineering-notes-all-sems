# Administration for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Administration is the process of managing the affairs of an organization or a state by implementing policies, rules and regulations.
- Administration in India has a long and diverse history, reflecting the changing socio-political and cultural contexts of the subcontinent.
- Administration in India can be broadly divided into four phases: ancient, medieval, colonial and post-colonial.
- Ancient administration in India was based on the principles of dharma (duty, righteousness, law), artha (wealth, power, politics), kama (desire, pleasure, enjoyment) and moksha (liberation, salvation, transcendence).
- Ancient administration in India was characterized by the emergence of various forms of polity, such as monarchy, oligarchy, republic, confederation and empire, as well as the development of administrative institutions, such as the king, the council of ministers, the bureaucracy, the army, the judiciary and the revenue system.
- Ancient administration in India was influenced by the religious and philosophical traditions of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and other sects, as well as the cultural and intellectual exchanges with other regions, such as Persia, Greece, China and Central Asia.
- Medieval administration in India was marked by the advent of Islam and the establishment of various dynasties, such as the Delhi Sultanate, the Vijayanagara Empire, the Mughal Empire and the regional kingdoms.
- Medieval administration in India was characterized by the adaptation and integration of the Islamic and Persian models of administration, such as the sultan, the wazir, the diwan, the mansab, the jagir, the sharia and the ulama, with the existing indigenous systems of administration, such as the raja, the mantri, the samanta, the zamindar, the dharma and the panchayat.
- Medieval administration in India was influenced by the religious and cultural diversity of the subcontinent, as well as the political and economic challenges posed by the external invasions, such as the Mongols, the Turks, the Afghans and the Europeans.
- Colonial administration in India was initiated by the European powers, such as the Portuguese, the Dutch, the French and the British, who established their trading posts and colonies in various parts of India.
- Colonial administration in India was characterized by the domination and exploitation of the British East India Company and the British Crown, who imposed their political, economic, social and cultural policies, such as the doctrine of lapse, the permanent settlement, the ryotwari system, the subsidiary alliance, the civil service, the railways, the education, the census and the law, on the Indian people.
- Colonial administration in India was influenced by the resistance and reform movements of the Indian people, such as the revolt of 1857, the Indian National Congress, the Muslim League, the Swadeshi movement, the Gandhian movement, the Quit India movement and the Partition of India.
- Post-colonial administration in India was established by the Constitution of India, which came into force on 26 January 1950, after India attained independence from the British rule on 15 August 1947.
- Post-colonial administration in India is characterized by the adoption of a democratic, federal, secular and socialist system of governance, which is based on the principles of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.
- Post-colonial administration in India is characterized by the division of powers and functions between the three organs of the government, namely the legislature, the executive and the judiciary, as well as the three levels of the government, namely the union, the state and the local.
- Post-colonial administration in India is influenced by the challenges and opportunities of the contemporary world, such as the globalization, the liberalization, the privatization, the decentralization, the development, the diversity, the security and the human rights.
