# Northern Indian Languages & Literature

- Northern Indian languages are derived from the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family, which originated from the ancient Sanskrit language.
- The development of these languages was influenced by various factors, such as the migration of people, the contact with other cultures, the religious movements, the literary traditions, and the political changes .
- Some of the major Northern Indian languages are Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, Kashmiri, Nepali, and Sindhi .
- These languages have different scripts, such as Devanagari, Perso-Arabic, Gurmukhi, Bengali, Gujarati, and Marathi .
- These languages also have different dialects, such as Braj, Awadhi, Bhojpuri, Maithili, Magahi, Rajasthani, and Pahari in Hindi; Dakhini, Rekhta, and Modern Standard Urdu in Urdu; Malwai, Majhi, Doabi, and Pothwari in Punjabi; and Chittagonian, Sylheti, and Rangpuri in Bengali .
- Northern Indian literature is rich and diverse, reflecting the cultural and historical heritage of the region. It includes various genres, such as poetry, drama, prose, fiction, and non-fiction  .
- Some of the prominent themes of Northern Indian literature are love, devotion, heroism, social justice, nationalism, and modernity  .
- Some of the famous authors and works of Northern Indian literature are:

  - Kalidasa, who wrote classic Sanskrit plays like Mālavikāgnimitram, Abhijñānaśākuntalam and Vikramōrvaśīyam and poems like Raghuvaṃśa, Kumārasambhava, Ṛtusaṃhāra and Meghadūta .
  - Kabir, who composed mystical and devotional poems in Hindi and Urdu, expressing his views on God, religion, and society  .
  - Guru Nanak, who founded Sikhism and wrote hymns in Punjabi, Hindi, and Persian, emphasizing the unity of God and the equality of all people  .
  - Mirza Ghalib, who is regarded as the greatest Urdu poet, who wrote ghazals and letters, expressing his emotions, thoughts, and experiences  .
  - Rabindranath Tagore, who is the first Asian Nobel laureate in literature, who wrote poems, songs, novels, short stories, and essays in Bengali and English, exploring various aspects of human life and nature  .
  - Munshi Premchand, who is considered as the father of modern Hindi and Urdu fiction, who wrote novels and short stories, depicting the realistic and social issues of the rural and urban India  .
  - Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay, who is one of the most popular Bengali writers, who wrote novels and short stories, focusing on the themes of love, family, and society  .
  - Mahadevi Varma, who is one of the leading Hindi poets of the 20th century, who wrote lyrical poems, expressing her views on feminism, nature, and spirituality  .
  - Saadat Hasan Manto, who is one of the most controversial and celebrated Urdu writers, who wrote short stories, essays, and sketches, portraying the harsh realities of the Partition of India and Pakistan  .
  - Amrita Pritam, who is one of the most prominent Punjabi writers, who wrote poems, novels, and essays, exploring the themes of love, war, feminism, and identity  .
