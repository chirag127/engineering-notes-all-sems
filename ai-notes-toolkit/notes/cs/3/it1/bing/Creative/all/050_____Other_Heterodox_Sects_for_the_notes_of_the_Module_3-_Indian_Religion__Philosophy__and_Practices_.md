# Other Heterodox Sects

- Heterodox sects are those that do not accept the authority of the Vedas and the Brahmanical tradition.
- They emerged in the 6th century BCE as a reaction to the rigid social hierarchy, ritualism and metaphysics of the orthodox schools.
- They challenged the concepts of caste, karma, rebirth, ritual sacrifice, and the existence of God or soul.
- They offered alternative paths of liberation based on ethical conduct, rational inquiry, and ascetic practices.
- Some of the heterodox sects are:

  - **Ajivika**: Founded by Makkhali Gosala, this sect believed in fatalism, i.e., everything is predetermined by fate and nothing can be changed by human effort. They rejected the notions of free will, karma, and moral responsibility. They practiced extreme asceticism and nudity. They were popular among the lower castes and had royal patronage from Bindusara, the father of Ashoka. They declined after the Mauryan period and survived only in some parts of South India until the 14th century CE .
  - **Unchedvadi**: Also known as Lokayata or Charvaka, this sect was founded by Brihaspati, a mythical sage. They advocated materialism, i.e., only matter is real and there is no soul, God, or life after death. They rejected the notions of karma, rebirth, and liberation. They valued sensory perception as the only source of knowledge and dismissed inference, analogy, and testimony as unreliable. They promoted hedonism, i.e., the pursuit of pleasure as the highest good. They were criticized by the orthodox and other heterodox schools for their lack of morality and spirituality. They had some influence on the Indian intellectual tradition until the medieval period .
  - **Nityavadi**: This sect was founded by Purana Kassapa, a contemporary of the Buddha. They believed in eternalism, i.e., everything is eternal and unchanging. They denied the notions of causation, action, and result. They argued that there is no difference between good and evil, and no consequences for one's deeds. They were opposed by the Buddha and other heterodox teachers for their moral nihilism .
  - **Sandehvadi**: This sect was founded by Sanjaya Belatthiputta, another contemporary of the Buddha. They believed in agnosticism, i.e., nothing can be known for certain. They suspended judgment on all metaphysical and ethical questions. They neither affirmed nor denied the existence of God, soul, karma, rebirth, or liberation. They were criticized by the Buddha and other heterodox teachers for their indecisiveness and skepticism .
  - **Akriyavadi**: This sect was founded by Pakudha Kaccayana, yet another contemporary of the Buddha. They believed in amoralism, i.e., there is no moral value or obligation. They claimed that there are seven eternal and unchanging elements: earth, water, fire, air, pleasure, pain, and soul. They asserted that these elements do not interact with each other and are not affected by any action. They denied the notions of karma, rebirth, and liberation. They were refuted by the Buddha and other heterodox teachers for their irrational and absurd views .
