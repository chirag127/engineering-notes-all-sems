# The Upanishads

- The Upanishads are late Vedic Sanskrit texts that supplied the basis of later Hindu philosophy.
- The Upanishads are commonly referred to as Vedānta, which means "the end of the Veda" or "the highest purpose of the Veda".
- The aim of all Upanishads is to investigate the nature of Ātman (self), and direct the enquirer toward it.
- There are more than 200 known Upanishads, but the most influential ones are the 12 principal Upanishads, which date from the 6th to the 3rd century BCE .
- The Upanishads present a vision of an interconnected universe with a single, unifying principle behind the apparent diversity in the cosmos, any articulation of which is called brahman.
- The Upanishads teach that brahman resides in the atman, the unchanging core of the human individual, and that the goal of life is to realize this identity and attain liberation (moksha) from the cycle of rebirth (samsara).
- The Upanishads also introduce concepts such as karma (action and its consequences), dharma (duty or law), and yoga (discipline or practice) as means to achieve this realization .
- The Upanishads are the source of many philosophical and spiritual ideas that have influenced Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and other traditions .
- The Upanishads are also considered as the foundation of Indian culture, literature, art, and science, as they contain insights on ethics, psychology, cosmology, metaphysics, and aesthetics .
