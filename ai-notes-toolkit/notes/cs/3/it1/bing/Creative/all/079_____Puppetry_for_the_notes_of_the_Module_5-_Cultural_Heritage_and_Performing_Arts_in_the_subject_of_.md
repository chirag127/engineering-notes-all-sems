# Puppetry

Puppetry is an ancient art form that involves storytelling through manipulating puppets. Puppets are figures that represent humans, animals, or mythical beings, and are controlled by strings, rods, wires, or hands. Puppetry is a form of performing arts that can convey various themes, emotions, and messages through the puppets' movements, gestures, and voices.

## Puppetry in India

India is said to be the home of puppets, as almost all types of puppets are found in India. Puppetry in India dates back to thousands of years, and has references in folk tales, ballads, and songs. Puppetry in India has a rich and diverse tradition, and reflects the cultural and regional diversity of the country. Puppetry in India is also a family business, as the skills and knowledge of puppet making and performing are passed down from generation to generation.

### Types of Puppetry in India

There are four main types of puppetry in India, based on the way the puppets are controlled. They are:

- **String puppets**: These are puppets that are controlled by strings attached to their limbs and head. The strings are manipulated by the puppeteer from above, using a wooden device called a kath or a kundal. String puppets are also known as kathputli, gombeyatta, bommalattam, or kundhei, depending on the region. String puppets are the most popular and widespread form of puppetry in India, and are found in states like Rajasthan, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Orissa, and West Bengal. String puppets are usually made of wood, cloth, leather, or paper, and are decorated with bright colors and costumes. String puppets can perform complex movements and expressions, and are used to depict stories from epics, legends, history, and folklore.

- **Rod puppets**: These are puppets that are controlled by rods attached to their limbs and head. The rods are manipulated by the puppeteer from below, using one or both hands. Rod puppets are also known as putul nautch, yampuri, or kathi kandhe, depending on the region. Rod puppets are found in states like West Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha. Rod puppets are usually made of wood, cloth, or bamboo, and are larger and heavier than string puppets. Rod puppets can perform simple movements and expressions, and are used to depict stories from epics, legends, history, and folklore.

- **Shadow puppets**: These are puppets that are made of translucent or opaque materials, and are projected on a screen using a light source. The puppets are manipulated by the puppeteer from behind the screen, using rods or wires. Shadow puppets are also known as tholu bommalata, tolg bommalattam, ravanchhaya, or chamadyacha bahulya, depending on the region. Shadow puppets are found in states like Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Odisha. Shadow puppets are usually made of leather, paper, or cloth, and are cut and painted in intricate designs. Shadow puppets can create various effects and illusions, and are used to depict stories from epics, legends, history, and folklore.

- **Glove puppets**: These are puppets that are worn on the hands of the puppeteer, and are controlled by the fingers. The puppeteer uses one or both hands to manipulate the puppets, and also provides the voice and narration. Glove puppets are also known as pavakoothu, pavai koothu, or gopalila kundhei, depending on the region. Glove puppets are found in states like Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, and Odisha. Glove puppets are usually made of cloth, leather, or wood, and are decorated with costumes and accessories. Glove puppets can perform various movements and expressions, and are used to depict stories from epics, legends, history, and folklore.
