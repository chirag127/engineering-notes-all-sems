# Brahmi Script

- Brahmi script is the earliest writing system developed in India after the Indus script.
- It is one of the most influential writing systems; all modern Indian scripts and several hundred scripts found in Southeast and East Asia are derived from Brahmi.
- It appeared as a fully developed script in the third century BCE.
- It is mentioned in the ancient Indian texts of Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism, as well as their Chinese translations.
- It is a syllabic script, meaning that each symbol represents a syllable, usually consisting of a consonant and a vowel.
- It is written from left to right, with spaces between words.
- It has 33 basic consonants and 9 basic vowels, as well as various modifiers and diacritics.
- It has two main variants: northern Brahmi and southern Brahmi, which differ in the shapes and forms of some letters.
- It was used for writing various languages, such as Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali, Tamil, and others.
- It was gradually replaced by its descendants, the Brahmic scripts, from around the 5th century CE onwards.
- Some of the Brahmic scripts that evolved from Brahmi are Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Gurmukhi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Sinhala, Thai, Lao, Tibetan, and Khmer.
- Brahmi script is considered a cultural and linguistic heritage of India and Asia.
