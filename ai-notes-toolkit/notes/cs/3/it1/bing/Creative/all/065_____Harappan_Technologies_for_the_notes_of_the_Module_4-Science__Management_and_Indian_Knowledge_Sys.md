# Harappan Technologies

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world, dating from 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappans developed various technologies and crafts, such as metallurgy, seal carving, pottery, jewelry, weights and measures, and urban planning.
- Some of the main features of Harappan technologies are:

  - Metallurgy: The Harappans used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, vessels, and ornaments. They knew how to alloy copper with tin to make bronze, which was harder and more durable. They also used the lost-wax process to cast metal figurines of men and animals.
  - Seal carving: The Harappans carved distinctive seals, usually made of steatite, a soft stone, with various motifs and inscriptions. They used these seals to identify property, stamp clay on trade goods, and possibly as amulets or tokens of authority. The seals often depicted animals, such as bulls, elephants, rhinoceroses, and unicorns, as well as human or divine figures.
  - Pottery: The Harappans produced fine pottery, mostly wheel-made, with various shapes, sizes, and designs. They used red clay, often coated with a red or black slip, and decorated with geometric patterns, floral motifs, or animal figures. Some of the common types of pottery were storage jars, cooking pots, dishes, bowls, cups, and spouted vessels.
  - Jewelry: The Harappans were skilled in making jewelry from materials such as gold, silver, copper, bronze, ivory, shell, bone, and beads. They made necklaces, bracelets, earrings, rings, pendants, and brooches, often with elaborate designs and inlays. They also used gemstones, such as carnelian, lapis lazuli, turquoise, and agate, to enhance the beauty and value of their jewelry.
  - Weights and measures: The Harappans developed a system of standardized weights and measures, based on a decimal and binary system. They used cubical or spherical weights, made of chert, limestone, or steatite, ranging from 0.05 grams to 500 grams. They also used linear measures, such as rods, cords, and rulers, to measure length, area, and volume. Their system of weights and measures was remarkably accurate and consistent, and facilitated trade and commerce.
  - Urban planning: The Harappans built well-planned cities, with a grid layout, fortified walls, and drainage systems. They had a hierarchy of settlements, from large urban centers, such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro, to smaller towns and villages. They also had public buildings, such as granaries, baths, citadels, and temples, as well as private houses, workshops, and markets. They used baked bricks, stone, wood, and mortar as building materials.
