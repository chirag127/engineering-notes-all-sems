# Society in Ancient India

- Society in ancient India was based on the concept of **varna**, which was a four-fold division of people into four classes: Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers).  This system was derived from the ancient Vedic scriptures and was influenced by the Indo-Aryan culture.
- Another aspect of society in ancient India was the concept of **ashrama**, which was a four-fold division of the life of each individual into four stages: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (retired), and Sannyasa (renunciant).  These stages were meant to guide the spiritual and moral development of a person according to their age and duties.
- Society in ancient India also followed the practice of **samskara**, which were rites of passage that marked the important transitions in a person's life, such as birth, initiation, marriage, and death. These ceremonies were performed by the Brahmins and involved rituals, prayers, and offerings to the gods. 
- Family was the basic unit of society in ancient India, and it was usually patriarchal, meaning that the father or the eldest male was the head of the household. The family was also extended, meaning that it included not only the parents and children, but also the grandparents, uncles, aunts, cousins, and other relatives. 
- Marriage was an important institution in society in ancient India, and it was usually arranged by the parents or the elders of the family. The marriage was based on the compatibility of the horoscopes, the social status, and the consent of the bride and groom. The marriage ceremony involved various rituals, such as the exchange of garlands, the circumambulation of the sacred fire, and the taking of seven vows. 
- The position of women in society in ancient India varied according to the time period, the region, and the social class. In general, women were subordinate to men and had limited rights and freedoms. However, some women also enjoyed respect and dignity as mothers, wives, teachers, poets, and rulers. Some examples of influential women in ancient India are Gargi, Maitreyi, Sita, Draupadi, Mirabai, and Razia Sultana. 
- Society in ancient India also witnessed the emergence of various civilizations, such as the Indus Valley Civilization, the Mauryan Empire, the Gupta Empire, and the Harsha Empire. These civilizations contributed to the development of art, architecture, literature, science, technology, religion, and philosophy in ancient India. Some of the achievements of these civilizations include the urban planning, the drainage and sewer systems, the decimal system, the zero, the concept of pi, the surgery, the metallurgy, the astronomy, the Buddhism, the Jainism, the Hinduism, and the Sanskrit language.
