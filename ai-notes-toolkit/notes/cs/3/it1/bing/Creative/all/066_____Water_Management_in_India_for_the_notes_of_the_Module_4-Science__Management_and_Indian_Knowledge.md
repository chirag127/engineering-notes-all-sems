# Water Management in India

Water management in India is the process of planning, developing, distributing and managing the optimum use of water resources in the country. It is a key determinant of health security and economic growth in India. Water management in India faces several challenges, such as:

- Large parts of India have already become water stressed, meaning that the demand for water exceeds the available supply.
- Rapid growth in demand for water due to population growth, urbanization and changing lifestyle pose serious challenges to water security.
- Over 50% of agriculture is still rain-fed, making it vulnerable to climate variability and droughts.
- Groundwater extraction has risen exponentially across India, leading to overexploitation and depletion of aquifers.
- Pollution of surface and groundwater sources from domestic, industrial and agricultural activities has degraded the quality of water and affected human and ecological health.
- Inadequate infrastructure and institutional capacity to provide safe and reliable water supply and sanitation services to rural and urban areas.
- Frequent occurrence of floods and droughts that cause loss of life, property and livelihoods.

To address these challenges, India has adopted various water management strategies, such as:

- Providing drinking water to rural areas through the Jal Jeevan Mission, which aims to ensure piped water supply to every rural household by 2024.
- Improving groundwater management through the Atal Bhujal Yojana, which is India's largest community-led groundwater management program, covering seven states with the highest rates of groundwater depletion.
- Improving access to water on dry rainfed lands through watershed development, soil and water conservation, rainwater harvesting and micro-irrigation schemes.
- Cleaning the Ganga river and its tributaries through the Namami Gange program, which involves sewage treatment, riverfront development, biodiversity conservation and public awareness campaigns.
- Managing floods and droughts through improved forecasting, early warning, disaster preparedness and response systems.
- Enhancing dam safety in India through the Dam Rehabilitation and Improvement Project, which supports the rehabilitation of about 223 dams across seven states.
- Promoting integrated water resources management, which is a holistic approach that considers the social, economic and environmental aspects of water use and allocation.
- Strengthening water governance and institutions, which involves improving the legal and regulatory framework, enhancing the participation and accountability of stakeholders, and fostering cooperation and coordination among different levels and sectors of water management.
