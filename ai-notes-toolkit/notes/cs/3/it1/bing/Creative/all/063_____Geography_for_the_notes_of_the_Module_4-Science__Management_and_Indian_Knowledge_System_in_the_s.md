# Geography

- Geography is the study of the physical features of the earth and its atmosphere, and of human activity as it affects and is affected by these.
- Geography has two main branches: physical geography and human geography.
- Physical geography deals with the natural phenomena of the earth, such as climate, landforms, water, soil, vegetation, and wildlife.
- Human geography deals with the spatial aspects of human society and culture, such as population, settlement, urbanization, agriculture, industry, trade, and geopolitics.
- Geography also has many sub-disciplines, such as cartography, geomorphology, hydrology, meteorology, biogeography, environmental geography, economic geography, political geography, cultural geography, and historical geography.
- Geography is an interdisciplinary field that draws on knowledge from various disciplines, such as geology, biology, ecology, sociology, anthropology, history, economics, and politics.
- Geography is also a practical field that applies its concepts and methods to various problems and issues, such as natural hazards, resource management, environmental conservation, regional development, and global change.
- Geography has a long and rich history of development, dating back to ancient civilizations, such as the Mesopotamians, Egyptians, Greeks, Romans, Chinese, and Indians.
- Geography has been influenced by various philosophical and scientific traditions, such as the classical, medieval, Renaissance, Enlightenment, modern, and postmodern periods.
- Geography has also been shaped by various cultural and social factors, such as religion, ideology, nationalism, colonialism, imperialism, globalization, and postcolonialism.
- Geography has contributed to the advancement of human knowledge and civilization, as well as to the understanding and appreciation of the diversity and complexity of the world.
