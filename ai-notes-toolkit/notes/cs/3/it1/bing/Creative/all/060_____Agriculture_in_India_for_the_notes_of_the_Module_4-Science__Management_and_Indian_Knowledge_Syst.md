# Agriculture in India

Agriculture is one of the most important sectors of the Indian economy, contributing about 16% of the GDP and employing about 58% of the population. India has a diverse agro-climatic zone, ranging from tropical to temperate, and produces a variety of crops, such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugarcane, cotton, jute, tea, coffee, and rubber. India is also the world's largest producer and consumer of milk, and has a large livestock population, including cattle, buffaloes, goats, sheep, poultry, and fish. 

Some of the major features and challenges of agriculture in India are:

- **Small and marginal farmers**: About 86% of the farmers in India own less than 2 hectares of land, and face problems such as low productivity, lack of access to credit, inputs, markets, and extension services, and vulnerability to climate change and natural disasters.
- **Irrigation and water management**: About 52% of the net sown area in India is rainfed, and depends on the monsoon rainfall, which is often erratic and uneven. Irrigation facilities are inadequate, and water use efficiency is low. Water scarcity, groundwater depletion, and water pollution are some of the major issues affecting agriculture.
- **Soil health and fertility**: Soil degradation, nutrient imbalance, salinity, alkalinity, and erosion are some of the factors that affect soil health and fertility in India. The use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides has also led to environmental and health problems. There is a need to promote organic farming, integrated nutrient management, and soil conservation practices.
- **Crop diversification and value addition**: India has a large potential to diversify its crop production and add value to its agricultural products, such as processing, packaging, branding, and marketing. This can help in increasing farmers' income, reducing wastage, and enhancing food security and nutrition. However, there are constraints such as lack of infrastructure, technology, skills, and policies to support crop diversification and value addition.
- **Agricultural research and innovation**: India has a strong agricultural research system, with institutions such as the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR), State Agricultural Universities (SAUs), and Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs). However, there is a need to strengthen the linkages between research and extension, and to foster innovation and entrepreneurship in agriculture. There is also a need to adopt new technologies such as biotechnology, nanotechnology, precision agriculture, and digital agriculture.
- **Agricultural policies and programs**: The government of India has launched various policies and programs to support agriculture, such as the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY), Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN), National Food Security Mission (NFSM), Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY), and National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA). However, there are issues such as implementation gaps, fund utilization, monitoring and evaluation, and coordination among different agencies and stakeholders.
- **Agricultural trade and markets**: India is a net exporter of agricultural products, such as rice, wheat, cotton, spices, tea, coffee, and marine products. However, India's share in the global agricultural trade is low, and it faces challenges such as non-tariff barriers, quality standards, and competition from other countries. India also needs to improve its domestic agricultural markets, by removing distortions, enhancing infrastructure, promoting e-NAM, and ensuring fair prices and remunerative markets for farmers.
- **Agricultural education and extension**: India has a large network of agricultural education and extension institutions, such as SAUs, KVKs, Agricultural Technology Management Agencies (ATMAs), and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs). However, there is a need to improve the quality and relevance of agricultural education and extension, by updating the curriculum, enhancing the skills and competencies of the faculty and staff, and using ICT and mass media for effective dissemination of information and knowledge.
