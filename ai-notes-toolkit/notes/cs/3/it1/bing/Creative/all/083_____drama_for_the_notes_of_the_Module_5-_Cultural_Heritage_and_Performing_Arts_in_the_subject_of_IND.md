# Drama in Indian Culture

Drama is a form of performing arts that involves the enactment of stories using dialogue, music, gesture and dance. Drama in Indian culture has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic times. Drama in Indian culture can be classified into two broad categories: classical and folk.

## Classical Drama

Classical drama in India is based on the principles of the Nātyaśāstra, a treatise on dramaturgy attributed to Bharata Muni, a sage who lived around the 3rd-4th century BCE. The Nātyaśāstra defines drama as a combination of rasa (sentiment), bhava (mood), abhinaya (expression) and vritti (style). The Nātyaśāstra also lays down the rules for the structure, language, music, costumes, stage, audience and performance of drama.

Classical drama in India can be further divided into two types: Sanskrit and Prakrit. Sanskrit drama is the most ancient and prestigious form of classical drama, written in the Sanskrit language and following the conventions of the Nātyaśāstra. Some of the famous Sanskrit dramatists are Kālidāsa, Bhāsa, Śūdraka, Viśākhadatta and Harsha. Some of the famous Sanskrit plays are Abhijñānaśākuntalam, Mālavikāgnimitram, Mṛcchakaṭikā, Mudrārākṣasa and Nāgānanda.

Prakrit drama is a later development of classical drama, written in the Prakrit languages such as Pali, Ardhamagadhi, Sauraseni and Magadhi. Prakrit drama is more realistic, humorous and secular than Sanskrit drama, and deals with the lives of common people. Some of the famous Prakrit dramatists are Hāla, Rājaśekhara, Śrīharṣa and Mahendravarman. Some of the famous Prakrit plays are Sattasaī, Karpūramañjarī, Ratnāvalī and Mattavilāsa.

## Folk Drama

Folk drama in India is a diverse and vibrant form of drama that reflects the regional, linguistic, religious and cultural diversity of the country. Folk drama in India is performed by various communities, castes, tribes and sects, and is influenced by local myths, legends, rituals, festivals and social issues. Folk drama in India is usually performed in open-air venues, such as streets, courtyards, temples, fields and forests, and involves the participation of the audience.

Folk drama in India can be classified into various types, such as Yatra/Jatra, Ramleela, Raasleela, Swang, Nautanki, Dashavatar, Kariyala, Khyal, Tamasha, Bhavai, Yakshagana, Therukoothu, Bhaona, Ankiya Nāt, Chhau, Maach, Bhand Pather, Ras, Bhagavata Mela and Kutiyattam. Each of these types has its own distinctive features, such as language, music, dance, costumes, masks, puppets, props, themes and characters. Some of the famous folk dramatists and performers are Ratan Thiyam, Habib Tanvir, Utpal Dutt, B.V. Karanth, Kanhailal, Raja Paranjape, Shanta Gandhi, Bansi Kaul and Ravi Baswani.
