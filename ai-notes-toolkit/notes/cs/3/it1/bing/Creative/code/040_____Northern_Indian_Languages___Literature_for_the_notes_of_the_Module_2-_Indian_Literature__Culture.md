### Northern Indian Languages and Literature

- Northern India is a diverse region with many languages and literary traditions that have evolved over time.
- The languages of northern India belong to three main families: Indo-Aryan, Dravidian and Tibeto-Burman.
- The Indo-Aryan languages are derived from Sanskrit, the ancient language of the Vedas and other classical texts. They include Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, Rajasthani, Kashmiri, Sindhi, Assamese, Oriya and Nepali.
- The Dravidian languages are spoken mainly in the south, but some are also found in the north, such as Brahui, Malto and Kurukh. They are not related to Sanskrit, but have borrowed many words and features from it. They include Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam and Tulu.
- The Tibeto-Burman languages are spoken mainly in the Himalayan region and the northeast, and are related to the languages of Tibet, China and Southeast Asia. They include Tibetan, Ladakhi, Bhutia, Manipuri, Bodo, Mizo, Garo, Khasi and Naga.
- The languages of northern India have developed their own scripts, alphabets, grammar, vocabulary and literature over the centuries. Some of the scripts are Devanagari, Nastaliq, Gurmukhi, Bengali, Gujarati, Oriya and Tibetan.
- The literature of northern India reflects the rich cultural, religious, historical and social diversity of the region. It includes poetry, prose, drama, epics, folktales, myths, legends, songs, hymns, biographies, histories, essays, novels, short stories and more.
- Some of the prominent literary genres and forms of northern India are:
  - Bhakti poetry: devotional poems expressing love and devotion to a personal god or goddess, such as Rama, Krishna, Shiva, Durga, Kali, etc. Some of the famous bhakti poets are Kabir, Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai, Nanak, Namdev, Ravidas, etc.
  - Sufi poetry: mystical poems expressing the longing for union with the divine, often using metaphors of love, wine, music, etc. Some of the famous Sufi poets are Amir Khusrau, Rumi, Hafiz, Bulleh Shah, Baba Farid, etc.
  - Classical poetry: poems composed in Sanskrit or Persian, following strict rules of meter, rhyme, imagery, etc. Some of the famous classical poets are Kalidasa, Bhartrihari, Jayadeva, Amir Khusrau, Firdausi, etc.
  - Modern poetry: poems composed in various languages, influenced by the social, political and cultural changes of the modern era, such as nationalism, colonialism, feminism, etc. Some of the famous modern poets are Rabindranath Tagore, Sarojini Naidu, Iqbal, Faiz, Nirala, Mahadevi Verma, etc.
  - Drama: plays written and performed for entertainment, education or social commentary, often using music, dance, costumes, etc. Some of the famous dramatists are Kalidasa, Bhasa, Bhavabhuti, Bharata, Vishakhadatta, etc.
  - Epic: long narrative poems or prose works that tell the stories of heroic deeds, wars, adventures, etc. Some of the famous epics are Ramayana, Mahabharata, Shahnameh, etc.
  - Folktales: stories passed down orally from generation to generation, often involving animals, magic, humor, etc. Some of the famous folktale collections are Panchatantra, Jataka, Hitopadesha, Kathasaritsagara, etc.
  - Myth: stories that explain the origin and nature of the world, gods, humans, etc. Some of the famous myths are Vedas, Puranas, Upanishads, etc.
  - Legend: stories that are based on historical or semi-historical events or characters, often embellished with supernatural or fantastical elements. Some of the famous legends are Prithviraj Raso, Padmavat, Akbar-Birbal, etc.
