### Buddhist and Jain literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Pali and Prakrit are two languages that were used to write the religious texts of Buddhism and Jainism, respectively. Pali is an old form of Prakrit that combines several dialects, while Prakrit is a term that refers to any language other than the standard one, Sanskrit.
- The earliest Buddhist works were written in Pali, which was spoken in Magadha and South Bihar. These works include the Tripitaka or the Three Baskets, which consist of the Vinaya Pitaka (rules of conduct for monks and nuns), the Sutta Pitaka (discourses of the Buddha and his disciples) and the Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical and psychological analysis of the teachings).
- The Tripitaka contains various genres of literature, such as stories, poems, dialogues, parables, aphorisms, etc. Some of the famous texts in the Tripitaka are the Dhammapada (a collection of verses on ethics and wisdom), the Jataka (stories of the Buddha's previous lives), the Milindapanha (a dialogue between King Milinda and a Buddhist monk) and the Atthakatha (commentaries on the scriptures).
- The Buddhist literature in Pali also includes non-canonical works, such as the Mahavamsa (a chronicle of the history of Sri Lanka), the Visuddhimagga (a manual of Buddhist meditation and doctrine) and the Therigatha (verses of the elder nuns).
- The Buddhist literature in Sanskrit and Prakrit emerged later, as Buddhism spread to other regions and developed into different schools and sects. Some of the prominent texts in these languages are the Mahayana sutras (such as the Lotus Sutra, the Heart Sutra, the Diamond Sutra, etc.), the Madhyamaka and Yogacara treatises (such as the Mulamadhyamakakarika, the Vijnaptimatratasiddhi, etc.), the Tantric texts (such as the Guhyasamaja Tantra, the Hevajra Tantra, etc.) and the biographies of the Buddha (such as the Lalitavistara, the Buddhacarita, etc.).
- The Jain literature in Prakrit and Sanskrit consists of the canonical texts, known as the Agamas, and the non-canonical texts, known as the Anuyogas. The Agamas are divided into two main categories: the Angas (the primary texts) and the Angabahyas (the secondary texts). - The Anuyogas are four types of non-canonical texts that deal with various aspects of Jainism, such as cosmology, ethics, history, logic, etc. They are the Dharmakathas (stories of the Jain saints and sages), the Charitras (biographies of the Jain leaders and teachers), the Prakirnakas (miscellaneous texts on various topics) and the Shastras (systematic treatises on Jain doctrine and philosophy).
