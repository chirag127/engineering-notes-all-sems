### Political Ideals in Ancient India

- The political ideals in ancient India were based on the concepts of **dharma**, **raja**, **samaj** and **artha**. Dharma refers to the moral and religious duties of the individual and the society. Raja refers to the king or the ruler who is responsible for maintaining order and justice. Samaj refers to the social organisation and the collective consciousness of the people. Artha refers to the material and economic well-being of the society.
- The political ideals in ancient India were also influenced by the **Vedic** and **Upanishadic** literature, which expressed the democratic principles and ideals of the people. The Rig Veda, for example, made democracy a deity and called it **Samjnana**, meaning the collective wisdom of the people. The Upanishads, on the other hand, emphasised the unity of the individual and the universal soul, and the importance of self-realisation and liberation.
- The political ideals in ancient India were also shaped by the **epics** and the **smritis**, which narrated the stories of the kings and the sages, and prescribed the codes of conduct and the laws for the society. The epics, such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, depicted the ideal qualities of the king, such as **Rama** and **Yudhishthira**, who ruled according to dharma and the welfare of the people. The smritis, such as the Manusmriti and the Arthashastra, laid down the rules and regulations for the administration, taxation, diplomacy, warfare and punishment.
- The political ideals in ancient India were also influenced by the **philosophical** and **religious** schools of thought, such as the **Samkhya**, **Yoga**, **Nyaya**, **Vaisheshika**, **Mimamsa**, **Vedanta**, **Buddhism** and **Jainism**. These schools of thought provided different perspectives on the nature of reality, the role of reason, the sources of knowledge, the goals of life, the ethics of action, and the paths of liberation. Some of these schools of thought also challenged the authority of the Vedas, the caste system, and the ritualism of the Brahmanical religion.
- The political ideals in ancient India were also reflected in the **historical** and **archaeological** evidence, such as the **inscriptions**, **coins**, **sculptures**, **temples**, **stupas**, and **monuments**. These sources reveal the political and cultural achievements of the various dynasties and kingdoms that ruled over different regions of India, such as the **Mauryas**, **Guptas**, **Kushanas**, **Satavahanas**, **Cholas**, **Pallavas**, **Rashtrakutas**, **Pratiharas**, **Pala**, **Senas**, **Chalukyas**, **Hoysalas**, **Vijayanagara**, **Delhi Sultanate**, and **Mughals**. These sources also show the diversity and the unity of the Indian civilisation, and the interactions and influences of the foreign cultures, such as the **Persians**, **Greeks**, **Scythians**, **Parthians**, **Sakas**, **Kushanas**, **Huns**, **Arabs**, **Turks**, **Mongols**, **Afghans**, and **Europeans**.
