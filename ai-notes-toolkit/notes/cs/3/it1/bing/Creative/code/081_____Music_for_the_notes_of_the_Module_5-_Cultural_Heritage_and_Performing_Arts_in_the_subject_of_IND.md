### Music for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Music is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of intangible cultural heritage in India. It reflects the creativity, diversity and spirituality of the Indian people. Music is also an integral part of other domains of intangible cultural heritage such as rituals, festive events, oral traditions and performing arts.
- There are two major traditions of Indian classical music: Carnatic music and Hindustani music. Carnatic music is associated with South India and is based on a system of ragas (melodic modes) and talas (rhythmic cycles). Hindustani music is associated with North India and is influenced by Persian and Islamic cultures. It also uses ragas and talas, but with more flexibility and improvisation.
- The earliest tradition of Indian music may be traced to Sama Veda, which contained the slokas (verses) that were put to music. Chanting of Vedic hymns with prescribed pitch and accent still form a part of religious rituals. The earliest text dealing exclusively with performing arts is Bharata's Natyashastra (compiled between second century BC and second century AD), which has six chapters on music. Another major text is Matanga's Brihaddesi (compiled between eighth and ninth century AD), in which ragas were first named and discussed at great length. Sangeet Ratnakara, written by Sarangdeva in the thirteenth century, mentions 264 ragas. A variety of string and wind instruments were invented over the period of time. In ancient texts, references have been made to flutes, drums, veena (a plucked string instrument), and cymbals. Many rulers such as Samudragupta, King Bhoja of Dhara and King Someshvara of Kalyana patronised music. The Gupta monarch Samudragupta was himself an accomplished musician. In some of his coins, he is shown playing on the veena. Music was also associated with the worship of gods and goddesses in the temples. In the twelfth century, Jayadeva of Orissa produced the most brilliant raga kavya (poetic composition), the Gita Govinda, each song of which was set in a raga and was composed on the theme of love of Radha and Krishna. Abhinavagupta's (993-1055) Abhinavabharati provides useful information about music.
- Tamil music has a number of terms and concepts parallel to what is found in Sanskrit texts. The Saivite Nayanars and Vaishnavite Alvars too set their psalms (poems) to music. The Tamil epic Silappadikaram (The Tale of an Anklet) contains many references to music and dance. The Sangam literature (300 BC-300 AD) also mentions various types of music and instruments. The Tamil musical tradition is known as Pann, which is based on seven notes or swaras. The ancient Tamil music was also influenced by the folk and tribal music of the region.
- Indian music has also been influenced by various external cultures and movements. The arrival of Islam in India brought new musical forms and instruments such as qawwali (devotional music), ghazal (lyrical poetry), sitar (a plucked string instrument), sarod (a fretless string instrument), and tabla (a pair of drums). The Bhakti movement (15th-17th centuries) gave rise to many devotional songs and hymns in various languages such as Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, and Kannada. The Sufi movement (13th-18th centuries) also contributed to the musical diversity of India with its mystical and ecstatic songs and dances. The colonial period (18th-20th centuries) introduced Western musical influences such as harmonium (a keyboard instrument), violin, guitar, and brass bands. The modern period (20th-21st centuries) witnessed the emergence of new genres and styles of music such as film music, pop music, rock music, jazz music, fusion music, and electronic music.
- Indian music has also been recognised and appreciated globally for its richness and uniqueness.
