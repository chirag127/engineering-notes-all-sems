### Indian Architecture

Indian architecture is the expression of the history, culture, and religion of India. It reflects the diversity and richness of the Indian civilization, as well as the influences of various invaders, rulers, and traders who shaped the Indian subcontinent. Indian architecture can be classified into several styles and traditions, each with its own characteristics and features. Some of the most prominent ones are:

- **Hindu temple architecture**: This is the oldest and most widespread form of Indian architecture, dating back to the ancient Vedic period. Hindu temples are sacred structures that house the images of gods and goddesses, and are designed to facilitate worship and rituals. Hindu temples typically have a square or rectangular plan, with one or more towers (shikhara) rising above the sanctum (garbhagriha). The temple walls and ceilings are often decorated with sculptures, paintings, and carvings depicting various aspects of Hindu mythology and cosmology. Some of the most famous examples of Hindu temple architecture are the Kailasa temple at Ellora, the Brihadeshwara temple at Thanjavur, the Khajuraho temples, and the Meenakshi temple at Madurai.

- **Indo-Islamic architecture**: This is the style of architecture that emerged from the interaction of Islamic and Hindu cultures, especially during the medieval period. Indo-Islamic architecture combines elements of Islamic art and architecture, such as domes, arches, minarets, and calligraphy, with indigenous forms and motifs, such as lotus, elephants, and peacocks. Indo-Islamic architecture can be further divided into sub-styles, such as Rajput architecture, Mughal architecture, Deccani architecture, and Indo-Saracenic architecture. Some of the most renowned examples of Indo-Islamic architecture are the Qutub Minar, the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, and the Charminar.

- **South Indian architecture**: This is the style of architecture that developed in the southern part of India, especially in the states of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh. South Indian architecture is influenced by the Dravidian culture and language, as well as by the Pallava, Chola, Chalukya, Hoysala, and Vijayanagara dynasties. South Indian architecture is characterized by the use of stone, brick, and wood, and by the elaborate ornamentation of pillars, ceilings, and doorways. South Indian architecture also includes distinctive features, such as gopurams (gateway towers), mandapas (pillared halls), and vimanas (pyramidal roofs). Some of the most notable examples of South Indian architecture are the Rameshwaram temple, the Hampi ruins, the Mahabalipuram monuments, and the Padmanabhaswamy temple.

- **Modern Indian architecture**: This is the style of architecture that emerged in the 20th and 21st centuries, in response to the challenges and opportunities of the contemporary world. Modern Indian architecture is influenced by various factors, such as colonialism, nationalism, globalization, urbanization, and environmentalism. Modern Indian architecture also draws inspiration from the ancient and medieval traditions of India, as well as from the international movements of modernism, postmodernism, and regionalism. Modern Indian architecture is diverse and innovative, and reflects the aspirations and identities of the Indian people. Some of the most influential figures of modern Indian architecture are Rabindranath Tagore, Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, Le Corbusier, Charles Correa, B.V. Doshi, Raj Rewal, and Balkrishna Doshi. Some of the most remarkable examples of modern Indian architecture are the Sabarmati Ashram, the Chandigarh Capitol Complex, the Lotus Temple, the Akshardham Temple, and the Infosys campus.
