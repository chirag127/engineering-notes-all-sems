### Famous Sanskrit Authors

- Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most influential languages of India and the world. It has a rich and diverse literature that spans various genres, periods and regions.
- Some of the famous Sanskrit authors and their works are:

  - **Valmiki**: He is regarded as the first poet (Adi Kavi) and the author of the epic Ramayana, which narrates the life and deeds of Rama, the ideal king and hero. He also composed the Yoga Vasistha, a philosophical text that deals with the nature of reality and liberation.
  - **Vyasa**: He is the legendary sage and compiler of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. He is also credited with composing the Mahabharata, the longest epic poem in the world, which contains the Bhagavad Gita, the most revered text of Hinduism. He also wrote the Puranas, the ancient histories and legends of gods and sages.
  - **Kalidasa**: He is considered to be the greatest poet and dramatist of classical Sanskrit literature. He wrote three plays: Abhijnana Shakuntalam (The Recognition of Shakuntala), Vikramorvashiya (The Story of Urvashi and Pururava) and Malavikagnimitram (The Love Affair of Malavika and Agnimitra). He also wrote two epics: Kumarasambhava (The Birth of Kumara) and Raghuvamsha (The Dynasty of Raghu). He also composed several lyrical poems, such as Meghaduta (The Cloud Messenger) and Ritusamhara (The Seasons). 
  - **Bhasa**: He is one of the earliest and most prolific Sanskrit dramatists, who wrote about 13 plays, most of which are based on the Mahabharata and the Ramayana. Some of his famous plays are Svapnavasavadatta (The Dream of Vasavadatta), Pratijnayaugandharayana (The Vow of Yaugandharayana), Madhyamavyayoga (The Middle One) and Urubhanga (The Broken Thigh).
  - **Bharavi**: He is the author of Kiratarjuniya (The Encounter of Arjuna and the Kirata), an epic poem that describes the episode of the Mahabharata, where Arjuna meets Lord Shiva in the guise of a hunter and fights with him for a boar. The poem is known for its vivid descriptions, rhetorical devices and moral lessons.
  - **Magha**: He is the author of Shishupalavadha (The Slaying of Shishupala), an epic poem that narrates the story of the Mahabharata, where Krishna kills Shishupala, the king of Chedi, who insults him at the Rajasuya sacrifice. The poem is famous for its ornate style, complex metaphors and alliterations.
  - **Harsha**: He is the author of Naishadhiyacharita (The Life of Nala and Damayanti), an epic poem that tells the story of the Mahabharata, where Nala, the king of Nishadha, loses his kingdom and wife Damayanti to a dice game and suffers many hardships before being reunited with her. The poem is admired for its poetic beauty, emotional appeal and moral values.
  - **Asvaghosa**: He is a Buddhist poet and philosopher, who composed the Buddhacharita (The Acts of the Buddha), the first Sanskrit biography of the Buddha, and the Saundarananda (The Handsome Nanda), a poem that describes the conversion of Nanda, the Buddha's half-brother, from a hedonist to a monk. He also wrote the Sariputraprakarana (The Play of Sariputra), a drama that depicts the life and teachings of Sariputra, the chief disciple of the Buddha.
  - **Bhartrihari**: He is a grammarian and philosopher, who wrote the Vakyapadiya (The Treatise on Sentences and Words), a seminal work on the philosophy of language and grammar.
