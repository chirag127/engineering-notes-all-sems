### The Seven Limbs of the State

- The seven limbs of the state (saptanga) are a political concept given by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra, which deals with the art of governance and statecraft .
- The seven limbs are: King, Amatya (Bureaucrats), Janapada (Territory), Durga (Fort), Kosa (Treasure), Danda (Coercive Authority), and Mitra (Ally)  .
- The king is the head of the state and the source of law and justice. He is responsible for the welfare and security of his subjects and the maintenance of the state .
- The amatya are the ministers and officials who assist the king in administration and policy-making. They are selected on the basis of merit and loyalty and are accountable to the king .
- The janapada is the territory or population of the state. It is the basis of the state's economic and military strength. The king should protect and expand his territory and ensure the prosperity and happiness of his people .
- The durga is the fort or the capital city of the state. It is the center of the state's political and cultural activities and the seat of the king's authority. It should be well-fortified and well-supplied to withstand any attack or siege .
- The kosa is the treasury or the financial resources of the state. It is the source of the state's income and expenditure. The king should manage his treasury wisely and avoid unnecessary expenses or corruption .
- The danda is the coercive authority or the military force of the state. It is the instrument of the state's power and security. The king should maintain a strong and disciplined army and use it for defense or offense as per the situation .
- The mitra is the ally or the friendly state that supports the state in times of need. It is the factor of the state's stability and diplomacy. The king should cultivate good relations with his allies and honor his treaties and obligations .
- The seven limbs of the state are interdependent and interrelated. The king should balance and coordinate them for the optimal functioning of the state .
