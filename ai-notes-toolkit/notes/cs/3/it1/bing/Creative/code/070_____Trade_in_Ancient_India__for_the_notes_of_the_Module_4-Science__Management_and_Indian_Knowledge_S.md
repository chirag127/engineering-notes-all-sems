### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange between different regions and civilizations.
- India has a long history of trade since the Harappan civilization (c. 2600-1900 BCE), which had extensive contacts with Mesopotamia, Egypt, and Central Asia. The Harappans traded mainly gold, copper, ivory, beads, textiles, and pottery. 
- After the decline of the Harappan civilization, trade continued in the post-Vedic period (c. 1500-600 BCE) with the rise of new urban centers such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura. These cities were connected by roads and rivers and facilitated the exchange of goods and ideas. Trade in this period involved textiles, spices, precious metals, gems, pearls, horses, elephants, and slaves. 
- Trade in ancient India reached its peak during the Mauryan Empire (c. 321-185 BCE) and the Gupta Empire (c. 320-550 CE), which expanded the political and economic influence of India across Asia and beyond. The Mauryans established a centralized administration and a network of roads, forts, and ports to regulate and protect trade. The Guptas promoted trade and commerce through patronage of art, literature, science, and religion. Trade in this period involved silk, cotton, indigo, iron, steel, ivory, coral, glass, and ceramics. 
- Trade in ancient India was not only domestic but also international, as India had maritime contacts with various regions such as Southeast Asia, China, Persia, Arabia, Africa, and Europe. India exported spices, textiles, perfumes, medicines, gems, and metals, and imported gold, silver, copper, tin, lead, coral, wine, and horses. India also participated in the Silk Road, a network of land and sea routes that connected China with the Mediterranean world.  
- Trade in ancient India was facilitated by various institutions and agents, such as guilds, merchants, bankers, shippers, and brokers. Trade was also regulated by various laws and customs, such as taxes, tariffs, weights, measures, and contracts. Some of the important sources of information on trade in ancient India are the Arthashastra of Kautilya, a treatise on statecraft and economy, the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, a Greek account of navigation and trade in the Indian Ocean, and the writings of Chinese travelers such as Faxian and Xuanzang, who visited India during the Gupta period.
