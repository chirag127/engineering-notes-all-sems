### Āshrama or the Stages of Life

- Āshrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras.
- The four āshramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Gṛhastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate)  .
- The Āshrama system is one facet of the Dharma concept in Hinduism  .
- The Āshrama system is believed to have been prevalent since the 5th century B.C.E. in Hindu society.
- The Āshrama system aims to guide the individual through four stages of spiritual development, each with its own duties, responsibilities, and goals .

#### Brahmacharya (Student)

- Brahmacharya is the first stage of life, lasting until around age 25.
- Brahmacharya is marked by chastity, devotion, and obedience to one’s teacher.
- Brahmacharya is a period of formal education, where the student learns the Vedas, the sciences, the arts, and the skills necessary for his or her profession .
- Brahmacharya is also a time of self-discipline, self-control, and self-purification.

#### Gṛhastha (Householder)

- Gṛhastha is the second stage of life, lasting from around age 25 to 50.
- Gṛhastha is marked by marriage, the begetting of children, sustaining one’s family and society, and fulfilling one’s worldly duties.
- Gṛhastha is the most important and dominant stage of life, as it supports the other three stages and serves as the basis of social and economic life .
- Gṛhastha is also a time of enjoying the pleasures of life, such as wealth, sex, and power, within the limits of Dharma.

#### Vanaprastha (Forest Walker/Forest Dweller)

- Vanaprastha is the third stage of life, lasting from around age 50 to 75.
- Vanaprastha is marked by retirement, detachment, and renunciation of worldly affairs.
- Vanaprastha is a period of transition, where the individual gradually withdraws from the active involvement in family and society, and prepares for the final stage of life .
- Vanaprastha is also a time of spiritual pursuit, where the individual devotes more time to meditation, pilgrimage, and service to humanity.

#### Sannyasa (Renunciate)

- Sannyasa is the fourth and final stage of life, lasting from around age 75 to death.
- Sannyasa is marked by complete renunciation of all worldly attachments, possessions, and relationships.
- Sannyasa is a state of liberation, where the individual transcends the cycle of birth and death, and attains the highest goal of life, Moksha .
- Sannyasa is also a state of universal compassion, where the individual sees the self in all beings, and serves as a spiritual guide and teacher for others.
