### Challenges faced by Women in India

- Women in India face various socio-cultural, economic, and political challenges that limit their opportunities and rights. Some of these challenges are:

  - **Gender discrimination and stereotypes**: Women are often seen as inferior, subordinate, or dependent on men in many aspects of life. They are expected to conform to traditional gender roles and norms, such as being obedient, domestic, and nurturing. They face discrimination and bias in education, employment, health care, and decision-making. They are also subjected to stereotypes and prejudices that affect their self-esteem and confidence .

  - **Violence and harassment**: Women are vulnerable to various forms of violence and harassment, such as domestic violence, sexual assault, rape, dowry-related violence, honor killings, acid attacks, and trafficking. These acts of violence not only cause physical and psychological harm, but also violate their human dignity and rights. According to the most recent government report on crimes in the country, India logged 31,677 cases of rape in 2021, or 86 cases per day on average, while there were approximately 49 incidences of violence against women reported every single hour.

  - **Lack of access to resources and opportunities**: Women face barriers and challenges in accessing and utilizing various resources and opportunities, such as education, health care, employment, credit, property, and political participation. They are often denied or deprived of these resources and opportunities due to poverty, illiteracy, social norms, legal constraints, or patriarchal structures. For example, women suffer as a result of a lack of sufficient sanitation facilities in the workplace. According to Indian Census figures from 2011, there are 587 million women in India, accounting for 48 percent of the population. Despite this, only 29% of women over the age of fifteen participate in the labor force.
