# Theatre

Theatre is a form of performing art that involves actors, dialogue, music, dance, and stagecraft to present a story or a message to an audience. Theatre is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of cultural expression in India, with a rich and varied history that spans across regions, languages, genres, and styles.

Some of the main points to note about theatre in India are:

- Theatre in India originated as a narrative form, in which recitation, singing and dancing were integral elements. The encompassing of all forms of literature and fine arts into its physical presentation and the emphasis on narrative elements made the classical Indian drama theatrical right from the beginning.
- Theatre in India is influenced by various sources, such as the Vedas, the epics, the Puranas, the folk traditions, the regional cultures, the foreign invasions, the colonial rule, and the modern movements. Theatre in India reflects the diversity and complexity of the Indian society, as well as its creativity and resilience.
- Theatre in India can be broadly classified into two categories: classical and folk. Classical theatre refers to the theatre that follows the rules and conventions laid down by ancient texts, such as the Natyashastra, the oldest surviving treatise on dramaturgy in the world. Folk theatre refers to the theatre that is rooted in the local traditions, customs, beliefs, and practices of the people, and is often performed in the open air, in festivals, or in religious occasions.
- Some of the examples of classical theatre in India are Sanskrit drama, Kutiyattam, Koodiyattam, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Yakshagana, and Bhavai. Some of the examples of folk theatre in India are Nautanki, Tamasha, Jatra, Bhand Pather, Swang, Rasleela, Ramleela, and Chhau.
- Theatre in India also underwent significant changes and innovations in the modern period, especially under the influence of the Western theatre and the nationalist movement. Some of the pioneers of modern theatre in India are Rabindranath Tagore, Bharatendu Harishchandra, Bal Gandharva, Ebrahim Alkazi, Prithviraj Kapoor, Utpal Dutt, Vijay Tendulkar, Badal Sircar, Girish Karnad, Mahesh Dattani, and Naseeruddin Shah.
- Theatre in India is also a vibrant and dynamic field today, with many theatre groups, festivals, institutions, and platforms that promote and showcase the diversity and richness of the Indian theatre. Some of the prominent theatre festivals in India are Prithvi Theatre Festival, Bharat Rang Mahotsav, Jairangam, Nandikar's National Theatre Festival, and Thespo. Some of the prominent theatre institutions in India are National School of Drama, Film and Television Institute of India, Drama School Mumbai, and Indian People's Theatre Association.
- Theatre in India is not only a form of entertainment, but also a form of education, communication, social critique, and cultural preservation. Theatre in India has the potential to create awareness, inspire change, challenge stereotypes, celebrate diversity, and foster dialogue and harmony among the people. Theatre in India is a living and evolving art form that reflects the spirit and identity of the nation.
