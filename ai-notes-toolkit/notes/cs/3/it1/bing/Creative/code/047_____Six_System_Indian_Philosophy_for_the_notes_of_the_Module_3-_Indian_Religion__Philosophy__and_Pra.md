### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta. These are the orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy that accept the authority of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of India. Each system has its own founder, texts, concepts and methods of inquiry. Here is a brief overview of each system:

- Nyaya: The founder of Nyaya is Akshapada Gautama and it dates back to 2nd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with logic and epistemology. It aims to provide a valid means of knowledge (pramana) and a criterion of truth (pramanya). It uses a method of logical analysis and debate (tarka) to establish the validity of an inference (anumana) or a testimony (shabda). It also discusses the nature of the self (atman), the categories of reality (padartha), the sources of error (khyati) and the means of liberation (moksha)   .

- Vaisheshika: The founder of Vaisheshika is Kanada and it dates back to 6th Century BCE. It is a school that deals with metaphysics and ontology. It proposes a theory of atomism, according to which the ultimate reality consists of indivisible and eternal atoms (anu) of different types, such as earth, water, fire, air and ether. It also postulates six other categories of reality: substance (dravya), quality (guna), action (karma), generality (samanya), particularity (vishesha) and inherence (samavaya). It also discusses the concepts of causation (karya-karana), time (kala), space (dik), mind (manas), self (atman) and liberation (moksha)   .

- Samkhya: The founder of Samkhya is Kapila and it dates back to 6th Century BCE. It is a school that deals with cosmology and psychology. It proposes a dualistic theory of reality, according to which there are two fundamental principles: purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter). Prakriti consists of three modes (gunas) of nature: sattva (purity), rajas (activity) and tamas (inertia). Prakriti evolves into various forms of existence, such as the five elements (mahabhuta), the five senses (indriya), the five objects of senses (tanmatra), the mind (manas), the intellect (buddhi) and the ego (ahamkara). Purusha is the witness (sakshi) of the manifestations of prakriti, but remains unaffected and detached. The goal of Samkhya is to realize the distinction between purusha and prakriti and attain liberation (kaivalya) from the cycle of rebirth (samsara)   .

- Yoga: The founder of Yoga is Patanjali and it dates back to 2nd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with ethics and practice. It is closely related to Samkhya, but differs in accepting the existence of a supreme self (ishvara) as the guide and teacher of the individual selves (atman). It prescribes an eightfold path (ashtanga) of discipline and meditation to achieve the union (yoga) of the self with the supreme self and attain liberation (kaivalya). The eight limbs of Yoga are: restraint (yama), observance (niyama), posture (asana), breath control (pranayama), withdrawal of senses (pratyahara), concentration (dharana), meditation (dhyana) and absorption (samadhi)   .
