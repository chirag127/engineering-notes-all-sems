### Mathematics in India

- Mathematics in India emerged in the Indian subcontinent from 1200 BCE until the end of the 18th century.
- In the classical period of Indian mathematics (400 CE to 1200 CE), important contributions were made by scholars like Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara II, and Varāhamihira.
- Indian mathematics developed concepts such as the decimal place-value system, the use of zero as a number, algebra, trigonometry, geometry, and calculus .
- Indian mathematics also influenced the development of mathematics in other regions, such as China, the Middle East, and Europe .
- Some of the main topics and achievements of Indian mathematics are:

  - The Sulba Sutras: These are the earliest extant texts on geometry in India, dating from the 8th to 2nd century BCE. They deal with the construction of altars and fire pits for Vedic rituals, and contain rules for finding the areas and perimeters of various shapes, such as squares, rectangles, circles, and triangles .
  - The Jaina Mathematics: The Jains were a religious sect that emphasized non-violence and logic. They developed a sophisticated system of mathematics, including the concepts of infinity, infinitesimals, fractions, irrational numbers, and combinatorics. They also used a notation for powers of ten that was similar to the modern scientific notation .
  - The Aryabhatiya: This is a treatise on astronomy and mathematics written by Aryabhata in the 5th century CE. It contains the earliest known use of zero as a number, and the earliest exposition of the decimal place-value system in India. It also contains formulas for finding the areas and volumes of various shapes, such as circles, spheres, and cylinders, and the solutions of linear and quadratic equations .
  - The Brahmasphutasiddhanta: This is a work on astronomy and mathematics written by Brahmagupta in the 7th century CE. It contains the first clear and correct rules for arithmetic operations with zero and negative numbers, and the first use of the terms "positive" and "negative" for numbers. It also contains methods for finding the solutions of quadratic, cubic, and higher-degree equations, and theorems on geometry and trigonometry .
  - The Siddhanta Siromani: This is a compendium of four works on astronomy and mathematics written by Bhaskara II in the 12th century CE. It contains the first systematic treatment of differential and integral calculus in India, and the first proof of the Pythagorean theorem by a general method. It also contains formulas for finding the sine, cosine, and tangent of an angle, and the solutions of various types of indeterminate equations .
  - The Kerala School of Mathematics: This was a group of mathematicians and astronomers who flourished in the 14th to 16th centuries CE in the present-day state of Kerala. They developed techniques for finding the infinite series expansions of trigonometric functions, such as the sine, cosine, and arctangent, and used them to calculate the value of pi to several decimal places. They also developed methods for finding the solutions of polynomial equations, and the concepts of convergence, divergence, and limits .
