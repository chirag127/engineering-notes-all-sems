### Coins

Coins are metal objects that are used as a medium of exchange or as a store of value. Coins have been used in India since ancient times and reflect the cultural, political, and economic aspects of different periods of Indian history. Some of the main features of coins in Indian culture are:

- The earliest coins in India were **punch-marked coins** made of silver and copper. They were issued by various kingdoms and republics in the 6th-4th centuries BCE. They had symbols or marks punched on them, such as geometric shapes, animals, plants, or human figures .
- The **Mauryan Empire** (4th-2nd centuries BCE) issued **cast coins** made of copper, silver, or gold. They had circular, square, or rectangular shapes and had images of animals, plants, or the king on them. Some of the coins also had legends in Brahmi or Kharosthi scripts .
- The **Indo-Greek** and **Indo-Scythian** rulers (2nd-1st centuries BCE) introduced **die-struck coins** in India. They had portraits of the kings, Greek or Kharosthi legends, and symbols of Greek or Indian gods and goddesses on them. They were made of silver or copper and had a high artistic quality .
- The **Kushan Empire** (1st-3rd centuries CE) issued **gold coins** that were influenced by Roman and Persian coins. They had portraits of the kings, Greek or Brahmi legends, and images of Indian, Iranian, or Greek deities on them. They also had **double-faced coins** that had two different images on the obverse and reverse sides .
- The **Gupta Empire** (4th-6th centuries CE) issued **gold and silver coins** that are considered the finest examples of Indian coinage. They had realistic portraits of the kings, Sanskrit legends, and images of Hindu gods and goddesses, animals, or scenes from Hindu mythology on them. They also had **standardized weights and measures** for their coins  .
- The **Indo-Sassanian** coins (6th-13th centuries CE) were influenced by the coins of the Sassanian Empire of Persia. They had busts of the kings, Pahlavi or Brahmi legends, and fire altars or Hindu symbols on them. They were made of silver or copper and were issued by various dynasties such as the Maitrakas, Gurjara-Pratiharas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, and Palas .
- The **Chola Empire** (9th-13th centuries CE) issued **gold and copper coins** that had images of the kings, Tamil legends, and symbols of Hindu gods and goddesses, animals, or plants on them. They also had **multi-lingual coins** that had legends in Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, or Nagari scripts. They also had **multi-metal coins** that had different metals such as gold, silver, copper, or lead in different parts of the coin .
- The **Delhi Sultanate** (13th-16th centuries CE) issued **silver and copper coins** that had Islamic inscriptions, names and titles of the rulers, and dates and places of minting on them. They also had **bilingual coins** that had legends in Arabic and Persian or Hindi scripts. They also had **token coins** that had a lower value than their face value and were used for small transactions .
- The **Mughal Empire** (16th-18th centuries CE) issued **gold, silver, and copper coins** that had Islamic inscriptions, names and titles of the rulers, and dates and places of minting on them. They also had **portrait coins** that had realistic images of the emperors, such as Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan. They also had **commemorative coins** that marked special occasions or events, such as coronations, marriages, or victories .
