### Martial Arts Traditions

Martial arts are systems of codified practices and traditions of training for combat. They are used for self-defense, physical fitness, mental and spiritual development, entertainment, and cultural preservation.

India has a rich and diverse heritage of martial arts that dates back to ancient times. Some of the martial arts traditions in India are:

- **Kushti**: A form of wrestling that originated in the Mughal era, when the local sport of Malla Yuddha was combined with the Persian sport of Varzesh-e-Bastani. It involves grappling, striking, and throwing techniques, as well as the use of a loincloth called langot. Kushti is popular in northern and western India, especially in Maharashtra, Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh.
- **Silambam**: A form of stick fighting that originated in Tamil Nadu and has been mentioned in the Sangam literature. It involves the use of a bamboo staff called silambam, as well as other weapons such as swords, daggers, spears, and shields. Silambam is known for its fluid and circular movements, as well as its use of footwork and acrobatics. Silambam is practiced in southern India, especially in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka .
- **Kuttu Varisai**: A form of unarmed combat that originated in Tamil Nadu and is closely related to Silambam. It involves the use of punches, kicks, elbows, knees, locks, and throws, as well as the use of animal-inspired postures and movements. Kuttu Varisai is also known for its use of breathing techniques and meditation. Kuttu Varisai is practiced in southern India, especially in Tamil Nadu and Kerala.
- **Thoda**: A form of archery that originated in Himachal Pradesh and is influenced by the local culture and mythology. It involves the use of wooden bows and arrows, as well as the use of traditional costumes and music. Thoda is a team sport, where two teams of archers shoot at each other's targets, which are attached to the ends of long poles. Thoda is practiced in northern India, especially in Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand .
- **Gatka**: A form of sword fighting that originated in Punjab and is associated with the Sikh religion and history. It involves the use of a curved sword called kirpan, as well as other weapons such as daggers, axes, and chakrams. Gatka is known for its fast and agile movements, as well as its use of rhythmic patterns and chants. Gatka is practiced in northern India, especially in Punjab, Haryana, and Delhi .
- **Thang Ta**: A form of sword and spear fighting that originated in Manipur and is influenced by the local culture and history. It involves the use of a single-edged sword called thang, and a spear called ta, as well as other weapons such as shields, daggers, and axes. Thang Ta is known for its precise and powerful strikes, as well as its use of acrobatics and dance. Thang Ta is practiced in northeastern India, especially in Manipur, Assam, and Tripura .
- **Kalaripayattu**: A form of martial art that originated in Kerala and is considered to be one of the oldest and most comprehensive martial arts in the world. It involves the use of various weapons such as swords, daggers, spears, shields, and sticks, as well as unarmed techniques such as kicks, punches, locks, and throws. Kalaripayattu is also known for its use of body conditioning, healing, and spiritual practices. Kalaripayattu is practiced in southern India, especially in Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka .
- **Mardani Khel**: A form of martial art that originated in Maharashtra and is influenced by the local culture and history. It involves the use of 14 styles of wielding weapons such as swords, daggers, spears, shields, and whips, as well as unarmed techniques such as kicks, punches, and throws. Mardani Khel is known for its fierce and aggressive style, as well as its use of folk music and dance. Mardani Khel is practiced in western India, especially in Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Goa.
