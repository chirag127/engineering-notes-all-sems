### Geography for the Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System in the subject of Indian Traditions, Culture and Society

- Geography is the study of the physical features of the earth and its atmosphere, and of human activity as it affects and is affected by these.
- Geography has been an important part of Indian knowledge system since ancient times, as evidenced by the texts such as Rig Veda, Ramayana, Mahabharata, Arthashastra, etc.
- Geography in India can be divided into three broad categories: physical geography, human geography and regional geography.
- Physical geography deals with the natural features of the land, water, climate, soil, vegetation, minerals, etc. of India and its regions.
- Human geography deals with the spatial distribution and characteristics of the population, culture, economy, politics, etc. of India and its regions.
- Regional geography deals with the study of the specific areas of India and their physical and human features, such as the Himalayas, the Indo-Gangetic Plain, the Deccan Plateau, the Coastal Plains, the Islands, etc.
- Geography in India has also been influenced by the concepts and theories of other disciplines, such as history, sociology, economics, ecology, etc.
- Geography in India has also contributed to the development of science, management and policy making in various fields, such as agriculture, irrigation, urban planning, disaster management, environmental conservation, etc.
- Geography in India has also reflected the diversity and unity of the Indian culture and society, as well as the interactions and influences of other cultures and civilizations.
