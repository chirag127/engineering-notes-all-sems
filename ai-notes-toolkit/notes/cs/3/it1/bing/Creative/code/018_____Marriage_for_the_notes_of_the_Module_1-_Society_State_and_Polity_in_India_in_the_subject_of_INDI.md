### Marriage

Marriage is a social and legal institution that regulates the relationship between two individuals and their families. Marriage is considered to be a sacred bond in many cultures and religions, especially in India. Marriage in India is influenced by various factors, such as region, religion, community, personal preferences, and legal norms. Some of the main aspects of marriage in India are:

- **Types of marriage**: There are different types of marriage in India, such as arranged marriage, love marriage, inter-caste marriage, inter-faith marriage, and live-in relationship. Arranged marriage is the most common type, where the bride and groom are selected by their families or intermediaries, based on factors such as caste, education, income, horoscope, etc. Love marriage is where the bride and groom choose each other based on their mutual attraction and compatibility. Inter-caste marriage is where the bride and groom belong to different castes or sub-castes, which may face social opposition or discrimination. Inter-faith marriage is where the bride and groom belong to different religions, which may require conversion or special laws. Live-in relationship is where the couple cohabits without getting married, which may not be legally recognized or socially accepted in some cases .

- **Marriage laws**: There are different laws that govern marriage in India, depending on the religion and personal status of the parties. The main laws are the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937, the Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872, the Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936, the Special Marriage Act, 1954, and the Foreign Marriage Act, 1969. These laws define the conditions, procedures, rights, and obligations of marriage, such as age, consent, registration, solemnization, maintenance, divorce, etc. The minimum legal age of marriage for women in India is 18 years, and for men is 21 years .

- **Marriage rituals**: There are different rituals and ceremonies that are performed during marriage in India, depending on the region, religion, and community of the bride and groom. Some of the common rituals are engagement, mehendi, sangeet, haldi, baraat, kanyadaan, pheras, sindoor, mangalsutra, etc. These rituals symbolize the various aspects of marriage, such as commitment, auspiciousness, joy, purity, union, blessing, etc. The rituals may vary in their order, duration, and significance, depending on the customs and traditions of the families .
