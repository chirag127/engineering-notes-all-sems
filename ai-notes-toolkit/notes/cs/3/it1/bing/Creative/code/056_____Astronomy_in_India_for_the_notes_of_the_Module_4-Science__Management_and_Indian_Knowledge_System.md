### Astronomy in India

- Astronomy has a long history in India, stretching from pre-historic to modern times.
- Astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy from the 4th century BCE and by Islamic astronomy from the 8th century CE.
- Astronomy was also developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism .
- Some of the main contributions of Indian astronomy include:
  - The concept of zero and the decimal place-value system, which were essential for astronomical calculations .
  - The recognition of the precession of the equinoxes and the calculation of its rate .
  - The accurate determination of the length of the sidereal year and the sidereal day .
  - The development of various astronomical instruments, such as the gnomon, the water clock, the armillary sphere, and the astrolabe .
  - The formulation of various astronomical systems, such as the Siddhantas, the Aryabhatiya, the Surya Siddhanta, and the Pancha Siddhantika .
  - The discovery of the heliocentric model of the solar system by Aryabhata in the 5th century CE .
  - The calculation of the value of pi and the approximation of the value of the sine function by Madhava in the 14th century CE .
  - The discovery of the infinite series expansions of trigonometric functions by Madhava and his followers in the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics in the 15th and 16th centuries CE .
  - The observation of various celestial phenomena, such as eclipses, comets, supernovas, and transits .
- Astronomy in India continues to flourish in the modern era, with the establishment of various research institutes, observatories, and space agencies, such as the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, the Indian Astronomical Observatory, and the Indian Space Research Organisation .
