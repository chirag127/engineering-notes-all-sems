### Jainism

Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that prescribes a path of non-violence for all forms of living beings in this world. Its philosophy and practice rely mainly on self-effort to progress one's soul on the spiritual ladder to God consciousness.

- Jainism is a nontheistic religion founded in India in the 6th century BC as a reaction against the teachings of orthodox Brahmanism, and still practiced there.
- The spiritual goal of Jainism is to become liberated from the endless cycle of rebirth and to achieve an all-knowing state called moksha.
- Jainism teaches a path to spiritual purity and enlightenment through disciplined nonviolence (ahimsa, literally “non-injury”) to all living creatures.
- Jainism came to prominence in the 6th century B.C., when Lord Mahavira propagated the religion.
- Jain philosophy or Jaina philosophy refers to the ancient Indian philosophical system of the Jain religion. It comprises all the philosophical investigations and systems of inquiry that developed among the early branches of Jainism in ancient India following the parinirvāṇa of Mahāvīra (c. 5th century BCE).
