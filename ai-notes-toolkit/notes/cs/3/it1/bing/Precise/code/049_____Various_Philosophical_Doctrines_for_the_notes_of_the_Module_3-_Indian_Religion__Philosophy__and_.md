### Various Philosophical Doctrines

Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

1. The majority of Asian Indians practice Hinduism. The other major religions are Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, Christianity, and Islam; and a small percentage of the population practice Judaism, Zoroastrianism, and Baha’I’ Faith .
2. Hindu philosophy includes several sub-schools of theistic philosophies that integrate ideas from two or more of the six orthodox philosophies, such as the realism of the Nyāya, the naturalism of the Vaiśeṣika, the dualism of the Sāṅkhya, the non-dualism and knowledge of Self as essential to liberation of Advaita, the self-discipline of Yoga and the asceticism and elements of theistic ideas .
3. The nature of the religious beliefs and practices of the Aryans is also known from the Rig Veda. They believed in many gods like Indra, Varuna, Agni, Surya, and Rudra. Religious Practices included Sacrifices, and ritual offering of food and drink to fire in honor of the Gods .
4. Hinduism is a major world religion originating on the Indian subcontinent and comprising several and varied systems of philosophy, belief, and ritual. Although the name Hinduism is relatively new, having been coined by British writers in the first decades of the 19th century, it refers to a rich cumulative tradition of texts and practices .
5. Indian philosophy includes both orthodox (astika) systems, namely, the Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva-Mimamsa (or Mimamsa), and Vedanta schools of philosophy, and unorthodox (nastika) systems, such as Buddhism and Jainism .
6. In ancient India, philosophy and religion complemented each other. Philosophy provided a correct view of reality, while religion showed the correct way of life; philosophy provided the vision, while religion brought about the fulfillment; philosophy was the theory, and religion was the practice .
