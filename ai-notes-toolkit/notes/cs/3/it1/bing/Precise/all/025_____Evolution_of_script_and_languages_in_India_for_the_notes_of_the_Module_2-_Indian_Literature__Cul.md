### Evolution of script and languages in India

1. The origin of most of the scripts for the Indic languages can ultimately be traced to Brahmi, which is of North Semitic derivation.
2. Devanagari, a development of Brahmi, is used for Nepali, Marathi, and Kashmiri (by Hindus), as well as for Hindi, Sanskrit, and the Prakrits.
3. The evolution of Brahmi into so many scripts over time in India does however raise the question of what individuals and scribes thought about the changes upon becoming aware.
4. Inscriptions in multiple different Indian scripts have been found together, like Kannada with Devanagari.
5. The linguistic landscape of the subcontinent changed dramatically during the 2nd millennium BCE.
6. It is impossible to determine if there is a connection between the IVC script and the next clearly attested script in India, the Brahmi script found in the inscriptions of the Mauryan Emperor Ashoka (ruled 268-232 BCE).
7. The people of the ancient India wrote in many scripts which largely have common roots.
