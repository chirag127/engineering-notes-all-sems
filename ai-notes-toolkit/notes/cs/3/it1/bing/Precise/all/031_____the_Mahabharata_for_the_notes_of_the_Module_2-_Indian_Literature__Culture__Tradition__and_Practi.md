# The Mahabharata

The Mahabharata is one of the two great Sanskrit epics of the Hindus, existing in its present form since c. . Along with the other all-India epic, the Ramayana, it is one of the two most important factors that created the "Hindu" culture of India and still exerts tremendous cultural influence throughout India and Southeast Asia .

- **Influence on Literature**: The Mahabharata has considerable influence on literature. The famous playwright Bhasa's works such as Dūtvākya, Karnabhār, Pancharātra, Urubhanga, Dūtaghototkacha and Madhyamvyayog are taken from the Mahabharata .

- **Influence on Art and Architecture**: The Mahabharata has also influenced architecture, sculpture, and painting. Its various incidents have been portrayed in stone, notably in sculptured reliefs at Angkor Wat and Angkor Thom in Cambodia, and in Indian miniature paintings .

- **Oral Tradition**: The Mahabharata is a brilliant example of the oral tradition down the ages. The inherited learning not only endures in the traditional centers but vibrates in the mechanisms of transmitting the tradition, such as katha pravachan and other popular cultural and social practices .

- **Philosophical Themes**: The basic themes in the Mahabharata—a grave injustice against the heroine, an unjust exile of the heroes, and a cataclysmic war against a villainous enemy—are ones found in the other major epic of India, the Ramayana. However, the scope of the Mahabharata is much broader and its narrative structure more complex. It is also much more philosophical .
