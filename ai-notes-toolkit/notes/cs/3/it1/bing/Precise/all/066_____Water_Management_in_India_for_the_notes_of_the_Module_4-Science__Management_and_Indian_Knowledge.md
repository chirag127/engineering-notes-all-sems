### Water Management in India

Water management in India is a complex issue due to the country's large population, diverse geography, and varying climate conditions. The government has been responsible for providing drinking water to 833 million rural people in some 640,000 villages across the country. However, rapid growth in demand for water due to population growth, urbanization, and changing lifestyle pose serious challenges to water security.

Some of the key areas of focus for water management in India include:

1. Providing Drinking Water to Rural Areas: The government has been working to improve access to clean drinking water in rural areas.
2. Groundwater Management: India has just 4% of the world’s water resource, supporting 17.1% of its population. The Atal Bhujal Yojana, India’s largest community-led groundwater management program, is helping improve rural livelihoods and build resilience in 7 Indian states which have the highest rates of groundwater depletion.
3. Improving Access to Water on Dry Rainfed Lands: This is important for ensuring food security and improving the livelihoods of farmers in these areas.
4. Cleaning the Ganga: The Ganga river is considered sacred by many in India and is also an important source of water for millions of people. Efforts are being made to clean the river and improve its water quality.
5. Managing Floods and Droughts: India is prone to both floods and droughts, and effective management of these natural disasters is crucial for minimizing their impact on people and the economy.
6. Enhancing Dam Safety in India: Dams play an important role in water management in India, and ensuring their safety is crucial for preventing disasters.

Water management in India must be holistic in nature, involving the Centre, the States, and local governance institutions. The goal is to ensure access to a minimum quantity of potable water for essential health and hygiene to all citizens, available within easy reach of the household.
