## Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

1. **Society:** Society refers to a group of people who share a common culture, language, and territory. In India, society is diverse and complex, with a rich history and a wide range of cultural practices and traditions.

2. **State:** The state refers to the political organization of a country, including its government, laws, and institutions. In India, the state is a federal parliamentary democratic republic, with a President as the head of state and a Prime Minister as the head of government.

3. **Polity:** Polity refers to the system of government and the distribution of power within a state. In India, the polity is based on the Constitution, which outlines the powers and responsibilities of the different branches of government and the rights and duties of citizens.

4. **Constitution of India:** The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the land, and it provides the framework for the governance of the country. It was adopted on 26th November 1949 and came into effect on 26th January 1950.

5. **Federalism:** India is a federal country, which means that power is divided between the central government and the state governments. The Constitution outlines the powers and responsibilities of the central and state governments, and provides for the resolution of disputes between them.

6. **Parliamentary Democracy:** India is a parliamentary democracy, which means that the executive branch of government is accountable to the legislature. The President is the head of state, but the real executive power is vested in the Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister, who is appointed by the President.

7. **Judiciary:** The judiciary is the branch of government responsible for interpreting and enforcing the law. In India, the judiciary is independent of the executive and legislative branches, and it has the power of judicial review, which allows it to strike down laws that are unconstitutional.

8. **Fundamental Rights:** The Constitution of India guarantees certain fundamental rights to all citizens, including the right to equality, the right to freedom of speech and expression, the right to life and personal liberty, and the right to religious freedom.

9. **Directive Principles of State Policy:** The Directive Principles of State Policy are guidelines for the government to follow in making policies and laws. They are not enforceable by the courts, but they provide a framework for the government to work towards the welfare of the people.

10. **Secularism:** India is a secular country, which means that the state does not have an official religion and does not discriminate against any religion. The Constitution guarantees the right to freedom of religion, and the government is required to treat all religions equally.
