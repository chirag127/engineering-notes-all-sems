# Four-class Classification for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

1. The four-class classification, also known as the Varna system, is a social hierarchy in Hinduism that divides society into four classes or varnas.
2. The four classes are Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (laborers and artisans).
3. The Varna system is based on the principle of division of labor and is believed to have originated from the Purusha Sukta hymn in the Rigveda.
4. The system has been criticized for perpetuating social inequality and discrimination, particularly against the lower castes.
5. The Indian Constitution has abolished the practice of untouchability and discrimination based on caste, and has implemented affirmative action policies to promote social equality.
