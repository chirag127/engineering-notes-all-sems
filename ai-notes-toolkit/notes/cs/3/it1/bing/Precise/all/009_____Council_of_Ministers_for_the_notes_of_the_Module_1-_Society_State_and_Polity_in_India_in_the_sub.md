### Council of Ministers

The Council of Ministers is the collective decision-making body of the Government of India, composed of the Prime Minister and other Union Ministers. It is responsible for the administration of the Union and is accountable to the Parliament.

- The Prime Minister is the head of the Council of Ministers and is appointed by the President of India.
- The other members of the Council of Ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.
- The Council of Ministers is divided into three categories: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, and Deputy Ministers.
- The Cabinet Ministers are the senior-most members of the Council and hold important portfolios.
- The Ministers of State may either hold independent charge of a ministry or assist a Cabinet Minister.
- The Deputy Ministers assist the Cabinet Ministers and Ministers of State in their work.
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, the lower house of the Parliament.
- The Council of Ministers must resign if it loses the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
- The President of India acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers in the exercise of his functions.
