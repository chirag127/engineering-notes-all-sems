# Modern Religious Practices in India

India is a country with a diverse range of religious practices and beliefs. According to the 2011 census, 79.8% of the population of India practices Hinduism, 14.2% adheres to Islam, 2.3% adheres to Christianity, 1.7% adheres to Sikhism, 0.7% adheres to Buddhism and 0.4% adheres to Jainism. Zoroastrianism, Sanamahism and Judaism also have an ancient history in India, and each has several thousands of Indian adherents.

- **Hinduism** is the dominant religion of India, with around 80% of the population identifying as Hindu.
- **Islam** is the second most prevalent religion in India, with about 14% of the population adhering to it.
- **Christianity** is the third most prevalent religion in India, with about 2% of the population adhering to it.
- **Sikhism** is the fourth most prevalent religion in India, with about 1.7% of the population adhering to it.
- **Buddhism** is the fifth most prevalent religion in India, with about 0.7% of the population adhering to it.
- **Jainism** is the sixth most prevalent religion in India, with about 0.4% of the population adhering to it.

Religion still plays a significant role in the lives of most Indians, even though India is a secular country. Indians overwhelmingly say that religion is very important in their life and regularly visit their houses of worship. Most Indians also give to charitable causes. After living side by side for generations, India’s minority groups often engage in practices or hold beliefs that are more closely associated with Hindu traditions than with their own.
