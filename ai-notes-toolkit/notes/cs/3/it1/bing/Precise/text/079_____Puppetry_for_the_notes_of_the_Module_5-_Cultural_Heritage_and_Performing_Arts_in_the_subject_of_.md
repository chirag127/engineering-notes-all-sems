### Puppetry

Puppetry is an ancient performance art form in India, popularly known as Kathputli. It involves storytelling through the playing of puppets. Puppeteers travel from place to place to entertain people by performing mythological stories, historical romances, and other tales .

India is said to be the home of puppets, with almost all types of puppets found in the country. Puppetry has held an important place in traditional entertainment throughout the ages. Like traditional theatre, themes for puppet theatre are mostly based on epics and legends .

The origin of puppetry in India cannot be dated, but mythology provides at least two stories that are witness to the importance of this art. According to one legend, the creator Brahma gave life to the adi, the first nat puppeteer, and created the first puppet for the entertainment of his wife Saraswati .

Puppetry in India is a family business. Children start their apprenticeship by observing the work of their elders. A puppeteer’s work includes not only manufacturing and operating the puppets but also memorizing the texts. Family troupes bequeath the puppets from generation to generation; they are a family treasure, sometimes also used as a bride’s dowry .

Puppetry has philosophical significance in Indian culture, in addition to being an art form. God is characterized as a puppeteer in the Bhagavad Geeta, directing the cosmos with three strings: Satta, Raja, and Tama. In Indian theatre, the storyteller is known as Sutradhar, which means "string bearer" .

One of the most popular forms of Indian puppetry is Kathputli, a string puppet theatre native to Rajasthan. Being a string marionette, it is controlled by a single string that passes from the top of the puppet over the puppeteers .
