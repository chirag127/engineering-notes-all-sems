### Four-class Classification for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

1. The four-class classification, also known as the Varna system, is a social hierarchy in Hinduism that divides society into four classes or varnas.
2. The four varnas are Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (laborers and artisans).
3. This system is based on the principle of division of labor and is believed to have originated from the Purusha Sukta hymn in the Rigveda.
4. The Varna system has been criticized for perpetuating social inequality and discrimination, particularly against the lower castes.
5. In modern India, the caste system is illegal and efforts have been made to address caste-based discrimination through affirmative action policies and legislation.
6. However, the caste system continues to have a significant impact on Indian society, particularly in rural areas.
