### Sangama Literature

- Sangama Literature, historically known as 'the poetry of the noble ones', is the ancient Tamil literature and the earliest known literature of South India.
- The word Sangam refers to college, and the commentary of Iraiyanar Agapporul (c. AD 750) alludes to three Sangams that lasted at long intervals over 9900 years. These were at Then Madurai, Kapalapuram and Madurai respectively.
- Broadly, Sangam literature consists of poems, grammar, epics and Tirrukural- all broadly being composed between 200 BCE and 1200 AD. There are a total of 2279 poems of lengths varying from three lines to over eight hundred, by 473 poets (including some women), besides 102 anonymous pieces.
- Sangam writings are possibly unique in early Indian literature, which is almost entirely religious. The poems are concerned with two main topics: those of the first five collections are on love (akam), and those of the next two are on heroism (puram), including the praise of kings and their deeds.
- The Sangam literature had fallen into oblivion for much of the second millennium of the common era, but were preserved by and rediscovered in the monasteries of Hinduism, particularly those related to Shaivism near Kumbakonam, by colonial-era scholars in the late nineteenth century.
