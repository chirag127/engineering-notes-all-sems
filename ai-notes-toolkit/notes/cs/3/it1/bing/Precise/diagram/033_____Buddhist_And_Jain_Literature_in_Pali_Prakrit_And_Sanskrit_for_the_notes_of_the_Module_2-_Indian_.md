### Buddhist and Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit

#### Module 2: Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

#### Indian Traditions, Culture, and Society

- Buddhist and Jain literature is a rich and diverse body of work that has been written in a variety of languages, including Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit.
- Pali is a Middle Indo-Aryan language that is closely related to Sanskrit and was used as the liturgical language of Theravada Buddhism.
- Prakrit is a term used to describe a group of Middle Indo-Aryan languages that were used in ancient and medieval India. These languages were used for both secular and religious literature, including Jain texts.
- Sanskrit is an ancient Indo-Aryan language that has been used for religious and philosophical texts, as well as for secular literature. It is considered to be the primary liturgical language of Hinduism and was also used for Buddhist and Jain texts.
- Buddhist literature in these languages includes a wide range of texts, including scriptures, commentaries, and philosophical treatises. These works cover a variety of topics, including the teachings of the Buddha, the nature of reality, and the path to enlightenment.
- Jain literature in these languages is similarly diverse, including scriptures, commentaries, and philosophical works. These texts discuss the teachings of the Jain Tirthankaras, the nature of the soul, and the path to liberation.
- Both Buddhist and Jain literature in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit have had a profound impact on the development of Indian literature, culture, and thought. These works continue to be studied and revered by followers of these traditions today.
