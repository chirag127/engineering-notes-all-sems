### Water Management in India

Water management in India is a complex issue due to the country's large population, rapid urbanization, and changing lifestyles. The government has historically been responsible for providing drinking water to rural areas, with a population of 833 million people in some 640,000 villages across the country .

Groundwater extraction has risen exponentially across India over the past few decades, and India is now the world's largest user of groundwater . The Atal Bhujal Yojana, India’s largest community-led groundwater management program, is helping improve rural livelihoods and build resilience in 7 Indian states which have the highest rates of groundwater depletion .

In addition to providing drinking water and managing groundwater, other key areas of focus for water management in India include improving access to water on dry rainfed lands, cleaning the Ganga river, managing floods and droughts, and enhancing dam safety .

Water management in India must be holistic in nature, with the Centre, the States, and local governance institutions working together to ensure access to a minimum quantity of potable water for essential health and hygiene to all citizens, available within easy reach of the household .
