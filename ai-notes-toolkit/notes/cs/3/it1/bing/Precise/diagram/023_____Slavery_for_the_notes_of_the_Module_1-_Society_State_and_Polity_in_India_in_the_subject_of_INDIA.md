### Slavery in India

- Slavery in India continued through the 18th and 19th centuries. During the colonial era, Indians were taken into different parts of the world as slaves by various European merchant companies as part of the Indian Ocean slave trade.
- Slavery in India escalated during the Muslim domination of northern India after the 11th century, when Muslim rulers re-introduced slavery to the Indian subcontinent.
- Slavery is still present in India today, with estimates ranging between 14 and 18 million people living in modern slavery. It is a lucrative business, netting traffickers around $150 billion a year, with one-third of the profits being produced in developed countries.
- Modern slavery in India can be found in brick kilns, rice mills, embroidery factories, quarries, farms, roadside brothels, and private homes.
- The Global Slavery Index estimates that on any given day in 2016 there were nearly 8 million people living in modern slavery in India, with a prevalence of 6.1 victims for every thousand people.
