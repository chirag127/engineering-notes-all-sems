### Varnāshrama System

The Varnāshrama system is a social structure in Hinduism that organizes society into four classes, or varnas, and four stages of life, or ashramas. The purpose of the Varnāshrama system is to provide a structure that allows people to work according to their natural tendencies and to organize society so that everyone, regardless of their position, makes spiritual advancement.

- **Varna System:** The Varna system refers to the segmental division of Hindu society into four classes: Brahmanas, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Sudras. The social divisions are based on qualities and work.
- **Ashrama System:** The Ashrama system refers to the segmental division of an individual's life into four stages: Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, and Sannyasa.
- **Dharma:** Dharma refers to the duty of an individual.

According to Hindu texts, Varnāshrama-dharma is not a man-made system but refers to natural classifications that appear to various degrees in all human societies. Individuals have different innate tendencies for work and exhibit a variety of personal qualities.
