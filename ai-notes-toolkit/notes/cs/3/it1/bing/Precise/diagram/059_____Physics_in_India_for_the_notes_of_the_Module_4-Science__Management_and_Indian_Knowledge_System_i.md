### Physics in India

1. The Department of Physics at the Indian Institute of Science in Bangalore was established in 1933 by Nobel Laureate C.V. Raman. It has since evolved into a leading center for advanced postgraduate education and cutting-edge research in physics.
2. The department has produced the largest number of Ph.D.s in physics in India, with 110 since 1998. Graduates from the department have gone on to distinguished careers in science and occupy leading positions in research institutes in India and the world over.
3. Ancient Indian philosophies such as Nyaya and Vaisheshika encouraged a scientific culture in ancient India. These aspects are dealt with in present-day physics through the laws of motion, gravitation, thermodynamics, wave mechanics, hydrostatics, classical and quantum mechanics, nuclear and atomic physics, and electromagnetic theory.
4. India has produced many famous physicists, including Homi J. Bhabha and Jayant Narlikar.
5. In 1945, nuclear physicist Homi Bhabha established the internationally respected Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) in Mumbai.
