### The Mahabharata

The Mahabharata is one of the two great Sanskrit epics of the Hindus, existing in its present form since c. . It is considered one of the most important factors that created the "Hindu" culture of India, along with the other all-India epic, the Ramayana . The Mahabharata story has been retold in written and oral Sanskrit and vernacular versions throughout South and Southeast Asia .

- The Mahabharata is a brilliant example of the oral tradition down the ages .
- The intellectual tradition of the Ganga Yamuna doab, the core region of the Mahabharata, is highlighted by A.L. Basham in The Wonder that was India (1954) .
- In early India, an oral tradition was maintained, especially in the Ganga Yamuna Saraswati region, for comprehending, examining, and preserving knowledge traditions .
- The Mahabharata is best understood as a narrative tradition rather than a discrete text, given that there are so many versions of it (oral, textual, and performative) .
- At around 100,000 verses in Sanskrit, it is one of the world's longest (and oldest) epics .
