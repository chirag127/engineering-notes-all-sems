# Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century

The socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India provided the soil for the growth of Indian Nationalism. The main reason for the emergence of these reform movements was the spread of western education and liberal ideas. These reforms started in Bengal and soon spread to all parts of India.

Some of the prominent socio-religious reform movements of the 19th century in India were:

1. **Brahmo Samaj**: Founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828, it aimed to purify Hinduism and to preach monotheism.
2. **Arya Samaj**: Founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875, it aimed to revive Vedic learning and to preach monotheism.
3. **Prarthana Samaj**: Founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867, it aimed to reform Hinduism by promoting monotheism and by discarding idol worship.
4. **Aligarh Movement**: Founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1875, it aimed to promote western education among Muslims and to bring about a reconciliation between Muslims and the British government .

These movements aimed to introduce regeneration and reform to Hinduism, both in a religious or spiritual and in a societal sense. They were a period of transition and contestation in the socio-religious sphere as three streams: of reform, revival, and rejection of ancient cultural values were woven around the socio-religious movement or social reform movement initiated by eminent intelligentsia of vision and foresight.
