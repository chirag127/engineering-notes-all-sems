## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions and philosophies that have influenced its culture and society.
- The major religions in India are Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam, and Christianity. There are also smaller groups of Jews, Zoroastrians, and Bahá'ís.
- Hinduism is the oldest and largest religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a complex and diverse tradition that encompasses many schools of thought, sects, and practices. Hinduism is based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures that contain hymns, rituals, and philosophy. Hinduism believes in the concepts of karma, dharma, reincarnation, and moksha (liberation from the cycle of birth and death).
- Buddhism is a religion and philosophy that originated in India in the 6th century BCE by Siddhartha Gautama, who became known as the Buddha (the enlightened one). Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths, which are the reality of suffering, the cause of suffering, the cessation of suffering, and the path to the cessation of suffering. The path consists of the Eightfold Path, which are ethical and mental disciplines that lead to wisdom and compassion. Buddhism also believes in the concepts of karma, rebirth, and nirvana (the state of peace and freedom from suffering).
- Jainism is a religion and philosophy that originated in India in the 6th century BCE by Mahavira, who is considered the last of the 24 Tirthankaras (ford-makers or teachers). Jainism teaches the principle of ahimsa (non-violence) towards all living beings, and the practice of asceticism and self-control. Jainism also believes in the concepts of karma, reincarnation, and liberation, but rejects the authority of the Vedas and the existence of a supreme creator god.
- Sikhism is a religion and philosophy that originated in the Punjab region of India in the 15th century CE by Guru Nanak, who is the first of the 10 Gurus (spiritual teachers). Sikhism is based on the teachings of the Gurus, which are compiled in the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy scripture of the Sikhs. Sikhism teaches the belief in one God, who is formless and omnipresent, and the equality of all human beings. Sikhism also emphasizes the values of service, justice, and social responsibility.
- Islam is a religion and philosophy that originated in Arabia in the 7th century CE by Muhammad, who is regarded as the final prophet of God. Islam is based on the Quran, the holy book of the Muslims, and the Sunnah, the example and teachings of Muhammad. Islam teaches the belief in one God, who is the creator and sustainer of the universe, and the submission to his will. Islam also prescribes the five pillars of faith, which are the declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage.
- Christianity is a religion and philosophy that originated in Palestine in the 1st century CE by Jesus Christ, who is believed to be the son of God and the savior of humanity. Christianity is based on the Bible, the holy book of the Christians, which consists of the Old Testament and the New Testament. Christianity teaches the belief in one God, who is a trinity of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and the salvation by grace through faith in Jesus Christ. Christianity also follows the teachings and commandments of Jesus Christ, such as love, forgiveness, and service.
