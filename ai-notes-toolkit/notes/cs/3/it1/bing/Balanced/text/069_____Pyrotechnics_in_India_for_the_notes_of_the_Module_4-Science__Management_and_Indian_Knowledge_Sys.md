### Pyrotechnics in India

- Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating and using fireworks for entertainment, military, or industrial purposes.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows have a long history in India, dating back to ancient times when they were used for religious festivals, royal celebrations, and special occasions.
- Some of the earliest references to pyrotechnics in India are found in Sanskrit texts such as Kautukachintamani, a 16th-century treatise by Gajapati Prataparudradeva, which describes various formulas and mixtures for making fireworks.
- Fireworks were also introduced to India by foreign visitors and traders, such as the Arabs, the Chinese, the Portuguese, and the Mughals, who brought their own styles and techniques of pyrotechnics.
- Fireworks became a popular form of entertainment and spectacle in India, especially during Diwali, the festival of lights, when people light up the sky with various types of crackers, rockets, sparklers, and fountains.
- Fireworks are also used for other occasions, such as weddings, birthdays, inaugurations, sports events, and cultural shows, as well as for political and social protests and demonstrations.
- However, fireworks also have negative impacts on the environment and human health, as they cause noise and air pollution, fire hazards, injuries, and accidents.
- According to a study by the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur, fireworks contribute to about 30% of the particulate matter (PM) emissions during Diwali in Delhi, one of the most polluted cities in the world.
- Fireworks also contain harmful chemicals, such as barium, strontium, copper, lead, and potassium, which can cause respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, skin allergies, eye irritation, and cancer.
- In 2018, the Supreme Court of India banned the sale and use of most fireworks, except for a few "green crackers", which are supposed to have less noise and smoke, and contain eco-friendly materials.
- The government also imposed strict regulations on the manufacture, storage, transport, and sale of fireworks, and set a time limit of two hours for bursting crackers during Diwali and other festivals.
- The ban and the regulations have faced resistance and criticism from various stakeholders, such as the fireworks industry, which employs about 8 lakh people, mostly in Tamil Nadu, the religious groups, who consider fireworks as a part of their tradition and culture, and the consumers, who enjoy the thrill and excitement of fireworks.
- The enforcement of the ban and the regulations has also been challenging, as there is a lack of awareness, compliance, and monitoring among the public and the authorities.
- The debate on the use of fireworks in India is still ongoing, as there are conflicting views and interests among different sections of society, and there is a need for a balance between the environmental, health, social, cultural, and economic aspects of pyrotechnics.
