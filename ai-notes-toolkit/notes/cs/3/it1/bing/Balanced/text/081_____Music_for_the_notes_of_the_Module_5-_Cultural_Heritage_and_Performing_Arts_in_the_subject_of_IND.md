### Music

Music is an integral part of Indian culture and society. It reflects the diversity and richness of India's heritage, traditions, religions, languages and regions. Music has been used for various purposes, such as expressing devotion, celebrating festivals, performing rituals, entertaining audiences, communicating emotions, and creating art. Music has also influenced and been influenced by other forms of art, such as dance, drama, poetry, and painting.

Some of the main points to note about music in Indian culture and society are:

- Music has a long and ancient history in India, dating back to the Vedic period (around 1500 BCE). The Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, contain hymns and chants that are considered sacred and powerful. The Vedas also mention various musical instruments, such as stringed, wind, and percussion instruments.
- Music in India is classified into two broad categories: classical and folk. Classical music is based on a complex system of ragas (melodic modes) and talas (rhythmic cycles) that are derived from the ancient texts of music theory, such as the Natyashastra and the Sangita Ratnakara. Classical music is further divided into two main traditions: Hindustani (North Indian) and Carnatic (South Indian). Folk music is more regional and diverse, reflecting the local cultures, languages, and lifestyles of the people. Folk music includes genres such as bhajans (devotional songs), qawwalis (Sufi songs), bhangra (Punjabi dance music), lavani (Maharashtrian dance music), and baul (Bengali mystic songs).
- Music in India is closely linked to religion and spirituality. Music is used as a medium of worship, meditation, and enlightenment in various faiths, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam, and Christianity. Music is also associated with various gods and goddesses, saints and sages, and sacred places and symbols. For example, Saraswati is the goddess of music and learning, Nataraja is the god of dance and cosmic rhythm, Kabir and Tulsidas are famous poets and singers of bhajans, Amir Khusrau and Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan are renowned composers and performers of qawwalis, and the Taj Mahal and the Golden Temple are examples of architectural marvels that incorporate musical motifs and elements.
- Music in India is also a form of social and cultural expression and identity. Music reflects the diversity and unity of India's people, who belong to different castes, classes, genders, ethnicities, and regions. Music also serves as a medium of communication and dialogue, as well as a source of resistance and protest, among various social and political groups and movements. For example, music has been used to promote nationalism, patriotism, and freedom during the colonial and post-colonial periods, to assert the rights and dignity of the oppressed and marginalized communities, such as the Dalits and the tribals, to challenge the norms and stereotypes of the dominant and mainstream cultures, such as the Bollywood and the pop music industries, and to raise awareness and action on various issues, such as environmentalism, feminism, and human rights.
