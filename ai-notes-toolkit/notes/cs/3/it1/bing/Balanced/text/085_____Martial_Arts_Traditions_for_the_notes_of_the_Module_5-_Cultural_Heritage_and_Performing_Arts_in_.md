### Martial Arts Traditions

- Martial arts are systems of codified practices and traditions of training for combat. They are used for self-defense, physical fitness, mental and spiritual development, entertainment, and cultural preservation.
- India has an ancient and diverse tradition of martial arts. Some of the most prominent and popular styles are:
  - **Kalaripayattu**: Originated in Kerala in the 13th century. It is considered the oldest martial art in the world and the mother of all martial arts. It involves strikes, kicks, grappling, weaponry, and healing methods. It is based on the concept of vital points (marmas) on the human body.   
  - **Gatka**: Originated in Punjab and is associated with the Sikh community. It is a weapon-based martial art that uses swords, daggers, sticks, and shields. It was developed as a means of self-defense against the Mughal invaders. It is also a sport and a cultural performance.  
  - **Lathi Khela**: Originated in North India and is popular in Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, and West Bengal. It is a martial art that uses a bamboo staff (lathi) as a weapon. It also involves kicks, punches, and wrestling techniques. It is practiced by farmers and villagers as a form of exercise and entertainment.  
  - **Silambam**: Originated in Tamil Nadu and is one of the oldest martial arts in India. It is a stick-fighting martial art that also uses other weapons such as knives, swords, spears, and fire pots. It is influenced by Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. It is known for its fast and fluid movements.   
  - **Thang-ta and Sarit Sarak**: Originated in Manipur and are the two main aspects of the martial art known as Huyen Langlon. Thang-ta means sword and spear and involves the use of various weapons and unarmed combat. Sarit Sarak means fighting while escaping and involves the use of acrobatic and evasive maneuvers. It is influenced by the Meitei culture and religion.   
  - **Mardaani Khel**: Originated in Maharashtra and is a weapon-based martial art that uses swords, spears, axes, and daggers. It was developed by the Maratha warriors as a means of guerrilla warfare against the Mughal and British armies. It is also known as Maratha fencing or dandpatta.  
  - **Inbuan Wrestling**: Originated in Mizoram and is a form of folk wrestling that is popular among the Mizo people. It is a test of strength, skill, and endurance. It involves grappling, throwing, and pinning the opponent. It has strict rules that prohibit kicking, punching, biting, and holding the opponent's hair or clothes.  
  - **Kushti**: Originated in North India and is one of the most popular martial arts in India. It is a form of traditional wrestling that combines Malla Yuddha, a local sport, and Varzesh-e-Bastani, a Persian sport. It involves grappling, striking, and submission techniques. It is practiced in a clay or dirt pit called an akhara.   
- These martial arts reflect the rich and diverse cultural heritage and performing arts of India. They are not only methods of fighting, but also ways of living, learning, and expressing. They have influenced and been influenced by other forms of art, such as dance, music, theater, and literature. They have also contributed to the development of other martial arts around the world, such as karate and kung-fu.
