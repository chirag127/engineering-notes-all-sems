### The Seven Limbs of the State

- The seven limbs of the state (saptanga) are a political concept given by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra, which deals with the art of governance and statecraft.
- The seven limbs are: King, Amatya (Bureaucrats), Janapada (Territory), Durga (Fort), Kosa (Treasure), Danda (Coercive Authority), and Mitra (Ally)   .
- The king is the supreme authority and the head of the state. He is responsible for the peace, justice, and stability of the state. He should be virtuous, wise, and benevolent .
- The amatya are the ministers and officials who assist the king in administration and policy-making. They should be loyal, competent, and efficient .
- The janapada is the territory or the land of the state. It should be fertile, prosperous, and well-populated. It should also be well-protected from external and internal threats .
- The durga is the fort or the capital of the state. It should be strategically located, well-fortified, and well-supplied. It should also be the center of culture, commerce, and learning .
- The kosa is the treasury or the wealth of the state. It should be sufficient, well-managed, and well-distributed. It should also be used for the welfare of the people and the defense of the state .
- The danda is the coercive authority or the military of the state. It should be strong, disciplined, and loyal. It should also be used for the protection of the state and the enforcement of the law  .
- The mitra is the ally or the friend of the state. It should be reliable, supportive, and cooperative. It should also be used for the mutual benefit and security of the state .

- These seven limbs are interdependent and interrelated. They should work in harmony and balance for the welfare and prosperity of the state  .
