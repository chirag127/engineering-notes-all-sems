### Marriage

Marriage is a social and legal institution that regulates the relationship between two individuals and their families. Marriage is considered to be a sacred bond in many cultures and religions, especially in India. Marriage in India has the following characteristics:

- **Regional diversity**: Weddings in India vary according to the region, the religion, the community and the personal preferences of the bride and groom. Different regions have different rituals, customs, attire, cuisine and music for the wedding ceremonies. For example, in North India, the bride and groom exchange garlands and take seven rounds around the sacred fire, while in South India, the bride and groom tie a sacred thread around each other's necks and walk around the fire three times.
- **Legal requirements**: In order to be married legally, both the bride and groom must complete certain requirements that are laid down by the laws. These include the minimum age of marriage, which is 21 years for men and 18 years for women, the consent of both parties, the absence of any prohibited degree of relationship, and the registration of the marriage. India has different personal laws for different religions, such as the Hindu Marriage Act, the Muslim Personal Law, the Christian Marriage Act, the Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, and the Special Marriage Act.
- **Arranged marriages**: Arranged marriages are still the norm in India, but there’s a growing trend for some women to choose their own partners – or to not marry at all. Arranged marriages are marriages where the parents or relatives of the bride and groom find a suitable match for them, based on factors such as caste, education, income, horoscope, etc. The bride and groom may or may not have a say in the final decision. Arranged marriages are seen as a way of preserving the family honor, social status and cultural values.
- **Inter-caste and interfaith marriages**: There are few inter-caste or interfaith marriages in India. Marrying within your caste remains an essential feature of marriage in India. In a 2014 survey of more than 70,000 people, fewer than 5% of marriages were inter-caste and fewer than 2% were interfaith. Inter-caste and interfaith marriages are often opposed by the families and the society, and may even lead to violence and honor killings. However, some couples defy the norms and marry outside their caste or religion, asserting their right to life and liberty under Article 21 of the Constitution.
- **Foreign marriages**: Getting married in India as a foreigner can be a complicated and lengthy process. At a minimum, you’ll need the following documents in advance of your wedding day: Proof of Residency and Address in India for at least 30 days prior to the application, for at least one of the parties; A valid passport for both parties; Original birth certificate for both parties; Visa or Ration Card; Affidavit of marital status and consent; and Certificate of No Impediment. You’ll also need to apply for a marriage certificate at the local registrar office and pay the fees. Depending on your religion, you may also need to follow the specific personal laws or opt for a civil marriage under the Special Marriage Act.
