### Astronomy in India

- Astronomy has a long history in the Indian subcontinent, stretching from pre-historic to modern times. Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilisation or earlier.
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism. The Vedanga Jyotisha, attributed to Lagadha, is one of the earliest texts on astronomy in India, dating from around the 6th century BCE.
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, Sanskrit translations of Greek texts disseminated from the 2nd century CE. These texts introduced concepts such as the zodiac, the ecliptic, the planets, and the epicycles to Indian astronomy.
- Indian astronomers developed sophisticated mathematical and observational techniques for calculating the positions and motions of celestial bodies, such as the use of trigonometry, algebra, geometry, and spherical astronomy. Some of the notable Indian astronomers include Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Lalla, and Madhava.
- Indian astronomy also had a strong connection with astrology, which was considered a branch of science in ancient India. The ancient Indians believed that the position of the planets determined our fortunes, particularly Saturn and Mars. They also devised various systems of calendars and timekeeping based on the cycles of the Sun, the Moon, and the planets.
- Indian astronomy reached its peak in the medieval period, with the development of the Siddhanta school of astronomy, which produced several authoritative texts on astronomy, such as the Surya Siddhanta, the Aryabhatiya, the Brihat Samhita, the Pancha Siddhantika, and the Siddhanta Shiromani. These texts incorporated elements from various sources, such as the old Saura Siddhanta, the Greek astronomy, and the Islamic astronomy.
- Indian astronomy declined after the 16th century, due to the lack of patronage, the rise of Western astronomy, and the suppression of indigenous knowledge by the colonial powers. However, some Indian astronomers continued to make contributions to astronomy, such as Jai Singh II, who built several astronomical observatories in the 18th century, and Sawai Raja Jaisingh, who translated several Western astronomical works into Sanskrit in the 19th century.
- In the 20th century, Indian astronomy revived with the establishment of modern institutions, such as the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, the Indian Space Research Organisation, and the Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics. Indian astronomers have made significant contributions to various fields of astronomy, such as solar physics, stellar astronomy, radio astronomy, cosmology, and space exploration.
- Indian astronomy has a rich and diverse heritage, reflecting the cultural and intellectual diversity of the Indian subcontinent. It has also been influenced by and has influenced other astronomical traditions, such as the Greek, the Islamic, and the Western. Indian astronomy is a testament to the human quest for understanding the mysteries of the universe.
