### Telugu Literature

- Telugu literature is the body of works written in the Telugu language, a Dravidian language spoken mainly in the states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in India.
- Telugu literature consists of various genres, such as poems, short stories, novels, plays, and song lyrics, among others.
- Telugu literature dates back to at least the middle of the first millennium CE, but the first extant works are from the 11th century CE, when the Mahabharata was first translated into Telugu by Nannaya.
- Telugu literature can be broadly divided into four periods: Ancient (11th to 14th century), Medieval (15th to 18th century), Modern (19th to 20th century), and Contemporary (21st century).
- Some of the prominent features and themes of Telugu literature are:

  - The influence of Sanskrit literature, especially the epics, Puranas, and classical poetry .
  - The development of native literary forms, such as the prabandha (a long narrative poem), the padya-kavya (a verse epic), the śataka (a collection of 100 verses), and the campu (a mixed prose and verse form) .
  - The expression of bhakti (devotion) to various deities, especially Vishnu, Shiva, and Shakti, through the works of saint-poets, such as Annamacharya, Tyagaraja, Ramadasu, and Bhadrachala Ramadasu .
  - The reflection of social and political changes, such as the rise and fall of dynasties, the emergence of regional identities, the impact of colonialism, and the struggle for independence .
  - The exploration of human emotions, relationships, values, and aesthetics, through the works of poets, such as Nannaya, Tikkana, Srinatha, Potana, Allasani Peddana, Krishnadevaraya, Pingali Suranna, Kshetrayya, Gurajada Apparao, Viswanatha Satyanarayana, Sri Sri, and C. Narayana Reddy .
  - The innovation and experimentation with new forms, styles, and themes, such as the novel, the short story, the drama, the essay, the autobiography, the satire, the modern poetry, and the Dalit literature .
