### Mystical Theory for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Mystical theory is one of the theories that attempts to explain the origin of the state in ancient India.
- According to this theory, the state is a divine institution that is created by God or a supernatural power for the welfare of the people.
- The king is the representative of God or the chosen one who has the divine right to rule over the people.
- The king is also the protector of the dharma, the cosmic order and moral law that governs the universe and the society.
- The king is expected to follow the dharma and uphold justice, righteousness, and welfare of the people.
- The king is also guided by the brahmins, the priestly class, who are the custodians of the sacred scriptures and rituals.
- The king is supported by the council of ministers, the administration, and the army, who are the other limbs of the state.
- The society is divided into four varnas or classes, namely the brahmins, the kshatriyas, the vaishyas, and the shudras, based on their birth and occupation.
- The society is also divided into various jatis or castes, which are sub-groups within the varnas, based on their profession, region, and lineage.
- The society is governed by the rules of varnashrama dharma, which prescribe the duties and responsibilities of each varna and ashrama or stage of life.
- The society is also influenced by the various schools of thought, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Epics, the Puranas, the Dharmashastras, the Arthashastra, the Buddhist and Jain texts, and the Bhakti and Sufi movements.
- The society is also enriched by the various forms of art, literature, architecture, music, dance, and drama, which reflect the cultural diversity and creativity of the people.
