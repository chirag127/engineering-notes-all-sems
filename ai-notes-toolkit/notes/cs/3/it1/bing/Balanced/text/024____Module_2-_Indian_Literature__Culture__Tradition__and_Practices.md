## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is the literature produced in the Indian subcontinent and its diaspora. It includes works in many languages, such as Sanskrit, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Malayalam, Kannada, and English. Indian literature reflects the diversity and richness of Indian culture, history, philosophy, and religion. Some of the major genres of Indian literature are epics, poetry, drama, novels, short stories, and essays. Some of the prominent writers of Indian literature are Kalidasa, Valmiki, Vyasa, Tulsidas, Kabir, Rabindranath Tagore, Munshi Premchand, R.K. Narayan, Salman Rushdie, Arundhati Roy, and Amitav Ghosh  .
- Indian culture is the collective way of life, values, beliefs, customs, traditions, and practices of the people of India. It is influenced by various factors, such as geography, history, religion, language, ethnicity, and foreign invasions. Indian culture is famous across the world for its diversity, unity, tolerance, and spirituality. Some of the important aspects of Indian culture are music, dance, art, architecture, cuisine, clothing, festivals, and rituals. Some of the distinctive features of Indian culture are the concept of dharma (duty), the caste system, the joint family system, the respect for elders, the reverence for nature, and the practice of yoga and meditation  .
- Indian tradition is the set of norms, values, and behaviors that are passed down from generation to generation in Indian society. It is based on the teachings of the ancient scriptures, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, the Ramayana, and the Mahabharata. Indian tradition also incorporates the influences of various religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam, Christianity, and Zoroastrianism. Some of the common elements of Indian tradition are the respect for elders, the importance of education, the celebration of festivals, the practice of hospitality, the belief in karma (action and consequence), and the value of non-violence  .
- Indian practices are the specific actions, behaviors, and rituals that are performed by the people of India in their daily lives. They are derived from the cultural, religious, and social norms of Indian society. Some of the examples of Indian practices are greeting with a namaste (a gesture of folding hands), wearing a bindi (a dot on the forehead), applying henna (a natural dye) on hands and feet, wearing a saree (a long piece of cloth) or a kurta (a loose shirt), eating with hands, fasting on certain days, worshipping idols and symbols, visiting temples and shrines, performing puja (a ritual of offering prayers), celebrating Diwali (the festival of lights), and playing Holi (the festival of colors)  .
