### Purusārtha

- Purusārtha is a Sanskrit term that means "the object or aim of human life" .
- It is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life .
- The four purusārthas are: dharma, artha, kāma, and mokṣa .
- Dharma means "duty, righteousness, or moral law" . It is the ethical and social obligation of a person according to their stage and role in life .
- Artha means "wealth, prosperity, or material success" . It is the pursuit of economic and worldly well-being in a lawful and ethical manner .
- Kāma means "desire, pleasure, or enjoyment" . It is the fulfillment of sensual and aesthetic desires in a moderate and lawful way .
- Mokṣa means "liberation, release, or emancipation" . It is the ultimate goal of human life that involves the realization of one's true nature and the liberation from the cycle of birth and death .
- The four purusārthas are not mutually exclusive, but rather interdependent and complementary . They provide a framework for living a balanced, meaningful, and fulfilling life at the deepest and most holistic level .
- The concept of purusārtha reflects the diversity and complexity of human aspirations and values in Hinduism . It also shows the importance of individual choice and responsibility in pursuing one's goals .
