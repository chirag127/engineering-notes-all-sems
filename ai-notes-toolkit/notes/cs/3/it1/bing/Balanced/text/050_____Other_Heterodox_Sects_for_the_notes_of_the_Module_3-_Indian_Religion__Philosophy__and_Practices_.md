### Other Heterodox Sects

- Heterodox sects are those that do not accept the authority of the Vedas and the Brahmanical tradition.
- They emerged in the 6th century BCE as a reaction to the social, economic and religious changes in India.
- They challenged the caste system, the ritual sacrifices, the priestly domination and the metaphysical speculation of the orthodox schools.
- They offered alternative paths of salvation based on ethical conduct, rational inquiry and personal experience.
- Some of the heterodox sects are:

  - **Ajivika**: A fatalistic sect that believed in the doctrine of niyati (determinism) and denied the role of free will, karma and rebirth. They followed a strict ascetic lifestyle and practiced extreme forms of self-mortification. They were founded by Makkhali Gosala, a contemporary of Mahavira and Buddha .
  - **Unchedvadi**: A materialistic sect that rejected the existence of soul, God, afterlife and moral values. They advocated the pursuit of sensual pleasures and worldly happiness. They were also known as Lokayata or Charvaka, and their main exponent was Brihaspati.
  - **Nityavadi**: An eternalist sect that believed in the permanence of all things and denied the possibility of change, causation and liberation. They were also called Sasvata or Pudgalavada, and their main representative was Purana Kassapa.
  - **Sandehvadi**: An agnostic sect that suspended judgment on all metaphysical and religious questions and doubted the validity of knowledge and perception. They were also called Ajnana or Ahetuvada, and their chief proponent was Sanjaya Belatthiputta.
  - **Akriyavadi**: An amoralist sect that denied the existence of karma and its consequences, and the efficacy of action and morality. They were also called Akiriyavada or Antinomianism, and their leader was Pakudha Kaccayana.
