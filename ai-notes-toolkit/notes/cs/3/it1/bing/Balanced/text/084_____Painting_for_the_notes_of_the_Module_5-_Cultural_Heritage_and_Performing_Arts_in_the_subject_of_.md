### Painting

- Painting is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India.
- Indian paintings can be broadly classified as murals, miniatures and paintings on cloth.
- Murals are large works executed on the walls of solid structures, such as temples, caves and palaces. They depict religious, mythological, historical or secular themes. Some of the famous examples of murals are the Ajanta Caves, the Kailashnath temple, the Brihadeshwara temple and the Mattancherry Palace.
- Miniatures are small-scale paintings done on perishable materials such as paper, cloth, palm leaf, wood or ivory. They are usually made for books, albums, manuscripts or court records. They reflect the styles and influences of different regions, dynasties and religions. Some of the prominent schools of miniature painting are the Pala, the Mughal, the Rajput, the Deccan and the Pahari.
- Paintings on cloth are also known as patachitra, which means picture on cloth. They are usually made of cotton or silk and painted with natural or synthetic dyes. They often depict stories from Hindu epics, folk tales, local deities or festivals. They are used for religious, educational or entertainment purposes. Some of the well-known forms of paintings on cloth are the Kalighat, the Madhubani, the Warli, the Kalamkari and the Thangka.
- Indian paintings have a long history and tradition that spans from the prehistoric times to the modern era. They reflect the cultural, social, religious and political diversity of the Indian subcontinent. They also show the influence of foreign invaders, traders and colonizers, such as the Greeks, the Persians, the Arabs, the Turks, the Mongols, the Europeans and the British.
- Indian paintings are a rich source of information and inspiration for the study of Indian traditions, culture and society. They reveal the values, beliefs, customs, practices, aesthetics, skills and creativity of the Indian people. They also express the emotions, thoughts, aspirations and challenges of the Indian artists and their patrons.
