### Challenges faced by Women in India

- Women in India face various socio-cultural, economic, and political challenges that limit their opportunities and rights. Some of these challenges are:

  - **Gender discrimination and stereotypes**: Women are often seen as inferior, subordinate, or dependent on men in many aspects of life. They are expected to conform to traditional gender roles and norms, such as being obedient, domestic, and nurturing. They face discrimination and bias in education, employment, health care, and decision-making. They are also subject to harmful practices and beliefs, such as child marriage, dowry, female foeticide, and domestic violence  .

  - **Sexual abuse and harassment**: Women are vulnerable to sexual violence and exploitation, both in public and private spaces. They face harassment, molestation, rape, trafficking, and forced prostitution. They also face difficulties in accessing justice and support services, due to social stigma, victim-blaming, and lack of awareness and sensitivity among law enforcement and judicial authorities  .

  - **Lack of economic empowerment and opportunities**: Women have lower participation and representation in the formal labor market, due to various barriers such as lack of education, skills, mobility, and social support. They are often confined to low-paid, informal, and precarious work, such as domestic work, agriculture, and home-based industries. They face wage gaps, occupational segregation, and discrimination in hiring, promotion, and benefits. They also have limited access and control over productive resources, such as land, credit, and technology  .

  - **Lack of political participation and representation**: Women have lower involvement and influence in the political sphere, due to various factors such as patriarchal norms, social pressure, violence, and lack of education and awareness. They face underrepresentation and marginalization in elected bodies, political parties, and public administration. They also have limited voice and agency in shaping policies and laws that affect their lives and rights  .

- These challenges are interrelated and mutually reinforcing, and they require holistic and multi-sectoral responses that address the root causes and structural factors that perpetuate gender inequality and discrimination in India.
