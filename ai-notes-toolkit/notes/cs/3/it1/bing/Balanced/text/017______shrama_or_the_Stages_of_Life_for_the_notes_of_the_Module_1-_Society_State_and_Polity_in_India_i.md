### Āshrama or the Stages of Life

- Āshrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras.
- The four āshramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Gṛhastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate)  .
- The Āshrama system is one facet of the Dharma concept in Hinduism  .
- The Āshrama system is believed to have been prevalent since the 5th century B.C.E. in Hindu society.
- The Āshrama system aims to guide the individual through four stages of spiritual development, each with its own duties, responsibilities, and goals  .

#### Brahmacharya: The Celibate Student

- Brahmacharya is a period of formal education lasting until around age 25, during which the student learns about the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, as well as the skills and values necessary for life.
- Brahmacharya is marked by chastity, devotion, and obedience to one’s teacher (guru) .
- Brahmacharya is considered the foundation of the other three stages, as it prepares the student for the moral and spiritual challenges of life .

#### Gṛhastha: The Householder

- Gṛhastha is the stage of the householder, requiring marriage, the begetting of children, sustaining one’s family and contributing to society.
- Gṛhastha is the longest and most important stage, as it involves fulfilling one’s duties (dharma) as a spouse, parent, and citizen .
- Gṛhastha is also the stage where one can enjoy the pleasures (kāma) and wealth (artha) of the world, within the limits of morality and law .

#### Vanaprastha: The Forest Dweller

- Vanaprastha is the stage of the forest dweller, or the retired hermit, which begins around the age of 50, or after one’s children are grown up and settled.
- Vanaprastha is a time of gradual withdrawal from the worldly attachments and responsibilities, and a shift of focus to spiritual pursuits and self-realization .
- Vanaprastha is often marked by a symbolic ritual of renunciation, such as giving up one’s possessions, shaving one’s head, or wearing saffron robes .
- Vanaprastha is a stage of transition between the active life of the householder and the detached life of the renunciate .

#### Sannyasa: The Renunciate

- Sannyasa is the stage of the renunciate, or the ascetic, who renounces all worldly ties and possessions, and lives a life of celibacy, austerity, and meditation.
- Sannyasa is the final and most difficult stage, as it involves giving up one’s identity, family, caste, and social status, and seeking liberation (moksha) from the cycle of birth and death (samsara) .
- Sannyasa is not obligatory for all Hindus, and only a few choose to take this radical step of renunciation .
- Sannyasa is considered the highest and most noble stage, as it represents the ultimate goal of Hindu spirituality .
