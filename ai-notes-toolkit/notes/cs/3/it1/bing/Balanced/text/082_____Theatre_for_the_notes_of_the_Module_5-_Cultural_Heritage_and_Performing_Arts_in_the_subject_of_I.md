### Theatre

- Theatre is one of the most ancient forms of art and expression in India, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Theatre in India began as a narrative form, in which recitation, singing and dancing were integral elements. The encompassing of all forms of literature and fine arts into its physical presentation and the emphasis on narrative elements made the classical Indian drama theatrical right from the beginning.
- The earliest evidence of theatre in India is found in the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, which contain hymns, rituals and dialogues that were performed by priests and sages. The Natya Shastra, a treatise on dramaturgy attributed to Bharata Muni, is the most comprehensive and authoritative source of classical Indian theatre. It covers various aspects of theatre, such as the origin, purpose, structure, genres, styles, techniques, costumes, makeup, music, dance, acting, directing and criticism of theatre.
- The classical Indian theatre was mainly divided into two types: Sanskrit theatre and folk theatre. Sanskrit theatre was based on the literary works composed in Sanskrit, the ancient language of India. It followed the rules and conventions laid down by the Natya Shastra and was patronized by the royal courts and the elite classes. The most famous playwrights of Sanskrit theatre were Kalidasa, Bhasa, Shudraka, Vishakhadatta and Harsha. Some of the popular Sanskrit plays are Abhijnana Shakuntalam, Malavikagnimitram, Mrichchhakatika, Mudrarakshasa and Ratnavali.
- Folk theatre was based on the local languages, cultures and traditions of different regions and communities of India. It was more flexible, spontaneous and diverse than Sanskrit theatre and was performed by the common people for the common people. Folk theatre incorporated various elements of music, dance, puppetry, masks, acrobatics and magic. Some of the prominent forms of folk theatre are Jatra (Bengal), Tamasha (Maharashtra), Nautanki (Uttar Pradesh), Bhavai (Gujarat), Yakshagana (Karnataka), Koodiyattam (Kerala) and Therukoothu (Tamil Nadu).
- The modern theatre in India emerged during the colonial period, under the influence of the Western theatre brought by the British. It was mainly performed in English, Hindi and Hinglish (a mix of Hindi and English) and addressed the social, political and cultural issues of the contemporary times. Some of the pioneers of modern Indian theatre were Rabindranath Tagore, Bal Gandharva, Prithviraj Kapoor, Habib Tanvir, Vijay Tendulkar, Girish Karnad, Badal Sircar and Mahesh Dattani. Some of the notable modern Indian plays are Ghashiram Kotwal, Tughlaq, Hayavadana, Evam Indrajit and Dance Like a Man.
- Theatre in India is not only a form of entertainment, but also a medium of education, communication and social change. It reflects the diversity, richness and vitality of the Indian culture and society. It also showcases the creativity, innovation and experimentation of the Indian theatre artists. Theatre in India is a living and evolving tradition that continues to inspire and influence the audiences across the world.
