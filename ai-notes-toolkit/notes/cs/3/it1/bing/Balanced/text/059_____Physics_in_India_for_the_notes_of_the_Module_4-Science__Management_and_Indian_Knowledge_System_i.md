### Physics in India

- Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy.
- India has a rich and ancient tradition of physics, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Some of the notable contributions of Indian physicists are:

  - The concept of atomism, which states that all matter is composed of indivisible and indestructible atoms, was developed by the Vaisheshika school of philosophy, founded by Kanada (6th century BCE).
  - The concept of zero, which is essential for mathematics and physics, was invented by the Indian mathematicians around the 5th century CE.
  - The concept of shunya (void or emptiness), which is related to the quantum vacuum and the cosmological constant, was explored by the Buddhist and Jain philosophers, such as Nagarjuna (2nd century CE) and Umasvati (5th century CE).
  - The concept of anekaantavada (many-sidedness or relativity), which is similar to the principle of complementarity in quantum mechanics, was proposed by the Jain philosopher Mahavira (6th century BCE).
  - The concept of prana (life force or energy), which is analogous to the concept of field in physics, was studied by the Yoga and Ayurveda schools of thought, such as Patanjali (2nd century BCE) and Charaka (2nd century CE).
  - The concept of chakra (energy centers or vortexes), which is related to the concept of vortex in fluid dynamics and electromagnetism, was described by the Tantric and Kundalini schools of practice, such as Gorakshanath (11th century CE) and Abhinavagupta (10th century CE).
  - The concept of shabda (sound or vibration), which is connected to the concept of wave in physics, was analyzed by the Mimamsa and Nyaya schools of logic, such as Jaimini (3rd century BCE) and Gautama (2nd century CE).
  - The concept of rasa (taste or essence), which is linked to the concept of chemical reaction in physics, was investigated by the Rasashastra and Alchemy schools of science, such as Nagarjuna (8th century CE) and Nityanatha (12th century CE).
  - The concept of yantra (machine or device), which is associated with the concept of mechanism in physics, was invented by the Sthapatya Veda and Engineering schools of art, such as Vishvakarma (1st millennium BCE) and Bhoja (11th century CE).

- Some of the modern Indian physicists who have made significant contributions to the field are:

  - Jagadish Chandra Bose (1858-1937), who pioneered the study of radio waves and plant physiology, and invented the crescograph, a device to measure plant growth.
  - Satyendra Nath Bose (1894-1974), who derived the Bose-Einstein statistics for quantum particles, and collaborated with Albert Einstein on the Bose-Einstein condensate, a state of matter at very low temperatures.
  - Meghnad Saha (1893-1956), who formulated the Saha ionization equation, which explains the spectral lines of stars and the solar chromosphere, and founded the Indian Institute of Astrophysics.
  - C. V. Raman (1888-1970), who discovered the Raman effect, the scattering of light by molecules, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930.
  - Homi J. Bhabha (1909-1966), who proposed the Bhabha scattering, the interaction of electrons and positrons, and established the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and the Atomic Energy Commission of India.
  - Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (1910-1995), who developed the Chandrasekhar limit, the maximum mass of a white dwarf star, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1983.
  - Vikram Sarabhai (1919-1971), who initiated the Indian space program, and founded the Physical Research Laboratory and the Indian Space Research Organization.
  - Abdus Salam (1926-1996), who developed the electroweak theory, which unifies the electromagnetic and weak forces, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1979.
  - Raja Ramanna (1925-2004), who led the first nuclear test of India in 1974, and served as the director of the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre and the chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission of India.
