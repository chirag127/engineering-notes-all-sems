### Indian Architecture

- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India. It reflects the diversity and richness of Indian civilization and its interactions with other cultures over time.
- Indian architecture can be classified into several styles and traditions, such as Hindu temple architecture, Indo-Islamic architecture, South Indian architecture, Indo-Saracenic architecture, and modern architecture.
- Hindu temple architecture is characterized by the use of stone, brick, or wood to create elaborate structures with towers, domes, halls, and sanctuaries. The temples are often decorated with sculptures, carvings, and paintings depicting gods, goddesses, and mythological scenes. The temples are also aligned with the cardinal directions and follow the principles of vastu shastra, the ancient science of architecture.
- Indo-Islamic architecture is the result of the fusion of Islamic and Indian elements, especially during the medieval period. It includes the styles of Rajput architecture, Mughal architecture, and Deccan architecture. Rajput architecture is known for its forts, palaces, and temples with ornate facades, balconies, and arches. Mughal architecture is famous for its monuments, such as the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, and the Jama Masjid, which feature domes, minarets, and geometric patterns. Deccan architecture is influenced by Persian and Turkish styles, and showcases the use of stucco, plaster, and glazed tiles.
- South Indian architecture is distinguished by its Dravidian style, which originated in the Tamil region. It features pyramid-shaped towers, called gopurams, that rise above the entrances of the temples. The temples are also surrounded by walls and courtyards, and contain pillared halls, called mandapas, and shrines, called vimanas. The temples are adorned with sculptures, paintings, and inscriptions that depict the history and legends of the region.
- Indo-Saracenic architecture is a hybrid style that emerged during the British colonial rule in India. It combines elements of Indian, Islamic, and Gothic architecture, and was used to construct public buildings, such as railway stations, museums, and universities. It is characterized by the use of domes, arches, spires, and turrets, and the incorporation of motifs, such as lotus, peacock, and elephant.
- Modern architecture in India is influenced by the global trends and movements, such as Art Deco, Bauhaus, and International Style. It also reflects the social and political changes that occurred after India's independence in 1947. Some of the prominent modern architects in India are Le Corbusier, who designed the city of Chandigarh, Charles Correa, who designed the Gandhi Smarak Sangrahalaya, and B.V. Doshi, who designed the Indian Institute of Management.
