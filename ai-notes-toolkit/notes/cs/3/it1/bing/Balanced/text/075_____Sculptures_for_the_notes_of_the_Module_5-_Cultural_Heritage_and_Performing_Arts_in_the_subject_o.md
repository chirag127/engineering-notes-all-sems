### Sculptures

- Sculpture is one of the oldest and most important forms of artistic expression in India. Sculpture was used to adorn buildings, temples, monuments, and shrines, as well as to create images of gods, goddesses, saints, and heroes. Sculpture was also a medium of communication, education, and devotion for the followers of various religions in India, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Islam.
- Indian sculpture reflects the diversity and richness of the Indian culture, history, and geography. Indian sculpture can be classified into various styles, periods, and regions, such as the Indus Valley Civilization, the Mauryan Empire, the Gupta Empire, the Chola Dynasty, the Mughal Empire, and the British Raj. Each style and period has its own distinctive features, influences, and achievements.
- Some of the famous and historic sculptures of the Indian subcontinent are:

  - The Dancing Girl, Mohen Jo Daro, c. 2500 BC: This is a small bronze statue of a young girl in a dancing pose, found in the archaeological site of Mohen Jo Daro, one of the major cities of the Indus Valley Civilization. The statue shows the skill and sophistication of the metalworkers of that time, as well as the artistic and cultural aspects of the Indus Valley people. The statue is now displayed in the National Museum of India in New Delhi.
  - Ashoka Pillar, Sarnath, 3rd Century BC: This is a stone pillar erected by Emperor Ashoka, one of the most influential rulers of ancient India, who converted to Buddhism after witnessing the horrors of war. The pillar has a four-lion capital on top, which is the emblem of India. The pillar also has inscriptions in Brahmi script, which contain the edicts of Ashoka, which promote the principles of dharma, or righteous conduct, among his subjects. The pillar is a symbol of the spread of Buddhism and the power and benevolence of Ashoka.
  - Buddha Statue, Sarnath, 5th Century AD: This is a sandstone statue of the Buddha, the founder of Buddhism, in a seated posture, with his right hand touching the ground in a gesture of calling the earth to witness his enlightenment. The statue was carved during the Gupta period, which is considered the golden age of Indian art and culture. The statue shows the idealized and serene features of the Buddha, as well as the grace and elegance of the Gupta style. The statue is now housed in the Sarnath Museum.
  - Nataraja, Chola Bronze Sculpture, 11th Century AD: This is a bronze statue of Shiva, the Hindu god of destruction and creation, in his cosmic dance form, known as Nataraja, or the Lord of Dance. The statue was made during the Chola dynasty, which ruled over southern India and parts of Southeast Asia, and which patronized the art of bronze casting. The statue depicts Shiva dancing in a circle of fire, with his four arms holding various symbols, such as a drum, a flame, a trident, and a gesture of blessing. The statue represents the cycle of creation and destruction, as well as the harmony and balance of the universe.
  - Taj Mahal, Agra, 17th Century AD: This is a white marble mausoleum built by Shah Jahan, the fifth Mughal emperor, for his beloved wife, Mumtaz Mahal, who died in childbirth. The mausoleum is considered one of the most beautiful and romantic monuments in the world, and a masterpiece of Islamic architecture. The mausoleum is decorated with intricate carvings, inlays, calligraphy, and motifs, inspired by Persian, Turkish, and Indian styles. The mausoleum is also surrounded by gardens, fountains, and other buildings, creating a symmetrical and harmonious complex.
