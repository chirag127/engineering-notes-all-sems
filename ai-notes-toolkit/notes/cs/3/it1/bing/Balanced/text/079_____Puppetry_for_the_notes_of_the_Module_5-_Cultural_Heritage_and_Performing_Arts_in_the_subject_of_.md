### Puppetry

Puppetry is an ancient art form of storytelling that involves manipulating puppets to perform a narrative. Puppetry is a part of the cultural heritage and performing arts of India, as it reflects the social, religious, and historical aspects of the country. Puppetry in India can be classified into four main types, based on the material and technique of the puppets:

- **String puppets**: These are puppets that are controlled by strings attached to their limbs and head. The strings are manipulated by the puppeteer from above, who also narrates the story. The most popular form of string puppetry in India is Kathputli, which originated in Rajasthan. Kathputli puppets are made of wood and cloth, and depict characters from folk tales, epics, and legends. Kathputli shows are accompanied by music and songs. Other forms of string puppetry in India include Kundhei in Odisha, Gombeyatta in Karnataka, and Bommalattam in Tamil Nadu.
- **Shadow puppets**: These are flat puppets that are made of leather, paper, or cardboard, and are cut out in the shape of the characters. The puppets are held against a translucent screen, and a light source behind them creates their shadows on the screen. The puppeteer manipulates the puppets with rods or wires, and also provides the voice and sound effects. The most common form of shadow puppetry in India is Tholu Bommalata, which originated in Andhra Pradesh. Tholu Bommalata puppets are made of leather and are painted with bright colors. They depict scenes from the Ramayana and the Mahabharata. Other forms of shadow puppetry in India include Togalu Gombeyatta in Karnataka, Ravanachhaya in Odisha, and Chamadyache Bahulya in Maharashtra.
- **Rod puppets**: These are puppets that are controlled by rods attached to their limbs and head. The rods are held by the puppeteer from below, who also narrates the story. The puppets are usually made of wood, cloth, or clay, and are dressed in elaborate costumes and accessories. The most prominent form of rod puppetry in India is Putul Nautch, which originated in West Bengal. Putul Nautch puppets are made of wood and are carved with intricate details. They depict characters from the Puranas, the Ramayana, and the Mahabharata. Putul Nautch shows are accompanied by music and songs. Other forms of rod puppetry in India include Yampuri in Bihar, and Sakhi Kundhei in Odisha.
- **Glove puppets**: These are puppets that are worn on the hands of the puppeteer, who also narrates the story. The puppets are usually made of cloth, leather, or paper, and have a hollow head and a cloth body. The puppeteer inserts his fingers into the head and the arms of the puppet, and moves them to create expressions and gestures. The most popular form of glove puppetry in India is Pavakoothu, which originated in Kerala. Pavakoothu puppets are made of leather and are painted with natural colors. They depict scenes from the Ramayana and the Mahabharata. Pavakoothu shows are performed in temples and festivals, and are accompanied by music and songs. Other forms of glove puppetry in India include Chamadyache Bahulya in Maharashtra, and Kundhei Nach in Odisha.
