### Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya is a religious title used by the heads of monasteries called mathas in the Advaita Vedanta tradition of Hinduism.
- The title derives from the name of Adi Shankara, an 8th-century Indian philosopher and theologian who is considered the founder of the Advaita Vedanta school of philosophy .
- Adi Shankara was born in Kaladi village in Kerala and traveled across India to propagate his teachings and establish mathas.
- He wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras and the Bhagavad Gita, as well as original works such as the Vivekachudamani and the Upadesasahasri.
- He taught that the ultimate reality is Brahman, the impersonal and transcendent essence of all existence, and that the individual self (atman) is identical with Brahman.
- He advocated the path of knowledge (jnana) as the means to attain liberation (moksha) from the cycle of birth and death (samsara).
- He also defended the authority of the Vedas and the importance of the four stages of life (ashramas) and the four goals of life (purusharthas).
- He is revered as a saint and a teacher by various Hindu traditions and is credited with reviving the Vedic religion and unifying the diverse schools of Hindu thought.
