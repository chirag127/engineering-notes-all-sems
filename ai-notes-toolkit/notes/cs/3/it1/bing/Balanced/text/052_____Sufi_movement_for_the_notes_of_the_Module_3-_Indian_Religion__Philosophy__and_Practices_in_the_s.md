### Sufi Movement

- Sufism is a mystical and ascetic form of Islam that emphasizes the direct experience of God through love, devotion, and self-purification.
- Sufism originated in the Middle East and Central Asia in the early centuries of Islam, and spread to India through the travels and teachings of various Sufi saints or sheikhs.
- Sufism in India was influenced by the local religious and cultural traditions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism, and developed various schools or orders (silsilas) with different doctrines and practices.
- Some of the prominent Sufi orders in India are:
  - Chishti order: Founded by Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti in the 12th century, it is the most popular and influential Sufi order in India. It emphasizes love, service, and tolerance towards all, and rejects ritualism and formalism. It also incorporates music, poetry, and dance (qawwali and sama) as means of spiritual expression. Some of the famous Chishti saints are Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki, Khwaja Fariduddin Ganjshakar, Khwaja Nizamuddin Auliya, and Amir Khusrau.
  - Suhrawardi order: Founded by Sheikh Shihabuddin Suhrawardi in the 12th century, it is a more orthodox and conservative Sufi order that follows the Sharia law strictly and emphasizes the role of reason and logic in the path of God. It also advocates the concept of wahdat al-wujud (unity of being), which holds that God is the only reality and everything else is His manifestation. Some of the famous Suhrawardi saints are Sheikh Bahauddin Zakariya, Sheikh Ruknuddin Razi, and Sheikh Hamiduddin Nagori.
  - Qadiri order: Founded by Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani in the 12th century, it is a Sufi order that focuses on the cultivation of moral and spiritual qualities, such as sincerity, humility, patience, and gratitude. It also teaches the doctrine of wahdat al-shuhud (unity of witnessing), which holds that God is transcendent and distinct from His creation, but can be witnessed through His signs and attributes. Some of the famous Qadiri saints are Sheikh Nizamuddin Auliya, Sheikh Nasiruddin Chiragh Dehlavi, and Sheikh Abdul Haq Muhaddis Dehlavi.
  - Naqshbandi order: Founded by Sheikh Baha-ud-Din Naqshband in the 14th century, it is a Sufi order that stresses the importance of silent meditation (zikr-i-khafi) and the observance of the Sunnah (the practices of the Prophet Muhammad). It also teaches the concept of wahdat al-adyan (unity of religions), which holds that all religions are valid paths to God and should be respected. Some of the famous Naqshbandi saints are Sheikh Ahmad Sirhindi, Sheikh Mujaddid Alf Sani, and Sheikh Shah Waliullah.
- The Sufi movement in India had a significant impact on the religious, social, and cultural life of the people. It contributed to the spread of Islam, the development of regional languages and literature, the promotion of communal harmony and tolerance, and the emergence of various forms of art and architecture.
