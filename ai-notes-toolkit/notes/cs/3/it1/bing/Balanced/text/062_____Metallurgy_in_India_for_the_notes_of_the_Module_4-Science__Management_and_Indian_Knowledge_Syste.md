### Metallurgy in India

- Metallurgy is the science and technology of extracting and processing metals from ores or other sources.
- The history of metallurgy in India dates back to the prehistoric times, as evidenced by the discovery of copper and bronze artifacts from the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1900 BCE) and other sites.
- The Vedic texts (c. 1500-500 BCE) mention various metals and metallurgical processes, such as smelting, casting, forging, soldering, and alloying. The term ayas (आयस) in Sanskrit means metal, and is often used to refer to iron.
- The Indian metallurgy reached its peak during the Mauryan (c. 322-185 BCE) and Gupta (c. 320-550 CE) periods, when iron and steel production flourished and many innovations were made, such as the wootz steel, the iron pillar of Delhi, and the rust-resistant iron beams of Konark Sun Temple.
- The Indian metallurgy also influenced and benefited from the cultural and commercial contacts with the Near East, the Greco-Roman world, and China. For example, the Indian zinc smelting technique was adopted by the Chinese in the 17th century CE, and the Indian crucible steel was exported to the Middle East and Europe since the medieval times.
- The Indian metallurgy continued to develop and diversify under the Mughal Empire (c. 1526-1857 CE), which produced excellent firearms, cannons, swords, and armors. The Mughal metallurgy was influenced by the Persian and Turkish traditions, as well as the European technology introduced by the Portuguese and the British.
- The Indian metallurgy faced a decline during the colonial period (c. 1757-1947 CE), when the British imposed heavy taxes and restrictions on the indigenous metal industries, and imported cheap and mass-produced iron and steel from Britain. The Indian metallurgy was revived after the independence in 1947, with the establishment of public and private sector enterprises, such as the Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL), the Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO), and the Hindustan Zinc Limited (HZL).
- Today, India is one of the leading producers and consumers of metals and minerals in the world, with a rich and diverse mineral resource base. India produces 95 minerals, including coal, iron ore, bauxite, copper, zinc, gold, silver, and rare earths. India also has a strong base of metal processing and manufacturing industries, such as steel, aluminum, copper, brass, bronze, and alloys. India is also developing new and advanced technologies, such as nanotechnology, biotechnology, and green technology, to enhance the quality and efficiency of its metallurgy sector.
