# Music for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURE AND SOCIETY

- Music is an integral part of Indian culture and society, reflecting its diversity, history and spirituality.
- Music in India can be classified into two broad categories: classical and folk. Classical music is based on a complex system of ragas (melodic modes) and talas (rhythmic cycles) that have evolved over centuries. Folk music is more regional and varied, often associated with specific communities, festivals and rituals.
- Music in India is also influenced by various religions, such as Hinduism, Islam, Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, Christianity and Zoroastrianism. Each religion has its own sacred music, such as bhajans (devotional songs), qawwalis (Sufi songs), shabads (Sikh hymns), chants (Buddhist and Jain prayers) and hymns (Christian songs).
- Music in India is also a medium of expression, communication and social cohesion. Music can convey emotions, stories, values and messages, as well as create a sense of identity and belonging. Music can also foster dialogue, harmony and peace among different groups and cultures.
- Music in India is also a source of creativity, innovation and excellence. India has produced many renowned musicians, composers, singers, instrumentalists and dancers who have contributed to the global musical heritage. Some examples are Ravi Shankar, Lata Mangeshkar, A.R. Rahman, Zakir Hussain, Pandit Ravi Shankar, Ustad Bismillah Khan, M.S. Subbulakshmi, Kishori Amonkar, Rukmini Devi Arundale and Birju Maharaj.
