### Varnāshrama System

- The term Varnāshrama System consists of three words: Varna, Ashrama, and Dharma.
- Varna refers to the segmental division of the Hindu society into four classes: Brahmanas (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Sudras (servants and laborers)  .
- Ashrama refers to the segmental division of life of an individual into four stages: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate)  .
- Dharma refers to the duty of an individual according to their Varna and Ashrama  .
- The Varnāshrama System was a way of organizing the society and the individual life according to the natural talents, abilities, and inclinations of each person .
- The Varnāshrama System was also a way of ensuring the spiritual progress of each person by providing them with the appropriate work, education, and lifestyle according to their stage of life .
- The Varnāshrama System was not a rigid or hereditary system, but a flexible and dynamic one that allowed for mobility and change according to the individual's qualities and actions .
- The Varnāshrama System was based on the principle of cooperation and mutual support among the different classes and stages of life, and not on the principle of exploitation or discrimination .
- The Varnāshrama System was a part of the Hindu tradition and culture that aimed at creating a harmonious and balanced society and individual life .
