### Buddhist and Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Pali and Prakrit are two languages that were used to write the religious literature of Buddhism and Jainism, respectively. Pali is an old form of Prakrit that combines several dialects, while Prakrit is a term that refers to any language other than the standard one, Sanskrit.
- The earliest Buddhist works were written in Pali, which was spoken in Magadha and South Bihar. These works include the Tripitaka or the Three Baskets, which consist of the Sutta Pitaka (the discourses of the Buddha), the Vinaya Pitaka (the rules of monastic discipline), and the Abhidhamma Pitaka (the philosophical and psychological analysis of the teachings).
- The Pali literature also contains commentaries, chronicles, biographies, and other genres. Some of the famous works are the Dhammapada (a collection of verses on ethics and wisdom), the Jataka (stories of the Buddha's previous lives), the Milindapanha (a dialogue between King Milinda and a Buddhist monk), and the Mahavamsa (a historical chronicle of Sri Lanka).
- The Buddhist literature in Sanskrit and Prakrit includes the Mahayana sutras, which expound the doctrines of the Great Vehicle, such as emptiness, compassion, and bodhisattva ideal. Some of the well-known sutras are the Prajnaparamita Sutras (the perfection of wisdom), the Lotus Sutra (the universal salvation of all beings), and the Vimalakirti Sutra (the teachings of a lay bodhisattva).
- The Jain literature is written in Ardhamagadhi, a Prakrit language, and Sanskrit. The canonical texts of the Jains are called the Agamas, which are divided into two groups: the Angas (the limbs) and the Angabahyas (the external limbs). The Angas consist of 12 texts that deal with the teachings of Mahavira, the 24th and last Tirthankara (ford-maker) of Jainism, and his predecessors. The Angabahyas consist of 34 texts that deal with various topics such as cosmology, ethics, logic, and grammar.
- The Jain literature also includes commentaries, legends, biographies, and other genres. Some of the notable works are the Kalpasutra (a biography of Mahavira and the rules of monastic conduct), the Uttaradhyayana Sutra (a collection of sermons by Mahavira), the Tattvartha Sutra (a summary of the Jain philosophy), and the Lives of the 63 Illustrious Persons (a series of stories of the Jain heroes and saints).
