# Jainism

- Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that teaches a path to spiritual purity and enlightenment through disciplined nonviolence (ahimsa) to all living creatures .
- The name Jainism derives from the Sanskrit verb ji, meaning "to conquer". It refers to the ascetic battle that Jain renunciants (monks and nuns) must fight against the passions and bodily senses to gain omniscience and purity of soul.
- Jainism is considered to be an eternal dharma with the tirthankaras (spiritual teachers) guiding every time cycle of the cosmology. The three main pillars of Jainism are ahimsa (non-violence), anekantavada (non-absolutism), and aparigraha (asceticism).
- Jainism is a transtheistic religion, holding that the universe was not created, and will exist forever. It is independent, having no creator, governor, judge, or destroyer. In this, it is unlike the Abrahamic religions and the theistic strands of Hinduism, but similar to Buddhism.
- Jainism has two major sects: Digambara (sky-clad) and Svetambara (white-clad). They differ mainly in their views on the status of women, the practice of nudity, and the authenticity of certain scriptures .
- Jainism has a rich tradition of literature, art, architecture, and ethics. Some of the famous Jain texts are the Agamas, the Kalpa Sutra, the Tattvartha Sutra, and the Uttaradhyayana Sutra. Jain art and architecture include temples, sculptures, paintings, and manuscripts. Jain ethics emphasize the principles of nonviolence, truthfulness, non-stealing, chastity, and non-attachment  .
- Jainism has influenced and been influenced by other Indian religions, especially Hinduism and Buddhism. Jainism has also contributed to the development of Indian culture, science, philosophy, and politics. Some of the notable Jain figures are Mahavira, the 24th and last tirthankara, Bhadrabahu, the leader of the Digambara sect, Haribhadra, a prolific scholar and philosopher, and Mahatma Gandhi, a leader of the Indian independence movement and a follower of ahimsa  .
