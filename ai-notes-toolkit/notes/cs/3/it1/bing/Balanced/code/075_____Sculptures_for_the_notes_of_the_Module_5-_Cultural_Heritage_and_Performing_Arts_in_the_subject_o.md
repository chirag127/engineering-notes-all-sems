### Sculptures

Sculptures are three-dimensional art forms that depict the human or animal forms, or abstract shapes, using various materials such as stone, metal, wood, clay, etc. Sculptures are often used to express the religious, cultural, or aesthetic values of a society or an individual.

#### Sculptures in Indian Culture

- Sculpture was the favoured medium of artistic expression on the Indian subcontinent. Indian buildings were profusely adorned with it and indeed are often inseparable from it.
- The subject matter of Indian sculpture was almost invariably abstracted human forms that were used to instruct people in the truths of the Hindu, Buddhist, or Jain religions. The nude was used both to represent the body as a symbol of spirit and to reveal the imagined shapes of the gods.
- Sculpture art is one of the oldest art forms to exist in India, rooting itself as a widespread practice even in the ancient Indus Valley Civilisation (c.3300-1300 BCE).
- Some of the famous historic sculptures of the Indian subcontinent are:
  - The Dancing Girl, Mohen Jo Daro, c. 2500 BC: The statue of the famous 'Dancing Girl, found to be in the times of Harappa. It is made of bronze and shows a young girl with a bangle on her left arm and her right hand on her hip. It is one of the earliest examples of realistic human representation in Indian art.
  - Ashoka Pillar, Sarnath, 3rd Century B.C.: The most celebrated capital (the four-lion one at Sarnath (Uttar Pradesh)) erected by Emperor Ashoka circa 250 BC. also called the "Ashoka Column". It is made of sandstone and has four lions facing the four cardinal directions, symbolizing the spread of Buddhism. It also has the Ashoka Chakra, the wheel of law, and the Dharma Chakra, the wheel of righteousness, carved on it.
  - Buddha Statue, Sarnath: A sandstone statue of Buddha from the 5th century CE, depicting him in the Dharmachakra Mudra, the gesture of teaching. It is one of the finest examples of the Gupta style of art, which is characterized by grace, elegance, and serenity.
  - Nataraj, Chola Bronze Sculpture: A bronze statue of Shiva as the Lord of Dance, from the 10th century CE, belonging to the Chola dynasty. It shows Shiva dancing in a ring of fire, symbolizing the cosmic cycle of creation and destruction. It is one of the most iconic representations of Hinduism and Indian culture.
