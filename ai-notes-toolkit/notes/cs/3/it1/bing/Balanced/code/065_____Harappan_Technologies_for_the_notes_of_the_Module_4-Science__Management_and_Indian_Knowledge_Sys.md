### Harappan Technologies

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was a Bronze Age civilization that flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from about 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappans developed various technologies and crafts, such as metallurgy, seal carving, pottery, jewelry, weights and measures, urban planning, drainage systems, and writing.
- Some of the notable features of Harappan technologies are:

  - Metallurgy: The Harappans used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, vessels, and ornaments. They knew how to alloy copper with tin to make bronze, which was harder and more durable. They also knew how to cast metal by using molds and the lost-wax process.
  - Seal carving: The Harappans carved distinctive seals, usually made of steatite, with various motifs and inscriptions. They used these seals to stamp clay on trade goods, to identify property, and possibly to perform religious rituals. The seals often depicted animals, such as bulls, elephants, rhinoceroses, and unicorns.
  - Pottery: The Harappans produced fine pottery, mostly wheel-made, with various shapes, sizes, and designs. They used red clay, often coated with a red or black slip, and decorated with geometric patterns, floral motifs, or animal figures. Some of the common types of pottery were storage jars, cooking pots, dishes, bowls, and perforated vessels.
  - Jewelry: The Harappans made jewelry from materials such as gold, silver, copper, bronze, ivory, shell, bone, and beads. They crafted necklaces, bracelets, earrings, rings, and pendants, often with intricate designs and inlays. They also used gemstones, such as carnelian, lapis lazuli, turquoise, and agate, to adorn their jewelry.
  - Weights and measures: The Harappans developed a system of standardized weights and measures, based on a decimal and binary system. They used cubical or spherical weights, made of chert, limestone, or steatite, ranging from 0.05 grams to 500 grams. They also used linear measures, such as rods, cords, and rulers, to measure length and area. Their system was remarkably accurate and consistent across the civilization.
  - Urban planning: The Harappans built well-planned cities, with grid-like streets, fortified walls, and public buildings. They also had sophisticated drainage systems, with covered channels, wells, and bathrooms. They built granaries, warehouses, and docks to store and transport goods. They also constructed monumental structures, such as the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro, which may have been used for ritual bathing.
  - Writing: The Harappans developed a script, which is still undeciphered, consisting of about 400 signs. They wrote on various materials, such as seals, pottery, copper plates, and stone tablets. They used the script for administrative, commercial, and religious purposes. The script may have been influenced by or related to other scripts, such as the Proto-Elamite and the Indus-Mesopotamian scripts.

- The Harappan technologies reflect the high level of civilization, culture, and trade that the Harappans achieved. They also show the interaction and exchange of ideas and goods with other regions, such as Mesopotamia, Central Asia, and the Persian Gulf. The Harappan technologies are a testament to the ancient Indian knowledge and innovation.
