### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange since the Harappan civilization (c. 2600-1900 BCE)  .
- The Harappans traded gold, copper, ivory, beads, textiles, spices, and other goods with Mesopotamia, Egypt, Persia, and Central Asia  .
- The Harappan civilization declined around 1900 BCE, but trade continued with the rise of new urban centers such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura  .
- These cities were connected by land and river routes that facilitated the movement of goods and people across the subcontinent  .
- Trade also expanded to the sea, as India established maritime contacts with Southeast Asia, China, Arabia, and the Mediterranean  .
- India exported commodities such as cotton, silk, indigo, spices, pearls, gems, ivory, and perfumes, and imported metals, horses, wine, glass, and coral  .
- Trade in ancient India was regulated by the state, guilds, and merchants, who followed various laws and customs  .
- Trade also influenced the social, religious, and artistic aspects of Indian culture, as it fostered the exchange of ideas, beliefs, and styles  .
