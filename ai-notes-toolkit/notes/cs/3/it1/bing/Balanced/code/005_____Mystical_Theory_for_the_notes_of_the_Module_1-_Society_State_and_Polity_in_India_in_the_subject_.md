### Mystical Theory
- This theory explains the origin and nature of the state in India as a divine institution based on the mystical concept of Dharma.
- Dharma is a complex and multifaceted term that can be translated as law, duty, righteousness, morality, order, justice, etc. It is derived from the root word dhri, which means to uphold, sustain, or support.
- Dharma is the cosmic principle that governs the universe and all its beings. It is also the ethical and social norm that regulates human conduct and relationships. It is the source of legitimacy and authority for the rulers and the ruled in India.
- According to this theory, the state in India is not a human creation, but a manifestation of Dharma. The state is a sacred trust entrusted to the king by the gods. The king is the representative of Dharma on earth and the protector of the people. The king is bound by Dharma to rule with justice, benevolence, and wisdom. The king is also accountable to Dharma and subject to its sanctions and punishments.
- The people are also bound by Dharma to obey the king and perform their respective duties according to their varna (class) and ashrama (stage of life). The people are also entitled to the protection and welfare provided by the king and the state. The people are also expected to uphold Dharma and resist any violation or corruption of it.
- The state in India is thus a harmonious and organic entity based on the mutual interdependence and cooperation of the king and the people under the guidance and supervision of Dharma. The state is not a coercive or contractual institution, but a moral and spiritual one. The state is not a means to an end, but an end in itself. The state is not a secular or temporal entity, but a sacred and eternal one.
