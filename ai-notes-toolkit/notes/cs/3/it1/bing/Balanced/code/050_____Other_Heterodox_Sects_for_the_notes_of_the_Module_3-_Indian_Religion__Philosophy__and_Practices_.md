### Other Heterodox Sects

- Heterodox sects are those that do not accept the authority of the Vedas or the Brahmanical rituals and practices.
- They are also known as nāstika or "deniers" in contrast to the āstika or "affirmers" who follow the Vedic tradition.
- Some of the heterodox sects that emerged in ancient India are:

  - **Ājīvika**: Founded by Makkhali Gosāla, a contemporary and rival of Mahavira and the Buddha. Ājīvikas believed in the doctrine of niyati or fate, which means that everything is predetermined and nothing can be changed by human effort or intervention. They also rejected the concepts of karma, rebirth, liberation, and moral responsibility. They practiced extreme asceticism and renunciation of worldly pleasures. They were influential in some regions of India until the 14th century CE .

  - **Cārvāka**: Also known as Lokāyata, meaning "worldly" or "materialistic". Cārvāka was a school of philosophy that denied the existence of anything beyond the material world. They rejected the notions of soul, afterlife, God, and supernatural causation. They advocated a hedonistic lifestyle based on sensory pleasures and rational inquiry. They also criticized the Vedas, the caste system, and the rituals and sacrifices of the Brahmins. They were considered the most radical and iconoclastic of the heterodox sects.

  - **Sāṃkhya**: One of the six orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy, but also considered heterodox by some because of its rejection of the existence of a personal God or creator. Sāṃkhya is based on the dualism of puruṣa (spirit) and prakṛti (matter), which are the two ultimate realities of the universe. Sāṃkhya holds that the goal of human life is to achieve kaivalya (isolation) of the puruṣa from the bondage of prakṛti and its evolutes. Sāṃkhya is also the basis of Yoga, another orthodox school of Hindu philosophy that emphasizes the practice of meditation and ethical discipline.

- These are some of the other heterodox sects that challenged and enriched the religious and philosophical diversity of ancient India.
