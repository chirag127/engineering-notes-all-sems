# The Mahabharata

The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, along with the Ramayana. It is a long and complex narrative that covers the history, culture, religion, and philosophy of the Indian subcontinent. It is also a story of a dynastic struggle for the throne of Hastinapura, a kingdom ruled by the Kuru clan.

## Main Characters and Plot

- The main characters of the Mahabharata are the **Pandavas** and the **Kauravas**, two branches of the same family who are cousins and rivals. The Pandavas are the five sons of Pandu, the younger brother of Dhritarashtra, the blind king of Hastinapura. The Kauravas are the hundred sons of Dhritarashtra and his wife Gandhari, who blindfolded herself to share her husband's condition. The eldest of the Kauravas is Duryodhana, who is the main antagonist of the story.
- The Mahabharata begins with the birth of the Pandavas and the Kauravas, and their upbringing in the royal court. The Pandavas are favored by their elders and the gods, while the Kauravas are jealous and hostile. The Kauravas try to kill the Pandavas several times, but fail. The Pandavas also acquire a common wife, Draupadi, who is the daughter of King Drupada and the reincarnation of a goddess. Draupadi is won by Arjuna, the third Pandava, in a contest, but she agrees to marry all five brothers as per a previous vow.
- The main conflict of the Mahabharata arises when Dhritarashtra decides to divide the kingdom between his sons and nephews. He gives the Pandavas a barren and forested land called Khandava, which they transform into a prosperous city called Indraprastha. The Kauravas, however, are not satisfied with their share and plot to take over Indraprastha. They invite the Pandavas to a game of dice, in which Duryodhana cheats with the help of his uncle Shakuni. The Pandavas lose everything, including their kingdom, their wealth, their wife, and their freedom. They are forced to go into exile for 13 years, with the condition that they must remain incognito in the last year or face another 12 years of exile.
- The Pandavas spend their exile in various places, facing many hardships and adventures. They also meet many sages, gods, and allies who help them in their quest. They also learn various skills and arts, especially Arjuna, who is the best archer in the world. He receives divine weapons and teachings from Lord Shiva, Indra, and Krishna, who is the eighth incarnation of Vishnu and the supreme god of the Mahabharata. Krishna becomes the friend and guide of the Pandavas, especially Arjuna, and plays a crucial role in the outcome of the story.
- The Mahabharata reaches its climax when the Pandavas complete their exile and demand their kingdom back from the Kauravas. The Kauravas refuse to give even a needlepoint of land, and war becomes inevitable. The two sides gather their armies and allies, and prepare for the great battle of Kurukshetra, which lasts for 18 days. The Mahabharata describes the war in detail, with many episodes of heroism, treachery, violence, and death. The war also involves the intervention of gods, demons, and other supernatural beings, who favor one side or the other. The most famous episode of the war is the **Bhagavad Gita**, a dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna, in which Krishna reveals his true identity and teaches Arjuna the essence of dharma, karma, and yoga.
- The war ends with the victory of the Pandavas, but at a great cost. Almost all the warriors on both sides are killed, including the Kauravas, their allies, and four of the Pandavas. Only Yudhishthira, the eldest Pandava, survives, and he is crowned as the king of Hastinapura. He rules for 36 years, and then decides to renounce the world and go to heaven.
