### Theatre

- Theatre is one of the most ancient forms of art and expression in India, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Theatre in India began as a narrative form, in which recitation, singing and dancing were integral elements. The encompassing of all forms of literature and fine arts into its physical presentation and the emphasis on narrative elements made the classical Indian drama theatrical right from the beginning.
- The earliest evidence of theatre in India is found in the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, which contain hymns, rituals and dialogues that were performed by priests and sages. The Natya Shastra, a treatise on dramaturgy attributed to Bharata Muni, is the most comprehensive and authoritative source of classical Indian theatre. It covers various aspects of theatre, such as the origin, purpose, structure, genres, styles, techniques, costumes, makeup, music, dance, acting, directing and criticism of theatre .
- The classical Indian theatre was mainly divided into two types: Sanskrit theatre and folk theatre. Sanskrit theatre was based on the literary works of Sanskrit poets and playwrights, such as Kalidasa, Bhasa, Shudraka, Vishakhadatta and Harsha. Sanskrit theatre followed the rules and conventions of the Natya Shastra and was performed in royal courts and temples. The most popular genres of Sanskrit theatre were nataka (heroic drama), prakarana (social drama), bhana (monologue), vyayoga (minor heroic drama), samavakara (mixed drama), dima (farce), anka (episodic drama) and utsrishtikanka (fragmentary drama) .
- Folk theatre was based on the local traditions, myths, legends and stories of different regions and communities of India. Folk theatre was more flexible, spontaneous and diverse than Sanskrit theatre and was performed in open spaces, such as streets, fields, courtyards and village squares. Folk theatre used various languages, dialects, costumes, props, masks, puppets, music and dance to create a lively and colorful spectacle. Some of the prominent forms of folk theatre are jatra (Bengal), tamasha (Maharashtra), nautanki (Uttar Pradesh), bhavai (Gujarat), therukoothu (Tamil Nadu), yakshagana (Karnataka), koodiyattam (Kerala) and bhand pather (Kashmir) .
- The modern theatre in India emerged during the colonial period, under the influence of the Western theatre and the social and political movements of the time. The modern theatre in India comprises mainly of English, Hindi and Hinglish (comprising of a mix of Hindi and English dialogues) plays. English theatre was brought to India during the British rule and was watched mostly by art connoisseurs of the rich, upper class. Some of the notable English playwrights and theatre groups in India are Rabindranath Tagore, Sri Aurobindo, Harindranath Chattopadhyay, Ebrahim Alkazi, Prithvi Theatre, Indian People's Theatre Association and Theatre of the Absurd. Hindi theatre was inspired by the nationalist and socialist movements and the revival of the folk theatre. Some of the prominent Hindi playwrights and theatre groups in India are Mohan Rakesh, Vijay Tendulkar, Badal Sircar, Girish Karnad, Habib Tanvir, Nandikar, Rangmandal and Rangashankara. Hinglish theatre is a contemporary and popular form of theatre that blends Hindi and English languages and cultures and appeals to the urban and cosmopolitan audiences. Some of the famous Hinglish playwrights and theatre groups in India are Mahesh Dattani, Dinesh Thakur, Lillete Dubey, Feroz Khan, Akvarious Productions and Rage Productions.
- Theatre in India is not only a form of entertainment, but also a medium of education, awareness, social change and cultural heritage. Theatre in India reflects the diversity, richness and complexity of the Indian society and its history, values, beliefs, traditions and aspirations. Theatre in India is a living and evolving art form that continues to experiment, innovate and adapt to the changing times and contexts  .
