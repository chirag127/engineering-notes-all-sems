## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is the expression of the diverse and rich cultural heritage of India, which includes oral and written forms in various languages and scripts.
- Indian literature can be classified into four broad categories: classical, folk, modern, and tribal.
- Classical literature refers to the ancient texts that are considered authoritative and sacred by various religious traditions, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Puranas, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Agamas.
- Folk literature refers to the oral traditions and folklores that are transmitted from generation to generation, often through songs, stories, proverbs, riddles, and legends. Some examples of folk literature are the Panchatantra, the Jataka tales, the Kathasaritsagara, the Hitopadesha, and the folk epics of various regions, such as the Ramcharitmanas, the Padmavat, the Alha, and the Mahabharata of the Dungri Bhils.
- Modern literature refers to the literature that emerged after the colonial period, influenced by the Western literary forms and movements, as well as the social and political changes in India. Some of the prominent writers of modern literature are Rabindranath Tagore, Munshi Premchand, R.K. Narayan, Mulk Raj Anand, Raja Rao, Sarojini Naidu, Mahadevi Varma, Ismat Chughtai, Saadat Hasan Manto, Amrita Pritam, Kamala Das, Salman Rushdie, Arundhati Roy, and Vikram Seth.
- Tribal literature refers to the literature that is produced by the indigenous and marginalized communities of India, often reflecting their struggles, aspirations, and worldviews. Some examples of tribal literature are the songs and poems of the Santals, the Mundas, the Gonds, the Bhils, the Khasis, the Nagas, and the Adivasis of Bastar.

- Indian culture is the collective way of life, values, beliefs, customs, and traditions of the people of India, which is influenced by the diversity of religions, languages, regions, and histories of the country.
- Indian culture is characterized by the concept of "unity in diversity", which means that despite the differences, there is a common thread of harmony and tolerance that binds the people together.
- Some of the salient features of Indian culture are:

  - The respect for elders, teachers, and guests, as well as the reverence for nature, animals, and plants.
  - The practice of various rituals, ceremonies, and festivals, such as Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas, Guru Nanak Jayanti, Pongal, Onam, Durga Puja, and Navratri, that celebrate the diversity of faiths and cultures.
  - The diversity of cuisines, costumes, music, dance, art, architecture, and literature, that reflect the regional and historical influences of various communities and civilizations.
  - The philosophy of non-violence, peace, and ahimsa, as well as the ideals of dharma, karma, and moksha, that guide the ethical and spiritual aspects of life.
  - The contribution of India to the fields of science, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, yoga, and meditation, as well as the development of various schools of thought, such as Vedanta, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Sufism.

- Indian tradition is the set of practices, norms, and values that are inherited from the past and are followed by the present generation, often with some modifications and adaptations.
- Indian tradition is based on the concept of "sanatana dharma", which means the eternal law or order that governs the universe and the human society.
- Some of the aspects of Indian tradition are:

  - The family system, which is usually patriarchal, joint, and extended, and emphasizes the roles and responsibilities of each member, as well as the bonds of kinship and affection.
