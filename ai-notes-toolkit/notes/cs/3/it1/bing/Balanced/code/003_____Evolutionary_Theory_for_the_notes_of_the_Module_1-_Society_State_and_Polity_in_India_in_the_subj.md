### Evolutionary Theory

- Evolutionary theory is a perspective that explains social change and development in terms of biological and cultural evolution.
- Evolutionary theory assumes that societies evolve from simple to complex, from homogeneous to heterogeneous, and from primitive to civilized over time.
- Evolutionary theory also assumes that there is a linear progression of stages or phases that societies go through, such as savagery, barbarism, and civilization.
- Evolutionary theory was influenced by the ideas of Charles Darwin, Herbert Spencer, Lewis Henry Morgan, and Edward Burnett Tylor, among others.
- Evolutionary theory was popular in the 19th and early 20th centuries, but was criticized for being ethnocentric, deterministic, and teleological.
- Evolutionary theory was challenged by other perspectives, such as historical particularism, functionalism, structuralism, and Marxism, that emphasized the diversity, complexity, and dynamism of social phenomena.
- Evolutionary theory has been revived and modified in recent decades by scholars who use concepts such as cultural evolution, sociobiology, evolutionary psychology, and memetics to explain social change and development.
