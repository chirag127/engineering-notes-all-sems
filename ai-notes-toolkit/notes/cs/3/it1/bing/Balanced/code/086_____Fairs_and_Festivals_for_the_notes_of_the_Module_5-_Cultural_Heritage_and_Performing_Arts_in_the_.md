### Fairs and Festivals

- Fairs and festivals are an integral part of the Indian culture and heritage. They reflect the diversity, richness and vibrancy of the Indian society and its people.
- Fairs and festivals are celebrated for various reasons, such as religious, seasonal, historical, cultural, artistic, social, etc. They provide an opportunity for people to express their faith, joy, gratitude, creativity, solidarity and unity.
- Fairs and festivals are also a source of entertainment, education, tourism, trade and commerce. They showcase the local arts, crafts, cuisines, costumes, music, dance, literature and traditions of different regions and communities of India.
- Some of the major fairs and festivals of India are:

  - **Diwali**: The festival of lights, celebrated in October or November, to mark the victory of good over evil and the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya after defeating Ravana. People light lamps, candles and firecrackers, exchange gifts and sweets, and worship Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha for prosperity and wealth.
  - **Holi**: The festival of colours, celebrated in March, to mark the arrival of spring and the triumph of Prahlad over Hiranyakashipu. People smear each other with colours, play with water, sing and dance, and enjoy delicacies like gujiya and thandai.
  - **Durga Puja**: The festival of worshiping Goddess Durga, celebrated in September or October, to commemorate her victory over the demon Mahishasura. People erect pandals, idols and decorations, perform rituals and prayers, and immerse the idols in water on the last day. The festival is especially popular in West Bengal, Assam, Odisha and Tripura.
  - **Ganesh Chaturthi**: The festival of celebrating the birthday of Lord Ganesha, the elephant-headed god of wisdom and success, celebrated in August or September. People install clay idols of Ganesha in their homes or public places, offer prayers and modaks, and immerse the idols in water after 10 days. The festival is especially popular in Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka and Telangana.
  - **Pongal**: The harvest festival of Tamil Nadu, celebrated in January, to thank the Sun God, the Earth and the cattle for their bounty. People cook and offer pongal, a sweet rice dish, to the Sun God, decorate their houses with kolam, and celebrate with bull-taming, folk dances and music.
  - **Pushkar Fair**: The largest camel fair in the world, held in November, in the holy town of Pushkar in Rajasthan. Thousands of camels, horses and cattle are traded, decorated and raced, along with cultural and religious activities, such as folk music, dance, puppet shows, camel beauty contests, and bathing in the sacred Pushkar lake .
  - **Konark Dance Festival**: A classical dance festival, held in December, in the backdrop of the magnificent Sun Temple in Konark, Odisha. Renowned dancers from various styles, such as Odissi, Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kuchipudi, Manipuri and Mohiniattam, perform and enchant the audience with their grace and skill .
  - **Goa Carnival**: A colourful and lively festival, held in February or March, before the Lent season, in Goa. The festival is a legacy of the Portuguese rule and features parades, floats, masks, costumes, music, dance, and street parties. The highlight of the festival is the crowning of King Momo, who declares the festivities open .
