### Harappan Script
- Harappan script is the writing system used by the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC), which flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from about 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- Harappan script is also known as Indus script, Indus-Sarasvati script, or Indus Valley script.
- Harappan script consists of about 400 signs, most of which are pictographic or ideographic, representing objects, animals, plants, or abstract concepts.
- Harappan script is written from right to left, sometimes in boustrophedon (alternating direction) style, and usually on seals, tablets, pottery, or other artifacts made of clay, stone, metal, or ivory.
- Harappan script has not been deciphered yet, despite many attempts by scholars and enthusiasts. The main challenges are the lack of bilingual texts, the brevity of the inscriptions, the diversity of the signs, and the uncertainty of the underlying language or languages.
- Harappan script is one of the oldest writing systems in the world, and a remarkable achievement of the IVC. It reflects the complex and sophisticated culture, trade, and administration of the Harappan civilization. It also provides clues to the possible connections and influences between the IVC and other ancient civilizations, such as Mesopotamia, Egypt, and China.
