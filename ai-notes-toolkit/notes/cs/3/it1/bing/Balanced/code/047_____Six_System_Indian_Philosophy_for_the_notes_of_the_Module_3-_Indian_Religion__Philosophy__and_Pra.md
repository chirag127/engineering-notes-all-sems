### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Vedanta. They are also known as shad-darshana, meaning six views or perspectives. Each system has its own founder, texts, concepts and methods of inquiry. They represent different aspects of the intellectual and spiritual traditions of Hinduism.

- Nyaya: The founder of Nyaya is Akshapada Gautama and it dated back to 2nd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with logic and epistemology. It aims to provide a valid means of knowledge (pramana) and a criterion of truth (prameya). It uses a method of logical analysis and debate (tarka) to establish the validity of an inference (anumana). It also accepts perception (pratyaksha), comparison (upamana) and testimony (shabda) as other sources of knowledge. It discusses topics such as the nature of reality, the self, the mind, causation, liberation and God. Some of the important texts of Nyaya are Nyaya Sutras, Nyaya Bhashya, Nyaya Varttika and Nyaya Kusumanjali.  

- Vaisheshika: The founder of Vaisheshika is Kanada and it dated back to 6th Century BCE. It is a school that deals with metaphysics and ontology. It proposes a theory of atomism, according to which the ultimate reality consists of six categories of substances (dravya): earth, water, fire, air, ether and self. These substances are composed of indivisible atoms (anu) that combine in different ways to form complex entities. It also accepts qualities (guna), actions (karma), generality (samanya), particularity (vishesha), inherence (samavaya) and non-existence (abhava) as other categories of reality. It discusses topics such as the nature of matter, the self, the mind, causation, liberation and God. Some of the important texts of Vaisheshika are Vaisheshika Sutras, Padartha Dharma Sangraha and Prashastapada Bhashya.  

- Samkhya: The founder of Samkhya is Kapila and it dated back to 6th Century BCE. It is a school that deals with cosmology and psychology. It proposes a dualistic theory of reality, according to which there are two fundamental principles: purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter). Purusha is the passive witness of prakriti, which is the active source of all creation. Prakriti consists of three qualities (guna): sattva (purity), rajas (activity) and tamas (inertia). These qualities manifest in various forms of evolution (parinama) and involution (apavarga). It discusses topics such as the nature of reality, the self, the mind, the senses, the elements, the evolution of the universe, the bondage and liberation of the self. Some of the important texts of Samkhya are Samkhya Sutras, Samkhya Karika and Samkhya Pravachana Sutras.  

- Yoga: The founder of Yoga is Patanjali and it dated back to 2nd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with ethics and practice. It is closely related to Samkhya in terms of its metaphysical and psychological views, but it differs in its emphasis on the discipline of the mind and the body. It aims to provide a practical method of attaining liberation (kaivalya) through the control of the fluctuations of the mind (chitta vritti). It prescribes an eightfold path (ashtanga) of practice, consisting of moral restraints (yama), observances (niyama), postures (asana), breath control (pranayama), withdrawal of the senses (pratyahara), concentration (dharana), meditation (dhyana) and absorption (samadhi).
