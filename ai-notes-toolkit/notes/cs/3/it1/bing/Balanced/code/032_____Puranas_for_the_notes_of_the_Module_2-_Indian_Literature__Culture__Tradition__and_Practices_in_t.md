### Puranas

- Puranas are a vast genre of Indian literature that deal with various topics, especially legends and traditional lore .
- Puranas are considered as one of the most ancient and important sources of Hindu culture, religion, history, and cosmology .
- Puranas are classified as Smriti (remembered) texts, as opposed to Shruti (heard) texts like the Vedas .
- Puranas are composed in Sanskrit verse, mostly in the form of narrative couplets .
- Puranas date from the 4th century BCE to about 1000 CE, and vary greatly in their origin, content, and style .
- Puranas are traditionally divided into two categories: Mahapuranas (major Puranas) and Upapuranas (minor Puranas) .
- Mahapuranas are 18 in number and are considered more authoritative and canonical than the Upapuranas .
- Mahapuranas are further divided into three groups based on their affinity to one of the three main Hindu deities: Vishnu, Shiva, and Shakti .
- Upapuranas are more than 18 in number and are considered less authoritative and canonical than the Mahapuranas .
- Upapuranas are also divided into various groups based on their affinity to different deities, sects, regions, or topics .
- Puranas contain various types of information, such as myths, legends, genealogies, cosmologies, rituals, festivals, temples, pilgrimages, ethics, philosophy, and theology  .
- Puranas are known for their intricate layers of symbolism, allegory, and metaphor, and their use of multiple perspectives and narratives .
- Puranas are also known for their adaptability and flexibility, as they reflect the changing social, religious, and cultural contexts of their times  .
- Puranas are not only texts, but also oral traditions that are transmitted and performed by various communities of storytellers, priests, and devotees  .
- Puranas are still relevant and influential in the contemporary Hindu world, as they provide guidance, inspiration, and identity to millions of Hindus  .
