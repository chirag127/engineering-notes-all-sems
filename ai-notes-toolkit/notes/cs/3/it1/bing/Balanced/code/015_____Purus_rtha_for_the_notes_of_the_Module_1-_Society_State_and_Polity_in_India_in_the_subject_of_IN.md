### Purusārtha

- Purusārtha is a Sanskrit term that means "the object or aim of human pursuit".
- It is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life. They are: dharma, artha, kāma, and mokṣa.
- Dharma means duty, morality, or righteousness. It is the ethical and social obligation of a person according to their stage and role in life.
- Artha means wealth, prosperity, or material success. It is the pursuit of economic and political well-being that enables a person to fulfill their dharma and support their family and society.
- Kāma means desire, pleasure, or enjoyment. It is the pursuit of sensual and aesthetic gratification that enriches a person's life and motivates them to act.
- Mokṣa means liberation, emancipation, or release. It is the ultimate goal of a human life that transcends the cycle of birth and death (saṃsāra) and attains union with the supreme reality (brahman).
- The four purusārthas are not mutually exclusive, but interdependent and complementary. They provide a framework for evaluating one's life and making good decisions.
- The four purusārthas are also influenced by the four stages of life (āśramas) and the four social classes (varṇas) in Hinduism. Different purusārthas may be more or less emphasized depending on one's age, occupation, and social status.
- The concept of purusārtha reflects the diversity and complexity of human experience and aspiration in Hinduism. It also shows the importance of balance, harmony, and fulfillment in one's life.
