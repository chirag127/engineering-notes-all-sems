### Pyrotechnics in India

- Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating and using fireworks for entertainment, military, or industrial purposes.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows have a long history in India, dating back to ancient times when they were used for religious festivals, royal celebrations, and special occasions.
- Some of the earliest references to pyrotechnics in India are found in Sanskrit texts such as Kautukachintamani, a 16th-century treatise by Gajapati Prataparudradeva, which describes various formulas and mixtures for fireworks.
- Fireworks were also introduced to India by foreign visitors and traders, such as the Arabs, Persians, Chinese, Portuguese, and Mughals, who brought different types of pyrotechnics and techniques to the subcontinent.
- Fireworks became an integral part of Indian culture and tradition, especially during festivals such as Diwali, the festival of lights, when people light up the sky with colorful and dazzling displays of pyrotechnics.
- Fireworks are also used for other occasions such as weddings, birthdays, inaugurations, sports events, and political rallies, as a symbol of joy, prosperity, and victory.
- However, fireworks also have a negative impact on the environment and human health, as they cause air and noise pollution, fire accidents, injuries, and deaths.
- According to a study by the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur, fireworks contribute to about 30% of the particulate matter (PM) pollution in Delhi during Diwali, which can cause respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, asthma, and cancer.
- In 2018, the Supreme Court of India banned the sale and use of most types of fireworks, except for a few "green crackers", which are supposed to have less harmful chemicals and emit less noise and smoke.
- The government and the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) have also developed and promoted the use of green crackers, which are made of eco-friendly materials and have reduced sound and emission levels.
- However, the implementation and enforcement of the ban and the availability and affordability of green crackers remain a challenge, as many people still prefer the traditional fireworks and are unaware or unconvinced of the environmental and health hazards of pyrotechnics.
- Therefore, there is a need for more awareness, education, and regulation of pyrotechnics in India, as well as more research and innovation to develop safer and greener alternatives for fireworks.
