# Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions, philosophies, and practices that have influenced its culture and history.
- The major religions in India are Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam, and Christianity. There are also smaller groups of Jews, Zoroastrians, and Baháʼís.
- Hinduism is the oldest and most dominant religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a complex and diverse tradition that encompasses many schools of thought, sects, and practices. Hinduism is based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures that contain hymns, rituals, and philosophy. Hinduism also recognizes other sources of authority, such as the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, the Puranas, and the epics of Ramayana and Mahabharata.
- Hinduism has many gods and goddesses, who are manifestations of the supreme reality called Brahman. Some of the most popular deities are Vishnu, Shiva, Ganesha, Lakshmi, Durga, and Saraswati. Hindus worship these deities in temples, shrines, and homes, and celebrate various festivals and ceremonies throughout the year. Hinduism also teaches the concepts of karma, dharma, samsara, and moksha, which are related to the cycle of rebirth and the liberation of the soul.
- Buddhism originated in India in the 6th century BCE, when Siddhartha Gautama, also known as the Buddha, attained enlightenment under a bodhi tree. Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths, which are the truths of suffering, the cause of suffering, the cessation of suffering, and the path to the cessation of suffering. The path to the cessation of suffering is the Eightfold Path, which consists of right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. Buddhism also teaches the concepts of anatman, or no-self, and nirvana, or the extinction of suffering and ignorance.
- Buddhism spread throughout India and beyond, and developed into many schools and traditions, such as Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana. Some of the most influential figures in Buddhism are the Dalai Lama, the leader of Tibetan Buddhism, and Ambedkar, the leader of the Buddhist movement among the Dalits, or the oppressed castes in India. Buddhism has about 9% of the population in India, mostly concentrated in the states of Maharashtra, Bihar, and Sikkim.
- Jainism is another ancient religion that originated in India, around the same time as Buddhism. Jainism teaches the principles of ahimsa, or non-violence, anekantavada, or non-absolutism, and aparigraha, or non-attachment. Jainism also teaches the concepts of jiva, or soul, and ajiva, or non-soul, and the cycle of rebirth and liberation. Jainism has 24 tirthankaras, or spiritual teachers, who have attained enlightenment and shown the way to others. The most recent and famous tirthankara is Mahavira, who lived in the 6th century BCE.
- Jainism has two main sects, Digambara and Svetambara, which differ in their practices and doctrines. Jainism has about 0.4% of the population in India, mostly concentrated in the states of Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Maharashtra. Jains are known for their strict vegetarianism, asceticism, and philanthropy.
- Sikhism is a relatively young religion that originated in the 15th century CE, when Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, had a mystical experience of the divine. Sikhism teaches the belief in one God, who is called Waheguru, or the Wonderful Lord. Sikhism also teaches the concepts of nam, or the divine name, shabad, or the divine word, and sangat, or the holy congregation. Sikhism has 10 gurus, or spiritual guides, who have shaped the history and teachings of Sikhism. The last and eternal guru is the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy scripture of Sikhism, which contains the hymns and writings of the gurus and other saints.
