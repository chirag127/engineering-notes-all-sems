# Water Management in India

Water management is the planning, development, allocation, utilization and conservation of water resources for various purposes such as drinking, irrigation, industry, energy, sanitation, ecology and recreation. Water management is essential for ensuring water security, health, livelihoods and economic growth in India.

Some of the key aspects of water management in India are:

- Providing drinking water to rural areas: The government has been responsible for providing drinking water to 833 million rural people in some 640,000 villages across the country. However, due to various challenges such as declining groundwater levels, contamination, poor infrastructure, weak institutions and low tariffs, the service delivery has been inadequate and unsustainable. The World Bank has been supporting the government in improving the rural water supply through community participation, decentralization, demand responsiveness, convergence, capacity building and innovation.

- Groundwater management: Groundwater is the main source of water for domestic, agricultural and industrial uses in India, accounting for about 63% of the total irrigation and 85% of the rural domestic water supply. However, groundwater is also overexploited, polluted and poorly regulated in many parts of the country, leading to depletion of aquifers, deterioration of water quality, land subsidence, saltwater intrusion and reduced base flows to rivers. The World Bank has been assisting the government in implementing the Atal Bhujal Yojana, India’s largest community-led groundwater management program, which aims to improve rural livelihoods and build resilience in seven Indian states that have the highest rates of groundwater depletion.

- Improving access to water on dry rainfed lands: About 60% of India’s net sown area is rainfed, which supports 40% of the population and 60% of the livestock. Rainfed agriculture is vulnerable to climate variability, droughts, floods, soil erosion, low productivity and poverty. The World Bank has been supporting the government in enhancing the water availability and productivity on rainfed lands through watershed development, soil and water conservation, rainwater harvesting, micro-irrigation, crop diversification, agroforestry and livelihood interventions.

- Cleaning the Ganga: The Ganga is the most sacred and the longest river in India, which supports about 43% of the population and 25% of the land area. However, the river is also severely polluted by domestic, industrial and agricultural wastes, affecting the water quality, biodiversity, health and livelihoods of millions of people. The World Bank has been supporting the government in implementing the National Ganga River Basin Project, which aims to improve the management and restoration of the Ganga through institutional strengthening, wastewater treatment, solid waste management, riverfront development, ecological conservation and public awareness.

- Managing floods and droughts: India is prone to frequent and severe floods and droughts, which cause huge losses of lives, livelihoods, assets and infrastructure. The World Bank has been supporting the government in enhancing the resilience and preparedness of the country to cope with these disasters through flood forecasting and early warning systems, flood risk assessment and mapping, flood protection and drainage works, drought monitoring and mitigation, crop insurance and disaster recovery and reconstruction.

- Enhancing dam safety in India: India has about 5,200 large dams, which provide irrigation, water supply, hydropower, flood control and recreation benefits. However, many of these dams are old, poorly maintained and unsafe, posing risks to the downstream population and environment. The World Bank has been supporting the government in improving the safety and performance of selected dams through structural and non-structural measures, such as dam rehabilitation and improvement, dam safety institutional strengthening, dam safety guidelines and regulations, dam safety emergency action plans and dam safety public awareness.
