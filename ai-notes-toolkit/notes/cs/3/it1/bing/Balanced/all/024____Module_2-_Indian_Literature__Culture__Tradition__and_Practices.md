## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is the expression of the diverse and rich cultural heritage of India, which includes oral and written forms in various languages and dialects. Indian literature reflects the themes of religion, philosophy, mythology, history, society, and art. Some of the oldest and most influential literary works of India are the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Puranas. Indian literature also includes classical and modern genres such as poetry, drama, fiction, and non-fiction. Some of the prominent writers and poets of Indian literature are Kalidasa, Valmiki, Vyasa, Bhartrihari, Rabindranath Tagore, Sarojini Naidu, R.K. Narayan, Raja Rao, Salman Rushdie, Arundhati Roy, and Amitav Ghosh  .

- Indian culture is the collective way of life, values, beliefs, customs, and traditions of the people of India, which has been shaped by the history, geography, diversity, and spirituality of the country. Indian culture is known for its unity in diversity, as it encompasses various religions, languages, ethnicities, cuisines, arts, and festivals. Some of the core values of Indian culture are respect for elders, hospitality, tolerance, non-violence, and harmony. Some of the distinctive features of Indian culture are the caste system, the joint family system, the concept of karma and dharma, the practice of yoga and meditation, the reverence for nature and animals, and the celebration of life and death  .

- Indian tradition is the transmission of the customs, rituals, and practices of the ancestors and the society to the present and future generations. Indian tradition is based on the principles of the Vedas and the teachings of the sages and the saints. Indian tradition is manifested in various aspects of life, such as the rites of passage, the festivals, the ceremonies, the worship, the food, the clothing, the music, the dance, and the architecture. Some of the examples of Indian tradition are the namaste greeting, the tilak mark, the bindi dot, the sindoor powder, the sacred thread, the turban, the sari, the henna, the rangoli, the diya, the puja, the aarti, the prasad, the mantra, the bhajan, the kirtan, the classical dances, the folk dances, the temples, the stupas, and the monuments  .

- Indian practices are the actions and behaviors that are performed by the people of India, either individually or collectively, for various purposes and occasions. Indian practices are influenced by the religious, cultural, social, and personal factors of the people. Indian practices can be classified into four categories: religious practices, cultural practices, social practices, and personal practices. Some of the examples of religious practices are fasting, pilgrimage, charity, sacrifice, and conversion. Some of the examples of cultural practices are art, literature, music, dance, and drama. Some of the examples of social practices are marriage, divorce, inheritance, adoption, and education. Some of the examples of personal practices are hygiene, diet, dress, etiquette, and hobbies  .
