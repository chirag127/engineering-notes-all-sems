# The Upanishads

- The Upanishads are a collection of 108 texts that form the final part of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism.
- The Upanishads explore the nature of the self (Atman) and its relation to the ultimate reality (Brahman), which is the source and essence of everything.
- The Upanishads teach that the self is not the body, mind, or senses, but a pure and eternal consciousness that transcends the physical world.
- The Upanishads also teach that Brahman is not a personal god, but a universal and impersonal principle that pervades all existence and manifests as various gods and beings.
- The Upanishads emphasize the importance of knowledge (jnana) over ritual (karma) as the means to attain liberation (moksha) from the cycle of rebirth (samsara).
- The Upanishads contain various methods of inquiry, meditation, and ethical conduct to help the seeker realize the identity of the self and Brahman, and experience the bliss and peace of liberation.
- The Upanishads are the foundation of the philosophical school of Vedanta, which has influenced many other traditions such as Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Neo-Vedanta.
