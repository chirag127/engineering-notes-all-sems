# Dance

Dance is an important part of Indian culture. It is used for many different purposes, such as:

- Worshiping the gods and goddesses
- Celebrating festivals and occasions
- Expressing emotions and feelings
- Preserving traditions and values
- Showcasing beauty and talent
- Entertaining and educating audiences

Dance in India has a long and rich history. It can be traced back to the ancient times, when it was depicted in sculptures, paintings, and literature. There are many forms of dance in India, which can be broadly classified into two categories: classical and folk.

## Classical Dance

Classical dance is a refined and sophisticated form of dance that follows strict rules and principles. It is based on the ancient texts of Natyashastra and Abhinaya Darpana, which describe the theory and practice of dance. Classical dance is performed by trained and skilled dancers, who use gestures, expressions, and movements to convey a story or a theme. Classical dance is usually accompanied by classical music and instruments.

There are eight recognized forms of classical dance in India, which are:

- Bharatanatyam: Originated in Tamil Nadu, it is one of the oldest and most popular forms of classical dance. It is known for its grace, elegance, and precision. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who wear elaborate costumes and jewelry. It is based on the concept of bhava (emotion), raga (melody), and tala (rhythm).
- Kathak: Originated in North India, it is a form of dance that narrates stories through gestures and expressions. It is influenced by the Mughal and Persian cultures, and has elements of Hindu and Islamic traditions. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who wear colorful costumes and bells on their ankles. It is based on the concept of tatkar (footwork), chakkar (spins), and abhinaya (expression).
- Kathakali: Originated in Kerala, it is a form of dance-drama that depicts the epics of Ramayana and Mahabharata. It is known for its elaborate makeup, costumes, and masks. It is performed by male dancers, who use exaggerated facial expressions and eye movements to convey emotions. It is based on the concept of navarasas (nine emotions), mudras (hand gestures), and kalasams (rhythmic patterns).
- Kuchipudi: Originated in Andhra Pradesh, it is a form of dance-drama that combines dance, music, and dialogue. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who wear traditional costumes and ornaments. It is based on the concept of vachika (speech), angika (body), aharya (costume), and sattvika (mind).
- Manipuri: Originated in Manipur, it is a form of dance that is inspired by the Vaishnava tradition of Hinduism. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who wear simple and elegant costumes. It is based on the concept of lasya (grace), tandava (energy), and nritta (pure dance).
- Mohiniyattam: Originated in Kerala, it is a form of dance that is performed by female dancers, who portray the character of Mohini, the female avatar of Vishnu. It is known for its graceful and feminine movements, and its expressive eyes and face. It is based on the concept of atma (soul), sringara (love), and bhakti (devotion).
- Odissi: Originated in Odisha, it is a form of dance that is influenced by the temple sculptures and paintings of the region. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who wear elaborate costumes and jewelry. It is based on the concept of tribhangi (three bends), chauka (square stance), and pallavi (floral motif).
- Sattriya: Originated in Assam, it is a form of dance that is associated with the Vaishnava monasteries of the state. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who wear simple and modest costumes. It is based on the concept of nritya (dance), geet (song), and bhaona (drama).
