# Drama in Indian Culture and Society

- Drama is one of the oldest and most popular forms of performing arts in India. It has a rich and diverse history that spans from ancient times to the present day.
- Drama in India is influenced by various religious, social, and cultural factors. It reflects the values, beliefs, and aspirations of the people who create and perform it.
- Drama in India can be broadly classified into two categories: traditional and modern. Traditional drama includes various forms of folk and classical theatre that have been practiced for centuries in different regions of India. Modern drama includes various forms of urban and contemporary theatre that have emerged since the colonial and post-colonial periods.

## Traditional Drama in India

- Traditional drama in India is also known as rural or village theatre. It is performed during different social and religious occasions, such as festivals, fairs, weddings, and rituals. It is usually based on mythological, historical, or moral stories that convey a message or a lesson to the audience.
- Traditional drama in India is characterized by its use of colorful costumes, masks, makeup, music, dance, and props. It often involves improvisation, audience participation, and comic elements. It is performed in open-air spaces, such as courtyards, fields, or streets, with minimal or no stage settings.
- Some of the famous traditional dramas or theatres of India are:

  - Ramleela: A dramatization of the life and deeds of Lord Rama, based on the epic Ramayana. It is performed in various parts of North India, especially during the festival of Dussehra.
  - Rasleela: A dramatization of the love and playfulness of Lord Krishna and his consort Radha, based on the Bhagavata Purana. It is performed in various parts of North and East India, especially during the festival of Holi.
  - Yakshagana: A form of musical theatre that combines dialogue, dance, and music. It is based on the stories from the epics, Puranas, and local legends. It is performed in various parts of South India, especially in Karnataka and Kerala.
  - Kathakali: A form of dance-drama that combines elaborate gestures, facial expressions, and eye movements. It is based on the stories from the epics, Puranas, and local legends. It is performed in various parts of South India, especially in Kerala.
  - Bhavai: A form of folk theatre that depicts the social and religious issues of the rural and urban communities. It is performed in various parts of West India, especially in Gujarat and Rajasthan.
  - Tamasha: A form of folk theatre that combines comedy, satire, and romance. It is based on the stories from the folk literature, history, and contemporary life. It is performed in various parts of West India, especially in Maharashtra.
  - Jatra: A form of folk theatre that combines drama, music, dance, and spectacle. It is based on the stories from the epics, Puranas, history, and contemporary life. It is performed in various parts of East India, especially in West Bengal, Odisha, and Assam.
  - Bhaona: A form of religious theatre that depicts the stories of Lord Krishna and his devotees, based on the works of the saint-poet Sankardeva. It is performed in various parts of East India, especially in Assam.

## Modern Drama in India

- Modern drama in India is also known as urban or contemporary theatre. It emerged as a result of the exposure to the Western forms of theatre and literature during the colonial and post-colonial periods. It reflects the social and political realities of the modern Indian society and its challenges and aspirations.
- Modern drama in India is characterized by its use of realistic or experimental techniques, themes, and styles. It often deals with issues such as nationalism, identity, gender, caste, class, religion, and violence. It is performed in closed spaces, such as auditoriums, halls, or studios, with elaborate or minimal stage settings.
