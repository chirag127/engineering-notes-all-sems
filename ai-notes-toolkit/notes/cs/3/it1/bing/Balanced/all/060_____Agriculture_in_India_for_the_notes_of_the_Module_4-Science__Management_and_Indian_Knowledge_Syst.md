# Agriculture in India

Agriculture is one of the most important sectors of the Indian economy, contributing about 16% of the GDP and employing about 58% of the population. India has a diverse agro-climatic zone, ranging from tropical to temperate, and produces a variety of crops, such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugarcane, cotton, jute, etc. India is also the world's largest producer and consumer of milk, and a major producer and exporter of livestock products, such as meat, eggs, and leather.

Some of the key features of agriculture in India are:

- India has the world's largest area under cultivation, with about 156 million hectares of arable land and 195 million hectares of net sown area.
- India has the world's largest irrigation network, with about 70 million hectares of irrigated area, accounting for about 36% of the net sown area.
- India has the world's largest number of small and marginal farmers, with about 86% of the total holdings being less than 2 hectares in size.
- India has a rich diversity of crops and cropping systems, with about 15 major agro-climatic zones and 127 sub-zones, and a wide range of soil types and rainfall patterns.
- India has a strong tradition of agricultural research and extension, with a network of agricultural universities, research institutes, and extension agencies, and a large pool of agricultural scientists and professionals.

Some of the major challenges and opportunities for agriculture in India are:

- Climate change and extreme weather events, such as droughts, floods, heat waves, cyclones, etc., which pose a threat to crop production, water availability, soil health, and biodiversity.
- Population growth and urbanization, which increase the demand for food, feed, fiber, and fuel, and also reduce the availability of land, water, and labor for agriculture.
- Market volatility and price fluctuations, which affect the income and livelihood of farmers, and also influence the cropping decisions and investment in agriculture.
- Policy reforms and institutional support, which are needed to enhance the productivity, profitability, and sustainability of agriculture, and also to address the issues of farmers' welfare, food security, and environmental protection.
- Technological innovation and adoption, which are essential to improve the efficiency and quality of agricultural inputs, outputs, and processes, and also to cope with the changing climate and consumer preferences.

Some of the key initiatives and programs for agriculture in India are:

- Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), which is a crop insurance scheme that provides financial support to farmers in case of crop failure due to natural calamities, pests, and diseases.
- Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY), which is a scheme that aims to enhance the irrigation coverage and water use efficiency in agriculture, and also to promote the integrated management of water resources.
- Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN), which is a scheme that provides a direct income support of Rs. 6000 per year to small and marginal farmers, in three equal installments.
- National Food Security Mission (NFSM), which is a scheme that aims to increase the production and productivity of rice, wheat, pulses, coarse cereals, and oilseeds, through the provision of quality seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, farm machinery, etc.
- National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA), which is a scheme that aims to promote the adoption of sustainable and climate-resilient agricultural practices, such as organic farming, rainfed agriculture, soil health management, etc.
