# Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- Cultural heritage is the legacy of physical and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations.
- Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art, and artefacts), intangible culture (such as folklore, traditions, language, and knowledge), and natural heritage (such as culturally significant landscapes, and biodiversity).
- Performing arts are forms of art that use the body, voice, or objects to convey artistic expression, such as music, dance, theatre, opera, mime, puppetry, circus arts, and performance art.
- Performing arts are often performed in front of an audience, but may also be performed for a specific purpose, such as ritual, education, or therapy.
- Performing arts are part of the intangible cultural heritage of many cultures and play a vital role in preserving and transmitting the values, beliefs, and identity of a group or society.
- Performing arts can also reflect human creativity and innovation, and contribute to the social, economic, and cultural development of a community or a country.
- Some examples of performing arts that are recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage are:

  - Urtiin Duu, traditional folk long song from Mongolia, which is sung in a specific vocal technique that requires great skill and endurance.
  - Wayang puppet theatre from Indonesia, which is a form of storytelling that uses flat leather puppets, accompanied by a gamelan orchestra and a narrator.
  - Flamenco from Spain, which is a musical and dance expression that combines singing, guitar playing, hand clapping, finger snapping, and dancing, influenced by various cultures and regions.
  - Kabuki from Japan, which is a classical theatre form that combines drama, music, dance, and elaborate costumes and make-up, and is based on historical events, moral conflicts, and love stories.
  - Tango from Argentina and Uruguay, which is a social dance and musical genre that originated in the late 19th century among the lower classes of Buenos Aires and Montevideo, and is characterized by its sensual and expressive movements and rhythms.

- To learn more about cultural heritage and performing arts, you can:

  - Explore the UNESCO website on intangible cultural heritage, which provides information, resources, and examples of the diverse forms of performing arts around the world.
  - Watch documentaries, films, or online videos that showcase the history, diversity, and richness of performing arts in different cultures and contexts.
  - Visit museums, galleries, theatres, or cultural centers that exhibit, preserve, or promote cultural heritage and performing arts in your area or online.
  - Participate in or attend cultural festivals, events, or workshops that celebrate, showcase, or teach performing arts from various traditions and backgrounds.
  - Learn a new performing art form or practice an existing one, either individually or with others, and express yourself creatively and artistically.
