# Slavery in Ancient India

- Slavery was a universal social evil and had its origin even from the early period of history. In India, its origin is closely associated with the caste system .
- The early history of slavery in the Indian subcontinent is contested because it depends on the translations of terms such as dasa and dasyu, which could mean either slave or enemy.
- Greek writer Megasthenes in his work Indika, while describing the Maurya Empire, states that slavery was banned in Indian society.
- However, other sources indicate that slavery was prevalent in ancient India, especially in the context of war, debt, crime, and domestic service.
- Slaves in ancient India were mostly of foreign origin, such as Greeks, Persians, Scythians, and Kushans, who were captured in wars or sold by their own people.
- Slaves were also acquired through trade, especially with Africa, where many Africans were brought to India as slaves and traders, and some of them rose to high positions in Indian society.
- Slaves in ancient India had some legal rights and protections, such as the right to be freed after six years of service, the right to own property, and the right to seek asylum.
- Slaves were also subject to various duties and restrictions, such as the obligation to serve their masters faithfully, the prohibition to marry without their consent, and the liability to be punished or sold for misconduct.
- Slavery in ancient India was not based on racial or religious discrimination, but rather on social and economic factors.
- Slavery in ancient India was influenced by the laws and customs of various regions and dynasties, such as the Mauryas, the Guptas, the Cholas, the Mughals, and the British .
