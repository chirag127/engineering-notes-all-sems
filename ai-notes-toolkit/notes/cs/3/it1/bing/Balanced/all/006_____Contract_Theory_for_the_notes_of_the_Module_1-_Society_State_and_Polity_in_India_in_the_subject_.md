# Contract Theory

Contract theory is the study of how people and organizations construct and develop legal agreements. It analyzes how parties with conflicting interests build formal and informal contracts, even tenancy.

Contract theory covers the implied trust between the different parties and investigates the formation of contracts in the presence of asymmetric information, which occurs when one party to an economic transaction possesses greater material knowledge than the other party.

Contract theory examines a decision maker’s behavior under specific structures. Under these structures, contract theory aims to input an algorithm that will optimize the individual’s decisions.

Some of the topics covered by contract theory are:

- Agency theory: This is the study of how principals (such as shareholders or employers) can design contracts to align the incentives of agents (such as managers or employees) with their own interests.
- Incomplete contract theory: This is the study of how parties can design contracts that are flexible and adaptable to unforeseen contingencies, and how the allocation of residual rights affects the bargaining power and efficiency of the parties.
- Mechanism design theory: This is the study of how to design rules or institutions that elicit truthful information or desirable actions from self-interested agents, such as auctions, voting systems, or social choice functions.

Contract theory has applications in various fields, such as law, economics, political science, sociology, and psychology. It can help us understand and improve the performance of contracts in different contexts, such as employment, corporate governance, insurance, regulation, and public policy.
