# Purusārtha

- Purusārtha is a Sanskrit term that means "the object or aim of human life" .
- It is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life .
- The four purusārthas are: 
  - Dharma: the duty, morality, ethics, law, and righteousness that guide one's actions and conduct in society  .
  - Artha: the wealth, prosperity, security, and material well-being that enable one to fulfill one's dharma and support one's family and society  .
  - Kāma: the desire, pleasure, enjoyment, and love that enrich one's life and motivate one to pursue artha and dharma  .
  - Mokṣa: the liberation, freedom, and release from the cycle of birth and death (saṃsāra) that is the ultimate goal of human existence  .
- The purusārthas are the inherent values of the universe and the blueprint for human fulfillment .
- They offer a way for evaluating one's life and making good decisions .
- They also reflect the different stages of life (āśramas) and the different social classes (varṇas) in Hindu society  .
- The purusārthas are not mutually exclusive, but rather interdependent and complementary  .
- They are also subject to interpretation and adaptation according to one's context and circumstances  .
- The purusārthas are the basis of Hindu ethics and spirituality   .
