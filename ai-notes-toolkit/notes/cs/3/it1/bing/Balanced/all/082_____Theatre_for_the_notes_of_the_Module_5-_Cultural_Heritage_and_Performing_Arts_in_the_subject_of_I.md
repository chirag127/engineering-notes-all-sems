# Theatre

Theatre is a form of performing art that involves actors performing live on a stage for an audience. Theatre can include various elements such as dialogue, music, dance, gesture, costume, scenery, lighting, and sound.

## Theatre in India

- Theatre in India is one of the most ancient forms of theatre and it features a detailed textual, sculptural, and dramatic effects which emerged in mid first millennium BC.
- Theatre in India began as a narrative form, in which recitation, singing and dancing were integral elements. The encompassing of all forms of literature and fine arts into its physical presentation and the emphasis on narrative elements made the classical Indian drama theatrical right from the beginning.
- The earliest form of theatre in India was the Sanskrit theatre, which flourished from the 1st century CE to the 10th century CE. The Sanskrit theatre was based on the Natyashastra, a treatise on drama written by Bharata Muni. The Natyashastra defined the rules and principles of theatre, such as the types of drama, the structure of the plot, the modes of expression, the classification of characters, the use of music and dance, and the aesthetics of theatre.
- The Sanskrit theatre gave rise to various regional forms of theatre, such as the Prakrit theatre, the Tamil theatre, the Odia theatre, the Bengali theatre, the Kannada theatre, the Malayalam theatre, the Marathi theatre, and the Telugu theatre. These regional forms of theatre developed their own styles, themes, and conventions, influenced by the local culture, history, and literature.
- The medieval period in India saw the emergence of various forms of folk theatre, such as the Nautanki, the Tamasha, the Bhavai, the Yakshagana, the Kathakali, the Koodiyattam, the Chhau, the Bhand Pather, the Jatra, the Ramleela, and the Rasleela. These forms of folk theatre were popular among the masses and often incorporated elements of comedy, satire, social criticism, and religious devotion. They also used various forms of music, dance, puppetry, and masks to enhance the theatrical experience.
- The modern theatre in India comprises mainly of English, Hindi and Hinglish (comprising of a mix of Hindi and English dialogues) plays. English theatre was brought to India during the British rule and was watched mostly by art connoisseurs of the rich, upper class. Hindi theatre emerged as a response to the colonial domination and the nationalist movement, and was influenced by the Parsi theatre, the IPTA (Indian People's Theatre Association), and the progressive writers' movement. Hinglish theatre is a contemporary form of theatre that reflects the urban and cosmopolitan culture of India, and often deals with issues such as identity, sexuality, globalization, and social media.
- Theatre in India is also influenced by various forms of western theatre, such as realism, naturalism, expressionism, absurdism, and postmodernism. Some of the prominent playwrights and theatre personalities of India are Rabindranath Tagore, Mohan Rakesh, Vijay Tendulkar, Girish Karnad, Badal Sircar, Mahesh Dattani, Habib Tanvir, Ebrahim Alkazi, Prithviraj Kapoor, Shyam Benegal, Naseeruddin Shah, and Anupam Kher.
- Theatre in India is also celebrated through various festivals and awards, such as the Prithvi Theatre Festival, the Bharat Rang Mahotsav, the Jairangam - Jaipur Theatre Festival, the Nandikar's National Theatre Festival, the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award, and the Mahindra Excellence in Theatre Awards.
