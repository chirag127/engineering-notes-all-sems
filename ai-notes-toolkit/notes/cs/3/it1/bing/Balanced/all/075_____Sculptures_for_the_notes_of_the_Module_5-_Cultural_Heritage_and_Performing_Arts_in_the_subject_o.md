# Sculptures

Sculptures are three-dimensional art forms that are created by carving, modeling, casting, or assembling various materials. Sculptures can be realistic, abstract, or symbolic, and can represent human, animal, or divine figures, as well as abstract shapes or concepts.

## Indian Sculptures

Indian sculptures are the sculptural traditions, forms, and styles of the civilizations of the Indian subcontinent. Indian sculptures have a long and rich history, dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 3300-1300 BCE), and continuing to the present day. Indian sculptures reflect the diverse religious, cultural, and artistic influences that have shaped the Indian society over the centuries. Some of the major themes and characteristics of Indian sculptures are:

- Sculpture was the favoured medium of artistic expression on the Indian subcontinent. Indian buildings were profusely adorned with sculptures and indeed are often inseparable from them.
- The subject matter of Indian sculptures was almost invariably abstracted human forms that were used to instruct people in the truths of the Hindu, Buddhist, or Jain religions. Sculptures also depicted mythological stories, historical events, and natural phenomena.
- Indian sculptures show a remarkable variety of styles, techniques, and materials, ranging from stone, metal, wood, ivory, clay, terracotta, to stucco, plaster, and cement. Indian sculptures also display a high degree of skill, creativity, and aesthetic sensibility.
- Indian sculptures can be classified into two broad categories: relief and free-standing. Relief sculptures are carved on a flat surface, such as a wall, pillar, or rock, and project outwards. Free-standing sculptures are carved in the round and can be viewed from all sides.
- Indian sculptures can also be classified into three main periods: ancient, medieval, and modern. Ancient sculptures (c. 3300 BCE-1200 CE) include the Indus Valley, Mauryan, Shunga, Kushan, Gupta, Pallava, Chalukya, and Chola periods. Medieval sculptures (c. 1200-1700 CE) include the Delhi Sultanate, Mughal, Vijayanagara, Hoysala, and Rajput periods. Modern sculptures (c. 1700-present) include the colonial, post-colonial, and contemporary periods.

## Examples of Famous Indian Sculptures

Some of the famous historic sculptures of the Indian subcontinent are:

- The Dancing Girl, Mohen Jo Daro, c. 2500 BCE: The statue of the famous 'Dancing Girl, found to be in the times of Harappa. It is a small bronze figurine of a young girl with a bangle on her left arm and her right hand on her hip. It is one of the earliest examples of Indian metal sculpture and shows the artistic sophistication of the Indus Valley Civilization.
- Ashoka Pillar, Sarnath, 3rd Century BCE: The most celebrated capital (the four-lion one at Sarnath (Uttar Pradesh)) erected by Emperor Ashoka circa 250 BCE. also called the "Ashoka Column". It is a stone pillar with a polished surface and a carved capital of four lions standing back to back on a circular base. It is a symbol of the Buddhist faith and the Mauryan empire, and also bears the edicts of Ashoka that preach the principles of dharma (righteousness).
- Buddha Statue, Sarnath, 5th Century CE: The statue of the Buddha in the Dharmachakra Mudra (gesture of turning the wheel of law) at Sarnath, where he delivered his first sermon after attaining enlightenment. It is a sandstone sculpture of the Buddha with a serene expression, elongated earlobes, curly hair, and a monastic robe. It is one of the finest examples of the Gupta style of sculpture, which is known for its grace, elegance, and idealism.
- Nataraj, Chola Bronze Sculpture, 11th Century CE: The statue of Nataraj, the cosmic dancer and a form of Shiva, the Hindu god of destruction and creation. It is a bronze sculpture of Shiva dancing in a circle of fire, with his four arms holding various attributes, such as a drum, a flame, a trident, and a gesture of fearlessness. It is a symbol of the dynamic and rhythmic energy of the universe, and the balance of opposites. It is one of the masterpieces of the Chola bronze sculpture, which is known for its realism, dynamism, and fluidity.
