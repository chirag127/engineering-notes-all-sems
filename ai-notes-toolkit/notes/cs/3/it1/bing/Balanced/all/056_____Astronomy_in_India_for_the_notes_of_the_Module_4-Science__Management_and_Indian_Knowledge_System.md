# Astronomy in India

- Astronomy has a long history in the Indian subcontinent, stretching from pre-historic to modern times.
- Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilisation or earlier, when ancient Indians observed the movements of celestial bodies and developed calendars, mathematical techniques, and astronomical instruments .
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, the sacred texts of Hinduism. The Vedanga Jyotisha, attributed to Lagadha, is one of the earliest texts on astronomy in India, dating from around the 6th century BCE .
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, Sanskrit translations of Greek texts disseminated from the 2nd century CE . These texts introduced concepts such as the zodiac, the ecliptic, the epicycles, and the equinoxes to Indian astronomy .
- The classical period of Indian astronomy, from the 5th to the 10th centuries CE, saw the development of various schools of astronomy, such as the Siddhanta, the Aryabhatiya, the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, and the Suryasiddhanta, which refined and expanded the astronomical knowledge and techniques of the earlier periods . Some of the notable astronomers of this period include Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and Lalla .
- The medieval period of Indian astronomy, from the 11th to the 18th centuries CE, witnessed the emergence of new astronomical traditions, such as the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, the Islamic astronomy in India, and the Sikh astronomy . Some of the prominent astronomers of this period include Sripati, Madhava, Nilakantha Somayaji, Al-Biruni, Fathullah Shirazi, and Guru Nanak .
- The modern period of Indian astronomy, from the 19th century CE onwards, saw the establishment of various observatories, institutions, and organisations for the advancement of astronomy in India, such as the Madras Observatory, the Asiatic Society, the Indian Association for the Cultivation of Science, the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, the Indian Space Research Organisation, and the National Centre for Radio Astrophysics . Some of the influential astronomers of this period include Radhanath Sikdar, C.V. Raman, Meghnad Saha, Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, Jayant Narlikar, and U.R. Rao .
