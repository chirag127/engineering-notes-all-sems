# Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange between different regions and civilizations.
- India has a long history of trade since the Harappan civilization (2600-1900 BCE), which had extensive contacts with Mesopotamia, Egypt, and Central Asia. The Harappans traded mainly in gold, copper, ivory, beads, textiles, and pottery.
- After the decline of the Harappan civilization, new urban centers emerged in the Gangetic plains, such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura. These cities became the hubs of trade and political power in ancient India. They also developed coinage, writing, and art.
- Trade in ancient India was facilitated by various means of transport, such as roads, rivers, and sea routes. The Mauryan empire (321-185 BCE) built a network of roads across the subcontinent, connecting the major cities and regions. The rivers, such as the Ganges, the Indus, and the Brahmaputra, were also used for trade and navigation. The sea routes connected India with the rest of the world, especially the Mediterranean, the Arabian Peninsula, and Southeast Asia.
- Trade in ancient India involved a variety of goods, such as spices, perfumes, gems, pearls, silk, cotton, metals, ivory, and timber. India was famous for its spices, such as pepper, cardamom, cinnamon, and ginger, which were in high demand in the Roman and Persian markets. India also exported textiles, such as muslin, calico, and chintz, which were admired for their quality and design. India imported goods, such as wine, glass, coral, gold, silver, and horses, from other countries.
- Trade in ancient India was regulated by various laws and institutions, such as the Arthashastra of Kautilya, the guilds of merchants and artisans, and the customs and taxes. The Arthashastra was a treatise on statecraft, economy, and law, written by Kautilya, the prime minister of Chandragupta Maurya. It prescribed rules and regulations for trade and commerce, such as weights and measures, quality standards, prices, contracts, and duties. The guilds were associations of traders and craftsmen, who organized and protected their interests and rights. The customs and taxes were levied by the state and local authorities, to ensure revenue and security. 
- Trade in ancient India had a significant impact on the economy, society, and culture of the subcontinent and beyond. It stimulated the growth of urbanization, agriculture, industry, and banking. It also fostered the exchange of ideas, beliefs, and technologies, such as Buddhism, Hinduism, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and art. Trade in ancient India contributed to the development of a rich and diverse civilization, which influenced and was influenced by other cultures of the world.
