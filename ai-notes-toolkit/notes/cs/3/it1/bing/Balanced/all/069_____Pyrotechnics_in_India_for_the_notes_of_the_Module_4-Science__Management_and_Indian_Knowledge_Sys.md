# Pyrotechnics in India

Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating and using fireworks for entertainment, military, or industrial purposes. Pyrotechnics has a long and rich history in India, dating back to ancient times. Here are some points to note about pyrotechnics in India:

- According to some sources, fireworks were invented in India by the alchemists of the Gupta dynasty (320-550 CE) who experimented with various chemical mixtures and explosives. They used fireworks for religious ceremonies, festivals, and warfare. 
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows were a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events, and special occasions like weddings. Some of the earliest records of pyrotechnical shows in India are made by foreign travelers and ambassadors, such as Abdur Razzaq, who visited the court of the Vijayanagar king Devaraya II in 1443. 
- Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of Odisha. The book contains recipes for various types of fireworks, such as rockets, fountains, wheels, and crackers. 
- India is one of the largest producers and consumers of fireworks in the world, especially during the festival of Diwali, which is celebrated with lights and crackers. The fireworks industry in India is mainly concentrated in Sivakasi, Tamil Nadu, which accounts for about 90% of the domestic production and 80% of the exports. The industry employs about 800,000 people, mostly women and children, who work in hazardous conditions and low wages. 
- Fireworks are a major source of air pollution in India, especially during Diwali, when the levels of particulate matter, nitrogen oxides, sulfur dioxide, and carbon monoxide increase significantly, affecting the health and environment of millions of people. Fireworks also cause noise pollution, fire accidents, injuries, and deaths. 
- In 2018, the Supreme Court of India banned the sale and use of most types of fireworks, except for a few "green crackers" that have reduced emission levels and noise levels. The court also imposed time limits and designated areas for bursting crackers. The ban was aimed at curbing the air pollution and protecting the right to life and health of the citizens. 
- The government of India, along with various research institutes and private companies, has been working on developing and promoting "green crackers" that are eco-friendly and safer than conventional fireworks. Some of the features of green crackers are: they use less harmful chemicals, they produce water vapor and air as byproducts, they have a unique identification number and a QR code for tracking and verification, and they have a sound limit of 125 decibels.
