### Marriage

Marriage is a legally and socially recognized union of two or more people, usually involving rights and obligations of the partners. Marriage is one of the most important institutions in Indian society, as it shapes the family, kinship, and social structure.

Some of the main points to note about marriage in India are:

- Marriage in India varies according to the region, the religion, the community and the personal preferences of the bride and groom.
- The average age of marriage for women in India has increased to 21 years, according to the 2011 Census of India. In 2009, about 7% of women got married before the age of 18.
- Arranged marriages have long been the norm in Indian society, where the families of the bride and groom play a major role in selecting the partner and negotiating the terms of the marriage .
- However, there is a growing trend for some women to choose their own partners or to not marry at all, especially in urban areas and among educated and economically independent groups.
- Technology is also replacing traditional methods of matchmaking. Instead of relying on family connections, many young Indians and their parents are turning to online marriage sites to find a partner.
- There are few inter-caste or interfaith marriages in India, as marrying within one's caste remains an essential feature of marriage in India. In a 2014 survey of more than 70,000 people, fewer than 5% of marriages were inter-caste and fewer than 2% were interfaith.
- Marriage laws in India vary according to the religion of the parties, as there is no uniform civil code that applies to all citizens. There are different personal laws for Hindus, Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains and Parsis, which govern matters such as marriage, divorce, inheritance, adoption and maintenance.
- In order to be married legally in India, both the bride and groom must complete certain requirements that are laid down by the laws, such as age, consent, mental capacity, and absence of prohibited relationships. They must also register their marriage with the appropriate authority and obtain a marriage certificate .
- Marriage in India is not only a legal and social contract, but also a religious and cultural ceremony that involves various rituals, customs, and traditions. Some of the common elements of a wedding in India are the exchange of vows, the tying of the mangalsutra (a sacred necklace), the application of sindoor (a red powder) on the forehead of the bride, the seven rounds around the sacred fire, and the blessings of the elders.
