### The Mahabharata

- The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, the other being the Ramayana .
- It is a long and complex narrative that tells the story of the struggle for the throne of Hastinapura between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas   .
- The epic consists of 18 books, or parvas, and contains about 74,000 verses, as well as prose sections and appendices.
- The Mahabharata is not only a historical and literary work, but also a source of information on the development of Hinduism, dharma (moral law), and philosophy.
- The most famous part of the Mahabharata is the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between the Pandava prince Arjuna and the god Krishna, who acts as his charioteer and guide in the Kurukshetra War.
- The Mahabharata also contains many other stories and teachings, such as the legend of Savitri and Satyavan, the story of Nala and Damayanti, the code of conduct for kings, the description of the four stages of life, and the doctrine of karma and reincarnation .
- The Mahabharata is regarded by Hindus as both a historical account of their ancestors and a sacred scripture that reveals the truth of the cosmos and the human condition.
