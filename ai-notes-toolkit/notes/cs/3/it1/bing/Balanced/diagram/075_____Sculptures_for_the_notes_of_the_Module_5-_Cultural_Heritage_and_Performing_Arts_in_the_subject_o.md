### Sculptures

Sculptures are three-dimensional art forms that depict the human or animal forms, or abstract shapes, using materials such as stone, metal, wood, clay, or ivory. Sculptures are one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in the Indian subcontinent, dating back to the Indus Valley civilization (3300–1700 BCE) and continuing to the present day. Sculptures have been used to represent the religious, cultural, and aesthetic values of the Indian people, as well as to commemorate historical events, rulers, and heroes.

Some of the main features and characteristics of Indian sculptures are:

- They are often integrated with the architecture of temples, monuments, caves, or stupas, creating a harmonious and dynamic relationship between the structure and the sculpture.
- They are influenced by various religious traditions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam, and Christianity, and reflect the diversity and syncretism of the Indian culture.
- They are rich in symbolism, iconography, and mythology, depicting the various aspects and attributes of the gods, goddesses, and other divine or semi-divine beings, as well as the stories and teachings of the scriptures and legends.
- They are stylistically diverse, varying according to the region, period, and school of art, such as the Mauryan, Gandhara, Gupta, Chola, Hoysala, Mughal, Rajput, and modern styles.
- They are expressive and dynamic, conveying the emotions, gestures, and movements of the figures, as well as the concepts of beauty, grace, power, and transcendence.

Some of the famous examples of Indian sculptures are:

- The Dancing Girl, a small bronze figurine from the Indus Valley civilization, depicting a young woman in a confident and naturalistic pose .
- The Ashoka Pillar, a stone column erected by Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, bearing the edicts of his dharma (law) and the emblem of the four lions, which is now the national symbol of India.
- The Buddha Statue, a sandstone sculpture from Sarnath, Uttar Pradesh, dating from the 5th century CE, showing the Buddha in the Dharmachakra Mudra (gesture of turning the wheel of law), which marks his first sermon after enlightenment.
- The Nataraj, a bronze sculpture from the Chola dynasty, dating from the 10th century CE, depicting Shiva as the lord of dance, performing the cosmic dance of creation and destruction .
- The Khajuraho Temples, a group of Hindu and Jain temples in Madhya Pradesh, built between the 10th and 12th centuries CE, famous for their erotic sculptures that celebrate the human sexuality and the divine union .
