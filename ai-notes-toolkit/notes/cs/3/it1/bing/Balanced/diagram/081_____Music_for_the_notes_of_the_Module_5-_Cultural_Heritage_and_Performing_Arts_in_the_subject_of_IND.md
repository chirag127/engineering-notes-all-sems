### Music

Music is an integral part of Indian culture and society. It reflects the diversity and richness of India's heritage, traditions, religions, languages and regions. Music has been used for various purposes, such as expressing devotion, celebrating festivals, communicating emotions, enhancing social bonding, and creating aesthetic pleasure. Music in India can be broadly classified into two categories: classical and folk.

- Classical music is based on a complex system of ragas (melodic modes) and talas (rhythmic cycles) that have been developed over centuries by various schools and traditions. Classical music is performed by trained musicians who follow strict rules and conventions. Classical music can be further divided into two main styles: Hindustani (North Indian) and Carnatic (South Indian). Hindustani music is influenced by Persian, Arabic and Islamic elements, while Carnatic music is more rooted in the ancient Vedic and Sanskrit traditions. Both styles have vocal and instrumental forms, and use a variety of instruments such as sitar, sarod, tabla, veena, mridangam, flute, violin and harmonium.
- Folk music is the music of the common people, reflecting their regional, ethnic, linguistic and religious identities. Folk music is more flexible and spontaneous than classical music, and often incorporates elements of popular culture, such as film songs, modern instruments and social issues. Folk music is performed in various contexts, such as weddings, fairs, festivals, rituals, dances and dramas. Folk music can be further divided into many genres, such as bhajan (devotional song), qawwali (Sufi song), bhangra (Punjabi dance music), lavani (Maharashtrian dance music), baul (Bengali mystic song), dandiya (Gujarati stick dance music), and ghazal (poetic song).

Music in India has a strong connection with religion and spirituality. Music is considered a form of worship, a way of attaining salvation, and a means of transcending the mundane world. Music is also used to invoke the presence and blessings of various gods and goddesses, saints and gurus, and to express one's love and devotion to them. Music is also a medium of social and cultural expression, reflecting the values, beliefs, customs and history of different communities and groups. Music is also a source of entertainment, education and inspiration, enriching the lives of millions of people in India and around the world.
