### Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people who lived in the Indus Valley or Harappan civilization, which flourished from about 2500 to 1500 BCE.
- Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indo-Aryan peoples who entered northwestern India from Persia around 1500 BCE and composed the sacred texts known as the Vedas .

#### Pre-Vedic Religion

- The Pre-Vedic religion was characterized by the worship of both male and female aspects of nature, such as trees, animals, rivers, mountains, and the sun and the moon.
- The Pre-Vedic religion also had a concept of a supreme deity, who was represented by a horned figure, possibly a proto-Shiva, and a mother goddess, possibly a proto-Parvati.
- The Pre-Vedic religion also practiced ritual bathing, fire worship, animal sacrifice, and the use of seals and amulets for protection and identification.

#### Vedic Religion

- The Vedic religion was characterized by the worship of many gods, such as Indra, Varuna, Agni, Surya, and Rudra, who were associated with natural phenomena, such as thunder, rain, fire, sun, and storms .
- The Vedic religion also had a concept of a universal order, called rita, which was maintained by the gods and the performance of sacrifices by the priests, called brahmins.
- The Vedic religion also practiced hymn singing, fire worship, animal sacrifice, and the use of sacred formulas, called mantras, for invoking the gods and obtaining their favors .
