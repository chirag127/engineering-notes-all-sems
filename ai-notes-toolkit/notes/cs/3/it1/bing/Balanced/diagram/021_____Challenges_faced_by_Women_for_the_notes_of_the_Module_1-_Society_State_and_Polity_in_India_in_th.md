### Challenges faced by Women for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Women in India have made significant progress in terms of education, employment, political participation and legal rights, but they still face many challenges and barriers that limit their full potential and empowerment.
- Some of the major challenges faced by women in India are:

  - Gender discrimination and stereotypes: Women are often seen as inferior, subordinate, dependent and weak by the patriarchal society and culture. They are expected to conform to traditional gender roles and norms, such as being obedient, submissive, domestic and nurturing. They are also subjected to various forms of discrimination and harassment in the family, workplace, public spaces and institutions .
  - Violence and abuse: Women are vulnerable to various forms of violence and abuse, such as domestic violence, sexual violence, dowry-related violence, honour killings, female foeticide, female infanticide, trafficking, forced prostitution and child marriage. These acts of violence violate their human rights and dignity, and have serious physical, psychological and social consequences  .
  - Economic and social exclusion: Women face many barriers and constraints in accessing and controlling economic and social resources, such as land, property, credit, education, health, employment, income and decision-making. They are often denied equal opportunities and fair wages, and face discrimination and exploitation in the labour market. They also bear a disproportionate burden of unpaid domestic and care work, which limits their time and mobility .
  - Political underrepresentation: Women are underrepresented and marginalized in the political sphere, both as voters and as leaders. They face many obstacles and challenges in participating in the electoral process, such as lack of awareness, education, information, confidence, security and support. They also face discrimination and resistance from the male-dominated political parties and institutions, which often ignore or undermine their issues and interests .

- To overcome these challenges, women in India need to be empowered and supported by various stakeholders, such as the government, civil society, media, education, family and community. They need to be given equal rights, opportunities, voice and representation in all spheres of life. They also need to be educated, aware, organized and mobilized to challenge and change the existing structures and systems that oppress and exploit them.
