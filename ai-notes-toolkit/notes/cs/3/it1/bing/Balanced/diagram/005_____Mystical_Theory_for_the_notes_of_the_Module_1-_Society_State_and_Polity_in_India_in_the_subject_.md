### Mystical Theory for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Mystical theory is one of the theories that attempts to explain the origin of the state in ancient India.
- According to this theory, the state is a divine institution that is established by God or his representative on earth.
- The king is the embodiment of God or his chosen one, who rules by divine right and grace.
- The subjects owe absolute obedience and loyalty to the king, who is the protector and benefactor of the people.
- The king is also the upholder of dharma, the cosmic order and moral law, which he enforces through his administration and justice.
- The mystical theory is based on the belief that the state is a sacred and eternal entity, which transcends human history and politics.
- The theory is supported by various religious texts and traditions, such as the Vedas, the Puranas, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, and the Manusmriti.
- The theory also reflects the influence of the Brahmanical ideology, which legitimized the caste system and the supremacy of the Brahmins over the other varnas.
- The theory has been criticized for being authoritarian, hierarchical, and oppressive, as it denies the rights and freedoms of the people and the role of human agency in the formation and development of the state.
