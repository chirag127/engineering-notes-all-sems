### Coins

Coins are metal objects that are used as a medium of exchange or as a symbol of value. Coins have been used in India since ancient times, and they reflect the cultural, political, and economic aspects of Indian history. Here are some points to note about the coinage of India:

- The earliest coins of India were **punch-marked coins**, which were made of silver or copper and had symbols or marks punched on them. These coins were used from the 6th century BCE to the 1st century CE by various kingdoms and republics in northern India, such as the Mahajanapadas, the Nandas, the Mauryas, and the Sungas.    
- The first coins with inscriptions or legends were issued by the **Indo-Greeks**, who ruled parts of northwestern India from the 2nd century BCE to the 1st century CE. These coins were made of silver or gold and had Greek and Indian scripts and images of deities, kings, and animals. Some of the famous Indo-Greek rulers who issued coins were Menander, Apollodotus, and Philoxenus.   
- The **Sakas**, **Parthians**, and **Kushans** were foreign invaders who conquered parts of northern and western India from the 1st century BCE to the 3rd century CE. They also issued coins with inscriptions and images, influenced by Greek, Persian, and Indian styles. The Sakas and Parthians used silver and copper coins, while the Kushans used gold, silver, and copper coins. Some of the notable rulers who issued coins were Azes, Gondophares, Kanishka, and Huvishka.   
- The **Guptas** were the most powerful dynasty of northern India from the 4th to the 6th century CE. They issued coins of gold, silver, and copper, with inscriptions in Sanskrit and images of kings, queens, gods, goddesses, and animals. The Gupta coins are considered to be the finest examples of Indian art and numismatics. Some of the famous Gupta rulers who issued coins were Chandragupta I, Samudragupta, Chandragupta II, and Kumaragupta.   
- The **Pallavas**, **Chalukyas**, **Rashtrakutas**, and **Cholas** were the major dynasties of southern India from the 6th to the 13th century CE. They issued coins of gold, silver, and copper, with inscriptions in Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Sanskrit, and images of kings, gods, goddesses, and symbols. The coins of these dynasties show the regional and cultural diversity of southern India. Some of the prominent rulers who issued coins were Narasimhavarman I, Pulakeshin II, Amoghavarsha, and Rajaraja I.   
- The **Delhi Sultanate** was the first Islamic state in India, which ruled from the 13th to the 16th century CE. The sultans issued coins of gold, silver, and copper, with inscriptions in Arabic and Persian, and images of kings, names of caliphs, religious symbols, and mint names. The coins of the Delhi Sultanate show the influence of Islamic culture and politics on Indian coinage. Some of the notable sultans who issued coins were Iltutmish, Alauddin Khalji, Muhammad bin Tughlaq, and Sikandar Lodi.
