### Telugu Literature

- Telugu literature is the body of works written in the Telugu language, one of the classical languages of India. It has a rich and diverse history that spans over a thousand years.
- Telugu literature can be broadly divided into four periods: Ancient, Medieval, Modern and Contemporary.
- Ancient Telugu literature (11th century BCE to 14th century CE) includes the earliest known Telugu inscriptions, the Telugu translations of Sanskrit epics and puranas, and the original works of poets such as Nannaya, Tikkana and Errana, who are collectively known as Kavitrayam (the trinity of poets).
- Medieval Telugu literature (14th century to 19th century CE) is marked by the emergence of various literary genres, such as prabandha (narrative poetry), champu (mixed prose and verse), yakshagana (musical drama), kavya (courtly poetry), padya (metrical poetry) and gadya (prose). Some of the prominent writers of this period are Srinatha, Potana, Allasani Peddana, Pingali Surana, Tenali Ramakrishna and Krishnadevaraya.
- Modern Telugu literature (19th century to mid-20th century CE) is influenced by the social and political changes brought by the British colonial rule, the nationalist movement, the reformist movements and the Western literary trends. It witnessed the development of novel, short story, essay, biography, autobiography, criticism, journalism and other forms of prose and poetry. Some of the notable writers of this period are Gurajada Apparao, Kandukuri Veeresalingam, Viswanatha Satyanarayana, Sri Sri, Devulapalli Krishnasastri and Jashuva.
- Contemporary Telugu literature (mid-20th century CE to present) reflects the diverse and dynamic aspects of the Telugu society and culture in the post-independence era. It explores the themes of identity, gender, caste, class, religion, politics, environment, globalization and modernity. Some of the prominent writers of this period are C. Narayana Reddy, Gunturu Seshendra Sarma, Varavara Rao, Volga, K. Siva Reddy and Yendluri Sudhakar.
