### Varnāshrama System

- The Varnāshrama system is a concept of social and spiritual organization in Hinduism that consists of four varnas (classes) and four ashramas (stages of life).
- The four varnas are:
  - Brahmanas: the priests, teachers, and intellectuals who perform religious rituals and study the Vedas.
  - Kshatriyas: the warriors, rulers, and administrators who protect the society and uphold justice.
  - Vaishyas: the merchants, farmers, and artisans who produce and trade goods and services.
  - Sudras: the laborers, servants, and workers who perform manual tasks and serve the other varnas.
- The four ashramas are:
  - Brahmacharya: the student stage of life, in which one learns the scriptures, values, and skills under a guru (teacher).
  - Grihastha: the householder stage of life, in which one marries, raises a family, and contributes to the society.
  - Vanaprastha: the forest dweller stage of life, in which one retires from worldly duties, renounces material attachments, and pursues spiritual goals.
  - Sannyasa: the renunciate stage of life, in which one renounces all social and personal ties, and dedicates oneself to the supreme reality.
- The Varnāshrama system is based on the idea that each person has a natural tendency and ability for a particular type of work and a corresponding duty (dharma) to perform it.
- The Varnāshrama system is also meant to provide a framework for the spiritual progress of an individual, by enabling one to fulfill one's material and moral obligations, and gradually detach oneself from worldly desires and attachments.
- The Varnāshrama system is not rigid or fixed, but flexible and dynamic, allowing for mobility and change according to one's qualifications, actions, and circumstances.
- The Varnāshrama system is not a system of oppression or discrimination, but a system of harmony and cooperation, respecting the diversity and interdependence of human society.
