### Agriculture in India

- Agriculture is one of the major sectors of the Indian economy, contributing about 16% of the GDP and employing about 58% of the population .
- India has a diverse agro-climatic zone, ranging from tropical to temperate, and produces a variety of crops, such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugarcane, cotton, jute, etc.
- India is the world's largest producer of many fresh fruits, such as banana, mango, guava, papaya, lemon, and vegetables, such as chickpea, okra, and milk, major spices, such as chili pepper, ginger, fibrous crops, such as jute, staples, such as millets, and castor oilseed .
- India is also the world's largest cattle herd (buffaloes), and the largest producer of milk, with an estimated production of 208 million tonnes in 2020-21 .
- India's agriculture sector is facing many challenges, such as low productivity, declining soil fertility, water scarcity, climate change, market volatility, and policy constraints.
- India's agriculture sector is also undergoing a transformation, with the adoption of new technologies, such as biotechnology, precision farming, digital agriculture, etc., and the emergence of new markets, such as organic farming, contract farming, agro-processing, etc.
- India's agriculture sector is also influenced by the socio-cultural and political factors, such as the role of women, caste, religion, land reforms, farmers' movements, etc.
- India's agriculture sector is also guided by the national and state policies, such as the National Agriculture Policy, the National Food Security Act, the Minimum Support Price, the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana, etc.
- India's agriculture sector is also linked to the global trade and development, such as the World Trade Organization, the Food and Agriculture Organization, the International Fund for Agricultural Development, etc.
