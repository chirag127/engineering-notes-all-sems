### Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century

- The socio-religious reform movement of 19th century was a wave of reforming activities that aimed to modernize and rationalize the Indian society and religion in the light of western education and liberal ideas .
- The movement started in Bengal and soon spread to other parts of India, such as Bombay, Madras, Punjab, and Hyderabad .
- The movement was influenced by various factors, such as the colonial rule, the Christian missionaries, the orientalists, the Indian Renaissance, the social evils, and the nationalist aspirations .
- The movement had different types and dimensions, such as the Brahmo Samaj, the Arya Samaj, the Prarthana Samaj, the Aligarh movement, the Ramakrishna mission, the Theosophical Society, the Singh Sabha movement, the Satyashodhak Samaj, and the reform movements among the lower castes and the tribal communities   .
- The movement had various impacts and outcomes, such as the emancipation and education of women, the abolition of social evils, the promotion of social equality and justice, the revival and reinterpretation of Hindu scriptures, the emergence of new religious sects and cults, the development of a modern and scientific outlook, and the growth of Indian nationalism and identity  .
