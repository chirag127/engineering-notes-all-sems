### Jainism

Jainism is one of the oldest religions of India, with a history of more than 2500 years. It is based on the teachings of 24 spiritual leaders called tirthankaras, who attained enlightenment and liberation from the cycle of rebirth. The last and most famous of these tirthankaras was Mahavira, who lived in the 6th century BCE.

Some of the main features of Jainism are:

- It teaches a path of spiritual purity and enlightenment through disciplined nonviolence (ahimsa) to all living beings, including animals and plants.
- It believes in the existence of an eternal soul (jiva) in every living being, which can be liberated from the bondage of karma (the consequences of actions) by following the Jain ethical principles.
- It rejects the idea of a supreme creator, ruler, or judge of the universe, and considers the universe to be self-sustaining and independent of any external power.
- It emphasizes the importance of rationality, logic, and evidence, and rejects the authority of scriptures or revelation. It advocates a pluralistic and relativistic approach to truth, known as anekantavada (non-absolutism).
- It promotes a simple and ascetic lifestyle, avoiding attachment to worldly possessions and pleasures. It encourages renunciation of worldly life for those who seek spiritual perfection, and prescribes strict rules of conduct for monks and nuns.
- It has a rich and diverse tradition of art, literature, philosophy, and culture, which has influenced and been influenced by other Indian religions, especially Hinduism and Buddhism.
