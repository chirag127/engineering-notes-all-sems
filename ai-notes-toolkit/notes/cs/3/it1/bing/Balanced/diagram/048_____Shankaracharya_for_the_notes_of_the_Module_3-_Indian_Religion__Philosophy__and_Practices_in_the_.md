### Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya is a title given to the heads of monastic institutions (mathas) in the Advaita Vedanta tradition of Hinduism. The title means "teacher of the way of Shankara", referring to the 8th-century philosopher and theologian Adi Shankara, who is considered the founder of the Advaita Vedanta school.
- Adi Shankara was a Vedic scholar and teacher who traveled across India to propagate his teachings of non-dualism (advaita), which assert that the ultimate reality is one and the same as the individual self (atman). He wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, as well as original works such as the Vivekachudamani and the Upadesasahasri.
- Adi Shankara also established four mathas in the four corners of India, each associated with one of the four Vedas. These are the Sringeri Sharada Peetham in the south, the Dwarka Sharada Peetham in the west, the Jyotirmath in the north, and the Govardhana Matha in the east. The heads of these mathas are called Shankaracharyas and are regarded as the spiritual successors of Adi Shankara.
- Shankaracharya is a revered and influential figure in Hinduism, especially in the Advaita Vedanta tradition. His teachings have influenced various schools of thought, such as the Neo-Vedanta of Swami Vivekananda and the Ramakrishna Mission, the Hindu reform movements of the 19th and 20th centuries, and the modern Hindu revivalism of the 21st century.
