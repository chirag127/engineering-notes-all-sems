### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Vedanta. They are also known as shad-darshana, meaning six views or perspectives. Each system has its own founder, texts, concepts and methods of inquiry. They represent different aspects of the intellectual and spiritual traditions of Hinduism.

- Nyaya: The school of logic and epistemology, founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE. It deals with the sources and validity of knowledge, the nature of reality, the means of inference, the categories of existence, the relation between words and objects, and the methods of debate and argumentation. The main texts of Nyaya are the Nyaya Sutras and the Nyaya Bhashya.
- Vaisheshika: The school of atomism and naturalism, founded by Kanada in the 6th century BCE. It deals with the classification and analysis of the physical world, the nature and properties of substances, the atomic theory of matter, the laws of causation, the concept of dharma (moral order), and the liberation of the soul. The main text of Vaisheshika is the Vaisheshika Sutras.
- Samkhya: The school of dualism and realism, founded by Kapila in the 6th century BCE. It deals with the distinction and interaction between purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter), the evolution of the manifest world from the unmanifest prakriti, the enumeration and description of the 25 tattvas (principles or categories) of existence, the theory of gunas (qualities or modes) of prakriti, the concept of samsara (cycle of rebirth) and the liberation of the soul. The main text of Samkhya is the Samkhya Karika.
- Yoga: The school of discipline and practice, founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE. It deals with the methods and stages of meditation, the control and purification of the mind and body, the ethical and moral codes of conduct, the eight limbs of yoga (yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana and samadhi), the concept of chakras (energy centers) and kundalini (serpent power), the attainment of siddhis (supernatural powers) and the liberation of the soul. The main text of Yoga is the Yoga Sutras.
- Purva Mimamsa: The school of ritual and exegesis, founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE. It deals with the interpretation and application of the Vedas, the authority and validity of the Vedic scriptures, the nature and purpose of the sacrificial rites, the concept of apurva (unseen merit), the theory of karma (action and its consequences), the concept of dharma (duty and righteousness), and the liberation of the soul. The main text of Purva Mimamsa is the Mimamsa Sutras.
- Vedanta: The school of metaphysics and monism, founded by Badarayana in the 2nd century BCE. It deals with the essence and meaning of the Upanishads, the identity and relation between Brahman (the supreme reality) and Atman (the individual self), the concept of maya (illusion or ignorance), the doctrine of advaita (non-duality), the concept of moksha (liberation or enlightenment), and the various schools and sub-schools of Vedanta. The main text of Vedanta is the Brahma Sutras.
