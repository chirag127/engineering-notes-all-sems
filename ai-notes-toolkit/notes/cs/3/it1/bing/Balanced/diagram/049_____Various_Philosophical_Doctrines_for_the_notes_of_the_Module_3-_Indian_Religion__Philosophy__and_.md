### Various Philosophical Doctrines in Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- Indian philosophy is the systems of thought and reflection that were developed by the civilizations of the Indian subcontinent. It includes both orthodox (astika) and unorthodox (nastika) schools of philosophy.
- The orthodox schools accept the authority of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism, while the unorthodox schools reject or question the Vedas.
- The six orthodox schools are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva-Mimamsa, and Vedanta. The unorthodox schools include Buddhism, Jainism, Charvaka, and Ajivika.
- The main philosophical problems that Indian thought has been concerned with are the nature of the world (cosmology), the nature of reality (metaphysics), logic, the nature of knowledge (epistemology), ethics, and the philosophy of religion.
- Some of the common themes and concepts that are shared by various schools of Indian philosophy are the following:
  - The doctrine of karma, which is the universal law of cause and effect that governs the actions and consequences of all beings .
  - The doctrine of samsara, which is the continuous cycle of birth, death, and rebirth that all beings are subject to until they attain liberation (moksha or nirvana) .
  - The doctrine of atman, which is the belief in the existence of a soul or a self that is distinct from the body and the mind .
  - The doctrine of brahman, which is the belief in the ultimate reality or the supreme spirit that is the source and essence of all things.
  - The doctrine of dharma, which is the moral law or the duty that each individual has to follow according to their social role, stage of life, and personal nature.
  - The doctrine of maya, which is the illusion or the ignorance that prevents one from seeing the true nature of reality and causes one to be attached to the worldly phenomena.
- The following table summarizes some of the main features and differences of the six orthodox schools of Indian philosophy:

| School | Founder | Main Texts | Main Concepts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nyaya | Gautama | Nyaya Sutras | Logic, inference, valid sources of knowledge, categories of reality, theory of causation, proofs for the existence of God |
| Vaisheshika | Kanada | Vaisheshika Sutras | Atomism, pluralism, classification of substances, qualities, actions, universals, inherence, and negation |
| Samkhya | Kapila | Samkhya Karika | Dualism, distinction between purusha (spirit) and prakriti (matter), evolution of the 24 principles of prakriti, three gunas (qualities) of prakriti, liberation through discrimination |
| Yoga | Patanjali | Yoga Sutras | Discipline, meditation, eight limbs of yoga, control of the mind, psychic powers, union with purusha |
| Purva-Mimamsa | Jaimini | Mimamsa Sutras | Exegesis, interpretation of the Vedas, performance of rituals, theory of verbal cognition, theory of dharma |
| Vedanta | Badarayana | Brahma Sutras | Monism, identity of atman and brahman, interpretation of the Upanishads, various schools of Vedanta such as Advaita, Vishishtadvaita, and Dvaita |
