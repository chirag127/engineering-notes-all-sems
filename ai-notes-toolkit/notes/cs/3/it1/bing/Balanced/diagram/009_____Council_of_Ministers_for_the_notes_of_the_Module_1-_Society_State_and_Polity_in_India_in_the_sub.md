### Council of Ministers

- The Council of Ministers is the principal executive organ of the Government of India, which functions as the senior decision making body of the executive branch.
- It is chaired by the Prime Minister, who is the leader of the executive of the Government of India.
- It consists of the heads of each of the executive government ministries, who are further classified as Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, and Ministers of State (Independent Charge) .
- The President of India appoints the members of the Council of Ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister.
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, the lower house of the Parliament of India.
- The Council of Ministers exercises executive authority in India and implements the policies and laws enacted by the Parliament.
- The Council of Ministers also advises the President on various matters, such as appointment of judges, pardons, emergency powers, etc.
- The Council of Ministers meets regularly to discuss and decide on important issues of national and international importance.
- The Cabinet is the core group of the Council of Ministers, which consists of the most senior and influential ministers.
- The Cabinet is responsible for coordinating the policies and actions of the different ministries and departments.
- The Cabinet also acts as the crisis management group in times of emergency or war.
- The Cabinet is headed by the Prime Minister, who has the power to appoint and remove any minister.
- The Cabinet Secretariat is the administrative arm of the Cabinet, which provides support and assistance to the Prime Minister and the Cabinet.
- The Cabinet Secretariat is headed by the Cabinet Secretary, who is the senior most civil servant in the country.
- The Cabinet Secretary acts as the chief coordinator of the central government and the link between the Prime Minister and the civil service.
