### Purusārtha

- Purusārtha is a Sanskrit word that means "object (ive) of men" or "human endeavour".
- It is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life.
- The four purusārthas are:
  - Dharma: the moral duty, righteousness, law, ethics, and social order. It is the principle of living in harmony with the cosmic order and one's own nature. Dharma guides the other three purusārthas and ensures that they are pursued in a virtuous manner. 
  - Artha: the material wealth, prosperity, security, and power. It is the means of fulfilling one's basic needs and desires, as well as achieving social and political success. Artha should be pursued in accordance with dharma, and not at the expense of the other purusārthas. 
  - Kāma: the sensual pleasure, enjoyment, love, and happiness. It is the aesthetic and emotional aspect of life, as well as the expression of one's creativity and talents. Kāma should be pursued in moderation and balance, and not in conflict with dharma and artha. 
  - Mokṣa: the liberation, freedom, and release from the cycle of birth and death (saṃsāra). It is the ultimate goal of life, and the realization of one's true self (ātman) and identity with the supreme reality (brahman). Mokṣa can be attained through various paths, such as knowledge (jñāna), devotion (bhakti), action (karma), or yoga. 
- The purusārthas are the blueprint for human fulfillment and the basis of Hindu ethics. They offer a way of evaluating one's life and making good decisions. They also reflect the diversity and complexity of human nature and aspirations.
