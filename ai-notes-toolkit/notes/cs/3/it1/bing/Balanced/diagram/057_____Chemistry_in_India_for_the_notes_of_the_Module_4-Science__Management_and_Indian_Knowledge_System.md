### Chemistry in India

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the study of matter, its composition, structure, properties, and reactions. Chemistry has a wide scope and applications in various fields such as medicine, agriculture, industry, environment, and education. Chemistry is also an integral part of the Indian culture and heritage, as it has a long and rich history of development and achievements in this domain.

Some of the main points to note about chemistry in India are:

- Chemistry in India can be traced back to the ancient times, when the Indus Valley or Harappan civilization (3rd millennium BCE) used various chemical techniques such as metallurgy, dyeing, tanning, pottery, and cosmetics.
- The Vedic period (2nd millennium BCE to 6th century BCE) witnessed the emergence of Ayurveda, the traditional system of medicine that used a variety of minerals, metals, plants, and animal products for healing and wellness. Ayurveda also played an important role in the development of chemistry, as it involved the preparation, purification, and analysis of various substances.
- The post-Vedic period (6th century BCE to 5th century CE) saw the growth of various schools of philosophy, such as Samkhya, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, and Buddhism, that contributed to the understanding of the nature and classification of matter, atoms, and elements. The Vaisheshika school, founded by Kanada, was especially influential in developing the atomic theory of matter.
- The classical period (5th century CE to 12th century CE) was marked by the flourishing of alchemy, the older form of chemistry, that aimed at the transmutation of metals, the discovery of the elixir of life, and the attainment of spiritual enlightenment. Alchemy was also known as Rasayan Shastra, and it involved the use of various devices, instruments, and processes for chemical experiments. Some of the prominent alchemists of this period were Nagarjuna, Nityanatha, and Vagbhata .
- The medieval period (12th century CE to 18th century CE) witnessed the invasion and influence of foreign cultures, such as the Arabs, Turks, Mongols, and Europeans, on the Indian chemistry. This period also saw the development of various branches of chemistry, such as mineralogy, metallurgy, pharmacy, dyeing, distillation, and perfumery. Some of the notable chemists of this period were Al-Biruni, Abul Fazl, Akbar, Jahangir, and Aurangzeb.
- The modern period (18th century CE to present) is characterized by the advancement and innovation of chemistry in India, in response to the challenges and opportunities posed by the colonial rule, the independence movement, and the post-independence era. This period also saw the emergence of various institutions, organizations, and personalities that shaped the chemistry education, research, and industry in India. Some of the eminent chemists of this period were Prafulla Chandra Ray, C.V. Raman, S.S. Bhatnagar, M.N. Saha, and C.N.R. Rao .

Chemistry in India is a rich and diverse field that reflects the cultural and scientific heritage of the country. It has made significant contributions to the development and progress of the nation and the world. Chemistry in India is also a dynamic and evolving field that continues to explore new frontiers and challenges in the 21st century.
