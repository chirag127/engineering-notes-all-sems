### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times

- Pre-colonial India refers to the period before the British occupation of India, which started in 1757 with the Battle of Plassey and ended in 1947 with the Indian independence.
- Pre-colonial India was characterized by a rich cultural and religious diversity, with various kingdoms, empires, and dynasties ruling over different regions and peoples.
- Some of the major political entities that dominated pre-colonial India were:
  - The Mauryan Empire (321-185 BCE), which was the first pan-Indian empire and one of the largest and most powerful in the ancient world. It was founded by Chandragupta Maurya and reached its peak under Ashoka, who promoted Buddhism and a policy of non-violence.
  - The Gupta Empire (320-550 CE), which is considered the golden age of Indian culture, art, science, and literature. It was founded by Chandragupta I and expanded by his successors, especially Samudragupta and Chandragupta II. It fostered the development of Hinduism, Sanskrit, and the decimal system.
  - The Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526 CE), which was a Muslim dynasty that ruled over most of northern India and parts of southern India. It was founded by Qutb al-Din Aibak, a former slave of Muhammad of Ghor, and reached its zenith under Alauddin Khalji and Muhammad bin Tughluq, who implemented reforms and expanded the territory. It faced challenges from the Mongols, the Vijayanagara Empire, and the Bhakti movement.
  - The Vijayanagara Empire (1336-1646 CE), which was a Hindu kingdom that ruled over most of southern India and resisted the Muslim invasions. It was founded by Harihara and Bukka, two brothers who rebelled against the Delhi Sultanate, and reached its glory under Krishnadevaraya, who patronized art, literature, and architecture. It collapsed after the Battle of Talikota in 1565, when it was defeated by a coalition of Muslim states.
  - The Mughal Empire (1526-1857 CE), which was the last and most influential pre-colonial empire in India. It was founded by Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, who defeated the Delhi Sultanate at the First Battle of Panipat. It reached its peak under Akbar, who consolidated the empire and promoted religious tolerance and cultural synthesis. It declined under Aurangzeb, who imposed a strict version of Islam and faced rebellions from the Sikhs, the Marathas, and the Rajputs.
- Pre-colonial India was also a major economic and cultural force in the world, with a high degree of urbanization, trade, and innovation. Some of the achievements and contributions of pre-colonial India were:
  - The development of the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1900 BCE), which was one of the earliest and most advanced urban civilizations in the world. It had a sophisticated system of writing, sanitation, irrigation, and trade. It is also known for its artistic and religious expressions, such as the seals, the figurines, and the Great Bath.
  - The invention of zero, the decimal system, and the concept of infinity by Indian mathematicians, such as Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, and Bhaskara. These concepts were crucial for the advancement of algebra, calculus, and astronomy, and were transmitted to the Islamic world and Europe through trade and cultural exchange.
  - The creation of the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, and other sacred texts that form the basis of Hinduism, one of the oldest and most diverse religions in the world. These texts contain philosophical, ethical, and spiritual insights, as well as stories, poems, and hymns that reflect the Indian culture and worldview.
