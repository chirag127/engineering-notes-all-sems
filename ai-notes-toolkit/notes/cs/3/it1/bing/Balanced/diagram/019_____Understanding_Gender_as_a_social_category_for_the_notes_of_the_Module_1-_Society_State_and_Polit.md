### Understanding Gender as a Social Category

- Gender is a social category that refers to the social and cultural meanings, expectations, and practices associated with being male or female in a given society .
- Gender is not the same as sex, which is a biological classification based on body parts. Sex is assigned at birth, while gender is learned and performed through socialization.
- Gender is a social construct, meaning that it is not natural or fixed, but rather shaped by historical, cultural, and political contexts .
- Gender is also a social relation, meaning that it affects how people interact with each other, how they access and distribute resources, and how they exercise power and authority .
- Gender is not a binary or a dichotomy, but rather a spectrum or a continuum that allows for diversity and variation in gender identities and expressions .
- Gender is influenced by and intersects with other social categories, such as class, race, ethnicity, religion, age, sexuality, and disability . These intersections create multiple and complex forms of oppression and privilege for different groups of people.
- Gender is a dynamic and contested category, meaning that it is constantly changing and evolving, and that it is subject to different interpretations and challenges by various actors and movements .
- Gender is a relevant and important category for understanding society, state, and polity in India, as it shapes the experiences, opportunities, and challenges of different groups of people in various domains, such as education, health, work, family, religion, politics, and culture.
