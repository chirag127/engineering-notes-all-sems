### Dance

Dance is an ancient and celebrated cultural tradition in India. It is a form of expression, communication, devotion, and entertainment. Dance in India comprises numerous styles of dances, generally classified as classical or folk. As with other aspects of Indian culture, different forms of dances originated in different parts of India, developed according to the local traditions and also imbibed elements from other parts of the country.

- Classical dances are those that have a codified set of rules and a well-defined repertoire based on ancient texts and scriptures. They are usually performed on stage or in temples and have a spiritual or religious significance. There are six forms of classical dances recognised by India on a national level. They are Bharatnatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Manipuri, Kuchipudi, and Odissi . Each of these dances has its own distinctive style, technique, costume, music, and theme. Classical dances are considered as a discipline and a way to devote oneself to God through art.

- Folk dances are those that are performed by the common people of a region or community, often during festivals, celebrations, or social gatherings. They reflect the diversity and richness of the Indian culture and heritage. Folk dances are usually spontaneous, energetic, and expressive. They do not have a fixed set of rules or a standardised repertoire, but rather vary according to the local customs, traditions, and influences. Some of the popular folk dances of India are Bhangra, Garba, Dandiya, Bihu, Ghoomar, Lavani, and Chhau.

- In traditional Indian culture, the function of dance was to give symbolic expression to religious ideas. The figure of Lord Shiva as Nataraja represents the creation and destruction of the cosmic cycle. The popular image of Shiva in the form of Nataraja clearly shows the popularity of dance form on the Indian people. Dance and song also feature heavily in Indian cinema (so-called “Bollywood” films), which have a global appeal and influence. Dance in India is a living and evolving art form that continues to adapt and innovate with changing times and contexts.
