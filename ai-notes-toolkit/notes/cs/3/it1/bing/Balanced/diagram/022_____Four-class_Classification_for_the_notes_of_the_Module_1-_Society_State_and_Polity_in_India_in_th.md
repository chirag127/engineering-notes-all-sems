### Four-class Classification for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- The four-class classification, or varna system, is a social hierarchy based on the division of labor and the qualities of individuals in ancient India .
- The four classes are: Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers) .
- The varna system is derived from the Purusha Sukta, a hymn in the Rig Veda, which describes the creation of the cosmic being Purusha and the four classes from his body parts .
- The varna system is also related to the concept of purushartha, or the four goals of human life: dharma (duty and morality), artha (wealth and power), kama (pleasure and desire), and moksha (liberation and salvation) .
- The varna system is not rigid or fixed, but flexible and dynamic, depending on the context and the individual's actions and qualities .
- The varna system is also influenced by the concept of ashrama, or the four stages of life: brahmacharya (student), grihastha (householder), vanaprastha (retiree), and sannyasa (renunciant) .
- The varna system is criticized by some scholars and reformers as a source of inequality, oppression, and discrimination, especially for the lower classes and the outcastes .
- The varna system is challenged by some alternative movements and ideologies, such as Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Dalitism, which reject the authority of the Vedas and the Brahmins and advocate for social justice and human dignity .
