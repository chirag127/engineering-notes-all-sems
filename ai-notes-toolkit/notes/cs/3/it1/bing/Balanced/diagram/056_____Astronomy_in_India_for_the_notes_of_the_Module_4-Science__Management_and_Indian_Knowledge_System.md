### Astronomy in India

- Astronomy has a long history in India, stretching from pre-historic to modern times.
- Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilisation or earlier, when ancient Indians observed the movements of celestial bodies and developed calendars, star maps and astronomical instruments .
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism.
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, Sanskrit translations of Greek texts disseminated from the 2nd century.
- In the first millennium CE, Indian astronomers developed sophisticated mathematical and observational techniques, such as the use of trigonometry, algebra, geometry, calculus, and the concept of zero.
- Some of the prominent Indian astronomers of this period include Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Lalla, and Sripati.
- Indian astronomy reached its peak in the Siddhantic era, from the 5th to the 15th century CE, when various schools of astronomy emerged, such as the Kerala school, the Aryabhatiya school, and the Surya Siddhanta school.
- These schools produced many astronomical treatises, known as Siddhantas, that covered topics such as planetary motions, eclipses, cosmology, timekeeping, and astrology.
- Indian astronomy also influenced other cultures, such as the Islamic world, China, Tibet, Southeast Asia, and Europe .
- Some of the notable contributions of Indian astronomy to the world include the heliocentric model of the solar system, the concept of precession of the equinoxes, the discovery of the lunar and solar nodes, the calculation of the value of pi, the use of decimal fractions, and the invention of the sine and cosine functions .
- In the modern era, Indian astronomy has made significant advances in the fields of astrophysics, cosmology, space exploration, and satellite technology.
- Some of the current institutions and projects related to Indian astronomy are the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA), the Aryabhatta Research Institute of Observational Sciences (ARIES), the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope (GMRT), the Indian Astronomical Observatory (IAO), and the Thirty Meter Telescope (TMT).
