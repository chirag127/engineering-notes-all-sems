# Geography

- Geography is the study of the physical features of the earth and its atmosphere, and of human activity as it affects and is affected by these features.
- Geography has two main branches: physical geography and human geography.
- Physical geography deals with the natural phenomena of the earth, such as climate, landforms, water, soil, vegetation, and wildlife.
- Human geography deals with the spatial aspects of human society and culture, such as population, settlement, urbanization, agriculture, industry, trade, and geopolitics.
- Geography is an interdisciplinary subject that draws on various fields of knowledge, such as history, economics, sociology, anthropology, and environmental science.
- Geography is also a part of Indian knowledge system, which has a long and rich tradition of studying the earth and its environment from various perspectives, such as cosmology, astronomy, astrology, cartography, and ecology.
- Some of the ancient Indian texts that deal with geography are the Vedas, the Puranas, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Arthashastra, and the Brihat Samhita.
- Some of the notable Indian geographers and scholars who contributed to the development of geography are Varahamihira, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Al-Biruni, Amir Khusrau, and Raja Todar Mal.
- Some of the concepts and themes that are unique to Indian geography are the idea of panchabhuta (five elements), the concept of chaturvarna (four social classes), the notion of desh-kala-patra (place-time-context), the classification of regions into jambudvipa, bharatvarsha, and sapta sindhu, and the depiction of sacred geography in the form of mandalas, yantras, and shilpas.
