### Bhakti Movement

- The Bhakti Movement was a socio-religious movement that emerged in medieval India (8th-18th centuries CE) and aimed at reforming Hinduism by emphasizing devotion (bhakti) to a personal god or goddess as the way to salvation.
- The Bhakti Movement challenged the dominance of the Brahmin priests, the ritualistic and hierarchical aspects of Hinduism, and the caste system. It also promoted the idea of equality and universal brotherhood among all people, regardless of their caste, gender, or status.
- The Bhakti Movement was influenced by various sources, such as the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, the Puranas, the Tamil devotional literature (Alvars and Nayanars), the Sufi mystics of Islam, and the teachings of various saints and poets who spread the message of bhakti across India.
- The Bhakti Movement gave rise to many sects and schools of thought, such as the Vaishnavism, Shaivism, Shaktism, Sikhism, and the Sant Mat. Each of these sects had their own doctrines, practices, and traditions, but they all shared the common belief in the power of love and devotion to a supreme being.
- The Bhakti Movement also had a significant impact on the development of Indian literature, art, music, and culture. Many bhakti poets and saints composed hymns, songs, poems, and stories in various regional languages, such as Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, and Punjabi. These works expressed the emotions, experiences, and visions of the devotees and inspired millions of people. Some of the famous bhakti poets and saints are Ramanuja, Basavanna, Shankara, Ramananda, Kabir, Nanak, Mirabai, Tulsidas, Surdas, Chaitanya, Vallabha, Narsi Mehta, Tukaram, Ramdas, and Guru Gobind Singh.
