### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and meaningful way, usually in the form of tables, charts, graphs, or dashboards.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and deriving insights from data, usually with the help of statistical or analytical tools and techniques.
- Reporting and analysis are both important aspects of big data, but they have different purposes and outcomes.
- Reporting aims to answer questions such as what, when, where, and how much, while analysis aims to answer questions such as why, how, and what if.
- Reporting provides a snapshot of the current or past state of data, while analysis provides a deeper understanding of the underlying patterns, trends, and relationships in data.
- Reporting is often used for monitoring, tracking, and summarizing data, while analysis is often used for discovering, explaining, and predicting data.
- Reporting is usually based on predefined metrics, dimensions, and filters, while analysis is usually based on ad hoc queries, hypotheses, and experiments.
- Reporting is more suitable for structured and standardized data, while analysis is more suitable for unstructured and diverse data.
- Reporting is more focused on accuracy, consistency, and reliability, while analysis is more focused on creativity, curiosity, and exploration.
