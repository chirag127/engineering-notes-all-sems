## Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data

- Big data is a term that refers to the large, complex, and diverse datasets that are generated from various sources at high speed and volume.
- Big data challenges the traditional methods of data processing, storage, analysis, and visualization, and requires new technologies and techniques to handle it effectively and efficiently.
- Big data has many characteristics, such as volume, velocity, variety, veracity, value, and variability, that make it different from conventional data.
- Big data can be classified into three types: structured, semi-structured, and unstructured, depending on the format and organization of the data.
- Big data can be used for various purposes, such as business intelligence, decision making, innovation, social good, and scientific discovery, among others.
- Big data can also pose many challenges and risks, such as privacy, security, ethics, governance, quality, and scalability, that need to be addressed and managed carefully.
- Big data analytics is the process of applying various methods and tools to extract meaningful insights and knowledge from big data.
- Big data analytics can be performed using different approaches, such as descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, prescriptive, and cognitive analytics, depending on the goal and scope of the analysis.
- Big data analytics can also be categorized into four types: batch, real-time, interactive, and streaming analytics, depending on the latency and frequency of the data processing.
- Big data analytics can benefit from various technologies and frameworks, such as cloud computing, parallel and distributed computing, NoSQL databases, Hadoop, Spark, and machine learning, among others.
