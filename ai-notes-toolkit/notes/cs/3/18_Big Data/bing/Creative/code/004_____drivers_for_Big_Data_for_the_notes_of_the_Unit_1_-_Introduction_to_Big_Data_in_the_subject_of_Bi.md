### Drivers for Big Data

Big data is the term used to describe the large and complex datasets that are generated from various sources and require advanced techniques and technologies to store, process, and analyze. Big data has become a valuable asset for many organizations and industries that want to gain insights, improve decision making, and create new products and services. However, big data also poses many challenges and opportunities that require understanding the drivers and motivations behind its adoption and use.

Some of the main drivers for big data are:

- **The digitization of society**: The widespread use of digital devices, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, and sensors, has enabled the creation and collection of massive amounts of data from various domains, such as e-commerce, health, education, entertainment, and social media. These data can capture the behaviors, preferences, opinions, and interactions of individuals and groups, as well as the characteristics and dynamics of physical and virtual environments. The digitization of society has also increased the demand for data-driven solutions and applications that can enhance the quality of life, productivity, and innovation.
- **The drop in technology costs**: The rapid advancement and innovation in information and communication technologies (ICT) have made it possible and affordable to store, process, and analyze big data. The development of cloud computing, distributed computing, parallel computing, and in-memory computing has enabled the scalability, elasticity, and efficiency of big data platforms and systems. The improvement of data storage, transmission, and compression technologies has reduced the cost and complexity of handling big data. The emergence of new data sources, such as social media, web logs, sensors, and satellites, has increased the variety and velocity of big data.
- **Connectivity through cloud computing**: Cloud computing is a model of delivering ICT services over the internet, such as data storage, processing, and analysis, without requiring the users to own or manage the physical infrastructure. Cloud computing provides several benefits for big data, such as on-demand access, pay-per-use pricing, scalability, elasticity, and reliability. Cloud computing also enables the integration and interoperability of different data sources and platforms, as well as the collaboration and sharing of data and insights among different users and organizations.
- **Increased knowledge about data science**: Data science is the interdisciplinary field that combines methods, techniques, and tools from statistics, mathematics, computer science, and domain knowledge to extract value from big data. Data science has become a popular and important discipline that can help organizations and industries to solve complex problems, discover new patterns, and generate new knowledge from big data. Data science also requires a new set of skills and competencies, such as data literacy, data visualization, data mining, machine learning, and artificial intelligence, that are in high demand in the market.
- **Social media applications**: Social media is a term that refers to the online platforms and applications that enable users to create, share, and consume content, such as text, images, videos, and audio, as well as to communicate and interact with other users. Social media has become a major source and driver of big data, as it generates huge volumes of data that reflect the opinions, sentiments, emotions, and behaviors of millions of users. Social media also provides a platform for data analysis and dissemination, as users can access, filter, and comment on the data and insights generated by other users or organizations.
- **The rise of Internet-of-Things (IoT)**: IoT is a term that describes the network of physical objects, such as devices, vehicles, appliances, and machines, that are embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity, and can collect, exchange, and act on data. IoT has the potential to transform various domains and industries, such as manufacturing, transportation, energy, health, and agriculture, by enabling the automation, optimization, and monitoring of processes, systems, and environments. IoT also generates massive amounts of data that require advanced techniques and technologies to store, process, and analyze.
