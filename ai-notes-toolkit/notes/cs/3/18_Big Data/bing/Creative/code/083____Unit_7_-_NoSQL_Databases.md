## Unit 7 - NoSQL Databases

- NoSQL databases are non-relational databases that store and retrieve data in different ways than relational databases.
- NoSQL databases are designed to handle large volumes of unstructured, semi-structured, or dynamic data, such as social media posts, documents, or sensor data.
- NoSQL databases are also suitable for applications that require high scalability, availability, or performance, such as web applications, real-time analytics, or distributed systems.
- NoSQL databases can be classified into four main types: key-value, document, column, and graph databases.

### Key-value databases
- Key-value databases store data as pairs of keys and values, where the key is a unique identifier and the value can be any type of data.
- Key-value databases are simple, fast, and scalable, but they do not support complex queries or relationships between data.
- Examples of key-value databases are Redis, Memcached, and DynamoDB.

### Document databases
- Document databases store data as documents, which are collections of fields and values, similar to JSON or XML files.
- Document databases are flexible, expressive, and schema-less, meaning that the structure of the documents can vary and evolve over time.
- Document databases support rich queries and indexing, but they may have challenges with consistency and joins.
- Examples of document databases are MongoDB, CouchDB, and Elasticsearch.

### Column databases
- Column databases store data as columns, which are collections of values that share the same attribute or property.
- Column databases are efficient, compact, and distributed, meaning that they can store large amounts of data across multiple nodes or clusters.
- Column databases support fast aggregation and analysis, but they may have limitations with complex queries and transactions.
- Examples of column databases are Cassandra, HBase, and Bigtable.

### Graph databases
- Graph databases store data as nodes and edges, which represent entities and relationships, respectively.
- Graph databases are powerful, expressive, and schema-full, meaning that they have a predefined structure and constraints for the data.
- Graph databases support complex queries and traversals, but they may have challenges with scalability and performance.
- Examples of graph databases are Neo4j, OrientDB, and Titan.
