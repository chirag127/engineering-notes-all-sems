# Big Data Analytics

## Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data

### What is Big Data?

- Big data is a term that describes the large volume, variety, and velocity of data that is generated by various sources and requires new methods and technologies to store, process, and analyze it.
- Big data can be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured, and can come from different domains, such as web, mobile, email, social media, sensors, and machines.
- Big data can be measured by the 5 Vs: volume, variety, velocity, veracity, and value.

### What is Big Data Analytics?

- Big data analytics is the process of applying advanced analytical techniques, such as predictive models, statistical algorithms, and what-if analysis, to big data sets to derive insights and support decision making.
- Big data analytics can help organizations to discover hidden patterns, trends, correlations, and anomalies in their data, and to gain a competitive advantage, improve customer satisfaction, reduce costs, and increase revenue.
- Big data analytics can be performed using various tools and applications, such as cloud computing, distributed computing, machine learning, artificial intelligence, and data visualization.

### Why is Big Data Analytics Important?

- Big data analytics is important because it can help organizations to leverage the potential of their data and to transform it into actionable information and knowledge.
- Big data analytics can help organizations to:

  - Understand their customers better and provide personalized and customized services and products.
  - Enhance their operational efficiency and productivity by optimizing their processes and resources.
  - Innovate and create new products, services, and business models based on data-driven insights.
  - Mitigate risks and prevent fraud by detecting and preventing anomalies and threats.
  - Comply with regulations and standards by ensuring data quality and security.
