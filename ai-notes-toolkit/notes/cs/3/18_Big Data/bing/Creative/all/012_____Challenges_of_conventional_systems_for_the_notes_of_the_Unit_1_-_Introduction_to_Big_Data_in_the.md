# Challenges of conventional systems for big data

Big data is the storage and analysis of large and complex data sets that can be both structured or unstructured. They are so large that it is not possible to work on them with traditional analytical tools. Conventional systems are built on top of the relational data model, which assumes that data is organized in tables with predefined schemas and relationships. Conventional systems face several challenges when dealing with big data, such as:

- **It cannot work on unstructured data efficiently**: Unstructured data refers to data that does not have a fixed format or structure, such as text, images, audio, video, social media posts, etc. Unstructured data accounts for a large proportion of big data, and it is difficult to process and analyze using conventional systems that rely on SQL queries and fixed schemas .
- **It is batch oriented and we need to wait for nightly ETL (extract, transform and load) and transformation jobs to complete before the required insight is obtained**: ETL is a process of extracting data from various sources, transforming it into a common format, and loading it into a data warehouse for analysis. Conventional systems use ETL to perform batch processing of data, which means that data is processed in large chunks at regular intervals, such as daily or weekly. This leads to latency and delays in obtaining insights from big data, which may require real-time or near-real-time processing .
- **Parallelism in a traditional analytics system is achieved through costly hardware like MPP (Massively Parallel Processing) systems**: MPP systems are specialized hardware that can perform parallel processing of data by distributing the workload among multiple processors or nodes. Conventional systems use MPP systems to scale up their performance and handle large volumes of data. However, MPP systems are expensive and complex to maintain, and they may not be able to cope with the variety and velocity of big data .
- **The high-velocity nature of big data is not kind to traditional storage systems, which can be a root cause of project failure or unrealized ROI**: Big data is generated at a high speed and frequency, such as from sensors, web logs, social media, etc. Traditional storage systems are not designed to handle such high-velocity data, and they may suffer from performance degradation, data loss, or data inconsistency. Big data requires modern storage solutions that can provide high availability, scalability, reliability, and fault tolerance.
- **Disparate data sources, lack of standards, and outdated technology make it challenging to understand how brands operate**: Big data comes from various sources, such as internal databases, external websites, third-party applications, etc. Each source may have different formats, structures, quality, and semantics. Conventional systems may not be able to integrate and harmonize these disparate data sources, and they may lack the standards and tools to ensure data quality and consistency. Moreover, conventional systems may use outdated technology that is not compatible with the latest trends and innovations in big data.
- **Big data has advancements toward information management and handling challenges like big data analysis, knowledge diversity, knowledge extraction and reducing, integration and cleansing and several other tools for analysis and mining**: Big data analysis refers to the process of applying various techniques and methods to discover patterns, trends, and insights from big data. Knowledge diversity refers to the variety and heterogeneity of data sources, types, and domains. Knowledge extraction and reducing refer to the process of extracting and summarizing relevant information from big data. Integration and cleansing refer to the process of combining and improving the quality of data from different sources. Conventional systems may not be able to support these advanced processes and tools, and they may require new skills and expertise to handle big data.
