### Challenges of Conventional Systems

1. **Scalability**: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the large volumes of data that are generated by modern applications. As the volume of data grows, these systems become increasingly difficult to scale, leading to performance issues and increased costs.

2. **Data Variety**: Conventional systems are designed to handle structured data, but modern applications generate a wide variety of data, including unstructured and semi-structured data. This data variety can be difficult for conventional systems to handle, leading to data silos and a lack of integration.

3. **Real-time Processing**: Conventional systems are not designed for real-time processing of large volumes of data. This can be a challenge for applications that require real-time insights, such as fraud detection or recommendation engines.

4. **Cost**: Conventional systems can be expensive to scale and maintain, particularly as the volume of data grows. This can be a challenge for organizations that need to store and process large volumes of data.

5. **Complexity**: Conventional systems can be complex to set up and maintain, requiring specialized skills and knowledge. This can be a challenge for organizations that do not have the resources to manage these systems in-house.

6. **Data Quality**: Conventional systems may not have the necessary mechanisms to ensure data quality, leading to issues with data accuracy and completeness. This can be a challenge for applications that rely on high-quality data for decision-making.

7. **Security**: Conventional systems may not have the necessary security measures in place to protect sensitive data, leading to potential data breaches and loss of trust. This can be a challenge for organizations that need to ensure the security and privacy of their data.

These are some of the challenges that conventional systems face when dealing with big data. To overcome these challenges, organizations are turning to big data technologies that are designed to handle the volume, variety, velocity, and veracity of big data.
