### History of Big Data Innovation

- Big Data is a term that refers to the collection, storage, analysis, and use of large and complex datasets that exceed the capabilities of traditional data processing systems.
- The history of Big Data can be traced back to the origins of data science and computing, as well as the emergence of new technologies and applications that generate and consume massive amounts of data.
- Some of the key milestones in the history of Big Data are:

  - **Before 1980**: Companies used mainframe computers to store and analyse data. Two key technologies were critical to the first formations of data centers as we think of them today: relational databases and magnetic tape storage .
  - **1980s**: The concept of data warehousing was introduced, which involved the integration of data from multiple sources into a central repository for analysis and reporting. The term "business intelligence" was also coined, which referred to the use of data to support decision making.
  - **1990s**: The World Wide Web and the Internet became widely accessible, leading to an exponential growth of data from various sources, such as web pages, emails, social media, online transactions, etc. The term "Big Data" was first used by John Mashey, a computer scientist at Silicon Graphics, to describe the challenges and opportunities of data-intensive computing .
  - **2000s**: The emergence of cloud computing, distributed computing, and open source software enabled the development of new platforms and tools for Big Data processing, such as Hadoop, MapReduce, Spark, NoSQL, etc. The term "data science" was popularized, which referred to the interdisciplinary field of extracting insights from Big Data using various methods, such as statistics, machine learning, visualization, etc.
  - **2010s**: The advancement of artificial intelligence, especially deep learning, enabled the creation of new applications and services that leverage Big Data, such as natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, recommendation systems, etc. The term "data-driven" was used to describe the culture and strategy of using data as a key asset and source of competitive advantage for organizations.
  - **2020s**: The evolution of Big Data continues, with new trends and challenges, such as edge computing, Internet of Things, quantum computing, data privacy, data ethics, data literacy, etc. The term "data ecosystem" was used to describe the complex and dynamic network of data producers, consumers, intermediaries, and regulators that interact and exchange value through data.
