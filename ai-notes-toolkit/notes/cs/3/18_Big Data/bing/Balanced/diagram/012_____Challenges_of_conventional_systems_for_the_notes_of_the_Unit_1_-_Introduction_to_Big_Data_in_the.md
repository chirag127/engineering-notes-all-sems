### Challenges of conventional systems

Conventional systems are the traditional data processing and storage systems that are based on the relational data model and use structured query language (SQL) for data manipulation. They are designed to handle structured data that can fit into predefined tables and schemas. However, they face several challenges when dealing with big data, which is the term used to describe large, complex, and diverse data sets that are generated at high velocity and require new methods and technologies for analysis and management. Some of the challenges of conventional systems for big data are:

- **Scalability**: Conventional systems have limited scalability, which means they cannot handle the increasing volume and variety of data without compromising on performance and cost. They rely on expensive hardware such as massively parallel processing (MPP) systems or clusters of servers to achieve parallelism and distribute the workload. However, these systems are not flexible and efficient enough to cope with the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of big data, which may require frequent changes in the data schema, format, and quality. Moreover, conventional systems have difficulty in scaling out, which means adding more nodes or machines to the system, as they have to maintain consistency and coordination among the nodes, which increases the complexity and overhead of the system.

- **Unstructured data**: Conventional systems are not designed to work with unstructured data, which is the data that does not have a predefined format or structure, such as text, images, audio, video, social media posts, web logs, sensor data, etc. Unstructured data accounts for a large proportion of big data and contains valuable information and insights that can be used for various purposes such as sentiment analysis, recommendation systems, fraud detection, etc. However, conventional systems cannot process unstructured data efficiently, as they require the data to be transformed and loaded into a relational database, which is a time-consuming and resource-intensive process. Moreover, conventional systems cannot handle the semantic and contextual aspects of unstructured data, which require advanced techniques such as natural language processing, computer vision, machine learning, etc.

- **Batch processing**: Conventional systems are batch-oriented, which means they process data in batches or groups, rather than in real-time or near-real-time. This is suitable for data that is static and does not change frequently, such as historical records, financial transactions, etc. However, big data is dynamic and volatile, which means it changes rapidly and may lose its value or relevance over time. For example, social media data, stock market data, sensor data, etc. are generated continuously and require timely analysis and action. Batch processing cannot meet the demands of big data, as it introduces latency and delays in the data processing and analysis, which may result in missed opportunities, inaccurate decisions, or outdated information.

- **Security and privacy**: Conventional systems have well-established security and privacy mechanisms, such as encryption, authentication, authorization, access control, etc. However, these mechanisms may not be sufficient or applicable for big data, which poses new and complex challenges for data security and privacy. For example, big data may contain sensitive or personal information that needs to be protected from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure. However, big data may also be distributed across multiple locations, platforms, or organizations, which increases the risk of data breaches, leaks, or theft. Moreover, big data may require sharing or exchanging data with external parties, such as customers, partners, or regulators, which may raise legal or ethical issues regarding data ownership, consent, or compliance. Therefore, conventional systems need to adopt new and innovative methods and technologies to ensure the security and privacy of big data, such as differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, blockchain, etc.
