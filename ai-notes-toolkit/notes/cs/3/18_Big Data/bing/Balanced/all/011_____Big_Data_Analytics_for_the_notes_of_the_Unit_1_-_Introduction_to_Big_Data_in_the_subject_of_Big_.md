# Big Data Analytics

## Definition

- Big data analytics is the process of collecting, examining, and analyzing large amounts of data to discover market trends, insights, and patterns that can help companies make better business decisions.
- Big data analytics involves complex applications with elements such as predictive models, statistical algorithms and what-if analysis powered by analytics systems.
- Big data analytics uses advanced analytic techniques against very large, diverse big data sets that include structured, semi-structured and unstructured data, from different sources, and in different sizes from terabytes to zettabytes.

## Benefits

- Big data analytics can help companies improve customer satisfaction, operational efficiency, product innovation, risk management, and competitive advantage.
- Big data analytics can enable faster and more accurate decision making, as well as new insights that were previously hidden or inaccessible.
- Big data analytics can also reduce costs, increase revenues, and create new business opportunities.

## Challenges

- Big data analytics requires specialized skills, tools, and infrastructure to handle the volume, velocity, and variety of data.
- Big data analytics also poses ethical, legal, and social issues, such as data privacy, security, ownership, and governance.
- Big data analytics may encounter technical, organizational, and cultural barriers, such as data quality, integration, scalability, and adoption.
