# Analysis vs Reporting

- Analysis and reporting are two different processes that are often used in data-driven decision making.
- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and understandable format, such as tables, charts, dashboards, etc. Reporting aims to answer questions like what, when, where, and how much.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and explaining data, using statistical, mathematical, or logical methods. Analysis aims to answer questions like why, how, and what if.
- Reporting is usually done on a regular basis, such as daily, weekly, or monthly, to monitor the performance of a business or a project. Analysis is usually done on an ad hoc basis, when there is a specific problem or opportunity to investigate.
- Reporting is often based on predefined metrics and dimensions, such as revenue, sales, customers, etc. Analysis is often based on custom queries and calculations, such as correlations, trends, outliers, etc.
- Reporting is often consumed by a wide audience, such as managers, stakeholders, or customers. Analysis is often consumed by a narrow audience, such as analysts, experts, or decision makers.
- Reporting and analysis are complementary processes that can provide valuable insights and recommendations for data-driven decision making. Reporting can provide the facts and figures, while analysis can provide the context and meaning. Reporting can highlight the areas of interest, while analysis can explore the causes and effects. Reporting can show the current state, while analysis can predict the future state.
