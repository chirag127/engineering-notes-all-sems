### Challenges of conventional systems for big data

- Conventional systems are based on the relational data model, which assumes that data is structured, consistent, and can be queried using SQL. However, big data is often unstructured, heterogeneous, and requires different types of analysis, such as text mining, sentiment analysis, or machine learning .
- Conventional systems are designed for batch processing, which means that data is collected, transformed, and loaded (ETL) into a data warehouse before it can be analyzed. This process can take hours or days, which is not suitable for big data applications that require real-time or near-real-time insights  .
- Conventional systems rely on expensive hardware, such as massively parallel processing (MPP) systems, to achieve parallelism and scalability. However, big data is too large and complex to be handled by a single system, and requires distributed and fault-tolerant architectures, such as Hadoop or Spark, that can leverage commodity hardware and cloud computing  .
- Conventional systems face challenges in data quality, integration, and governance, as big data comes from various sources, formats, and domains. Data quality issues, such as missing values, outliers, or inconsistencies, can affect the accuracy and reliability of the analysis. Data integration issues, such as schema mismatch, semantic heterogeneity, or entity resolution, can hinder the interoperability and usability of the data. Data governance issues, such as privacy, security, or ethics, can pose legal and social risks for the data owners and users .
