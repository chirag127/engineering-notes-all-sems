## Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data

1. What is Big Data?
- Big Data refers to extremely large data sets that may be analyzed computationally to reveal patterns, trends, and associations, especially relating to human behavior and interactions.
- The data is too large or complex for traditional data processing application software to adequately deal with.

2. The 3 Vs of Big Data
- Volume: Refers to the huge amount of data.
- Velocity: Refers to the speed at which the data is generated and processed.
- Variety: Refers to the different types and formats of data like structured, unstructured, images, videos, etc.

3. Importance of Big Data
- Big Data enables companies to make better business decisions and strategic moves by analyzing customer behavior and market trends.
- It enables improved understanding and optimization of processes.
- It leads to the development of new products and services.
- It enables personalized and customized user experiences.

4. Challenges with Big Data
- Capture: The volume and velocity of data makes it difficult to capture and store the data.
- Search: It is difficult to search, share and retrieve data due to the volume and variety.
- Analysis: The diversity and complexity of data makes analysis difficult.
- Visualization: It is challenging to visualize and comprehend Big Data.
- Security: There are data security and privacy concerns with Big Data.

5. Technologies for Big Data
- Hadoop: An open-source software framework for distributed storage and processing of Big Data sets.
- NoSQL databases: Non-relational databases for scalable storage and retrieval of unstructured data.
- Data mining and machine learning algorithms: For analysis of large data sets to discover patterns and insights.
- Cloud computing: Provides the infrastructure to store and process Big Data.
