## Unit 7 - NoSQL Databases

NoSQL databases are non-relational databases that provide flexible data models and horizontal scalability. They are designed to handle large volumes of unstructured data and offer high-performance and high-availability.

Here are some important points to understand about NoSQL databases:

- NoSQL databases use a variety of data models, such as document-oriented, key-value, column-family, and graph-based.
- The most popular NoSQL databases include MongoDB, Cassandra, Redis, and Neo4j.
- NoSQL databases offer high scalability and can handle large amounts of data without compromising performance.
- NoSQL databases are often used in big data and real-time applications, such as social media, e-commerce, and IoT.
- NoSQL databases do not have a fixed schema, allowing for more flexibility in data modeling and easier data storage and retrieval.
- NoSQL databases typically use eventual consistency, which means that data updates may not be immediately reflected across all nodes in the database.
- NoSQL databases are often used in conjunction with traditional relational databases to handle specific types of data or workloads.

In conclusion, NoSQL databases are a valuable tool for handling large volumes of unstructured data in real-time applications. They offer flexible data models, horizontal scalability, and high-performance capabilities, making them a popular choice for modern data-driven businesses.
