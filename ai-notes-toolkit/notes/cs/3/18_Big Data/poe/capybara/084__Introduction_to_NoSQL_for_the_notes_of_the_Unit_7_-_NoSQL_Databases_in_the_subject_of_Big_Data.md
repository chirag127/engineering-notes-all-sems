### Introduction to NoSQL for the notes of the Unit 7 - NoSQL Databases in the subject of Big Data.

NoSQL databases have become increasingly popular over the past decade due to their flexibility and scalability. In this unit, we will cover the basics of NoSQL databases, their advantages, and how they differ from traditional relational databases.

Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- NoSQL stands for "not only SQL." It is a term used to describe a variety of non-relational databases that store and manage data differently than traditional relational databases.
- In a NoSQL database, data is stored in a flexible and non-tabular format, such as key-value pairs or document-based structures. This allows for more efficient scaling and handling of large amounts of data.
- NoSQL databases are often used in Big Data applications where the volume, velocity, and variety of data make it difficult to manage with traditional relational databases.
- NoSQL databases are also known for their ability to handle unstructured data, such as social media feeds, log files, and sensor data.
- Some popular NoSQL databases include MongoDB, Cassandra, and Redis.
- NoSQL databases use different types of data models, such as document-based, key-value, column-family, and graph-based models. Each model is optimized for specific use cases and data structures.
- NoSQL databases typically offer flexible schema designs, which means that data can be added or changed without needing to modify the entire database schema.
- NoSQL databases also often provide horizontal scaling, which allows for adding additional nodes to a cluster to increase capacity and performance.

In conclusion, NoSQL databases offer a flexible and scalable alternative to traditional relational databases. Understanding the basics of NoSQL databases is an essential skill for anyone working with Big Data applications.
