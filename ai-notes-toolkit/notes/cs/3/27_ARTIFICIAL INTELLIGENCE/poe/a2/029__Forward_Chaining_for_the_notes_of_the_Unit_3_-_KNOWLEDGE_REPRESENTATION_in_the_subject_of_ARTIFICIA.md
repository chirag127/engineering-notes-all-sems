### Forward Chaining for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS.

1. Forward chaining is a inference method that starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data and conclusions.
2. It is also known as data-driven reasoning or bottom-up reasoning.
3. The process begins with a set of initial facts. Rules are then applied to these facts to infer new facts. These inferred facts are then used to infer more facts, and so on, until a goal is reached or no more inferences can be made.
4. The major advantage of forward chaining is that it can find all possible conclusions that can be drawn from a set of facts. However, a major disadvantage is that the process may continue indefinitely if there are loops in the inference rules or if no goal is specified.
5. Forward chaining is commonly used in expert systems and other artificial intelligence applications such as automated diagnosis and planning systems. It is opposite to backward chaining which starts with a goal and works backwards to find the facts that can achieve it.
