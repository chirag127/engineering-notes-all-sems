### Argumentation among Agents

- Software agents may have conflicting goals and thus may get into argumentation or negotiation with each other to resolve conflicts and reach an agreement.
- For example, in e-commerce systems, buyer and seller agents may argue on price, features, quality, etc. to finalize a deal.
- In such situations, agents exchange proposals and counter-proposals backed by arguments and justifications.
- The key steps in agent argumentation are:

1. Recognize a conflict or a disagreement
2. Exchange arguments to support each agent's position
3. Evaluate arguments and counter-arguments to determine the strength of positions
4. Revise positions or make concessions if a position is weakened
5. Repeat steps 2-4 until agreements/resolutions are reached or negotiations break down

- The arguments put forth by agents may be based on their beliefs, goals, domain knowledge, ethics, etc. The outcome of argumentation depends on how arguments are evaluated and how agents respond to arguments.
- Automated agents can employ logical reasoning and machine learning techniques to generate, evaluate, and respond to arguments to achieve acceptable resolutions. However, modeling argumentation in complex domains with imperfect information is challenging.
