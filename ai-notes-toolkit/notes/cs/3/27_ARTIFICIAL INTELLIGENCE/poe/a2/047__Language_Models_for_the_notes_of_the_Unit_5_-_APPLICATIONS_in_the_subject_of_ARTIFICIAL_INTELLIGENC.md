### Language Models

- Language models are statistical models that assign probabilities to sequences of words. They are used to predict the next word in a sentence or to complete a sentence.
- The most common types of language models are:
- N-gram models: These models consider the previous N words to predict the next word. For example, a 2-gram (or bigram) model considers the previous 2 words, a 3-gram (or trigram) model considers the previous 3 words, and so on.
- Recurrent neural networks: These models use neural networks that operate on a sequence of inputs to predict the next word in a sequence.
- Transformer models: These are a type of neural network architecture that processes input data in the form of a sequence and is often used for language modeling tasks due to its strong performance.

The key benefits of language models are:

- Predicting the next word in a sentence which enables autocompletion and spell check.
- Generating new sentences that mimic the style of a document or author. This can be used to generate article summaries or even fiction text.
- Translation by using a source language model to translate a sentence into a mid-level representation and then using a target language model to generate the translation.
- Applications such as speech recognition and machine translation utilize language models to identify the most likely transcription or translation based on the sequence of inputs.
