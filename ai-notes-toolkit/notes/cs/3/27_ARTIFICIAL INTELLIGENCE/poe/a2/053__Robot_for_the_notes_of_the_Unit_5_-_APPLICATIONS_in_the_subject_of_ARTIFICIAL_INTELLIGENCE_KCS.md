### Robot for the notes of the Unit 5 - APPLICATIONS in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

1. Robotics is the design and construction of robots, which are physical agents that can perform a complex series of actions automatically. Robots are widely used in many industries today, including manufacturing, space exploration, and the military.

2. The key components of a robot are:

- Sensors: detects the environment and senses the state of the robot. Examples include cameras, ultrasonic rangefinders, touch sensors, etc.
- Controllers: the "brain" of the robot that receives input from sensors, processes the data, and send output to actuators. Often a microprocessor is used to run software for control.
- Actuators: the motors that provide motion and action to the robot. Examples are motors, legs, arms, grippers, etc.

3. There are different types of robots based on their purpose:

- Industrial robots: used in manufacturing for repetitive and precise tasks like assembly, pick and place, welding, painting, etc.
- Mobile robots: can move around in their environment like self-driving cars, autonomous underwater vehicles, Mars rovers, etc. They use sensors and localization algorithms to navigate.
- Social robots: designed to interact with and assist humans. Examples include social companion robots, medical rehabilitation robots, etc. They require additional capabilities like natural language processing, emotion recognition, etc.

4. Challenges in robotics include:

- Limited sensing and perception: robots have limited senses and cannot perceive the world as well as humans. Improving perception is an active area of research.
- Autonomy: it is difficult to make robots fully autonomous and capable of making their own decisions in complex, unpredictable environments. More research is needed in robot planning, learning, and AI.
- adaptability: robots are specialized tools designed for specific tasks. They are not very adaptable to new situations. More work is needed to make robots more flexible and capable of adapting to new environments and tasks.
