### Typical Intelligent Agents

1. Expert Systems: Systems that contain encoded knowledge and inference procedures to solve problems in a specific domain. They are designed to mimic human experts. For example, systems that diagnose diseases, recommend treatments, or identify faults in machines.
2. Fuzzy Systems: Systems that can make decisions based on imprecise or uncertain information. They are designed to mimic human decision making. For example, systems that control air conditioning systems, washing machines, or microwave ovens.
3. Neural Networks: Systems inspired by biological neural networks. They are designed to learn how to perform a task by analyzing large amounts of data. For example, systems that recognize speech, detect objects in images, or predict trends.
4. Genetic Algorithms: Systems that are inspired by biological evolution and natural selection. They are designed to find optimal solutions to complex problems by mimicking processes of evolution. For example, systems that optimize schedules, allocate resources, or design complex structures.
5. Robotics: Systems that can perform physical tasks in the real world. They are designed to exhibit some degree of physical agency or autonomy. For example, systems that assemble cars, deliver items, assist in surgeries, or explore other planets.
