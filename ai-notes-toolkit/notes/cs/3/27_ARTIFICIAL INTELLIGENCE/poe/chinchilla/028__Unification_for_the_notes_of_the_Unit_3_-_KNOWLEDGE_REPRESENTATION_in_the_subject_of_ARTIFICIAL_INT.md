- Unification is the process of finding a substitution that makes two or more expressions identical.
- Two expressions are said to be unifiable if they can be made identical by substituting some variables with constants or other variables.
- Unification is used in many areas of AI, such as natural language processing, expert systems, and automated theorem proving.
- The most common algorithm used for unification is the Robinson's algorithm, which uses a set of rules to find the substitution that makes two expressions identical.
- Unification can also be used to solve equations, find commonalities in different theories, and simplify expressions.
- Unification is a powerful tool in Knowledge Representation, as it allows us to represent knowledge in a structured form and manipulate it using logical rules.
- Unification is also used in other areas of computer science, such as programming languages and compilers.
