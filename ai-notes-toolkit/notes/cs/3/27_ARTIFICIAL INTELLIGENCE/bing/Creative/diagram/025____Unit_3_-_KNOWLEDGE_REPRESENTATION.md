## Unit 3 - Knowledge Representation

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding information about a domain in a form that can be used by an intelligent agent to reason and act.
- Knowledge representation involves two aspects: syntax and semantics.
  - Syntax defines the structure and format of the symbols and expressions used to represent knowledge.
  - Semantics defines the meaning and interpretation of the symbols and expressions.
- Knowledge representation also requires a trade-off between expressiveness and tractability.
  - Expressiveness refers to the ability of a representation to capture complex and diverse aspects of a domain.
  - Tractability refers to the ease and efficiency of performing inference and computation with a representation.
- There are different types of knowledge representation schemes, such as logic, semantic networks, frames, production rules, and ontologies.
  - Logic is a formal system of symbols and rules that can be used to represent and reason about propositions, facts, and relations.
  - Semantic networks are graphs that use nodes to represent concepts and links to represent relations between concepts.
  - Frames are data structures that use slots to store attributes and values of concepts and inheritance to share common properties among concepts.
  - Production rules are conditional statements that specify actions to be performed when certain conditions are met.
  - Ontologies are hierarchical structures that define the concepts, relations, and axioms of a domain.
- Knowledge representation schemes can be evaluated based on criteria such as representation, inference, learning, and communication.
  - Representation refers to how well a scheme can capture the relevant and essential aspects of a domain.
  - Inference refers to how efficiently and effectively a scheme can support reasoning and problem-solving.
  - Learning refers to how easily and accurately a scheme can acquire and update knowledge from data and feedback.
  - Communication refers to how clearly and naturally a scheme can convey and exchange knowledge with humans and other agents.
