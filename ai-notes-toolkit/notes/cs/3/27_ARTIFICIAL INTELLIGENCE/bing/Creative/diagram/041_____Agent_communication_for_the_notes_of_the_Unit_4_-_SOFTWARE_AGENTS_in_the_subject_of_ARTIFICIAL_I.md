### Agent communication

- Agent communication is the process of exchanging information and knowledge between intelligent agents in a multi-agent system.
- Agent communication is based on the use of agent communication languages (ACLs) and protocols that define the syntax, semantics and pragmatics of the messages.
- Agent communication languages are inspired by the speech act theory, which states that utterances are not only expressions of information, but also actions that have effects on the mental states and behaviors of the listeners.
- Some examples of agent communication languages are KQML (Knowledge Query and Manipulation Language), FIPA-ACL (Foundation for Intelligent Physical Agents Agent Communication Language) and KIF (Knowledge Interchange Format).
- Agent communication protocols are rules that specify the sequence and content of the messages exchanged by the agents in a given context or scenario.
- Some examples of agent communication protocols are Contract Net Protocol, Query-Ref Protocol and Request-When Protocol.
- Agent communication enables the agents to coordinate, cooperate and negotiate with each other, as well as to share and acquire knowledge from different sources .
- Agent communication also enhances the interoperability, scalability and flexibility of the multi-agent system.
- Agent communication can be classified into two types: direct and indirect.
  - Direct communication is when the agents send and receive messages directly to and from each other, using a common communication channel.
  - Indirect communication is when the agents communicate through a shared environment, such as a blackboard, a database or a bulletin board.
- Agent communication can also be classified into two modes: synchronous and asynchronous.
  - Synchronous communication is when the agents expect an immediate response from the receiver, and block their execution until they receive it.
  - Asynchronous communication is when the agents do not expect an immediate response from the receiver, and continue their execution after sending the message.
- Agent communication can also be classified into two levels: content and conversation.
  - Content level is the level of the information and knowledge that the agents exchange, such as facts, beliefs, goals, plans, etc.
  - Conversation level is the level of the social and pragmatic aspects of the communication, such as the intention, the context, the protocol, the politeness, etc.
- Agent communication can also be classified into two roles: initiator and participant.
  - Initiator is the agent that initiates the communication by sending the first message.
  - Participant is the agent that responds to the initiator's message, or joins an ongoing communication.
