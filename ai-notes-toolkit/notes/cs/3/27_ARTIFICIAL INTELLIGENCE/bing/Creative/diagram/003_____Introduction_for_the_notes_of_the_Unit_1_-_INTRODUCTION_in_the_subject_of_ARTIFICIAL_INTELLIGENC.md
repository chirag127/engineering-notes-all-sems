### Introduction

- Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that aims to create machines and software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, robotics, etc.
- AI can be classified into two main categories: weak AI and strong AI. Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the AI that is designed to perform a specific task or domain, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or driving a car. Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can do, such as understanding natural language, solving complex problems, or creating original art. Weak AI is currently more prevalent and achievable than strong AI, which is still a hypothetical and elusive goal.
- AI can also be classified into two main approaches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI. Symbolic AI, also known as classical AI or good old-fashioned AI (GOFAI), is the AI that uses symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge, such as logic, ontologies, expert systems, etc. Sub-symbolic AI, also known as connectionist AI or neural AI, is the AI that uses numerical values and mathematical operations to model and learn from data, such as artificial neural networks, deep learning, evolutionary algorithms, etc.
- AI has many applications and benefits in various fields and domains, such as education, health care, entertainment, business, security, etc. AI can also pose some challenges and risks, such as ethical, social, legal, and technical issues, such as bias, privacy, accountability, transparency, safety, etc. Therefore, AI requires careful design, evaluation, and regulation to ensure its positive and responsible use for the benefit of humanity and society.
