### Typical Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is an autonomous entity that acts upon an environment using sensors and actuators to achieve its goals  .
- An intelligent agent may also learn from the environment to improve its performance .
- Examples of intelligent agents are driverless cars, virtual assistants, chatbots, robots, etc  .
- There are different types of intelligent agents based on their degree of rationality, learning ability, and environmental complexity  .
- The main types of intelligent agents are:
  - **Simple reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept (sensory input) and ignore the previous history. They have a fixed set of rules or condition-action pairs that determine their actions. They are suitable for simple and predictable environments .
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept and a model of the world that they maintain internally. They use the model to keep track of the state of the environment and update it based on the percept. They are suitable for partially observable and dynamic environments .
  - **Goal-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, and a goal that they want to achieve. They use the model to predict the outcomes of their actions and choose the ones that bring them closer to the goal. They are suitable for complex and uncertain environments .
  - **Utility-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, a goal, and a utility function that measures how desirable a state is. They use the utility function to evaluate the outcomes of their actions and choose the ones that maximize their expected utility. They are suitable for environments with multiple and conflicting goals .
  - **Learning agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, a goal, a utility function, and a learning component that improves the agent's performance over time. They use the learning component to modify the agent's components (such as the rules, the model, the goal, or the utility function) based on the feedback from the environment. They are suitable for environments that change and require adaptation .
