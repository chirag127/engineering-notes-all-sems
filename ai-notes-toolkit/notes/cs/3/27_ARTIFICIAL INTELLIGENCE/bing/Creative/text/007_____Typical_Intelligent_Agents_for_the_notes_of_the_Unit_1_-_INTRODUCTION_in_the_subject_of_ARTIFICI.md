### Typical Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is an autonomous entity that acts upon an environment using sensors and actuators to achieve its goals .
- An intelligent agent may also learn from the environment and adapt its behavior accordingly.
- An intelligent agent can be described as a software entity that conducts operations in the place of users or programs after sensing the environment.
- Intelligent agents are closely related to agents in economics, cognitive science, ethics, and philosophy of practical reason.
- Intelligent agents are studied in many interdisciplinary fields such as socio-cognitive modeling and computer social simulations.
- Some examples of intelligent agents are driverless cars, virtual assistants, chatbots, robots, and smart home devices .

- There are different types of intelligent agents based on their characteristics, such as :

  - **Simple reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, without considering the history of percepts. They have a fixed set of rules that map the percept to the action. For example, a vacuum cleaner that moves based on the presence of dirt on the floor.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept and a model of the environment that they maintain internally. They use the model to keep track of the state of the environment and update it based on the percept. For example, a driverless car that uses a map of the road and traffic conditions to navigate.
  - **Goal-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the environment, and a goal that they want to achieve. They use the model to predict the outcomes of their actions and choose the one that brings them closer to the goal. For example, a chess-playing program that evaluates the possible moves and selects the one that maximizes the chance of winning.
  - **Utility-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the environment, a goal, and a utility function that measures how desirable a state is. They use the utility function to compare the outcomes of their actions and choose the one that maximizes their utility. For example, a stock-trading program that buys and sells shares based on the expected profit and risk.
  - **Learning agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the environment, a goal, a utility function, and a learning component that improves their performance over time. They use the learning component to update their model, goal, utility function, or action rules based on the feedback from the environment. For example, a virtual assistant that learns from the user's preferences and behavior to provide better service.
