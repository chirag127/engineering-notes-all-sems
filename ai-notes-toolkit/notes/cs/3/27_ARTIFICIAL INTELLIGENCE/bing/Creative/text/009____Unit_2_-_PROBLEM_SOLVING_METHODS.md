## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- Problem solving methods are strategies or techniques that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until finding one that works or eliminates the problem.
  - Algorithm: Following a step-by-step procedure that guarantees a correct solution if followed correctly.
  - Heuristic: Using a rule of thumb or a general guideline that can help narrow down the possible solutions or speed up the search process.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a large or complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved more easily.
  - Backtracking: Retracing the steps or decisions made in a problem solving process to find and correct errors or explore alternative solutions.
  - Analogy: Using a similar or familiar problem or situation to help understand or solve a new or unfamiliar problem.
  - Brainstorming: Generating as many ideas or solutions as possible without judging or evaluating them until later.
  - Means-ends analysis: Comparing the current state of the problem with the desired goal state and identifying the actions or steps that can reduce the difference or gap between them.
  - Hill climbing: Choosing the solution or action that leads to the most improvement or progress towards the goal state at each step of the problem solving process.
  - Working backwards: Starting from the goal state and working backwards to find the initial state or the steps that can lead to the goal.
