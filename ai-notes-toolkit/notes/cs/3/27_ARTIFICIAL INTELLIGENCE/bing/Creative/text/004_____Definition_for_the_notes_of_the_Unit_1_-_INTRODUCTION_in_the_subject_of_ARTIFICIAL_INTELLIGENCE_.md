### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the **ability of a machine** to perform tasks that **require human intelligence** such as learning, reasoning, perception, decision making, and problem solving.    
- AI can be classified into **two main types**: weak AI and strong AI. Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is designed to perform a specific task or function, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or driving a car. Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the hypothetical goal of creating a machine that can **understand and reason** about any domain of knowledge, and **exhibit human-like intelligence and consciousness**.  
- AI can also be categorized based on the **techniques** or **approaches** used to achieve its goals, such as symbolic AI, connectionist AI, evolutionary AI, or hybrid AI. Symbolic AI uses **symbols and rules** to represent and manipulate knowledge, such as logic, expert systems, and knowledge graphs. Connectionist AI uses **artificial neural networks** to learn from data and perform tasks, such as deep learning, natural language processing, and computer vision. Evolutionary AI uses **biological principles** such as natural selection, mutation, and crossover to evolve solutions to problems, such as genetic algorithms, genetic programming, and artificial life. Hybrid AI combines **different techniques** to leverage their strengths and overcome their limitations, such as neuro-symbolic AI, neuro-evolutionary AI, and multi-agent systems.  
- AI can also be distinguished based on the **degree of human involvement** or **supervision** required for its learning and operation, such as supervised learning, unsupervised learning, semi-supervised learning, reinforcement learning, or self-learning. Supervised learning is a type of machine learning where the machine is **trained with labeled data** and learns to predict the output for new inputs. Unsupervised learning is a type of machine learning where the machine is **given unlabeled data** and learns to discover patterns, clusters, or features from the data. Semi-supervised learning is a type of machine learning where the machine is **trained with a mix of labeled and unlabeled data** and learns to improve its performance with less human intervention. Reinforcement learning is a type of machine learning where the machine is **given a goal and feedback** and learns to optimize its actions through trial and error. Self-learning is a type of machine learning where the machine is **able to learn from its own experience** and improve its performance without external guidance.  
- AI has many **applications** and **benefits** across different domains and industries, such as finance, healthcare, education, entertainment, security, agriculture, and manufacturing. AI can help to **enhance productivity, efficiency, accuracy, and innovation** by automating tasks, analyzing data, generating insights, providing recommendations, creating content, and interacting with humans. AI can also help to **address global challenges** such as climate change, poverty, health, and education by providing solutions, predictions, interventions, and awareness.   
- AI also poses some **challenges and risks** that need to be considered and addressed, such as ethical, social, legal, and technical issues. AI can raise **ethical questions** such as fairness, accountability, transparency, privacy, and human dignity, and can have **social impacts** such as unemployment, inequality, discrimination, and polarization. AI can also create **legal challenges** such as liability, regulation, governance, and compliance, and can face **technical limitations** such as scalability, reliability, security, and explainability.   
- AI is a **dynamic and interdisciplinary** field that draws from and contributes to various disciplines, such as computer science, mathematics, statistics, engineering, psychology, neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy, and biology. AI is also a **rapidly evolving and expanding** field that constantly develops new techniques, applications, and challenges, and requires continuous learning and adaptation.
