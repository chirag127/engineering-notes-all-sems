### Knowledge Representation
- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding and organizing information about a domain of interest in a form that can be used by an intelligent agent to perform reasoning and problem-solving tasks.
- Knowledge representation involves two aspects: syntax and semantics.
  - Syntax: the rules and symbols that define the structure and format of the knowledge.
  - Semantics: the meaning and interpretation of the knowledge.
- Knowledge representation also requires a trade-off between expressiveness and efficiency.
  - Expressiveness: the ability to capture the complexity and diversity of the domain knowledge.
  - Efficiency: the ability to manipulate and process the knowledge in a timely and accurate manner.
- There are different types of knowledge representation schemes, each with its own advantages and disadvantages. Some of the common ones are:
  - Logic: a formal system of rules and symbols that can be used to make deductions and inferences based on facts and rules. Logic can be divided into propositional logic, predicate logic, modal logic, etc.
  - Semantic networks: a graphical representation of concepts and their relationships using nodes and links. Semantic networks can capture hierarchical, associative, and causal knowledge.
  - Frames: a representation of objects and their attributes using slots and values. Frames can capture the properties and behaviors of objects and their subclasses.
  - Scripts: a representation of stereotypical situations and events using sequences of actions and roles. Scripts can capture the common sense and expectations of a domain.
  - Production rules: a representation of conditional actions using IF-THEN statements. Production rules can capture the procedural and declarative knowledge of a domain.
  - Ontologies: a representation of the concepts and their relationships in a domain using a formal vocabulary and a set of axioms. Ontologies can capture the shared and standardized knowledge of a domain.
