- Prolog is a **logic programming language** that has an important role in **artificial intelligence** .
- Unlike many other programming languages, Prolog is intended primarily as a **declarative programming language**. In Prolog, logic is expressed as **relations** (called as **facts** and **rules**) .
- Prolog is based on the idea of **resolution** and **unification**. Resolution is a method of **inference** that uses the rules and facts to derive new information or answer queries. Unification is a process of **matching** two terms by finding a substitution that makes them identical .
- Prolog has a built-in **backtracking** mechanism that allows it to explore different possibilities and find all possible solutions to a query. Prolog also supports **recursion**, **lists**, **arithmetic operations**, **cut** (a control operator that prunes the search space), and **meta-programming** (the ability to manipulate programs as data) .
- Prolog is widely used for **knowledge representation and reasoning** in artificial intelligence. Prolog can represent complex and structured knowledge using **terms**, **predicates**, **variables**, and **functors**. Prolog can also reason about knowledge using **modus ponens** (a rule of inference that derives a conclusion from a premise and an implication), **negation as failure** (a method of handling negation by assuming that something is false if it cannot be proved to be true), and **non-monotonic reasoning** (a type of reasoning that allows for revising or retracting conclusions based on new information)   .
- Prolog can also be extended with various **paradigms** and **frameworks** that enhance its expressiveness and functionality. Some examples are **constraint logic programming** (a paradigm that integrates constraints into logic programming), **answer set programming** (a framework that uses the answer set semantics to model non-monotonic reasoning), **logic programming with defaults and argumentation** (a framework that combines default logic and argumentation theory to handle conflicting and incomplete information), and **AILog** (a knowledge-representation and reasoning system built on top of Prolog)   .
