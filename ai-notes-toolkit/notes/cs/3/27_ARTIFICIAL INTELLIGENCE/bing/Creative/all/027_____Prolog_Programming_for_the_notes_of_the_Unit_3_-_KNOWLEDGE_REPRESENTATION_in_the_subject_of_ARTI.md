# Prolog Programming for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Prolog is a **logic programming language** that has an important role in **artificial intelligence** .
- Unlike many other programming languages, Prolog is intended primarily as a **declarative programming language**. In Prolog, logic is expressed as **relations** (called as **facts** and **rules**) .
- Prolog can be used for **knowledge representation and reasoning** (KRR), which is at the heart of the artificial intelligence enterprise   .
- KRR is the process of encoding and manipulating knowledge in a formal and computable way, and using it to draw conclusions and make decisions  .
- Prolog supports various KRR paradigms, such as **first-order logic**, **non-monotonic logic**, **frame-based systems**, **semantic networks**, **description logics**, **ontologies**, and **common-sense reasoning**  .
- Prolog programs consist of a **database** of facts and rules, and a **query** that asks for a goal to be proved or a variable to be instantiated  .
- Prolog uses a **backtracking** algorithm to search for possible solutions to a query, by trying to match it with the facts and rules in the database, and applying **unification** and **resolution** techniques  .
- Prolog also allows for **meta-programming**, which is the ability to write programs that manipulate other programs, and **meta-reasoning**, which is the ability to reason about the reasoning process itself  .
- Prolog is a powerful and expressive language for KRR, but it also has some limitations, such as **incompleteness**, **inefficiency**, **lack of modularity**, and **difficulty of debugging**  .
- To overcome some of these limitations, various extensions and variants of Prolog have been developed, such as **A-Prolog**, **Datalog**, **Constraint Logic Programming**, **Answer Set Programming**, and **Logic Programming with Defaults and Argumentation**  .
