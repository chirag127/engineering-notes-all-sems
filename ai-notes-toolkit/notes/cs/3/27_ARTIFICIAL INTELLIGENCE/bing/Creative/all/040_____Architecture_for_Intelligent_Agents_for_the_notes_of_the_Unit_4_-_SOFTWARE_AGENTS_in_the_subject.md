# Architecture for Intelligent Agents

An intelligent agent is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors. An agent can be classified according to its degree of autonomy, reactivity, proactiveness, and social ability. The architecture of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together. There are different types of agent architectures, depending on the level of complexity and intelligence of the agent. Some of the common types are:

- **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They do not have any internal state or memory, and they act only based on the current percept. They do not have any goals or plans, and they do not reason about the consequences of their actions. Reactive agents are fast and robust, but they are limited in their flexibility and adaptability. An example of a reactive agent is a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current room temperature.

- **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents are an improvement over reactive agents. They have an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment, and they update this state based on the percepts. They also have a set of rules that specify how to act based on the state and the percept. Model-based reflex agents can handle partially observable and dynamic environments, but they still do not have any goals or plans. An example of a model-based reflex agent is a taxi driver that follows the traffic rules and avoids collisions based on the current road situation.

- **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents are more sophisticated than model-based reflex agents. They have an internal state, a set of rules, and also a set of goals that they want to achieve. They can choose the best action that leads to the fulfillment of their goals, based on their state and the percept. They can reason about the future and the outcomes of their actions. Goal-based agents can handle complex and uncertain environments, but they may not always find the optimal solution. An example of a goal-based agent is a chess player that tries to win the game based on the current board position.

- **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents are an extension of goal-based agents. They have an internal state, a set of rules, a set of goals, and also a utility function that assigns a numerical value to each state. They can choose the best action that maximizes their expected utility, based on their state, the percept, and the utility function. They can handle trade-offs and preferences among different goals. Utility-based agents can find the optimal solution, but they may require a lot of computation. An example of a utility-based agent is a stock trader that buys and sells stocks based on the current market situation and the expected profit.

- **Learning agents**: Learning agents are the most advanced type of AI agent. They have an internal state, a set of rules, a set of goals, a utility function, and also a learning component that allows them to improve their performance over time. They can learn from their own experience and from the feedback of other agents or humans. They can adapt to changing environments and situations. Learning agents can achieve superhuman performance, but they may also face ethical and social challenges. An example of a learning agent is a self-driving car that learns from its own driving data and from the feedback of other drivers and pedestrians.
