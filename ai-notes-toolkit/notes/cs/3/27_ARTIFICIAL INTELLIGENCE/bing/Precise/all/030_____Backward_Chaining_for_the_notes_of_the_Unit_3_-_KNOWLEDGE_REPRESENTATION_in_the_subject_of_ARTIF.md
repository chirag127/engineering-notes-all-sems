# Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method used in artificial intelligence for automated reasoning and problem-solving. It is a part of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS.

Here are some key points to remember about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining is a type of reasoning that starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to find the evidence or premises that support it.
2. It is often used in expert systems and rule-based systems to derive a conclusion from a set of facts and rules.
3. In backward chaining, the system starts with a list of goals and tries to find a rule that can be used to prove the goal.
4. If a rule is found, the system then tries to prove the premises of the rule using the same process.
5. This process continues until all the premises of the rule have been proven, or until no more rules can be found to prove the goal.
6. Backward chaining can be useful in situations where there are many possible solutions, and the system needs to find the most likely one.
7. It can also be useful when the system has incomplete information, as it can help to identify what additional information is needed to reach a conclusion.
