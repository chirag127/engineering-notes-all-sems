# Heuristics for the notes of the Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Heuristics are problem-solving methods that use shortcuts to produce good-enough solutions given a limited time frame or deadline.
- Heuristics are also known as "rules of thumb", "educated guesses", "intuitive judgments", or "common sense".
- Heuristics can be contrasted with algorithms, which are step-by-step procedures for solving problems that guarantee a correct solution.
- Heuristics are often used when a problem is too complex or time-consuming to solve using an algorithm.
- Heuristics can be useful in many situations, but they can also lead to errors and biases.
- Some common heuristics include the availability heuristic, the representativeness heuristic, and the anchoring and adjustment heuristic.
- The availability heuristic is a mental shortcut that relies on immediate examples that come to mind when evaluating a specific topic, concept, method, or decision.
- The representativeness heuristic is a mental shortcut that involves judging the likelihood of an event by comparing it to an existing prototype that already exists in our minds.
- The anchoring and adjustment heuristic is a mental shortcut that involves making decisions based on an initial value, known as the anchor, and then adjusting that value to reach a final decision.
- Heuristics can be useful in artificial intelligence, particularly in the field of machine learning, where they can be used to guide the search for solutions to complex problems.
