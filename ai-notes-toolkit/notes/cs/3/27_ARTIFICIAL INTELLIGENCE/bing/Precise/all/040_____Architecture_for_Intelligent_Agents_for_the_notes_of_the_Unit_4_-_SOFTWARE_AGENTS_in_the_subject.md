# Architecture for Intelligent Agents

## Introduction
In Unit 4 of the subject of Artificial Intelligence, the topic of architecture for intelligent agents is covered. This section will introduce some of the major architecture types and applications for which they can be used.

## Types of Architectures
1. **Reactive architectures:** Reactive architectures are based on the idea that intelligent behavior can be achieved without the need for complex internal representations or reasoning processes.
2. **Deliberative architectures:** Deliberative architectures are based on the idea that intelligent behavior can be achieved through the use of explicit, symbolic representations of the world and reasoning processes that operate on these representations.
3. **Blackboard architectures:** Blackboard architectures are based on the idea of a shared, global data structure that can be accessed and modified by multiple, independent processes.
4. **Belief-desire-intention (BDI) architecture:** The BDI architecture is based on practical reasoning by Bratman’s philosophical theory of human practical reasoning. A BDI agent is a particular type of bounded rational software agent, imbued with particular mental attitudes, viz: Beliefs, Desires and Intentions (BDI).
5. **Hybrid architectures:** Hybrid architectures combine elements of reactive and deliberative architectures in an attempt to achieve the best of both worlds.
6. **Mobile architectures:** Mobile architectures are designed to support agents that can move between different host systems.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, there are several different types of architectures for intelligent agents, each with its own strengths and weaknesses. The choice of architecture will depend on the specific requirements of the application and the characteristics of the environment in which the agent will operate.
