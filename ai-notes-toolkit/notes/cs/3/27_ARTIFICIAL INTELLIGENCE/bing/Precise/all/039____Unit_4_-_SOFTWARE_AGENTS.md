# Unit 4 - SOFTWARE AGENTS

Software agents are computer programs that act autonomously on behalf of a user or system. They are designed to perform tasks that would otherwise require human intelligence, such as decision-making, problem-solving, and learning.

Some key characteristics of software agents include:

1. Autonomy: Agents operate without the direct intervention of humans or other agents, and have control over their actions and internal state.
2. Social ability: Agents interact with other agents and humans in order to achieve their goals.
3. Reactivity: Agents perceive their environment and respond in a timely manner to changes that occur in it.
4. Proactivity: Agents do not simply act in response to their environment, but are able to exhibit goal-directed behavior by taking the initiative.

Software agents can be classified into several types, including:

- Collaborative agents: These agents work together with other agents or humans to achieve a common goal.
- Interface agents: These agents act as intermediaries between the user and the computer system, providing a more natural and intuitive interface.
- Mobile agents: These agents can move from one computer system to another, carrying out tasks on behalf of the user.
- Information agents: These agents gather, filter, and process information from various sources, and present it to the user in a useful and organized manner.

Software agents have a wide range of applications, including e-commerce, information retrieval, and network management. They are also used in artificial intelligence research, where they serve as models for intelligent behavior.
