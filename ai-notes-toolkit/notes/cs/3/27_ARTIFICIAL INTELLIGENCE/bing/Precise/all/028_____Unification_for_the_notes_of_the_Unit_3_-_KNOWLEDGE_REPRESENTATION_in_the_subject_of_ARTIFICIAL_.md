# Unification

Unification is a process in automated reasoning that is used to make two expressions syntactically identical by finding a substitution for the variables in the expressions. It is used in various fields of artificial intelligence, including natural language processing, automated theorem proving, and logic programming.

Here are some key points to remember about unification:

1. Unification is a syntactic process, meaning that it operates on the structure of expressions rather than their meaning.
2. The goal of unification is to find a substitution for the variables in two expressions that makes them syntactically identical.
3. Unification is used in various fields of artificial intelligence, including natural language processing, automated theorem proving, and logic programming.
4. Unification can be used to solve equations, to find the most general unifier (mgu) of two expressions, and to perform type inference.
5. Unification algorithms can be implemented using various data structures, including trees and graphs.
