### Forward Chaining

Forward chaining is a method of reasoning in artificial intelligence where the inference engine starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data until a goal is reached. It is also known as data-driven reasoning or bottom-up reasoning.

Here are some key points to remember about forward chaining:

1. Forward chaining is a popular method used in rule-based expert systems.
2. The inference engine starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data.
3. The process continues until a goal is reached or no more data can be extracted.
4. Forward chaining is a data-driven or bottom-up approach to reasoning.
5. It is useful when the system has a large amount of data to start with and the goal is to find a specific piece of information.
6. Forward chaining can be computationally expensive as it may require the evaluation of many rules before reaching the goal.
