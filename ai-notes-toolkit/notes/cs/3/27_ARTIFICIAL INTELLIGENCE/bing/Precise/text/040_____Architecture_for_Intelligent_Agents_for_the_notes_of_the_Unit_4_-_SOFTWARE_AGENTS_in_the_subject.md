### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

1. **Reactive Architecture:** This architecture is based on the concept of stimulus-response. The agent responds to changes in the environment without any internal representation or reasoning. This architecture is simple and fast, but it lacks flexibility and adaptability.

2. **Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) Architecture:** This architecture is based on the concept of mental attitudes. The agent has beliefs about the world, desires or goals to achieve, and intentions to act. This architecture allows for more complex reasoning and decision-making, but it can be slower and more computationally demanding.

3. **Layered Architecture:** This architecture combines different levels of abstraction, with lower levels responsible for reactive behavior and higher levels responsible for more complex reasoning and decision-making. This architecture allows for both fast responses and complex behavior, but it can be difficult to design and implement.

4. **Hybrid Architecture:** This architecture combines different architectures, such as reactive and BDI, to take advantage of their strengths and mitigate their weaknesses. This architecture can be very powerful, but it can also be complex and difficult to design and implement.

These are some of the common architectures used for designing intelligent agents in the field of Artificial Intelligence. Each architecture has its strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of architecture depends on the specific requirements of the application.
