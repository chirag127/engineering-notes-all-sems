### Forward Chaining

Forward chaining is a method of reasoning in artificial intelligence where the inference engine starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data until a goal is reached. It is also known as data-driven reasoning or bottom-up reasoning.

Here are some key points to remember about forward chaining:

1. Forward chaining is a popular method used in rule-based expert systems.
2. It starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data.
3. The process continues until a goal is reached or no more data can be extracted.
4. Forward chaining is a data-driven approach, meaning that the reasoning process is driven by the available data.
5. It is also known as bottom-up reasoning because it starts with the available data and works its way up to a conclusion.
6. Forward chaining is useful in situations where the available data is incomplete or uncertain.
7. It is also useful in situations where the goal is not clearly defined.
