### Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method of reasoning used in artificial intelligence and is commonly used in expert systems. It is a form of inference that starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to determine the facts or evidence that support the conclusion.

Here are some key points to remember about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining is used in rule-based systems to determine if a conclusion is true or false.
2. The process starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to find the evidence that supports it.
3. If the evidence is found, the conclusion is considered true. If the evidence is not found, the conclusion is considered false.
4. Backward chaining is commonly used in expert systems, where the system has a set of rules and facts to determine the outcome of a situation.
5. It is a form of deductive reasoning, where the conclusion is derived from the premises.
