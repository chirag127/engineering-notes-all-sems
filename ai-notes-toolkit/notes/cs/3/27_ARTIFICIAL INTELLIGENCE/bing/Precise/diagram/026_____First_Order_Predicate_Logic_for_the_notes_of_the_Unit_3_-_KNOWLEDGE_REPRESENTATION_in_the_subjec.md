### First Order Predicate Logic

First-order predicate logic is a formal system used in mathematics, philosophy, linguistics, and computer science. It is also known as first-order logic, predicate calculus, or quantification theory. It is a powerful tool for representing and reasoning about knowledge in artificial intelligence.

Here are some key points to remember about first-order predicate logic:

1. First-order predicate logic extends propositional logic by introducing the concept of quantifiers, which allow us to make statements about collections of objects.
2. The two most common quantifiers are the universal quantifier, denoted by the symbol ∀, and the existential quantifier, denoted by the symbol ∃.
3. The universal quantifier is used to make statements that are true for all objects in a given domain, while the existential quantifier is used to make statements that are true for at least one object in the domain.
4. First-order predicate logic also introduces the concept of predicates, which are used to represent relationships between objects.
5. Predicates are denoted by capital letters and can take one or more arguments, which represent the objects involved in the relationship.
6. In first-order predicate logic, we can also use functions to represent the result of applying an operation to one or more objects.
7. First-order predicate logic allows us to represent complex knowledge by combining predicates, quantifiers, and logical connectives in a precise and unambiguous manner.
