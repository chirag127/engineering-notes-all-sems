## Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding information about a domain in a form that can be used by an intelligent agent to perform reasoning and problem-solving tasks.
- Knowledge representation involves choosing a suitable representation language, defining the syntax and semantics of the language, and constructing a knowledge base that contains facts and rules about the domain.
- Some of the criteria for evaluating a knowledge representation language are expressiveness, efficiency, inferential adequacy, inferential efficiency, and acquisitional efficiency.
- Some of the common types of knowledge representation languages are propositional logic, first-order logic, description logic, semantic networks, frames, production rules, and scripts.
- Propositional logic is a simple language that uses symbols to represent atomic propositions and logical connectives to form complex propositions. It can be used to represent facts that are either true or false, but not facts that involve variables, quantifiers, or relations.
- First-order logic is an extension of propositional logic that allows the use of variables, constants, predicates, functions, and quantifiers to represent facts that involve objects, properties, and relations. It can be used to represent facts that are more expressive and general than propositional logic, but it is also more complex and computationally expensive.
- Description logic is a subset of first-order logic that focuses on representing concepts and their relationships using a restricted syntax and semantics. It can be used to represent hierarchical and taxonomic knowledge, such as classes, subclasses, instances, and properties. It is the basis of many ontology languages, such as OWL and RDF.
- Semantic networks are graphical representations of knowledge that use nodes to represent concepts and links to represent relations. They can be used to represent associative and causal knowledge, such as inheritance, similarity, and causation. They are easy to visualize and understand, but they may lack formal semantics and inference mechanisms.
- Frames are data structures that represent knowledge using slots and values. They can be used to represent stereotypical and default knowledge, such as categories, attributes, and values. They allow inheritance and overriding of slot values, but they may not handle exceptions and contradictions well.
- Production rules are conditional statements that represent knowledge using IF-THEN format. They can be used to represent procedural and declarative knowledge, such as actions, goals, and facts. They allow forward and backward chaining of inference, but they may suffer from redundancy and inconsistency of rules.
- Scripts are structures that represent knowledge using sequences of events and actions. They can be used to represent common-sense and episodic knowledge, such as scenarios, plans, and narratives. They allow prediction and explanation of behavior, but they may not handle variations and exceptions well.
