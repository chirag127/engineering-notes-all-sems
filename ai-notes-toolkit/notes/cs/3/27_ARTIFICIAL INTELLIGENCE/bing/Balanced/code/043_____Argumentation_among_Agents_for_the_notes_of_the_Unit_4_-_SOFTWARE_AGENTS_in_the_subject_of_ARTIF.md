### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify actions, explain phenomena, or learn from each other.
- Argumentation can be formalized using various logical frameworks, such as abstract argumentation, structured argumentation, or probabilistic argumentation, that capture different aspects of argument structure, validity, strength, or uncertainty.
- Argumentation can be implemented using various computational techniques, such as argumentation-based dialogues, argumentation schemes, argument mining, or argument visualization, that enable agents to construct, exchange, or evaluate arguments in different domains and contexts.
- Argumentation is an interdisciplinary area of research that draws from philosophy, logic, linguistics, psychology, and artificial intelligence, and has applications in various fields, such as law, medicine, education, or social media.
