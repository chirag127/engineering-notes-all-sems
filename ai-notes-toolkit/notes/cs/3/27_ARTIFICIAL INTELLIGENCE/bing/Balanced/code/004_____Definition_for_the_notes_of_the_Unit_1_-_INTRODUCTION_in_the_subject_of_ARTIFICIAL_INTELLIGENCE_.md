### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of computer science that studies and develops machines and software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, and perception .
- AI can be classified into two main types: weak AI and strong AI. Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the type of AI that can only perform specific tasks or functions, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages. Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the type of AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can, such as understanding natural language, solving problems, or creating art .
- AI can also be categorized based on the methods or techniques used to achieve its goals, such as symbolic AI, connectionist AI, evolutionary AI, or hybrid AI. Symbolic AI, also known as classical AI or rule-based AI, is the type of AI that uses symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge, such as logic, expert systems, or search algorithms. Connectionist AI, also known as neural network AI or deep learning AI, is the type of AI that uses artificial neural networks to learn from data and perform tasks, such as image recognition, natural language processing, or speech synthesis. Evolutionary AI, also known as genetic AI or evolutionary computation, is the type of AI that uses evolutionary algorithms to optimize solutions, such as genetic algorithms, genetic programming, or evolutionary strategies. Hybrid AI, also known as integrated AI or multi-agent AI, is the type of AI that combines different methods or techniques to achieve more complex or robust results, such as fuzzy logic, reinforcement learning, or swarm intelligence .
- AI is being used across different industries and domains, such as finance, healthcare, education, entertainment, security, or transportation. Some of the applications and benefits of AI include fraud detection, diagnosis, recommendation systems, chatbots, video games, face recognition, self-driving cars, and many more  . However, AI also poses some challenges and risks, such as ethical, social, legal, or technical issues, such as bias, privacy, accountability, security, or singularity .
