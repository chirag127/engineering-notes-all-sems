### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify decisions, explain actions, and learn from each other.
- Argumentation can be formalized using various logical frameworks, such as abstract argumentation, structured argumentation, or dialogical argumentation, that capture different aspects of argument structure, content, and dynamics.
- Argumentation can be implemented using various computational techniques, such as argumentation-based reasoning, argumentation-based dialogue, argumentation-based negotiation, argumentation-based decision making, and argumentation-based learning, that enable agents to perform various argumentative tasks and roles.
- Argumentation can be applied to various domains and scenarios, such as law, medicine, education, politics, e-commerce, social media, and human-computer interaction, that involve complex and uncertain situations that require rational and social reasoning.
