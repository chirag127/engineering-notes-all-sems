### Introduction

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that deals with creating machines or software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, etc.
- AI can be classified into different types based on the degree of human-like intelligence and the scope of the problem domain. Some of the common types are:
  - Weak AI or Narrow AI: This type of AI can only perform a specific task or a narrow range of tasks within a predefined domain. For example, a chess-playing program, a face recognition system, a spam filter, etc. Weak AI does not have general intelligence or self-awareness.
  - Strong AI or Artificial General Intelligence (AGI): This type of AI can perform any intellectual task that a human can do, across different domains and contexts. For example, a hypothetical AI system that can understand natural language, solve complex problems, reason about the world, create art, etc. Strong AI has general intelligence and self-awareness, but it is not yet achieved by any existing AI system.
  - Artificial Superintelligence (ASI): This type of AI can surpass human intelligence and capabilities in all aspects, including creativity, wisdom, social skills, etc. For example, a hypothetical AI system that can invent new technologies, discover new scientific laws, manipulate matter and energy, etc. Artificial superintelligence is the ultimate goal of some AI researchers, but it is also considered a potential existential threat to humanity by some philosophers and ethicists.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the approach or the technique used to achieve the desired goal. Some of the common types are:
  - Symbolic AI or Classical AI: This type of AI uses symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge and logic. For example, a rule-based expert system, a logic-based theorem prover, a semantic network, etc. Symbolic AI is based on the assumption that intelligence can be reduced to symbol manipulation and that human knowledge can be explicitly encoded in a formal language.
  - Subsymbolic AI or Connectionist AI: This type of AI uses artificial neural networks to learn from data and perform tasks that involve pattern recognition, classification, prediction, etc. For example, a deep learning model, a convolutional neural network, a recurrent neural network, etc. Subsymbolic AI is based on the assumption that intelligence can be modeled by the parallel and distributed processing of simple units that mimic the neurons and synapses of the brain.
  - Evolutionary AI or Genetic AI: This type of AI uses evolutionary algorithms to optimize the performance of a system or a solution by simulating the natural processes of selection, mutation, crossover, etc. For example, a genetic algorithm, a genetic programming, a neuroevolution, etc. Evolutionary AI is based on the assumption that intelligence can be evolved by the adaptation and survival of the fittest individuals or populations.
  - Hybrid AI: This type of AI combines different types or techniques of AI to achieve a better result or a more robust system. For example, a neuro-symbolic system, a fuzzy logic system, a multi-agent system, etc. Hybrid AI is based on the assumption that intelligence can be enhanced by the integration and cooperation of different paradigms and methods.
