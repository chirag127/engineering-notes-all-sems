### Alpha

- Alpha is a general term for a class of artificial intelligence systems that use deep learning and reinforcement learning to solve complex problems.
- Alpha systems are developed by DeepMind, a subsidiary of Google, and are named after the Greek letter alpha, which represents the first or best in a series.
- Some examples of Alpha systems are:
  - AlphaGo: A system that can play the board game Go at a superhuman level, defeating the world champion Lee Sedol in 2016 and the top-ranked player Ke Jie in 2017.
  - AlphaZero: A system that can master multiple board games, such as chess, shogi, and Go, by learning from scratch, without any human knowledge or data.
  - AlphaFold: A system that can predict the three-dimensional structure of proteins, a key challenge in biology and medicine, with unprecedented accuracy and speed.
  - AlphaCode: A system that can compete in programming contests, such as Codeforces, by generating and testing code for various tasks that require critical thinking and creativity.
- Alpha systems use a combination of problem-solving methods in artificial intelligence, such as:
  - Search algorithms: Techniques that explore a large space of possible solutions, guided by heuristics or evaluation functions, to find the optimal or near-optimal solution.
  - Evolutionary algorithms: Techniques that mimic the process of natural selection, by generating and mutating a population of candidate solutions, and selecting the fittest ones for the next generation.
  - Knowledge representation: Techniques that encode the relevant information about the problem domain, such as facts, rules, concepts, and constraints, in a formal and logical way.
  - Reasoning: Techniques that infer new knowledge or conclusions from the existing knowledge, using deductive or inductive logic, or probabilistic methods.
- Alpha systems demonstrate the potential of artificial intelligence for solving challenging and diverse problems, and advancing the scientific and technological frontiers.
