### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors.
- The **architecture** of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together.
- The architecture of an agent determines how it processes the information from its sensors and how it selects the actions to perform with its effectors.
- There are different types of architectures for intelligent agents, depending on their level of complexity, autonomy, and rationality.
- Some of the common types of architectures are  :
  - **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They do not have any internal state or memory, and they act only based on the current percept. They are fast and robust, but they cannot handle complex or dynamic environments.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents have an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment. They use this state to update their beliefs based on the current percept and their previous actions. They can handle partially observable and dynamic environments, but they still lack goals and planning abilities.
  - **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents have an internal state that represents their goals, which are the desired situations or outcomes. They use their state and the current percept to generate and evaluate possible actions, and they choose the one that leads them closer to their goals. They can handle complex and dynamic environments, but they still lack a measure of success or utility.
  - **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents have an internal state that represents their utility function, which is a measure of how desirable a situation or outcome is. They use their state and the current percept to generate and evaluate possible actions, and they choose the one that maximizes their expected utility. They can handle complex and dynamic environments, and they can trade off between conflicting goals and uncertain outcomes.
  - **Learning agents**: Learning agents have an internal state that represents their learning component, which is a mechanism that allows them to improve their performance over time. They use their state and the current percept to generate and evaluate possible actions, and they choose the one that maximizes their expected utility. They can also use their state and the feedback from the environment to update their learning component, which in turn affects their future actions. They can handle complex and dynamic environments, and they can adapt to changing situations and goals.
