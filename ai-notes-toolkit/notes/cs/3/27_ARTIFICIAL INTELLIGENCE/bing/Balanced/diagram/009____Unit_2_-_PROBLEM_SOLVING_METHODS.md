## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- Problem solving methods are systematic ways of approaching and solving problems, using tools and techniques such as algorithms, heuristics, diagrams, etc.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - **Trial and error**: Trying different solutions until finding one that works. This method is simple but inefficient and may not guarantee an optimal solution.
  - **Divide and conquer**: Breaking down a large problem into smaller and simpler subproblems, and solving them recursively. This method is efficient and can reduce the complexity of the problem, but may require more memory and time.
  - **Backtracking**: Exploring different paths or choices in a problem space, and going back to a previous state if a dead end or a contradiction is reached. This method is useful for finding all possible solutions or the best solution, but may also require more memory and time.
  - **Greedy**: Choosing the best option available at each step, without considering the future consequences. This method is fast and easy, but may not lead to an optimal or feasible solution.
  - **Dynamic programming**: Storing and reusing the results of subproblems that are solved repeatedly, to avoid recomputation. This method is efficient and optimal, but may require more memory and a careful analysis of the problem structure.
  - **Branch and bound**: Pruning the search space by eliminating the branches that cannot lead to a better solution than the current best one. This method is optimal and can reduce the time and memory required, but may also depend on the quality of the bounding function.
  - **Heuristic**: Using rules of thumb or intuition to guide the search for a solution, based on some domain knowledge or experience. This method is fast and flexible, but may not be accurate or reliable.
