# Problem Solving Methods

- Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given situation or goal, given some initial conditions and constraints.
- Problem solving methods are general strategies or techniques that can be applied to different types of problems, such as search, planning, optimization, constraint satisfaction, etc.
- Problem solving methods can be classified into two categories: **knowledge-based** and **search-based**.

## Knowledge-based methods

- Knowledge-based methods rely on domain-specific knowledge and rules to solve problems, such as expert systems, logic programming, rule-based systems, etc.
- Knowledge-based methods can be efficient and accurate, but they require a lot of human effort to acquire and represent the knowledge, and they may not be able to handle uncertainty, inconsistency, or incompleteness of the knowledge.

## Search-based methods

- Search-based methods do not require much domain-specific knowledge, but they use general algorithms to explore a large space of possible solutions, such as heuristic search, genetic algorithms, neural networks, etc.
- Search-based methods can be flexible and adaptable, but they may be computationally expensive, and they may not guarantee to find the optimal or even a feasible solution.

## Examples of problem solving methods

- **Heuristic search**: A search-based method that uses a heuristic function to guide the search towards the most promising solutions, such as A*, hill climbing, simulated annealing, etc.
- **Genetic algorithms**: A search-based method that mimics the process of natural evolution to generate and improve solutions, using operators such as selection, crossover, and mutation.
- **Neural networks**: A search-based method that uses a network of interconnected nodes (neurons) to learn and approximate complex functions, such as feedforward, recurrent, and convolutional neural networks.
- **Expert systems**: A knowledge-based method that uses a set of rules and facts to infer conclusions and recommendations, such as MYCIN, DENDRAL, etc.
- **Logic programming**: A knowledge-based method that uses a declarative language (such as Prolog) to express facts and rules, and a logical inference engine to answer queries and solve problems.
- **Rule-based systems**: A knowledge-based method that uses a set of if-then rules to represent and manipulate knowledge, such as production systems, fuzzy systems, etc.
