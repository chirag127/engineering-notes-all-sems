## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given situation or task that meets some criteria or constraints.
- Problem solving methods are systematic approaches or strategies that can be applied to different types of problems.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until one works or the problem is solved.
  - Heuristics: Using rules of thumb or shortcuts based on experience or intuition to guide the search for solutions.
  - Algorithms: Following a set of precise and unambiguous steps or rules that guarantee a solution if executed correctly.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved more easily.
  - Backtracking: Exploring different branches of a solution space and going back to a previous state if a dead end or a contradiction is reached.
  - Hill climbing: Moving from one solution to another that is slightly better or closer to the goal until a local or global optimum is reached.
  - Means-ends analysis: Comparing the current state and the goal state and finding actions that reduce the difference between them.
  - Analogy: Using a similar or familiar problem and its solution as a model or inspiration for solving a new problem.
  - Brainstorming: Generating as many ideas or solutions as possible without judging or evaluating them until later.
  - Lateral thinking: Using creative or unconventional thinking to find new or unexpected solutions or perspectives.
