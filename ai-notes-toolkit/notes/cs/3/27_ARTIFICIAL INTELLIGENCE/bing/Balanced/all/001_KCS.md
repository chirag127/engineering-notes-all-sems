# KCS

- KCS stands for Knowledge-Centered Service, a methodology for creating and maintaining knowledge as a key asset of an organization.
- KCS is based on four basic principles: 
  - Integrate the creation and maintenance of knowledge into the problem-solving process.
  - Evolve content based on demand and usage.
  - Develop a knowledge base of collective experience.
  - Reward learning, collaboration, sharing, and improvement.
- KCS has two main processes: the Solve Loop and the Evolve Loop.
  - The Solve Loop focuses on capturing, structuring, and reusing knowledge at the point of interaction with customers or users.
  - The Evolve Loop focuses on analyzing, improving, and governing the knowledge base and the KCS practices.
- KCS has four levels of adoption: 
  - Level 1: Awareness - The organization recognizes the value of knowledge and the need for a consistent approach to capture and reuse it.
  - Level 2: Foundation - The organization implements the basic elements of KCS, such as roles, workflows, content standards, and tools.
  - Level 3: Proficiency - The organization achieves proficiency in KCS practices, such as searching, linking, flagging, and updating knowledge articles.
  - Level 4: Optimization - The organization leverages the knowledge base for continuous improvement, innovation, and strategic decision making.
- KCS has six core concepts that guide its implementation and adoption: 
  - Context - Knowledge is created and used in a specific situation and may not be applicable to other situations.
  - Content Standard - Knowledge articles should follow a consistent structure and format that enable easy creation, retrieval, and reuse.
  - Workflow - Knowledge creation and maintenance should be integrated into the natural workflow of solving problems and providing service.
  - Just-in-Time - Knowledge should be captured and updated at the time of need, rather than after the fact or in anticipation of future needs.
  - Demand-Driven - Knowledge should be driven by the demand and usage of the customers or users, rather than by the supply and assumptions of the providers or creators.
  - Trust - Knowledge should be trusted by the customers or users and the providers or creators, and trust should be built through transparency, feedback, and collaboration.
