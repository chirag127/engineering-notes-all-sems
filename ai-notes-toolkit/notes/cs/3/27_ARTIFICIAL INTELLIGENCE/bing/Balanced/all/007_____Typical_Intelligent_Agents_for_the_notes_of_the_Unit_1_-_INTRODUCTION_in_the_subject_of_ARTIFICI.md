# Typical Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is an autonomous entity that acts upon an environment using sensors and actuators to achieve its goals .
- An intelligent agent may also learn from the environment to improve its performance.
- An intelligent agent can be described as a software entity that conducts operations in the place of users or programs after sensing the environment.
- An intelligent agent can also be a physical entity, such as a robot or a self-driving car.
- An intelligent agent is closely related to agents in economics, cognitive science, ethics, and philosophy.
- There are different types of intelligent agents based on their characteristics, such as:
  - **Simple reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, without considering the history of percepts. They are the simplest and most limited type of agents. For example, a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept and a model of the world that they maintain. They can handle partially observable environments by using the model to keep track of the hidden state. For example, a self-driving car that uses a map and sensors to navigate the road.
  - **Goal-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, and a goal that they want to achieve. They can choose the best action that leads to the goal, even if it is not immediate. For example, a chess-playing program that evaluates the possible moves and chooses the best one.
  - **Utility-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, a goal, and a utility function that measures how desirable a state is. They can choose the best action that maximizes the expected utility, taking into account the uncertainty and the trade-offs. For example, a stock-trading program that buys and sells shares based on the market trends and the risk preferences.
  - **Learning agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, a goal, a utility function, and a learning component that updates the agent's knowledge and behavior. They can improve their performance over time by learning from their experience and feedback. For example, a Siri virtual assistant that adapts to the user's preferences and requests .
