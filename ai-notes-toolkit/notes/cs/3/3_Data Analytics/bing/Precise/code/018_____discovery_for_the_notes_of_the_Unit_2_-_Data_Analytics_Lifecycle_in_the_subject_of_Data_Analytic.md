### Discovery

The discovery phase is the first phase of the Data Analytics Lifecycle. It involves the following steps:

1. **Identifying the business problem:** The first step in the discovery phase is to identify the business problem that needs to be solved. This involves understanding the business objectives and the key performance indicators (KPIs) that need to be improved.

2. **Defining the scope of the project:** Once the business problem has been identified, the scope of the project needs to be defined. This involves determining the data sources that will be used, the data that will be analyzed, and the analytical methods that will be employed.

3. **Formulating the initial hypothesis:** Based on the business problem and the scope of the project, an initial hypothesis is formulated. This hypothesis will be tested and refined during the course of the project.

4. **Assessing the available data:** The next step is to assess the available data to determine if it is sufficient to support the project. This involves evaluating the quality and completeness of the data, as well as its relevance to the business problem.

5. **Preparing the data:** If the available data is deemed sufficient, it is then prepared for analysis. This involves cleaning the data, transforming it into a suitable format, and integrating it with other data sources if necessary.

6. **Exploring the data:** The final step in the discovery phase is to explore the data to gain a better understanding of its characteristics and relationships. This involves using techniques such as data visualization and descriptive statistics to identify patterns and trends in the data.

In summary, the discovery phase is a crucial first step in the Data Analytics Lifecycle. It involves identifying the business problem, defining the scope of the project, formulating an initial hypothesis, assessing the available data, preparing the data, and exploring the data to gain a better understanding of its characteristics and relationships.
