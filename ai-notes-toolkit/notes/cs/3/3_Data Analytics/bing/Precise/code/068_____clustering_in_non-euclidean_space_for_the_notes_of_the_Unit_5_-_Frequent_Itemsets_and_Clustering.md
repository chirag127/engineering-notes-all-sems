### Unit 5 - Frequent Itemsets and Clustering
#### Clustering in Non-Euclidean Space

1. Clustering is the process of grouping similar data points together based on some measure of similarity or distance.
2. Non-Euclidean space refers to any space that is not Euclidean, meaning it does not follow the axioms of Euclidean geometry.
3. Clustering in non-Euclidean space can be more challenging than in Euclidean space because traditional distance measures, such as Euclidean distance, may not be appropriate.
4. Some common non-Euclidean spaces include spaces with different curvature, such as hyperbolic or spherical space, and spaces with different topologies, such as graphs or networks.
5. In these spaces, alternative distance measures, such as geodesic distance or shortest path distance, may be more appropriate for clustering.
6. Clustering algorithms that can be used in non-Euclidean spaces include hierarchical clustering, spectral clustering, and density-based clustering.
7. These algorithms can be adapted to use appropriate distance measures for the specific non-Euclidean space in question.
8. Clustering in non-Euclidean space has applications in many fields, including computer vision, natural language processing, and social network analysis.
