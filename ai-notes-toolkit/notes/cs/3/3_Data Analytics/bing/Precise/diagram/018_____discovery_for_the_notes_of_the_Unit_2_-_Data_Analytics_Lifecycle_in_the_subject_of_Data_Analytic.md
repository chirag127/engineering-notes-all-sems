### Discovery

The discovery phase is the first stage of the Data Analytics Lifecycle. It involves the following steps:

1. **Identifying the business problem:** The first step in the discovery phase is to identify the business problem that needs to be addressed. This involves understanding the business context, goals, and objectives.

2. **Determining the scope of the project:** Once the business problem has been identified, the scope of the project needs to be determined. This involves defining the boundaries of the project, including the data sources that will be used, the time frame for the analysis, and the expected outcomes.

3. **Formulating the initial hypothesis:** Based on the business problem and the scope of the project, an initial hypothesis is formulated. This hypothesis will guide the data collection and analysis process.

4. **Assessing the available data:** The next step is to assess the available data to determine if it is sufficient to address the business problem. This involves evaluating the quality, completeness, and relevance of the data.

5. **Identifying the required data:** If the available data is not sufficient, additional data may be required. In this case, the required data needs to be identified and a plan for collecting it should be developed.

6. **Developing a project plan:** The final step in the discovery phase is to develop a project plan. This plan should outline the steps that will be taken to address the business problem, including the data collection and analysis methods that will be used.

In summary, the discovery phase is a crucial first step in the Data Analytics Lifecycle. It involves identifying the business problem, determining the scope of the project, formulating an initial hypothesis, assessing the available data, identifying the required data, and developing a project plan. These steps help to ensure that the data analysis project is well-defined and has a clear direction.
