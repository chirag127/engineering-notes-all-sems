### Unit 3 - Data Analysis: Linear Systems Analysis & Nonlinear Dynamics

1. Linear dynamical systems can be solved exactly, in contrast to most nonlinear ones.
2. Occasionally, a nonlinear system can be solved exactly by a change of variables to a linear system.
3. The solutions of (almost) any nonlinear system can be well-approximated by an equivalent linear system near its fixed points.
4. Dynamical systems describe the evolution of a state variable in time in the form of ordinary differential equations or as discrete mappings.
5. Dynamical systems theory studies the solutions of such equations and mappings and their dependence on initial conditions and parameters.
6. For linear analysis, all structural components are elastic, and only elastic properties are needed for analysis.
7. For nonlinear analysis, some components can yield, and additional inelastic properties are needed for these components.
8. These properties are more complicated than the elastic properties.
9. Linear stability analysis of continuous-time nonlinear systems involves finding an equilibrium point of the system, calculating the Jacobian matrix of the system at the equilibrium point, and calculating the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix.
10. Introduction to applied linear algebra and linear dynamical systems includes applications to circuits, signal processing, communications, and control systems.
11. Topics include least-squares approximations of over-determined equations and least-norm solutions of underdetermined equations.
12. Experimental technique and data analysis have seen such dramatic progress that most fundamental properties of nonlinear dynamical systems have been observed in the laboratory.
13. Great efforts are being made to exploit ideas from chaos theory wherever the data displays more structure than can be captured by traditional methods.
