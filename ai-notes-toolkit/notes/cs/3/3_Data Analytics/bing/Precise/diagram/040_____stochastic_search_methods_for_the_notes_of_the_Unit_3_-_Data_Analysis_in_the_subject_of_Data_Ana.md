### Stochastic Search Methods

Stochastic search methods are a class of optimization algorithms that use randomness to find solutions to problems. These methods are particularly useful when the search space is large, complex, or poorly understood. Some common stochastic search methods include:

1. **Simulated Annealing**: This method is inspired by the process of annealing in metallurgy, where a material is heated and then slowly cooled to reduce defects and improve its structure. In simulated annealing, the algorithm starts with a random solution and then iteratively makes small changes to it. At each step, the new solution is accepted with a probability that depends on the difference in the objective function values of the two solutions and a temperature parameter that decreases over time.

2. **Genetic Algorithms**: These algorithms are inspired by the process of natural selection and evolution. A population of potential solutions is maintained, and at each iteration, new solutions are generated by combining and mutating existing solutions. The best solutions are then selected to survive to the next generation.

3. **Particle Swarm Optimization**: This method is inspired by the social behavior of bird flocks or fish schools. A population of particles moves through the search space, with each particle's movement influenced by its own best position and the best position of the entire swarm. Over time, the swarm converges towards an optimal solution.

These are just a few examples of stochastic search methods. These methods can be powerful tools for solving complex optimization problems, but they require careful tuning of their parameters to achieve good performance.
