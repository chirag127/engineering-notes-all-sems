### Regression modeling

- Regression modeling is a statistical technique that aims to describe the relationship between a dependent variable (also called the response or outcome variable) and one or more independent variables (also called the predictors or explanatory variables).
- Regression models can be used for various purposes, such as:
  - Exploring the nature and strength of the relationship between the variables of interest.
  - Testing hypotheses about the effects of the independent variables on the dependent variable.
  - Estimating the expected value of the dependent variable for a given set of values of the independent variables.
  - Predicting the future values of the dependent variable based on new or unseen values of the independent variables.
- There are different types of regression models, depending on the characteristics of the dependent variable and the independent variables, such as:
  - Linear regression: The dependent variable is continuous and the relationship between the variables is linear (i.e., can be represented by a straight line).
  - Logistic regression: The dependent variable is binary (i.e., has only two possible outcomes, such as yes/no, success/failure, etc.) and the relationship between the variables is nonlinear (i.e., cannot be represented by a straight line).
  - Multiple regression: There are more than one independent variables that affect the dependent variable.
  - Polynomial regression: The relationship between the variables is nonlinear and can be represented by a polynomial function (i.e., a function that involves powers of the independent variables, such as quadratic, cubic, etc.).
  - Nonparametric regression: The relationship between the variables is nonlinear and cannot be represented by a specific function, but rather by a flexible curve that adapts to the data.
- To perform a regression analysis, the following steps are usually involved:
  - Selecting the appropriate type of regression model based on the research question and the data characteristics.
  - Fitting the regression model to the data using a suitable method, such as the least squares method, the maximum likelihood method, etc.
  - Evaluating the quality and validity of the regression model using various criteria, such as the coefficient of determination (R-squared), the standard error of the estimate, the p-values and confidence intervals of the regression coefficients, the residual analysis, etc.
  - Interpreting the results of the regression model in terms of the research question and the domain context.
  - Reporting the results of the regression model using appropriate tables, graphs, and narratives.
