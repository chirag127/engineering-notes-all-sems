### Stochastic Search Methods

- Stochastic search methods are optimization techniques that use randomness in some way, either in the objective function or in the search algorithm.
- Stochastic search methods are useful for finding approximate solutions to complex problems that are hard to solve exactly or efficiently by deterministic methods.
- Some examples of stochastic search methods are:
  - Simulated annealing: a method that mimics the physical process of cooling a metal to find a low-energy state. It starts with a high temperature and a random solution, and gradually lowers the temperature while exploring the neighborhood of the current solution. It accepts worse solutions with some probability that depends on the temperature, to escape from local optima.
  - Genetic algorithms: a method that mimics the biological process of evolution to find a high-fitness solution. It starts with a population of random solutions, and iteratively applies genetic operators such as selection, crossover, and mutation to generate new solutions. It maintains a diversity of solutions and exploits the best ones to converge to a global optimum.
  - Particle swarm optimization: a method that mimics the social behavior of a flock of birds or a school of fish to find a high-quality solution. It starts with a swarm of particles, each with a position and a velocity, and iteratively updates them based on their own best position, the global best position, and some random factors. It balances exploration and exploitation to reach a consensus on the best solution.
- Some practical considerations when using stochastic search methods are:
  - Repeated evaluations: since stochastic search methods are based on randomness, they may produce different results in different runs. Therefore, it is advisable to repeat the search multiple times and compare the results to assess the robustness and reliability of the method.
  - Parameter tuning: stochastic search methods often have some parameters that control the behavior of the search, such as the temperature, the population size, the mutation rate, etc. These parameters may affect the performance and the convergence of the method, and they may need to be tuned for different problems or objectives.
  - Stopping criteria: stochastic search methods do not guarantee to find the optimal solution, and they may run indefinitely if there is no stopping criterion. Therefore, it is important to define some criteria to terminate the search, such as a maximum number of iterations, a minimum improvement threshold, a target value, etc.
