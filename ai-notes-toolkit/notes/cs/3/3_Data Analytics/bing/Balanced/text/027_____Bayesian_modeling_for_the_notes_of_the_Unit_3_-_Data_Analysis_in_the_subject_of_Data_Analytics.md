### Bayesian modeling

- Bayesian modeling is a statistical model where probability is influenced by the belief of the likelihood of a certain outcome.
- Bayesian modeling uses Bayes' theorem to compute and update probabilities after obtaining new data.
- Bayes' theorem describes the conditional probability of an event based on data as well as prior information or beliefs about the event or conditions related to the event.
- The general form of Bayes' theorem is:

    P(A|B) = P(B|A)P(A) / P(B)

    where A and B are events, P(A) and P(B) are the prior probabilities of A and B, P(A|B) and P(B|A) are the conditional probabilities of A given B and B given A, respectively.

- Bayesian modeling can be applied to various types of data analysis and parameter estimation problems, such as regression, classification, clustering, hypothesis testing, and decision making.
- Bayesian modeling can handle complex and uncertain situations by incorporating prior knowledge and data into a coherent framework.
- Bayesian modeling can also account for the uncertainty regarding the input (aka parameters) to the model, which are often unknown or estimated from data.
- Bayesian modeling can be implemented using various computational methods, such as Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), variational inference, and expectation propagation.
- Bayesian modeling can be extended to hierarchical models, where the parameters of the model are themselves modeled by another level of parameters, and so on.
- Bayesian modeling is a powerful tool for decision-making, as it can provide a quantitative assessment of a variety of complex risks associated with exposure to pollutants, for example.
