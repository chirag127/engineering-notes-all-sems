### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and meaningful way, usually using tables, charts, graphs, or dashboards.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and deriving insights from data, usually using statistical, mathematical, or logical methods.
- Reporting answers the question of what happened, while analysis answers the question of why it happened and what can be done about it.
- Reporting is often descriptive, historical, and factual, while analysis is often inferential, predictive, and actionable.
- Reporting is useful for monitoring performance, compliance, and trends, while analysis is useful for identifying problems, opportunities, and solutions.
- Reporting and analysis are complementary and interdependent processes that support data-driven decision making.
