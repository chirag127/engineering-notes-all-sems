### Visualization before analysis
- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information.
- Visualization can help to explore, understand, communicate, and present data or information in a meaningful and effective way.
- Visualization can also help to identify patterns, trends, outliers, relationships, and insights that might not be apparent from numerical or textual data alone.
- Visualization can be used before analysis to:
  - Explore the data and get a sense of its characteristics, such as distribution, shape, range, variability, and quality.
  - Generate hypotheses and questions about the data and its potential implications or insights.
  - Select appropriate analytical methods and techniques based on the data and the questions of interest.
  - Communicate and share the data and the questions with others, such as stakeholders, collaborators, or audiences.
- Visualization can be used during and after analysis to:
  - Monitor and evaluate the progress and results of the analysis, such as checking for errors, validity, and accuracy.
  - Refine and modify the analysis based on the feedback and insights from the visualization.
  - Compare and contrast different analytical methods, techniques, or outcomes.
  - Communicate and present the findings and recommendations of the analysis in a clear, concise, and compelling way.
- Visualization can be done using various tools, such as software, applications, libraries, or frameworks, that can create different types of visualizations, such as charts, graphs, maps, diagrams, dashboards, or interactive displays.
- Visualization can be done using various principles, such as design, aesthetics, cognition, perception, or storytelling, that can enhance the effectiveness, efficiency, and appeal of the visualization.
- Visualization can be done using various practices, such as data cleaning, transformation, aggregation, or filtering, that can prepare the data for the visualization.
- Visualization can be done using various challenges, such as data complexity, volume, variety, or velocity, that can affect the feasibility, scalability, or performance of the visualization.
