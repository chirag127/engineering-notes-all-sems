- Descriptive statistics are methods of summarizing and displaying data in a meaningful way, such as tables, charts, graphs, or numerical measures.
- Descriptive statistics can help to explore the characteristics of a data set, such as its shape, center, spread, outliers, or trends.
- Descriptive statistics can also help to compare different groups or variables within a data set, such as using side-by-side boxplots, scatterplots, or correlation coefficients.
- Some common descriptive statistics include:
  - Measures of central tendency, such as mean, median, or mode, which indicate the typical or most frequent value in a data set.
  - Measures of variability, such as range, standard deviation, or interquartile range, which indicate how much the data values vary or deviate from the center.
  - Measures of relative position, such as percentiles, quartiles, or z-scores, which indicate how a data value compares to the rest of the data set or to a standard distribution.
  - Measures of association, such as covariance, correlation, or regression, which indicate how two or more variables are related or influence each other.
- Some common graphical displays of descriptive statistics include:
  - Frequency tables and histograms, which show the distribution of a single categorical or numerical variable, respectively.
  - Bar charts and pie charts, which show the relative frequencies or proportions of a single categorical variable, respectively.
  - Dot plots and stem-and-leaf plots, which show the individual data values of a single numerical variable, respectively.
  - Boxplots and violin plots, which show the five-number summary (minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile, maximum) and the density of a single numerical variable, respectively.
  - Scatterplots and line plots, which show the relationship between two numerical variables, respectively.
  - Contingency tables and mosaic plots, which show the joint and marginal frequencies or proportions of two categorical variables, respectively.
  - Heat maps and choropleth maps, which show the distribution of a numerical variable across a geographical area, respectively.
