### Data Preparation for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Data Analytics Lifecycle

Data preparation is the process of transforming raw data into a suitable format for analysis. It involves tasks such as accessing, loading, structuring, cleansing, unifying, and adjusting data to ensure its quality and consistency. Data preparation is an essential stage in the data analytics lifecycle, which is a systematic approach to conducting a data analytics project. The data analytics lifecycle consists of six phases:

- **Phase 1: Business Understanding**. This phase involves defining the business problem, the objectives, and the success criteria of the project. It also involves identifying the stakeholders, the data sources, and the analytical methods to be used.
- **Phase 2: Data Understanding**. This phase involves collecting, exploring, and describing the data to gain insights into its characteristics, quality, and potential issues. It also involves identifying the relevant variables, the relationships, and the patterns in the data.
- **Phase 3: Data Preparation**. This phase involves transforming the data into a suitable format for analysis. It includes tasks such as:

  - **Accessing and loading data**. This involves retrieving data from various sources, such as databases, files, web services, or APIs, and loading it into a scalable platform, such as an analytical sandbox, where it can be processed and analyzed.
  - **Structuring data**. This involves organizing data into a logical and consistent structure, such as tables, columns, rows, and keys, that facilitates analysis and querying. It also involves converting data into appropriate data types, such as numeric, categorical, or text, and applying data formats, such as dates, currencies, or units.
  - **Cleansing data**. This involves detecting and correcting errors, outliers, missing values, duplicates, or inconsistencies in the data that may affect the quality and validity of the analysis. It also involves applying data quality rules, such as validation, standardization, or normalization, to ensure the data conforms to the business requirements and expectations.
  - **Unifying data**. This involves integrating and combining data from different sources, formats, or structures into a single and coherent data set. It also involves resolving data conflicts, such as different identifiers, labels, or definitions, and applying data transformations, such as aggregation, filtering, or joining, to create new variables or features.
  - **Adjusting data**. This involves modifying or enhancing the data to improve its suitability for analysis. It also involves applying data techniques, such as scaling, encoding, discretization, or imputation, to handle data issues, such as skewed distributions, high dimensionality, or missing values.

- **Phase 4: Modeling**. This phase involves applying analytical methods, such as statistics, machine learning, or optimization, to the prepared data to create models that can answer the business questions or solve the business problems. It also involves selecting, testing, and evaluating the models to ensure they meet the performance and accuracy criteria.
- **Phase 5: Evaluation**. This phase involves assessing the results and the outcomes of the models to determine their validity, reliability, and usefulness for the business objectives. It also involves comparing the models with the success criteria, the baseline, or the benchmarks, and identifying the strengths, weaknesses, and limitations of the models.
- **Phase 6: Deployment**. This phase involves implementing the models into the business processes, systems, or applications, where they can provide value and insights to the stakeholders and the decision-makers. It also involves monitoring, maintaining, and updating the models to ensure they remain relevant, accurate, and effective over time.

Data preparation is a critical and time-consuming phase in the data analytics lifecycle, as it affects the quality and the outcome of the subsequent phases. Therefore, data preparation requires careful planning, execution, and documentation, as well as the use of appropriate tools, techniques, and best practices. Data preparation also requires collaboration and communication among the data analysts, the data scientists, the data engineers, and the business users, to ensure the data meets the needs and the expectations of the project.
