### Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics are numerical or graphical methods that summarize and display the characteristics of a data set. They help to describe and understand the features of a data set, such as its center, spread, shape, and outliers. Descriptive statistics do not make any inference or prediction about the population from which the data are drawn. They are often used to explore and present data before conducting further analysis.

Some of the common types of descriptive statistics are:

- **Measures of central tendency**: These are statistics that describe the center or typical value of a data set. They include the mean, median, and mode. The mean is the average of all the values in the data set. The median is the middle value of the data set when it is sorted in ascending or descending order. The mode is the most frequent value in the data set.
- **Measures of variability**: These are statistics that describe the spread or variation of the data set. They include the range, interquartile range, standard deviation, and variance. The range is the difference between the maximum and minimum values in the data set. The interquartile range is the difference between the third and first quartiles of the data set, which divide the data into four equal parts. The standard deviation is the average distance of the values from the mean. The variance is the square of the standard deviation.
- **Frequency distribution**: This is a table or graph that shows the frequency or count of each value or category in the data set. It can be used to display the shape and outliers of the data set. A frequency distribution can be presented as a frequency table, a histogram, a bar chart, a pie chart, or a stem-and-leaf plot.
