- Learning is the process of finding patterns or relationships in data that can be used to make predictions, decisions, or actions.
- Generalisation is the ability of a learning model to perform well on new or unseen data that is not part of the training data.
- Learning and generalisation are related but not the same. A model that learns well on the training data may not generalise well on the test data, and vice versa. This is known as the trade-off between bias and variance.
- Bias is the error that results from the model's assumptions or simplifications of the data. A model with high bias may not capture the complexity or variability of the data, and may underfit the data.
- Variance is the error that results from the model's sensitivity or instability to the data. A model with high variance may capture the noise or randomness of the data, and may overfit the data.
- The goal of learning and generalisation is to find a model that has low bias and low variance, or a good balance between them. This is known as the optimal complexity or the sweet spot of the model.
- There are different methods or techniques to improve the learning and generalisation performance of a model, such as cross-validation, regularisation, feature selection, dimensionality reduction, ensemble methods, etc.
