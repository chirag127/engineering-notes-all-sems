# Analysis vs Reporting

- Analysis and reporting are two different processes that are often confused or used interchangeably in data analytics.
- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and understandable format, such as tables, charts, dashboards, etc. Reporting aims to answer questions like what, when, where, and how much.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and explaining data to find patterns, trends, insights, and recommendations. Analysis aims to answer questions like why, how, what if, and what next.
- Reporting is usually descriptive, meaning it summarizes what has happened in the past or present. Analysis is usually predictive, meaning it forecasts what will happen in the future or prescriptive, meaning it suggests what actions to take.
- Reporting is usually based on predefined metrics, dimensions, and filters that are relevant to the business context. Analysis is usually based on hypotheses, assumptions, and models that are tested and validated with data.
- Reporting is usually static, meaning it does not change unless the data source is updated. Analysis is usually dynamic, meaning it can be modified or refined based on new data or feedback.
- Reporting is usually standardized, meaning it follows a consistent format and structure that can be easily understood and compared. Analysis is usually customized, meaning it varies depending on the data, problem, and audience.
- Reporting is usually periodic, meaning it is done at regular intervals, such as daily, weekly, monthly, etc. Analysis is usually ad hoc, meaning it is done on demand or as needed.
- Reporting is usually retrospective, meaning it looks at the past performance or results. Analysis is usually prospective, meaning it looks at the future potential or opportunities.
