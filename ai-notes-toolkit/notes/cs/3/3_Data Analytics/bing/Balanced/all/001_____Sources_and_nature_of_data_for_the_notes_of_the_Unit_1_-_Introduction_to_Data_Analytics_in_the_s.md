# Sources and nature of data

- Data is any type of information that can be measured, accessed or used in any way in a given study.
- Data is the backbone of any data analysis work done in the research process.
- Data can be gathered from two places: internal and external sources.
- Internal sources are those that are within the organization or the researcher's own records, such as sales data, customer feedback, employee surveys, etc.
- External sources are those that are outside the organization or the researcher's own records, such as government publications, industry reports, online databases, social media, etc.
- The sources of data can be classified into two types: statistical and non-statistical .
- Statistical sources refer to data that is gathered for some official purposes, such as censuses, and officially administered surveys .
- Non-statistical sources refer to the collection of data for other administrative purposes or for the private sector, such as company records, market research, media reports, etc .
- The nature of data can be classified into two types: qualitative and quantitative.
- Qualitative data refers to data that is descriptive, subjective, and non-numerical, such as opinions, feelings, attitudes, etc.
- Quantitative data refers to data that is numerical, objective, and measurable, such as counts, percentages, ratios, etc.
- Data can also be classified into two types based on the time of collection: primary and secondary.
- Primary data refers to data that is collected by the researcher for the first time and for a specific purpose, such as interviews, questionnaires, experiments, etc.
- Secondary data refers to data that is already collected by someone else and for a different purpose, such as books, journals, websites, etc.
