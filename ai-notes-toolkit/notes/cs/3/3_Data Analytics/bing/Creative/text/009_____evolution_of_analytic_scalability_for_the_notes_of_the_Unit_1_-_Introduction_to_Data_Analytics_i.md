### Evolution of analytic scalability

- Analytic scalability refers to the ability to handle large and complex data sets for analysis purposes.
- In the past, analytic scalability was limited by the constraints of the data environment, such as the storage capacity, processing speed, and data integration challenges.
- To overcome these limitations, analysts had to pull the data together in a separate analytics environment and then perform analysis, often using sampling or aggregation techniques to reduce the data size.
- However, this approach had several drawbacks, such as the loss of information, the difficulty of merging data from different sources, and the inconsistency of results.
- With the advent of big data, the need for analytic scalability increased exponentially, as the volume, variety, velocity, and veracity of data grew beyond the capabilities of traditional data environments.
- To address this need, several new paradigms emerged that enabled greater scalability and efficiency for analytics, such as:
  - The convergence of the analytic and data environments, which means that analytics can be performed directly on the data without moving or copying it, thus reducing the latency and complexity of analysis  .
  - The massively parallel processing (MPP) architectures, which use multiple nodes or servers to distribute the data and the computation, thus increasing the scalability and performance of analytics  .
  - The cloud computing, which provides on-demand access to scalable and elastic resources for data storage and analytics, thus reducing the cost and complexity of managing the data environment  .
  - The grid computing, which uses a network of computers to share the data and the computation, thus enabling distributed and parallel analytics across multiple locations  .
  - The MapReduce framework, which is a programming model that simplifies the processing of large and unstructured data sets by dividing the task into two phases: map and reduce, which can be executed in parallel on multiple nodes  .
- These paradigms have enabled new levels of analytic scalability and have opened up new possibilities for discovering insights from big data  .
- However, they also pose new challenges and require new skills and processes for analysts, such as the understanding of the data architecture, the optimization of the data and the code, the validation of the results, and the deployment of the analytics  .
- Therefore, the evolution of analytic scalability is an ongoing process that requires constant adaptation and innovation from the analysts and the organizations  .
