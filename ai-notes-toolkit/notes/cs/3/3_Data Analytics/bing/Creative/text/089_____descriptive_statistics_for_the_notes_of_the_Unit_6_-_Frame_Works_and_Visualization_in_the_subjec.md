### Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics is a branch of statistics that summarizes or describes the characteristics of a data set. It does not make any inference or prediction about the population that the data set represents, but only provides information about the sample itself. Descriptive statistics can be used to organize, visualize, and communicate data in a meaningful way.

Descriptive statistics consists of three basic categories of measures:

- **Measures of central tendency**: These measures describe the center or typical value of the data set. They include the mean, median, and mode.
- **Measures of variability**: These measures describe the spread or dispersion of the data set. They include the range, standard deviation, variance, and interquartile range.
- **Frequency distribution**: This is a table or graph that shows how often each value or category occurs in the data set. It can be represented by a frequency table, a histogram, a bar chart, a pie chart, or a stem-and-leaf plot.

Some examples of descriptive statistics are:

- The average height of students in a class is 170 cm.
- The median income of households in a city is $50,000.
- The mode of colors of cars in a parking lot is blue.
- The range of test scores in a math exam is 40 points.
- The standard deviation of blood pressure readings in a clinic is 15 mmHg.
- The variance of monthly sales in a store is $10,000.
- The frequency distribution of grades in a course is shown in the following table:

| Grade | Frequency |
|-------|-----------|
| A     | 10        |
| B     | 15        |
| C     | 20        |
| D     | 5         |
| F     | 0         |

Descriptive statistics can help us understand the distribution, shape, and outliers of a data set. They can also help us compare different data sets or groups. Descriptive statistics are often used in exploratory data analysis, data visualization, and data presentation.
