### Visualization before analysis

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information to communicate insights, patterns, trends, or relationships.
- Visualization can be used before analysis to explore the data, understand its characteristics, identify potential problems or outliers, and generate hypotheses or questions for further investigation.
- Visualization can also be used after analysis to present the results, findings, or conclusions of the analysis in a clear, concise, and compelling way.
- Visualization can help to:
  - Enhance the understanding of complex or large data sets
  - Simplify the comparison of different data sets or variables
  - Highlight the most important or relevant information
  - Reveal hidden patterns or structures in the data
  - Stimulate curiosity and interest in the data
  - Support decision making and problem solving
- Visualization can be done using various tools, techniques, and methods, such as:
  - Charts, graphs, maps, diagrams, tables, dashboards, etc.
  - Colors, shapes, sizes, symbols, labels, legends, etc.
  - Interactivity, animation, zooming, filtering, etc.
  - Data transformation, aggregation, normalization, etc.
  - Data storytelling, narration, annotation, etc.
- Visualization requires careful consideration of the following aspects:
  - The purpose and audience of the visualization
  - The type and format of the data
  - The appropriate visual elements and design principles
  - The ethical and legal implications of the visualization
  - The evaluation and feedback of the visualization
