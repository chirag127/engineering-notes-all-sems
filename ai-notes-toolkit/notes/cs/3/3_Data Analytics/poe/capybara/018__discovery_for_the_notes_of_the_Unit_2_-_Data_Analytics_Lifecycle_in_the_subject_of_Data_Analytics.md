### Discovery

In the data analytics lifecycle, the discovery stage is crucial for understanding the business problem and identifying the data sources that can help solve it. Here are some key points to keep in mind during the discovery stage:

- **Define the problem:** Start by clearly defining the business problem that needs to be solved. This will help in identifying the relevant data sources and defining the scope of the analysis.
- **Identify the data sources:** Once the problem is defined, the next step is to identify the data sources that can provide insights into the problem. This may involve gathering data from internal databases, third-party sources, or even social media platforms.
- **Assess data quality:** Before proceeding with the analysis, it is important to assess the quality of the data. This includes checking for completeness, accuracy, and consistency. Data that is incomplete or inaccurate can lead to incorrect insights and analysis.
- **Explore the data:** Once the data sources are identified and assessed, it's time to explore the data. This involves looking for patterns, trends, and relationships in the data. Data visualization tools can be helpful in identifying these patterns.
- **Refine the problem:** Based on the insights gained from exploring the data, it may be necessary to refine the business problem. This can help in identifying new data sources or focusing on a specific aspect of the problem.
- **Document the findings:** Finally, it is important to document the findings from the discovery stage. This includes summarizing the insights gained from exploring the data and any refinements made to the business problem. This documentation will serve as a reference point for the rest of the data analytics lifecycle.
