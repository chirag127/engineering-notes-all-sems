### Bayesian modeling for the notes of the Unit 3 - Data Analysis in the subject of Data Analytics.

1. Bayesian modeling is a statistical modeling approach that uses Bayes' theorem to update the probability for a hypothesis as additional evidence or information becomes available.
2. It allows you to combine prior beliefs about a hypothesis with observed evidence to calculate a "posterior" probability. The posterior probability is a conditional probability that represents the likelihood of the hypothesis given the evidence.
3. The key components of Bayesian modeling are:
- Prior distribution: The initial probabilities of the hypotheses. It encodes your initial beliefs about the hypotheses.
- Likelihood function: The probability of observing the evidence given each hypothesis. It is used to update the prior distribution based on the observed evidence.
- Posterior distribution: The updated probability distribution over the hypotheses after combining the prior distribution and the likelihood function. It incorporates the observed evidence to calculate the likelihood of each hypothesis.
4. Some advantages of Bayesian modeling are:
- It allows you to include your initial beliefs in the modeling process.
- It provides a coherent framework to update beliefs in the face of new evidence.
- It produces probability distributions over hypotheses that quantify uncertainty.
- It can be applied sequentially as more data becomes available to continually refine the posterior distribution.
