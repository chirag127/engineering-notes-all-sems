
### Analysis vs. Reporting

1. Analysis is the process of examining data to draw conclusions and identify patterns. It involves the use of techniques such as statistical modeling, data mining, and machine learning to identify underlying trends and relationships in the data.
2. Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured way for the purpose of communicating information. Reports typically include visuals such as charts and graphs to make the data easier to understand.
3. Analysis is used to answer questions such as “What is driving sales?” or “What is the most efficient way to reach customers?”
4. Reporting is used to present the results of analysis in a format that can be easily understood and acted upon.
5. Analysis can be used to identify opportunities and risks, while reporting is used to communicate those opportunities and risks to stakeholders.
6. Analysis requires a deep understanding of the data and the ability to identify meaningful patterns, while reporting requires an understanding of how to present data in a way that is easy to understand.
