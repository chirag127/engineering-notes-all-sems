### History of Web and Internet for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Web Technology

1. Introduction to Networks and The Internet
- In the 1960s, the US government funded research projects to build robust and fault-tolerant computer networks. This led to the development of the Internet Protocol (IP) and transmission control protocol (TCP) that form the basis of the Internet.
- In the 1970s, the US government created the first experimental TCP/IP network called ARPAnet. It connected major universities and research centers. Email and file transfer applications were developed during this time.
- In the 1980s, the US National Science Foundation (NSF) created NSFNET and introduced the first high-speed network backbone. This led to growth of the network and development of remote login and network news applications.

2. Introduction of the World Wide Web
- In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web while at CERN.
- He created the first web browser computer program in 1990, and the first web server and the first web pages in 1991.
- The web's growth was initially slow but started accelerating after the introduction of the Mosaic browser in 1993 and Netscape Navigator in 1994.
- The introduction of HTML, HTTP, and URL standards paved the way for the web as we know it today. Web services and ecommerce applications were soon developed using these technologies.

3. Growth and Commercialization of the Internet
- In the 1990s, the Internet and the web gained commercial interest and witnessed exponential growth.
- The NSFNET was decommissioned in 1995 removing the last restrictions on the use of the Internet for commercial purposes.
- Web browsers like Internet Explorer and Mozilla Firefox were introduced making the web more visually appealing and usable.
- This decade saw the burst of the dot-com bubble and the growth of ecommerce websites like Amazon and eBay.
- Social networks and blogging platforms gained popularity in the 2000s and Wikipedia was launched in 2001.
