## Unit 1 - Introduction

- In this unit, you will learn about the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI).
- AI is the study of how to create machines and systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, perception, decision making, and natural language processing.
- AI can be divided into two main branches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI.
- Symbolic AI is based on the idea of using symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge. Examples of symbolic AI include expert systems, logic programming, and knowledge representation and reasoning.
- Sub-symbolic AI is based on the idea of using numerical and statistical methods to model and learn from data. Examples of sub-symbolic AI include neural networks, evolutionary algorithms, and machine learning.
- AI can also be classified into different types according to the level of intelligence and the domain of application. Some of the common types are:
  - Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI): AI that can perform a specific task or a narrow range of tasks at or above human level, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages.
  - Artificial General Intelligence (AGI): AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can, such as understanding and reasoning about the world, communicating with natural language, and exhibiting creativity and self-awareness.
  - Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI): AI that can surpass human intelligence in all aspects, such as solving problems, inventing new technologies, and creating new forms of art and culture.
  - Weak AI: AI that only simulates human intelligence, but does not actually possess it. Weak AI systems are designed to perform specific tasks, but do not have any understanding or awareness of what they are doing or why they are doing it.
  - Strong AI: AI that actually has human-like intelligence, and can understand and explain its own actions and goals. Strong AI systems are not only able to perform tasks, but also to learn from their experiences, reason about their environment, and communicate with other agents.
  - Applied AI: AI that is used for practical purposes, such as solving real-world problems, providing services, or enhancing human capabilities. Applied AI systems are often domain-specific, and can be based on either symbolic or sub-symbolic AI techniques.
  - General AI: AI that is not limited to a specific domain, but can adapt to different situations and tasks. General AI systems are often based on symbolic AI techniques, and aim to achieve human-like intelligence and versatility.
- AI is an interdisciplinary field that draws from various disciplines, such as computer science, mathematics, logic, psychology, philosophy, linguistics, neuroscience, and engineering.
- AI has many applications and benefits for society, such as improving health care, education, entertainment, security, and productivity. However, AI also poses some challenges and risks, such as ethical, social, legal, and moral issues, as well as the potential for misuse, abuse, and harm.
