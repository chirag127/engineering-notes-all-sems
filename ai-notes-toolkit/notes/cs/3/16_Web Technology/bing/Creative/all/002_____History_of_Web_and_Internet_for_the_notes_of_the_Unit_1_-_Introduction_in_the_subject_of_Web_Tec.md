# History of Web and Internet

- The **Internet** is a system of interconnected computer networks that use a common set of protocols to communicate and exchange data. It originated in the **1970s** as a project of the US Department of Defense, called **ARPANET**, which aimed to create a resilient and decentralized network for military and research purposes.
- The **World Wide Web** (WWW) is an application that runs on the Internet and allows users to access and share information using **hypertext** documents, which can contain text, images, audio, video, and links to other resources. The WWW was invented in **1989** by **Tim Berners-Lee**, a British scientist working at **CERN**, a European research organization based in Geneva, Switzerland .
- Berners-Lee created the first **web browser** and **web server** software, as well as the first **web page**, which described the project and its goals. He also developed the **HyperText Transfer Protocol (HTTP)**, which defines how web browsers and servers communicate, and the **HyperText Markup Language (HTML)**, which defines the structure and formatting of web pages .
- Berners-Lee made his invention freely available to the public, without any patents or royalties, in order to encourage its widespread adoption and development. He also founded the **World Wide Web Consortium (W3C)**, an international organization that sets standards and guidelines for the web .
- The WWW became popular in the **early 1990s**, with the introduction of more user-friendly web browsers, such as **Mosaic** and **Netscape Navigator**, and the emergence of various web services, such as **search engines**, **online shopping**, **social media**, and **online education**. The WWW has since become a global phenomenon, with billions of users and websites, and a significant impact on society, culture, economy, and politics .
