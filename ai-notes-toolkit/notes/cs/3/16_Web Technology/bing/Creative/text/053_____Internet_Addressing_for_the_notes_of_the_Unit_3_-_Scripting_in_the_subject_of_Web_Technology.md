### Internet Addressing

- Internet addressing is the process of assigning a unique identifier to each device or host that communicates over the internet.
- Internet addresses are used by the Internet Protocol (IP) and other higher-level protocols to route packets through the network.
- Internet addresses are also known as IP addresses, and they consist of four numbers separated by dots in IPv4, or eight groups of four hexadecimal digits separated by colons in IPv6.
- Each IP address is associated with virtual ports in a computer that act as a doorway that allows web applications or websites to send and receive data or information on your device.
- The internet is made up of interconnected large networks that are each responsible for certain blocks of IP addresses; these large networks are known as autonomous systems (AS).
- A variety of routing protocols, such as Border Gateway Protocol (BGP), help route packets across ASes based on their destination IP addresses.
- Internet addresses can be classified into two types: static and dynamic.
  - Static IP addresses are fixed and do not change over time. They are usually assigned manually or by a network administrator. Static IP addresses are useful for hosting servers, websites, or other services that need to be accessed by a consistent address.
  - Dynamic IP addresses are assigned automatically by a server or a router using a protocol such as Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol (DHCP). Dynamic IP addresses can change over time, depending on the availability of the address pool and the lease duration. Dynamic IP addresses are useful for devices that do not need a permanent address, such as laptops, smartphones, or tablets.
