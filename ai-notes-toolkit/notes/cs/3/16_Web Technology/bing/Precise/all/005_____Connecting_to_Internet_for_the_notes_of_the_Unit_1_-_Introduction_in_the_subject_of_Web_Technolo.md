### Connecting to Internet

Unit 1 - Introduction

Web Technology

1. The Internet is a global network of interconnected computer networks that communicate using the standard Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP).
2. To connect to the Internet, a device must have access to a network that is connected to the Internet.
3. This can be achieved through various means, such as a wired connection (e.g. Ethernet), a wireless connection (e.g. Wi-Fi), or a mobile data connection (e.g. 3G, 4G, 5G).
4. Once connected, the device can access the Internet and its resources, such as websites, email, and online services.
5. Internet Service Providers (ISPs) provide access to the Internet by connecting their customers to their network, which is connected to the Internet.
6. ISPs may offer various types of Internet connections, such as broadband, DSL, or dial-up, with varying speeds and data allowances.
7. To access the Internet, a device must have the appropriate hardware and software, such as a network interface card and a web browser.
8. The device must also be configured with the appropriate network settings, such as an IP address and DNS server addresses.
9. Once connected, the device can communicate with other devices on the Internet, send and receive data, and access online services and resources.
