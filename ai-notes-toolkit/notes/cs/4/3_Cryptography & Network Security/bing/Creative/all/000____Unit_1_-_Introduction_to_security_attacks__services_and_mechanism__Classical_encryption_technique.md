## Unit 1 - Introduction to security attacks, services and mechanism, Classical encryption techniquessubstitution ciphers and transposition ciphers, cryptanalysis, steganography, Stream and block ciphers. - Security attacks are any actions that compromise the security of information systems or networks, such as unauthorized access, modification, or destruction of data or resources.
- Security services are the functions that provide protection to the information systems or networks, such as confidentiality, integrity, authentication, non-repudiation, or availability.
- Security mechanisms are the methods or tools that implement the security services, such as encryption, digital signatures, firewalls, or access control.
- Classical encryption techniques are the methods of transforming plaintext into ciphertext using simple mathematical operations, such as substitution or transposition.
- Substitution ciphers are the methods of replacing each letter or symbol in the plaintext with another letter or symbol, according to a fixed rule or key, such as Caesar cipher or Vigenere cipher.
- Transposition ciphers are the methods of rearranging the order of the letters or symbols in the plaintext, according to a fixed rule or key, such as rail fence cipher or columnar transposition cipher.
- Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking the encryption schemes, by finding the key or the plaintext from the ciphertext, using various techniques, such as frequency analysis, brute force, or chosen plaintext attack.
- Steganography is the art of hiding the existence of a message within another message or medium, such as an image, a sound, or a text, using various techniques, such as least significant bit, masking, or embedding.
- Stream ciphers are the methods of encrypting each bit or character of the plaintext with a bit or character of a keystream, which is generated by a pseudorandom number generator, such as RC4 or A5/1.
- Block ciphers are the methods of encrypting a fixed-length block of plaintext with a fixed-length block of ciphertext, using a secret key, such as DES or AES.
- Block cipher principles are the basic concepts that govern the design and analysis of block ciphers, such as confusion, diffusion, round function, key schedule, or avalanche effect.
- Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion is a framework that describes the desirable properties of a secure cipher, such as making the relationship between the plaintext, the ciphertext, and the key as complex and unpredictable as possible, and spreading the influence of each bit of the plaintext or the key over many bits of the ciphertext.
- Fiestal structure is a common way of implementing a block cipher, which consists of several rounds of processing, each involving a substitution and a permutation operation, such as S-boxes and P-boxes, and a round key derived from the secret key, such as in DES or AES.
- Data encryption standard (DES) is a widely used symmetric block cipher, which encrypts a 64-bit block of plaintext with a 56-bit key, using 16 rounds of fiestal structure, and has a total of 2^56 possible keys.
- Strength of DES is the measure of how resistant DES is to cryptanalysis, which depends on the key size, the block size, the number of rounds, and the design of the S-boxes and the P-boxes. DES is considered insecure today, as it can be broken by brute force or differential cryptanalysis in a reasonable time.
- Idea of differential cryptanalysis is a technique of breaking a block cipher, by analyzing the differences between pairs of plaintexts and ciphertexts, and finding a differential characteristic that has a high probability of occurring, and then using it to recover the key or some subkeys, such as in DES or AES.
- Block cipher modes of operation are the methods of using a block cipher to encrypt or decrypt a message that is longer than the block size, such as electronic codebook (ECB), cipher block chaining (CBC), cipher feedback (CFB), output feedback (OFB), or counter (CTR).
- Triple DES is a variant of DES, which encrypts a 64-bit block of plaintext with three 56-bit keys, using three rounds of DES encryption and decryption, and has a total of 2^168 possible keys. Triple DES is more secure than DES, but also slower and less efficient.
