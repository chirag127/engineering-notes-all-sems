# Introduction to security attacks

- Security attacks are attempts to compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information or resources in a system.
- Security attacks can be classified into two categories: passive attacks and active attacks.
- Passive attacks are those that do not alter the data or the system, but only observe or analyze the data or the system. Examples of passive attacks are eavesdropping, traffic analysis, or ciphertext-only attacks.
- Active attacks are those that modify the data or the system, or create false data or messages. Examples of active attacks are replay, modification, deletion, insertion, fabrication, or man-in-the-middle attacks.
- Security attacks can also be classified into four types based on the origin and the target of the attack: interception, interruption, modification, or fabrication.
- Interception is an attack where an unauthorized party gains access to the data or the system. Examples of interception are eavesdropping, wiretapping, or sniffing.
- Interruption is an attack where an authorized party loses access to the data or the system. Examples of interruption are denial-of-service, jamming, or cutting.
- Modification is an attack where an unauthorized party alters the data or the system. Examples of modification are tampering, changing, or rewriting.
- Fabrication is an attack where an unauthorized party creates false data or messages. Examples of fabrication are spoofing, impersonation, or forgery.

# Security services and mechanisms

- Security services are the goals or objectives that a system should achieve to protect itself from security attacks. Security services can be classified into five categories: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and non-repudiation.
- Confidentiality is the service that ensures that the data or the system is accessible only to the authorized parties. Examples of confidentiality are encryption, access control, or hiding.
- Integrity is the service that ensures that the data or the system is not modified by unauthorized parties or in an unauthorized manner. Examples of integrity are checksum, digital signature, or hash.
- Availability is the service that ensures that the data or the system is available to the authorized parties when needed. Examples of availability are redundancy, backup, or recovery.
- Authentication is the service that ensures that the parties involved in a communication or a transaction are who they claim to be. Examples of authentication are password, biometric, or certificate.
- Non-repudiation is the service that ensures that the parties involved in a communication or a transaction cannot deny their participation or their actions. Examples of non-repudiation are digital signature, timestamp, or audit.

- Security mechanisms are the methods or tools that are used to implement the security services. Security mechanisms can be classified into two categories: preventive mechanisms and detective mechanisms.
- Preventive mechanisms are those that prevent or deter security attacks from happening. Examples of preventive mechanisms are encryption, access control, or firewall.
- Detective mechanisms are those that detect or identify security attacks that have happened or are happening. Examples of detective mechanisms are checksum, digital signature, or intrusion detection system.
