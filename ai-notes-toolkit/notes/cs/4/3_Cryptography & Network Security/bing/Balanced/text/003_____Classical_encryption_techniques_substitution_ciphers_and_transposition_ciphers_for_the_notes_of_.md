### Classical encryption techniquessubstitution ciphers and transposition ciphers

- Classical encryption techniques are based on two basic components: substitution and transposition.
- Substitution ciphers replace each plaintext symbol with a different ciphertext symbol, according to a fixed rule or key. For example, Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, polyalphabetic cipher, etc.
- Transposition ciphers rearrange the order of the plaintext symbols, according to a fixed rule or key. For example, rail fence cipher, columnar transposition cipher, etc.
- Cryptanalysis is the art of breaking encryption schemes, by finding the plaintext or the key from the ciphertext and some knowledge of the encryption algorithm.
- Steganography is the art of hiding information in other information, such as images, audio, video, etc. The goal is to conceal the existence of the secret message, rather than its content.
- Stream ciphers encrypt each plaintext symbol with a different keystream symbol, which is generated by a pseudorandom process. The keystream can be synchronous or self-synchronizing. For example, one-time pad, RC4, etc.
- Block ciphers encrypt each plaintext block with the same key, using a complex mathematical function. The block size is usually fixed, such as 64 bits or 128 bits. For example, DES, AES, etc.
