# Prime and Relative Prime Numbers

- A **prime number** is a whole number greater than 1 whose only factors are 1 and itself. For example, 2, 3, 5, and 7 are prime numbers .
- A **composite number** is a whole number greater than 1 that has more than two factors. For example, 4, 6, 8, and 9 are composite numbers.
- Two numbers are **relatively prime** or **coprime** or **mutually prime** when they have no common factors other than 1. In other words, no number other than 1 can divide them both exactly (without any remainder)   . For example, 8 and 15 are relatively prime because their only common factor is 1.
- Two numbers are **not relatively prime** when they have at least one common factor other than 1. For example, 12 and 18 are not relatively prime because they have common factors 2, 3, and 6.
- Any two prime numbers are always relatively prime. For example, 19 and 17 are relatively prime and prime numbers too .
- A prime number is relatively prime with any other number because prime numbers are the numbers that can be divided by one or themselves. Thus, if we pair up any prime number with other numbers the result will be relatively prime because the common factor will be one .
- Some properties of relatively prime numbers are:
  - If n is a prime number, then all integers less than or equal to n are relatively prime to n.
  - If a and b are two relatively prime numbers, then a + b is relatively prime to a × b.
  - If a and b are relatively prime numbers, then HCF (a, b) = 1 and LCM (a, b) = a × b.
