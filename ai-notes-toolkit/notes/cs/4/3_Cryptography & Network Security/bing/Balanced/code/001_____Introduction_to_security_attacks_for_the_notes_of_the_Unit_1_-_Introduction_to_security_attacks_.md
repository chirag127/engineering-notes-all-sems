### Introduction to security attacks

- Security attacks are any actions that compromise the security goals of confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity, or non-repudiation of information or systems.
- Security attacks can be classified into two categories: passive attacks and active attacks.
- Passive attacks are those that do not alter the data or system, but only observe or analyze it. Examples of passive attacks are eavesdropping, traffic analysis, or ciphertext-only attacks.
- Active attacks are those that modify the data or system, or create false data or messages. Examples of active attacks are replay, modification, deletion, insertion, fabrication, or man-in-the-middle attacks.
- Security attacks can also be classified based on the source of the attack: insider attacks or outsider attacks.
- Insider attacks are those that are launched by authorized users or entities within the system or network. Examples of insider attacks are disgruntled employees, malicious insiders, or compromised accounts.
- Outsider attacks are those that are launched by unauthorized users or entities outside the system or network. Examples of outsider attacks are hackers, cybercriminals, or state-sponsored actors.
- Security attacks can have various impacts on the system or network, such as data loss, data corruption, data leakage, denial of service, unauthorized access, identity theft, or reputation damage.
