- Security attacks: Any action that compromises the security of information owned by an organization or an individual. Security attacks can be classified into two categories: passive attacks and active attacks. Passive attacks are those that do not affect the normal functioning of the system, but only attempt to eavesdrop or monitor the communication. Active attacks are those that alter or disrupt the normal functioning of the system, such as modifying, deleting, or injecting data, or denying service.
- Security services: The services that are intended to counter security attacks and provide a secure communication environment. Security services can be classified into five categories: confidentiality, integrity, authentication, non-repudiation, and availability. Confidentiality is the service that ensures that the information is accessible only to those who are authorized to access it. Integrity is the service that ensures that the information is not modified or corrupted by unauthorized parties. Authentication is the service that verifies the identity of the parties involved in the communication. Non-repudiation is the service that prevents the parties from denying their involvement in the communication. Availability is the service that ensures that the information and the resources are accessible to the authorized parties whenever needed.
- Security mechanisms: The methods or tools that are used to implement security services. Security mechanisms can be classified into two categories: preventive mechanisms and detective mechanisms. Preventive mechanisms are those that prevent security attacks from happening, such as encryption, digital signatures, access control, firewalls, etc. Detective mechanisms are those that detect security attacks after they have happened, such as intrusion detection systems, audit trails, checksums, etc.
- Classical encryption techniques: The techniques that are based on the substitution and transposition of plaintext characters or bits to produce ciphertext. Substitution ciphers are those that replace each plaintext character or bit with a different one, according to a predefined rule or key. Transposition ciphers are those that rearrange the order of the plaintext characters or bits, according to a predefined rule or key. Cryptanalysis is the process of breaking or analyzing encryption techniques, by exploiting the weaknesses or patterns in the ciphertext or the encryption algorithm. Steganography is the technique of hiding information within another information, such as an image, a sound, or a text, without altering the appearance or the meaning of the cover information.
- Stream and block ciphers: The techniques that are based on the encryption of plaintext bits or bytes in a continuous or discrete manner, respectively. Stream ciphers are those that encrypt each plaintext bit or byte with a different key bit or byte, generated by a pseudorandom key stream. Block ciphers are those that encrypt each plaintext block of fixed size with the same key, using a complex mathematical function. Stream ciphers are faster and simpler than block ciphers, but are more vulnerable to attacks such as replay, insertion, or deletion. Block ciphers are more secure and flexible than stream ciphers, but are more complex and slower.
- Modern block ciphers: The block ciphers that are based on the principles of confusion and diffusion, fiestal structure, and multiple rounds of encryption. Confusion and diffusion are the properties of encryption that make the relationship between the plaintext and the ciphertext as complex and random as possible, to resist cryptanalysis. Confusion is achieved by using substitution operations, such as S-boxes, that map each input bit or byte to a different output bit or byte. Diffusion is achieved by using transposition operations, such as P-boxes, that permute the order of the input bits or bytes. Fiestal structure is a generic structure of block ciphers that consists of multiple rounds of encryption, each round consisting of a substitution layer, a transposition layer, and a key mixing layer. Data encryption standard (DES) is a widely used block cipher that has a block size of 64 bits and a key size of 56 bits. It uses 16 rounds of encryption, each round consisting of an S-box, a P-box, and a key mixing operation. Strength of DES is measured by its resistance to various attacks, such as brute force, differential cryptanalysis, linear cryptanalysis, etc. DES is considered to be insecure due to its small key size and its vulnerability to some attacks.
