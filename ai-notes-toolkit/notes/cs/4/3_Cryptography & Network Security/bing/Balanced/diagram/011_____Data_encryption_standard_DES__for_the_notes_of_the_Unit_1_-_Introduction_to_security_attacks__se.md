# Data Encryption Standard (DES)

- DES is a symmetric-key block cipher that was adopted as a standard by the US government in 1977.
- DES operates on 64-bit blocks of plaintext and ciphertext, using a 56-bit key (plus 8 parity bits).
- DES uses a Feistel structure, which consists of 16 rounds of encryption or decryption, each involving a subkey derived from the main key and a function that combines the subkey with half of the data block.
- DES applies the principles of confusion and diffusion, as proposed by Shannon, to increase the security of the cipher. Confusion means that each bit of the ciphertext should depend on several bits of the key, and diffusion means that each bit of the ciphertext should depend on several bits of the plaintext.
- DES has been widely used for encryption and decryption of data in various applications, such as banking, e-commerce, and military communications.
- DES has also been extensively analyzed and attacked by cryptographers, who have found several weaknesses and methods to break the cipher, such as differential cryptanalysis, linear cryptanalysis, and brute-force attacks.
- DES is considered insecure today, as the key size is too small to resist brute-force attacks, and the cipher structure is vulnerable to various cryptanalytic techniques. DES has been superseded by more secure and efficient ciphers, such as Triple DES (3DES), Advanced Encryption Standard (AES), and Blowfish.
