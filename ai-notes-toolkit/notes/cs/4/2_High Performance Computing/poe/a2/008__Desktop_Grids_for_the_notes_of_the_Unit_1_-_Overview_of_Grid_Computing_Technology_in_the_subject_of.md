### Desktop Grids

1. Desktop Grids refer to the use of unused computing resources of desktop/personal computers for high throughput computing.
2. The computers are connected over the internet and the resources are aggregated to solve a large computational problem.
3. The grid is decentralized and the resources are contributed voluntarily by the users.
4. The software runs in the background and utilizes the idle CPU cycles to perform the required computations.
5. Desktop Grids are suitable for embarrassingly parallel applications that can be broken into independent tasks that can be computed in parallel.
6. Examples of Desktop Grids are Berkeley Open Infrastructure for Network Computing (BOINC) and Grid.org.
7. Advantages:
    - Inexpensive as utilizes existing resources.
    - Wide reach as can connect numerous desktop computers.
8. Disadvantages:
    - Limited reliability as resources are contributed voluntarily. The users can switch off their computers at any time.
    - Heterogeneity of resources can be challenging to handle. The computers can have different configurations and the workload may not be balanced.
    - Security and privacy concerns as the data is processed in a decentralized manner on user-contributed computers.
