# Capabilities for the notes of the Unit 2 - Open Grid Services Architecture in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Open Grid Services Architecture (OGSA) is a service-oriented architecture for a grid computing environment for business and scientific use.
- OGSA defines a set of core capabilities and behaviors that address key concerns in grid systems, such as resource discovery, allocation, management, security, and self-management.
- OGSA uses most of Web service technologies, notably WSDL and SOAP, but it aims to be largely agnostic in relation to the transport-level handling of data upon the grid.
- The Open Grid Services Architecture, Version 1.5 described these capabilities:

  - **Infrastructure services**: These are the basic services that provide the foundation for the OGSA framework, such as service creation, lifetime management, notification, and registry.
  - **Execution Management services**: These are the services that manage the execution of user tasks on the grid, such as job submission, scheduling, monitoring, and accounting.
  - **Data services**: These are the services that provide access to and manipulation of data on the grid, such as data transfer, replication, catalog, and provenance.
  - **Resource Management services**: These are the services that manage the allocation and sharing of resources on the grid, such as reservation, negotiation, and policy.
  - **Security services**: These are the services that provide security mechanisms for the grid, such as authentication, authorization, encryption, and auditing.
  - **Self-management services**: These are the services that enable the grid to adapt to changing conditions and requirements, such as fault tolerance, load balancing, and optimization.
  - **Information services**: These are the services that provide information about the grid resources, services, and activities, such as discovery, monitoring, and aggregation.
