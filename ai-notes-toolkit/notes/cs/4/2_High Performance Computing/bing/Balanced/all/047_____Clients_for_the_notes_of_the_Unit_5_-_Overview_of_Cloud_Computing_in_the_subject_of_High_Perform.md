# Clients for the notes of the Unit 5 - Overview of Cloud Computing in the subject of High Performance Computing

- A **cloud client** is a hardware device or software used to access a cloud service  .
- A cloud service is a type of computing service that is delivered over the internet and provides scalable and on-demand resources such as computing cycles, data storage, applications, and platforms .
- A cloud client depends on cloud services for functionality and is essentially useless without them .
- Examples of cloud clients include:
  - Computers, tablets, navigation devices, home automation devices, mobile phones and other smart devices that use cloud services for data storage, backup, synchronization, or processing  .
  - Operating systems and browsers that use cloud services for updates, security, or web applications  .
- Types of cloud clients include:
  - **Thin clients**: These are devices that have minimal or no local storage or processing capabilities and rely entirely on cloud services for their functionality. They are usually cheaper, more secure, and more energy-efficient than traditional devices, but they require a constant and reliable internet connection.
  - **Thick clients**: These are devices that have some local storage or processing capabilities and can function independently of cloud services for some tasks. They are usually more expensive, less secure, and less energy-efficient than thin clients, but they can work offline or with intermittent internet connection.
  - **Hybrid clients**: These are devices that can switch between thin and thick modes depending on the availability of cloud services or the user's preference. They are usually more flexible and adaptable than thin or thick clients, but they may have higher complexity and maintenance costs.
