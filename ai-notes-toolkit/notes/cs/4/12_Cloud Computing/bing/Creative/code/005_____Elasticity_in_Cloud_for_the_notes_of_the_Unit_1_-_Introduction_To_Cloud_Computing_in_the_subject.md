# Elasticity in Cloud

Elasticity in cloud computing is the ability of a cloud-based system to adapt to workload changes by provisioning and de-provisioning resources in an autonomic manner, such that at each point in time the available resources match the current demand as closely as possible.

Some of the benefits of elasticity in cloud computing are:

- It reduces the cost of over-provisioning or under-provisioning resources.
- It improves the performance and availability of the system by avoiding resource congestion or starvation.
- It enables the system to handle unpredictable or bursty workloads without compromising the quality of service.
- It allows the system to scale up or down according to the business needs and customer expectations.

Some of the challenges of elasticity in cloud computing are:

- It requires a sophisticated monitoring and control mechanism to detect and respond to workload changes in real time.
- It involves a trade-off between the speed and accuracy of resource allocation and deallocation.
- It depends on the availability and reliability of the underlying cloud infrastructure and services.
- It may introduce security and privacy risks due to the dynamic and distributed nature of the system.

Some of the examples of elasticity in cloud computing are:

- Cloudbursting: The ability to extend the on-premises infrastructure into the public cloud to meet a sudden or seasonal demand.
- Elastic load balancing: The ability to distribute the incoming traffic across multiple instances of an application to balance the load and improve the performance.
- Elastic storage: The ability to adjust the storage capacity and processing power with respect to the operational requirements.
- Elastic computing: The ability to scale the computing resources (CPU, memory, etc.) to meet the processing needs of an application.
