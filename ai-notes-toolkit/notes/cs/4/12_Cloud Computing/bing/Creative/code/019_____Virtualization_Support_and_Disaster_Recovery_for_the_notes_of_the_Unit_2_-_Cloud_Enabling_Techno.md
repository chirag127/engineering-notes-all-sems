### Virtualization Support and Disaster Recovery

- Virtualization is the process of creating virtual versions of physical resources, such as servers, storage, networks, and applications, that can run on a single or multiple physical machines.
- Virtualization can support and bolster disaster recovery strategy in the following ways   :
  - Simplify backup storage: Virtualization enables the observance of the 3-2-1 rule, which means having three copies of data on two different media, one of which is offsite. Virtualization reduces the need for multiple physical storage devices and allows for easier and faster backup and restore of virtual machines (VMs).
  - Provide hardware independence: Virtualization decouples the VMs from the underlying hardware, which means that the disaster recovery site does not have to have the exact same equipment as the production site. This reduces the cost and complexity of disaster recovery and enables faster and smoother failover and failback.
  - Enhance testing and verification: Virtualization allows for regular and automated testing and verification of disaster recovery plans and procedures, without affecting the production environment. This ensures that the disaster recovery site is always ready and functional, and that any issues or gaps can be identified and resolved in advance.
  - Improve scalability and flexibility: Virtualization enables the creation, modification, and deletion of VMs on demand, which allows for adjusting the disaster recovery resources according to the changing needs and priorities of the business. Virtualization also supports different disaster recovery scenarios, such as full-site, partial-site, or application-level recovery, depending on the nature and severity of the disaster.
- Virtualization also helps maintain business continuity as it minimizes the impact of disaster by storing every piece of data in the cloud servers. Cloud servers are accessible from anywhere and anytime, which means that the business can resume its operations as soon as possible after a disaster. Cloud servers also provide high availability, reliability, and security for the data and applications.
