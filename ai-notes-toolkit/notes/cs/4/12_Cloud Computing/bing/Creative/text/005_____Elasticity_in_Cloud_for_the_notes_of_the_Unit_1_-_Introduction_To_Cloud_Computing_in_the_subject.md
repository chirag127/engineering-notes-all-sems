### Elasticity in Cloud

- Elasticity in cloud computing is the ability to adjust the resources (such as computing, memory, and storage) used by a cloud-based application or service according to the changing workload or demand .
- Elasticity is a defining characteristic that differentiates cloud computing from previous computing paradigms, such as grid computing.
- Elasticity enables cloud users to optimize the performance, availability, and cost of their applications or services by dynamically provisioning and de-provisioning resources in an autonomic manner.
- Elasticity can be achieved by using various techniques, such as:
  - Horizontal scaling: adding or removing instances of the same resource type (such as virtual machines or containers) to handle the workload fluctuations.
  - Vertical scaling: increasing or decreasing the capacity of a single resource instance (such as CPU, RAM, or disk) to match the workload requirements.
  - Load balancing: distributing the workload across multiple resource instances to improve the responsiveness and availability of the application or service.
  - Auto-scaling: automating the scaling decisions based on predefined rules or metrics (such as CPU utilization, response time, or throughput) to maintain the desired performance level.
  - Cloud bursting: extending the on-premises infrastructure into the public cloud to meet a sudden or seasonal spike in demand.
- Elasticity has the following benefits for cloud users :
  - Improved user experience: by ensuring that the application or service can handle the varying workload without compromising the quality of service (such as latency, availability, or reliability).
  - Reduced operational costs: by avoiding over-provisioning or under-provisioning of resources, and paying only for the resources that are actually used.
  - Enhanced business agility: by enabling faster and easier deployment, modification, and scaling of applications or services according to the changing market or customer needs.
