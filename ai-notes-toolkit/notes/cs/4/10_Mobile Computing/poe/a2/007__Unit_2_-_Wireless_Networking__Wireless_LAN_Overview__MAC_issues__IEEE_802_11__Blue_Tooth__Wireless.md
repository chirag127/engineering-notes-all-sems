## Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

- Wireless LANs eliminate the need for physical cabling between devices, allowing increased mobility.
- However, wireless transmission is prone to interference, multipath fading, and eavesdropping, and has lower bandwidth than wired Ethernet.
- The Medium Access Control (MAC) protocol is responsible for coordinating access to the shared wireless medium.
- The IEEE 802.11 standard defines the MAC and physical layer specifications for Wireless LANs. 802.11a/b/g/n/ac are modulation techniques that differ in frequency, bandwidth, and performance.
- Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology primarily designed for low-power devices like headphones, speakers, and input devices. It uses frequency-hopping spread spectrum to prevent interference.
- Other wireless technologies include ZigBee, RFID, and cellular data like 3G/4G LTE. They have different range, bandwidth, power, and cost characteristics suitable for various applications.
