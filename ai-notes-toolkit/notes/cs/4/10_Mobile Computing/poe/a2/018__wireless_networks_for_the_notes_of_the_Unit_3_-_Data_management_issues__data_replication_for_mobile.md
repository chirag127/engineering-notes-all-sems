### Wireless Networks

- Wireless networks allow devices to connect to the internet or other network without the use of physical cables. This is useful for mobile devices like laptops, tablets, and smartphones.
- The most common types of wireless networks are:
-- Wi-Fi: Allows devices to connect to a local network (LAN) or the internet. Uses radio waves to create wireless network connections. Most smartphones, laptops, and tablets support Wi-Fi.
-- Cellular: Mobile network provided by cell phone carriers. Uses a mixture of radio frequencies to create wireless internet connectivity on-the-go. Data speeds and coverage can vary based on network load and location.
-- Bluetooth: Short-range wireless technology mainly used for connecting nearby devices like wireless headphones, speakers, keyboards, and other peripherals. Data transfer speeds are relatively slow but the range is limited to around 30 feet.
- Wireless networks are convenient but the wireless signals can potentially be intercepted by malicious actors. To help maintain security, wireless networks typically use encryption and passwords to restrict access to authorized devices only. Strong passwords and encryption algorithms help make wireless networks more secure.
