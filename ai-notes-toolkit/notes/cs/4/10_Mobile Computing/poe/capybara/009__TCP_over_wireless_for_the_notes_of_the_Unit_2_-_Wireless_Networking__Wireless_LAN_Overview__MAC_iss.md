### TCP over wireless for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

- TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) is a reliable transport protocol that is widely used in wired networks. However, it faces several challenges in wireless networks due to the nature of the wireless medium.

- The main challenge of TCP over wireless is the high error rate caused by radio interference, fading, and signal attenuation. The errors can cause packet loss, retransmission, and congestion, which can degrade the performance of TCP.

- To address the challenges of TCP over wireless, several techniques have been proposed, including TCP variants, link-layer protocols, and cross-layer designs.

- TCP variants such as TCP Vegas, TCP Westwood, and TCP SACK have been designed to improve the congestion control and flow control of TCP over wireless. 

- Link-layer protocols such as IEEE 802.11e and IEEE 802.11n have been designed to provide QoS (Quality of Service) support for TCP over wireless. 

- Cross-layer designs such as TCP over MAC (Media Access Control) and TCP-aware routing have been proposed to optimize the performance of TCP over wireless by taking advantage of the knowledge of the lower layers.

- Blue Tooth is a wireless technology that provides short-range communication between devices. It uses a frequency-hopping spread-spectrum (FHSS) technique to avoid interference and improve security.

- IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for wireless LANs (Local Area Networks). It defines the MAC layer and PHY (Physical) layer specifications for wireless communication.

- MAC (Media Access Control) issues in wireless LANs include channel access, contention, collisions, and hidden terminals. To address these issues, several MAC protocols have been proposed, including CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance), DCF (Distributed Coordination Function), and PCF (Point Coordination Function).

- Wireless LANs face several challenges, including interference, security, mobility, and scalability. To address these challenges, several technologies have been developed, including MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output), Wi-Fi Direct, and WiMAX (Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access).

In conclusion, TCP over wireless faces several challenges due to the high error rate of the wireless medium.
