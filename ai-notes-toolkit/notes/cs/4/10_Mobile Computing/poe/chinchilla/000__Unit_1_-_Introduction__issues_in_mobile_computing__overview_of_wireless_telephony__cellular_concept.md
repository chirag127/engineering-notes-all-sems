## Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM:

Mobile computing has become an integral part of our daily lives. With the increasing use of smartphones, tablets, and other mobile devices, the demand for mobile computing has skyrocketed. However, mobile computing comes with its own set of challenges and issues. This unit will introduce you to the basics of mobile computing, the issues that come with it, and an overview of wireless telephony, including the cellular concept and GSM technology.

### Introduction to Mobile Computing

Mobile computing refers to the use of mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets, to access and use computing resources. Mobile computing has become increasingly popular over the years due to the convenience it offers. However, there are several issues associated with mobile computing, including:

- Limited battery life: Mobile devices have limited battery life, which can be a significant issue, especially when you are on the go.
- Limited processing power: Mobile devices have limited processing power compared to desktop computers, which can limit the types of applications that can be run.
- Limited memory: Mobile devices have limited memory, which can restrict the amount of data that can be stored on them.
- Network connectivity issues: Mobile devices rely on network connectivity to access computing resources, and network connectivity issues can be a significant issue for mobile computing.

### Overview of Wireless Telephony

Wireless telephony refers to the use of wireless networks to transmit voice and data. The cellular concept is the foundation of wireless telephony. In the cellular concept, the service area is divided into small geographic areas called cells. Each cell has a base station that transmits and receives signals to and from the mobile devices in the cell. The base stations are connected to a central switching station, which connects to the public switched telephone network (PSTN).

GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications) is a standard for wireless telephony. GSM is used in over 80% of the world's mobile networks. GSM offers several advantages over other wireless telephony standards, including:

- Improved call quality: GSM offers better call quality compared to other wireless telephony standards.
- International roaming: GSM offers international roaming, which allows users to use their mobile devices in other countries.
- Security: GSM uses encryption to secure voice and data transmissions.
- Compatibility: GSM is compatible with a wide range of devices.

In conclusion, this unit has provided an introduction to mobile computing, the issues associated with it, and an overview of wireless telephony, including the cellular concept and GSM technology. Understanding these concepts is essential for anyone interested in mobile computing and wireless telephony.
