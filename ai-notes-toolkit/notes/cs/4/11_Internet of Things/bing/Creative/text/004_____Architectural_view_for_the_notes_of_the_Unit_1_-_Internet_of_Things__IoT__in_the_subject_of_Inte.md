### Architectural view for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- The architectural view of IoT is a way of describing the components, relationships, and functions of an IoT system.
- There are different architectural views for IoT, such as the three-layer view, the five-layer view, the cloud-centric view, and the fog-centric view.
- The three-layer view divides the IoT system into three layers: the perception layer, the network layer, and the application layer.
  - The perception layer is responsible for sensing, collecting, and processing data from the physical world using devices such as sensors, actuators, RFID tags, and cameras.
  - The network layer is responsible for transmitting, routing, and managing data from the perception layer to the application layer using technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular networks, and the Internet.
  - The application layer is responsible for providing services, applications, and user interfaces for the end-users using data from the network layer, such as smart home, smart health, smart city, and smart agriculture.
- The five-layer view adds two more layers to the three-layer view: the processing layer and the business layer.
  - The processing layer is responsible for performing data analysis, data fusion, data storage, and data security using technologies such as cloud computing, edge computing, and big data.
  - The business layer is responsible for managing the business models, policies, and strategies of the IoT system using technologies such as blockchain, smart contracts, and artificial intelligence.
- The cloud-centric view emphasizes the role of cloud computing in the IoT system, where the cloud provides the processing, storage, and application services for the IoT devices and data.
  - The cloud-centric view reduces the cost and complexity of the IoT devices and the network, but also introduces challenges such as latency, bandwidth, privacy, and security.
- The fog-centric view emphasizes the role of fog computing in the IoT system, where the fog provides the processing, storage, and application services at the edge of the network, closer to the IoT devices and data.
  - The fog-centric view improves the latency, bandwidth, privacy, and security of the IoT system, but also increases the cost and complexity of the fog nodes and the network.
