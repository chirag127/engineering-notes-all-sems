### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- Design principles are fundamental pieces of advice for you to make easy-to-use, pleasurable designs. You apply them when you select, create and organize elements and features in your work.
- Design principles represent the accumulated wisdom of researchers and practitioners in design and related fields. They can help you avoid common pitfalls and mistakes, and guide you towards best practices and solutions.
- Design principles for connected devices are especially important in the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), where devices need to interact with each other, with users, and with the environment in a seamless, secure, and scalable way .
- Some of the design principles for connected devices are:

  - **Calm technology**: A system that does not compete for attention but has a high utility when given attention, this type of technology is referred to as calm technology. In a connected devices environment multiple challenges come up. Devices should be able to communicate their status and information without being intrusive or distracting. They should also be able to adapt to the user's context and preferences, and provide feedback and control options when needed.
  - **Interoperability**: During IoT device design, consider everything the product could connect to, control or communicate with in the network, and design accordingly. If designers understand the bigger picture, it helps create a device that is interoperable and can integrate into more use cases. Interoperability also implies following common standards and protocols, such as REST, JSON-RPC, SOAP, or XML-RPC, that enable data exchange and communication among devices .
  - **Security**: Many organizations building and deploying IoT devices largely underestimate the critical societal need to embody the highest levels of cybersecurity in every network-connected device. Every connected device needs to be secured against network-based attacks, unauthorized access, data breaches, and malicious manipulation. Security should be built-in from the start, not added as an afterthought. Some of the properties of highly secure devices are hardware-based root of trust, small trusted computing base, defense in depth, compartmentalization, certificate-based authentication, renewable security, and failure reporting.
  - **Usability**: Usability is the degree to which a device is easy and satisfying to use. Usability depends on factors such as functionality, performance, reliability, accessibility, learnability, efficiency, and aesthetics. Usability can be evaluated by testing the device with real users in realistic scenarios, and measuring metrics such as task completion, error rate, satisfaction, and preference. Usability can be improved by applying user-centered design methods, such as user research, prototyping, and iteration.
  - **Scalability**: Scalability is the ability of a device to handle increasing amounts of data, users, or requests without compromising its performance, functionality, or quality. Scalability is essential for IoT devices, as they are expected to operate in large and dynamic networks, where the number and diversity of devices can change rapidly. Scalability can be achieved by designing devices that are modular, flexible, and adaptable, and that can leverage cloud computing, edge computing, or fog computing to distribute the workload and data processing.
