### MAC protocol survey for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- MAC (Medium Access Control) protocols are responsible for coordinating the access of multiple devices to a shared wireless medium in IoT networks .
- MAC protocols can be classified into two main categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based protocols allow devices to compete for the channel access without reservation or scheduling, such as CSMA/CA, ALOHA, and Slotted ALOHA. These protocols are simple, flexible, and scalable, but they suffer from low throughput, high delay, and high energy consumption in dense and large-scale IoT networks .
- Contention-free protocols allocate the channel access to devices based on reservation or scheduling, such as TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and OFDMA. These protocols can improve the throughput, delay, and energy efficiency of IoT networks, but they require synchronization, signaling, and coordination among devices, which may introduce overhead and complexity .
- IEEE 802.11ah, also known as WiFi HaLow, is a standard that adapts the WiFi technology for IoT applications, by extending the coverage range, supporting a large number of devices, and reducing the power consumption . It employs a contention-free MAC protocol based on restricted access window (RAW) mechanism, which divides the devices into groups and assigns them different time slots for channel access . It also supports various features such as power saving mode, target wake time, and relay operation .
- Other MAC protocols for IoT include LoRaWAN, SigFox, ZigBee, Bluetooth Low Energy, and 6LoWPAN, which have different characteristics and trade-offs in terms of coverage, data rate, scalability, reliability, and energy efficiency.
