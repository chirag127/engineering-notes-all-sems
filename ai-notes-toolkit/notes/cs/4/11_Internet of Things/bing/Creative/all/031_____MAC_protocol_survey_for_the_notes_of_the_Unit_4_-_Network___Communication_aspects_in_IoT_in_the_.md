# MAC protocol survey for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- MAC (Medium Access Control) protocol is the first protocol layer above the physical layer in wireless decentralized networks in IoT.
- MAC protocol aims to coordinate access among IoT devices to the shared wireless medium and avoid collisions and interference .
- MAC protocol can be classified into two main categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based MAC protocol allows devices to compete for the channel access without reservation or scheduling, but it may cause collisions and waste of bandwidth. Examples of contention-based MAC protocol are ALOHA, CSMA/CA, and CSMA/CD.
- Contention-free MAC protocol assigns channel access to devices based on reservation or scheduling, but it may cause overhead and delay. Examples of contention-free MAC protocol are TDMA, FDMA, and CDMA.
- MAC protocol can also be classified based on the coverage range: short-range and long-range.
- Short-range MAC protocol is suitable for IoT devices that are close to each other or to a central coordinator, such as in home or industrial networks. Examples of short-range MAC protocol are IEEE 802.15.4, Bluetooth, and ZigBee.
- Long-range MAC protocol is suitable for IoT devices that are far from each other or from a central coordinator, such as in smart city or rural networks. Examples of long-range MAC protocol are LoRaWAN, Sigfox, and NB-IoT.
- MAC protocol can also be classified based on the standardization: IEEE 802.11ah and non-IEEE 802.11ah .
- IEEE 802.11ah, also known as WiFi HaLow, is a scalable solution for medium-range communication in IoT that leverages various innovative medium access techniques, such as restricted access window, target wake time, and group addressed transmission .
- Non-IEEE 802.11ah MAC protocol is any other MAC protocol that is not based on the IEEE 802.11ah standard, such as those mentioned above .
- MAC protocol survey is a systematic review of the existing MAC protocols for IoT, their advantages and disadvantages, their performance and evaluation, and their future research directions   .
