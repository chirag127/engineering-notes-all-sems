# Architectural View of Internet of Things (IoT)

- Internet of Things (IoT) is a paradigm in which objects equipped with sensors, actuators, and processors communicate with each other to serve a meaningful purpose.
- IoT architecture is a complicated organization comprising many structural elements working in tandem. Their concerted efforts ensure the coordinated performance of any kind of IoT system, be it for industrial, business or personal use.
- IoT architecture can be broken down into four layers:
  - **Device layer**: This layer consists of the physical objects or things that are connected to the internet and can sense, actuate, and/or compute data. Examples of devices are sensors, actuators, RFID tags, smartphones, etc.
  - **Network layer**: This layer provides the communication infrastructure and protocols for transmitting data between devices and other layers. Examples of network technologies are Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, cellular, etc.
  - **Service layer**: This layer provides the functionalities and services for data processing, storage, and analysis. Examples of services are cloud computing, big data analytics, machine learning, etc.
  - **Application layer**: This layer provides the end-user applications and interfaces for interacting with the IoT system. Examples of applications are smart home, smart city, smart health, etc.
- Depending on the application areas and requirements of IoT, different architectures may be proposed with different numbers and names of layers. For example, a three-layer architecture defines the main idea of IoT as perception, network, and application, while a five-layer architecture adds a processing layer and a business layer to the four-layer architecture.
