# E-health

E-health is a term that refers to the use of electronic processes and communication technologies to support health and health-related fields. E-health can include various applications, such as:

- Telehealth: the delivery of health care services and information remotely via digital devices, such as computers, tablets, smartphones, etc. Telehealth can enable access to health care for people in rural or remote areas, reduce travel costs and time, and improve the quality and efficiency of health care. Telehealth can also include telemedicine, which is the use of telecommunication and information technology to provide clinical health care from a distance.
- Electronic health records (EHRs): the digital collection and storage of health information of individual patients or populations. EHRs can facilitate the sharing and exchange of health data among different health care providers, improve the accuracy and completeness of health information, and support clinical decision making and quality improvement.
- Mobile health (mHealth): the use of mobile devices and wireless technologies to support the delivery of health care and public health interventions. mHealth can enable the collection and transmission of health data, the provision of health education and behavior change communication, the delivery of diagnostic and treatment services, and the monitoring and evaluation of health outcomes.
- Health information systems (HIS): the systems that capture, store, manage, and transmit data related to the health of individuals or populations. HIS can support health planning, management, and evaluation, as well as health research and innovation. HIS can also include health information exchanges (HIEs), which are networks that enable the secure and interoperable sharing of health data among different health care organizations.
- E-learning: the use of electronic media and information and communication technologies to support learning and education in health and health-related fields. E-learning can include online courses, webinars, podcasts, simulations, games, etc. E-learning can enhance the accessibility, flexibility, and effectiveness of health education and training for health professionals and the general public.

E-health can offer various benefits for health and health-related fields, such as:

- Improving the access, quality, and efficiency of health care and public health services
- Enhancing the communication and collaboration among health care providers and between providers and patients
- Empowering patients and communities to participate in their own health care and health promotion
- Reducing health disparities and improving health equity
- Supporting health innovation and research
- Increasing the accountability and transparency of health systems

However, e-health also faces various challenges and barriers, such as:

- Lack of adequate infrastructure, equipment, and connectivity
- Lack of interoperability and standardization of health data and systems
- Lack of adequate policies, regulations, and governance for e-health
- Lack of adequate skills, training, and awareness of e-health among health professionals and users
- Lack of adequate evidence, evaluation, and impact assessment of e-health interventions
- Lack of adequate funding, sustainability, and scalability of e-health initiatives
- Ethical, legal, and social issues related to e-health, such as privacy, security, consent, liability, etc.
