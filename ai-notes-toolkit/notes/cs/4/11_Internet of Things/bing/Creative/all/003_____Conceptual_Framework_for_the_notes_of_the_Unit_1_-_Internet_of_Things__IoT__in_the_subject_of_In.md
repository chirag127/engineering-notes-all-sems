# Conceptual Framework for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- The Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical objects or things that are embedded with sensors, actuators, software, and connectivity to enable data exchange and communication with other devices or systems over the internet or other networks.
- The IoT enables various applications and services that can improve the quality of life, efficiency, productivity, and sustainability in different domains such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart agriculture, smart industry, etc.
- The IoT is composed of four main components: things, communication, computation, and services.
  - Things are the physical objects or devices that have sensing, actuating, or computing capabilities and can interact with the environment or other things.
  - Communication is the process of transferring data or information between things or systems using wired or wireless technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, cellular, etc.
  - Computation is the process of processing, analyzing, or storing data or information using cloud computing, edge computing, fog computing, or other paradigms.
  - Services are the applications or functions that provide value or benefits to the users or stakeholders of the IoT system, such as monitoring, control, optimization, prediction, etc.
- The IoT is characterized by some key features, such as heterogeneity, scalability, interoperability, security, privacy, and intelligence.
  - Heterogeneity refers to the diversity and variety of things, communication technologies, computation platforms, and services in the IoT system, which pose challenges for integration and coordination.
  - Scalability refers to the ability of the IoT system to handle the increasing number of things, data, and users, which require efficient and flexible management and allocation of resources.
  - Interoperability refers to the ability of the IoT system to exchange and use data or information across different devices, systems, or platforms, which require common standards and protocols.
  - Security refers to the protection of the IoT system from unauthorized access, modification, or damage, which require encryption, authentication, authorization, and other mechanisms.
  - Privacy refers to the protection of the personal or sensitive data or information of the users or stakeholders of the IoT system from unauthorized disclosure or misuse, which require anonymization, consent, and other mechanisms.
  - Intelligence refers to the ability of the IoT system to learn from data or information and provide smart or adaptive services or functions, which require artificial intelligence, machine learning, or other techniques.
