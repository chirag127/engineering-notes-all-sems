# Survey routing protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Routing protocols are the rules that determine how data packets are forwarded from one node to another in a network.
- Routing protocols for Internet of Things (IoT) are designed to meet the specific requirements and challenges of IoT networks, such as resource constraints, heterogeneity, scalability, mobility, and security.
- Routing protocols for IoT can be classified into different categories based on various criteria, such as network topology, routing strategy, data flow direction, and security and trust management.
- Some of the common categories of routing protocols for IoT are:

  - Flat routing protocols: These protocols treat all the nodes in the network as equal and do not form any hierarchy or cluster. They are suitable for small and static networks. Examples of flat routing protocols for IoT are Ad hoc On-Demand Distance Vector (AODV) , Dynamic Source Routing (DSR) , and Optimized Link State Routing (OLSR) .
  - Hierarchical routing protocols: These protocols divide the network into clusters or groups of nodes, and assign different roles and responsibilities to the nodes based on their cluster membership. They are suitable for large and dynamic networks. Examples of hierarchical routing protocols for IoT are Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH) , Threshold-sensitive Energy Efficient sensor Network (TEEN) , and Routing Protocol for Low-Power and Lossy Networks (RPL) .
  - Location-based routing protocols: These protocols use the geographic information of the nodes to make routing decisions. They are suitable for mobile and sparse networks. Examples of location-based routing protocols for IoT are Geographic Routing Protocol (GRP) , Greedy Perimeter Stateless Routing (GPSR) , and Distance Routing Effect Algorithm for Mobility (DREAM) .
  - Context-based routing protocols: These protocols use the contextual information of the nodes, such as their status, preferences, and environment, to make routing decisions. They are suitable for heterogeneous and adaptive networks. Examples of context-based routing protocols for IoT are Context Base Routing Protocol (CBRP) , Context-Aware Routing (CAR) , and Context-Aware Routing Protocol for IoT (CARP-IoT) .
  - Security and trust-based routing protocols: These protocols use the security and trust metrics of the nodes to make routing decisions. They are suitable for secure and reliable networks. Examples of security and trust-based routing protocols for IoT are Secure Routing Protocol for IoT (SRP-IoT) , Trust-Aware Routing Protocol for IoT (TARP-IoT) , and A Survey of IoT Routing Protocols based on Security and Trust Management (SIRP-IoT) .

- Routing protocols for IoT are still an active area of research and development, and there are many open challenges and opportunities for improvement and innovation. Some of the future research directions are:

  - Developing cross-layer and adaptive routing protocols that can optimize the performance and efficiency of IoT networks.
  - Developing energy-efficient and low-latency routing protocols that can prolong the lifetime and responsiveness of IoT networks.
  - Developing cooperative and collaborative routing protocols that can enhance the robustness and resilience of IoT networks.
  - Developing secure and trustworthy routing protocols that can protect the privacy and integrity of IoT networks.
  - Developing interoperable and scalable routing protocols that can support the diversity and growth of IoT networks.
