## Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

- Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical objects or devices that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies to collect and exchange data with other devices and systems over the internet.
- IoT enables various applications such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, smart agriculture, smart industry, and smart environment.
- IoT devices can communicate with each other using different protocols such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRaWAN, MQTT, CoAP, etc.
- IoT devices can be classified into three categories: sensors, actuators, and gateways.
  - Sensors are devices that measure physical quantities such as temperature, humidity, pressure, light, sound, etc. and convert them into digital signals.
  - Actuators are devices that perform physical actions such as switching on/off, opening/closing, moving, etc. based on the commands received from other devices or systems.
  - Gateways are devices that connect sensors and actuators to the internet or other networks and provide data processing, security, and management functions.
- IoT systems consist of four layers: perception layer, network layer, application layer, and business layer.
  - Perception layer is the lowest layer that consists of sensors and actuators that collect and control data from the physical world.
  - Network layer is the layer that provides connectivity and communication between the perception layer and the upper layers using various protocols and technologies.
  - Application layer is the layer that provides specific services and functions for different domains and scenarios using the data from the network layer.
  - Business layer is the highest layer that provides value-added services and business models for the IoT systems and users.
- IoT challenges include security, privacy, interoperability, scalability, reliability, and energy efficiency.
