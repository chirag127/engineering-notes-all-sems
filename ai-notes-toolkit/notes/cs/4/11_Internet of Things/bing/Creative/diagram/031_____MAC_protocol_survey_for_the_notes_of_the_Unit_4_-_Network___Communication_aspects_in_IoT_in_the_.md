### MAC protocol survey for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- MAC (Medium Access Control) protocol is the first protocol layer above the Physical Layer in ad hoc networks.
- The primary task of any MAC protocol is to control the access of the nodes to the shared wireless medium.
- MAC protocols for IoT have to deal with the challenges of scalability, energy efficiency, reliability, and heterogeneity of IoT devices.
- MAC protocols for IoT can be classified into two main categories: contention-based and contention-free .
- Contention-based protocols allow nodes to compete for the channel access, but may suffer from collisions, delays, and overheads .
- Contention-free protocols allocate the channel resources to nodes in advance, but may introduce wastage, complexity, and synchronization issues .
- Contention-based protocols can be further divided into protocols without reservation/scheduling and protocols with reservation/scheduling.
- Protocols without reservation/scheduling include Aloha, Slotted Aloha, CSMA, CSMA/CA, and CSMA/CD.
- Protocols with reservation/scheduling include TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and OFDMA.
- Contention-free protocols can be further divided into protocols for static and mobile networks.
- Protocols for static networks include polling, token passing, and reservation-based protocols.
- Protocols for mobile networks include handover, location management, and mobility-aware protocols.
- IEEE 802.11ah, also known as WiFi HaLow, is a scalable solution for medium-range communication in IoT .
- IEEE 802.11ah leverages various innovative medium access control techniques to support IoT and machine-to-machine communication .
- IEEE 802.11ah MAC protocols include Restricted Access Window, Target Wake Time, and Traffic Indication Map .
