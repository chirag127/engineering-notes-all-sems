### Mobiles for the notes of the Unit 7 - IoT Applications in the subject of Internet of Things

- Mobiles are devices that can communicate wirelessly with other devices or networks, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, etc.
- Mobiles can act as both IoT devices and IoT applications, depending on their role and functionality in the IoT system.
- As IoT devices, mobiles can use their built-in sensors, such as GPS, camera, microphone, accelerometer, etc., to collect and transmit data to the cloud or other devices.
- As IoT applications, mobiles can use their graphical user interface, such as touch screen, buttons, etc., to interact with and control other IoT devices, such as smart home appliances, wearable devices, etc.
- Mobiles can also use their computing and storage capabilities, such as CPU, RAM, ROM, etc., to process and store data locally or remotely, depending on the IoT architecture and requirements.
- Mobiles can leverage various wireless communication technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, cellular networks, etc., to connect to the IoT network and exchange data with other devices or servers.
- Mobiles can benefit from various IoT platforms and services, such as Azure IoT Central, AWS IoT, Google Cloud IoT, etc., to simplify the development, deployment, and management of IoT applications and devices.
- Mobiles can enable various IoT applications and use cases, such as:

  - Electric bikes tracking: Mobiles can use NB-IoT (Narrowband IoT) technology to monitor and locate electric bikes in real-time, and prevent theft, accidents, or fire hazards.
  - Smoke and gas detectors: Mobiles can use NB-IoT technology to receive alerts and notifications from smoke and gas detectors installed in buildings, and take appropriate actions in case of emergency.
  - Asset tracking: Mobiles can use LTE-M (Long Term Evolution for Machines) or NB-IoT technology to track and manage the location, status, and condition of various assets, such as vehicles, containers, equipment, etc.
  - Predictive maintenance: Mobiles can use IoT-based apps to collect and analyze data from various sensors and devices installed in machinery, and predict and prevent potential failures or malfunctions.
  - Inventory management and supply chain monitoring: Mobiles can use IoT-based apps to scan and update the inventory of various products and materials, and monitor their movement and delivery across the supply chain.
  - Smart home automation: Mobiles can use IoT-based apps to remotely control and adjust various smart home devices, such as lights, thermostats, security cameras, etc., and create personalized and comfortable living environments.
  - Wearable devices: Mobiles can use IoT-based apps to sync and display data from various wearable devices, such as fitness trackers, smart watches, smart glasses, etc., and provide health and wellness insights and recommendations.
