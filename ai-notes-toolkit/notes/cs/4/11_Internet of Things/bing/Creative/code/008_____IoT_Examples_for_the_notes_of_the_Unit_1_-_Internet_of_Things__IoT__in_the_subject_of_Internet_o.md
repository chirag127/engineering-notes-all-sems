### IoT Examples

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that can collect, process, exchange and utilize data interrelatedly, via the internet or other communications networks. IoT has many different applications in various domains, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart agriculture, smart healthcare, smart manufacturing, smart retail, and autonomous vehicles. Here are some examples of IoT devices and systems:

- **Connected cars**: These are vehicles that can communicate with other vehicles, infrastructure, and cloud services to provide enhanced safety, efficiency, and convenience. For example, Tesla's cars can receive over-the-air (OTA) software updates from Airbiquity, a software and engineering company that provides OTA solutions for the automotive industry . Connected cars can also use GPS tracking, diagnostics, and telematics services from companies like Zubie, which offers real-time fleet management and monitoring .
- **Smart appliances**: These are household devices that can be controlled remotely or automatically, and can provide useful information and feedback. For example, smart refrigerators can monitor the food items inside and alert the user when they are running low or expired. Smart ovens can adjust the temperature and cooking time based on the recipe and the user's preferences. Smart washers and dryers can optimize the water and energy consumption and notify the user when the cycle is done.
- **Connected security systems**: These are systems that can protect the home or business from intruders, fire, or other hazards, and can be accessed and controlled from anywhere. For example, Ring offers smart doorbells, cameras, and alarms that can stream live video, detect motion, and send alerts to the user's smartphone or tablet . Connected security systems can also integrate with other smart devices, such as smart locks, lights, and thermostats, to create a customized and automated environment.
- **Smart agriculture equipment**: These are devices and systems that can improve the productivity, quality, and sustainability of farming and food production. For example, smart irrigation systems can monitor the soil moisture and weather conditions and adjust the water supply accordingly. Smart drones can survey the crops and apply pesticides or fertilizers precisely. Smart sensors can track the health and location of livestock and alert the farmer of any anomalies.
- **Connected retail**: These are solutions that can enhance the customer experience, optimize the inventory management, and increase the sales and revenue of retail businesses. For example, smart shelves can detect the stock level and the expiration date of the products and send real-time data to the store manager. Smart mirrors can allow the customers to virtually try on different outfits and accessories and get personalized recommendations. Smart beacons can send targeted offers and promotions to the customers' smartphones based on their location and preferences.
- **Connected healthcare monitors**: These are devices and systems that can measure, record, and transmit the vital signs and health conditions of patients and provide timely and accurate diagnosis and treatment. For example, smart watches and fitness trackers can monitor the heart rate, blood pressure, and activity level of the user and send the data to the cloud or the doctor. Smart implants and prosthetics can adjust to the user's needs and provide feedback and alerts. Smart pills and patches can deliver drugs and monitor the drug response and adherence.
- **Connected manufacturing equipment**: These are machines and systems that can automate and optimize the production process and improve the quality and efficiency of the products. For example, smart robots can perform complex and repetitive tasks with precision and speed. Smart sensors can monitor the temperature, pressure, and vibration of the equipment and detect any faults or defects. Smart RFID tags can track the location and status of the materials and products throughout the supply chain.
- **Connected cities**: These are solutions that can improve the livability, sustainability, and resilience of urban areas and provide better services and infrastructure to the citizens. For example, smart street lights can adjust the brightness and color based on the traffic and weather conditions and save energy. Smart parking systems can guide the drivers to the nearest available parking spot and reduce congestion and pollution. Smart waste management systems can monitor the fill level and the quality of the waste bins and optimize the collection and disposal.
