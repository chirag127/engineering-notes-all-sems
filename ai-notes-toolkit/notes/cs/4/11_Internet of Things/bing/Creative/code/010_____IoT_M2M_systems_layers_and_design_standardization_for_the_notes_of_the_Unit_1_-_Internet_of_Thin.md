### IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization

- IoT/M2M systems are composed of interconnected devices, applications, and services that communicate and exchange data over the internet or other networks.
- IoT/M2M systems can be divided into three major domains: the application layer, the service layer, and the network layer .
- The application layer is the domain where the end-user applications and services reside. It provides the user interface, the business logic, and the data processing for the IoT/M2M system. Examples of application layer protocols are HTTP, MQTT, CoAP, etc.
- The service layer is the domain where the common functionalities and capabilities for the IoT/M2M system are provided. It enables interoperability, security, management, and discovery of devices, applications, and services. Examples of service layer standards are oneM2M, OGC SensorThings API, etc.
- The network layer is the domain where the physical and logical connectivity of the devices, applications, and services are established. It provides the data transmission, routing, addressing, and networking protocols for the IoT/M2M system. Examples of network layer technologies are 3GPP, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, etc.
- IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization aim to provide a common framework and architecture for the development, deployment, and operation of IoT/M2M systems. It also aims to ensure interoperability, scalability, security, and reliability of IoT/M2M systems across different domains, platforms, and vendors.
- Some of the organizations and initiatives that are involved in IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization are:
  - oneM2M: a global partnership project that develops technical specifications for a common service layer for IoT/M2M systems .
  - ETSI: a European standards organization that develops standards for various aspects of IoT/M2M systems, such as smart M2M communications, radio access, security, etc.
  - OGC: an international industry consortium that develops open standards for geospatial and location-based services, including sensors, IoT, and M2M.
  - NIST: a US federal agency that develops standards and guidelines for cyber-physical systems and IoT, such as frameworks, reference architectures, testbeds, etc.
