## Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT

- IoT stands for Internet of Things, which refers to the interconnection of physical devices, sensors, actuators, and other smart objects over the internet.
- IoT devices communicate with each other and with cloud-based services using various network and communication protocols, depending on the requirements and constraints of the application domain.
- Some of the key aspects of network and communication in IoT are:

  - **Local communications**: This is the method that the IoT device uses to speak with neighboring devices, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Z-Wave, etc. 
  - **Application protocol**: This is the framework that defines how information content is transported, such as MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, etc. 
  - **Gateways**: These are devices that translate and re-transmit information, typically linking local device networks to the internet, such as routers, bridges, hubs, etc. 
  - **Internet protocol**: This is the standard that enables different devices to communicate over the internet, such as IPv4, IPv6, 6LoWPAN, etc. 
  - **Networking technologies**: These are the technologies that enable IoT devices to communicate with other devices, applications, and services running in the cloud, such as cellular, satellite, LoRaWAN, NB-IoT, etc. 
  - **Network infrastructure**: This is the physical and logical structure that supports the communication and data exchange among IoT devices and cloud services, such as servers, switches, firewalls, routers, etc. 
  - **Security and privacy**: These are the measures that ensure the protection of IoT devices, data, and networks from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure, such as encryption, authentication, authorization, etc. 

- The choice of network and communication protocols for IoT depends on various factors, such as:

  - **Power consumption**: IoT devices often have limited battery life and need to conserve energy, so they may prefer low-power protocols, such as Bluetooth Low Energy, Zigbee, LoRaWAN, etc. 
  - **Data rate**: IoT devices may have different data transmission needs, from low to high, depending on the type and frequency of data they generate or consume, so they may choose protocols that match their data rate, such as Wi-Fi, cellular, satellite, etc. 
  - **Range**: IoT devices may have different communication distances, from short to long, depending on the location and coverage of the devices, so they may use protocols that suit their range, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular, satellite, etc. 
  - **Reliability**: IoT devices may have different levels of tolerance for data loss, delay, or corruption, depending on the criticality and timeliness of the data, so they may opt for protocols that ensure reliable delivery, such as TCP, MQTT, CoAP, etc. 
  - **Scalability**: IoT devices may have different numbers of devices and connections, from few to many, depending on the size and complexity of the network, so they may adopt protocols that support scalability, such as IPv6, 6LoWPAN, MQTT, etc. 
  - **Interoperability**: IoT devices may have different types and models of devices and platforms, from homogeneous to heterogeneous, depending on the diversity and compatibility of the devices, so they may use protocols that enable interoperability, such as HTTP, CoAP, MQTT, etc.
