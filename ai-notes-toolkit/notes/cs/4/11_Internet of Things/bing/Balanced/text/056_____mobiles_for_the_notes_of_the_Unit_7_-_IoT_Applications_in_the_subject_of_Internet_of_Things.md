### Mobiles for the notes of the Unit 7 - IoT Applications in the subject of Internet of Things

- Mobiles are devices that can communicate wirelessly with other devices or networks using cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or other technologies.
- Mobiles can act as both IoT devices and IoT controllers, depending on the use case and the app installed on them.
- Mobiles can collect, send, receive, and process data from sensors, actuators, cameras, GPS, and other sources, either on the device itself or on the cloud.
- Mobiles can also provide a user interface for interacting with IoT devices, such as sending commands, configuring settings, viewing data, or receiving notifications.
- Mobiles can leverage their built-in sensors, such as accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, microphone, or camera, to provide context-aware and personalized IoT services.
- Mobiles can also use their computing power, storage, and battery to perform edge computing, which is the processing of data close to the source, reducing latency and bandwidth consumption.
- Some examples of mobile IoT applications are:

  - Electric bikes tracking: Using NB-IoT, a low-power wide-area network technology, electric bikes can be tracked and monitored remotely, improving security, maintenance, and user experience.
  - Smoke and gas detectors: Using NB-IoT, smoke and gas detectors can send alerts and data to a central server, enabling faster response and prevention of fire hazards.
  - Asset tracking: Using LTE-M or NB-IoT, assets such as containers, pallets, or vehicles can be tracked and located in real-time, improving supply chain efficiency and visibility.
  - Predictive maintenance: Using mobile IoT-based apps, industrial machines and equipment can be monitored and diagnosed remotely, reducing downtime and repair costs.
  - Smart home: Using mobile IoT-based apps, users can control and manage their smart home devices, such as lights, thermostats, cameras, or locks, from anywhere.
  - Azure IoT Central: Using a mobile app, users can connect their smartphone as an Azure IoT device, and send and receive data from the cloud-based IoT platform.
