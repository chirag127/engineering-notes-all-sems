### Technology behind IoT

- IoT stands for Internet of Things, which is the concept of connecting any device with an on/off switch to the internet and to other connected devices.
- IoT devices can collect and share data about their usage and the environment around them, enabling various applications and services that can improve efficiency, convenience, security, and quality of life.
- IoT devices can communicate with each other and with a central area via wireless networking technologies, such as GSM cellular networks, Wi-Fi, ethernet, mesh networks, 5G, and Wi-Fi 6 .
- IoT devices can also use edge computing, which refers to the technology that increases the computing power at the edges of an IoT network, reducing communication latency and improving response time.
- IoT devices can use different types of hardware components, such as CPUs, MCUs, GPUs, security chips, FPGA, and edge gateways, depending on their functionality and requirements.
- IoT devices can also use various software platforms and protocols, such as MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, WebSockets, and LwM2M, to enable data exchange, device management, and interoperability.
- IoT devices can leverage cloud computing, which refers to the technology that provides on-demand access to computing resources and services over the internet, such as storage, processing, analytics, and artificial intelligence.
- IoT devices can also use blockchain, which refers to the technology that provides a distributed ledger of transactions that is secure, transparent, and immutable, enabling trust and verification among IoT devices and stakeholders.
