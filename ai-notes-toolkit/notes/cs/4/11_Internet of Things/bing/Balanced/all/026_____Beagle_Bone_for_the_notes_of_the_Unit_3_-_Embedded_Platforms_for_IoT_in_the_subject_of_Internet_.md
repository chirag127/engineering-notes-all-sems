# Beagle Bone

Beagle Bone is a series of low-cost, open-source, single-board Linux computers that can be used for embedded applications such as Internet of Things (IoT). Some of the features and benefits of Beagle Bone are:

- It has a small size (about the size of a credit card) and low power consumption (about 2W).
- It has a high-performance processor (ARM Cortex-A8 or ARM Cortex-A15) that can run various operating systems such as Android, Ubuntu, Debian, etc.
- It has plenty of input/output (I/O) interfaces and expansion capabilities, such as USB, Ethernet, HDMI, serial, analog, digital, etc. It can also be connected to various sensors, actuators, displays, cameras, etc. using plug-in boards called capes.
- It has a large and active community of developers and users who provide support, documentation, tutorials, projects, etc.
- It is affordable and accessible, with prices ranging from $25 to $100 depending on the model and features.

Some of the common models of Beagle Bone are:

- BeagleBone Black: The most popular and widely used model, with a 1GHz ARM Cortex-A8 processor, 512MB RAM, 4GB eMMC flash storage, and 3D graphics accelerator. It costs $60 and can boot Linux in under 10 seconds.
- BeagleBone Green: A variant of BeagleBone Black, with two Grove connectors for easy connection to sensors and actuators. It costs $70 and is compatible with most of the BeagleBone Black capes.
- BeagleBone Blue: A model designed for robotics and autonomous systems, with a 1GHz ARM Cortex-A8 processor, 512MB RAM, 4GB eMMC flash storage, and a built-in IMU, barometer, magnetometer, GPS, etc. It costs $80 and can be controlled by a smartphone or a computer.
- BeagleBone AI: A model designed for artificial intelligence and machine learning, with a 1.5GHz ARM Cortex-A15 processor, 1GB RAM, 16GB eMMC flash storage, and a dual-core DSP and dual-core GPU for accelerated computation. It costs $100 and can run TensorFlow, OpenCV, etc.

Beagle Bone can be used for various IoT applications, such as smart home, smart city, smart agriculture, smart industry, etc. Some examples of Beagle Bone projects are:

- 3D printer: A project that uses BeagleBone Black and a cape to control a 3D printer and monitor its status and progress.
- Arcade game: A project that uses BeagleBone Black and a cape to create a retro arcade game console with a joystick and buttons.
- Robot: A project that uses BeagleBone Blue and a cape to build a robot that can navigate, avoid obstacles, and perform tasks.
- Facial recognition: A project that uses BeagleBone AI and a camera to perform facial recognition and identification.
