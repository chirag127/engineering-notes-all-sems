# ARM Cortex for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT

- ARM Cortex is a family of processor cores designed by ARM Holdings for various applications, such as embedded systems, mobile devices, and IoT devices.
- ARM Cortex processors are based on the RISC (reduced instruction set computing) architecture, which aims to improve performance and efficiency by using simpler and fewer instructions.
- ARM Cortex processors are divided into three categories: Cortex-A, Cortex-R, and Cortex-M, each with different features and target markets.

## Cortex-A Processors
- Cortex-A processors are the highest performance and most complex ARM cores, designed for applications that require high performance, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, servers, and automotive systems.
- Cortex-A processors support multiple instruction sets, such as ARMv7-A, ARMv8-A, and ARMv9-A, and can run various operating systems, such as Linux, Android, Windows, and iOS.
- Cortex-A processors also support advanced features, such as virtualization, memory management, and security extensions, to enable more functionality and protection for the system.
- Some examples of Cortex-A processors are Cortex-A5, Cortex-A7, Cortex-A8, Cortex-A9, Cortex-A15, Cortex-A17, Cortex-A32, Cortex-A35, Cortex-A53, Cortex-A55, Cortex-A57, Cortex-A65, Cortex-A72, Cortex-A73, Cortex-A75, Cortex-A76, Cortex-A77, Cortex-A78, and Cortex-X1.

## Cortex-R Processors
- Cortex-R processors are optimized for real-time and safety-critical applications, such as automotive, industrial, medical, and aerospace systems.
- Cortex-R processors support the ARMv7-R and ARMv8-R instruction sets, and can run real-time operating systems, such as FreeRTOS, QNX, and VxWorks.
- Cortex-R processors also support features, such as memory protection, error correction, and lock-step operation, to ensure reliability and fault tolerance for the system.
- Some examples of Cortex-R processors are Cortex-R4, Cortex-R5, Cortex-R7, Cortex-R8, and Cortex-R52.

## Cortex-M Processors
- Cortex-M processors are the smallest and most energy-efficient ARM cores, designed for low-cost and low-power applications, such as microcontrollers, sensors, and IoT devices.
- Cortex-M processors support the ARMv6-M, ARMv7-M, and ARMv8-M instruction sets, and can run bare-metal or embedded operating systems, such as Zephyr, Mbed OS, and FreeRTOS.
- Cortex-M processors also support features, such as interrupt handling, bit manipulation, and low-power modes, to enable fast and flexible response and operation for the system.
- Some examples of Cortex-M processors are Cortex-M0, Cortex-M0+, Cortex-M1, Cortex-M3, Cortex-M4, Cortex-M7, Cortex-M23, Cortex-M33, Cortex-M35P, Cortex-M55, and Cortex-M85.

## Embedded Platforms for IoT
- Embedded platforms for IoT are hardware and software solutions that enable the development and deployment of IoT devices and applications.
- Embedded platforms for IoT typically consist of a microcontroller or a microprocessor, a memory, a communication interface, a sensor, an actuator, and a power source, as well as an operating system, a middleware, a driver, a library, and an application.
- Embedded platforms for IoT can be based on various ARM Cortex processors, depending on the requirements and constraints of the IoT device and application, such as performance, power consumption, cost, size, security, and connectivity.

  - Raspberry Pi: A single-board computer based on Cortex-A processors, such as Cortex-A72 and Cortex-A53, that can run Linux, Windows, or Android, and can be used for various IoT applications, such as smart home, robotics, and education.
