# Intel Galileo boards

- Intel Galileo boards are Arduino-certified development boards based on Intel x86 architecture and are designed for the maker and education communities.
- Intel Galileo boards are based on the Intel® Quark SoC X1000 Application Processor, a 32-bit Intel Pentium-class system on a chip .
- Intel Galileo boards are hardware and software pin-compatible with Arduino shields designed for the Uno R3.
- Intel Galileo boards have the following features :
  - 400 MHz 32-bit Intel® Quark™ SoC X1000 processor, with 512 KB embedded SRAM, 16 KB cache, and a micro-SD slot for additional storage.
  - 256 MB DDR3, 512 kb embedded SRAM, and 8 MB NOR Flash.
  - 10/100 Ethernet connector, USB 2.0 Host port, USB 2.0 Client port, and RS-232 serial port.
  - 14 digital input/output pins, 6 analog input pins, and 6 pulse width modulation (PWM) output pins.
  - Support for a wide range of operating systems, such as Linux, Windows, and Mac OS X.
  - Support for Arduino IDE, Intel® XDK IoT Edition, Intel® System Studio IoT Edition, and other software development tools.
- Intel Galileo boards can be used for various Internet of Things (IoT) applications, such as smart home, wearable, robotics, environmental monitoring, and more.
- Intel released two versions of Galileo, referred to as Gen 1 and Gen 2. The main differences between them are:
  - Gen 2 has a 12-bit pulse-width modulation (PWM) instead of 8-bit, and supports 256 KB of SRAM instead of 8 KB.
  - Gen 2 has a 6-pin 3.3V USB TTL UART header instead of a 3.5 mm jack RS-232 console port header.
  - Gen 2 has a 12V power-over-Ethernet (PoE) capable connector instead of a 5V one.
  - Gen 2 has a power regulation system that accepts power supplies from 7V to 15V instead of 5V.
- Intel discontinued the Galileo boards on June 19, 2017.
