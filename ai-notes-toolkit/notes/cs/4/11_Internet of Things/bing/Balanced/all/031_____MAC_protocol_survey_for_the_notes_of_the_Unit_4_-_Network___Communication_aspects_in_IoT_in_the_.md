# MAC protocol survey for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- MAC (Medium Access Control) protocols are responsible for coordinating the access of multiple devices to a shared wireless medium in IoT networks .
- MAC protocols can be classified into two main categories: contention-based and contention-free.
  - Contention-based protocols allow devices to compete for the channel access without reservation or scheduling. They are simple, flexible and scalable, but may suffer from collisions, delays and energy inefficiency. Examples of contention-based protocols are CSMA/CA, ALOHA and Slotted ALOHA.
  - Contention-free protocols allocate the channel access to devices based on reservation or scheduling mechanisms. They can provide guarantees on throughput, delay and energy consumption, but may incur overhead, complexity and lack of adaptability. Examples of contention-free protocols are TDMA, FDMA and CDMA.
- MAC protocols can also be designed for specific IoT scenarios, such as IEEE 802.11ah, also known as WiFi HaLow, which is a scalable solution for medium-range communication in IoT . IEEE 802.11ah leverages various innovative medium access techniques, such as restricted access window, target wake time and group addressed transmission, to support a large number of devices with low power consumption and high reliability .
- MAC protocols can also be adapted for static and mobile IoT networks, where the devices may have different mobility patterns and communication requirements.
