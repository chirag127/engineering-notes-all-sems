### Intel Galileo boards

- Intel Galileo boards are development boards based on Intel x86 architecture and are designed for the maker and education communities.
- Intel Galileo boards are compatible with Arduino shields designed for the Uno R3, which means they can use the same hardware and software components as Arduino boards.
- Intel Galileo boards are powered by the Intel Quark SoC X1000, a 32-bit Intel Pentium-class system on a chip that runs at 400 MHz and has 16 KB of cache and 512 KB of embedded SRAM .
- Intel Galileo boards have the following features :
  - 256 MB DDR3, 512 kb embedded SRAM, 8 MB NOR Flash, and 8 kb EEPROM standard on the board, plus support for microSD card up to 32 GB
  - 10/100 Ethernet connector, USB 2.0 Host port, USB 2.0 Client port, and RS-232 serial port
  - 14 digital input/output pins, 6 analog input pins, and 6 pulse width modulation (PWM) output pins
  - Support for 3.3V or 5V operating voltage
  - Programmable through the Arduino IDE, the Intel XDK IoT Edition, or the Intel System Studio IoT Edition
- Intel released two versions of Galileo, referred to as Gen 1 and Gen 2. The main differences between them are:
  - Gen 2 has a 6-pin 3.3V USB TTL UART header instead of a RS-232 serial port
  - Gen 2 has 12 GPIOs fully native for greater speed and improved drive strength
  - Gen 2 has a 12-bit pulse-width modulation (PWM) for more precise control of servos and smoother response
  - Gen 2 has console UART1 redirected to Arduino headers, which can be accessed by digital pins 0 (RX) and 1 (TX)
  - Gen 2 has a power regulation system that accepts power supplies from 7V to 15V
- Intel discontinued the Galileo boards on June 19, 2017.
