### Definition

- Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical objects or devices that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies to connect and exchange data with other devices and systems over the internet.
- IoT enables the interaction and integration of various domains, such as computing, communication, sensing, and actuation, to provide services and applications for various domains, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, smart agriculture, and smart industry.
- IoT can be seen as a paradigm shift from the traditional internet, where humans are the main actors and information sources, to a new internet, where things are the main actors and information sources, and humans can interact with them through various interfaces.
- IoT can be characterized by the following features:
  - Heterogeneity: IoT involves a variety of devices, platforms, protocols, and data formats, which pose challenges for interoperability and integration.
  - Scalability: IoT involves a large number of devices, which pose challenges for management, security, and privacy.
  - Mobility: IoT involves devices that can move and change their location, which pose challenges for connectivity, routing, and localization.
  - Dynamicity: IoT involves devices that can join and leave the network, which pose challenges for discovery, configuration, and adaptation.
  - Autonomy: IoT involves devices that can operate independently and collaboratively, which pose challenges for coordination, collaboration, and self-organization.
