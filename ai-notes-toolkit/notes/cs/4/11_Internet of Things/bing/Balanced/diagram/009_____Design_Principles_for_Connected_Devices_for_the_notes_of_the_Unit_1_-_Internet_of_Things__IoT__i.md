### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- Design principles are fundamental pieces of advice for you to make easy-to-use, pleasurable designs.
- Connected devices are devices that can communicate with other devices or systems over a network, such as the internet.
- Connected devices are also known as Internet of Things (IoT) devices, which are physical objects that have sensors, actuators, processors, and network interfaces.
- Designing connected devices requires considering the interaction with other elements in the network, the functionality and utility of the device, the security and privacy of the data, and the user experience and satisfaction .
- Some of the design principles for connected devices are:

  - **Calm technology**: A system that does not compete for attention but has a high utility when given attention, this type of technology is referred to as calm technology. Calm technology aims to reduce the cognitive load and distraction of the user, and to provide relevant and timely information when needed.
  - **Interoperability**: The ability of a device to work with other devices or systems in the network, regardless of the manufacturer, platform, or protocol. Interoperability enables more use cases and integration for the device, and enhances the user experience and satisfaction.
  - **Security**: The protection of the device and the data from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction. Security is essential for ensuring the reliability, integrity, and confidentiality of the device and the data, and for preventing potential harm to the user or the network.
  - **Privacy**: The respect for the user's personal information and preferences, and the control over the collection, use, and sharing of the data. Privacy is important for maintaining the trust and consent of the user, and for complying with the ethical and legal standards of the network.
  - **User-centered design**: The process of designing a device based on the needs, wants, and limitations of the user, and involving the user in the design and evaluation stages. User-centered design ensures that the device is useful, usable, and desirable for the user, and that it meets the user's expectations and goals.
