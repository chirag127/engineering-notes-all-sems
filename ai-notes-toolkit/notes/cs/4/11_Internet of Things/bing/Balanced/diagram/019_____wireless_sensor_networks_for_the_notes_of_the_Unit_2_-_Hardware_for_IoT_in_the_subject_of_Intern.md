### Wireless Sensor Networks

- Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are networks of spatially dispersed and dedicated sensors that monitor and record the physical conditions of the environment and forward the collected data to a central location.
- WSNs can measure environmental conditions such as temperature, sound, pollution levels, humidity and wind.
- WSNs consist of a set of connected tiny sensor nodes, which communicate with each other and exchange information and data.
- WSNs are characterized as infrastructure less, fault tolerant and self-organizing networks which provide opportunities for low-cost, easy-to-apply, rapid and flexible installations in an environment for various applications.
- WSNs have the power of distributed communication, computing, and sensing features.
- A WSN consists of two main components: sensors and radio nodes .
  - Sensors: Sensors in WSNs are used to capture the environmental variables and which is used for data acquisition. Sensor signals are converted into digital signals by an analog-to-digital converter (ADC) and then transmitted to the radio nodes.
  - Radio Nodes: Radio nodes are used to receive the data produced by the sensors and send it to the wireless local area network (WLAN) access point. They consist of a microcontroller, a transceiver, and a battery. They can also perform some data processing and routing functions.
- A WSN also has one or more sink nodes (also called base stations) that collect data from the sensors and forward it to the end users or applications.
- WSNs can be classified into different types based on their topology, communication, architecture, and application.
  - Topology: The topology of a WSN refers to the way the sensor nodes are arranged and connected in the network. Some common topologies are star, tree, mesh, and cluster.
  - Communication: The communication of a WSN refers to the way the sensor nodes communicate with each other and with the sink node. Some common communication methods are single-hop, multi-hop, direct, and indirect.
  - Architecture: The architecture of a WSN refers to the way the sensor nodes are organized and managed in the network. Some common architectures are flat, hierarchical, and hybrid.
  - Application: The application of a WSN refers to the purpose and functionality of the network. Some common applications are environmental monitoring, health care, military, smart home, and industrial control.
