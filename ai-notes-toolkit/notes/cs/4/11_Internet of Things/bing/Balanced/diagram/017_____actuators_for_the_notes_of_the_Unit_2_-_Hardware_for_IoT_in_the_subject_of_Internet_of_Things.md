### Actuators for IoT

- An actuator is a device that converts energy into motion. It does this by taking an electrical signal and combining it with an energy source.
- In an IoT system, the actuator can act on data collected by sensors to create an outcome as determined by the chosen settings of the user.
- Actuators enable a physical action based on data that originates with one or more sensors.
- The conversion of sensor data to activity follows this sequence:
  - Sensors detect an event in the physical environment.
  - The sensor data is transmitted to a gateway or cloud platform via a communication network.
  - The gateway or cloud platform processes the sensor data and applies logic or rules to determine the appropriate action.
  - The gateway or cloud platform sends a command to the actuator via the same or a different communication network.
  - The actuator receives the command and executes the action by converting energy into motion.
- There are different types of actuators for IoT, depending on the energy source, the motion type, and the application domain.
- Some common types of actuators for IoT are :
  - Servo Motors: A servomotor is a rotary actuator or linear actuator that allows for precise control of angular or linear position, velocity and acceleration. Servo motors are widely used in robotics, industrial automation, and aerospace applications.
  - Stepper Motors: Stepper motors are DC motors that move in discrete steps. Each step corresponds to a specific angle of rotation. Stepper motors are used for precise positioning and speed control, such as in 3D printers, CNC machines, and cameras.
  - DC Motors (Continuous Rotation Motors): DC motors are two wire (power & ground), continuous rotation motors. They can vary their speed and direction by changing the polarity and voltage of the power supply. DC motors are used for applications that require high torque and speed, such as in electric vehicles, fans, and pumps.
  - Linear Actuators: Linear actuators are devices that produce linear motion by converting electrical, hydraulic, or pneumatic energy. They can be used to push, pull, lift, or lower objects. Linear actuators are used for applications that require linear movement, such as in medical devices, industrial machinery, and home automation.
  - Thermal/Magnetic Actuators: Thermal/magnetic actuators are actuated by thermal or mechanical energy. Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs) or Magnetic Shape Memory Alloys (MSMAs) are materials that can change their shape when heated or exposed to a magnetic field. Thermal/magnetic actuators are used for applications that require low power consumption, high force, and fast response, such as in valves, switches, and locks.
  - Mechanical Actuators: Mechanical actuators execute movement by converting rotary motion into linear motion. They use gears, screws, or levers to achieve the desired motion. Mechanical actuators are used for applications that require high load capacity, long stroke, and low cost, such as in car jacks, clamps, and presses.
  - Soft Actuators: Soft actuators are made of flexible or elastic materials that can deform or bend when stimulated by electrical, chemical, or pneumatic signals. They can mimic the movements of natural muscles or organs. Soft actuators are used for applications that require softness, compliance, and adaptability, such as in wearable devices, soft robots, and biomedical implants.
