### Survey Routing Protocols for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication Aspects in IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

In the Internet of Things (IoT), routing protocols play a crucial role in ensuring efficient communication between devices. Here are some of the routing protocols used in IoT:

1. **RPL (IPv6 Routing Protocol for Low-Power and Lossy Networks):** RPL is a widely adopted routing protocol for low-power and lossy networks (LLNs) in IoT. It is designed to support IPv6-based communication between devices with limited processing power and memory. RPL is a proactive routing protocol that builds a tree-like structure to route packets from the source to the destination.

2. **6LoWPAN Routing Protocol:** 6LoWPAN (IPv6 over Low Power Wireless Personal Area Networks) is a protocol that enables IPv6 communication over low-power wireless networks. It is designed specifically for IoT devices with limited resources, such as sensors and actuators. 6LoWPAN uses mesh networking to connect devices and supports multiple routing protocols such as RPL and AODV.

3. **AODV (Ad Hoc On-Demand Distance Vector):** AODV is a reactive routing protocol that is widely used in mobile ad hoc networks (MANETs) and IoT. It is designed to establish routes on-demand and is suitable for networks with dynamic topologies. AODV is a hop-by-hop routing protocol that uses distance vectors to calculate the shortest path between nodes.

4. **OLSR (Optimized Link State Routing):** OLSR is a proactive routing protocol that is used in wireless mesh networks. It is designed to be highly scalable and efficient in large networks. OLSR uses a link-state algorithm to build a routing table and supports multiple metrics such as hop count and link quality.

5. **BATMAN (Better Approach To Mobile Adhoc Networking):** BATMAN is a proactive routing protocol that is used in wireless mesh networks. It is designed to be highly efficient in mobile ad hoc networks and supports multiple metrics such as link quality and throughput. BATMAN uses a distributed algorithm to build a routing table and can handle both unicast and multicast traffic.

In conclusion, routing protocols are an essential component of IoT networks, and choosing the right protocol depends on the specific requirements of the network. The above-mentioned routing protocols are widely adopted in IoT and have their strengths and weaknesses. It is essential to evaluate each protocol carefully and choose the one that best suits the network's needs.
