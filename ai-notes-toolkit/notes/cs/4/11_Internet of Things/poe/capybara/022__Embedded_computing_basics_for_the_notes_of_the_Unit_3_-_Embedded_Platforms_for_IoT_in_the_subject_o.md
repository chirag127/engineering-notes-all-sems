### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing is an integral part of the Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem. In this unit, we will learn about the basics of embedded computing and how it is used in IoT.

Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Embedded computing refers to the use of computer systems that are integrated into other devices or products. These systems are designed to perform specific functions and are not intended to be general-purpose computers.

- Embedded systems are typically designed to be low-power, low-cost, and highly reliable. They are often used in applications where traditional computers would be too large, expensive, or power-hungry.

- Embedded systems are often programmed in low-level languages such as C or assembly language. This is because these languages provide greater control over the hardware and allow for more efficient code.

- Embedded systems can be built using a variety of hardware platforms, including microcontrollers, system-on-chips (SoCs), and field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs).

- Microcontrollers are small, low-power chips that are commonly used in embedded systems. They typically include a processor, memory, and input/output (I/O) peripherals.

- SoCs are more complex than microcontrollers and typically include multiple processors, memory, and I/O peripherals on a single chip. They are often used in more advanced embedded systems.

- FPGAs are programmable logic devices that can be customized to perform specific tasks. They are often used in applications where high performance and flexibility are required.

- In addition to hardware platforms, embedded systems also require software to control their operation. This software is typically written in low-level languages and is highly optimized for the specific hardware platform.

- Finally, embedded systems often require specialized tools and development environments for programming and testing. These tools are designed to work with the specific hardware platform and can include hardware debuggers, logic analyzers, and simulation software.

By understanding the basics of embedded computing, we can better understand how it is used in IoT and other applications.
