
### Survey routing protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

1. Routing protocols are an essential part of the Internet of Things (IoT). They are used to enable communication between different devices and networks.
2. Routing protocols can be divided into two main categories: link-state and distance-vector routing protocols.
3. Link-state routing protocols, such as Open Shortest Path First (OSPF) and Intermediate System to Intermediate System (IS-IS), are proactive and determine the best route to any destination in the network.
4. Distance-vector routing protocols, such as Routing Information Protocol (RIP) and Enhanced Interior Gateway Routing Protocol (EIGRP), are reactive and use a "distance" metric to determine the best route.
5. Routing protocols can also be classified as distance-vector or link-state based on their nature, such as unicast, multicast, and broadcast.
6. Multicast routing protocols, such as Multicast Open Shortest Path First (MOSPF) and Protocol Independent Multicast (PIM), are used to send data to multiple destinations simultaneously.
7. Routing protocols can also be classified based on the type of traffic they are used for, such as Voice over IP (VoIP), video, or data.
8. Finally, routing protocols can be classified based on the type of network they are used in, such as local area networks (LANs) or wide area networks (WANs).
