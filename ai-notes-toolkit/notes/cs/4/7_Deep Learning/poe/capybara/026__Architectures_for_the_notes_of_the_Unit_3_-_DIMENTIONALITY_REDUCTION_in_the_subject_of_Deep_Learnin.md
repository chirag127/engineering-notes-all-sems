### Architectures for the notes of the Unit 3 - DIMENSIONALITY REDUCTION in the subject of Deep Learning

In deep learning, Dimensionality reduction is a crucial technique that helps to simplify the data by reducing its dimensions while retaining relevant information. There are various architectures available for dimensionality reduction, some of which are listed below:

- Principal Component Analysis (PCA): PCA is a linear technique that projects high-dimensional data onto a lower-dimensional space. It is used to reduce the dimensionality of the data while retaining as much information as possible. PCA is widely used in various fields like image recognition and natural language processing.

- Autoencoders: Autoencoders are neural networks that learn to reconstruct the input data after reducing its dimensionality. An autoencoder consists of two parts, an encoder, and a decoder. The encoder takes the high-dimensional input data and produces a lower-dimensional representation, while the decoder takes the lower-dimensional representation and reconstructs the original data.

- t-SNE (t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding): t-SNE is a nonlinear technique that is used to visualize high-dimensional data in a two-dimensional or three-dimensional space. It is commonly used for visualizing complex data such as images, text, and speech.

- Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF): NMF is a linear technique that factorizes the input data matrix into two non-negative matrices - a low-rank matrix and a sparse matrix. It is used to reduce the dimensionality of the data while retaining the structure of the data.

- Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA): LDA is a linear technique that is used for feature extraction and dimensionality reduction. It is commonly used in the field of image recognition and speech processing.

These are some of the commonly used architectures for dimensionality reduction in deep learning. Each of these techniques has its strengths and weaknesses and can be chosen based on the specific requirements of the problem at hand. Understanding these architectures is crucial for anyone working in the field of deep learning.
