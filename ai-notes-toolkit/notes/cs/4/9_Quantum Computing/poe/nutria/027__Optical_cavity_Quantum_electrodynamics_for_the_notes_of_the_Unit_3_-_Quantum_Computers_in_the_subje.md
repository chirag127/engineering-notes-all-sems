
### Optical Cavity Quantum Electrodynamics 

1. Optical cavity quantum electrodynamics (QED) is a branch of quantum physics that studies the interaction of light with matter in a cavity. 
2. It is a field of research that has been studied since the late 1970s, and has since become an important part of quantum computing. 
3. In optical cavity QED, light is trapped in a cavity, such as a laser cavity, and interacts with the atoms inside the cavity. 
4. This interaction is known as the Jaynes-Cummings model, and is described by a Hamiltonian. 
5. The Hamiltonian describes how the light and atoms interact, and how the energy of the system changes. 
6. This model is used to study the quantum properties of light, such as entanglement and superposition. 
7. Optical cavity QED is used in quantum computing to create qubits, which are the building blocks of quantum computers. 
8. In quantum computing, qubits are used to store information, and can be manipulated to perform calculations. 
9. Optical cavity QED is also used to study quantum effects, such as the Casimir effect, which is the force between two objects in a vacuum. 
10. Optical cavity QED is a rapidly growing field of research, and has applications in many areas of quantum computing.
