### Quantum Computation for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

1. Qubits: Quantum bits or Qubits are the basic units of information in a quantum computer. They can hold values 0, 1 or a superposition of both.
2. Superposition: Qubits can be in a superposition of states where the qubit can have a probability of collapsing to 0 or 1 upon measurement. The state of a qubit can be represented as a|0> + b|1> where a and b are complex numbers and |0> and |1> are the basis states.
3. Entanglement: When two or more qubits are entangled, their states are dependent on each other, even if they are separated by a large distance. The measurement of one qubit will instantly influence the other, which leads to quantum correlations between the qubits.
4. Quantum Gates: Quantum gates are the quantum equivalent of logic gates in classical computing. They are unitary operators that can transform the state of one or more qubits. Examples include Hadamard gate, Pauli-X gate, Phase shift gate, etc.
5. Quantum Circuits: Quantum circuits are made up of quantum gates that are applied to qubits to perform operations on input qubits to generate output qubits. The sequence of gates applied gives the algorithm for the quantum computation.
6. Measurement: The final step of a quantum algorithm is to measure the state of the output qubits which collapses the qubits to definite states. The probabilities of different outcomes can be calculated from the final state of the qubits.
