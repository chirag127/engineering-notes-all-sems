### Basic properties of Entropy

1. Entropy is a measure of uncertainty or randomness in a system. It quantifies the amount of information missing to fully describe the state of the system.
2. Entropy is always positive. The entropy of a system can never be negative.
3. The entropy of an isolated system always increases over time. This is also known as the Second Law of Thermodynamics. The disorder or uncertainty of an isolated system will always increase if left alone.
4. The entropy of a system depends on the probabilities of its microstates. The more uniform the probability distribution of the microstates, the higher the entropy. If all microstates are equally probable, the entropy is maximum.
5. The entropy of a composite system is the sum of entropies of its constituent subsystems if they are independent. For dependent subsystems, the total entropy is less than the sum of individual entropies.

The above points cover the key properties of entropy which is a crucial concept in information theory and thermodynamics. In the context of quantum error correction, entropy is useful to quantify the amount of noise or decoherence in a quantum system. By keeping the entropy low, we can mitigate errors and preserve quantum information.
