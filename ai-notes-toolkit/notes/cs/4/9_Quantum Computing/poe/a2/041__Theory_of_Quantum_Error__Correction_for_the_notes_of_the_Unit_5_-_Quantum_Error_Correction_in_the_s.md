### Theory of Quantum Error –Correction

1. Quantum error correction is required to build a quantum computer that can perform useful computation because quantum states are fragile and prone to decoherence from the environment.
2. Quantum error correction codes encode quantum information in a redundant way so that it can be protected from noise and decoherence.
3. The three main steps in quantum error correction are:

- Error encoding: The quantum information is encoded in a higher-dimensional quantum state (called a codeword) that can tolerate some errors.
- Error detection: By measuring the encoded state, one can detect the occurrence of errors.
- Error correction: Depending on the measurement outcomes, one applies correction operations to recover the original quantum state.

4. The quantum code specifies which states are used as codewords and which correction operations to apply based on the measurement results. A good quantum code enables the recovery of the quantum state with high fidelity even after errors occur.
5. Theoretical research in quantum error correction has revealed that if the error rate is below a certain threshold value, then it is possible to perform arbitrary quantum computation with arbitrarily high fidelity. This is a key result that gives hope for the eventual scalability of quantum computers.
