## Unit 4 - Quantum Information

1. Qubits - Quantum bits are the basic units of quantum information. Qubits can be in a superposition of states, meaning they can act as both 0 and 1 at the same time. This allows quantum computers to perform certain calculations much faster than traditional computers.
2. Quantum Entanglement - When two or more quantum particles become "entangled", their properties directly depend on each other, even if they are separated by a large distance. If you measure a property of one particle, such as spin, you instantly know the corresponding property of the other particle. No matter how far apart the particles are, they will always be in this quantum connection. This "spooky action at a distance" as Einstein called it, is a bizarre yet fundamental feature of quantum mechanics.
3. Quantum Teleportation - The transfer of quantum information from one particle to another without moving the physical matter. While not teleporting physical matter, this is useful for transferring data with perfect accuracy while maintaining the quantum state. This is done by transferring exact state information between two particles that may be very far apart.
4. Quantum Algorithms - Algorithms that run on a quantum computer and solve certain problems much more efficiently than traditional algorithms running on a classical computer. A few examples are Shor's algorithm for integer factorization and Grover's algorithm for search. As quantum computers become more advanced, quantum algorithms will tackle more complex problems that are currently intractable for classical supercomputers.
