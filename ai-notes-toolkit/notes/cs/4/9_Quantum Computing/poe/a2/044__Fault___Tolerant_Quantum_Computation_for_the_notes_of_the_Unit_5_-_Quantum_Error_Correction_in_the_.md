### Fault – Tolerant Quantum Computation

- Quantum error correction is required to build a large-scale quantum computer that can run quantum algorithms for a long time while maintaining quantum coherence.
- Fault-tolerant quantum computation allows quantum computers to function even with a certain amount of noise and imperfections. The key idea is to encode quantum information in a redundant way and continuously check for and correct errors.
- The three main steps in fault-tolerant quantum computation are:
	1. Error detection - Continuous checking if any errors have occurred. This can be done using quantum error-correcting codes that encode quantum information in a redundant way.
	2. Error correction - If an error is detected, then it needs to be corrected. This requires delicate switching between the different components of the encoded state and possibly applying correction operations.
	3. Fault tolerance - A quantum system needs sufficient redundancy and error correction capabilities to tolerate a certain amount of imperfections. The error rate that a quantum system can tolerate depends on the strength of the quantum error correction code.
- Significant challenges still remain to build a large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computer. The key requirements are a low error rate for quantum operations and a high degree of control over the quantum system. With rapid recent advances, fault-tolerant quantum computing may be within reach in the near future.
