### Quantum Bits for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing.

1. Qubits: Quantum bits or qubits are the basic units of information in a quantum computer. They are similar to the bits used in traditional computing which can have a value of either 0 or 1. However, qubits can be in a superposition of states, having a probability distribution of 0 and 1 at once. This allows a quantum computer to explore many possible solutions in parallel.
2. Superposition: Superposition is a fundamental concept of quantum mechanics. It refers to the ability of a quantum system to exist in multiple states simultaneously. For example, a qubit can be in a superposition of states 0 and 1 at once. The strengths of the probabilities of the two states are represented using a wavefunction. Superposition allows quantum algorithms to perform certain tasks much more efficiently than classical algorithms.
3. Entanglement: When two or more qubits are entangled, their properties directly depend on each other, even if they are separated by a large distance. An action on one qubit will instantaneously influence the other, allowing quantum correlations between the qubits. Entanglement is a key feature that enables quantum algorithms to be more powerful than classical algorithms. It is a mysterious phenomenon but has been demonstrated experimentally many times.
4. Measurement: In quantum mechanics, measurement causes the superposition of states to collapse into a definite state. So when a qubit is measured, it gives a random output of either 0 or 1, with probabilities determined by the qubit's wavefunction. The outcome is inherently unpredictable, but the measurement process can be repeated multiple times to determine the probability distribution of outputs. Repeated measurements are used in quantum algorithms to obtain the solution to a problem.
