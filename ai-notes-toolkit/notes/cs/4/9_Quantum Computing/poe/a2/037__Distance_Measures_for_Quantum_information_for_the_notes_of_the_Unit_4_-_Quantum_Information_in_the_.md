### Distance Measures for Quantum information

1. Fidelity - It is a measure of how close two quantum states are. It is defined as the absolute value of the inner product of the two states. It takes values between 0 and 1. Higher the fidelity, closer the two states are.
2. Trace distance - It is the sum of the absolute values of the differences between the corresponding matrix elements of the two states. It is a true metric and satisfies all the properties of a distance measure. It is easier to calculate than fidelity but is a cruder measure of distance between two states.
3. Bures distance - It is a geometric distance between two states and is defined as the square root of 1 minus the fidelity between the states. It satisfies the properties of a true metric and is a smoother measure of distance than trace distance.
4. Relative entropy - It is a measure of distinguishability between two states. It is defined as the difference between the von Neumann entropies of the two states. It is a measure of how much additional work is required to distinguish two states and is asymmetric in the two states. It does not satisfy all properties of a true metric.

The above are some of the commonly used distance measures between two quantum states which find applications in quantum information processing tasks like quantum error correction, quantum state discrimination, etc. The choice of a particular distance measure depends on the specifics of the task and the properties that are desired from a distance measure.
