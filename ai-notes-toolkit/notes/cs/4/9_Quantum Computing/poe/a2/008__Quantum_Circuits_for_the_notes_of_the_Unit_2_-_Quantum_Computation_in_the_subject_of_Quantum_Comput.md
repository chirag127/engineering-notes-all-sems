### Quantum Circuits for the notes of the Unit 2 - Quantum Computation

1. Quantum circuits are the blueprint for quantum algorithms. They are made up of quantum gates that operate on qubits.
2. Qubits are the basic units of information in a quantum circuit. They can be represented by particles, photons, ions, etc. and can be in a superposition of states.
3. Quantum gates are the building blocks of quantum circuits. They are unitary operators that manipulate the state of one or more qubits. Some important quantum gates are:
- Hadamard gate: Puts a qubit into a superposition of states.
- Pauli gates: Apply operations like bit-flip, phase-flip and bit-phase flip.
- CNOT gate: Applies a controlled-not operation on two qubits.
- Swap gate: Exchanges the states of two qubits.
4. Quantum circuits can be represented visually using quantum circuit diagrams. The qubits are represented by lines and the quantum gates are represented by blocks. The order of the blocks denotes the order in which operations are applied.
5. A quantum algorithm is a sequence of quantum gates that are applied in a quantum circuit to solve a problem. The output is read by measuring the state of the qubits. Some well-known quantum algorithms are Deutsch-Jozsa algorithm, Simon's algorithm, Shor's algorithm, Grover's algorithm, etc.
