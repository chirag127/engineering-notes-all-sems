### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

1. Quantum error correction is required to build a large-scale quantum computer as quantum states are fragile and prone to decoherence from the environment. Error correction codes are used to detect and correct errors to preserve the quantum information.
2. The three main types of quantum error correcting codes are:
- Quantum stabilizer codes: Uses the stabilizer formalism to describe the codespace and uses Pauli operators for error correction. Examples include Steane and surface codes.
- Quantum subsystem codes: Uses encoding into a subsystem of some large Hilbert space and the remaining subsystem is used for error correction.
- Quantum concatenated codes: Uses a concatenation of smaller quantum codes to improve the error threshold. For e.g. the 7-qubit Steane code can be concatenated to improve the threshold.
3. The distance of a quantum code determines the number of errors it can correct. A larger distance allows for correcting more errors but requires a larger number of qubits. The quantum error threshold determines the error rate below which a quantum computer can perform arbitrarily long quantum computations.
4. Active error correction is required to continually detect and correct errors as the computation proceeds. This can be done using gadgets which perform error syndrome extraction and error correction without damaging the encoded quantum state. The resource overhead of active error correction leads to the requirement of improving the passive error tolerance of quantum systems.
