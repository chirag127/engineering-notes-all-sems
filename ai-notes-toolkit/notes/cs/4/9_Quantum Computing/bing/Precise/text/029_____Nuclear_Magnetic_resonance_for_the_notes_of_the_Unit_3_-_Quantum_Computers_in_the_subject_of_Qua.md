### Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR)

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) is a physical phenomenon in which atomic nuclei in a magnetic field absorb and re-emit electromagnetic radiation. This energy is at a specific resonance frequency which depends on the strength of the magnetic field and the magnetic properties of the isotope of the atoms.

NMR is used in quantum computing to control and manipulate the state of qubits, which are the basic units of quantum information. NMR quantum computers use the spin states of atomic nuclei as qubits.

Some key points to remember about NMR in the context of quantum computing are:
- NMR is based on the interaction between atomic nuclei and magnetic fields.
- The resonance frequency of NMR depends on the strength of the magnetic field and the magnetic properties of the isotope.
- NMR is used to control and manipulate the state of qubits in quantum computers.
- NMR quantum computers use the spin states of atomic nuclei as qubits.

This is a brief overview of NMR in the context of quantum computing. It is important to study this topic in more detail to fully understand its applications and implications in the field of quantum computing.
