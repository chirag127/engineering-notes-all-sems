### Quantum Computation

Quantum computation is a computation model that uses quantum physical properties to solve problems. It is at the early stage of large-scale adoption in various industry domains to take advantage of the algorithmic speed-ups it has to offer.

#### Fundamental Concepts

1. **Superposition**: Superposition is the ability of a quantum system to be in multiple states simultaneously. The go-to example of superposition is the flip of a coin, which consistently lands as heads or tails—a very binary concept.

2. **Entanglement**: Quantum superposition and entanglement are fundamental concepts for quantum mechanics and computing. Superposition states that, like waves in classical physics, any two (or more) quantum states can be superposed or added together, and the result will be a valid quantum state.

3. **Interference**: Interference is another fundamental concept in quantum mechanics and computing. It refers to the interaction of two or more waves, resulting in a new wave pattern.

Quantum computers have an innate ability to learn about the world, dealing in probability, as they explore multiple answers to come up with complex decisions. Quantum computing focuses on the principles of quantum theory, which deals with modern physics that explain the behavior of matter and energy of an atomic and subatomic level. Quantum computing makes use of quantum phenomena, such as quantum bits, superposition, and entanglement to perform data operations.

#### Quantum Network

A quantum network is a device consisting of quantum logic gates whose computational steps are synchronized in time. The outputs of some of the gates are connected by wires to the inputs of others. The size of the network is the number of gates it contains. The most common quantum gate is the Hadamard gate, a single qubit gate.
