### Postulates of Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics is a fundamental theory in physics that provides a description of the physical properties of nature at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles. The postulates of quantum mechanics are the basic assumptions that underlie the theory. Here are the main postulates of quantum mechanics:

1. **The State Postulate**: The state of a quantum mechanical system is completely specified by a function that depends on the coordinates of the particle(s) and on time. This function, called the wave function or state function, has the important property that is the probability that the particle lies in the volume element located at at time .

2. **The Observable Postulate**: To every observable in classical mechanics, there corresponds a linear, Hermitian operator in quantum mechanics .

3. **The Measurement Postulate**: The result of a measurement of an observable is one of the eigenvalues of the corresponding operator .

4. **The Evolution Postulate**: The time evolution of the wave function is governed by the Schrödinger equation .

These postulates provide the foundation for the mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics, which uses mainly a part of functional analysis, especially Hilbert spaces, which are a kind of linear space .
