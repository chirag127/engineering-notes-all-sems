### Shannon Entropy

Shannon entropy is a measure of the uncertainty or randomness of a random variable. It is named after Claude Shannon, who introduced the concept in his 1948 paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication". Shannon entropy is commonly used in information theory and is defined as:

H(X) = -∑ p(x) log₂ p(x)

where X is a discrete random variable, p(x) is the probability mass function of X, and the summation is taken over all possible values of X.

In the context of quantum error correction, Shannon entropy can be used to quantify the amount of information that can be transmitted through a noisy quantum channel. The capacity of a quantum channel is defined as the maximum amount of information that can be reliably transmitted through the channel per use. The Shannon entropy of the input and output states of the channel can be used to calculate the channel capacity.

Some key points to remember about Shannon entropy are:

- Shannon entropy is a measure of the uncertainty or randomness of a random variable.
- It is defined as the negative sum of the probability of each possible value of the random variable multiplied by the logarithm of the probability.
- Shannon entropy can be used to quantify the amount of information that can be transmitted through a noisy quantum channel.
- The capacity of a quantum channel is defined as the maximum amount of information that can be reliably transmitted through the channel per use.
- The Shannon entropy of the input and output states of the channel can be used to calculate the channel capacity.
