### Conditions for Quantum Computation

Quantum computation is a type of computation that uses quantum bits (qubits) to store and process information. In order for quantum computation to be possible, certain conditions must be met:

1. **Initialization**: The qubits must be able to be initialized to a known state, typically the |0⟩ state.

2. **Unitary Transformation**: The system must be able to perform unitary transformations on the qubits, which are reversible operations that preserve the norm of the state vector.

3. **Measurement**: The system must be able to perform measurements on the qubits, which collapse the state of the qubits to one of the basis states.

4. **Decoherence**: The system must be able to maintain coherence for a sufficient amount of time to perform the computation. Decoherence is the loss of quantum information due to interaction with the environment.

5. **Scalability**: The system must be scalable, meaning that it must be possible to increase the number of qubits in the system without significantly increasing the complexity of the system.

These conditions are necessary for the successful implementation of quantum computation. By meeting these conditions, a quantum computer can perform calculations that are not possible with classical computers.
