# Unit 3 - Quantum Computers: Optical Photon Quantum Computer

- Optical Photon Quantum Computers use photons as information carriers and mainly use linear optical elements to process quantum information. 
- This paradigm of quantum computation is known as Linear Optical Quantum Computing (LOQC) and allows for universal quantum computation .
- LOQC uses photon detectors and quantum memories to detect and store quantum information .
- The classic approach to photonic quantum computing relies on qubits each based on a single photon. This strategy manipulates photons with mirrors, beam splitters, and phase shifters. Single photon detectors are then used to help read the results of what these devices have done .
- In 2001, all-optical quantum computing became feasible with the discovery that scalable quantum computing is possible using only single-photon sources, linear optical elements, and single-photon detectors. However, the massive resource overhead made the scheme practically daunting .
- Quantum physics theories make it possible to encode information (a qubit) in single particles of light (a photon) and to circulate them in an optical fiber in a highly secure way  .
