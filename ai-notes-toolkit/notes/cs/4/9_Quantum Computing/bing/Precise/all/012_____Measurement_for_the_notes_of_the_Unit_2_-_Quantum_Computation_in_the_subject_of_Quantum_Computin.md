### Measurement

Measurement is a fundamental concept in quantum computation and is one of the key differences between classical and quantum computing. In classical computing, the state of a bit can be measured and the result will always be either 0 or 1. However, in quantum computing, the state of a qubit can exist in a superposition of both 0 and 1, and the result of a measurement is not as straightforward.

Here are some key points to remember about measurement in quantum computation:

1. Measurement is the process of observing the state of a qubit.
2. The result of a measurement is probabilistic, meaning that the outcome is not certain and can vary each time the measurement is performed.
3. The probability of obtaining a particular outcome is determined by the state of the qubit before the measurement.
4. After a measurement, the state of the qubit collapses to the observed state.
5. Measurement is a destructive process, meaning that the original state of the qubit is lost after the measurement.
6. Measurement is an essential part of many quantum algorithms and is used to extract information from the quantum system.

These are some of the key points to remember about measurement in quantum computation. It is an important concept to understand when studying quantum computing and is essential for the implementation of many quantum algorithms.
