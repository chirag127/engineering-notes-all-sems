### Quantum algorithms

Quantum algorithms are algorithms designed to run on a quantum computer. Compared to traditional computing done by a classical computer, a quantum computer should be able to store much more information and operate with more efficient algorithms. This translates to solving extremely complex tasks faster .

One of the goals of quantum computing research is to study which problems can be solved by a quantum computer faster than a classical computer and how large the speedup can be. Two well-known examples are Grover's algorithm and Shor's algorithm, which yield a polynomial and an exponential speedup, respectively, over their classical counterparts .

The main purpose of the lecture notes is to introduce quantum phase estimation (QPE) and ``post-QPE'' methods such as block encoding, quantum signal processing, and quantum singular value transformation, and to demonstrate their applications in solving eigenvalue problems, linear systems of equations, and differential equations .

- Grover's algorithm: a quantum algorithm that searches an unsorted database with N entries in O(sqrt(N)) time.
- Shor's algorithm: a quantum algorithm that can factorize large numbers exponentially faster than the best-known classical algorithm.
- Quantum phase estimation (QPE): a quantum algorithm that estimates the eigenvalues of a unitary operator.
- Block encoding: a technique for representing a matrix as a quantum operator.
- Quantum signal processing: a framework for designing quantum algorithms that manipulate quantum states.
- Quantum singular value transformation: a quantum algorithm that approximates the singular values of a matrix.
