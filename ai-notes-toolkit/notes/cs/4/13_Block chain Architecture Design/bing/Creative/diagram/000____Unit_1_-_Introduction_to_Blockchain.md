## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

- A blockchain is a **digital ledger** that stores data of any kind across a network of computers .
- A blockchain can record information about **cryptocurrency transactions**, **NFT ownership** or **DeFi smart contracts**.
- A blockchain is **decentralized**, meaning that no single entity controls or owns the data .
- A blockchain is **distributed**, meaning that the data is replicated and synchronized among all the nodes in the network .
- A blockchain is **public**, meaning that anyone can access and verify the data .
- A blockchain is **secure**, meaning that the data is protected by **cryptographic hashes** and **consensus algorithms** that prevent tampering or fraud .
- A blockchain is **immutable**, meaning that the data cannot be altered or deleted once it is recorded .
- A blockchain consists of **blocks** that are linked together by **chains** .
- Each block contains a **cryptographic hash** of the previous block, a **timestamp**, and **transaction data** .
- A blockchain is updated by **adding new blocks** to the chain, which requires the **validation** of the network participants .
- A blockchain is a **system** in which a record of transactions made in **bitcoin** or another **cryptocurrency** is maintained across several computers that are linked in a **peer-to-peer network** .
- A blockchain is a **technology** that enables the creation of **decentralized applications** (DApps) that can run on the network without intermediaries or censorship .
- A blockchain is a **revolutionary** innovation that has the potential to transform various industries and sectors, such as **finance**, **supply chain**, **healthcare**, **gaming**, **art**, and more .
