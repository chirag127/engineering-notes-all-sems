### Blockchain for Government

- Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that enables secure and transparent transactions among multiple parties without intermediaries.
- Blockchain can improve government services and foster fair and transparent citizen rights by:
  - Streamlining processes and reducing costs, fraud, waste, and abuse  .
  - Protecting sensitive data and eliminating single points of failure using cryptography  .
  - Increasing trust and accountability by providing immutable and verifiable records of transactions  .
- Some of the use cases of blockchain for government are:
  - Land registry: Blockchain can enable secure and efficient registration and transfer of land and property rights, as well as prevent disputes and corruption . For example, the Georgian government’s land registry department pioneered a blockchain-based tool to track land ownership and real estate transactions within the country’s borders.
  - Identity management: Blockchain can provide a decentralized and self-sovereign identity system that allows citizens to control their own data and access government services without intermediaries  . For example, Estonia’s e-Residency program allows anyone in the world to obtain a digital identity and access various online services offered by the Estonian government.
  - Voting: Blockchain can enable secure and transparent voting systems that prevent fraud, manipulation, and coercion, as well as increase voter turnout and engagement  . For example, Sierra Leone used a blockchain-based platform to verify the results of its 2018 presidential election.
  - Supply chain management: Blockchain can enhance the traceability and accountability of government procurement and distribution of goods and services, as well as reduce inefficiencies and risks  . For example, the World Food Programme used a blockchain-based system to deliver food aid to Syrian refugees in Jordan.
  - Central bank digital currencies: Blockchain can enable central banks to issue and manage digital versions of their national currencies, which can improve financial inclusion, stability, and efficiency . For example, the Bahamas launched the world’s first central bank digital currency, the Sand Dollar, in 2020.
