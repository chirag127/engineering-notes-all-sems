### Consensus protocols for Permissioned Blockchains

- A consensus protocol enables all the parties of the blockchain network to come to a common agreement (consensus) on the present data state of the ledger .
- In a permissioned blockchain, all the participating nodes are known and chosen, but consensus is still required because we can’t assume that every node is trustworthy .
- Choosing the right consensus protocol for permissioned blockchain depends on factors like the extent of decentralization required, the level of trust among the participants, the performance and scalability of the network, and the security and fault tolerance of the system .
- Some of the common consensus protocols for permissioned blockchains are:

  - **Proof of Authority (PoA)**: This protocol assigns a set of validators who have the authority to validate transactions and create new blocks. The validators are selected based on their reputation and identity, and they are incentivized to act honestly and maintain the network integrity. PoA is suitable for private or consortium blockchains where the validators are known and trusted entities. PoA offers high speed, low cost, and energy efficiency, but it sacrifices some degree of decentralization and censorship resistance.
  - **Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT)**: This protocol is based on the Byzantine Generals' Problem, which is a classic problem of achieving consensus among distributed nodes that may be faulty or malicious. PBFT requires that each node in the network broadcasts its proposed value to all other nodes, and then collects and verifies the responses from a majority of nodes. If the majority agrees on the same value, then the consensus is reached and the value is committed to the ledger. PBFT can tolerate up to one-third of faulty nodes, and it provides finality and consistency of the ledger. PBFT is suitable for small to medium-sized networks that require high performance and security, but it has scalability limitations due to the high communication overhead.
  - **Raft**: This protocol is a simplified version of PBFT that uses a leader-follower model to achieve consensus. Raft elects a leader node among the network, and the leader is responsible for proposing new blocks and replicating them to the followers. The followers accept the leader's proposals and acknowledge them. If the leader fails or becomes unresponsive, a new leader is elected through a randomized timer mechanism. Raft ensures that the ledger is always in a consistent state, and it provides fast and deterministic finality. Raft is suitable for networks that do not require high fault tolerance or adversarial resistance, but it offers simplicity and efficiency.
  - **Tendermint**: This protocol is a hybrid of PBFT and PoS that uses a rotating leader and a voting process to achieve consensus. Tendermint divides the consensus process into two phases: propose and pre-commit. In the propose phase, a leader node is randomly selected and proposes a new block. In the pre-commit phase, the other nodes vote on the proposed block, and if more than two-thirds of the nodes agree, the block is committed to the ledger. If the leader is faulty or the block is invalid, the leader is changed and the process is repeated. Tendermint can tolerate up to one-third of faulty nodes, and it provides instant finality and high performance. Tendermint is suitable for networks that require a balance of decentralization, security, and scalability.
