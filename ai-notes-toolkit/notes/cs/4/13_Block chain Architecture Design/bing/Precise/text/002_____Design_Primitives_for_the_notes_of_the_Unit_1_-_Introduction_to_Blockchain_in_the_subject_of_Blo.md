### Design Primitives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

1. **Blockchain**: A blockchain is a distributed database that is managed by a peer-to-peer network. It is a type of distributed ledger technology that allows multiple parties to share access to the same data, while ensuring that the data remains tamper-proof and transparent.

2. **Blocks**: A block is a collection of data that is stored in a blockchain. Each block contains a record of multiple transactions, a timestamp, and a reference to the previous block in the chain.

3. **Hashing**: Hashing is a cryptographic process that converts data of any size into a fixed-size string of characters. It is used in blockchain technology to ensure the integrity of the data stored in blocks.

4. **Consensus Mechanisms**: Consensus mechanisms are algorithms that are used to ensure that all participants in a blockchain network agree on the state of the shared data. Some common consensus mechanisms used in blockchain technology include Proof of Work and Proof of Stake.

5. **Smart Contracts**: Smart contracts are self-executing contracts that are stored on a blockchain. They contain the terms of the agreement between parties and automatically execute when the conditions specified in the contract are met.

6. **Decentralization**: Decentralization refers to the distribution of power and decision-making among multiple parties. In the context of blockchain technology, it means that no single entity has control over the data stored in the blockchain, and all participants have an equal say in the decision-making process.

7. **Immutability**: Immutability means that data, once stored in a blockchain, cannot be altered or deleted. This is achieved through the use of cryptographic hashing and consensus mechanisms, which ensure that any attempt to tamper with the data is detected and rejected by the network.

8. **Transparency**: Transparency means that all participants in a blockchain network have access to the same data and can verify its authenticity. This is achieved through the use of public key cryptography, which allows anyone to verify the identity of the parties involved in a transaction and the authenticity of the data stored in the blockchain.

These are some of the key design primitives of blockchain technology. Understanding these concepts is essential for anyone studying blockchain architecture design.
