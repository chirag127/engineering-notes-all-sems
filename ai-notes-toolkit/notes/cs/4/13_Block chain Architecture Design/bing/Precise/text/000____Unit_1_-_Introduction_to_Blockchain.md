## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

1. **Definition**: A blockchain is a distributed database that is managed by a network of computers. It is a type of distributed ledger technology (DLT) that allows multiple parties to share access to the same data, while ensuring that the data remains secure and tamper-proof.

2. **History**: The concept of blockchain technology was first introduced in 2008 by an individual or group of individuals using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto. It was initially used as the underlying technology for the cryptocurrency Bitcoin.

3. **Key features**: Some of the key features of blockchain technology include decentralization, immutability, and transparency. Decentralization refers to the fact that the data on a blockchain is stored on a network of computers, rather than on a single central server. Immutability means that once data is added to the blockchain, it cannot be altered or deleted. Transparency refers to the fact that all participants on the network can view the data stored on the blockchain.

4. **Applications**: Blockchain technology has a wide range of potential applications, including in finance, supply chain management, and voting systems. It is also being explored for use in areas such as identity management, healthcare, and energy trading.

5. **Challenges**: Despite its potential, there are also several challenges associated with the use of blockchain technology. These include issues related to scalability, interoperability, and regulatory compliance. Additionally, there are concerns about the environmental impact of the energy-intensive process used to secure some blockchain networks.
