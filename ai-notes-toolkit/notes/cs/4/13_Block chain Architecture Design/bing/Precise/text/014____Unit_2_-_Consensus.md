## Unit 2 - Consensus

1. Consensus refers to a general agreement among the members of a group or community.
2. It is a decision-making process that seeks the consent of all participants.
3. Consensus is often used in situations where collaboration and cooperation are important, such as in the development of standards or policies.
4. The goal of consensus is to reach a decision that is acceptable to all members of the group, even if it is not the first choice of each individual.
5. Consensus decision-making can be time-consuming and may require compromise and negotiation.
6. Some of the benefits of consensus decision-making include increased participation, improved understanding of different perspectives, and greater commitment to the final decision.
7. Consensus decision-making can be challenging when there are strongly held opposing views or when the group is large and diverse.
8. There are various methods and techniques that can be used to facilitate consensus decision-making, such as brainstorming, discussion, and voting.
9. Consensus is not always possible, and in some cases, alternative decision-making processes may be more appropriate.
10. It is important to establish clear guidelines and procedures for consensus decision-making to ensure that the process is fair and effective.
