## Unit 2 - Consensus

Consensus refers to a general agreement among the members of a group or community. It is a decision-making process that seeks the consent of all participants.

- Consensus decision-making is a process that involves discussion, negotiation, and compromise to reach a decision that is acceptable to all members of the group.
- Consensus is not the same as unanimity. Unanimity requires that all members of the group agree, while consensus requires that all members consent to the decision, even if they do not fully agree with it.
- Consensus decision-making is often used in groups where members have a high level of trust and respect for each other, and where the goal is to make decisions that are in the best interest of the group as a whole.
- Consensus decision-making can be time-consuming and may not be practical for all situations. In some cases, a majority vote or other decision-making methods may be more appropriate.
- Consensus decision-making can be challenging when there are conflicting interests or opinions within the group. In these situations, it is important for the group to engage in open and respectful communication to find a solution that is acceptable to all members.
- Consensus decision-making can be an effective way to build group cohesion and foster a sense of ownership and commitment to the decisions made by the group. It can also help to ensure that the decisions made are fair and equitable, and that all members of the group have a voice in the decision-making process.
