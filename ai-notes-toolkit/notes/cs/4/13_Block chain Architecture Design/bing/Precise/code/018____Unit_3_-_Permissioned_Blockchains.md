## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

1. **Definition**: A permissioned blockchain is a type of blockchain where access to the network is restricted to a specific group of participants. 
2. **Access Control**: Participants must be authorized to join the network, and their actions on the network are subject to predefined rules and regulations. 
3. **Use Cases**: Permissioned blockchains are commonly used in enterprise and business settings, where sensitive data and transactions need to be kept private and secure. 
4. **Examples**: Some examples of permissioned blockchains include Hyperledger Fabric, Corda, and Quorum. 
5. **Advantages**: Permissioned blockchains offer greater control and security over the data and transactions on the network, as well as faster transaction speeds and scalability. 
6. **Disadvantages**: The trade-off for these benefits is a loss of decentralization and transparency, as the network is controlled by a select group of participants. 
7. **Comparison with Public Blockchains**: In contrast to permissioned blockchains, public blockchains are open to anyone to join and participate, and the data and transactions on the network are transparent and publicly accessible. 
8. **Hybrid Blockchains**: There are also hybrid blockchains, which combine elements of both permissioned and public blockchains, allowing for a balance between control and transparency.
