### Unit 6 - Use case 1: Insurance in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

1. Insurance is a means of protection from financial loss.
2. It is a form of risk management, primarily used to hedge against the risk of a contingent or uncertain loss.
3. An entity which provides insurance is known as an insurer, insurance company, insurance carrier or underwriter.
4. A person or entity who buys insurance is known as an insured or as a policyholder.
5. The insurance transaction involves the insured assuming a guaranteed and known relatively small loss in the form of payment to the insurer in exchange for the insurer's promise to compensate the insured in the event of a covered loss.
6. The loss may or may not be financial, but it must be reducible to financial terms, and usually involves something in which the insured has an insurable interest established by ownership, possession, or pre-existing relationship.
7. The insured receives a contract, called the insurance policy, which details the conditions and circumstances under which the insurer will compensate the insured.
8. The amount of money charged by the insurer to the insured for the coverage set forth in the insurance policy is called the premium.
9. If the insured experiences a loss which is potentially covered by the insurance policy, the insured submits a claim to the insurer for processing by a claims adjuster.
10. Block chain technology can be used in the insurance industry to increase efficiency, reduce costs, and improve the customer experience.
11. Block chain can be used to securely store and share data, automate processes through smart contracts, and reduce the risk of fraud.
12. Some potential use cases for block chain in the insurance industry include streamlining the claims process, improving underwriting, and enabling peer-to-peer insurance.
