### Design Primitives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

1. **Blockchain**: A blockchain is a distributed database that is managed by a network of computers. It is a decentralized and distributed ledger that records transactions across many computers in such a way that the record cannot be altered retroactively without the alteration of all subsequent blocks and the consensus of the network.

2. **Block**: A block is a container data structure that holds a list of transactions. Each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and transaction data.

3. **Hash**: A hash is a fixed-size string of characters that is generated by a hash function from a variable-size input. In the context of blockchain, the hash of a block is used to ensure the integrity of the data contained in the block.

4. **Consensus mechanism**: A consensus mechanism is a process used by a blockchain network to reach agreement on the state of the distributed ledger. Common consensus mechanisms include Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS).

5. **Node**: A node is a computer that participates in a blockchain network. Nodes can have different roles, such as validating transactions, creating new blocks, or storing a copy of the blockchain.

6. **Transaction**: A transaction is an operation that changes the state of the distributed ledger. In the context of cryptocurrencies, a transaction typically involves the transfer of funds between two parties.

7. **Smart contract**: A smart contract is a self-executing contract with the terms of the agreement between buyer and seller being directly written into lines of code. Smart contracts can be used to automate the execution of transactions on a blockchain.

8. **Distributed ledger**: A distributed ledger is a database that is spread across a network of computers. In the context of blockchain, the distributed ledger records all transactions on the network and is maintained by the consensus mechanism.

9. **Decentralization**: Decentralization refers to the distribution of power and decision-making away from a central authority. In the context of blockchain, decentralization means that no single entity has control over the network and the data stored on it.

10. **Cryptography**: Cryptography is the practice of secure communication in the presence of third parties. In the context of blockchain, cryptography is used to secure transactions and to control the creation of new units of a cryptocurrency.

These are some of the key design primitives that are essential to understanding the basics of blockchain architecture design. They provide a foundation for further study and exploration of this exciting and rapidly evolving field.
