## Unit 2 - Consensus

Consensus refers to a general agreement among the members of a group or community. It is a decision-making process that seeks the consent of all participants.

- Consensus decision-making is a process that involves collaboration and compromise to reach a decision that is acceptable to all members of the group.
- The goal of consensus decision-making is to reach a decision that is in the best interest of the group as a whole, rather than just a majority.
- Consensus decision-making is often used in situations where there is a high level of collaboration and cooperation required, such as in community organizations, cooperatives, and non-hierarchical workplaces.
- The process of reaching consensus can involve discussion, negotiation, and compromise to find a solution that is acceptable to all members of the group.
- Consensus decision-making can be time-consuming and may require a high level of commitment and participation from all members of the group.
- Some of the benefits of consensus decision-making include increased participation, improved communication, and a greater sense of ownership and commitment to the decision.
