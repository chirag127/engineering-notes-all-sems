### Hash for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Blockchain Architecture Design

Below are the key points to take note of when studying the introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Blockchain Architecture Design:

1. What is Blockchain?
   - A distributed ledger technology that allows for secure and transparent storage and transfer of information.
   - It is decentralized, meaning there is no need for a central authority to validate transactions.
   - It is immutable, meaning once data is stored on the blockchain, it cannot be altered or deleted.
   
2. How does Blockchain work?
   - Transactions are grouped into blocks and added to the blockchain in a chronological order.
   - Each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, creating a chain of blocks that are linked together.
   - Consensus mechanisms, such as proof of work or proof of stake, are used to validate transactions and maintain the integrity of the blockchain.
   
3. What is a hash?
   - A hash function is a mathematical algorithm that takes input data of any size and produces a fixed-size output.
   - The output, also known as a hash, is unique to the input data and cannot be reversed to obtain the original input.
   - Hashes are used to ensure data integrity and security in the blockchain.
   
4. Types of Blockchain
   - Public blockchain: Anyone can join and participate in the network. Examples include Bitcoin and Ethereum.
   - Private blockchain: Only authorized parties can join and participate in the network. Examples include Hyperledger Fabric and R3 Corda.
   - Consortium blockchain: A hybrid of public and private blockchain where a group of organizations come together to form a network. Examples include Quorum and MultiChain.
   
5. Applications of Blockchain
   - Cryptocurrencies: A digital currency that uses blockchain technology for secure and transparent transactions.
   - Supply chain management: Using blockchain to track the movement of goods and ensure authenticity and transparency.
   - Identity management: Using blockchain to store and verify identity information.
   - Smart contracts: Self-executing contracts that run on blockchain and automatically enforce the terms of an agreement.
   
By understanding these key points, you will have a solid foundation for further studying Blockchain in the subject of Blockchain Architecture Design.
