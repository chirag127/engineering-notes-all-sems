## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

Blockchain is a decentralized, distributed ledger technology that records transactions on a secure, transparent, and immutable platform. It was originally developed for Bitcoin, but its potential applications go far beyond just digital currencies.

In this unit, we will introduce the basic concepts of blockchain technology and its underlying architecture. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- A blockchain is essentially a database that stores information across a network of computers. It is decentralized, meaning that there is no central authority controlling the database.
- Each block in the chain contains a unique cryptographic hash, which serves as a digital fingerprint of the data it contains. This makes it virtually impossible to alter past transactions without the network detecting the change.
- Transactions are validated by a network of users, known as nodes, who use consensus mechanisms to ensure that the information being added to the blockchain is legitimate.
- Consensus mechanisms can take various forms, such as proof-of-work, proof-of-stake, or delegated proof-of-stake. Each mechanism has its own strengths and weaknesses, and can be tailored to suit the needs of a particular blockchain network.
- Blockchain technology has the potential to revolutionize a wide range of industries, from finance and healthcare to supply chain management and voting systems. Its transparency, security, and immutability make it an attractive option for businesses and governments looking to streamline their operations and increase trust among their users.

In the following units, we will delve deeper into the various components of blockchain technology, and explore some of its most promising use cases. By the end of this course, you will have a solid understanding of blockchain's potential to transform the way we interact with each other and the world around us.
