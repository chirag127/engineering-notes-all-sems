
## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

1. Blockchain is a type of distributed ledger technology (DLT) that stores records in a secure and immutable form. It is a decentralized and distributed digital ledger that records transactions between two or more parties in a secure and verifiable way. 
2. The blockchain network is made up of a chain of blocks that contain records of all transactions that have taken place on the network. Each block is linked to the previous block, forming a chain that is used to verify the authenticity of the records. 
3. Blockchain technology is used to store and transfer digital assets such as cryptocurrency, digital documents, and other digital data. It can also be used to create smart contracts, which are digital agreements that are executed automatically when certain conditions are met. 
4. Blockchain technology has a number of advantages over traditional databases, including increased security, improved transparency, and faster transaction speeds. It also eliminates the need for a third-party intermediary, making transactions faster and more cost-effective. 
5. Despite its advantages, there are some challenges associated with blockchain technology, including scalability, energy consumption, and privacy concerns. Additionally, the technology is still relatively new and is not yet widely adopted.
