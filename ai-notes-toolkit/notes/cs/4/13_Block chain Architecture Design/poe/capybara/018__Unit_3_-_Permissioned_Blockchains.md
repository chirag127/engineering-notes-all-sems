## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

In this unit, we will cover permissioned blockchains, which are also known as private or enterprise blockchains. These are blockchain networks that are designed for use within an organization or a group of organizations. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

- Permissioned blockchains are different from public blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, which are open to anyone who wants to participate in the network.
- In permissioned blockchains, access to the network is restricted to a specific group of users who have been granted permission to participate.
- The main benefit of permissioned blockchains is that they offer greater control and privacy compared to public blockchains. This makes them well-suited for use in enterprise settings where data privacy and security are a top priority.
- Permissioned blockchains typically use a consensus mechanism that is different from that of public blockchains. One common consensus mechanism used in permissioned blockchains is known as a "proof of authority" (PoA) consensus algorithm. This involves a group of trusted validators who are responsible for processing and validating transactions on the network.
- Another key feature of permissioned blockchains is the ability to set up smart contracts. Smart contracts are self-executing contracts with the terms of the agreement between buyer and seller being directly written into lines of code. They allow for more efficient and secure transactions within the network.
- Some examples of permissioned blockchains include Hyperledger Fabric, R3 Corda, and Quorum.

In conclusion, permissioned blockchains offer a secure and private way for organizations to share and manage data within their network. They provide greater control and customization compared to public blockchains and offer a range of enterprise-focused features such as smart contracts and consensus algorithms. Understanding the basics of permissioned blockchains is an essential part of any blockchain education.
