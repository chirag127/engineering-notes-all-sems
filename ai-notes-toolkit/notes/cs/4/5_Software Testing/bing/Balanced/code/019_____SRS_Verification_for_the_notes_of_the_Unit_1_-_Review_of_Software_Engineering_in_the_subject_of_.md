### SRS Verification

- SRS stands for Software Requirements Specification, which is a document that describes the functional and non-functional requirements of a software system.
- SRS verification is the process of checking whether the SRS document is complete, consistent, correct, unambiguous, verifiable, and traceable.
- SRS verification can be done by using different techniques, such as:
  - Reviews: A formal or informal examination of the SRS document by the stakeholders, such as customers, developers, testers, managers, etc. Reviews can identify errors, omissions, ambiguities, and inconsistencies in the SRS document.
  - Prototyping: A technique of creating a simplified or partial version of the software system based on the SRS document. Prototyping can help validate the feasibility, usability, and functionality of the requirements.
  - Inspection: A technique of systematically analyzing the SRS document by following a predefined checklist of quality criteria. Inspection can detect defects, deviations, and violations in the SRS document.
  - Testing: A technique of executing the software system or its components based on the SRS document. Testing can verify whether the software system meets the specified requirements and expectations.
- SRS verification is important for ensuring the quality and reliability of the software system. It can help avoid or reduce the cost of rework, errors, and failures in the later stages of software development. It can also improve the communication and understanding among the stakeholders and facilitate the software design and testing activities.
