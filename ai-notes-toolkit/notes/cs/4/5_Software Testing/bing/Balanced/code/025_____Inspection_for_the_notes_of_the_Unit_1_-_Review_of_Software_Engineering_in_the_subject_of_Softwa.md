### Inspection for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Inspection is a formal and systematic technique for finding defects in software artifacts such as requirements, design, code, and test cases.
- Inspection involves a team of reviewers who follow a well-defined process to examine the artifact, identify defects, and suggest improvements.
- Inspection has several benefits, such as:
  - Improving the quality of the software product by detecting and removing defects early in the development cycle.
  - Reducing the cost and time of testing and maintenance by preventing defects from propagating to later stages.
  - Enhancing the communication and collaboration among the development team members by sharing feedback and best practices.
  - Increasing the confidence and satisfaction of the stakeholders by demonstrating the adherence to standards and specifications.
- Inspection consists of four main phases, namely:
  - Planning: The inspection leader selects the artifact to be inspected, the inspection team, the roles and responsibilities, the inspection checklist, and the schedule.
  - Overview: The inspection leader or the author of the artifact provides an overview of the artifact to the inspection team, explaining its purpose, scope, structure, and main features.
  - Preparation: The inspection team members individually study the artifact and identify potential defects using the inspection checklist and their own expertise.
  - Meeting: The inspection team meets to discuss the findings, classify the defects, and propose actions for correction and verification.
- Inspection requires some supporting activities, such as:
  - Logging: The inspection team records the defects and their attributes, such as severity, location, type, and cause, in a defect log.
  - Metrics: The inspection team collects and analyzes metrics, such as defect density, defect detection rate, inspection effort, and inspection coverage, to measure and improve the inspection process and its outcomes.
  - Follow-up: The inspection leader monitors and verifies the actions taken to correct and prevent the defects, and reports the status and results of the inspection to the stakeholders.
