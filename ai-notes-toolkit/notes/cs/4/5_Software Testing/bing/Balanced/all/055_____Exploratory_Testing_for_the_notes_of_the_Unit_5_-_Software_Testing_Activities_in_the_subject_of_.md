# Exploratory Testing

- Exploratory testing is an approach to software testing that is concisely described as simultaneous learning, test design and test execution.
- Exploratory testing seeks to find out how the software actually works, and to ask questions about how it will handle difficult and easy cases. The quality of the testing is dependent on the tester's skill of inventing test cases and finding defects.
- Exploratory testing is often contrasted with scripted testing, which is based on predefined test cases and expected results. Scripted testing is more suitable for verifying that the software meets the specified requirements, while exploratory testing is more suitable for discovering new information and uncovering unknown risks.
- Exploratory testing is not a technique, but rather a mindset or an attitude that encourages testers to be curious, creative, and critical. Exploratory testing is not random or ad hoc, but rather structured and disciplined. Exploratory testing is not a replacement for other types of testing, but rather a complement that can be used at any stage of the software development life cycle.
- Exploratory testing is based on the following principles:
  - The tester is actively involved in the testing process and uses his or her knowledge, experience, and intuition to design and execute tests.
  - The tester is free to explore the software and follow any interesting or suspicious paths that may reveal defects or insights.
  - The tester is constantly learning from the software and adjusting the testing strategy based on the feedback and observations.
  - The tester is documenting the testing activities and results in a concise and meaningful way, such as using notes, screenshots, videos, or bug reports.
  - The tester is collaborating with other testers, developers, or stakeholders to share information, ideas, and feedback.
- Exploratory testing can be performed using various techniques, such as:
  - Chartering: defining a specific scope, goal, or mission for the testing session, such as a feature, a user story, a scenario, or a risk area.
  - Session-based testing: conducting the testing in time-boxed sessions, usually between 60 and 120 minutes, and reporting the results using metrics such as session progress, session coverage, and defects found.
  - Heuristic testing: applying general rules or guidelines that can help the tester to generate test ideas, such as using mnemonics, checklists, or oracles.
  - Exploratory test automation: using tools or scripts to assist the tester in performing exploratory testing, such as generating test data, executing test cases, or checking results.
