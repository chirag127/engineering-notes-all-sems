# Tailoring Software Quality Assurance Program by Reviews

Software Quality Assurance (SQA) is a process that ensures that developed software meets and complies with defined or standardized quality specifications. SQA is an ongoing process within the software development life cycle (SDLC) that routinely checks the developed software to ensure it meets desired quality measures.

One way to tailor an SQA program is by conducting reviews. Reviews can be done at various stages of the SDLC and can help to identify and address issues early on, before they become more difficult and costly to fix.

Some common types of reviews that can be used to tailor an SQA program include:

1. **Requirements Review:** This review is conducted early on in the SDLC, during the requirements gathering and analysis phase. The goal of this review is to ensure that the requirements are complete, accurate, and testable.

2. **Design Review:** This review is conducted during the design phase of the SDLC. The goal of this review is to ensure that the design is sound and that it meets the requirements.

3. **Code Review:** This review is conducted during the coding phase of the SDLC. The goal of this review is to ensure that the code is of high quality, is maintainable, and meets the requirements.

4. **Test Review:** This review is conducted during the testing phase of the SDLC. The goal of this review is to ensure that the testing is thorough and that all requirements have been tested.

By conducting these reviews at various stages of the SDLC, an SQA program can be tailored to ensure that the developed software meets the desired quality standards. Additionally, these reviews can help to identify and address issues early on, before they become more difficult and costly to fix.
