# Inspection

Inspection is a type of peer review technique used in software engineering. It is a systematic examination of software artifacts, such as requirements, design, and code, by a team of individuals, with the goal of identifying defects and improving the quality of the software.

Here are some key points to remember about inspection in the context of software engineering:

1. Inspection is a formal process that involves a team of individuals, including the author of the artifact being inspected, a moderator, and one or more inspectors.
2. The goal of inspection is to identify defects and improve the quality of the software.
3. Inspection can be applied to various software artifacts, including requirements, design, and code.
4. The inspection process typically involves several stages, including planning, overview, preparation, inspection meeting, and rework.
5. During the inspection meeting, the team systematically examines the artifact and identifies defects.
6. After the inspection meeting, the author of the artifact is responsible for addressing the identified defects and making any necessary changes.
7. Inspection is a cost-effective technique for improving software quality, as it can help identify defects early in the development process, when they are easier and less expensive to fix.
