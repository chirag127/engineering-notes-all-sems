### Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

Verification is the process of evaluating the artifacts of a software development process to ensure that they meet the specified requirements. It is a static process, meaning that it does not involve executing the code. Verification is typically performed through reviews, inspections, and walkthroughs.

In the context of Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing, verification can be applied to various artifacts, including:

1. Requirements: Verification of requirements involves ensuring that they are complete, unambiguous, and testable.
2. Design: Verification of design involves ensuring that the design meets the requirements and adheres to design standards and best practices.
3. Code: Verification of code involves ensuring that the code meets the design and adheres to coding standards and best practices.
4. Test cases: Verification of test cases involves ensuring that they are complete, unambiguous, and testable.

Verification is an important part of the software development process as it helps to ensure the quality of the software being developed. It is typically performed throughout the development process, with each artifact being verified before moving on to the next stage of development. By performing verification, developers can identify and address issues early in the development process, reducing the likelihood of defects being introduced into the final product.
