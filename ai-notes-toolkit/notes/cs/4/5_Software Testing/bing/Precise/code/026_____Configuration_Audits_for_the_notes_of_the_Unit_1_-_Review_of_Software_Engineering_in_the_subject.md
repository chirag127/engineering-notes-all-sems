### Configuration Audits

Configuration audits are a type of review that is conducted to ensure that the development and operation of a system or product is in compliance with its specified requirements. These audits are typically performed as part of the configuration management process and are essential for maintaining the integrity and quality of the system or product.

There are two main types of configuration audits:

1. **Functional Configuration Audit (FCA):** This type of audit is focused on verifying that the functional and performance characteristics of the system or product are in compliance with its specified requirements. This includes verifying that all required features and capabilities are present and that the system or product performs as expected.

2. **Physical Configuration Audit (PCA):** This type of audit is focused on verifying that the physical characteristics of the system or product are in compliance with its specified requirements. This includes verifying that all required components and materials are present and that the system or product is built according to its design specifications.

Configuration audits are typically performed at key points in the development process, such as before the release of a new version or before the system or product is put into operation. They can also be performed on an ongoing basis to ensure that the system or product continues to meet its specified requirements.

In summary, configuration audits are an essential part of the configuration management process and play a critical role in ensuring the quality and integrity of a system or product. They help to verify that the system or product meets its specified requirements and performs as expected.
