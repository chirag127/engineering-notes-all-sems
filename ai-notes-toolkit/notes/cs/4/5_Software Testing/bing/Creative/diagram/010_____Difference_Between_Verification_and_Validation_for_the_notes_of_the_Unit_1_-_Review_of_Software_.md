### Difference Between Verification and Validation

- Verification and validation are two important activities in software engineering that ensure the quality of software products and processes.
- Verification is the process of checking whether the software conforms to the specified requirements and design. It answers the question: "Are we building the product right?"
- Validation is the process of checking whether the software meets the expectations and needs of the end-users and stakeholders. It answers the question: "Are we building the right product?"
- Verification and validation can be performed at different stages of the software development life cycle, such as planning, analysis, design, coding, testing, and maintenance.
- Verification and validation can use different techniques and methods, such as reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, audits, testing, prototyping, simulation, and demonstration.
- Verification and validation have different goals and objectives, such as ensuring correctness, completeness, consistency, reliability, usability, security, and performance of the software.
- Verification and validation have different outcomes and deliverables, such as verification reports, validation reports, defect reports, test cases, test results, and user feedback.
