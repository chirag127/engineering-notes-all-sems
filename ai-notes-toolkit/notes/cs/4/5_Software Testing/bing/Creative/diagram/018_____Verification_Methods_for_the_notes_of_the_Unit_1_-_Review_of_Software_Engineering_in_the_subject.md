### Verification Methods

Verification methods are the techniques that are used to check that a software product meets its specifications and requirements. Verification ensures that the software is built correctly and does what it is supposed to do. Verification is also known as static testing, as it does not involve executing the software.

Some common verification methods in software engineering are:

- **Peer reviews**: This is an informal and easy way of reviewing the documents or the source code of the software with a group of peers, looking for errors, inconsistencies, and improvements. Peer reviews can be done by anyone who has some knowledge of the software, such as developers, testers, managers, or customers. Peer reviews can be done at any stage of the software development life cycle, and can help to improve the quality and reliability of the software. Peer reviews can also help to share knowledge and best practices among the team members.  

- **Walk-throughs**: This is a formal and systematic way of reviewing the documents or the source code of the software with a group of experts, looking for errors, inconsistencies, and missing information. Walk-throughs are usually led by the author of the document or the code, who explains the logic and the design of the software to the reviewers. The reviewers can ask questions, make suggestions, and point out defects. Walk-throughs can be done at any stage of the software development life cycle, and can help to detect and correct errors early, before they become costly and difficult to fix. Walk-throughs can also help to ensure that the software meets the customer's expectations and needs. 

- **Inspections**: This is a formal and rigorous way of reviewing the documents or the source code of the software with a group of experts, looking for errors, inconsistencies, and deviations from standards. Inspections are usually led by a moderator, who guides the process and records the findings. The reviewers use a checklist of criteria to evaluate the quality and correctness of the software. The author of the document or the code is not present during the inspection, but is responsible for fixing the defects found. Inspections can be done at any stage of the software development life cycle, and can help to ensure that the software conforms to the specifications and requirements. Inspections can also help to improve the productivity and efficiency of the software development process.  

- **Analysis**: This is a method of verifying the software by using mathematical or logical techniques, such as models, simulations, algorithms, or proofs. Analysis can be used to check the feasibility, consistency, completeness, correctness, and performance of the software. Analysis can also be used to verify the properties and behavior of the software, such as safety, security, reliability, or usability. Analysis can be done at any stage of the software development life cycle, and can help to reduce the risk and uncertainty of the software. Analysis can also help to optimize the design and implementation of the software.
