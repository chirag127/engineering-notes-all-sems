### Tailoring Software Quality Assurance Program by Reviews

- Software quality assurance (SQA) is the process of ensuring that software products meet the specified requirements and standards of quality, reliability, usability, and security.
- SQA involves planning, implementing, monitoring, and improving the software development processes and practices to achieve the desired quality goals.
- SQA reviews are the activities of examining and evaluating the software products and processes to identify and correct any defects, deviations, or non-conformities that may affect the quality of the software.
- SQA reviews can be performed at different stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC), such as requirements analysis, design, coding, testing, deployment, and maintenance.
- SQA reviews can be classified into two types: formal and informal.
  - Formal reviews are the ones that follow a predefined process, have a specific objective, involve a structured team of reviewers, and produce a documented outcome. Examples of formal reviews are inspections, audits, and walkthroughs.
  - Informal reviews are the ones that do not follow a formal process, have a general or vague objective, involve a small or ad hoc group of reviewers, and do not produce a formal record. Examples of informal reviews are peer reviews, desk checks, and buddy checks.
- SQA reviews can be tailored to suit the specific needs and characteristics of the software project, such as the size, complexity, scope, domain, methodology, standards, and regulations.
- Tailoring SQA reviews involves selecting the appropriate type, level, frequency, and scope of reviews, as well as defining the roles, responsibilities, criteria, and procedures for conducting and reporting the reviews.
- Tailoring SQA reviews can help to achieve the following benefits:
  - Improve the effectiveness and efficiency of the SQA process by focusing on the most critical and relevant aspects of the software quality.
  - Reduce the cost and time of the SQA process by avoiding unnecessary or redundant reviews and activities.
  - Increase the satisfaction and confidence of the stakeholders by ensuring that the software products meet their expectations and requirements.
  - Enhance the communication and collaboration among the software development team and the SQA team by establishing clear and consistent expectations and feedback mechanisms.
  - Support the continuous improvement and learning of the software development process and practices by identifying and resolving the root causes of the quality issues.
