### Configuration Audits

- Configuration audits are a process of verifying that the software product conforms to the specified requirements and design documents.
- Configuration audits are performed by an independent team or authority that is not involved in the development or testing of the software product.
- Configuration audits help to ensure the quality, reliability, and consistency of the software product, as well as to identify and resolve any discrepancies or defects .
- Configuration audits are conducted at various stages of the software development life cycle, such as after the completion of a major milestone, a release, or a change request.
- Configuration audits are divided into two types: Functional Configuration Audit (FCA) and Physical Configuration Audit (PCA).
  - FCA verifies that the software product meets the functional requirements and specifications.
  - PCA verifies that the software product matches the physical characteristics and attributes defined in the design documents.
- Configuration audits are supported by configuration management, which is a process of controlling and documenting the changes to the software product and its configuration items (CIs).
- Configuration audits are essential for ensuring the traceability, accountability, and reproducibility of the software product and its CIs .
