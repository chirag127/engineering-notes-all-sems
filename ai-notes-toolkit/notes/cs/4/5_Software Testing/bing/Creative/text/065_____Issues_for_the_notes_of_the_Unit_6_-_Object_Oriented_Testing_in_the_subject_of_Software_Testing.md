### Issues for the notes of the Unit 6 - Object Oriented Testing in the subject of Software Testing

- Object oriented testing (OOT) is a testing approach that is based on the concepts of object oriented programming, such as encapsulation, inheritance, and polymorphism.
- OOT faces some challenges and issues that are different from those of traditional testing methods, such as procedural or functional testing.
- Some of the issues in OOT are:

  - **Testing base classes and derived classes**: OOT must ensure that the base classes and the derived classes are tested separately and together, taking into account the effects of inheritance and dynamic binding. Inheritance can introduce new faults or propagate existing faults from the base class to the derived class. Dynamic binding can make it difficult to determine which method will be executed at run time, depending on the type of the object .
  - **Testing abstract classes and interfaces**: OOT must also test the abstract classes and the interfaces that define the contracts or specifications for the subclasses or implementers. Abstract classes and interfaces cannot be instantiated, so they require special techniques to test their functionality and behavior .
  - **Testing encapsulation and information hiding**: OOT must respect the principle of encapsulation and information hiding, which means that the internal details of an object are not accessible or visible to the outside world. This can make it hard to test the state and behavior of an object, especially if it has private or protected attributes or methods. OOT may need to use reflection, stubs, or drivers to access or simulate the hidden parts of an object .
  - **Testing polymorphism and overloading**: OOT must deal with the complexity and diversity of polymorphism and overloading, which allow an object to have different forms or behaviors depending on the context or the arguments. Polymorphism can be achieved by inheritance, abstract classes, interfaces, or generics. Overloading can be applied to methods or operators. OOT must ensure that all the possible forms or behaviors of an object are tested and verified .
  - **Testing object interactions and collaborations**: OOT must also test the interactions and collaborations among objects, which form the basis of the object oriented design. Objects communicate with each other by sending and receiving messages, which can be synchronous or asynchronous, and can involve multiple objects or threads. OOT must check the correctness and consistency of the messages, the sequence and timing of the interactions, and the coordination and synchronization of the collaborations .
  - **Testing object states and lifecycles**: OOT must also test the states and lifecycles of objects, which determine how an object behaves and responds to messages. Objects can have different states, such as created, initialized, active, passive, or destroyed. Objects can also have different lifecycles, such as transient, persistent, or immortal. OOT must ensure that the state transitions and the lifecycle management of objects are correct and consistent .

- These issues in OOT require different techniques and strategies to address them, such as class testing, integration testing, system testing, regression testing, mutation testing, fault injection testing, etc  .
