### SRS Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- SRS stands for Software Requirements Specification, which is a document that describes the functional and non-functional requirements of a software system.
- SRS verification is the process of checking whether the SRS document is consistent, complete, correct, feasible, testable, and traceable.
- SRS verification can be done by using various techniques, such as:
  - Reviews: A formal or informal examination of the SRS document by the stakeholders, such as customers, developers, testers, managers, etc. Reviews can identify errors, ambiguities, omissions, and inconsistencies in the SRS document.
  - Prototyping: A technique of creating a simplified or partial version of the software system based on the SRS document. Prototyping can help validate the feasibility, usability, and functionality of the requirements.
  - Inspection: A technique of systematically analyzing the SRS document by following a predefined checklist of quality criteria. Inspection can detect defects, deviations, and violations in the SRS document.
  - Walkthrough: A technique of presenting the SRS document to a group of peers or experts, who ask questions, raise issues, and provide feedback. Walkthrough can improve the clarity, completeness, and accuracy of the SRS document.
  - Analysis: A technique of applying mathematical or logical methods to the SRS document to verify its properties, such as consistency, completeness, correctness, and testability. Analysis can prove or disprove the validity of the requirements.
- SRS verification is important because it can:
  - Ensure that the SRS document meets the expectations and needs of the stakeholders.
  - Reduce the risk of errors, defects, and failures in the software system.
  - Improve the quality, reliability, and maintainability of the software system.
  - Facilitate the design, development, testing, and maintenance of the software system.
  - Save time, cost, and effort in the software development life cycle.
