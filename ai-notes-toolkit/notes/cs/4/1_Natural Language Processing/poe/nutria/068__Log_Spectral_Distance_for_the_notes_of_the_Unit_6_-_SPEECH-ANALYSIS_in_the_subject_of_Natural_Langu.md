

### Log–Spectral Distance for Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in Natural Language Processing

1. Log–Spectral Distance (LSD) is a measure of the similarity between two speech signals. It is used to compare the phonetic content of two speech signals and measure the similarity between them. 
2. LSD is based on the idea that the spectral features of two speech signals can be used to measure the similarity between them. The spectral features are the frequencies and amplitudes of the speech signals. 
3. LSD is calculated by taking the logarithm of the ratio of the spectral features of the two signals. The logarithm is used to normalize the ratio and make it easier to interpret. 
4. LSD is used in many applications of Natural Language Processing, such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, and speech synthesis. It is also used to measure the similarity of two languages. 
5. LSD is a powerful tool for measuring the similarity between two speech signals. It is simple to implement and can be used to compare the phonetic content of two speech signals.
