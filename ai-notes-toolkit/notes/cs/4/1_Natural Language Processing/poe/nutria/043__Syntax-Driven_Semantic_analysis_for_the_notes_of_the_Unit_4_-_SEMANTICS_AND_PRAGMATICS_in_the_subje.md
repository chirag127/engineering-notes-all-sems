
### Syntax-Driven Semantic Analysis

Semantics and pragmatics are two branches of linguistics that are closely related. Semantics deals with the meaning of words and sentences, while pragmatics deals with the context of language use. Syntax-driven semantic analysis is a method of analyzing language that combines the two fields.

1. Syntax-driven semantic analysis takes into account the syntactic structure of a sentence when determining its meaning. This means that it uses the structure of the sentence (such as the order of the words) to determine the meaning of the sentence.

2. Syntax-driven semantic analysis also takes into account the context in which the sentence is used. This means that it looks at the situation in which the sentence is used in order to determine its meaning.

3. Syntax-driven semantic analysis is based on the idea that the meaning of a sentence is not determined solely by the words in the sentence, but also by the way in which the words are arranged.

4. Syntax-driven semantic analysis can be used to help determine the meaning of words and phrases that are not explicitly stated in a sentence. For example, it can be used to determine the meaning of idioms and metaphors.

5. Syntax-driven semantic analysis can also be used to help determine the meaning of words and phrases that have multiple meanings. For example, it can be used to determine the meaning of words that have multiple meanings in different contexts.

6. Syntax-driven semantic analysis can be used to help determine the meaning of words and phrases that are used in a figurative or metaphorical sense. For example, it can be used to determine the meaning of words and phrases that are used to describe abstract concepts.
