### Grammar Rules for English

In the study of Natural Language Processing, understanding grammar rules is essential for syntactic analysis. The following points outline some of the most important grammar rules for English:

- **Subject-Verb Agreement**: A sentence must have a subject and a verb that agree in number. For example, "He walks" is correct, while "He walk" is incorrect.

- **Punctuation**: Proper use of punctuation is essential in English grammar. Commas, periods, question marks, exclamation marks, and other punctuation marks have specific rules for usage.

- **Verb Tenses**: English has multiple verb tenses, including past tense, present tense, and future tense. Each tense has its own set of rules for usage, and it's essential to use the appropriate tense in a sentence.

- **Noun Phrases**: Noun phrases are essential elements of English grammar. They consist of a noun and any associated modifiers, such as adjectives, articles, and prepositional phrases.

- **Adjectives and Adverbs**: Adjectives describe nouns, while adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs. Proper use of adjectives and adverbs is essential for accurate communication.

- **Prepositions**: Prepositions are words that show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. Proper use of prepositions is essential for accurate communication.

- **Conjunctions**: Conjunctions are words that connect other words, phrases, or clauses in a sentence. Common conjunctions in English include "and," "but," "or," and "yet."

- **Passive Voice**: In English, a sentence can be written in either active or passive voice. Active voice is generally preferred, as it is more direct and concise.

- **Modifiers**: Modifiers are words or phrases that provide additional information about a noun, verb, or other word in a sentence. Proper use of modifiers is essential for accurate communication.

- **Word Order**: The order in which words are placed in a sentence can affect its meaning. In English, the typical word order is subject-verb-object, but this can vary depending on the sentence structure.

By understanding these grammar rules, you can better analyze the syntactic structure of English sentences and improve your overall language skills.
