### Requirements for representation for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

The study of semantics and pragmatics in natural language processing requires a clear understanding of the following representation requirements:

- **Lexical Semantics**: It is necessary to understand the meaning of individual words in order to analyze and understand the meaning of a sentence. A lexical semantics representation should include information about the part of speech, morphological structure, and meaning of each word.

- **Syntactic Structure**: The syntactic structure of a sentence provides valuable information about the relationship between words and the overall meaning of the sentence. A syntactic representation should include information about the grammatical structure of the sentence, such as the subject, predicate, and object.

- **Semantic Roles**: In order to understand the meaning of a sentence, it is essential to identify the semantic roles of the words in the sentence, such as the agent, patient, and instrument. A semantic role representation should include information about the role of each word in the sentence.

- **Pragmatics**: Pragmatics refers to the study of how context affects the meaning of language. A pragmatic representation should take into account the context in which a sentence is used, including the speaker, the audience, and the purpose of the conversation.

- **Discourse Structure**: Discourse structure refers to the organization of sentences and paragraphs in a larger text. A discourse representation should include information about the relationships between sentences and paragraphs, such as whether they are part of the same topic or argument.

- **Conceptual Knowledge**: Finally, a representation should capture the conceptual knowledge that underlies language use, such as the relationships between concepts and the ways in which they are connected.

Having a clear understanding of these requirements for representation is essential for studying semantics and pragmatics in natural language processing. By analyzing and understanding these representations, we can gain deeper insights into the meaning of language and develop more accurate and effective natural language processing tools.
