### Evaluating N-grams for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing.

N-grams are an essential concept in natural language processing. N-grams are a sequence of N words that occur together in a sentence or document. In this unit, we will be discussing the importance of N-grams in word level analysis and how to evaluate them.

Here are some points to help you understand the evaluation of N-grams:

1. __Frequency count__: The frequency count of an N-gram is the number of times it appears in a corpus. A high-frequency count indicates that the N-gram is common in the corpus, while a low-frequency count indicates that the N-gram is rare.

2. __Coverage__: Coverage is the percentage of the corpus covered by the N-grams. It is calculated by dividing the number of unique N-grams in the corpus by the total number of N-grams.

3. __Perplexity__: Perplexity is a measure of how well a language model predicts a given text. It is calculated by taking the inverse probability of the test set normalized by the number of words. A lower perplexity score indicates better prediction accuracy.

4. __Precision and Recall__: Precision is the ratio of the number of correctly identified N-grams to the total number of identified N-grams. Recall is the ratio of the number of correctly identified N-grams to the total number of N-grams in the corpus.

5. __Cross-validation__: Cross-validation is a technique used to evaluate the performance of a language model by splitting the corpus into training and testing sets. The model is trained on the training set and then tested on the testing set. This helps to determine the accuracy of the model and identify overfitting or underfitting.

6. __Smoothing__: Smoothing is a technique used to handle N-grams that are not present in the corpus. It involves adding a small constant to the frequency count of each N-gram to avoid zero counts.

In conclusion, N-grams are an essential concept in natural language processing, and their evaluation is crucial for building accurate language models. By understanding the frequency count, coverage, perplexity, precision and recall, cross-validation, and smoothing techniques, you can evaluate and improve the performance of your language model.
