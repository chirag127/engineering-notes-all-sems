### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

English morphology is the study of the internal structure of words and how they are formed. In this unit, we will discuss the basics of English morphology and its importance in natural language processing.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying English morphology:

1. Words in English can be broken down into smaller units called morphemes. A morpheme is the smallest unit of meaning in a language. For example, the word "unhappy" can be broken down into two morphemes: "un-" and "happy". "Un-" is a prefix that means "not", and "happy" is the root word.

2. English has two types of morphemes: free morphemes and bound morphemes. Free morphemes can stand alone as words, such as "cat" or "happy". Bound morphemes, on the other hand, cannot stand alone and must be attached to a free morpheme. For example, the suffix "-ness" is a bound morpheme that can be added to the end of "happi-" to form the word "happiness".

3. English morphology also includes the study of inflectional and derivational morphemes. Inflectional morphemes are added to a word to change its grammatical function, such as adding "-s" to the end of a verb to make it third person singular. Derivational morphemes, on the other hand, are added to a word to create a new word with a different meaning, such as adding the suffix "-ly" to the adjective "quick" to form the adverb "quickly".

4. Irregularities in English morphology can make it difficult for natural language processing systems to accurately understand and analyze text. For example, irregular verbs like "go" have unique past tense forms like "went" that do not follow the standard "add -ed" rule.

5. English morphology is important in natural language processing because it allows for the identification and analysis of different types of words in text. By understanding the internal structure of words and how they are formed, natural language processing systems can better understand the meaning and context of text.

In conclusion, English morphology is a fundamental part of natural language processing. By understanding the basics of morphemes, inflectional and derivational morphemes, and irregularities in English morphology, we can better analyze and understand language in text.
