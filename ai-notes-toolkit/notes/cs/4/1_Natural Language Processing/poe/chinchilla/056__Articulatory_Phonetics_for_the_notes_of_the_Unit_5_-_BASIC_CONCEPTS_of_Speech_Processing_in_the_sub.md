### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Articulatory Phonetics is the study of how speech sounds are produced by the human vocal tract. It is a crucial aspect of speech processing and is essential for understanding the production of speech sounds.

Here are some key points to understand about Articulatory Phonetics:

- The human vocal tract consists of various organs such as the lungs, larynx, pharynx, tongue, lips, and teeth. These organs work together to produce speech sounds.

- Different speech sounds are produced by varying the position of the organs in the vocal tract. For example, the position of the tongue and lips determine the production of consonant sounds.

- Vowels are produced by varying the position of the tongue and the shape of the mouth. The position of the tongue determines whether the vowel is a front or a back vowel, while the shape of the mouth determines whether the vowel is a rounded or an unrounded vowel.

- Articulatory Phonetics also involves the study of speech disorders such as dysarthria and apraxia. Dysarthria is a motor speech disorder that affects the muscles used for speech production, while apraxia is a motor speech disorder that affects the ability to plan and coordinate the movements involved in speech production.

- Articulatory Phonetics is important for the development of speech recognition systems and speech synthesis systems. By understanding how speech sounds are produced, we can develop better algorithms for recognizing and synthesizing speech.

In conclusion, Articulatory Phonetics is a crucial aspect of speech processing and is essential for understanding the production of speech sounds. By studying Articulatory Phonetics, we can gain a deeper understanding of how speech sounds are produced and develop better algorithms for speech recognition and synthesis.
