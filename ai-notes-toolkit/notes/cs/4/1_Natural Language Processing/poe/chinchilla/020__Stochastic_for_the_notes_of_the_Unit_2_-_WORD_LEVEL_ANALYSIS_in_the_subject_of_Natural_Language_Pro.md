### Stochastic for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Stochastic techniques are widely used in natural language processing to model and analyze various aspects of language. Here are some key points to remember:

- Stochastic refers to the use of probability theory to model random processes. In the context of natural language processing, stochastic techniques are used to model the probability of various linguistic events, such as word occurrences, sentence structures, and semantic relationships.

- One popular stochastic technique in natural language processing is the n-gram model. An n-gram is a sequence of n words, and the n-gram model estimates the probability of a particular word given the preceding n-1 words. For example, a bigram model estimates the probability of a word based on the preceding word, while a trigram model estimates the probability based on the preceding two words.

- Another common stochastic technique is the Hidden Markov Model (HMM). HMMs are used to model sequences of observations, where the underlying states are hidden. In natural language processing, HMMs are often used for tasks such as part-of-speech tagging and speech recognition.

- Stochastic techniques can also be used for machine translation, where the goal is to translate text from one language to another. In this case, a probabilistic model is used to estimate the probability of a target sentence given a source sentence. This involves modeling the probability of word alignments, sentence structures, and other linguistic features.

- Stochastic techniques can be used for a variety of other natural language processing tasks as well, such as text classification, sentiment analysis, and named entity recognition. In each case, the goal is to model the probability of various linguistic events in order to make predictions about new data.

Overall, stochastic techniques are a powerful tool for modeling and analyzing language. By using probability theory to capture the inherent uncertainty of linguistic data, these techniques enable us to build more accurate and robust natural language processing systems.
