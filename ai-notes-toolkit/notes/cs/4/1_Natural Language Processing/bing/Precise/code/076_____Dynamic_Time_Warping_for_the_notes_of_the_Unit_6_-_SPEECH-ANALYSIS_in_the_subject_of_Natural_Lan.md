### Dynamic Time Warping

- Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is an approach that has been historically used for speech recognition .
- It is an algorithm for measuring similarity between two sequences that vary in time or speed .
- DTW has been used for speech and word recognition since the 1970s with sound waves as the source .
- DTW uses training data, which consists of time series values, in order to store a template for each anomaly .
- DTW is a method to measure the similarity of a pattern with different time zones .
- The smaller the distance produced, the more similar between the two sound patterns .
- Both sound patterns are similar, thus the two voices are said to be the same .
- The initial data on the speech recognition process is transformed into frequency waves .
- DTW is the most well-known speech recognition technique among the template-based technologies .
- In 1968, Vintsyuk proposed the use of dynamic programming (DP) methods for the element-by-element recognition of words .
- Such DP methods perform perfectly in an experimental test of the SD case .
