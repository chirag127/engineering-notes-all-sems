### Shallow Parsing

Shallow parsing, also known as light parsing or chunking, is a popular natural language processing technique of analyzing the structure of a sentence to break it down into its smallest constituents, which are tokens such as words, and group them together into higher-level phrases. This analysis is called shallow because it only goes one level deep and does not analyze the complete grammatical structure of the sentence.

Here are some key points to remember about shallow parsing:

1. Shallow parsing is used to identify the boundaries of higher-level phrases in a sentence, such as noun phrases, verb phrases, and prepositional phrases.
2. It is a faster and simpler alternative to full parsing, which involves analyzing the complete grammatical structure of a sentence.
3. Shallow parsing is commonly used in information extraction, text-to-speech synthesis, and other natural language processing applications.
4. There are several algorithms and techniques used for shallow parsing, including rule-based methods, machine learning methods, and hybrid methods that combine both approaches.
5. The output of a shallow parser is typically a tree structure that represents the grouping of words into phrases.
