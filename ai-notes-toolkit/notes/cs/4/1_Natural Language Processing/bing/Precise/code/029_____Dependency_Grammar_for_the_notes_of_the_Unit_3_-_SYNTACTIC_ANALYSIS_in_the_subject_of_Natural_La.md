### Dependency Grammar

Dependency grammar is a class of syntactic theories that focuses on the dependency relation between words in a sentence. It is a way of representing the syntactic structure of a sentence based on the idea that the syntactic structure of a sentence can be described in terms of binary asymmetric relations between words.

Some key points to remember about dependency grammar are:

1. Dependency grammar represents the syntactic structure of a sentence as a directed graph, where the nodes are the words in the sentence and the edges represent the dependency relations between the words.
2. In dependency grammar, the head of a phrase is the word that determines the syntactic category of the phrase and governs the other words in the phrase.
3. Dependency grammar is a non-configurational theory, meaning that the syntactic structure of a sentence is not determined by the linear order of the words in the sentence.
4. Dependency grammar is a monostratal theory, meaning that there is only one level of syntactic representation, unlike other syntactic theories that have multiple levels of representation.
5. Dependency grammar has been used in a wide range of natural language processing tasks, including parsing, machine translation, and information extraction.
