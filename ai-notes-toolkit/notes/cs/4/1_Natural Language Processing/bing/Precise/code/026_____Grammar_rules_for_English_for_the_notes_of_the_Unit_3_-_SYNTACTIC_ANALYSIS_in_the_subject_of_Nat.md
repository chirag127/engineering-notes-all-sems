### Grammar rules for English for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

1. **Subject-Verb Agreement**: The verb must agree with the subject in number and person. For example, "She sings" and "They sing".
2. **Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement**: A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number, gender, and person. For example, "John lost his keys" and "The girls lost their keys".
3. **Verb Tense Consistency**: The tense of the verb must be consistent throughout a sentence or a piece of writing. For example, "She walked to the store and bought some milk" and not "She walks to the store and bought some milk".
4. **Adjective and Adverb Usage**: Adjectives are used to describe nouns, while adverbs are used to describe verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs. For example, "She sings beautifully" and "The beautiful singer".
5. **Preposition Usage**: Prepositions show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. For example, "She is sitting on the chair" and "The book is under the table".
6. **Conjunction Usage**: Conjunctions are used to connect words, phrases, or clauses. For example, "She sings and dances" and "She sings, but she does not dance".
7. **Sentence Structure**: A sentence must have a subject and a predicate. For example, "She sings" and not "Sings she".
8. **Punctuation**: Punctuation marks are used to clarify the meaning of a sentence. For example, "She sings, dances, and acts" and not "She sings dances and acts".
9. **Capitalization**: The first word of a sentence and proper nouns must be capitalized. For example, "She is a singer" and "She is from New York".
10. **Spelling**: Words must be spelled correctly. For example, "She sings" and not "She singes".

These are some of the basic grammar rules for English that are important for syntactic analysis in natural language processing. It is important to have a good understanding of these rules in order to accurately analyze and understand natural language text.
