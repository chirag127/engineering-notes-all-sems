### Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION: English Morphology

Morphology is the study of the internal structure of words and the rules for forming words from their subparts, called morphemes. In the context of the English language, morphology covers the following key concepts:

1. **Morphemes:** The smallest units of meaning in a language. Morphemes can be either free (can stand alone as words) or bound (must be attached to other morphemes to form words).
2. **Roots and Affixes:** Roots are the core morphemes that carry the main meaning of a word. Affixes are bound morphemes that are added to roots to modify their meaning or grammatical function. Affixes can be prefixes (added to the beginning of a root), suffixes (added to the end of a root), or infixes (inserted into the middle of a root).
3. **Inflectional and Derivational Morphology:** Inflectional morphology deals with the changes in the form of a word to reflect grammatical information, such as tense, number, or case. Derivational morphology, on the other hand, deals with the formation of new words by adding affixes to roots or by other means, such as compounding or conversion.
4. **Word Formation Processes:** There are several ways in which new words can be formed in English, including affixation, compounding, conversion, clipping, blending, back-formation, and acronymy.

These are the basic concepts of English morphology that are covered in the first unit of the Natural Language Processing course. Understanding these concepts is essential for further study in the field.
