### Speech Distortion Measures

Speech distortion measures are used to evaluate the quality of speech signals after they have been processed by a speech enhancement algorithm. These measures provide an objective way to assess the performance of speech enhancement algorithms.

Some common speech distortion measures include:

1. Segmental Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNRseg): This measure calculates the average SNR of short segments of the speech signal. It is commonly used to evaluate the performance of noise reduction algorithms.

2. Perceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality (PESQ): This measure is based on a perceptual model of human hearing and is used to evaluate the quality of speech signals in terms of their perceived quality by human listeners.

3. Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR): This measure calculates the likelihood of the observed speech signal given the clean speech signal. It is commonly used to evaluate the performance of speech enhancement algorithms that aim to estimate the clean speech signal from the noisy speech signal.

4. Itakura-Saito (IS) Distance: This measure calculates the distance between the spectral envelopes of the clean and processed speech signals. It is commonly used to evaluate the performance of speech enhancement algorithms that aim to modify the spectral envelope of the speech signal.

5. Weighted Spectral Slope (WSS) Measure: This measure calculates the difference between the spectral slopes of the clean and processed speech signals. It is commonly used to evaluate the performance of speech enhancement algorithms that aim to modify the spectral slope of the speech signal.

These are some of the common speech distortion measures used in the field of Natural Language Processing. They provide an objective way to evaluate the performance of speech enhancement algorithms and can be useful for researchers and practitioners working in this field.
