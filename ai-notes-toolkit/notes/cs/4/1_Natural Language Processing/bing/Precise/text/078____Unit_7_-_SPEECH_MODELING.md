## Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING

Speech modeling is the process of representing human speech in a way that can be understood and manipulated by a computer. This involves analyzing the acoustic properties of speech and developing mathematical models to represent them. Some of the key concepts in speech modeling include:

1. **Acoustic Features**: These are the measurable properties of speech, such as pitch, intensity, and formants. These features are extracted from the speech signal and used to represent the speech in a computer-readable format.

2. **Phonemes**: These are the basic units of sound in a language. Each language has a unique set of phonemes, and speech modeling involves identifying the phonemes present in a given speech signal.

3. **Hidden Markov Models (HMMs)**: These are statistical models used to represent the temporal structure of speech. HMMs are commonly used in speech recognition systems to model the sequence of phonemes in a given speech signal.

4. **Neural Networks**: These are machine learning models that can be used to model complex relationships between inputs and outputs. Neural networks are increasingly being used in speech modeling to improve the accuracy of speech recognition systems.

5. **Text-to-Speech (TTS)**: This is the process of converting written text into synthetic speech. TTS systems use speech modeling techniques to generate speech that sounds natural and is easily understood by human listeners.

Speech modeling is a complex and rapidly evolving field, with ongoing research aimed at improving the accuracy and naturalness of computer-generated speech. It has numerous applications, including speech recognition, speech synthesis, and voice-based user interfaces.
