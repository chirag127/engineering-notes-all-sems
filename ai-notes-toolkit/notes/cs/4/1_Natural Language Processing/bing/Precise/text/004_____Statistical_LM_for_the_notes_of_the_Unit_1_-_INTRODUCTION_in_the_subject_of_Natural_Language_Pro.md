### Statistical LM for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Statistical Language Model (LM) is a probability distribution over sequences of words.
- It assigns a probability to a sequence of words, based on the likelihood of that sequence occurring in a given language.
- Statistical LMs are used in various natural language processing tasks, such as speech recognition, machine translation, and text generation.
- The goal of a statistical LM is to estimate the probability of a word given its previous words, i.e. P(w_n | w_1, w_2, ..., w_(n-1)).
- There are several types of statistical LMs, including n-gram models, neural network-based models, and latent variable models.
- N-gram models are the simplest type of statistical LM, where the probability of a word is estimated based on the previous n-1 words.
- Neural network-based models, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and transformers, can capture long-range dependencies between words and are commonly used in state-of-the-art natural language processing systems.
- Latent variable models, such as topic models and hidden Markov models, introduce hidden variables to capture the underlying structure of the text.
