## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics and pragmatics are two branches of linguistics that deal with meaning in language. Semantics is concerned with the meaning of words, phrases, and sentences, while pragmatics is concerned with how context influences the interpretation of meaning.

1. **Semantics**:
    - Deals with the meaning of words, phrases, and sentences.
    - Concerned with the relationship between linguistic expressions and the concepts they represent.
    - Studies the meaning of words in isolation and in context.
    - Includes the study of synonyms, antonyms, homonyms, and polysemy.
    - Also includes the study of figurative language, such as metaphors and idioms.

2. **Pragmatics**:
    - Deals with how context influences the interpretation of meaning.
    - Concerned with how speakers use language to achieve their communicative goals.
    - Studies how speakers use language to convey information, make requests, give orders, and perform other speech acts.
    - Includes the study of implicature, presupposition, and speech acts.
    - Also includes the study of politeness, face-saving, and other social aspects of language use.

In summary, semantics and pragmatics are two closely related branches of linguistics that deal with meaning in language. Semantics focuses on the meaning of words, phrases, and sentences, while pragmatics focuses on how context influences the interpretation of meaning. Both fields are important for understanding how language is used to convey meaning and achieve communicative goals.
