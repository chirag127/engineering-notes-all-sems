### Probabilistic CFG

Probabilistic Context-Free Grammar (PCFG) is a type of Context-Free Grammar (CFG) that associates a probability with each production rule. The probability of a production rule is the likelihood of its usage in generating a sentence in the language.

- A PCFG is defined as a 5-tuple (N, Σ, R, S, P), where N is a set of non-terminal symbols, Σ is a set of terminal symbols, R is a set of production rules, S is the start symbol, and P is a function that assigns a probability to each production rule in R.
- The probabilities of all production rules with the same left-hand side must sum to 1.
- The probability of a parse tree generated by a PCFG is the product of the probabilities of the production rules used to generate it.
- PCFGs can be used for syntactic analysis by finding the most probable parse tree for a given sentence.
- PCFGs can be learned from a treebank, which is a corpus of sentences annotated with their parse trees.
- The learning process involves estimating the probabilities of the production rules based on their frequency of occurrence in the treebank.
- PCFGs can also be used for language generation by sampling production rules based on their probabilities to generate sentences.
