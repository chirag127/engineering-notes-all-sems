### Transformation-based tagging for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Transformation-based tagging, also known as Brill tagging, is a rule-based approach to part-of-speech tagging.
- It was introduced by Eric Brill in 1995 and is based on the idea of applying a series of transformation rules to a text to improve the accuracy of the initial part-of-speech tags.
- The initial part-of-speech tags can be assigned using a simple method such as the most frequent tag for each word.
- The transformation rules are learned from a training corpus and are applied in a specific order to maximize the accuracy of the final tags.
- The rules are of the form "change tag A to tag B in the context C" and can capture linguistic phenomena such as agreement and government.
- Transformation-based tagging has been shown to be effective and efficient, and has been widely used in natural language processing tasks.
- It is particularly well-suited for languages with rich morphology, where the number of possible tags for a word can be very large.
- However, it requires a large annotated training corpus to learn the transformation rules, and the quality of the final tags depends on the quality of the initial tags and the training corpus.
