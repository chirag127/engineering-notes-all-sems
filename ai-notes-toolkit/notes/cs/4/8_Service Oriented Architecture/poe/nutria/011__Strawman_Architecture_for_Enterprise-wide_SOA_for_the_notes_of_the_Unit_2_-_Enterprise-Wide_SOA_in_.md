
### Strawman Architecture for Enterprise-wide SOA 

1. A strawman architecture for enterprise-wide SOA is a blueprint for how an organization can implement a service-oriented architecture. 
2. It typically involves a set of components that are connected and interoperable, enabling the organization to achieve its desired goals. 
3. The components of a strawman architecture may include service interfaces, service layers, service brokers, service registries, service containers, service providers, service consumers, and service repositories. 
4. Service interfaces are the connectors between two or more services and allow them to interact with each other. 
5. Service layers are the building blocks of a service-oriented architecture and provide the foundation for the services. 
6. Service brokers are responsible for managing the interactions between services and providing the necessary infrastructure. 
7. Service registries store information about the services, such as their capabilities, availability, and service level agreements. 
8. Service containers are the containers in which services are deployed and managed. 
9. Service providers are responsible for providing the services to the consumers. 
10. Service consumers are the clients of the services and use the services to fulfill their needs. 
11. Service repositories are the databases that store the services and their associated metadata. 
12. The implementation of a strawman architecture for an enterprise-wide SOA requires careful planning and consideration of the organization's goals, objectives, and resources.
