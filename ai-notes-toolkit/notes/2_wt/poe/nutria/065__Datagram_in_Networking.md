
#### Datagram in Networking

* A datagram is a self-contained, independent entity of data carrying sufficient information to be routed from the source to the destination computer without reliance on earlier exchanges between this source and destination computer and the transporting network.
* Datagrams are typically used in connectionless networks, such as the Internet Protocol (IP). In a connectionless network, each packet is routed independently, and is not acknowledged by the receiver.
* In contrast to connection-oriented networks, datagrams do not require a prior connection to be established before data is sent. This makes them suitable for applications that need to send data quickly, such as streaming audio and video.
* Datagrams are also used in applications that require low latency, such as voice over IP (VoIP).
* The main advantages of datagrams are their simplicity and low overhead. Since the data is sent without creating a connection, the overhead associated with setting up a connection is eliminated. This makes them suitable for applications that require low latency and high throughput.
