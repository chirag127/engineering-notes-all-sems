#### Internet Addressing in Networking

- Internet addressing is the process of assigning unique identifiers to devices and services on the Internet.
- The most common form of Internet addressing is the Internet Protocol (IP) addressing, which uses numerical labels to identify devices and networks on the Internet.
- IP addresses are divided into two versions: IPv4 and IPv6. IPv4 addresses are 32-bit numbers, while IPv6 addresses are 128-bit numbers.
- IPv4 addresses are written in dotted-decimal notation, such as 192.168.1.1, where each dot separates a byte (8 bits) of the address. IPv6 addresses are written in hexadecimal notation, such as 2001:db8::1, where each colon separates a 16-bit segment of the address.
- IP addresses are hierarchical, meaning that they consist of two parts: a network prefix and a host identifier. The network prefix identifies the network or subnet that the device belongs to, while the host identifier identifies the specific device within the network or subnet.
- The network prefix and the host identifier are separated by a slash (/) followed by a number that indicates the length of the network prefix in bits. For example, 192.168.1.1/24 means that the network prefix is 192.168.1 and the host identifier is 1, and the network prefix is 24 bits long.
- The length of the network prefix can vary depending on the size and structure of the network. The longer the network prefix, the smaller the host identifier, and vice versa. The network prefix can be assigned by an Internet service provider (ISP) or a network administrator, while the host identifier can be assigned by the device itself or by a DHCP server.
- IP addresses are not permanent and can change over time. A device can have multiple IP addresses, such as a public IP address that is visible to the Internet and a private IP address that is only used within a local network. A device can also have both an IPv4 address and an IPv6 address, which are called dual-stack devices.
- IP addresses are not enough to communicate on the Internet, as they only identify devices and networks, not services or applications. To identify services or applications, another form of Internet addressing is used, which is the port number. A port number is a 16-bit number that is appended to the IP address, separated by a colon (:). For example, 192.168.1.1:80 means that the device with the IP address 192.168.1.1 is running a service or application on port 80, which is the default port for HTTP (web) servers.
- Port numbers are divided into three ranges: well-known ports (0-1023), registered ports (1024-49151), and dynamic or ephemeral ports (49152-65535). Well-known ports are reserved for common services or applications, such as HTTP (80), FTP (21), and SSH (22). Registered ports are assigned by the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA) to specific services or applications, such as Skype (49175) and Minecraft (25565). Dynamic or ephemeral ports are randomly chosen by the device or the application when initiating a connection, and are usually used for short-lived or temporary connections.
- IP addresses and port numbers are the basic elements of Internet addressing, but they are not very user-friendly or memorable. To make Internet addressing more convenient and meaningful, another form of Internet addressing is used, which is the domain name. A domain name is a textual label that corresponds to an IP address or a group of IP addresses, such as www.google.com or www.wikipedia.org. Domain names are easier to remember and type than IP addresses, and they can also reflect the name or purpose of the service or application.
- Domain names are hierarchical, meaning that they consist of multiple parts separated by dots (.). The rightmost part of a domain name is called the top-level domain (TLD), which indicates the category or the country of the domain name, such as .com, .org, .edu, or .uk. The leftmost part of a domain name is called the second-level domain (SLD), which is usually the name of the organization or the service that owns the domain name, such as google, wikipedia, or amazon. The middle parts of a domain name are called subdomains, which are optional and can be used to further specify or organize the domain name, such as mail.google.com or en.wikipedia.org.
