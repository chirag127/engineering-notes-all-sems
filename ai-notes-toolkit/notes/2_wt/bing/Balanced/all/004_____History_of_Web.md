# History of Web

- The World Wide Web (WWW) is a system of interlinked documents and resources that can be accessed through the Internet using a web browser.
- The Web was invented in 1989 by Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist working at CERN, a European research organization based in Geneva, Switzerland  .
- Berners-Lee proposed a "universal linked information system" that used several concepts and technologies, such as hypertext, Uniform Resource Identifier (URI), HyperText Transfer Protocol (HTTP), and HyperText Markup Language (HTML).
- The first web page was served on the open internet by the end of 1990, and in 1991, people outside of CERN were invited to join the new web community.
- The Web grew rapidly in the 1990s, with the emergence of popular web browsers, such as Mosaic, Netscape, and Internet Explorer, and web services, such as Yahoo, Amazon, and eBay.
- The Web also enabled new forms of communication, collaboration, and creativity, such as email, social media, wikis, blogs, podcasts, and web applications.
- The Web has evolved over time, with the development of new standards, protocols, and technologies, such as Cascading Style Sheets (CSS), Extensible Markup Language (XML), JavaScript, Ajax, Web 2.0, Semantic Web, and Web 3.0.
- The Web has become a global platform for information, education, entertainment, commerce, and social interaction, with billions of users and websites worldwide.
- The Web also faces many challenges and opportunities, such as accessibility, security, privacy, censorship, digital divide, net neutrality, and artificial intelligence.
