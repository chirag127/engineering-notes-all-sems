# Knowledge Centric Service (KCS)

KCS is an approach to knowledge management that focuses on leveraging the collective knowledge of an organization to improve customer service. It is based on the idea that customer service agents should be able to quickly access the collective knowledge of the organization in order to provide the best possible service to customers.

KCS involves the following steps:

1. Collecting and organizing knowledge: Knowledge is collected from various sources, such as customer service agents, customer feedback, and other sources. This knowledge is then organized into a knowledge base.

2. Sharing knowledge: The knowledge base is then made available to customer service agents, either through a web-based portal or a mobile app.

3. Leveraging knowledge: Customer service agents use the knowledge base to quickly access the collective knowledge of the organization and provide the best possible service to customers.

4. Measuring success: The effectiveness of KCS is measured by tracking the number of customer inquiries that are resolved using the knowledge base.

KCS is an effective way to improve customer service and increase customer satisfaction. By leveraging the collective knowledge of the organization, customer service agents are able to quickly and accurately respond to customer inquiries.

### Mechanical and Electromechanical Sensors: Introduction

Mechanical and electromechanical sensors are devices that can detect and measure physical phenomena such as pressure, force, displacement, temperature, and acceleration. They can be used in a variety of applications, such as industrial automation, robotics, and medical monitoring. 

The most common types of mechanical and electromechanical sensors are:

- Resistive Potentiometer: A resistive potentiometer is a variable resistor that is used to measure displacement, force, or other physical phenomena. It consists of a resistive element and a movable contact that changes the resistance as it is moved.

- Strain Gauge: A strain gauge is a device that measures the strain or deformation of a material. It consists of a thin metal foil that is bonded to the surface of the material. As the material is strained, the resistance of the foil changes, which can be used to measure the strain.

- Resistance Strain Gauge: A resistance strain gauge is a type of strain gauge that is made up of a resistive element and a movable contact. The resistance of the element changes as the contact is moved, allowing it to measure the strain of the material.

- Semiconductor Strain Gauges: Semiconductor strain gauges are strain gauges that are made up of semiconductor materials. They are used to measure the strain of a material by detecting changes in the electrical resistance of the material.

- Inductive Sensors: Inductive sensors are devices that use an inductive coil to measure displacement, force, or other physical phenomena. They are commonly used in industrial automation and robotics.

- Sensitivity and Linearity of the Sensor: Sensitivity and linearity are two important characteristics of a sensor. Sensitivity is the ratio of output to input, while linearity is the degree to which the output of the sensor is proportional to the input.

- Types of Sensors: There are a variety of types of mechanical and electromechanical sensors, including capacitive sensors, electrostatic transducers, force/stress sensors using quartz resonators, and ultrasonic sensors. Each type of sensor has its own advantages and disadvantages, and is best suited for certain applications.




## Unit 2 - Thermal Sensors: Introduction

Thermal sensors are devices used to measure temperature. They are used in a variety of applications such as medical, industrial, and aerospace. Thermal sensors can be divided into two main categories: gas thermometric sensors and thermal expansion type thermometric sensors. 

### Gas Thermometric Sensors

Gas thermometric sensors measure temperature by measuring the pressure of a gas. The most common type of gas thermometric sensor is the thermocouple. A thermocouple is a device composed of two dissimilar metals that are connected together. When the temperature of the two metals is different, a voltage is generated. 

### Thermal Expansion Type Thermometric Sensors

Thermal expansion type thermometric sensors measure temperature by measuring the expansion of a material due to a change in temperature. The most common type of thermal expansion type thermometric sensor is the bimetallic strip. This device consists of two strips of different metals that are connected together. When the temperature changes, the two strips expand or contract at different rates, causing the strip to bend. This bending can be used to measure temperature. 

### Acoustic Temperature Sensor

An acoustic temperature sensor is a device that measures temperature by measuring the speed of sound in a material. This device works by sending a sound wave through the material and measuring the time it takes for the wave to travel a known distance. The speed of sound is dependent on the temperature of the material, so the time it takes for the wave to travel can be used to calculate the temperature.

### Electro Analytical Sensors: Introduction

- Electro analytical sensors are devices used to measure electrical potentials in chemical systems.
- They are used for a variety of purposes, including the measurement of pH, redox potentials, and the concentration of ions in solution.
- An electrochemical cell is composed of two electrodes, an anode and a cathode, which are separated by an electrolyte.
- The cell potential is the difference in electrical potential between the two electrodes.

### The Cell Potential

- The cell potential is the difference in electrical potential between the two electrodes.
- It is determined by the concentration of the ions in the electrolyte and the redox potential of the electrodes.
- The standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) is a reference electrode used to measure the cell potential.

### Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

- The standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) is a reference electrode used to measure the cell potential.
- It consists of a platinum wire immersed in a solution of hydrogen ions and has a potential of zero volts.
- The SHE is used as a reference point for measuring other cell potentials.

### Liquid Junction and Other Potentials

- The liquid junction potential is the difference in electrical potential between two solutions separated by a semipermeable membrane.
- This potential is due to the differences in the ionic concentration of the two solutions.
- Other potentials, such as the Nernst potential and the Galvani potential, are also used to measure the cell potential.

### Polarization

- Polarization is the tendency of a cell to lose its potential due to the flow of current.
- It occurs when the cell is subjected to a current, causing a decrease in the cell potential.
- Polarization can be minimized by increasing the cell area or reducing the current.

### Concentration Polarization

- Concentration polarization is the tendency of a cell to lose its potential due to the accumulation of ions at the electrodes.
- It occurs when the concentration of ions in the electrolyte is not uniform.
- It can be minimized by increasing the flow rate of the electrolyte or reducing the current.

### Reference Electrodes

- Reference electrodes are used to measure the cell potential.
- They are usually composed of a metal wire immersed in a solution of known ionic concentration.
- Reference electrodes are used to calibrate other electrodes and measure the cell potential.

### Sensor Electrodes

- Sensor electrodes are used to measure the cell potential in a variety of applications.
- They are usually composed of a metal wire or a semiconductor material, such as a metal oxide.
- Sensor electrodes are used to measure the pH, redox potential, and concentration of ions in solution.

### Electro Ceramics in Gas Media

- Electro ceramics are used to measure the cell potential in gas media.
- They are composed of a metal oxide, such as zirconia, which is used to measure the cell potential.
- Electro ceramics are used to measure the pH, redox potential, and concentration of gases in the atmosphere.




### Smart Sensors: Introduction

Smart sensors are devices that can sense physical parameters, process the information and transmit it to other systems. They are used in a variety of applications, such as industrial automation, robotics, healthcare, and military.

Primary Sensors

Primary sensors are the first step in the sensing process. They convert physical parameters such as temperature, pressure, light, sound, and force into electrical signals. These signals are then converted into digital signals by transducers.

Excitation

Excitation is the process of providing an external stimulus to the primary sensors. This can be done by providing an electrical voltage or current, or by using optical, mechanical, magnetic, or acoustic excitation.

Amplification

Amplification is the process of increasing the amplitude of the signal obtained from the primary sensors. This is usually done using operational amplifiers or other electronic components.

Filters

Filters are used to eliminate unwanted frequencies from the signal obtained from the primary sensors. This allows the signal to be more accurately measured.

Converters

Converters are used to convert the electrical signals obtained from the primary sensors into digital signals. These digital signals can then be processed by computers or other devices.

Compensation

Compensation is the process of removing any errors in the signals obtained from the primary sensors. This is usually done using software or hardware.

Information Coding/Processing

Information coding/processing is the process of converting the digital signals into a form that can be understood by computers or other devices. This is usually done using algorithms or software.

Data Communication

Data communication is the process of transferring the digital signals from the sensors to other systems. This is usually done using wired or wireless communication protocols.

Standards for Smart Sensor Interface

There are several standards for the interface between smart sensors and other systems. These standards include the IEEE 1451.1, IEEE 1451.2, and IEEE 1451.3.

The Automation

The automation of the sensing process is the process of using computers or other devices to control the sensing process. This is usually done using algorithms, software, or hardware.
