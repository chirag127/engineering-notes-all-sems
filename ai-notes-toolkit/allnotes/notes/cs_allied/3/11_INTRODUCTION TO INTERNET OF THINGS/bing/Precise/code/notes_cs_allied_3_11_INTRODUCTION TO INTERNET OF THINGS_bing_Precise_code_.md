## Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

### Vision
The vision of the Internet of Things (IoT) is to create a world where everyday objects and devices are connected to the internet, allowing them to collect and share data, and interact with each other to improve our lives.

### Definition
The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the network of physical objects or "things" embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet.

### Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components:
- **Sensors/Devices**: These are the physical objects or "things" that collect data from the environment.
- **Connectivity**: This refers to the communication technologies that allow the sensors/devices to connect to the internet and share data.
- **Data Processing**: This involves the processing and analysis of the data collected by the sensors/devices to extract useful information.
- **User Interface**: This is the interface through which users can interact with the IoT system and access the information generated by the data processing.

### Architectural View
The architectural view of IoT can be divided into three layers:
- **Perception Layer**: This layer consists of the sensors/devices that collect data from the environment.
- **Network Layer**: This layer is responsible for the transmission of data from the sensors/devices to the data processing systems.
- **Application Layer**: This layer is responsible for the processing and analysis of the data, as well as the presentation of the information to the users.

### Technology behind IoT
The technology behind IoT includes a wide range of hardware and software components, such as sensors, microcontrollers, communication technologies, cloud computing, and data analytics.

### Sources of the IoT
The sources of the IoT can include a wide range of devices and systems, such as smartphones, wearable devices, home automation systems, industrial control systems, and environmental monitoring systems.

### M2M Communication
Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication refers to the direct communication between devices without the need for human intervention. M2M communication is a key component of the IoT, allowing devices to exchange data and interact with each other.

### IoT Examples
Some examples of IoT applications include smart homes, where devices such as thermostats, lighting, and security systems are connected to the internet and can be controlled remotely; and smart cities, where IoT technologies are used to improve transportation, energy management, and public safety.

### Design Principles for Connected Devices
When designing connected devices for the IoT, some key principles to consider include:
- **IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization**: It is important to follow standardization in the design of IoT/M2M systems to ensure interoperability between devices and systems.
- **Communication technologies**: The choice of communication technology is an important consideration in the design of connected devices, as it affects the range, bandwidth, and power consumption of the device.
- **Data enrichment and consolidation**: The data collected by IoT devices should be enriched and consolidated to extract useful information and improve the accuracy of the data.
- **Ease of designing and affordability**: The design of connected devices should be easy and affordable to encourage widespread adoption of IoT technologies.

### Digital Sensors

Some common types of digital sensors used in IoT include:
1. Temperature sensors: These sensors measure the temperature of the environment and convert it into a digital signal. Examples include thermistors and thermocouples.
2. Humidity sensors: These sensors measure the amount of moisture in the air and convert it into a digital signal. Examples include capacitive and resistive humidity sensors.
3. Light sensors: These sensors measure the intensity of light and convert it into a digital signal. Examples include photodiodes and phototransistors.
4. Motion sensors: These sensors detect movement and convert it into a digital signal. Examples include accelerometers and gyroscopes.

### Actuators

Actuators are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical motion. They are used in a wide range of applications, including robotics, manufacturing, and transportation. In the context of IoT, actuators are used to control physical systems and perform actions based on data received from sensors.

There are several types of actuators, including:

1. **Linear actuators**: These actuators convert electrical energy into linear motion. They are commonly used in applications such as opening and closing valves, and positioning machinery.

2. **Rotary actuators**: These actuators convert electrical energy into rotational motion. They are commonly used in applications such as opening and closing doors, and positioning machinery.

3. **Hydraulic actuators**: These actuators use pressurized fluid to generate motion. They are commonly used in heavy machinery and industrial applications.

4. **Pneumatic actuators**: These actuators use compressed air to generate motion. They are commonly used in industrial applications, such as manufacturing and assembly.

5. **Electromagnetic actuators**: These actuators use magnetic fields to generate motion. They are commonly used in applications such as electric motors and speakers.

### Wireless Sensor Networks

They are characterized as infrastructure-less, fault-tolerant and self-organizing networks which provide opportunities for low-cost, easy-to-apply, rapid and flexible installations in an environment for various applications.

A WSN consists of spatially distributed sensors, and one or more sink nodes (also called base stations). Sensors monitor, in real-time, physical conditions, such as temperature, vibration, or motion, and produce sensory data. A sensor node could behave both as data originator and data router. A sink, on the other hand, collects data from sensors.

Components of WSN include sensors, which are used to capture the environmental variables and which is used for data acquisition. Sensor signals are converted into digital signals. Radio Nodes are used to receive the data produced by the Sensors and sends it to the WLAN access point.

### Wireless Medium Access Issues

Wireless medium access control (MAC) is a crucial component of wireless communication. It is responsible for managing access to the shared wireless medium, ensuring that multiple devices can communicate effectively without interfering with each other. Some of the issues that arise in wireless medium access include:

1. **Interference:** Interference occurs when multiple devices transmit simultaneously, causing their signals to overlap and degrade each other's performance. This can result in lost or corrupted data, reduced throughput, and increased latency.

2. **Hidden Node Problem:** The hidden node problem occurs when two devices are within range of a common receiver, but not within range of each other. This can result in collisions, where both devices transmit simultaneously, unaware of each other's presence.

3. **Exposed Node Problem:** The exposed node problem occurs when a device is prevented from transmitting because another device, which is outside of its range, is transmitting. This can result in reduced throughput and increased latency.

4. **Fairness:** Ensuring fair access to the wireless medium among multiple devices can be challenging. Some devices may have stronger signals or be closer to the receiver, giving them an advantage over other devices.

5. **Energy Efficiency:** Wireless devices often rely on battery power, making energy efficiency a critical concern. MAC protocols must balance the need for reliable communication with the need to conserve energy.

These are some of the key issues that must be addressed in the design of wireless MAC protocols. A variety of different approaches have been developed to address these challenges, including contention-based and schedule-based protocols. A survey of MAC protocols and their relative strengths and weaknesses is an important topic in the study of wireless communication.



### MAC Protocol Survey

The Medium Access Control (MAC) protocol is a sublayer of the Data Link Layer in the OSI model. It is responsible for controlling how devices in a network gain access to a shared medium and transmit data. In the context of wireless networks, the MAC protocol is responsible for managing the transmission of data over the shared wireless medium.

In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), the MAC protocol plays a crucial role in ensuring efficient and reliable communication between devices. There are several MAC protocols that have been proposed for use in IoT networks, each with its own strengths and weaknesses.

Some of the most commonly used MAC protocols in IoT networks include:

1. **TDMA (Time Division Multiple Access):** This protocol divides the available transmission time into time slots and assigns each device a specific time slot for transmission. This helps to avoid collisions and ensures that each device has an equal opportunity to transmit data.

2. **CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance):** This protocol is commonly used in wireless networks and is based on the principle of listening before transmitting. Devices using this protocol will first listen to the channel to determine if it is free before attempting to transmit data. If the channel is busy, the device will wait for a random period of time before attempting to transmit again.

3. **Slotted Aloha:** This protocol is a variation of the original Aloha protocol and divides time into discrete slots. Devices using this protocol will only transmit data at the beginning of a time slot, which helps to reduce the likelihood of collisions.

These are just a few examples of the many MAC protocols that have been proposed for use in IoT networks. Each protocol has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of protocol will depend on the specific requirements of the IoT network in question.



### Survey Routing Protocols

Routing protocols are used to determine the best path for data transmission in a network. In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), routing protocols are used to transmit data from sensor nodes to the base station or sink node. There are several routing protocols that have been proposed for use in IoT networks, including:

1. **Flat Routing Protocols:** In flat routing protocols, all nodes in the network have the same role and responsibility. Data is transmitted from the source node to the sink node using multi-hop communication. Examples of flat routing protocols include Sensor Protocols for Information via Negotiation (SPIN) and Directed Diffusion.

2. **Hierarchical Routing Protocols:** In hierarchical routing protocols, nodes are organized into clusters, with each cluster having a cluster head. The cluster head is responsible for transmitting data from the nodes in its cluster to the sink node. Examples of hierarchical routing protocols include Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH) and Threshold-Sensitive Energy-Efficient Sensor Network (TEEN).

3. **Location-Based Routing Protocols:** In location-based routing protocols, the location of the nodes is used to determine the best path for data transmission. Examples of location-based routing protocols include Geographic Adaptive Fidelity (GAF) and Geographic and Energy-Aware Routing (GEAR).

4. **Data-Centric Routing Protocols:** In data-centric routing protocols, the data itself is used to determine the best path for transmission. Examples of data-centric routing protocols include Rumor Routing and Gradient-Based Routing (GBR).

These are some of the survey routing protocols that can be used in IoT networks. Each protocol has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of protocol will depend on the specific requirements of the IoT network.
