### Conceptual Framework for IoT

A conceptual framework is a set of ideas, assumptions, and principles that guide the design and implementation of a system or a solution. A conceptual framework for IoT can help to understand the components, functions, and interactions of IoT systems, as well as the challenges and opportunities that IoT brings.

One possible conceptual framework for IoT is based on the following elements:

- Physical object: This is any tangible entity that can be sensed, controlled, or actuated by IoT devices, such as a car, a lamp, or a plant.
- Controller, sensor, and actuator: These are IoT devices that enable the communication and interaction between the physical object and the Internet. A controller is a device that manages the logic and behavior of the IoT system, such as a microcontroller or a gateway. A sensor is a device that measures a physical property or condition, such as temperature, humidity, or motion. An actuator is a device that performs a physical action, such as turning on a light, opening a door, or watering a plant.
- Internet: This is the network infrastructure that connects the IoT devices and enables data transmission and exchange. The Internet can include various protocols and technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, or LoRaWAN.
- IoT system: This is the combination of physical object, controller, sensor, and actuator that forms a functional unit that can perform a specific task or service, such as smart home, smart agriculture, or smart health.
- Data center, enterprise, or cloud server: This is the computing platform that receives, stores, processes, and analyzes the data generated by the IoT devices and provides feedback, commands, or services to the IoT system, such as data visualization, machine learning, or automation.

The conceptual framework can be represented as follows:

```
Physical object + Controller, Sensor and Actuator + Internet = IoT system
IoT system: Gather + Enrich + Stream + Manage + Acquire + Organize and Analyze = IoT system with connectivity to data center, enterprise, or cloud server
```

The conceptual framework can help to identify the main components and functions of an IoT system, as well as the challenges and opportunities that IoT brings. Some of the challenges include security, privacy, interoperability, scalability, and reliability. Some of the opportunities include innovation, efficiency, productivity, and sustainability.

# Technology behind IoT

The technology behind IoT refers to the hardware, software, and communication protocols that enable the interconnection and interaction of physical devices over the internet. Some of the key technologies used in IoT systems are:

- **Sensors and actuators**: These are devices that can measure or control physical parameters, such as temperature, humidity, motion, light, sound, etc. Sensors and actuators are the interface between the physical and digital worlds, and they can be embedded in various objects, such as appliances, vehicles, wearables, etc. Sensors and actuators can communicate with each other or with a central controller using wired or wireless connections. 
- **Microcontrollers and processors**: These are the computing units that provide the intelligence and functionality to the IoT devices. Microcontrollers (MCUs) are small, low-power, and inexpensive chips that can run simple programs and perform basic tasks, such as data collection, processing, and transmission. Processors (CPUs) are more powerful and complex chips that can run operating systems and applications, such as data analysis, machine learning, and user interfaces. Some IoT devices may also use graphics processing units (GPUs) or field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) to perform specialized tasks, such as image processing, encryption, or parallel computing. 
- **Communication technologies**: These are the technologies that enable the data transfer between the IoT devices and the cloud or other devices. Communication technologies can be classified into two categories: short-range and long-range. Short-range technologies, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Zigbee, or NFC, are used for local area networks (LANs) that connect devices within a limited range, such as a home or an office. Long-range technologies, such as cellular networks, LoRaWAN, Sigfox, or satellite, are used for wide area networks (WANs) that connect devices across large distances, such as a city or a country. Some IoT devices may also use mesh networks, which are networks of devices that relay data to each other without a central hub, to increase the coverage and reliability of the communication.  
- **Cloud computing**: This is the technology that provides the storage, processing, and analysis of the data generated by the IoT devices. Cloud computing enables the scalability, security, and accessibility of the IoT systems, as well as the integration with other services and applications. Cloud computing can be divided into three layers: infrastructure as a service (IaaS), platform as a service (PaaS), and software as a service (SaaS). IaaS provides the basic computing resources, such as servers, storage, and networks. PaaS provides the tools and frameworks for developing and deploying IoT applications, such as databases, analytics, and machine learning. SaaS provides the ready-made applications and solutions for specific IoT use cases, such as smart home, smart city, or smart industry. 
- **Edge computing**: This is the technology that enables the processing and analysis of the data at the edge of the network, near the IoT devices, rather than in the cloud. Edge computing reduces the communication latency and bandwidth consumption, as well as improves the response time and privacy of the IoT systems. Edge computing can be implemented using edge devices, such as gateways, routers, or servers, that can perform data filtering, aggregation, compression, encryption, or inference. Edge computing can also be implemented using edge nodes, which are IoT devices that have enhanced computing capabilities, such as MCUs, CPUs, GPUs, or FPGAs. 

These are some of the main technologies behind IoT, but there are also other technologies that support the IoT systems, such as security, identity, interoperability, and standardization. The technology behind IoT is constantly evolving and improving, as new devices, protocols, and applications emerge and create new opportunities and challenges for the IoT domain.

### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- Connected devices are products that use the Internet of Things (IoT) or machine-to-machine (M2M) communication to interact with other devices, systems, or users.
- Designing connected devices requires considering various aspects such as functionality, value, security, scalability, flexibility, serviceability, connectivity, and hybridity.
- Some of the design principles for connected devices are:

  - Do your research: Understand the purpose, context, and user needs of the connected device. Identify the problems or opportunities that the device can address and the benefits it can provide.
  - Concentrate on value: Align the features and functionality of the connected device with the user value proposition. Avoid adding unnecessary or complex features that may confuse or frustrate the users.
  - Don’t forget about the bigger picture: Consider the potential interactions and integrations of the connected device with other devices, systems, or networks. Ensure the device is interoperable, compatible, and adaptable to different use cases and scenarios .
  - Remember about the security: Protect the connected device and the data it generates or consumes from unauthorized access, manipulation, or theft. Implement appropriate security measures such as encryption, authentication, authorization, and auditing .
  - Build with the context in mind: Design the connected device according to the environment and conditions it will operate in. Consider the physical, social, and cultural factors that may affect the device performance, usability, or acceptance .
  - Make good use of prototypes: Test and validate the connected device design using prototypes and feedback from users, stakeholders, and experts. Iterate and improve the design based on the results and insights gained from the prototyping process.
  - Ensure scalability: Design the connected device to handle increasing or varying amounts of data, traffic, or users. Optimize the device performance, efficiency, and reliability to support the growth and evolution of the IoT or M2M system.
  - Enable flexibility: Design the connected device to be modular, configurable, and customizable. Allow the device to be updated, upgraded, or modified to meet changing user needs, preferences, or requirements .
  - Support serviceability: Design the connected device to be easy to maintain, repair, or replace. Provide clear and accessible documentation, instructions, and support for the device users, operators, or technicians .
  - Optimize connectivity: Design the connected device to use the most suitable communication technology, protocol, or standard for the IoT or M2M system. Ensure the device can connect reliably, securely, and efficiently with other devices, systems, or networks .
  - Embrace hybridity: Design the connected device to leverage the advantages of both cloud and edge computing. Balance the trade-offs between latency, bandwidth, storage, processing, and cost for the device data and functionality.



# IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization

- IoT/M2M systems are composed of devices, networks, platforms, and applications that enable communication and data exchange between machines and humans.
- IoT/M2M systems can be divided into three major domains: the application layer, the service layer, and the network layer .
- The application layer is the domain where the end-user interacts with the IoT/M2M system, such as through a web interface, a mobile app, or a dashboard. The application layer provides the functionality and logic for the specific use case of the IoT/M2M system, such as smart home, smart city, or smart agriculture.
- The service layer is the domain where the IoT/M2M system manages the data and resources of the devices, networks, and applications. The service layer provides common services and capabilities for the IoT/M2M system, such as device management, data aggregation, security, and analytics. The service layer also enables interoperability and integration between different IoT/M2M systems and platforms, such as through standardized APIs and protocols.
- The network layer is the domain where the IoT/M2M system connects the devices and applications through various communication technologies, such as cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, LoRa, or Zigbee. The network layer provides the connectivity and transmission of data and commands between the devices and applications, as well as between different IoT/M2M systems and platforms.
- IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization are important for ensuring the scalability, reliability, security, and interoperability of the IoT/M2M systems and platforms. Standardization also facilitates the development and deployment of IoT/M2M systems and applications, as well as the innovation and collaboration among different stakeholders and sectors.
- Some of the organizations and initiatives that are involved in IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization are:

### Survey routing protocols for IoT

- Routing protocols are responsible for finding and maintaining routes between nodes in a network, especially in wireless and dynamic environments.

- Several routing protocols have been proposed and standardized for IoT, such as:
