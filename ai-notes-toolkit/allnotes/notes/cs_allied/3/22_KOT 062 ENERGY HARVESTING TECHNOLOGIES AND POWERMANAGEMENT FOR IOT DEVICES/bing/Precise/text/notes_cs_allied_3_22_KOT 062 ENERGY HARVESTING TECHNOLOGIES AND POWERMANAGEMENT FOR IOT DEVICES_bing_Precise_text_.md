### Photovoltaic Cell Technologies

Photovoltaic (PV) cells, also known as solar cells, are devices that convert sunlight into electricity. There are several types of PV cell technologies, including:

1. **Crystalline Silicon (c-Si) Cells:** These cells are made from silicon wafers and are the most common type of PV cell. They have high efficiency and long lifespan, but are relatively expensive to produce.

2. **Thin-Film Cells:** These cells are made by depositing thin layers of semiconductor materials on a substrate. They are less efficient than c-Si cells, but are cheaper to produce and can be made flexible.

3. **Multi-Junction Cells:** These cells use multiple layers of different semiconductor materials to capture a wider range of the solar spectrum and achieve higher efficiencies.

4. **Organic Cells:** These cells use organic materials, such as polymers or small molecules, to convert sunlight into electricity. They are less efficient than other types of PV cells, but have the potential to be produced at low cost using roll-to-roll printing techniques.

Each of these technologies has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of technology depends on factors such as cost, efficiency, and application. In general, c-Si cells are used for large-scale power generation, while thin-film and organic cells are used for smaller-scale and portable applications.




### Generation of Electric Power in Semiconductor PV Cells

- Photovoltaic (PV) cells, also known as solar cells, are devices that convert sunlight directly into electricity.
- The PV cell is composed of semiconductor material; the “semi” means that it can conduct electricity better than an insulator but not as well as a good conductor like a metal.
- In such cells, a small electric voltage is generated when light strikes the junction between a metal and a semiconductor (such as silicon) or the junction between two different semiconductors .
- This is due to the photovoltaic effect, where photons of light (either natural or artificial) with enough energy penetrate a photovoltaic cell, they can excite electrons to a higher energy state.
- When the semiconductor is exposed to sunlight, it absorbs the light, transferring the energy to negatively charged particles called electrons.
- The electrons flow through the semiconductor as electrical current, because other layers of the PV cell are designed to extract the current from the semiconductor.
- The power generated by a single photovoltaic cell is typically only about two watts .

### Piezoelectric materials

Piezoelectric materials (PMs) can be broadly classified as either crystalline, ceramic, or polymeric. The most commonly produced piezoelectric ceramics are lead zirconate titanate (PZT), barium titanate, and lead titanate. Gallium nitride and zinc oxide can also be regarded as a ceramic due to their relatively wide band gaps.

Piezoelectric materials can be classified into the following categories: 
1. Single crystalline material (such as quartz)
2. Piezoceramics (such as lead zirconate titanate, PZTs)
3. Piezoelectric semiconductor (such as ZnO 2)
4. Polymer (such as polyvinylidene fluoride, PVDF)
5. Piezoelectric composites
6. Glass ceramics (such as Li 2 Si 2 O 5 and Ba 2 TiSiO 6).

The piezoelectric effect is widely encountered in nature and many synthetic materials. The best known natural piezoelectric material is quartz, SiO2, while among thousands of synthetic materials, the most widely used and best understood are ferroelectric ceramics belonging to solid solutions of lead zirconate and lead titanate, Pb (Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT).

Piezoelectric materials generate internal electrical charge from an applied mechanical stress. The inverse piezoelectric effect is an applied voltage producing a mechanical stress in piezoelectric materials. Piezoelectric crystals, ceramics and polymers have useful applications in many industries.

### Electromechanical modeling of Lumped parameter model and coupled distributed parameter models and closed-form solutions

1. **Lumped parameter model:** A lumped parameter model is a simplified representation of a physical system that assumes that the system's behavior can be accurately modeled using a finite number of discrete elements or parameters. These elements are typically masses, springs, and dampers, and they are connected in such a way as to represent the system's dynamic behavior.

2. **Coupled distributed parameter models:** Coupled distributed parameter models are used to represent systems that cannot be accurately modeled using lumped parameter models. These models take into account the spatial distribution of the system's properties, such as mass, stiffness, and damping, and they use partial differential equations to describe the system's behavior.

3. **Closed-form solutions:** Closed-form solutions are analytical solutions to mathematical problems that can be expressed in a finite number of standard mathematical operations. These solutions are often preferred over numerical solutions because they provide exact answers and can be more easily interpreted.

### Power Conditioning and Losses

Power conditioning is the process of modifying the characteristics of electrical power to make it suitable for use by a particular device or system. This is necessary because the power generated by energy harvesting sources may not be directly usable by the device or system it is intended to power.

Losses in power conditioning can occur due to several factors, including:

1. Conversion losses: These losses occur when converting power from one form to another, such as from AC to DC or from one voltage level to another.
2. Transmission losses: These losses occur when power is transmitted from one location to another, such as from the energy harvesting source to the device or system it is intended to power.
3. Storage losses: These losses occur when power is stored in a battery or other storage device, due to the inherent inefficiencies of the storage technology.
4. Control losses: These losses occur when power is regulated or controlled, such as when using a voltage regulator or a maximum power point tracking (MPPT) controller.

To minimize losses in power conditioning, it is important to carefully design the power conditioning system and select the appropriate components. This can include using high-efficiency converters, minimizing transmission distances, and selecting the most appropriate storage technology.
