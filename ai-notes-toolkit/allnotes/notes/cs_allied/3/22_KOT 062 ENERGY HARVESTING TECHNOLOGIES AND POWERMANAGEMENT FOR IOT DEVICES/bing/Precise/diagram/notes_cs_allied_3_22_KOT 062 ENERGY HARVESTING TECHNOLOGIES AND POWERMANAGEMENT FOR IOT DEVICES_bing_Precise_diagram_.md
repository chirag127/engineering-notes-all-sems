### Photovoltaic Cell Technologies

Photovoltaic cells, also known as solar cells, are devices that convert sunlight into electricity. There are several types of photovoltaic cell technologies, including:

1. **Crystalline Silicon (c-Si) Cells:** These are the most common type of photovoltaic cell and are made from silicon wafers. They are divided into two categories: monocrystalline and polycrystalline. Monocrystalline cells are made from a single crystal of silicon, while polycrystalline cells are made from multiple crystals. Monocrystalline cells are more efficient but also more expensive.

2. **Thin-Film Cells:** These cells are made by depositing a thin layer of photovoltaic material onto a substrate. The most common types of thin-film cells are made from cadmium telluride (CdTe), copper indium gallium selenide (CIGS), and amorphous silicon (a-Si). Thin-film cells are less efficient than crystalline silicon cells but are also less expensive.

3. **Multi-Junction Cells:** These cells are made by stacking multiple layers of photovoltaic material, each layer being sensitive to a different part of the solar spectrum. This allows the cell to capture more sunlight and convert it into electricity. Multi-junction cells are the most efficient type of photovoltaic cell but are also the most expensive.

4. **Organic Cells:** These cells are made from organic materials such as polymers or small molecules. They are less efficient than other types of photovoltaic cells but are also less expensive and can be made using low-cost manufacturing processes.

These are the main types of photovoltaic cell technologies used in energy harvesting systems. Each technology has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of technology will depend on the specific application and requirements.



### Generation of Electric Power in Semiconductor PV Cells

Photovoltaic (PV) cells, also known as solar cells, are semiconductor devices that convert sunlight into direct current (DC) electricity. The process of converting sunlight into electricity using PV cells is called the photovoltaic effect.

1. The photovoltaic effect occurs when photons of light are absorbed by a semiconductor material, such as silicon, in the PV cell.
2. The energy from the absorbed photons is transferred to the electrons in the semiconductor material, causing them to become excited and move freely.
3. These free electrons are then captured by an electric field within the PV cell, which causes them to flow in a specific direction, creating an electric current.
4. The electric current generated by the PV cell can then be used to power electrical devices or be fed into the electrical grid.

PV cells are typically made from silicon, which is a common semiconductor material. Silicon PV cells are made by doping two layers of silicon with impurities, such as phosphorus and boron, to create an electric field within the cell. When sunlight is absorbed by the silicon, the energy from the photons causes electrons to be knocked loose from their atoms, creating free electrons. These free electrons are then captured by the electric field and flow in a specific direction, creating an electric current.

PV cells can be connected together to form a PV module, which can then be used to generate electricity on a larger scale. PV modules can be combined to form a PV array, which can be used to generate even more electricity. PV arrays can be used to power homes, businesses, and even entire communities.

In summary, the generation of electric power in semiconductor PV cells involves the conversion of sunlight into electricity through the photovoltaic effect. This process involves the absorption of photons by a semiconductor material, the transfer of energy to electrons, and the capture of these electrons by an electric field to create an electric current. PV cells can be combined to form PV modules and arrays to generate electricity on a larger scale.

### Piezoelectric materials

Piezoelectric materials (PMs) can be broadly classified as either crystalline, ceramic, or polymeric. The most commonly produced piezoelectric ceramics are lead zirconate titanate (PZT), barium titanate, and lead titanate. Gallium nitride and zinc oxide can also be regarded as a ceramic due to their relatively wide band gaps.

Piezoelectric materials can be classified into the following categories: 
1. Single crystalline material (such as quartz)
2. Piezoceramics (such as lead zirconate titanate, PZTs)
3. Piezoelectric semiconductor (such as ZnO 2)
4. Polymer (such as polyvinylidene fluoride, PVDF)
5. Piezoelectric composites
6. Glass ceramics (such as Li 2 Si 2 O 5 and Ba 2 TiSiO 6).

The piezoelectric effect is widely encountered in nature and many synthetic materials. The best known natural piezoelectric material is quartz, SiO2, while among thousands of synthetic materials, the most widely used and best understood are ferroelectric ceramics belonging to solid solutions of lead zirconate and lead titanate, Pb (Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT).

Piezoelectric materials generate internal electrical charge from an applied mechanical stress. The inverse piezoelectric effect is an applied voltage producing a mechanical stress in piezoelectric materials. Piezoelectric crystals, ceramics and polymers have useful applications in many industries.

#### Power Conditioning and Losses

Power conditioning is the process of converting the raw power generated by an energy harvesting source into a form that can be used by the electronic devices. This process involves regulating the voltage, current, and frequency of the power to meet the requirements of the device.

Losses in power conditioning can occur due to several factors, including:

1. Conversion losses: These losses occur when converting the power from one form to another, such as from AC to DC or from one voltage level to another.
2. Transmission losses: These losses occur when transmitting power over a distance, due to the resistance of the transmission lines.
3. Switching losses: These losses occur when switching power on and off, due to the energy required to turn the switch on and off.
4. Heat losses: These losses occur when power is dissipated as heat, due to the resistance of the components in the power conditioning circuit.

To minimize power conditioning losses, it is important to use efficient power conversion techniques and components, and to minimize the distance between the energy harvesting source and the device being powered. Additionally, proper thermal management can help to reduce heat losses.
