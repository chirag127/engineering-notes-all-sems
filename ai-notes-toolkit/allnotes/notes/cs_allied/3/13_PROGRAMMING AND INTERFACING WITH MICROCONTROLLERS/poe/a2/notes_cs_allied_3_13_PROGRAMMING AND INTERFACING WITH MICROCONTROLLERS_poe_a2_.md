- Microcontrollers are integrated circuits that contain a processor core, memory, and programmable input/output peripherals.

-- Read input signals from sensors.
-- Control the pins' voltages to drive actuators like motors.
-- Interpret and display data on output devices like LCDs.
- Reading datasheets is important to understand the specific requirements and features of your microcontroller and other components.
- Common sensors for microcontrollers include temperature, light, touch, and motion sensors. Common actuators include LEDs, LCDs, motors, and speakers.

### Iterative coding methodology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

1. Problem statement: The problem statement defines what needs to be achieved by programming the microcontroller. It could be automating a process, interfacing a sensor, controlling a motor, etc. A clear and precise problem statement makes it easier to design the solution.

2. Identifying inputs and outputs: The next step is to identify all the inputs to the microcontroller and the expected outputs. The inputs could be from sensors, switches, computers, etc. The outputs could be to motors, displays, computers, etc. Identifying all the inputs and outputs upfront helps in gathering the required components and prevents rework.

3. Sequence of steps: The solution to the problem statement is broken down into smaller sequential steps. The steps could be a combination of reading inputs, processing the data, and generating outputs. Defining the sequence of steps provides a structured approach to programming.

4. Choosing functions and procedures: The sequential steps can be implemented using functions and procedures. Functions and procedures provide reusability and make the code modular. They also make the code easier to understand, debug, and modify.

5. Pseudocoding: The functions and procedures are represented using pseudocoding. Pseudocoding uses a combination of English and programming constructs to represent the logic of the steps. Pseudocoding helps in validating the logical flow of the program before writing the actual code.

6. Programming: The pseudocode is finally converted into the programming language statements to implement the logic. The code is compiled and tested to verify that it satisfies the problem statement.

7. Testing and debugging: The program is tested with different sets of inputs and expected outputs. If the actual outputs do not match the expected outputs, the code is debugged and fixed. This cycle of testing and debugging continues until the program functions as desired.

8. Documenting: The final program is well documented for future reference. The documentation includes comments on the purpose of the program, inputs and outputs, functions and procedures, and usage instructions. Good documentation makes it easy to reuse and maintain the code.

### Sensor Characterization

- Sensors are devices that convert physical quantities into electrical signals.
- The relationship between the physical quantity and the electrical signal is known as the sensor characteristic.
- Important characteristics of a sensor include:
- Sensitivity - The amount the sensor's output changes for a given change in the measured quantity. High sensitivity is desirable for precise measurement.
- Accuracy - How close the sensor's output is to the true value of the measured quantity. Accuracy depends on calibration, linearity and hysteresis.
- Linearity - The sensor's output changes in direct proportion to the measured quantity. Non-linearity can make calibration and interpretation of measurements difficult.
- Hysteresis - The sensor's output depends on past measurements and not just the present measured quantity. This can affect accuracy and repeatability.
- Repeatability - The variation in the sensor's output for repeated measurements of the same physical quantity. Low variation is desirable for precision.
- Response time - The time taken for the sensor's output to stabilize in response to a change in the measured quantity. Fast response is important for dynamic measurements.
- Durability - The sensor's ability to maintain its characteristics over time and in varying environmental conditions. Robustness and reliability are important for long-term use.

The above points cover the key characteristics to consider when selecting and evaluating a sensor for an application. Understanding the sensor characteristics helps in determining if it is suited for the particular measurement requirements.

### Circuit Theory

1. Circuit: A complete path that allows electric charge to flow is known as an electric circuit. It consists of a voltage source, wires that provide a path and components that control the flow of charge.
2. Voltage Source: A device that provides electrical pressure to drive charge carriers through a circuit. Eg. batteries, power supplies, etc. It is measured in volts (V).
3. Current: The rate of flow of charge carriers through a circuit. It is measured in amperes (A).
4. Resistance: It opposes the flow of current and converts electrical energy into heat. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
5. Ohm's Law: States that current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage applied across it, as long as the temperature and other physical conditions remain constant. It is represented by the equation V=IR, where V is voltage, I is current and R is resistance.
6. Series and Parallel Circuits:
    - Series Circuit: Components are connected end-to-end. Same current flows through all components. Total resistance is equal to the sum of individual resistances.
    - Parallel Circuit: Components are connected across each other. The voltage across each component is the same. Total current is equal to the sum of currents through each branch and total resistance is less than any individual resistance.

### Push and Pull

- Push: The microcontroller initiates the data transfer. It sends out a signal to get the data from the external device. For example, the microcontroller sends out a signal to get the data from a sensor.
- Pull: The external device initiates the data transfer. It sends a signal to the microcontroller to get its data read. For example, an interrupt from an external device signals the microcontroller to read its data.

Advantages of Push:
- Microcontroller is in control of the timing of data transfer.
- Wastes less time waiting for external devices.

Disadvantages of Push:
- External devices cannot inform the microcontroller of important events. The microcontroller has to frequently check the device.

Advantages of Pull:
- External devices can inform the microcontroller of important events.
- Microcontroller does not have to frequently check the external device.

Disadvantages of Pull:
- Timing of data transfer depends on the external device.
- May introduce unexpected delays.

In practice, a combination of push and pull is used based on the application requirements.

### Arduino for the notes of the Unit 4 - ADVANCED I/O INTERFACING

1. I2C Communication Protocol
- I2C is a serial communication protocol for connecting low-speed peripherals to a microcontroller.
- It uses only 2 wires: SDA (Serial Data) and SCL (Serial Clock).
- Multiple slaves can be connected to the same 2 wires by assigning each one a unique address.
- Arduino has a built-in library for I2C communication called Wire.

2. SPI Communication Protocol
- SPI is a serial communication interface standard for connecting peripheral devices.
- It uses 4 wires: MOSI (Master Out Slave In), MISO (Master In Slave Out), SCK (Serial Clock), and SS (Slave Select).
- Data is transferred in full duplex mode.
- Arduino has a built-in library for SPI communication called SPI.

3. Interfacing LCD with Arduino
- LCDs are commonly used to display data from sensors or outputs from microcontrollers.
- Connect the LCD to the Arduino using the HD44780 driver and appropriate pins.
- Include the LiquidCrystal library and initialize the LCD with the number of columns and rows.
- Use functions like setCursor(), print(), and clear() to control the LCD.

### Wired/Wireless Networking

#### Wired Networking
- Physical connection between devices using cables
- Fast speed and reliable
- Limited by cable length
- Examples: Ethernet, Token Ring

#### Wireless Networking
- No physical cables, uses radio waves
- Convenient but can be unreliable
- Speed and range can vary
- Examples: WiFi, Bluetooth, Zigbee

To set up a wired network:
- Connect devices to a router using Ethernet cables
- Configure router and device settings to be on the same network with unique IP addresses
- Test network connectivity

To set up a wireless network:
- Connect router to internet source and power
- Configure router wireless settings (SSID, password, security)
- Connect devices to wireless network using SSID and password
- Test wireless connectivity and speed

Factors affecting wireless performance:
- Distance from access point
- Interference (physical obstructions, other wireless networks)
- Wireless signal strength
- Device compatibility

### TCP/IP and UDP

* TCP/IP stands for Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol. It is a set of protocols that define how data should be processed and transmitted over the Internet.
* The two main protocols that make up TCP/IP are:
    * Transmission Control Protocol (TCP): Provides reliable, ordered, and error-checked delivery of transmitted data.
    * Internet Protocol (IP): Provides routing addressing to move data across networks.
* UDP stands for User Datagram Protocol. It is a simpler transport layer protocol, but it does not guarantee delivery or order of data packets. It provides fast transmission without the overhead of TCP.
* Applications that require high speed and low latency like real-time video/audio streaming use UDP. TCP is better suited for applications that require high reliability like email and web browsing.
* TCP provides flow control, congestion control, and reliable transmission, making it a complex but robust protocol. UDP provides fast transmission without these features, making it simpler but less reliable.

### Building peer to peer communication system using Bluetooth &WiFi for the notes of the Unit 5 - IoT, FUTURE AND PERSPECTIVES

1. Bluetooth: Bluetooth is a wireless technology standard for exchanging data over short distances (using UHF radio waves in the frequency range 2.4 to 2.485 gigahertz) from fixed and mobile devices, and building personal area networks (PANs).

2. WiFi: WiFi is a family of wireless networking technologies commonly used for local area networking of devices and internet access, based on the IEEE 802.11 standards. WiFi is used in mobile devices such as laptops, tablets, and smartphones.

3. Peer to peer communication: Peer to peer or P2P communication is a method of device interconnection without the need for a centralized administration authority or server. Devices in a P2P network act as both clients and servers to share files, computing resources, and other services. P2P systems are often used to connect devices via both Bluetooth and WiFi to share and distribute data or services.

4. Building a communication system: To build a peer to peer communication system, the following steps can be followed:

-Enable Bluetooth and WiFi on the devices to be connected
-Pair the Bluetooth devices to create a Bluetooth connection
-Connect to the same WiFi network to establish a WiFi connection
- Choose either Bluetooth or WiFi to establish a peer to peer connection
-Share data or access services over the P2P connection

The peer to peer communication system can allow devices to directly connect and share information which enables efficient device interconnection for IoT systems and other applications. The choice of Bluetooth and/or WiFi can be made based on factors like range, bandwidth, power consumption, etc.
