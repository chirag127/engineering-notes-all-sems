

# CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

Cyber forensic analytics is a branch of digital forensics that deals with the investigation of digital devices, networks, and systems to collect, analyze and preserve evidence that can be presented in a court of law. Here are some key points to understand about cyber forensic analytics:

## What is Cyber Forensic Analytics?

- Cyber forensic analytics involves the application of techniques and tools to investigate cyber-related incidents such as cyber-attacks, data breaches, intellectual property theft, and other cybercrimes.
- It is an interdisciplinary field that combines computer science, law, and criminology to identify, analyze, and preserve digital evidence.

## Why is Cyber Forensic Analytics Important?

- Cyber forensic analytics is important because it helps to identify and prosecute cyber criminals, protect intellectual property, and prevent future cyber incidents.
- It also helps to identify vulnerabilities in digital systems and networks, and to develop and implement measures to prevent cyber-attacks.

## Techniques Used in Cyber Forensic Analytics:

- **Data Acquisition:** The process of collecting data from digital devices and networks for analysis. This includes the use of forensic imaging tools and techniques to create a bit-by-bit copy of the digital evidence.

- **Data Analysis:** This involves the examination of digital evidence to identify patterns, anomalies, and other indicators of cybercrime. This includes the use of forensic software tools to analyze network traffic, email messages, system logs, and other digital artifacts.

- **Chain of Custody:** The process of documenting the handling of digital evidence to maintain its integrity and admissibility in court. This includes the use of digital signatures and other security measures to prevent tampering.

- **Expert Testimony:** The presentation of expert analysis and opinions in court to help judges and juries understand complex technical issues related to cybercrime.

## Challenges in Cyber Forensic Analytics:

- The rapid evolution of technology and the increasing complexity of digital systems and networks make it difficult to keep up with the latest forensic tools and techniques.

- Cyber criminals are becoming more sophisticated in their methods, making it harder to detect and prevent cyber-attacks.

- The legal and regulatory framework surrounding cybercrime is still evolving, making it difficult to develop consistent and effective investigative procedures.

## Career Opportunities in Cyber Forensic Analytics:

- Cyber forensic analysts are in high demand in both the public and private sectors, including law enforcement agencies, government agencies, and corporations.

- Some common job titles in cyber forensic analytics include digital forensic analyst, cybercrime investigator, and computer forensic examiner.

- A career in cyber forensic analytics requires a strong background in computer science, cybersecurity, and digital forensics, as well as knowledge of legal and regulatory frameworks related to cybercrime.

### Cyber Crime

Cybercrime refers to any criminal activity that involves a computer or network device, or the internet. Cybercriminals use technology to commit fraudulent activities, steal personal information, or damage computer systems.

Here are some types of cybercrime:

1. **Phishing**: Phishing is a type of cybercrime where cybercriminals send fake emails, messages, or websites that appear to be from a legitimate source. The goal is to trick the recipient into giving away sensitive information such as passwords or credit card numbers.

2. **Malware**: Malware is a type of software that is designed to harm or disrupt computer systems. Cybercriminals use malware to steal data, damage systems, or gain access to sensitive information.

3. **Ransomware**: Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts a victim's files and demands payment in exchange for the decryption key. Cybercriminals use ransomware to extort money from victims.

4. **Identity theft**: Identity theft is a type of cybercrime where a cybercriminal steals personal information such as names, addresses, and social security numbers. They then use this information to open credit card accounts or commit other fraudulent activities.

5. **Cyberbullying**: Cyberbullying is a type of cybercrime where a person uses technology to harass, intimidate, or threaten another person. Cyberbullying can have serious consequences for the victim, including emotional distress and even suicide.

6. **Hacking**: Hacking is a type of cybercrime where a cybercriminal gains unauthorized access to a computer system or network. Cybercriminals use hacking to steal data, damage systems, or gain access to sensitive information.

7. **Denial of Service (DoS) attacks**: DoS attacks are a type of cybercrime where a cybercriminal floods a computer system or network with traffic, causing it to crash or become unavailable. DoS attacks can be used to disrupt business operations or extort money from victims.

In conclusion, cybercrime is a serious problem that affects individuals, businesses, and governments around the world. It is essential for cybersecurity professionals to understand the different types of cybercrime and how to prevent and investigate them. Cyber forensic analytics is a vital field in combating cybercrime and protecting against future attacks.

### Jurisdictional Concerns

Cybercrime has become a global problem, and as such, it requires global solutions. The internet has no borders, and as a result, cybercriminals can launch attacks from anywhere in the world. This presents a significant challenge for law enforcement agencies as they must navigate jurisdictional concerns when investigating and prosecuting cybercrime cases. In this unit, we will explore the jurisdictional concerns associated with cybercrime investigations.

Here are some key points to consider:

- Jurisdiction refers to the authority of a court to hear and decide a case. In the context of cybercrime, jurisdiction can be a complex issue, especially when the crime is committed across international borders.
- Cybercrime investigations require cooperation between law enforcement agencies in different countries. This is because cybercriminals can launch attacks from one country, while the victims of the attack may be located in another country.
- Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties (MLATs) are agreements between countries that govern how law enforcement agencies can cooperate with each other in criminal investigations. These treaties provide a framework for requesting assistance from foreign law enforcement agencies and for providing assistance to foreign agencies.
- MLATs can be time-consuming and bureaucratic, which can delay investigations and lead to the loss of digital evidence. This is because different countries have different legal systems and procedures, which can make it difficult to share information and evidence.
- Some countries may not have laws that specifically address cybercrime, making it difficult to prosecute cybercriminals. In such cases, law enforcement agencies may need to rely on other laws, such as fraud or theft laws, to prosecute cybercriminals.
- Jurisdictional concerns can also arise when investigating cybercrimes that involve cloud computing. This is because cloud computing involves the storage of data on servers located in different countries. As a result, the data may be subject to the laws of multiple countries, which can complicate investigations and prosecutions.
- The rise of cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin has also created new jurisdictional challenges for law enforcement agencies. This is because cryptocurrencies are decentralized and can be used to facilitate criminal activities such as money laundering and ransomware attacks. The use of cryptocurrencies can make it difficult to trace the flow of money and identify the individuals behind cybercrimes.

In conclusion, jurisdictional concerns are a significant challenge for law enforcement agencies when investigating and prosecuting cybercrime cases. Cybercrime investigations require cooperation between law enforcement agencies in different countries, which can be complicated by different legal systems and procedures. MLATs provide a framework for international cooperation, but they can be time-consuming and bureaucratic. The rise of cloud computing and cryptocurrencies has created new jurisdictional challenges that must be addressed to effectively combat cybercrime.



### Jurisprudential Inconsistency

In the world of cybercrime, jurisprudential inconsistency is a significant issue that creates problems in legal proceedings. Here are some key points to understand this concept:

- Jurisprudential inconsistency refers to the lack of consistency in the application of laws across different jurisdictions. In the context of cybercrime, this refers to the differences in legal definitions and punishments for cyber crimes in different countries or regions.

- This inconsistency can create problems in legal proceedings when a cybercrime involves multiple jurisdictions. For example, if a crime is committed in one country but the victim and the perpetrator are in different countries, it can be challenging to determine which jurisdiction's laws apply.

- Different countries may have different definitions of what constitutes a cybercrime. For example, some countries may consider certain actions to be cyberbullying, while others may not. This can lead to confusion and inconsistencies when it comes to prosecuting cybercrimes.

- Punishments for cybercrimes also vary widely across different jurisdictions. Some countries may impose harsh penalties for cybercrimes, while others may have more lenient punishments. This can make it difficult to determine an appropriate punishment when a cybercrime involves multiple jurisdictions.

- Jurisprudential inconsistency is not just a problem in international cases. It can also create problems within a single country when different states or regions have different laws and punishments for cybercrimes.

- The lack of consistency in cybercrime laws can create challenges for law enforcement agencies, who may struggle to coordinate investigations and prosecutions across different jurisdictions.

- To address the issue of jurisprudential inconsistency, there have been efforts to create international agreements and conventions to establish uniform definitions and punishments for cybercrimes. However, progress in this area has been slow, and there is still a long way to go to achieve global consistency in cybercrime laws.

### Cyber Extortion

Cyber extortion is a type of cybercrime that involves the use of digital threats or attacks to demand money or other valuables from individuals, businesses, or organizations. This type of crime has become increasingly prevalent in recent years, and it poses a significant threat to cybersecurity.

Here are some important points to remember about cyber extortion:

- Cyber extortion can take many forms, including ransomware attacks, DDoS (distributed denial of service) attacks, and data theft.

- DDoS attacks are another common form of cyber extortion. In a DDoS attack, the attacker floods a victim's website or network with traffic, causing it to become unavailable. The attacker then demands payment in exchange for stopping the attack.
- Data theft is a less common form of cyber extortion, but it can be just as damaging. In a data theft attack, the attacker gains access to a victim's sensitive information and threatens to release it publicly unless a ransom is paid.
- Cyber extortion can have serious consequences for victims, including financial losses, reputational damage, and legal liabilities.
- Preventative measures such as strong passwords, regular backups, and security software can help to mitigate the risk of cyber extortion.
- In the event of a cyber extortion attack, victims should avoid paying the ransom if possible and instead seek the assistance of law enforcement and cybersecurity professionals.
- Cyber extortion is a rapidly evolving threat, and it is important for individuals and organizations to stay informed about the latest trends and best practices for cybersecurity.

### Cyber Weapons

In the world of cyber crime, attackers use a variety of tools and techniques to carry out their nefarious activities. These tools are often referred to as "cyber weapons" and can range from simple malware to sophisticated hacking tools. In this unit, we will explore some of the most common cyber weapons used by attackers.

Here are some of the most common cyber weapons:

1. Malware - Malware is a type of software that is designed to harm or exploit a computer system. Malware can take many forms, including viruses, worms, Trojans, and ransomware. Once installed on a victim's computer, malware can steal sensitive information, lock down files, or even take control of the entire system.

2. Phishing - Phishing is a technique used by attackers to trick victims into revealing sensitive information, such as passwords or credit card numbers. Phishing attacks typically involve the use of fake emails or websites that appear to be legitimate. Once the victim enters their information, the attacker can use it for their own purposes.

3. DDoS attacks - A DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) attack is a type of cyber attack that floods a website or server with traffic, causing it to crash. DDoS attacks are often carried out using a network of infected computers, known as a botnet. Attackers can use DDoS attacks to extort money from businesses or to silence dissenting voices online.

4. Exploits - Exploits are vulnerabilities in software or hardware that can be exploited by attackers to gain access to a system. Attackers can use exploits to install malware, steal data, or take control of a system. Exploits can be found in a wide range of software, from web browsers to operating systems.

5. Password cracking - Password cracking is the process of guessing or cracking a password in order to gain access to a system. Attackers can use a variety of techniques to crack passwords, including brute force attacks and dictionary attacks. Once an attacker has gained access to a system, they can use it for their own purposes or sell access to other hackers on the dark web.

In conclusion, understanding the various cyber weapons used by attackers is an important part of cyber forensic analytics. By understanding these tools and techniques, forensic analysts can better identify and track cyber criminals, helping to prevent future attacks.



### ATM Frauds

ATM frauds are becoming increasingly common in today's world. Criminals are using various techniques and tools to steal money from unsuspecting victims at ATMs. In this section, we will discuss some of the most common types of ATM frauds.

1. Card Skimming: This is one of the most common types of ATM frauds. Criminals use a small electronic device called a skimmer to steal the victim's card data. The skimmer is attached to the card reader of the ATM and reads the card data when the victim inserts their card. The criminals then use the stolen data to create a clone of the victim's card and withdraw money from their account.

2. PIN Theft: Criminals use different techniques to steal the victim's PIN. One of the most common methods is shoulder surfing, where the criminal stands behind the victim and observes them entering their PIN. Another method is the use of a fake keypad overlay, which looks like the original keypad but records the victim's keystrokes.

3. Data acquisition: It is the process of collecting data from digital devices and networks using various techniques such as imaging, live analysis, and remote collection.

3. File carving: It is the process of extracting files from unallocated space, damaged or partially overwritten files, and deleted files.

4. Data recovery: It is the process of recovering data from damaged or inaccessible storage media.

### Port Scans

Port scanning is the process of sending packets to a computer or server to discover which ports are open and listening. It can be used for both legitimate purposes, such as network administration or security testing, and malicious purposes, such as reconnaissance or attack preparation. As a digital forensic analyst, it is important to understand the various types of port scans and their implications.

#### Types of Port Scans

1. TCP Connect Scan: This involves attempting to complete a full TCP connection to the target system. If successful, it indicates that the port is open and listening.

2. SYN Scan: This involves sending a SYN packet to the target system's ports. If the port is open, it will respond with a SYN/ACK packet. If the port is closed, it will respond with a RST packet.

3. FIN Scan: This involves sending a FIN packet to the target system's ports. If the port is open, it will not respond. If the port is closed, it will respond with a RST packet.

4. XMAS Scan: This involves sending a packet with the FIN, URG, and PUSH flags set to the target system's ports. If the port is open, it will not respond. If the port is closed, it will respond with a RST packet.

#### Implications of Port Scans

1. Information Gathering: Port scans can be used to gather information about a target system's services and vulnerabilities. This information can be used to plan attacks or to identify potential security risks.

2. Intrusion Detection: Port scans can be used as a tool for intrusion detection. By monitoring network traffic for port scanning activity, security teams can detect and respond to potential threats.

3. Legitimate Uses: Port scanning can also be used for legitimate purposes, such as network administration or security testing. However, it is important to obtain proper authorization and to follow established protocols when conducting these activities.

#### Detecting Port Scans

1. Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS): NIDS can be used to detect port scanning activity by monitoring network traffic for suspicious patterns of activity.

2. Log Analysis: Analyzing system logs can also reveal port scanning activity. Look for patterns of unusual activity, such as a high volume of connection attempts to a single port.

3. Active Response: In some cases, it may be appropriate to respond to port scanning activity by blocking the IP address of the source or by redirecting the traffic to a honeypot.

#### Conclusion

Port scanning is a common technique used by both legitimate and malicious actors. As a digital forensic analyst, it is important to understand the various types of port scans and their implications. By monitoring network traffic and analyzing system logs, it is possible to detect and respond to port scanning activity, and to mitigate potential security risks.

- They can also be used to give attackers remote access to compromised systems, allowing them to carry out further attacks or use the system as a part of a botnet.

- Some common types of Trojans include RATs (Remote Access Trojans), keyloggers, and banking Trojans.

- RATs allow attackers to take control of the compromised system remotely and perform various actions, such as stealing files, capturing keystrokes, and recording audio and video.

- Keyloggers are designed to capture keystrokes made by users, allowing attackers to steal passwords and other sensitive information.

- Banking Trojans are specifically designed to steal banking credentials and other financial information.

#### Backdoors

- Backdoors are a type of malicious software that allows attackers to bypass normal authentication procedures and gain unauthorized access to systems or networks.

- Backdoors are often installed by attackers after they have gained initial access to a system through other means, such as a Trojan or a vulnerability in software.

- Once a backdoor is installed, attackers can use it to gain remote access to the system, steal information, or launch further attacks.

- Backdoors can be extremely difficult to detect, as they are often designed to blend in with legitimate system files and processes.

- Some common types of backdoors include rootkits, web shells, and backdoor implants.

- Rootkits are designed to hide the presence of the backdoor on the system by modifying system files and processes.

- Web shells are backdoors that are installed on web servers, allowing attackers to take control of the server and the websites hosted on it.

- Backdoor implants are malicious code that is implanted directly into the firmware of a device, allowing attackers to gain persistent access to the device even after it has been rebooted or restored to factory settings.

#### Detecting Trojans and Backdoors

- Detecting Trojans and Backdoors can be challenging, as they are often designed to evade detection by security software.

- Some common techniques for detecting Trojans and Backdoors include using antivirus software, monitoring network traffic for suspicious activity, and analyzing system logs for signs of compromise.

### DoS

Denial of Service (DoS) is a type of cyber attack that aims to bring down a website or network by overwhelming it with traffic or other forms of malicious activity. This can prevent legitimate users from accessing the system or network, causing significant damage to businesses and organizations. In this section, we will explore the different aspects of DoS attacks and how to detect and prevent them.

#### Types of DoS Attacks

1. SYN Flood Attack - this type of attack exploits the TCP/IP protocol's three-way handshake process by sending a large number of SYN requests to the target system, but never completing the handshake process. This causes the system to consume resources while waiting for the handshake to be completed, resulting in a denial of service.

2. UDP Flood Attack - this attack floods the target system with a large number of UDP packets, causing it to consume resources as it tries to process all the incoming packets. This can result in the system becoming unresponsive, leading to a denial of service.

3. HTTP Flood Attack - this attack involves overwhelming a web server with a large number of HTTP requests, causing it to slow down or crash. This is often done by using bots or compromised computers to send requests to the server.

#### Detecting DoS Attacks

1. Network Traffic Analysis - this involves monitoring network traffic for unusual patterns or high traffic volumes that may indicate a DoS attack is underway.

2. System Performance Monitoring - monitoring the performance of servers and systems can help identify sudden spikes in resource usage or other anomalies that may indicate a DoS attack.

3. Log Analysis - examining system and application logs may reveal patterns of activity that indicate a DoS attack, such as repeated login attempts or unusual traffic patterns.

#### Preventing DoS Attacks

1. Firewalls - firewalls can be configured to block traffic from known malicious IP addresses or to limit the number of connections from a single IP address.

2. Intrusion Detection/Prevention Systems - IDS/IPS systems can detect and block DoS attacks in real-time, preventing them from reaching their target.

3. Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) - CDNs can distribute traffic across multiple servers, preventing any one server from becoming overwhelmed by traffic.

In conclusion, DoS attacks are a significant threat to businesses and organizations, and it is essential to be aware of the different types of attacks and how to detect and prevent them. By implementing the appropriate security measures, organizations can minimize the risk of a DoS attack and ensure the continued availability of their systems and networks.



### DDoS Attacks

DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) is a type of cyber attack that aims to make a website, server, or network unavailable to its intended users by overwhelming it with traffic from multiple sources. In this attack, the attacker uses a network of compromised devices, often called a botnet, to send a large volume of traffic to the target system. DDoS attacks are a significant threat to businesses, governments, and other organizations that rely on their online presence to operate.

Here are some key points to understand about DDoS attacks:

- DDoS attacks can be launched using a variety of methods, including UDP flood, ICMP flood, SYN flood, and HTTP flood.
- UDP flood sends a large number of UDP packets to the target system, overwhelming its ability to process them.
- ICMP flood sends a large number of ICMP packets to the target system, consuming its bandwidth and resources.
- SYN flood exploits the vulnerability in the TCP/IP protocol by sending a large number of SYN packets to the target system, making it unable to accept legitimate connection requests.
- HTTP flood sends a large number of HTTP requests to the target system, consuming its resources and making it unavailable to legitimate users.
- DDoS attacks can be launched using botnets, which are networks of compromised devices that are controlled by a single attacker.
- The botnet can be created by infecting devices with malware or by exploiting vulnerabilities in software or hardware.
- DDoS attacks can cause significant damage to businesses by disrupting their operations, causing downtime, and damaging their reputation.
- Organizations can defend against DDoS attacks by implementing various measures such as firewalls, intrusion prevention systems, load balancers, and content delivery networks (CDNs).

- Cyber forensic analysts can investigate DDoS attacks by examining network traffic logs, server logs, and other digital evidence to identify the source of the attack and the methods used.

In conclusion, DDoS attacks are a significant threat to businesses and other organizations that rely on their online presence.
