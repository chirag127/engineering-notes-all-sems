

# CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

Cyber forensic analytics is the process of analyzing digital evidence to investigate cybercrimes. It involves the use of various techniques and tools to collect, preserve, and analyze digital evidence to support investigations and legal proceedings.

Some key points to consider when discussing cyber forensic analytics include:

1. **Digital Evidence**: Digital evidence is any information stored or transmitted in digital form that may be relevant to an investigation. This can include data from computers, mobile devices, and other digital storage media.

2. **Collection and Preservation**: The collection and preservation of digital evidence is a critical step in the forensic process. It is important to ensure that the evidence is collected in a manner that maintains its integrity and authenticity.

3. **Analysis**: The analysis of digital evidence involves the use of various techniques and tools to extract and interpret the data. This can include the use of specialized software to recover deleted files, analyze network traffic, and identify patterns of behavior.

4. **Legal Considerations**: The use of digital evidence in legal proceedings must comply with relevant laws and regulations. This includes ensuring that the evidence is collected and analyzed in a manner that is admissible in court.

5. **Challenges**: There are many challenges associated with cyber forensic analytics, including the rapidly evolving nature of technology, the increasing sophistication of cybercriminals, and the need for specialized skills and knowledge.

Overall, cyber forensic analytics plays a critical role in the investigation of cybercrimes and the protection of digital assets. It is a complex and challenging field that requires a combination of technical expertise, legal knowledge, and investigative skills.

### Jurisdictional Concerns

Jurisdictional concerns are a major issue in the investigation and prosecution of cyber crimes. Due to the global nature of the internet, cyber crimes can be committed from anywhere in the world, making it difficult to determine which jurisdiction has the authority to investigate and prosecute the crime. Some key points to consider when discussing jurisdictional concerns in the context of cyber crime include:

1. **Location of the crime:** Determining the location where a cyber crime was committed can be challenging, as the perpetrator may be located in one country, while the victim and the server hosting the illegal content may be located in different countries.

2. **International cooperation:** Due to the global nature of cyber crime, international cooperation is essential in investigating and prosecuting these crimes. This can involve sharing information and evidence between law enforcement agencies in different countries.

3. **Extradition:** In some cases, a suspect may be located in a country that does not have an extradition treaty with the country where the crime was committed. This can make it difficult to bring the suspect to trial.

4. **Differences in laws:** Cyber crime laws vary between countries, and what may be considered a crime in one country may not be illegal in another. This can create challenges in prosecuting cyber criminals who operate across borders.

Overall, jurisdictional concerns present significant challenges in the investigation and prosecution of cyber crimes. Cooperation between law enforcement agencies and harmonization of cyber crime laws between countries can help to address these challenges.

### ATM Frauds

ATM frauds are a type of cybercrime that involves the unauthorized withdrawal of funds from a victim's bank account through an ATM. This can be done through various methods, including:

1. **Card Skimming:** This involves the use of a device attached to the card reader of an ATM to capture the data from the magnetic strip of a bank card. The captured data is then used to create a counterfeit card, which is used to withdraw funds from the victim's account.

2. **PIN Capturing:** This involves the use of a hidden camera or a fake keypad overlay to capture the victim's PIN as they enter it into the ATM. The captured PIN is then used in conjunction with a counterfeit card to withdraw funds from the victim's account.

3. **Card Trapping:** This involves the use of a device inserted into the card slot of an ATM to trap the victim's card. The victim is then tricked into re-entering their PIN, which is captured by the fraudster. The trapped card is later retrieved and used to withdraw funds from the victim's account.

4. **Phishing:** This involves the use of fake emails, text messages, or phone calls to trick the victim into revealing their bank account details and PIN. The obtained information is then used to withdraw funds from the victim's account.

ATM frauds can result in significant financial losses for the victim and can also lead to identity theft. It is important for individuals to be vigilant when using ATMs and to take steps to protect their personal and financial information. This includes regularly checking bank statements, covering the keypad when entering a PIN, and being cautious of unsolicited emails, text messages, or phone calls requesting personal or financial information.

## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

Cyber Forensics, also known as Computer Forensics, is the practice of collecting, analyzing, and reporting on digital data in a way that is legally admissible. It can be used in the detection and prevention of crime and in any dispute where evidence is stored digitally.

1. **Digital Evidence**: Digital evidence is any information stored or transmitted in digital form that a party to a court case may use at trial. This can include data from computers, mobile phones, digital cameras, and other electronic devices.

2. **Forensic Imaging**: Forensic imaging is the process of creating an exact copy of a digital device, including all data and metadata. This is done to preserve the integrity of the evidence and to prevent any changes to the original data.

3. **Chain of Custody**: The chain of custody is a record of who has handled the evidence, when it was handled, and what was done with it. This is important to ensure that the evidence has not been tampered with and that it can be used in court.

4. **Data Recovery**: Data recovery is the process of retrieving data from damaged, failed, corrupted, or inaccessible storage media. This can be important in cases where data has been deleted or the storage device has been damaged.

5. **Data Analysis**: Data analysis is the process of examining, cleaning, transforming, and modeling data to discover useful information, draw conclusions, and support decision-making. This can include the use of specialized software to search for keywords, recover deleted files, and analyze data patterns.

6. **Reporting**: Reporting is the process of presenting the findings of a forensic investigation in a clear and concise manner. This can include the preparation of written reports, the creation of visual aids, and the presentation of evidence in court.

7. **Legal and Ethical Considerations**: Cyber forensic investigations must be conducted in accordance with the law and with respect for the rights of all parties involved. This can include considerations such as privacy, data protection, and the admissibility of evidence in court.

In conclusion, Cyber Forensics is a complex and rapidly evolving field that plays an important role in the investigation of digital crimes and the resolution of disputes involving digital evidence. It requires a combination of technical expertise, legal knowledge, and attention to detail to ensure that digital evidence is collected, analyzed, and presented in a manner that is legally admissible and useful in a court of law.

### Hard Disk

- A hard disk is a data storage device used for storing and retrieving digital information.
- It uses magnetic storage to store and retrieve digital data using one or more rigid rapidly rotating disks (platters) coated with magnetic material.
- The platters are paired with magnetic heads, usually arranged on a moving actuator arm, which read and write data to the platter surfaces.
- Hard disks are non-volatile storage devices, meaning that they retain stored data even when powered off.
- Hard disks are commonly used as the main storage device in computers, as well as in external storage devices and consumer electronics such as digital video recorders and gaming consoles.

### Data Carving

Data carving is a technique used in cyber forensics to recover data from a storage device without the assistance of the file system that originally organized the data. This technique is commonly used when the file system is damaged, corrupted, or otherwise inaccessible.

Here are some key points to remember about data carving:

1. Data carving can recover data from a variety of storage devices, including hard drives, USB drives, and memory cards.
2. Data carving works by searching for file headers and footers, which are unique sequences of bytes that identify the beginning and end of a file.
3. Once the headers and footers are located, the data between them can be extracted and saved as a new file.
4. Data carving can recover a wide range of file types, including documents, images, and videos.
5. Data carving is not always successful, as the data may be fragmented or overwritten.
6. There are several tools available for data carving, including open-source and commercial options.

### Steganography

Steganography is the practice of concealing a message within another message or a physical object. In computing/electronic contexts, a message could be a file, image, or video. The goal of steganography is to hide the existence of the message from a third party.

Here are some key points to remember about steganography:

1. Steganography is different from cryptography. Cryptography is the practice of encoding a message so that only the intended recipient can read it. Steganography, on the other hand, is the practice of hiding the message itself.

2. Steganography can be used for malicious purposes. For example, attackers can use steganography to hide malware within an image or other file.

3. There are many techniques for performing steganography. Some common techniques include least significant bit insertion, mask and filter techniques, and transformations.

4. Steganalysis is the practice of detecting the use of steganography. Steganalysis techniques include visual and statistical analysis.

5. Steganography has been used throughout history. For example, during World War II, the French Resistance sent messages hidden within drawings.




### Network Components

1. **Network Interface Card (NIC):** A hardware component that connects a computer to a network.
2. **Hub:** A device that connects multiple devices on a network and forwards data packets to all connected devices.
3. **Switch:** A device that connects multiple devices on a network and forwards data packets to the intended recipient only.
4. **Router:** A device that connects multiple networks and forwards data packets between them.
5. **Modem:** A device that modulates and demodulates signals to enable data transmission over a communication line.
6. **Firewall:** A network security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predetermined security rules.
7. **Wireless Access Point (WAP):** A device that allows wireless devices to connect to a wired network.
8. **Cables:** Physical medium used to connect devices on a network, such as Ethernet cables or fiber optic cables.

### Port Scans

Port scanning is the process of sending packets to specific ports on a host and analyzing the responses to identify open ports. It is a technique used to discover services that are available on a host. Port scanning is commonly used by attackers to identify potential targets and by administrators to verify the security of their networks.

Some common types of port scans include:

1. **TCP Connect Scan:** This type of scan attempts to establish a full TCP connection with the target host. If the connection is successful, the port is considered open.

2. **SYN Scan:** This type of scan sends a SYN packet to the target host and waits for a response. If the response is a SYN-ACK packet, the port is considered open.

3. **FIN Scan:** This type of scan sends a FIN packet to the target host and waits for a response. If the response is a RST packet, the port is considered closed.

4. **Xmas Scan:** This type of scan sends a packet with the FIN, URG, and PSH flags set to the target host and waits for a response. If the response is a RST packet, the port is considered closed.

5. **Null Scan:** This type of scan sends a packet with no flags set to the target host and waits for a response. If the response is a RST packet, the port is considered closed.

Port scanning can be detected and prevented using various techniques such as firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and rate limiting. It is important for administrators to regularly scan their own networks to identify potential vulnerabilities and to monitor for unauthorized scans.

- Viruses can cause damage to the computer system by corrupting or deleting files, or by using up system resources, causing the computer to slow down or crash.

- A worm is a type of malware that spreads by exploiting vulnerabilities in computer networks.

- Worms can spread rapidly through a network, and can infect a large number of computers in a short amount of time.

In conclusion, both viruses and worms are types of malware that can harm computer systems, but they differ in their behavior and the way they spread. It is important to have up-to-date antivirus software and to practice safe computing habits to protect against these threats.

### Digital Signature

A digital signature is a mathematical technique used to validate the authenticity and integrity of a message, software or digital document. It is the digital equivalent of a handwritten signature or stamped seal, but it offers far more inherent security.

Here are some key points to note about digital signatures:

1. Digital signatures are based on public key cryptography, also known as asymmetric cryptography. This involves the use of a public and private key pair: the private key is used to sign the document, and the public key is used to verify the signature.

2. The private key is kept secret by the owner, while the public key is widely distributed. Anyone can use the public key to verify the signature, but only the owner of the private key can create a valid signature.

3. Digital signatures provide non-repudiation, meaning that the signer cannot deny having signed the document. This is because the signature is unique to both the document and the signer, and it binds them together.

4. Digital signatures are used in electronic commerce, software distribution, and financial transactions, among other applications. They are an essential component of secure electronic communication and transactions.

5. Digital signatures are legally binding in many countries, including the United States, the European Union, and India. They are recognized as equivalent to handwritten signatures under the law.

6. Digital signatures are created using digital signature algorithms, such as RSA, DSA, and ECDSA. These algorithms are based on complex mathematical concepts and provide a high level of security.

7. Digital signatures are not the same as digital certificates. A digital certificate is an electronic document that uses a digital signature to bind a public key with an identity. Digital certificates are issued by certificate authorities, which verify the identity of the certificate holder.
