### Introduction - History and Evolution

Business Intelligence (BI) has a long and varied history. The term "business intelligence" was first used by Richard Miller Devens in his 1865 work, Cyclopaedia of Commercial and Business Anecdotes. However, technology did not advance to the point where it could be considered an agent of BI until well into the 20th century.

In the 1950s, during the dawn of the Digital Revolution, BI became an independent scientific process adopted by entrepreneurs to inform their business strategies. The number of BI vendors grew in the 1980s, as business people discovered the value of BI. An assortment of tools were developed during this time, with the goal of accessing and organizing data in simpler ways.

The first generation of BI software was largely managed by the IT department as the central guardian of all enterprise data. The Extract, Transform, Load (ETL) paradigm combined data from multiple systems to a single database, data store, or warehouse for legacy storage or analytics.

BI has continued to evolve, with advancements such as cloud BI and mobile BI, and the empowerment of end-users. Today, BI is understood as a set of analyses that derive value and insight from data.

### Data, Information, and Knowledge

Data, information, and knowledge are three key concepts in the field of business intelligence and analytics. These concepts are closely related, but they are not the same.

- **Data** refers to raw, unprocessed facts and figures. Data can be collected from various sources, such as transactions, sensors, and surveys. Data by itself has no meaning and needs to be processed to become useful.

- **Information** is data that has been processed, organized, and presented in a meaningful way. Information is used to make decisions and take actions. For example, sales data can be processed to generate a report on the total sales for a particular period.

- **Knowledge** is the understanding and insight gained from the analysis of information. Knowledge is used to make informed decisions and take actions. For example, analyzing sales data can provide knowledge on the best-selling products and the most profitable customers.

In summary, data is the raw material, information is the processed product, and knowledge is the understanding gained from the analysis of information. These concepts are essential for understanding the role of business intelligence and analytics in an organization.

### Functionalities & Classifications of Data Mining

Data mining is the process of discovering patterns and knowledge from large amounts of data. The data sources can include databases, data warehouses, the internet, and other sources.

4. **Regression:** This is the task of mapping a data item to a real-valued prediction variable.

5. **Summarization:** This provides a more compact representation of the data set, including visualization and report generation.

Data mining can be classified into two categories: descriptive data mining and predictive data mining.

- **Descriptive data mining** is concerned with finding patterns describing the data and is commonly used for exploring the properties of the data at hand.

- **Predictive data mining** is concerned with finding a model for the data that can be used to predict future outcomes.

### Data Modelling

Data modelling is the process of creating a conceptual representation of data objects and their relationships. It is an essential step in the design of databases and data warehouses, and is used to ensure that data is organized in a way that is efficient, accurate, and easy to access.

In the context of Business Intelligence and Analytics, data modelling is used to design data warehouses that support the analysis and reporting needs of an organization. This involves creating a logical model of the data, which defines the relationships between different data objects, and a physical model, which specifies how the data will be stored and accessed.

There are several approaches to data modelling, including:

1. **Entity-Relationship (ER) Modelling:** This approach involves identifying the entities (or objects) in the data, and defining the relationships between them. ER diagrams are used to represent the data model visually.

2. **Dimensional Modelling:** This approach is commonly used in data warehousing, and involves organizing data into facts and dimensions. Facts represent the measures or metrics that are being analyzed, while dimensions provide context and allow for slicing and dicing of the data.

3. **Object-Oriented Modelling:** This approach involves defining classes and objects, and the relationships between them. It is commonly used in object-oriented programming, but can also be applied to data modelling.

Data modelling is an iterative process, and involves close collaboration between data analysts, database designers, and business stakeholders. It is important to ensure that the data model accurately reflects the needs of the organization, and supports the analysis and reporting requirements of the business.



### Difference between OLAP and OLTP

OLAP (Online Analytical Processing) and OLTP (Online Transaction Processing) are two different types of database systems. They are designed to serve different purposes and have different characteristics.

1. **Purpose:** OLAP is designed for data analysis and decision making. It is used to extract insights from large amounts of data. OLTP, on the other hand, is designed for transaction processing. It is used to manage day-to-day business operations.

2. **Data Structure:** OLAP databases are structured in a multidimensional manner, allowing for complex queries and analysis. OLTP databases are structured in a relational manner, allowing for efficient data entry and retrieval.

3. **Data Volume:** OLAP databases typically handle large volumes of data, often in the range of terabytes or petabytes. OLTP databases typically handle smaller volumes of data, often in the range of gigabytes or terabytes.

4. **Query Complexity:** OLAP queries are often complex and can take a long time to execute. OLTP queries are typically simple and execute quickly.

5. **Data Update:** OLAP databases are updated less frequently, often on a daily, weekly, or monthly basis. OLTP databases are updated in real-time, as transactions occur.

6. **Data Aggregation:** OLAP databases often contain pre-aggregated data, allowing for faster query execution. OLTP databases do not contain pre-aggregated data.

7. **Data History:** OLAP databases often contain historical data, allowing for trend analysis. OLTP databases typically only contain current data.

These are some of the key differences between OLAP and OLTP. They are both important components of a business intelligence and analytics system, serving different purposes and providing different capabilities.



### Schema – Star and Snowflake Schemas

A schema is a logical representation of the structure of a database. In the context of data warehousing, two common types of schemas are the star schema and the snowflake schema.

#### Star Schema

- The star schema is a type of schema where a central fact table is connected to multiple dimension tables.
- The fact table contains the measures or facts of the data, while the dimension tables contain descriptive information about the facts.
- The dimension tables are denormalized, meaning that they contain redundant data to improve query performance.
- The star schema is simple and easy to understand, making it a popular choice for data warehousing.

#### Snowflake Schema

- The snowflake schema is a variation of the star schema where the dimension tables are normalized.
- This means that the dimension tables are split into multiple related tables to reduce data redundancy.
- The snowflake schema is more complex than the star schema, but it can result in more efficient storage and faster query performance in some cases.

### Data Reduction – Sampling

Data reduction is the process of reducing the amount of data that needs to be stored, processed, and analyzed while still maintaining the integrity and usefulness of the data. One of the techniques used for data reduction is sampling.

Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of the data to represent the entire population. This can be done in several ways, including:

1. **Simple random sampling:** In this method, each data point has an equal chance of being selected for the sample.

2. **Stratified sampling:** In this method, the population is divided into different strata or groups, and then a sample is taken from each stratum.

3. **Cluster sampling:** In this method, the population is divided into clusters, and then a sample of clusters is selected. All the data points within the selected clusters are included in the sample.

4. **Systematic sampling:** In this method, a starting point is selected, and then every nth data point is selected for the sample.

Sampling can be an effective way to reduce the amount of data that needs to be processed and analyzed while still maintaining the accuracy of the results. However, it is important to ensure that the sample is representative of the entire population to avoid bias in the results.

### PCA for the notes of the Unit 3 - BI – DATA PREPARTTION in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

- PCA stands for Principal Component Analysis.
- It is a statistical technique used to reduce the dimensionality of a dataset.
- It does this by transforming the data into a new coordinate system, where the axes are ordered by the amount of variance they explain.
- The first principal component explains the most variance, the second explains the second most, and so on.
- By selecting only the first few principal components, we can reduce the dimensionality of the data while retaining most of the variance.
- This can be useful for visualization, data compression, and noise reduction.
- PCA is commonly used in data pre-processing for machine learning and data mining applications.
- It is important to note that PCA is a linear technique and may not be suitable for datasets with complex, non-linear relationships between variables.
- To perform PCA, the data must first be standardized to have zero mean and unit variance.
- The principal components are then calculated using either eigendecomposition of the covariance matrix or singular value decomposition of the data matrix.
- The resulting principal components can be used to transform the original data into the new coordinate system.

### Root Cause Analysis

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is a systematic approach to identifying the underlying causes of problems or incidents. It is commonly used in the field of Business Intelligence and Analytics as a means of improving processes and preventing future issues. Here are some key points to consider when conducting a Root Cause Analysis:

1. **Define the problem:** Clearly define the problem or incident that needs to be investigated. This includes gathering data and information about the issue and its impact.

2. **Gather data:** Collect data and information related to the problem. This can include interviewing those involved, reviewing documentation, and analyzing data.

3. **Identify possible causes:** Use the data gathered to identify possible causes of the problem. This can be done using various techniques such as brainstorming, cause-and-effect diagrams, and the 5 Whys.

4. **Determine the root cause:** Analyze the possible causes to determine the underlying root cause of the problem. This may involve further investigation and data analysis.

5. **Develop and implement corrective actions:** Once the root cause has been identified, develop and implement corrective actions to address the issue and prevent it from recurring.

6. **Monitor and review:** Monitor the effectiveness of the corrective actions and review the process to ensure that the problem has been resolved and to identify any opportunities for further improvement.

Root Cause Analysis is an important tool in the implementation of Business Intelligence and can help organizations to improve their processes and prevent future issues. It is a key component of Unit 5 - Implementation of BI in the subject of Business Intelligence and Analytics KCS.
