

# Business Intelligence and Analytics

- Business intelligence (BI) and business analytics (BA) are related but distinct concepts that involve the use of data to support decision-making and performance improvement in organizations.
- BI refers to the process of collecting, integrating, transforming, and presenting data from various sources to provide insights and information for decision-makers .
- BA refers to the process of applying statistical, mathematical, and computational methods to data sets to discover patterns, trends, and relationships that can inform decision-making and action .
- BI and BA can be used together or separately, depending on the business objectives, data availability, and analytical skills of the users.
- BI and BA can be applied to various domains and functions, such as marketing, finance, operations, human resources, customer service, and more.
- BI and BA can provide various benefits to organizations, such as:
  - Improving efficiency and productivity by automating and streamlining data processes and workflows.
  - Enhancing quality and accuracy by reducing errors and inconsistencies in data collection and analysis.
  - Increasing profitability and competitiveness by identifying opportunities and threats, optimizing resources and costs, and creating value for customers and stakeholders.
  - Supporting innovation and growth by enabling experimentation, testing, and learning from data and feedback.
  - Fostering a data-driven culture by empowering and engaging employees, managers, and leaders with data and insights.



# KCS

- KCS stands for Knowledge-Centered Service, a methodology for creating and maintaining knowledge as a key asset of an organization.
- KCS is based on four basic principles: 
  - Integrate the creation and maintenance of knowledge into the problem-solving process.
  - Evolve content based on demand and usage.
  - Develop a knowledge base of collective experience to enable reuse and improvement.
  - Reward learning, collaboration, sharing, and improving.
- KCS has two main processes: the Solve Loop and the Evolve Loop.
  - The Solve Loop focuses on capturing, structuring, and reusing knowledge at the point of interaction with customers or users.
  - The Evolve Loop focuses on analyzing, improving, and governing the knowledge base and the KCS practices.
- KCS has six core practices that support the principles and processes: 
  - Capture: Capturing knowledge in the context of the customer's situation and in the customer's words.
  - Structure: Structuring knowledge using a standard template and metadata to make it easy to find and use.
  - Reuse: Reusing existing knowledge to solve problems and avoid recreating content.
  - Improve: Improving the quality and effectiveness of knowledge based on feedback and usage patterns.
  - Search: Searching the knowledge base before solving a problem or creating new content.
  - Link: Linking related knowledge articles to create a network of knowledge.



## Unit 1 - BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE – INTRODUCTION

- Business intelligence (BI) is a term that covers a variety of methods and technologies for collecting, storing, analyzing, and presenting data that supports business decision making  .
- BI can help businesses gain insights into their performance, customers, competitors, markets, and opportunities.
- BI can also enable businesses to automate and optimize their processes, enhance their efficiency and effectiveness, and create value for their stakeholders.
- BI can be divided into different types, such as descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analytics.
- Descriptive analytics summarizes what has happened in the past using historical data.
- Diagnostic analytics explains why something has happened using data mining and root cause analysis.
- Predictive analytics forecasts what is likely to happen in the future using statistical modeling and machine learning.
- Prescriptive analytics recommends what actions to take to achieve a desired outcome using optimization and simulation.
- BI can be delivered through various tools and platforms, such as reports, dashboards, charts, graphs, scorecards, and interactive visualizations  .
- BI tools can enable users to access and analyze different types of data, such as structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data, from various sources, such as internal databases, external data providers, and social media .
- BI tools can also provide features such as data cleansing, data integration, data transformation, data modeling, data governance, data security, data quality, and data visualization .
- BI tools can be classified into different categories, such as self-service BI, cloud-based BI, mobile BI, embedded BI, and augmented BI.
- Self-service BI allows users to create and customize their own reports and dashboards without relying on IT support.
- Cloud-based BI delivers BI solutions over the internet, reducing the need for hardware and software installation and maintenance.
- Mobile BI enables users to access and interact with BI data and applications on their mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets.
- Embedded BI integrates BI functionality into other applications, such as CRM, ERP, or e-commerce systems, to provide contextual and relevant insights.
- Augmented BI leverages artificial intelligence and machine learning to automate and enhance data analysis and presentation, such as by generating natural language summaries, recommendations, and explanations.



### Introduction - History and Evolution

- Business intelligence (BI) is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting data to support decision making in various domains of business.
- The term BI was coined in 1989 by Howard Dresner, but the concept of using information to gain a competitive advantage dates back to the 19th century.
- Some of the early examples of BI include:
  - Richard Millar Devens, a banker who used market trends and customer preferences to outperform his rivals in the 1860s.
  - Henry Ford, who used assembly line data to optimize production and reduce costs in the 1910s.
  - The DuPont Company, who developed the first performance dashboard to measure the efficiency and profitability of their operations in the 1920s.
- The evolution of BI can be divided into four stages:
  - Data collection and reporting: This stage began in the 1960s with the emergence of computerized databases and decision support systems (DSS) that enabled data storage and retrieval. The main focus was on generating reports and summaries of historical data for descriptive analysis.
  - Data warehousing and OLAP: This stage started in the 1980s with the development of data warehousing and online analytical processing (OLAP) technologies that allowed data integration and multidimensional analysis. The main focus was on creating a single source of truth and providing ad hoc queries and drill-down capabilities for diagnostic analysis.
  - Data mining and visualization: This stage emerged in the 1990s with the advancement of data mining and visualization tools that enabled data discovery and pattern recognition. The main focus was on finding hidden insights and trends in large and complex data sets for predictive analysis.
  - Data science and AI: This stage began in the 2000s with the rise of data science and artificial intelligence (AI) techniques that enabled data modeling and automation. The main focus was on creating data-driven solutions and recommendations for prescriptive analysis.
- The current trends and challenges in BI include:
  - Big data and cloud computing: The increasing volume, variety, and velocity of data require scalable and flexible platforms and architectures to store and process data in real time.
  - Self-service and agile BI: The growing demand for data democratization and user empowerment require user-friendly and interactive tools and interfaces to access and analyze data without relying on IT experts.
  - Data quality and governance: The expanding sources and types of data require robust and consistent methods and policies to ensure data accuracy, completeness, and security.
  - Data literacy and culture: The changing role and expectations of data users require enhanced skills and competencies to understand and communicate data effectively and ethically.

### Data Information and Knowledge for Business Intelligence and Analytics

- Data is the raw and unprocessed facts that are collected from various sources, such as transactions, sensors, surveys, etc. Data can be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured, depending on the format and organization of the data. Data by itself has no meaning or context, and needs to be processed and analyzed to extract value from it .
- Information is the result of processing, organizing, and interpreting data in a meaningful way. Information provides context and meaning to the data, and helps users understand the patterns, trends, and insights from the data. Information can be presented in various forms, such as tables, charts, graphs, reports, dashboards, etc. Information can be used to support decision making and problem solving  .
- Knowledge is the understanding and application of information to achieve a specific goal or outcome. Knowledge is derived from the experience, expertise, and intuition of the users, and can be tacit or explicit, depending on the degree of codification and transferability of the knowledge. Knowledge can be used to create new information, improve processes, innovate products or services, or enhance performance .
- Business Intelligence (BI) is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting data and information to support data-driven decision making in organizations. BI combines business analytics, data mining, data visualization, data tools and infrastructure, and best practices to help organizations gain a comprehensive view of their data and use it to drive actions and outcomes. BI can help organizations improve their performance, efficiency, profitability, customer satisfaction, and competitive advantage  .
- Business Analytics (BA) is the subset of BI that focuses on descriptive and diagnostic analytics, which answer questions of where the organization has been, where it is now, and why things are the way they are now. BA uses statistical and mathematical techniques to explore, summarize, and explain the data, and to identify the root causes of problems or issues. BA can help organizations monitor their performance, identify opportunities and threats, and optimize their processes and resources .
- Data Analytics (DA) is the process of converting raw data into meaningful information. DA involves data collection, data cleaning, data transformation, data analysis, and data visualization. DA can use various methods and tools, such as SQL, Python, R, Excel, Tableau, etc. to manipulate and explore the data, and to generate insights and recommendations. DA is a key tool in BI and BA, and can help organizations create value from their data .

### Role of mathematical models in business intelligence

- A mathematical model is an abstract representation of a real-world system or problem that uses mathematical language and symbols to describe its behavior and outcomes.
- A business intelligence system provides decision makers with information and knowledge extracted from data, through the application of mathematical models and algorithms .
- The role of mathematical models in business intelligence is to:
  - Understand the function and structure of the system or problem under study, such as customers, markets, products, processes, etc.
  - Analyze the data and identify patterns, trends, relationships, anomalies, etc. that can provide insights and support decision making.
  - Predict the future behavior and outcomes of the system or problem, such as sales, profits, risks, customer satisfaction, etc. based on historical data and assumptions.
  - Optimize the performance and efficiency of the system or problem, such as maximizing profits, minimizing costs, allocating resources, etc. by finding the best possible solutions or strategies.
  - Learn from the data and update the model as new information becomes available, such as feedback, results, changes, etc. to improve the accuracy and reliability of the model.
- Some examples of mathematical models used in business intelligence are:
  - Regression models, which estimate the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables, such as the effect of price on demand, or the impact of advertising on sales.
  - Classification models, which assign a label or category to an object or observation, such as whether a customer is likely to buy a product, or whether an email is spam or not.
  - Clustering models, which group similar objects or observations together, such as customer segments, market segments, product categories, etc.
  - Association models, which discover rules or patterns that describe the co-occurrence or correlation of items or events, such as the items that are frequently bought together, or the factors that influence customer loyalty.
  - Optimization models, which find the optimal values of decision variables that maximize or minimize an objective function, subject to some constraints, such as the optimal product mix, pricing strategy, inventory level, etc.
  - Simulation models, which mimic the behavior and dynamics of a complex system or process, such as the demand and supply of a product, the flow of customers in a store, the spread of a disease, etc.

### Data Mining - Introduction to Data Mining

- Data mining is the process of extracting useful information or knowledge from a large set of data, using software tools and techniques to look for patterns, trends, and relationships in the data .
- Data mining can be used by businesses and organizations for various purposes, such as understanding customer behavior, improving marketing strategies, increasing sales, reducing costs, detecting fraud, and enhancing decision making .
- Data mining involves four main steps: setting the business objectives, preparing the data, building and evaluating the models, and deploying the results.
- Data mining can be performed using different programming languages, such as Python, R, SQL, and Java, depending on the type and complexity of the data and the desired outcomes.
- Data mining can be classified into different types, such as descriptive, predictive, prescriptive, and exploratory, depending on the goal and scope of the analysis.
- Data mining can also be categorized into different techniques, such as clustering, classification, regression, association, and anomaly detection, depending on the type and structure of the data and the problem to be solved .



### Architecture of Data Mining and How Data Mining Works (Process)

- Data mining is the process of discovering meaningful new correlations, patterns, and trends by shifting through large amounts of data stored in repositories, using pattern recognition technologies as well as statistical and mathematical techniques.
- Data mining usually consists of four main steps: setting objectives, data gathering and preparation, applying data mining algorithms, and evaluating results.
- The data mining process involves several components, and these components constitute a data mining system architecture. Data mining architecture can be classified into three types: no coupling, loose coupling, and semi-tight coupling.
- The significant components of data mining systems are:
  - A data source, which provides the raw data to be mined. Data sources can be databases, data warehouses, flat files, web pages, etc.
  - A data mining engine, which performs the core data mining tasks such as association, classification, clustering, prediction, etc. Data mining engine can use various techniques such as neural networks, decision trees, genetic algorithms, etc.
  - A data warehouse server, which stores the data and metadata in a multidimensional structure for efficient analysis and retrieval. Data warehouse server can also perform data cleaning, integration, transformation, and loading operations.
  - A pattern evaluation module, which evaluates the patterns or models generated by the data mining engine and ranks them according to some criteria such as interestingness, novelty, usefulness, etc. Pattern evaluation module can also interact with the user or the knowledge base to guide the data mining process.
  - A graphical user interface, which allows the user to interact with the data mining system and visualize the results. Graphical user interface can also provide tools for data exploration, query formulation, report generation, etc.
  - A knowledge base, which stores the domain knowledge and user preferences that can be used to guide the data mining process and evaluate the patterns. Knowledge base can also store the previous results and feedback from the user.



### Functionalities & Classifications of Data Mining

- Data mining is the process of discovering useful patterns and insights from large and complex data sets.
- Data mining functionalities are used to represent the type of patterns that have to be discovered in data mining tasks.
- In general, data mining tasks can be classified into two types including descriptive and predictive.
- Descriptive mining tasks define the common features of the data in the database and the predictive mining tasks act inference on the current data to predict future outcomes or behaviors.
- Here are some of the data mining functionalities:

  - **Classification**: Classification is a process of determining a model that describes and differentiates data classes with the intention of using it to predict a class of unknown or anonymous objects. For example, classifying customers into different segments based on their preferences or behavior.
  - **Clustering**: Clustering is a process of grouping a set of data objects into clusters such that the objects within a cluster are more similar to each other than to those in other clusters. For example, clustering customers based on their geographic locations or demographic characteristics.
  - **Association analysis**: Association analysis is a process of finding the rules that describe the relationships or associations among a set of data items. For example, finding the frequent itemsets or market basket analysis that shows what products are often bought together by customers.
  - **Regression**: Regression is a process of finding a function that models the relationship between one or more independent variables and a dependent variable. For example, finding the relationship between the sales and the advertising expenditure of a product.
  - **Anomaly detection**: Anomaly detection is a process of identifying the data objects that deviate significantly from the normal behavior or expected pattern of the data. For example, detecting fraud transactions or network intrusions.
  - **Summarization**: Summarization is a process of providing a concise and compact representation of the data set, including visualization and report generation. For example, summarizing the sales performance of a company in a dashboard or a report.



### Representation of Input Data

- Data representation is the process of transforming raw data into a form that can be easily understood, analyzed, and communicated by humans or machines.
- Data representation techniques are essential for data mining, which is the process of discovering useful patterns and insights from large and complex datasets.
- Data representation techniques can be classified into two categories: data transformation and data visualization.

#### Data Transformation

- Data transformation is the process of converting data from one format or structure to another, such as from text to numeric, from relational to hierarchical, or from unstructured to structured.
- Data transformation can help to reduce the size, complexity, and redundancy of the data, as well as to enhance its quality, consistency, and usability.
- Data transformation techniques include:

  - Data cleaning: removing or correcting errors, outliers, missing values, and inconsistencies in the data.
  - Data integration: combining data from multiple sources into a unified view or schema.
  - Data reduction: reducing the dimensionality, granularity, or volume of the data to a smaller and simpler representation, while preserving its essential information and characteristics. Data reduction techniques include data cube aggregation, dimension reduction, data compression, data sampling, etc.
  - Data discretization: transforming continuous or numerical data into discrete or categorical data, such as by binning, clustering, or entropy-based methods.
  - Data normalization: scaling or standardizing the data to a common range or distribution, such as by min-max, z-score, or decimal scaling methods.
  - Data encoding: transforming the data into a different representation or format, such as by one-hot encoding, hashing, or encryption.

#### Data Visualization

- Data visualization is the process of presenting data in a graphical or pictorial form, such as charts, graphs, maps, or images, to facilitate human perception, cognition, and communication.
- Data visualization can help to reveal patterns, trends, outliers, and relationships in the data, as well as to convey insights, stories, and messages to the audience.
- Data visualization techniques include:

  - Basic charts: using simple geometric shapes, such as bars, lines, points, or pies, to represent the data values or frequencies, such as by bar charts, line charts, scatter plots, or pie charts.
  - Advanced charts: using more complex or specialized shapes, such as areas, bubbles, boxes, or radars, to represent the data values or distributions, such as by area charts, bubble charts, box plots, or radar charts.
  - Maps: using geographic or spatial coordinates, such as latitude and longitude, to represent the data values or regions, such as by choropleth maps, heat maps, or dot maps.
  - Networks: using nodes and edges, such as circles and lines, to represent the data entities and relationships, such as by graphs, trees, or hierarchies.
  - Images: using pixels, colors, or textures, to represent the data values or features, such as by heat maps, contour maps, or word clouds.



### Analysis Methodologies for Data Mining and Warehousing

- Data mining and warehousing are both business intelligence tools that are used to turn data into actionable knowledge.
- Data mining is a process of statistical analysis that applies algorithms to discover patterns and relationships in large datasets.
- Data warehousing is a process of storing, organizing, and integrating data from various sources into a central repository that supports analytical queries and reporting.
- Data mining and warehousing are related but have different methodologies and goals.

#### Data Mining Methodology

- Data mining methodology consists of the following steps:
  - Data selection: choosing the relevant data sources and subsets for analysis.
  - Data preprocessing: cleaning, transforming, and integrating the data to make it suitable for mining.
  - Data mining: applying data mining techniques such as classification, clustering, association, regression, etc. to extract knowledge from the data.
  - Data evaluation: assessing the validity, usefulness, and significance of the mined knowledge.
  - Data presentation: visualizing, summarizing, and communicating the results to the stakeholders.

#### Data Warehousing Methodology

- Data warehousing methodology is based on Extract, Transform, and Load (ETL) jobs. It consists of the following steps:
  - Data extraction: collecting data from various sources such as databases, files, web services, etc.
  - Data transformation: converting the data into a consistent format, resolving conflicts, and applying business rules.
  - Data loading: loading the transformed data into the data warehouse, which is usually organized in a star or snowflake schema.
  - Data analysis: querying and reporting on the data warehouse using Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) tools, which support multidimensional analysis and data mining.



### Data Warehousing - Introduction to Data Warehousing

- A data warehouse is a database designed to enable and support business intelligence (BI) activities, especially analytics .
- A data warehouse is a type of data management system that aggregates data from different sources into a single, central, consistent data store.
- A data warehouse is solely intended to perform queries and analysis and often contains large amounts of historical data.
- A data warehouse helps users understand and enhance their organization's performance by providing them with relevant, accurate, and timely information.
- A data warehouse can also support data mining, artificial intelligence (AI), and machine learning applications by providing them with high-quality and structured data.
- A data warehouse typically follows a multidimensional model that organizes data into facts and dimensions.
- A data warehouse can be implemented using various architectures, such as star schema, snowflake schema, or data vault.
- A data warehouse can be built using various technologies, such as relational databases, columnar databases, or cloud-based platforms .
- A data warehouse can be integrated with other data management systems, such as data lakes, data marts, or operational data stores .
- A data warehouse can provide various benefits to an organization, such as improved decision making, enhanced customer service, increased revenue, reduced costs, and competitive advantage .



### Data Mart

- A data mart is a **subset** of a data warehouse that is focused on a **particular subject area**, such as a department, a business function, or a product line .
- A data mart is a **relational database** that stores transactional data in rows and columns, which makes it easy to access, organize, and understand.
- A data mart contains **historical data** that can be used for analysis, reporting, and decision making by a specific group of users  .
- A data mart can be **independent** or **dependent** on a data warehouse. An independent data mart is created directly from source systems, while a dependent data mart is derived from a data warehouse.
- A data mart can also be classified as **top-down** or **bottom-up**. A top-down data mart is designed as part of a larger data warehouse project, while a bottom-up data mart is developed incrementally to meet the needs of individual business units.
- A data mart can have various benefits, such as:
  - Improving data quality and consistency by applying data cleansing and standardization rules.
  - Enhancing data security and privacy by restricting access to authorized users .
  - Increasing data performance and availability by reducing the load on the data warehouse and providing faster query response times  .
  - Supporting data analysis and visualization by providing relevant and customized data models and tools  .
  - Empowering business users and analysts by giving them more control and flexibility over their data  .



### Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) – Tools

- Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) is a technology that is used to organize large business databases and support business intelligence.
- OLAP tools enable to analyze multidimensional data interactively from multiple perspectives.
- OLAP involves relational database, report writing and data mining and consists of three basic analytical operations: consolidation, slicing and dicing, and drill down and roll up.
- Consolidation is the aggregation of data along one or more dimensions, such as summing up sales by region or by product category.
- Slicing and dicing is the selection and projection of data along specific dimensions, such as filtering sales by a certain time period or by a certain customer segment.
- Drill down and roll up are the navigation of data from higher to lower levels of detail or vice versa, such as going from quarterly sales to monthly sales or from product sales to total sales.
- OLAP tools can be classified into three types: MOLAP, ROLAP, and HOLAP .
- MOLAP or Multidimensional Online Analytical Processing uses a multidimensional cube that accesses stored data through various combinations of dimensions and measures.
- ROLAP or Relational Online Analytical Processing stores data in relational tables using columns and rows and queries data using SQL.
- HOLAP or Hybrid Online Analytical Processing connects attributes of both MOLAP and ROLAP and allows users to choose the best storage option for different types of data.
- Some examples of OLAP tools are Microsoft Excel, IBM Cognos, Oracle Essbase, SAP BusinessObjects, and MicroStrategy.



### Data Modelling

- Data modelling is the process of creating a visual representation of either a whole information system or parts of it to communicate connections between data points and structures  .
- Data modelling is a core data management discipline that helps pinpoint information needs for different business processes.
- Data modelling is an iterative process that should be repeated and refined as business needs change.
- Data modelling is used to define and analyze data requirements needed to support the business processes within the scope of corresponding information systems in organizations.
- Data modelling can be classified into different types, such as:
  - Conceptual data modelling: This type of data modelling focuses on the high-level business concepts and entities, and their relationships. It is independent of any specific technology or implementation details. It is used to communicate the overall data structure and meaning to stakeholders and users .
  - Logical data modelling: This type of data modelling describes the data in more detail, such as the attributes, keys, constraints, and data types. It is still independent of any physical implementation, but it is more specific and closer to the actual data design. It is used to validate the data requirements and rules with the business users .
  - Physical data modelling: This type of data modelling specifies how the data will be stored and accessed in a specific database system or technology. It includes the physical characteristics of the data, such as the table names, column names, data types, indexes, partitions, and so on. It is used to optimize the data performance and storage efficiency .
- Data modelling can also be categorized into different approaches, such as:
  - Relational data modelling: This approach is based on the relational model of data, which organizes data into tables (or relations) that consist of rows (or tuples) and columns (or attributes). Each table has a primary key that uniquely identifies each row, and foreign keys that link to other tables. Relational data modelling is widely used for relational database systems, such as SQL.
  - Dimensional data modelling: This approach is based on the star schema or snowflake schema, which organizes data into facts and dimensions. Facts are numerical measures that represent business events or transactions, and dimensions are descriptive attributes that provide context and meaning to the facts. Dimensional data modelling is commonly used for data warehousing and business intelligence applications, such as OLAP.
  - NoSQL data modelling: This approach is based on the non-relational or distributed model of data, which does not follow the rigid structure and rules of the relational model. NoSQL data modelling can handle various types of data, such as structured, semi-structured, unstructured, or polymorphic data. NoSQL data modelling is suitable for big data and cloud computing scenarios, such as document, key-value, graph, or columnar databases.



### Difference between OLAP and OLTP

- OLAP stands for Online Analytical Processing, while OLTP stands for Online Transaction Processing.
- OLAP is optimized for conducting complex data analysis for smarter decision-making, while OLTP is ideal for making simple updates, insertions and deletions in databases.
- OLAP systems allow you to extract data for complex analysis, often involving large numbers of records and multiple data facts, while OLTP systems focus on processing individual transactions quickly and accurately.
- OLAP databases have a multi-dimensional schema, so they can support complex queries of multiple data sets, such as aggregations, drill-downs, and slicing and dicing, while OLTP databases have a relational schema, so they can ensure data integrity and consistency across tables.
- OLAP systems are read-intensive, meaning they perform more queries than updates, while OLTP systems are write-intensive, meaning they perform more updates than queries.
- OLAP systems process data more slowly as compared to OLTP systems, because they have to deal with larger and more complex data sets, while OLTP systems process data more quickly, because they have to upload the latest information immediately for users to retrieve the most updated data.
- OLAP systems are usually used by business analysts, managers, and executives for strategic decision-making, while OLTP systems are usually used by clerks, customers, and operational staff for routine tasks.



### Schema – Star and Snowflake Schemas

- A schema is a logical representation of the structure and organization of data in a data warehouse or data mart.
- A schema consists of fact tables and dimension tables that store the data and metadata of the data warehouse or data mart.
- A fact table contains the measures or metrics of the business processes, such as sales, revenue, profit, etc.
- A dimension table contains the attributes or characteristics of the business entities, such as product, customer, location, time, etc.
- A schema can be designed using different approaches, such as star schema and snowflake schema, depending on the requirements and complexity of the data warehouse or data mart.

#### Star Schema

- A star schema is the simplest and most common type of schema in data warehousing and business intelligence projects.
- A star schema consists of a single fact table and multiple dimension tables that are directly connected to the fact table.
- A star schema is called so because its structure resembles a star, with the fact table at the center and the dimension tables as the points.
- A star schema has the following advantages:
  - It is easy to understand and implement, as it has a simple and denormalized structure.
  - It is efficient for querying and analysis, as it requires fewer joins and scans to retrieve the data.
  - It is flexible and scalable, as new dimensions and measures can be easily added or modified without affecting the existing structure.
- A star schema has the following disadvantages:
  - It may result in data redundancy and inconsistency, as the same data may be repeated in multiple dimension tables.
  - It may not capture the complex relationships and hierarchies among the dimensions, as each dimension table is independent and isolated from the others.

#### Snowflake Schema

- A snowflake schema is an extension and variation of the star schema, where some or all of the dimension tables are further normalized into sub-dimension tables.
- A snowflake schema is called so because its structure resembles a snowflake, with the fact table at the center and the dimension tables and sub-dimension tables as the branches.
- A snowflake schema has the following advantages:
  - It reduces data redundancy and inconsistency, as the same data is stored only once in the sub-dimension tables.
  - It captures the complex relationships and hierarchies among the dimensions, as each sub-dimension table is linked to its parent dimension table.
- A snowflake schema has the following disadvantages:
  - It is difficult to understand and implement, as it has a complex and normalized structure.
  - It is less efficient for querying and analysis, as it requires more joins and scans to retrieve the data.
  - It is less flexible and scalable, as new dimensions and measures may require changes in the existing structure.

### Data Transformation – Standardization and Feature Extraction

- Data transformation is the process of modifying the data to make it more suitable for analysis and modeling.
- Data transformation can involve various operations, such as scaling, encoding, imputing, aggregating, filtering, etc.
- Data transformation can improve the quality, consistency, and usability of the data, as well as reduce the dimensionality and complexity of the data.
- Data standardization and feature extraction are two common types of data transformation that are often used in data science and machine learning.

#### Data Standardization

- Data standardization is the process of transforming the data to have a common scale, format, or structure.
- Data standardization can help to eliminate the effects of different units, ranges, or distributions of the data, and make the data more comparable and compatible.
- Data standardization can also help to reduce the influence of outliers and improve the performance of some algorithms that are sensitive to the scale or distribution of the data, such as k-means clustering, linear regression, etc.
- Data standardization can be done in different ways, such as:

  - Z-score normalization: This method transforms the data to have a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one, by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation of each feature.
  - Min-max normalization: This method transforms the data to have a minimum of zero and a maximum of one, by subtracting the minimum and dividing by the range of each feature.
  - Decimal scaling: This method transforms the data to have a range between -1 and 1, by dividing each feature by a power of 10 that is larger than the maximum absolute value of the feature.
  - Unit vector normalization: This method transforms the data to have a unit length, by dividing each feature by the Euclidean norm of the feature vector.

#### Feature Extraction

- Feature extraction is the process of creating new features from the existing features, by applying some transformation or function to the data.
- Feature extraction can help to capture the underlying patterns, relationships, or information in the data, and reduce the dimensionality and redundancy of the data.
- Feature extraction can also help to enhance the interpretability and explainability of the data, and improve the performance of some algorithms that are based on the similarity or distance of the data, such as nearest neighbor, support vector machine, etc.
- Feature extraction can be done in different ways, such as:

  - Principal component analysis (PCA): This method transforms the data to a new set of features that are linear combinations of the original features, and that capture the maximum variance of the data.
  - Linear discriminant analysis (LDA): This method transforms the data to a new set of features that are linear combinations of the original features, and that maximize the separation between different classes of the data.
  - Kernel methods: These methods transform the data to a new feature space that is nonlinearly related to the original feature space, by applying a kernel function that measures the similarity or distance of the data points.
  - Feature selection: This method selects a subset of features that are relevant and informative for the analysis or modeling task, by applying some criteria or algorithm to rank or filter the features.



### Data Reduction – Sampling

- Data reduction is a technique used in data mining to reduce the size of a dataset while still preserving the most important information .
- Sampling is one of the data reduction methods that involves selecting a subset of the data to work with, rather than using the entire dataset .
- Sampling can be useful for reducing the size of a dataset while still preserving the overall trends and patterns in the data.
- There are four types of sampling data reduction methods:
  - Simple Random Sample Without Replacement (SRSWOR): This method selects a sample of a fixed size from the population without replacement, meaning that each element can be selected only once.
  - Simple Random Sample with Replacement (SRSWR): This method selects a sample of a fixed size from the population with replacement, meaning that each element can be selected more than once.
  - Cluster Sample: This method divides the population into groups or clusters, and then selects a sample of clusters to represent the population. The clusters are usually based on some geographic or demographic criteria.
  - Stratified Sample: This method divides the population into strata or subgroups based on some attribute or characteristic, and then selects a sample from each stratum to represent the population. The strata are usually based on some similarity or homogeneity among the elements.
- Sampling can reduce the data size and complexity, but it can also introduce some errors or biases in the data analysis. Therefore, it is important to choose an appropriate sampling method and sample size that can balance the trade-off between data reduction and data quality .

### PCA for the notes of the Unit 3 - BI – DATA PREPARTTION in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

- Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a technique that reduces the dimensionality of large data sets by transforming a large set of variables into a smaller one that still contains most of the information in the large set.
- PCA is useful for data analysis, visualization, compression, de-noising, and feature extraction  .
- PCA works by finding the directions of maximum variance in the data, called principal components (PCs), and projecting the data onto a lower-dimensional space spanned by the PCs.
- The PCs are orthogonal to each other and are ordered by the amount of variance they explain. The first PC explains the most variance, the second PC explains the next most variance, and so on.
- The PCs can be represented as linear combinations of the original variables, and the coefficients of these linear combinations are called loadings. The loadings indicate how much each variable contributes to each PC.
- A biplot is a graphical tool that displays both the PCs and the loadings in a single plot. The biplot can help to interpret the PCs and to identify patterns and outliers in the data.
- To perform PCA, the data should be standardized (mean-centered and scaled by the standard deviation) to avoid the influence of different scales and units on the PCs.
- PCA can be implemented in Python using libraries such as scikit-learn, pandas, and matplotlib .

- Data discretization can help to improve the performance and effectiveness of some machine learning algorithms that prefer or require categorical or ordinal input variables .
- Data discretization can also help to reduce the noise and outliers in the data, and to simplify the data analysis and visualization .
- Data discretization can be performed using different techniques, such as equal-width, equal-frequency, entropy-based, clustering-based, or supervised methods .

### Behavioral Data Analytics Process

- Analyze the data and derive insights: Use the analytics tool to segment the users into behavioral cohorts based on their actions, attributes, or outcomes. ### Business Process Management

- Business process management (BPM) is a discipline that uses various methods to discover, model, analyze, measure, improve and optimize business processes  .
- A business process is a set of activities that coordinate the behavior of people, systems, information and things to produce business outcomes in support of a business strategy.
- BPM aims to align business processes with the enterprise goals, improve efficiency and effectiveness, reduce costs and errors, enhance customer satisfaction and innovation, and enable agility and adaptability .
- BPM involves the following phases :
  - Design: Define the current and desired state of the business process, identify the gaps and opportunities for improvement, and specify the requirements and objectives for the new process.
  - Model: Represent the business process graphically using a notation such as Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN), and simulate the behavior and performance of the process under different scenarios and conditions.
  - Execute: Implement the business process using a software platform or system that supports the execution of the process, such as a business process management system (BPMS) or a workflow engine, and integrate the process with other systems and resources.
  - Monitor: Measure and analyze the performance and outcomes of the business process using key performance indicators (KPIs), metrics, dashboards, reports, and alerts, and identify deviations, bottlenecks, and issues.
  - Optimize: Evaluate the results and feedback from the monitoring phase, and identify and implement changes and improvements to the business process, such as redesigning, reengineering, or automating parts of the process.
  - Repeat: Continuously monitor and optimize the business process in a cyclical manner, and adapt the process to changing business needs and environments.
- BPM can be supported by various tools and technologies, such as :
  - BPM software: A software application or suite that provides the functionality and features to support the BPM lifecycle, such as process modeling, execution, monitoring, and optimization.
  - BPMS: A software platform or system that enables the execution and management of business processes, and provides integration, automation, orchestration, and collaboration capabilities.
  - Business rules engine: A software component that allows the definition and execution of business rules that govern the logic and decisions of a business process.
  - Business activity monitoring (BAM): A software component that allows the collection and analysis of real-time data from business processes and systems, and provides dashboards, reports, and alerts to monitor the performance and outcomes of the processes.
  - Business intelligence (BI): A software component that allows the collection and analysis of historical and current data from business processes and systems, and provides dashboards, reports, and insights to support decision making and strategic planning.
  - Artificial intelligence (AI): A software component that uses machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and other techniques to provide intelligent capabilities to a business process, such as prediction, recommendation, classification, recognition, and automation.

### Root Cause Analysis

- Root cause analysis (RCA) is a method of problem solving used for identifying the root causes of faults or problems.
- A root cause is the core issue that sets in motion the entire cause-and-effect reaction that ultimately leads to the problem.
- RCA assumes that it is much more effective to systematically prevent and solve for underlying issues rather than just treating ad hoc symptoms and putting out fires.
- RCA is a team approach methodology that works by understanding that systems and actions are related and that one action can trigger an event and that event can trigger another event and so on until you have a problem or multiple problems.
- There are four main steps in an RCA:
  - Identify the problem: Define the problem clearly and accurately, and gather data and evidence to support the problem statement.
  - Analyze the problem: Use tools and techniques such as brainstorming, fishbone diagrams, 5 whys, Pareto charts, etc. to identify all possible causes of the problem and narrow down the root cause(s).
  - Identify the solution: Generate and evaluate potential solutions that address the root cause(s) and prevent the problem from recurring.
  - Implement the solution: Plan and execute the solution, monitor the results, and verify the effectiveness of the solution.
