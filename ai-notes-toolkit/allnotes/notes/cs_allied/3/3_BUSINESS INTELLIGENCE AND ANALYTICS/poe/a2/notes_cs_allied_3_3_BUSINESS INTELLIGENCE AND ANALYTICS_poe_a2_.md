# KCS

Knowledge-Centered Service (KCS) is an approach to providing technical support which emphasizes:

1. Capturing and organizing knowledge - Technicians are required to document solutions to problems they solve, which are then reviewed and organized into a knowledge base.
2. Reusing knowledge - Before solving a new problem, technicians are required to search the knowledge base for relevant solutions or partial solutions.
3. Continuous improvement - The knowledge base is continually expanded and improved as new solutions are added and as technicians provide feedback on the usefulness of existing articles.

Benefits of KCS include:

- Faster resolution of problems through reuse of existing solutions.
- Higher quality solutions through review and collaborative improvement of knowledge base content.
- Reduced dependence on any one individual, since knowledge is collectively owned by the group.
- Ability to support growing numbers of problems and products as the knowledge base expands.

The success of a KCS deployment depends on:

- Strong management support and endorsement of knowledge-centered practices.
- Incentives and rewards for contributing to and reusing the knowledge base.
- Training on documentation and knowledge management for technical staff.
- Easy-to-use knowledge base authoring and searching tools.
- Tracking knowledge base usage and impact to continuously improve processes.

In summary, KCS is an approach focused on capturing and reusing technical knowledge to provide faster, higher quality support. When successfully implemented, KCS can improve support efficiency and scalability while reducing dependence on individual experts.

### Functionalities & Classifications of Data Mining

Data Mining has various functionalities and can be classified in multiple ways:

**Functionalities:**

- Prediction: Predicting unknown or future values based on the patterns derived from the data. e.g. Prediction of stock prices.
- Classification: Assigning objects to predefined classes or categories based on the data. e.g. Email classification into spam/non-spam.
- Clustering: Grouping objects based on similarities in the data. e.g. Customers segmentation based on shopping patterns.
- Association: Finding associations between variables in the data. e.g. Market basket analysis to find products bought together.
- Anomaly Detection: Identifying anomalies or unusual patterns in the data. e.g. Fraud detection.
- Summarization: Producing compact summaries of the data.

**Classifications:**

- Supervised vs Unsupervised Learning: Supervised learning uses labeled data to produce desired outputs while unsupervised learning uses unlabeled data to find hidden patterns.
- Regression vs Classification: Regression produces continuous values as output while classification produces discrete classes or categories as output.
- descriptive vs predictive: Descriptive mining finds patterns in the data while predictive mining uses the discovered patterns to predict future trends.

### Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) – Tools

OLAP tools are designed to support multidimensional analytical queries on aggregated data. OLAP tools provide a multidimensional view of data, allowing analysts to slice and dice data and view it from multiple perspectives. Some key features of OLAP tools are:

- Pre-aggregated data: Data is aggregated in advance for common queries, allowing fast response times.
- Multidimensional data model: Data is organized into cubes with dimensions and measures. This allows analysis from multiple perspectives.
- Calculations: Tools provide aggregations and calculations on measures. Common examples include sums, averages, percentages, rankings, etc.
- Drill down/up: Users can drill down into detail data or drill up to higher levels of aggregation.
- slicing and dicing: Tools allow users to slice data into subsets and dice data from multiple angles to interactively analyze the data.

Some popular OLAP tools are:

- Microsoft Analysis Services and Power BI
- Oracle OLAP
- SAP Business Warehouse
- Tableau
- Qlik

The key benefit of OLAP tools is fast query response time on aggregated data. However, the pre-aggregation may result in scalability issues and latency in reflecting the most recent data. OLAP tools are best suited for analyzing relatively static data to gain business insights through multidimensional analysis and calculations.

- Queries: OLAP queries are complex and involve aggregations while OLTP queries are simple and involve selections and updates
- Data access: OLAP has read-only or read-mostly data access while OLTP has read-write data access with frequent updates

### Schema – Star and Snowflake Schemas

- Star schema: The star schema consists of one or more fact tables referencing any number of dimension tables. The fact table is located at the center and dimension tables surround it like a star, hence the name star schema.
- Snowflake schema: The snowflake schema is an extension of the star schema, where the dimension tables are normalized. This means the dimension tables are broken down into additional tables. The snowflake schema gets its name from the fact that the diagram resembles a snowflake with its many branches.
- Advantages of star schema:
-- Simplicity: Easy to understand and implement.
-- Performance: Queries are efficient since only a single join is required to access the fact table.
-- Scalability: Easy to add new dimensions and fact tables.
- Advantages of snowflake schema:
-- Reduced redundancy: Normalized tables remove data duplication.
-- Better performance: Queries on the dimension tables are efficient since they are broken down into smaller tables.
-- Storage space: Due to data normalization, snowflake schemas require less space than star schemas.

2. Extract: The first step involves extracting data from the source systems. The source systems could be transactional databases, Excel sheets, text files, etc. The extraction is usually done using SQL queries or other extraction tools.

The ETL process plays a crucial role in maintaining the data warehouse by keeping it up-to-date with the changes in the source systems. A robust ETL process is essential for accurate reporting and analysis from the data warehouse.

### Data Reduction – Sampling

- Sampling is a technique used to reduce the size of data. It involves selecting a subset of data from the entire data set.
- Some reasons to use sampling are:
- The entire data set is too large to be processed and analyzed efficiently.
- The analysis does not require looking at the entire data set. A subset is sufficient to get meaningful insights.
- It reduces the time and resources required for data processing and analysis.
- Types of sampling:
- Simple random sampling: Every item of the population has an equal chance of being selected.
- Stratified sampling: The population is divided into homogeneous subgroups called strata and samples are drawn from each stratum.
- Cluster sampling: The population is divided into clusters and samples are drawn from the clusters.
- Systematic sampling: Items are selected from the population based on a fixed periodic interval.
- judgement sampling: Subject matter experts select samples based on their judgement.
- The key consideration while sampling is that the sample should be representative of the entire data set. A biased sample will lead to inaccurate analysis and insights.

### PCA for the notes of the Unit 3 - BI – DATA PREPARTTION in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

1. PCA stands for Principal Component Analysis. It is a dimensionality reduction technique.
2. It is used to transform a large set of variables into a smaller set of variables called principal components that still contain most of the information in the large set.
3. The primary goal of PCA is to identify the underlying variables (in a data set with many variables) that explain the maximum amount of variance in the data.
4. The first principal component accounts for as much of the variability in the data as possible, and each subsequent component accounts for as much of the remaining variability as possible.
5. The main steps in PCA:

a) Standardize the data (mean 0 and variance 1)
b) Calculate the covariance matrix
c) Calculate the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the covariance matrix
d) Sort the eigenvectors by eigenvalues in descending order
e) Choose k eigenvectors with the largest eigenvalues to form a new coordinate system

6. PCA is often used to simplify data visualization and preprocessing tasks like feature selection. It is a very popular technique for exploratory data analysis.

### Data Discretization for the notes of the Unit 3 - BI – DATA PREPARTTION in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

- Data discretization is the process of converting continuous numerical data into discrete data. It involves splitting the range of numerical variables into intervals and replacing the original numerical variable with theinterval in which its value falls.
- The main advantages of data discretization are:
    - It reduces the complexity of learning algorithms and speeds up the learning process.
    - It can reduce the effect of noise or outliers in the data.
    - It can improve the accuracy of algorithms that assume discrete values.
- Some common discretization techniques are:
    - Equal-width binning: Generates intervals of equal width. The cut-off points aredecided based on the range of values and desired number of intervals.
    - Equal-frequency binning: Generates intervals that contain approximately equalnumbers of instances. The cut-off points are decided dynamically based on thedistribution of values.
    - Entropy-based binning: Uses entropy to determine the cut-off points iteratively.It aims to maximize the information gain from the discretization.
- Data discretization is mainly used as a preprocessing step for algorithms that assume discrete input variables such as decision tree learners and rule-based classifiers. It can improve the accuracy, efficiency, and interpretability of these algorithms. However, it may not be suitable if the original continuous values are important or if the data has complex distributions.
