### Basic Electronics for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

In this unit, we will introduce the basic concepts of electronics that are necessary to understand the functioning of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything. The following points will cover the fundamental principles of electronics:

- **Electricity**: Electricity is the flow of electrons through a conductor. It is measured in units of current called amperes (A). The voltage is the force that drives the current, and it is measured in volts (V). The resistance is the opposition to the flow of current, and it is measured in ohms (Ω). The relationship between these three quantities is given by Ohm's law, which states that the current is directly proportional to the voltage and inversely proportional to the resistance.

- **Circuits**: A circuit is a closed path in which electrons can flow. It consists of a power source (battery or power supply), wires, and components (such as resistors, capacitors, and transistors). There are two types of circuits: series and parallel. In a series circuit, the components are connected in a single path, whereas in a parallel circuit, the components are connected in multiple paths.

- **Components**: Components are electronic devices that perform specific functions in a circuit. Some of the common components are:

  - Resistors: Resistors limit the flow of current in a circuit.
  - Capacitors: Capacitors store electrical charge.
  - Transistors: Transistors amplify or switch electronic signals.
  - Diodes: Diodes allow current to flow in one direction only.

- **Digital electronics**: Digital electronics deal with circuits that operate on binary signals (0 and 1). The most basic digital component is the logic gate, which performs Boolean logic operations such as AND, OR, and NOT. These gates are combined to form more complex circuits such as adders, multiplexers, and flip-flops.

- **Analog electronics**: Analog electronics deal with circuits that operate on continuous signals, such as sound and video. The most basic analog component is the operational amplifier (op-amp), which amplifies or attenuates an input signal. These op-amps are combined to form more complex circuits such as filters, oscillators, and amplifiers.

- **Integrated circuits**: Integrated circuits (ICs) are miniaturized electronic circuits that contain many components on a single chip. They are the building blocks of modern electronics and are used in everything from smartphones to spacecraft.

- **Sensors**: Sensors are electronic devices that detect and measure physical quantities such as temperature, pressure, and light. They are essential components of wearable computing and the Internet of Everything, enabling devices to interact with the environment.

In conclusion, understanding the basics of electronics is crucial for anyone working in the field of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything. The above points cover the fundamental principles of electronics, and familiarity with them will be essential for understanding the more advanced concepts in this subject.

### Software Frameworks

1. Definition: A software framework is a reusable set of libraries, tools, and APIs that provide a structure for building software applications.
2. Purpose: The primary purpose of a software framework is to simplify the development process by providing a pre-existing structure for developers to build on.
3. Types of Frameworks:
    - Front-end Frameworks: These frameworks are used for building user interfaces (UI) and enhancing user experience (UX). Examples include Angular, React, and Vue.js.
    - Back-end Frameworks: These frameworks are used for building server-side applications and APIs. Examples include Node.js, Django, and Laravel.
    - Full-Stack Frameworks: These frameworks combine both front-end and back-end development, providing a complete solution for building web applications. Examples include MEAN Stack and MERN Stack.
4. Advantages of Software Frameworks:
    - Faster development time: Frameworks provide a pre-existing structure, reducing the amount of code that needs to be written from scratch.
    - Improved code quality: Frameworks provide best practices and industry-standard techniques for coding, leading to more consistent and maintainable code.
    - Community support: Frameworks have large communities of developers who contribute to their development, providing support and resources for other developers.
5. Disadvantages of Software Frameworks:
    - Learning curve: Frameworks have their own set of rules and best practices, which can take time to learn.
    - Limited flexibility: Frameworks can limit the flexibility of developers since they have to work within the constraints of the framework.
    - Overhead: Frameworks often come with a lot of pre-built code, which can lead to slower performance and increased memory usage.

### Hardware Frameworks

1. Definition: A hardware framework is a set of rules and guidelines for designing and building computer hardware systems.
2. Purpose: The primary purpose of a hardware framework is to ensure that hardware systems are designed to be interoperable and compatible with other systems.
3. Types of Frameworks:
    - Processor Architecture Frameworks: These frameworks define the architecture of the processor and the instruction set that it uses. Examples include x86 and ARM.
    - System-on-a-Chip (SoC) Frameworks: These frameworks provide a complete system on a single chip, including the processor, memory, and input/output interfaces. Examples include Raspberry Pi and BeagleBone.
    - Networking Frameworks: These frameworks define the rules for communicating between different hardware devices, ensuring that they are interoperable.
4. Advantages of Hardware Frameworks:
    - Interoperability: Frameworks ensure that hardware systems are interoperable and compatible with other systems.
    - Standardization: Frameworks provide a standard set of rules and guidelines for designing hardware systems, ensuring consistency and reliability.
    - Reduced development time: Frameworks provide a pre-existing structure for building hardware systems, reducing the amount of time required to design and build them.
5. Disadvantages of Hardware Frameworks:
    - Limited flexibility: Frameworks can limit the flexibility of hardware designers since they have to work within the constraints of the framework.
    - Complexity: Hardware frameworks can be complex and difficult to understand, leading to a steep learning curve for designers.
    - Cost: Some hardware frameworks can be expensive to license or use, leading to higher development costs.

### I2C/IIC

I2C, which stands for Inter-Integrated Circuit, and IIC, which stands for I2C Inter-Integrated Circuit, are two terms used interchangeably to describe the same communication protocol. The I2C/IIC protocol is a two-wire communication protocol developed by Philips Semiconductors (now NXP Semiconductors) in the early 1980s. It is commonly used to connect low-speed peripherals to microcontrollers, such as sensors, EEPROMs, and other devices.

Here are some important points to understand about the I2C/IIC protocol:

- Two-wire communication: The I2C/IIC protocol uses only two wires for communication: the Serial Data Line (SDA) and the Serial Clock Line (SCL). This makes it a simple and cost-effective way to interface multiple devices with a microcontroller.
- Master/slave architecture: In an I2C/IIC communication, there is always one master device that initiates the communication and one or more slave devices that respond to the master's requests. The master device generates the clock signal and controls the communication sequence.
- Addressing: Each slave device on the I2C/IIC bus has a unique 7-bit address that is used by the master device to communicate with it. This allows multiple devices to be connected to the same bus without interfering with each other.
- Clock speed: The maximum clock speed of the I2C/IIC bus depends on the specific devices connected to it, but typically ranges from a few kilohertz to a few megahertz. Slower clock speeds are more reliable over longer distances, while faster clock speeds allow for faster data transfer.
- Data transfer: The I2C/IIC protocol supports two types of data transfer: byte-level transfer and bit-level transfer. In byte-level transfer, the master device sends or receives a byte of data to or from a slave device. In bit-level transfer, the master device sends or receives a single bit of data to or from a slave device, which can be useful for controlling individual bits of a device's register.
- Acknowledgment: Once a byte of data has been transferred, the receiving device sends an acknowledgment (ACK) or non-acknowledgment (NACK) signal back to the master device to indicate whether the transfer was successful or not. This allows for reliable communication between devices.

In summary, the I2C/IIC protocol is a simple and cost-effective way to interface multiple low-speed peripherals with a microcontroller. Its two-wire communication, master/slave architecture, addressing, clock speed, data transfer, and acknowledgment make it a reliable and efficient communication protocol for a wide range of applications.



### Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) Protocol

Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) is a serial communication protocol that allows multiple devices to communicate with each other using a two-wire interface. It was developed by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductors) in the early 1980s and has since become a popular standard for connecting various electronic devices in embedded systems, such as microcontrollers, sensors, and actuators.

#### Key Features

- Two-wire Interface: I2C uses a two-wire interface consisting of a serial clock line (SCL) and a serial data line (SDA) to communicate between devices. All devices on the bus share these two lines.

- Master-Slave Architecture: The I2C protocol uses a master-slave architecture, where one device acts as the master that initiates communication and controls the bus, while the other devices act as slaves that respond to the master's commands.

- Addressing Scheme: Each device on the I2C bus is assigned a unique 7-bit address (with some reserved addresses), which is used by the master to address a specific slave device for communication.

- Multi-Master Support: I2C supports multi-master configurations, which means that multiple masters can be connected to the same I2C bus, allowing for more complex system designs.

- Clock Speeds: The I2C protocol supports various clock speeds ranging from 100 kHz to 3.4 MHz, depending on the capabilities of the devices connected to the bus.

#### Communication Protocol

The I2C protocol uses a start-stop communication sequence between the master and slave devices. The following steps outline the basic communication protocol:

1. The master sends a start signal (a low-to-high transition on the SDA line while the SCL line is high) to initiate communication.

2. The master sends the slave address (7-bit address + read/write bit) of the target device it wants to communicate with.

3. The target device (slave) acknowledges the receipt of the address by pulling the SDA line low during the 9th clock cycle.

4. The master sends a command or data to the slave device, and the slave responds with an acknowledgement.

5. The master can continue to send additional commands or data to the slave, and the slave responds with an acknowledgement after each byte.

6. When the master is finished communicating with the slave, it sends a stop signal (a high-to-low transition on the SDA line while the SCL line is high) to indicate the end of communication.

#### Applications

The I2C protocol is widely used in various applications, including:

- Communication between microcontrollers and sensors/actuators in embedded systems.

- Control and configuration of system peripherals, such as power management and audio codecs.

- Communication between system boards in a computer or networking equipment.

- Reading and writing data to and from EEPROM and other memory devices.

#### Conclusion

The Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) protocol provides a simple and efficient way for multiple devices to communicate with each other over a two-wire interface. Its master-slave architecture, addressing scheme, and multi-master support make it an ideal choice for many embedded systems applications. - It is measured in bits per second (bps).

- The data bits can be 5, 6, 7, or 8 bits long.
- The stop bit is always a logic high (1), and indicates the end of a frame.

#### Flow Control

- Hardware flow control uses additional lines (such as CTS and RTS) to signal when the transmitter is ready to send data and when the receiver is ready to receive data.
- Software flow control uses special characters (such as XON and XOFF) to signal when the transmitter should pause or resume sending data.
