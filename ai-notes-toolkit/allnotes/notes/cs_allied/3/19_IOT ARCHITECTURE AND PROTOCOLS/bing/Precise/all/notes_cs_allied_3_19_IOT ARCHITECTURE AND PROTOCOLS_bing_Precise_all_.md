

# IOT ARCHITECTURE AND PROTOCOLS

IoT architecture refers to the many ways that IoT devices are structured to meet user needs. Based on complexity, IoT system elements are grouped into 3 to 7 layers, each with its own role. Notably, IoT architecture lacks standardized protocols, raising compatibility, security, and other challenges .

An IoT system has four types of transmission channels for data communication . Each IoT protocol in the IoT system architecture enables device-to-device, device-to-gateway, gateway-to-data center, or gateway-to-cloud communication, as well as communication between data centers .

There are several IoT protocols, including Message Queue Telemetry Transport (MQTT), Constrained Application Protocol (CoAP), and Advanced Message Queuing Protocol (AMQP) . These protocols ensure that information from one device or sensor gets read and understood by another device, a gateway, or a service .

In IoT architecture, the application layer serves as the interface between the user and the device within a given IoT protocol . The network layer transports traffic as needed by the applications, and the application layer need not know what type of physical network carries the data .

# M2M and IoT Technology Fundamentals

M2M (Machine to Machine) and IoT (Internet of Things) are two closely related technologies that are often used interchangeably. However, there are some key differences between the two.

1. **M2M** refers to the direct communication between devices, typically using a point-to-point connection. This communication can take place over a variety of wired or wireless networks, including cellular, Wi-Fi, and satellite.

2. **IoT**, on the other hand, refers to a broader concept of connecting devices and objects to the internet, allowing them to collect and share data. This data can then be used to improve efficiency, reduce costs, and enhance the user experience.

3. M2M is often seen as a subset of IoT, as it is one of the key technologies that enables IoT to function. However, IoT also includes other technologies, such as cloud computing, big data analytics, and artificial intelligence.

4. Both M2M and IoT rely on sensors and other devices to collect data. This data is then transmitted to a central location, where it can be analyzed and acted upon.

5. M2M and IoT have a wide range of applications, including smart homes, smart cities, healthcare, transportation, and manufacturing.

6. One of the key challenges in implementing M2M and IoT technologies is ensuring the security of the data being transmitted. This requires the use of strong encryption and other security measures.

7. Another challenge is ensuring the interoperability of devices and systems. This requires the use of common standards and protocols to ensure that devices from different manufacturers can communicate with each other.

8. M2M and IoT have the potential to revolutionize the way we live and work, by providing us with greater control over our environment and allowing us to make more informed decisions. However, there are also concerns about the potential impact on privacy and security, and it is important that these issues are addressed as the technology continues to evolve.

# Local and Wide Area Networking

Local Area Network (LAN) and Wide Area Network (WAN) are two types of computer networks that are used to connect devices and facilitate communication and data sharing.

## Local Area Network (LAN)

- A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited geographical area, such as a home, school, or office building.
- LANs are typically used to connect personal computers, printers, and other devices, and to share resources such as files and internet access.
- LANs can be connected using wired technologies such as Ethernet or wireless technologies such as Wi-Fi.
- LANs are typically owned and managed by a single organization or individual.

## Wide Area Network (WAN)

- A WAN is a network that connects devices over a large geographical area, such as between cities or countries.
- WANs are typically used to connect LANs and other types of networks, allowing devices in different locations to communicate and share resources.
- WANs can be connected using various technologies, including leased lines, satellite links, and public networks such as the internet.
- WANs are typically owned and managed by telecommunications companies or internet service providers.

In the context of IoT, LANs and WANs play a crucial role in connecting IoT devices and facilitating communication and data sharing between them. IoT devices can be connected to a LAN, allowing them to communicate with other devices on the same network, or to a WAN, allowing them to communicate with devices in different locations. This enables the collection, processing, and analysis of data from IoT devices, and the implementation of various IoT applications and services.

# IPv4

IPv4 (Internet Protocol version 4) is the fourth version of the Internet Protocol (IP). It is one of the core protocols of standards-based internetworking methods in the Internet and other packet-switched networks. IPv4 was the first version of the Internet Protocol to be widely deployed, and forms the basis for much of the current Internet infrastructure.

Some key points to note about IPv4 are:

- IPv4 is a connectionless protocol, meaning that it does not establish a connection before sending data.
- IPv4 uses 32-bit addresses, which limits the address space to 4,294,967,296 (2^32) addresses.
- IPv4 addresses are typically written in dotted-decimal notation, with four 8-bit numbers separated by periods, such as 192.168.1.1.
- IPv4 includes a checksum to verify the integrity of the header, but does not include any error correction or recovery mechanisms.
- IPv4 includes a Time to Live (TTL) field, which specifies the maximum number of hops a packet can take before being discarded.
- IPv4 supports fragmentation, which allows a large packet to be split into smaller packets for transmission over a network with a smaller maximum transmission unit (MTU).
- IPv4 includes options for security, source routing, and other special features, but these are not commonly used.

# 6LoWPAN

6LoWPAN stands for IPv6 over Low-Power Wireless Personal Area Networks. It is a networking technology or adaptation layer that allows IPv6 packets to be carried efficiently within small link layer frames, such as those defined by IEEE 802.15.4.

- 6LoWPAN is a mesh network that is robust, scalable, and can heal on its own. It delivers low-cost and secure communication in IoT devices.
- It uses IPv6 protocol and so it can be directly routed to cloud platforms. It offers one-to-many and many-to-one routing. In the network, leaf nodes can be in sleep mode for a longer duration of time.
- The 6LoWPAN network is connected to the IPv6 network using an edge router. The edge router handles three actions: 1) the data exchange between 6LoWPAN devices and the Internet (or other IPv6 network); 2) local data exchange between devices inside the 6LoWPAN; and 3) the generation and maintenance of the radio subnet (the 6LoWPAN network).
- The most basic requirements for a link layer to support 6LoWPAN are framing, unicast transmission and addressing. Addressing is required to differentiate between nodes on a link, and to form IPv6 addresses which are then elided by 6LoWPAN compression.




# 6TiSCH

This standard allows IPv6 addresses to pass through the TSCH mode, so that the IPv6 adaptation layer can be used for industrial automation and Low Power Lossy Networks (LLN).

The 6TiSCH architecture and protocol suite includes the 6TiSCH Operation Sublayer (6top), the 6top Protocol (6P), and how it uses 6LoWPAN, IP-in-IP encapsulation, and RPL. The suite also defined protocol extensions to transport routing information (RFC6553) and enable downward source routes (RFC6554). 6TiSCH provides a mechanism by which the link layer topology is matched with the routing topology, enabling the nodes to maintain synchronization with their best-connected parent.

The 6TiSCH network is intended to provide reliable and delay bounded communication in multi-hop and scalable Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT).

### Transport Layer

The Transport Layer is the fourth layer in the OSI model and is responsible for end-to-end communication between devices. It provides services such as connection-oriented data stream support, reliability, flow control, and multiplexing.

2. **Connection-oriented communication**: The Transport Layer can establish a connection between two devices before transmitting data. This ensures that the data is transmitted reliably and in the correct order.

3. **Flow control**: The Transport Layer can regulate the flow of data between devices to prevent the receiver from being overwhelmed by the sender.

4. **Error control**: The Transport Layer can detect and correct errors that may occur during transmission.

5. **Multiplexing**: The Transport Layer can multiplex multiple communication streams between devices, allowing multiple applications to communicate simultaneously.

Some common Transport Layer protocols include TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) and UDP (User Datagram Protocol). TCP is a connection-oriented protocol that provides reliable data transmission, while UDP is a connectionless protocol that provides faster but less reliable data transmission.

In summary, DCCP is a transport layer protocol that provides a way to send unreliable datagrams with congestion control. It is designed for applications that require fast delivery of data, but can tolerate some loss of data. DCCP is used in applications such as multimedia streaming, online gaming, and VoIP where some data loss is acceptable. It is not suitable for applications that require reliable delivery of data.



# SCTP - Stream Control Transmission Protocol

SCTP is a transport layer protocol that provides reliable, connection-oriented communication between two endpoints. It is used in the Transport & Session Layer Protocols unit of the IOT ARCHITECTURE AND PROTOCOLS subject.

Some key features of SCTP include:

1. **Multi-streaming**: SCTP allows multiple streams of data to be sent within a single connection, reducing head-of-line blocking.
2. **Multi-homing**: SCTP supports multiple IP addresses for each endpoint, providing fault tolerance and improved performance.
3. **Reliable transmission**: SCTP ensures reliable transmission of data through the use of acknowledgements and retransmissions.
4. **Congestion control**: SCTP implements congestion control mechanisms to avoid overwhelming the network.
5. **Selective acknowledgements**: SCTP uses selective acknowledgements to improve performance in the presence of packet loss.

SCTP is used in a variety of applications, including telephony signaling, web browsing, and file transfer. It is a versatile protocol that provides many benefits over traditional transport layer protocols such as TCP and UDP.



# Session Layer

The session layer is the fifth layer in the OSI model and is responsible for establishing, managing, and terminating connections between applications. This layer provides the mechanism for controlling the dialogue between the two end systems and managing data exchange. Some of the key functions of the session layer include:

1. **Session establishment, maintenance, and termination**: The session layer is responsible for setting up and maintaining the connection between two applications, as well as terminating the connection when it is no longer needed.

2. **Dialogue control**: The session layer allows two applications to engage in a conversation by managing the flow of data between them. This includes determining when one application can send data and when the other can receive it.

3. **Synchronization**: The session layer provides a mechanism for synchronizing the data exchange between two applications. This includes the ability to insert checkpoints into the data stream, allowing the applications to resume the data exchange from a specific point in case of a failure.

4. **Token management**: The session layer can also provide token management, which is a way of controlling access to a shared resource. This is done by passing a token between the applications, with only the application holding the token being allowed to access the resource.

The session layer is an important part of the OSI model, providing the necessary functions for managing the communication between two applications. It is responsible for ensuring that the data exchange is reliable and efficient, and that the connection is properly established, maintained, and terminated.



### HTTP

HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol) is an application-level protocol for transmitting hypermedia documents, such as HTML. It was designed for communication between web browsers and web servers, but it can also be used for other purposes. HTTP follows a classical client-server model, with a client opening a connection to make a request, then waiting until it receives a response.

Here are some key points to remember about HTTP:

1. HTTP is a request-response protocol, which means that a client sends a request to a server, and the server sends a response back to the client.
2. HTTP is stateless, which means that each request is treated independently and the server does not retain any information about previous requests.
3. HTTP uses a reliable transport protocol, typically TCP, to ensure that all data is transmitted correctly.
4. HTTP is extensible, which means that new methods, headers, and status codes can be added to the protocol as needed.
5. HTTP is used by web browsers to fetch web pages and other resources from web servers, but it can also be used by other types of clients and servers.

### AMQP

AMQP stands for Advanced Message Queuing Protocol. It is a session layer protocol that runs over the TCP layer. It is based on a publish/subscribe architecture, similar to the MQTT protocol architecture .

AMQP version 1.0 supports various broker architectures that may be used to receive, queue, route, and deliver messages or be used peer-to-peer. There are three major pieces specified in the scope of AMQP 1.0. These define the networking protocol, a representation for message envelope data, and the basic semantics of broker services .

Both MQTT and AMQP run over TCP connections, both are client-server in architecture and bi-directional. AMQP was designed to provide general-purpose high-performance enterprise messaging, whereas MQTT was created as an IoT protocol. AMQP has many features to cater to a range of messaging scenarios and is more complex than MQTT .

IoT Edge encrypts and streams real-time industrial data to IoT Hub by using AMQP 1.0 or MQTT 3.1.1 protocols. IoT Hub allows devices to use the following protocols for device-side communications: MQTT, MQTT over WebSockets, Advanced Message Queuing Protocol (AMQP), AMQP over WebSockets, and HTTPS  .



### MQTT

MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport) is a lightweight messaging protocol that is used for machine-to-machine (M2M) communication. It is designed for connections with remote locations where a small code footprint is required and/or network bandwidth is limited. MQTT is one of the most commonly used protocols in IoT and IIoT infrastructure.

#### MQTT Architecture

The MQTT architecture is made up of the following key parts: MQTT broker and MQTT client. MQTT uses a publisher-subscriber pattern and is ideal for small devices that require efficient bandwidth and battery use.

#### MQTT Protocol

MQTT’s support for persistent sessions reduces the time to reconnect the client with the broker. MQTT makes it easy to encrypt messages using TLS and authenticate clients using modern authentication protocols, such as OAuth.

#### MQTT Use Cases

Wireless IoT technologies such as Zigbee and LoRaWAN use MQTT for communication between clients and router. Protocols such as AMPQ, CoAP, and JMS also use a broker-based architecture.

In any IoT protocol, the transport layer enables and safeguards the communication of the data as it travels between layers. Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) is used in MQTT.

### MAC 802.15.4

MAC 802.15.4 is a standard that defines the operation of low-rate wireless personal area networks (LR-WPANs). It is a protocol that is used for communication in the service layer of the IoT architecture. Here are some key points about MAC 802.15.4:

- MAC 802.15.4 is designed to operate in the unlicensed frequency bands, including 2.4 GHz, 915 MHz, and 868 MHz.
- The standard specifies a maximum data rate of 250 kbps in the 2.4 GHz band.
- MAC 802.15.4 supports both star and peer-to-peer network topologies.
- The standard defines two types of devices: full-function devices (FFDs) and reduced-function devices (RFDs). FFDs can operate as coordinators or routers, while RFDs are typically used as end devices.

- The standard is widely used in applications such as home automation, industrial control, and medical monitoring.




# 6LoWPAN

6LoWPAN is a protocol definition that enables IPv6 packets to be carried on top of low power wireless networks, specifically IEEE 802.15.4. The concept was born from the idea that the Internet Protocol could and should be applied to even the smallest of devices.

The 6LoWPAN protocol stack includes different networking topologies such as star and mesh, low bandwidth (250/40/20 kbps), low power consumption, typically battery-operated, relatively low cost, scalable networks, reliability, mobility, and long sleep times.

In the network layer, IPv6 is used. This makes it fit to support several devices, hence preferred in building IoT based systems. The channels have varying data rates and are allocated into different frequency bands with a total of twenty-seven channels being defined in the layer.

6LoWPAN only specifies the operation of IPv6 over the IEEE 802.15.4 standard, edge routers may also support IPv6 transition mechanisms to connect 6LoWPAN networks to IPv4 networks, such as NAT64 defined in RFC 6146. These IPv6 transition mechanisms do not require the 6LoWPAN nodes to implement IPv4 in whole or in part.

The 6LoWPAN architecture is made up of low-power wireless area networks (LoWPANs), which are IPv6 stub networks. Three different kinds of LoWPANs have been defined: Simple LoWPANs, Extended LoWPANs, and Ad hoc LoWPANs.
