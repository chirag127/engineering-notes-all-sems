1. **Definition**: Grid computing is a distributed computing model that allows multiple computers to work together to solve complex problems.
2. **Purpose**: The main purpose of grid computing is to provide a way to share computing resources and data across multiple locations, organizations, and administrative domains.
3. **Architecture**: Grid computing systems are typically composed of multiple layers, including a fabric layer, a connectivity layer, a resource layer, a collective layer, and an application layer.
4. **Applications**: Grid computing has been used in a variety of applications, including scientific research, financial modeling, and data analysis.
5. **Challenges**: Some of the challenges associated with grid computing include security, data management, and resource allocation.




### History of Grid Computing

- The idea of grid computing originated in the 1990s as a metaphor for making computer power as easy to access as an electric power grid .
- Where parallel computing and supercomputers were primarily used in the '80s and '90s, grid computing began to take shape as an option by the mid-1990s .
- Early in the 1990s, Steve Tuecke, Ian Foster, and Carl Kesselman developed the concept of grid computing.
- They created the Globus Toolkit standard, which had grids for controlling data processing, data storage, and heavy computation.
- The power grid metaphor for accessible computing quickly became canonical when Ian Foster and Carl Kesselman published their seminal work, "The Grid: Blueprint for a new computing infrastructure" (1999).
- With the widespread availability of high-speed networks, it became feasible to outsource computing to remote providers and to federate resources from many locations.
- Such observations motivated the development, from the mid-1990s onwards, of a range of innovative Grid technologies, applications, and infrastructures.

### Peer-to-Peer Computing

- Peer-to-peer (P2P) computing is a distributed architecture in which individual nodes in the network act as both suppliers and consumers of resources.
- In a P2P network, tasks are divided among multiple nodes, allowing for more efficient use of resources and improved performance.
- P2P computing is often used for sharing files, such as music, videos, and documents, over the internet.
- P2P networks can be organized in various topologies, including unstructured, structured, and hybrid.
- Unstructured P2P networks do not have a fixed topology and rely on flooding or random walks to locate resources.
- Structured P2P networks use a distributed hash table (DHT) to organize nodes and locate resources.
- Hybrid P2P networks combine elements of both unstructured and structured networks.
- P2P computing has several advantages, including scalability, fault tolerance, and reduced costs.
- However, P2P networks can also present challenges, such as security and privacy concerns, and the potential for free riding.
- P2P computing is an important component of grid computing technology, which aims to harness the power of distributed resources to solve large-scale computational problems.

### Grid Computing Model and Protocols

Grid computing is a distributed computing model that allows multiple computers to work together to solve complex problems. This model is based on the idea of sharing resources, such as processing power, storage, and data, among multiple computers in a network.

The main protocols used in grid computing are:

1. **Open Grid Services Architecture (OGSA):** This is a set of standards that define how grid services should be implemented and how they should interact with each other. OGSA is based on web services standards and is designed to be platform-independent.

2. **Open Grid Services Infrastructure (OGSI):** This is a specification that defines the basic mechanisms for creating, managing, and exchanging information between grid services. OGSI is based on OGSA and provides a foundation for building grid applications.

3. **Grid Resource Allocation and Management (GRAM):** This is a protocol for managing the allocation of resources in a grid environment. GRAM allows users to request resources, such as processing power or storage, from the grid and to monitor the status of their requests.

4. **Grid Security Infrastructure (GSI):** This is a set of security protocols that provide authentication, authorization, and encryption services for grid computing. GSI is based on public key infrastructure (PKI) and provides a secure environment for grid applications.

These protocols provide the foundation for building grid computing systems and enable the sharing of resources among multiple computers in a network. They allow for the efficient and secure management of resources in a grid environment and enable the development of complex, distributed applications.

### Cluster Middleware and SSI

- Cluster Middleware is a software layer that provides a single system image (SSI) to the user, making the cluster appear as a single parallel machine .
- SSI is the illusion, created by software or hardware, that presents a collection of computing resources as one, more whole resource .
- In other words, it is the property of a system that hides the heterogeneous and distributed nature of the available resources and presents them to users and applications as a single unified computing resource .
- Cluster designers desire a cluster operating system or some middleware to support SSI at various levels, including the sharing of CPUs, memory, and I/O across all cluster nodes .
- An example of SSI Cluster Middleware is OpenMosix, which, after implementation, gives the impression that the entire Cluster system, consisting of tens or even hundreds of nodes, appears as a single computer system with very large resources .




### Resource Management and Scheduling

Resource management and scheduling are important aspects of cluster computing in high performance computing. Here are some key points to consider:

1. **Resource management** refers to the process of allocating and managing the resources of a cluster, such as CPU, memory, storage, and network bandwidth, to ensure that they are used efficiently and effectively.

2. **Scheduling** refers to the process of assigning tasks to resources in a way that optimizes the performance of the cluster. This can involve balancing the workload across the resources, prioritizing certain tasks, and managing dependencies between tasks.

3. Effective resource management and scheduling can help to improve the performance of a cluster by ensuring that resources are used efficiently and that tasks are completed in a timely manner.

4. There are several different approaches to resource management and scheduling, including static and dynamic scheduling, and centralized and decentralized approaches.

5. Static scheduling involves assigning tasks to resources in advance, based on a predetermined schedule. This approach can be effective for workloads that are predictable and have well-defined resource requirements.

6. Dynamic scheduling, on the other hand, involves making scheduling decisions on-the-fly, based on the current state of the cluster and the workload. This approach can be more flexible and can better handle unpredictable workloads.

7. Centralized approaches to resource management and scheduling involve a single entity, such as a scheduler or resource manager, making decisions for the entire cluster. Decentralized approaches, on the other hand, involve multiple entities making decisions independently.

8. There are several different algorithms and heuristics that can be used for resource management and scheduling, including first-come, first-served, shortest job first, and priority-based scheduling.

9. Effective resource management and scheduling can help to improve the performance, reliability, and scalability of a cluster, and is an important area of research in high performance computing.

### Types of Cloud

Clouds are classified into types based on their shape, altitude, and thickness. The classification of clouds into types was first proposed by Luke Howard in 1802 and we largely use the same system today. This splits clouds into three main types - stratus, cumulus, and cirrus, plus a fourth special type, nimbus .

1. **Stratus clouds** are low-level clouds that are usually uniform and flat, producing a gray layer of cloud cover which may be precipitation-free or may cause periods of light precipitation or drizzle.

2. **Cumulus clouds** are detached, generally dense clouds and have sharp outlines. They are usually white, and their tops are dome-shaped with protuberances while their bases are horizontal.

3. **Cirrus clouds** are high clouds that are thin, wispy, and composed of ice crystals. They are often white and predict fair weather.

4. **Nimbus clouds** are clouds that produce precipitation. They are often dark and featureless, and can be seen in various forms, including as a featureless layer or as individual, towering clouds.

Clouds can also be grouped by the altitude level or levels in the troposphere at which they are normally found. The list of cloud types groups all cloud genera as high (cirro-, cirrus), middle (alto-), multi-level (nimbo-, cumulo-, cumulus), and low (strato-, stratus).
