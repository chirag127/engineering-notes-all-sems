### Postulates of Quantum Mechanics

1. **The State of a System:** The state of a quantum mechanical system is completely specified by a function that depends on the coordinates of the particle(s) and on time. This function is called the wave function or state function and has the important property that it is the probability that the particle lies in the volume element located at a certain time.

2. **Physical Observables:** With every physical observable, there is associated an operator, which when operating upon the wavefunction associated with a definite value of that observable. To every observable in classical mechanics, there corresponds a linear, Hermitian operator in quantum mechanics.

3. **Composite Systems:** The tensor product postulate of quantum mechanics states that the Hilbert space of a composite system is the tensor product of the components’ Hilbert spaces. All current formalizations of quantum mechanics that do not contain this postulate contain some equivalent postulate or assumption (sometimes hidden).

4. **Mathematical Formulation:** The entire structure of quantum mechanics (including its relativistic extension) can be formulated in terms of states and operations in Hilbert space. We need rules that map the physical quantities such as states, observables, and measurements to the mathematical structure of vector spaces, vectors, and operators. The mathematical formulations of quantum mechanics are those mathematical formalisms that permit a rigorous description of quantum mechanics. This mathematical formalism uses mainly a part of functional analysis, especially Hilbert spaces, which are a kind of linear space.

### Quantum Fourier Transform

The quantum Fourier transform (QFT) is the quantum implementation of the discrete Fourier transform over the amplitudes of a wavefunction. It is part of many quantum algorithms, most notably Shor's factoring algorithm and quantum phase estimation.

The QFT acts on a quantum state vector (a quantum register), and the classical Fourier transform acts on a vector. Both types of vectors can be written as lists of complex numbers. In the quantum case, it is a sequence of probability amplitudes for all the possible outcomes upon measurement (called basis states, or eigenstates).

The QFT is a part of many quantum algorithms, notably Shor's algorithm for factoring and computing the discrete logarithm, the quantum phase estimation algorithm for estimating the eigenvalues of a unitary operator, and algorithms for the hidden subgroup problem. The quantum Fourier transform was discovered by Don Coppersmith.



### Phase Estimation

Phase estimation is an important algorithm in quantum computation. It is used to estimate the eigenvalue of a unitary operator, given one of its eigenvectors. Here are some key points to remember about phase estimation:

1. The algorithm uses two quantum registers: one to store the eigenvector and the other to store the estimated phase.
2. The first step of the algorithm is to apply a Hadamard gate to each qubit in the second register, creating an equal superposition of all possible states.
3. The second step is to apply a controlled unitary operation, where the control qubits are the qubits in the second register and the target qubit is the qubit in the first register.
4. The final step is to apply the inverse quantum Fourier transform to the second register, followed by a measurement of the qubits in the second register.
5. The result of the measurement is an estimate of the phase, which can be used to calculate the eigenvalue of the unitary operator.
6. The accuracy of the estimate increases with the number of qubits in the second register.

This is a brief overview of phase estimation in quantum computation. It is an important algorithm to understand and can be used in many applications, including quantum simulation and quantum algorithms for solving linear systems of equations.

### Quantum Search Algorithms

Quantum search algorithms are a type of quantum algorithm that can be used to search for a specific item within an unstructured database. These algorithms are quadratically faster than their classical counterparts, requiring only O(√N) operations, where N is the number of elements in the database .

One of the most well-known quantum search algorithms is Grover's algorithm, which was created by Lov Grover. This algorithm can find, with high probability, the unique input to a black box function that produces a particular output value, using just O(√N) evaluations of the function, where N is the size of the function's domain .

The centerpiece of the quantum search algorithm is a binary function, f(x), with the property that f(x) = 1 if and only if x is the label of the object we are searching for, f(τ) = 1 .

There are also hybrid quantum-classical search algorithms, which combine the strengths of both quantum and classical algorithms to achieve even better performance .

Quantum walks are another powerful tool for building quantum search algorithms or quantum sampling algorithms named the construction of quantum stationary state. However, the success probability of those algorithms are all far away from 1. Amplitude amplification is usually used to amplify success probability .



### Quantum Counting

Quantum counting is a quantum algorithm for efficiently counting the number of solutions for a given search problem. The algorithm is based on the quantum phase estimation algorithm and on Grover's search algorithm . Counting problems are common in diverse fields such as statistical estimation, statistical physics, networking, etc .

Some extensions of Grover's quantum searching algorithm have been studied, including the generalization of the Grover iteration in the light of a concept called amplitude amplification .



### Speeding up the solution of NP – complete problems

NP-complete problems represent the hardest problems in the NP set. A decision problem L is NP-complete if:
1. L is in NP (Any given solution for NP-complete problems can be verified quickly, but there is no efficient known solution).
2. Every problem in NP is reducible to L in polynomial time.

Although a solution to an NP-complete problem can be verified "quickly", there is no known way to find a solution quickly. However, there are some methods that can speed up the solution of NP-complete problems. One such method is the use of quantum computers. Grovers algorithm can give a square-root speedup, but the square root of an exponential function is also an exponential function. Another method is the use of recursive decomposition, which can obtain the full T(n) to tilde {O}(sqrt{T(n)}) speed-up for most of the many non-trivial exponential time algorithms for NP-hard problems.

In conclusion, while there is no known way to find a solution to NP-complete problems quickly, there are methods that can speed up the solution of these problems. These methods include the use of quantum computers and recursive decomposition.

### Conditions for Quantum Computation

Quantum computation is a rapidly developing field that aims to harness the power of quantum mechanics to perform calculations that are beyond the reach of classical computers. To achieve this, there are several conditions that must be met. These conditions are often referred to as the DiVincenzo criteria, named after the theoretical physicist David P. DiVincenzo, who proposed them in 2000.

1. **Long Coherence Time**: Long qubit coherence times are a prerequisite for quantum computing.
2. **High Scalability**: The ability to scale up the number of qubits in a quantum computer is essential for its practical use.
3. **High Fault Tolerance and Quantum Error Correction**: Quantum computers are highly susceptible to errors, so high fault tolerance and quantum error correction are necessary to ensure the accuracy of the calculations.
4. **Ability to Initialize Qubits**: The ability to initialize qubits in a known state is essential for quantum computation.
5. **Universal Quantum Gates**: A set of universal quantum gates is necessary to perform any quantum computation.
6. **Efficient Qubit-state Measurement Capability**: The ability to efficiently measure the state of qubits is essential for quantum computation.
7. **Faithful Transmission of Flying Qubits and Interconversion between Stationary and “Flying” Qubits**: The ability to transmit qubits between different parts of a quantum computer and to convert between stationary and flying qubits is necessary for quantum computation.

These are the seven key requirements for designing effective quantum computing systems.

### Quantum noise and Quantum Operations

Quantum noise is a term used to describe the effects of quantum decoherence on quantum computations. Quantum decoherence, or noise, is caused by imperfect control signals, interference from the environment, and unwanted interactions between qubits, which are the building blocks of a quantum computer.

Performing computations on a quantum computer involves a “quantum circuit,” which is a series of operations called quantum gates. These gates manipulate the state of the qubits to perform the desired computation.

Quantum systems with sufficiently uncorrelated and weak noise could be used to solve computational problems that are intractable with current digital computers.

Quantum noise is due to the apparently discrete nature of the small quantum constituents such as electrons, as well as the discrete nature of quantum effects, such as photocurrents. Quantified noise is similar to classical noise theory and will not always return an asymmetric spectral density.

### Quantum Operations

Quantum operations are mathematical operations that act on the state of a quantum system. They are used to describe the evolution of a quantum system over time, and to manipulate the state of the system. Here are some key points to remember about quantum operations:

1. Quantum operations are represented by unitary matrices. A unitary matrix is a matrix that satisfies the condition UU† = I, where U† is the conjugate transpose of U and I is the identity matrix.

2. Quantum operations preserve the norm of the state vector. This means that the sum of the squares of the absolute values of the coefficients of the state vector remains constant under the action of a quantum operation.

3. Quantum operations are reversible. This means that for every quantum operation, there exists an inverse operation that can undo its effect.

### Constructing Quantum Codes

Quantum error correction is an essential component of quantum computing, as it allows for the detection and correction of errors that may occur during quantum computation. In this section, we will discuss the construction of quantum codes for use in quantum error correction.

1. **Stabilizer Codes:** Stabilizer codes are a class of quantum error-correcting codes that are constructed using the stabilizer formalism. This formalism allows for the description of a quantum code using a set of commuting operators known as stabilizers. These stabilizers can be used to detect and correct errors that may occur during quantum computation.

2. **CSS Codes:** CSS codes, or Calderbank-Shor-Steane codes, are a type of stabilizer code that is constructed using classical error-correcting codes. These codes are named after their inventors, A. R. Calderbank, Peter Shor, and Andrew Steane. CSS codes are constructed by combining two classical error-correcting codes, one for detecting bit-flip errors and one for detecting phase-flip errors.

3. **Topological Codes:** Topological codes are a class of quantum error-correcting codes that are constructed using topological quantum field theory. These codes are designed to be robust against local errors, as the topology of the code space provides protection against such errors. Topological codes include the surface code and the color code.

4. **Concatenated Codes:** Concatenated codes are a type of quantum error-correcting code that is constructed by concatenating multiple smaller codes. This allows for the construction of codes with larger block sizes and higher error-correcting capabilities. Concatenated codes can be constructed using any of the above-mentioned code types.

These are some of the methods for constructing quantum codes for use in quantum error correction. Each method has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of code will depend on the specific requirements of the quantum computation being performed.

### Strong Sub Additivity

- Strong subadditivity is a property of the von Neumann entropy of quantum states.
- It states that for any tripartite quantum state, the sum of the entropies of any two of the subsystems is greater than or equal to the sum of the entropy of the third subsystem and the entropy of the entire system.
- Mathematically, for any tripartite state ρABC, the strong subadditivity property is expressed as: S(ρAB) + S(ρBC) ≥ S(ρB) + S(ρABC)
- This property is important in the study of quantum error correction as it provides a bound on the amount of information that can be transmitted through a noisy quantum channel.
- It also plays a role in the study of entanglement and quantum correlations.
- Strong subadditivity is a generalization of the subadditivity property of classical entropy, which states that the entropy of a joint system is less than or equal to the sum of the entropies of the individual subsystems.
- The proof of strong subadditivity relies on the properties of the relative entropy and the monotonicity of the quantum relative entropy under completely positive trace-preserving maps.




### Data Compression

Data compression is the process of encoding information using fewer bits than the original representation. It is used to reduce the amount of storage space required for data and to improve the efficiency of data transmission.

There are two main types of data compression: lossless and lossy. Lossless compression reduces the size of the data without losing any information, while lossy compression reduces the size of the data by discarding some information.

Some common lossless compression algorithms include Huffman coding, Lempel-Ziv-Welch (LZW) and Run-length encoding (RLE). Lossy compression algorithms include JPEG for images and MP3 for audio.

In the context of quantum computing, data compression can be used to reduce the number of qubits required to represent a quantum state. This can be achieved by using techniques such as quantum state tomography and quantum principal component analysis.
