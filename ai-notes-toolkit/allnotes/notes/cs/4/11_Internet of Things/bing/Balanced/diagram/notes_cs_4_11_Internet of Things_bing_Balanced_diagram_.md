### Architectural view for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- The architectural view of IoT is the way of describing the structure and behavior of an IoT system, which consists of many interconnected devices and applications that communicate and exchange data over the internet.
- There is no standard or universal architecture for IoT, as different IoT systems may have different requirements, functionalities, and implementations in various domains and sectors.
- However, some common architectural models have been proposed and used in the literature and practice, which can be classified into three main categories: three-layer, five-layer, and semantic models.

#### Three-layer architecture
- The three-layer architecture is the simplest and most basic model of IoT, which defines the main idea and components of an IoT system.
- The three layers are:

  - Perception layer: This is the lowest layer, which consists of physical devices, such as sensors, actuators, RFID tags, cameras, etc., that can sense, collect, and transmit data from the environment.
  - Network layer: This is the middle layer, which provides the connectivity and communication between the perception layer and the application layer. It uses various network technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, ZigBee, etc., to transfer the data over the internet.
  - Application layer: This is the highest layer, which provides the services and applications for the end-users, such as smart home, smart city, smart health, smart agriculture, etc. It processes, analyzes, and displays the data received from the network layer, and sends commands and feedback to the perception layer.

#### Five-layer architecture
- The five-layer architecture is an extension and refinement of the three-layer architecture, which adds two more layers to address some of the limitations and challenges of the three-layer architecture, such as data management, security, and scalability .
- The five layers are:

  - Perception layer: This is the same as the three-layer architecture, which consists of physical devices that can sense, collect, and transmit data from the environment.
  - Transport layer: This is the second layer, which is responsible for transferring the data from the perception layer to the processing layer, and vice versa. It uses various network protocols, such as TCP/IP, UDP, MQTT, CoAP, etc., to ensure reliable and efficient data transmission over the internet.
  - Processing layer: This is the third layer, which is also known as the middleware layer, which provides the data processing, storage, and management functions for the IoT system. It uses various technologies, such as cloud computing, fog computing, edge computing, etc., to perform data filtering, aggregation, compression, encryption, etc., and to store and manage the data in databases or data centers.
  - Application layer: This is the fourth layer, which is similar to the three-layer architecture, which provides the services and applications for the end-users, such as smart home, smart city, smart health, smart agriculture, etc. It uses various techniques, such as machine learning, artificial intelligence, big data analytics, etc., to process, analyze, and display the data received from the processing layer, and to send commands and feedback to the perception layer.
  - Business layer: This is the fifth and highest layer, which provides the business and management functions for the IoT system. It uses various methods, such as business models, policies, strategies, etc., to manage the resources, users, and profits of the IoT system, and to ensure the security, privacy, and quality of service of the IoT system.

### Technology behind IoT

- IoT stands for Internet of Things, which is the concept of connecting any device (so long as it has an on/off switch) to the Internet and to other connected devices.
- IoT devices can collect and share data about the way they are used and about the environment around them, enabling various applications and services.
- IoT works through a combination of wireless networking technology, physical devices, advanced data analytics and cloud computing.
- The basic process of how IoT works is as follows:
  - A group of physical devices is wired or wirelessly linked to each other and/or a central area.
  - The devices have sensors, actuators, or other components that can collect, process, and transmit data.
  - The data is sent to a cloud platform or a local server, where it is stored, analyzed, and processed.
  - The cloud platform or the local server can send commands or feedback to the devices, or to other applications and users.
  - The devices can respond to the commands or feedback, or perform actions based on the data analysis.
- Some of the technologies used in IoT systems may include:
  - Edge computing: It increases the computing power at the edges of an IoT network, reducing communication latency and improving response time. It allows smart devices to do more than just send or receive data to their IoT platform.
  - Cloud computing: It provides scalable, on-demand, and cost-effective computing resources and services for IoT applications. It enables data storage, analysis, processing, and management in the cloud.
  - Wireless networking: It enables data transmission and communication between IoT devices and the cloud or the local server. It can use various protocols and standards, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRa, 5G, etc.
  - Sensors and actuators: They are the components that can measure, monitor, or control physical phenomena, such as temperature, humidity, motion, light, sound, etc. They can convert physical signals into electrical signals, or vice versa.
  - Microcontrollers and microprocessors: They are the chips that can execute instructions and perform calculations on the data collected or received by the IoT devices. They can vary in size, power, and functionality, depending on the device requirements.
  - Security chips: They are the chips that can provide encryption, authentication, and protection for the data and the devices in the IoT network. They can prevent unauthorized access, tampering, or hacking of the IoT system.
  - FPGA: It stands for Field Programmable Gate Array, which is a chip that can be configured or programmed to perform specific functions or tasks. It can offer flexibility, performance, and efficiency for IoT applications.

### IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization

- IoT/M2M systems are composed of devices, networks, and applications that communicate and exchange data with each other.
- To enable interoperability and scalability of IoT/M2M systems, standardization is needed at different layers of the system architecture.
- One of the leading standardization initiatives for IoT/M2M systems is oneM2M, which was launched in 2012 by ETSI and 13 other founding members.
- The oneM2M architecture divides IoT functions into three major domains: the application layer, the service layer, and the network layer  .
- The application layer is responsible for providing the functionality and logic of the IoT/M2M applications, such as smart home, smart city, health care, etc. The application layer interacts with the service layer through IT-friendly APIs .
- The service layer is responsible for providing common services and capabilities for the IoT/M2M applications, such as device management, data management, security, discovery, etc. The service layer interacts with the network layer through network protocols .
- The network layer is responsible for providing the connectivity and transport of data between the devices and the service layer. The network layer supports a wide range of IoT technologies, such as cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, etc .
- The oneM2M architecture also defines a common service entity (CSE) as the main component of the service layer, which can be deployed in different locations, such as devices, gateways, servers, or cloud platforms .
- The oneM2M architecture aims to promote efficient, interoperable, and secure IoT/M2M systems, and to facilitate the development and deployment of IoT/M2M applications across different domains and industries  .
- Other standardization initiatives for IoT/M2M systems include Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC), which focuses on sensors and geographical information, and 3GPP, which focuses on radio layer technologies, such as LTE-M, NB-IoT, and EC-GSM-IoT.



### Communication Technologies for IoT

Communication technologies are the methods and protocols that enable the exchange of data between IoT devices and the internet. They vary in terms of range, power consumption, bandwidth, security, and cost. Some of the common communication technologies for IoT are:

- **Bluetooth**: A short-range wireless technology that uses radio waves to connect devices within a distance of up to 10 meters. Bluetooth is widely used for personal area networks (PANs) and wearable devices. It has low power consumption and high data rates. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) is a variant of Bluetooth that is optimized for IoT applications that require low energy and long battery life. 
- **Zigbee**: A low-power wireless technology that uses mesh networking to connect devices within a range of up to 100 meters. Zigbee is mainly used for industrial and home automation applications that require low data rates and high reliability. Zigbee supports multiple network topologies, such as star, tree, and mesh, and can handle up to 65,000 nodes per network. 
- **Z-Wave**: A low-power wireless technology that uses radio frequency (RF) signals to connect devices within a range of up to 30 meters. Z-Wave is designed for home automation and smart home applications that require simple and secure communication. Z-Wave supports up to 232 nodes per network and has a high level of interoperability among different manufacturers. 
- **Wi-Fi**: A wireless technology that uses radio waves to connect devices to the internet within a range of up to 100 meters. Wi-Fi is widely used for local area networks (LANs) and wireless hotspots. It has high data rates and supports various standards, such as 802.11a/b/g/n/ac/ax. Wi-Fi Direct is a variant of Wi-Fi that allows devices to communicate directly without a router or access point. 
- **Cellular**: A wireless technology that uses cellular networks to connect devices to the internet over long distances. Cellular networks are composed of base stations, towers, and antennas that provide coverage and service to mobile devices. Cellular technologies include 2G, 3G, 4G, and 5G, which differ in terms of speed, latency, bandwidth, and spectrum. Cellular is suitable for IoT applications that require wide area network (WAN) connectivity and high mobility. 
- **NFC**: A short-range wireless technology that uses magnetic fields to connect devices within a distance of up to 10 centimeters. NFC is mainly used for contactless payment, identification, and data transfer. NFC devices can operate in three modes: reader/writer, peer-to-peer, and card emulation. NFC has low power consumption and high security. 
- **LoRaWAN**: A low-power wide area network (LPWAN) technology that uses long-range radio waves to connect devices to the internet over distances of up to 15 kilometers. LoRaWAN is designed for IoT applications that require low data rates, long battery life, and large-scale deployment. LoRaWAN supports various classes of devices, such as Class A, B, and C, and has a high level of scalability and resilience. 

These are some of the communication technologies for IoT that enable data transmission and reception among IoT devices and the internet. Depending on the requirements and constraints of the IoT application, different communication technologies can be chosen and combined to achieve the desired functionality and performance.

## Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT

- Hardware for IoT refers to the physical devices and components that enable the connectivity, communication, sensing, and processing of data in the Internet of Things (IoT) systems.
- Hardware for IoT can be classified into four main categories: sensors, actuators, controllers, and gateways.
- Sensors are devices that measure physical quantities such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, motion, etc. and convert them into electrical signals that can be transmitted and processed by other devices.
- Actuators are devices that perform physical actions such as turning on/off a light, opening/closing a valve, moving a robot arm, etc. based on the electrical signals received from other devices.
- Controllers are devices that execute the logic and algorithms that govern the behavior and functionality of the IoT system. They can be embedded in the sensors or actuators, or they can be separate devices such as microcontrollers, microprocessors, or field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs).
- Gateways are devices that act as intermediaries between the sensors, actuators, controllers, and the cloud or the internet. They perform functions such as data aggregation, filtering, encryption, protocol conversion, and communication with other networks or devices.



### Sensors for IoT

- **Temperature sensors**: These sensors measure the amount of heat generated from an area or an object. - **Pressure sensors**: These sensors detect changes in a gas or liquid. - **Water quality sensors**: These sensors measure the physical and chemical properties of water, such as pH, conductivity, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, etc. Water quality sensors are used for IoT applications, such as water treatment, agriculture, aquaculture, environmental monitoring, etc. Water quality sensors can be based on different principles, such as electrochemical, optical, acoustic, etc.
- **Chemical and gas sensors**: These sensors measure the concentration of specific chemicals or gases in the air or liquid. - **Infrared sensors**: These sensors detect the infrared radiation emitted or reflected by objects. Infrared sensors are used for IoT applications, such as remote controls, night vision, thermal imaging, fire detection, etc. Infrared sensors can be based on different principles, such as thermopiles, pyroelectric, bolometers, etc.
- **Smoke sensors**: These sensors detect the presence of smoke particles in the air. Smoke sensors are used for IoT applications, such as fire alarms, air quality monitoring, etc. Smoke sensors can be based on different principles, such as optical, ionization, etc.
- **Motion sensors**: These sensors detect the movement of objects or people. Motion sensors are used for IoT applications, such as security systems, smart lighting, gesture recognition, etc. Motion sensors can be based on different principles, such as passive infrared (PIR), ultrasonic, microwave, etc.

### Actuators for IoT

- An actuator is a device that converts energy into motion. It does this by taking an electrical signal and combining it with an energy source.
- In an IoT system, the actuator can act on data collected by sensors to create an outcome as determined by the chosen settings of the user.
- Actuators enable a physical action based on data that originates with one or more sensors.
- The conversion of sensor data to activity follows this sequence:
  - Sensors detect an event in the physical environment.
  - The sensor data is transmitted to a gateway or cloud platform via a communication network.
  - The gateway or cloud platform processes the sensor data and applies logic or rules to determine the appropriate action.
  - The gateway or cloud platform sends a command to the actuator via the same or a different communication network.
  - The actuator receives the command and executes the action by converting energy into motion.
- There are different types of actuators for IoT, depending on the energy source, the motion type, and the application domain.
- Some common types of actuators for IoT are :
  - Servo Motors: A servomotor is a rotary actuator or linear actuator that allows for precise control of angular or linear position, velocity and acceleration. Servo motors are widely used in robotics, industrial automation, and aerospace applications.
  - Stepper Motors: Stepper motors are DC motors that move in discrete steps. Each step corresponds to a specific angle of rotation. Stepper motors are used for precise positioning and speed control, such as in 3D printers, CNC machines, and cameras.
  - DC Motors (Continuous Rotation Motors): DC motors are two wire (power & ground), continuous rotation motors. They can vary their speed and direction by changing the polarity and voltage of the power supply. DC motors are used for applications that require high torque and speed, such as in electric vehicles, fans, and pumps.
  - Linear Actuators: Linear actuators are devices that produce linear motion by converting electrical, hydraulic, or pneumatic energy. They can be used to push, pull, lift, or lower objects. Linear actuators are used for applications that require linear movement, such as in medical devices, industrial machinery, and home automation.
  - Thermal/Magnetic Actuators: Thermal/magnetic actuators are actuated by thermal or mechanical energy. Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs) or Magnetic Shape Memory Alloys (MSMAs) are materials that can change their shape when heated or exposed to a magnetic field. Thermal/magnetic actuators are used for applications that require low power consumption, high force, and fast response, such as in valves, switches, and locks.
  - Mechanical Actuators: Mechanical actuators execute movement by converting rotary motion into linear motion. They use gears, screws, or levers to achieve the desired motion. Mechanical actuators are used for applications that require high load capacity, long stroke, and low cost, such as in car jacks, clamps, and presses.
  - Soft Actuators: Soft actuators are made of flexible or elastic materials that can deform or bend when stimulated by electrical, chemical, or pneumatic signals. They can mimic the movements of natural muscles or organs. Soft actuators are used for applications that require softness, compliance, and adaptability, such as in wearable devices, soft robots, and biomedical implants.



### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- RFID technology uses radio waves to identify and track objects or people that have a wireless device or tag attached to them  .
- An RFID system consists of three main components: a tag, a reader and a database  .
  - A tag is a small device that contains an integrated circuit (IC) and an antenna. The tag can store information such as a unique identifier, product name, location, etc. The tag can be passive (no battery) or active (battery-powered) depending on the application .
  - A reader is a device that has one or more antennas that emit radio waves and receive signals from the tags. The reader can be fixed or mobile, and can communicate with multiple tags at once. The reader can also process and transmit the data from the tags to a database  .
  - A database is a system that stores and manages the data collected from the tags and the readers. The database can be accessed by authorized users or applications for various purposes such as inventory management, security, authentication, etc  .
- RFID technology has many advantages over other identification and tracking methods such as barcodes, optical scanners, etc. Some of the advantages are  :
  - RFID tags can be read from a distance without direct line of sight or physical contact, which reduces labor and human error.
  - RFID tags can store more information than barcodes, and can be updated or rewritten as needed.
  - RFID tags can withstand harsh environments such as high temperature, moisture, dust, etc.
  - RFID tags can be embedded or attached to various objects or materials such as metal, plastic, paper, etc.
  - RFID readers can scan multiple tags simultaneously and in real time, which increases efficiency and accuracy.
- RFID technology has many applications in various domains such as supply chain management, retail, healthcare, transportation, security, etc. Some of the examples are    :
  - RFID tags can be used to track the movement and status of goods along the supply chain, from the manufacturer to the distributor to the retailer to the customer.
  - RFID tags can be used to monitor the inventory and sales of products in a store, and to prevent theft and counterfeiting.
  - RFID tags can be used to identify and locate patients, staff and equipment in a hospital, and to ensure the safety and quality of medical procedures and drugs.
  - RFID tags can be used to facilitate the payment and access of vehicles in toll roads, parking lots, public transportation, etc.
  - RFID tags can be used to enhance the security and authentication of passports, ID cards, access cards, etc.



### Wireless Sensor Networks

- Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are networks of spatially dispersed and dedicated sensors that monitor and record the physical conditions of the environment and forward the collected data to a central location.
- WSNs can measure environmental conditions such as temperature, sound, pollution levels, humidity and wind.
- WSNs consist of a set of connected tiny sensor nodes, which communicate with each other and exchange information and data.
- WSNs are characterized as infrastructure less, fault tolerant and self-organizing networks which provide opportunities for low-cost, easy-to-apply, rapid and flexible installations in an environment for various applications.

  - Radio Nodes: Radio nodes are used to receive the data produced by the sensors and send it to the wireless local area network (WLAN) access point. They consist of a microcontroller, a transceiver, and a battery. They can also perform some data processing and routing functions.
- A WSN also has one or more sink nodes (also called base stations) that collect data from the sensors and forward it to the end users or applications.
- WSNs can be classified into different types based on their topology, communication, architecture, and application.
  - Topology: The topology of a WSN refers to the way the sensor nodes are arranged and connected in the network. Some common topologies are star, tree, mesh, and cluster.
  - Communication: The communication of a WSN refers to the way the sensor nodes communicate with each other and with the sink node. Some common communication methods are single-hop, multi-hop, direct, and indirect.
  - Architecture: The architecture of a WSN refers to the way the sensor nodes are organized and managed in the network. Some common architectures are flat, hierarchical, and hybrid.
  - Application: The application of a WSN refers to the purpose and functionality of the network. Some common applications are environmental monitoring, health care, military, smart home, and industrial control.

  - Cortex-M profile: for low-cost and energy-efficient applications, such as IoT devices, sensors, wearables, etc. Supports 32-bit instruction set, reduced instruction set, and optional security extensions .
  - Cortex-S profile: for secure applications, such as smart cards, SIM cards, etc. Supports 32-bit instruction set, reduced instruction set, and security extensions.

  - Clock speed: the frequency at which the core executes instructions, measured in MHz or GHz.
  - Pipeline: the number of stages in the core that process instructions in parallel, increasing the throughput and performance.
  - Cache: the small and fast memory that stores frequently used data and instructions, reducing the access time and latency.
  - Floating-point unit (FPU): the hardware unit that performs arithmetic operations on floating-point numbers, improving the accuracy and speed of calculations.
  - Digital signal processing (DSP) extensions: the additional instructions that optimize the core for signal processing applications, such as audio, video, image, etc.
  - Memory protection unit (MPU): the hardware unit that controls the access rights and permissions of different memory regions, enhancing the security and reliability of the system.
  - Memory management unit (MMU): the hardware unit that translates virtual addresses to physical addresses, enabling the use of virtual memory and memory mapping.
  - TrustZone: the security extension that creates a secure and non-secure world in the system, isolating the sensitive data and code from the normal applications and operating system.
  - Helium: the technology that extends the SIMD capabilities of the core, allowing the parallel processing of multiple data elements in a single instruction, boosting the performance of vector operations.

### Wireless Medium Access Issues for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Wireless medium access is the process of coordinating the transmission and reception of data among multiple devices that share a common wireless channel.
- Wireless medium access is challenging in IoT because of the following issues:
  - **Interference**: IoT devices may operate in the same frequency band as other wireless technologies, such as WLAN, Bluetooth, or cellular networks, and cause or suffer from interference that degrades the network performance .
  - **Energy efficiency**: IoT devices are often battery-powered and have limited energy resources, so they need to minimize the energy consumption of wireless communication, which is a major source of power drain .
  - **Scalability**: IoT networks may consist of a large number of devices with diverse capabilities and requirements, so they need to support scalable and flexible wireless medium access that can adapt to the network dynamics and traffic patterns .
  - **Reliability**: IoT applications may have strict quality of service (QoS) demands, such as low latency, high throughput, or high reliability, so they need to ensure reliable wireless medium access that can cope with the wireless channel variations and packet losses .
- Wireless medium access is controlled by the medium access control (MAC) protocol, which is a sublayer of the data link layer in the network stack.
- The MAC protocol is responsible for the following functions:
  - **Channel access**: The MAC protocol determines when and how a device can access the wireless channel to transmit or receive data, and how to resolve the contention or collision among multiple devices that want to access the channel simultaneously.
  - **Channel allocation**: The MAC protocol allocates the wireless channel resources, such as time slots, frequency bands, or codes, to the devices according to their needs and priorities, and manages the channel reuse or sharing among different devices or networks.
  - **Channel coordination**: The MAC protocol coordinates the communication among the devices, such as by exchanging control messages, signaling the channel status, or synchronizing the clocks, to facilitate the data transmission and reception.
  - **Channel adaptation**: The MAC protocol adapts to the wireless channel conditions, such as by adjusting the transmission power, modulation scheme, or coding rate, to optimize the network performance and efficiency.
- There are different types of MAC protocols for wireless medium access, such as:
  - **Contention-based MAC protocols**: These protocols allow the devices to access the channel in a random or probabilistic manner, without any centralized control or reservation. The devices compete for the channel access and may experience collisions or interference. Examples of contention-based MAC protocols are ALOHA, CSMA, and CSMA/CA.
  - **Reservation-based MAC protocols**: These protocols require the devices to reserve the channel access in advance, either by a centralized controller or by a distributed negotiation. The devices access the channel in a deterministic or scheduled manner, without any contention or collision. Examples of reservation-based MAC protocols are TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and OFDMA.
  - **Hybrid MAC protocols**: These protocols combine the features of contention-based and reservation-based MAC protocols, such as by using contention for channel reservation and reservation for data transmission, or by switching between contention and reservation modes according to the network conditions. Examples of hybrid MAC protocols are CSMA/CD, MACA, and MACAW.



### MAC protocol survey for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- MAC (Medium Access Control) protocol is the first protocol layer above the physical layer in wireless decentralized networks in IoT.
- MAC protocol aims to coordinate access among IoT devices to the shared wireless medium and avoid collisions and interference.
- MAC protocol can be classified into two main categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based MAC protocol allows devices to compete for the channel access without reservation or scheduling, but it may cause collisions and waste of bandwidth.
- Contention-free MAC protocol assigns channel access to devices based on reservation or scheduling, but it may incur overhead and delay.
- IEEE 802.11ah, also known as WiFi HaLow, is a scalable solution for medium-range communication in IoT that leverages various innovative MAC features, such as restricted access window, target wake time, and group addressed transmission .

### Survey routing protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Routing protocols are algorithms that determine the best path for data packets to reach their destination in a network.
- Routing protocols for Internet of Things (IoT) are designed to meet the specific requirements and challenges of IoT networks, such as resource constraints, heterogeneity, scalability, mobility, and security.
- Routing protocols for IoT can be classified into different categories based on various criteria, such as network topology, routing strategy, data flow direction, and security level.
- Some of the common categories of routing protocols for IoT are:

  - Flat routing protocols: These protocols treat all the nodes in the network as equal and do not form any hierarchy or cluster. They are suitable for small and static networks. Examples of flat routing protocols for IoT are Ad hoc On-Demand Distance Vector (AODV), Dynamic Source Routing (DSR), and Optimized Link State Routing (OLSR).
  - Hierarchical routing protocols: These protocols divide the network into clusters and assign different roles and responsibilities to the nodes based on their capabilities and positions. They are suitable for large and dynamic networks. Examples of hierarchical routing protocols for IoT are Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH), Threshold-sensitive Energy Efficient sensor Network (TEEN), and Hybrid Energy-Efficient Distributed clustering (HEED).
  - Location-based routing protocols: These protocols use the geographic information of the nodes to make routing decisions. They are suitable for mobile and sparse networks. Examples of location-based routing protocols for IoT are Geographic Routing Protocol (GRP), Greedy Perimeter Stateless Routing (GPSR), and Distance Routing Effect Algorithm for Mobility (DREAM).
  - Data-centric routing protocols: These protocols use the attributes or content of the data packets to make routing decisions. They are suitable for query-based and event-driven networks. Examples of data-centric routing protocols for IoT are Directed Diffusion, Rumor Routing, and SPIN.
  - Multipath routing protocols: These protocols use multiple paths to route the data packets to the destination. They are suitable for fault-tolerant and load-balanced networks. Examples of multipath routing protocols for IoT are Split Multipath Routing (SMR), Ad hoc On-demand Multipath Distance Vector (AOMDV), and Multipath Optimized Link State Routing (MP-OLSR).
  - Security-based routing protocols: These protocols use cryptographic techniques or trust mechanisms to ensure the security and privacy of the data packets and the nodes. They are suitable for sensitive and critical networks. Examples of security-based routing protocols for IoT are Secure Routing Protocol (SRP), Secure Efficient Ad hoc Distance vector routing protocol (SEAD), and Trust-based Routing Protocol (TBRP).

- Routing protocols for IoT are still an active area of research and development, as new challenges and opportunities arise with the advancement of IoT technologies and applications. Some of the future research directions are:

  - Developing adaptive and cross-layer routing protocols that can adjust to the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of IoT networks and optimize the performance of different layers of the network stack.
  - Developing energy-efficient and green routing protocols that can reduce the power consumption and environmental impact of IoT networks and devices.
  - Developing context-aware and semantic routing protocols that can leverage the rich information and intelligence of IoT networks and devices to provide more relevant and personalized services to the users.
  - Developing cooperative and collaborative routing protocols that can enhance the reliability and resilience of IoT networks and devices by exploiting the diversity and redundancy of the network resources.
  - Developing scalable and interoperable routing protocols that can support the massive and diverse growth of IoT networks and devices and enable seamless communication and integration among different IoT platforms and domains.



### Sensor deployment and node discovery in IoT

Sensor deployment and node discovery are two important aspects of network and communication in IoT. Sensor deployment refers to the process of placing sensor nodes in the environment to monitor physical phenomena, such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, etc. Node discovery refers to the process of identifying and locating sensor nodes in the network, as well as establishing communication links among them.

Some of the key points to consider for sensor deployment and node discovery in IoT are:

- Sensor deployment can be done in various ways, such as random, deterministic, or adaptive, depending on the application requirements, the environment characteristics, and the available resources. Random deployment is suitable for large-scale and dynamic scenarios, where the exact location of the sensor nodes is not known or controlled. Deterministic deployment is suitable for small-scale and static scenarios, where the sensor nodes are placed at predefined locations. Adaptive deployment is suitable for scenarios where the sensor nodes can adjust their positions or orientations based on the feedback from the environment or the network.
- Node discovery can be done in various ways, such as proactive, reactive, or hybrid, depending on the network topology, the communication protocol, and the energy consumption. Proactive node discovery is suitable for dense and stable networks, where the sensor nodes periodically broadcast their presence and information to their neighbors. Reactive node discovery is suitable for sparse and dynamic networks, where the sensor nodes only respond to queries from other nodes when needed. Hybrid node discovery is suitable for networks that combine both dense and sparse regions, where the sensor nodes use a combination of proactive and reactive methods to discover their neighbors.
- Sensor deployment and node discovery can affect the performance and efficiency of the IoT network, such as the coverage, connectivity, reliability, scalability, and security. Coverage refers to the degree to which the sensor nodes can sense the physical phenomena in the environment. Connectivity refers to the degree to which the sensor nodes can communicate with each other and with the base station or the gateway. Reliability refers to the degree to which the sensor nodes can deliver the sensed data to the destination without errors or losses. Scalability refers to the degree to which the network can accommodate more sensor nodes or more data traffic without degrading the performance. Security refers to the degree to which the network can protect the sensor nodes and the data from malicious attacks or unauthorized access.



### Data aggregation and dissemination in IoT

- Data aggregation is the process of collecting, filtering, and summarizing data from multiple sources in the Internet of Things (IoT) network.
- Data dissemination is the process of distributing data from a base station or a cloud server to multiple nodes or devices in the IoT network.
- Data aggregation and dissemination are essential for efficient and reliable data management in IoT, as they can reduce the communication overhead, save energy, and improve the quality of service.
- Data aggregation and dissemination can be performed in different ways, depending on the network topology, the data type, the application requirements, and the available resources.
- Some of the common methods for data aggregation and dissemination in IoT are:

  - **Centralized**: The base station or the cloud server collects all the data from the nodes and performs aggregation and dissemination functions. This method is simple and easy to implement, but it may cause high latency, congestion, and single point of failure.
  - **Distributed**: The nodes cooperate with each other to aggregate and disseminate data in a peer-to-peer manner. This method is more scalable and robust, but it may incur more communication overhead and complexity.
  - **Hierarchical**: The nodes are organized into clusters, and each cluster has a leader or a gateway that aggregates and disseminates data for the cluster members. This method can balance the load and reduce the communication cost, but it may require more coordination and maintenance.
  - **Hybrid**: The nodes use a combination of the above methods to achieve optimal performance and flexibility. For example, the nodes can use a distributed method within a cluster and a centralized method across clusters.

- Data aggregation and dissemination in IoT can also benefit from machine learning techniques, such as data compression, anomaly detection, feature extraction, and data fusion. Machine learning can help to reduce the data size, enhance the data quality, and extract useful information from the data.



## Unit 5 - Programming the Arduino

- Arduino is an open-source platform that consists of a hardware board and a software IDE (Integrated Development Environment) that can be used to create and program electronic projects.
- The hardware board is based on a microcontroller, which is a small computer that can run a single program repeatedly and interact with sensors and actuators.
- The software IDE is a program that allows you to write, compile, and upload code to the board using a simple programming language called Arduino C/C++.
- The code that you write for the Arduino is called a sketch, which consists of two main functions: setup() and loop().
- The setup() function runs once when the board is powered on or reset, and is used to initialize variables, pin modes, and libraries.
- The loop() function runs continuously after the setup() function, and is used to implement the main logic and behavior of the sketch.
- The Arduino IDE provides a serial monitor that can be used to communicate with the board and display messages or data sent by the sketch.
- The Arduino IDE also provides a library manager that can be used to install and manage additional libraries that extend the functionality of the board and the sketch.
- Some of the basic concepts and syntax of Arduino C/C++ are:

  - Comments: Comments are lines of text that are ignored by the compiler and are used to explain or document the code. Comments can be single-line (//) or multi-line (/* */).
  - Variables: Variables are names that represent values or data that can be used in the sketch. Variables have a type, a name, and an optional value. For example, int ledPin = 13; declares a variable of type int (integer) with the name ledPin and the value 13.
  - Constants: Constants are names that represent fixed values or data that cannot be changed in the sketch. Constants have a type, a name, and a value. For example, const float PI = 3.14; declares a constant of type float (floating-point number) with the name PI and the value 3.14.
  - Operators: Operators are symbols that perform operations on values or variables, such as arithmetic (+, -, *, /, %), logical (&&, ||, !), relational (==, !=, <, >, <=, >=), and assignment (=, +=, -=, *=, /=, %=).
  - Expressions: Expressions are combinations of values, variables, and operators that produce a result. For example, ledPin + 1 is an expression that adds 1 to the value of ledPin and returns the result.
  - Statements: Statements are lines of code that perform actions or control the flow of the sketch. Statements end with a semicolon (;). For example, pinMode(ledPin, OUTPUT); is a statement that sets the mode of the pin ledPin to OUTPUT.
  - Control structures: Control structures are statements that alter the flow of the sketch based on conditions or repetitions. For example, if, else, for, while, do...while, switch, case, break, continue, return, and goto are control structures.
  - Functions: Functions are blocks of code that perform a specific task and can be reused in the sketch. Functions have a name, a return type, a list of parameters, and a body. For example, void blink(int pin, int delay) { ... } defines a function named blink that takes two parameters of type int and returns nothing (void).
  - Arrays: Arrays are variables that store multiple values of the same type in a fixed-size sequence. Arrays have a name, a type, a size, and a list of values. For example, int numbers[5] = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}; declares an array of type int with the name numbers, the size 5, and the values 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5.
  - Strings: Strings are variables that store sequences of characters. Strings have a name, a type, and a value. For example, String message = "Hello, world!"; declares a string of type String with the name message and the value "Hello, world!".
  - Data types: Data types are categories of values that determine the size, range, and format of the data. For example, int, float, char, bool, String, and void are data types.
  - Scope: Scope is the area of the sketch where a variable or a function is visible and accessible. For example, a variable declared inside a function is local to that function and cannot be used outside of it, while a variable declared outside of any function is global and can be used anywhere in the sketch.

Arduino boards are the microcontroller development platform that will be at the heart of your projects. When making something you will be building the circuits and interfaces for interaction, and telling the microcontroller how to interface with other components. Here the anatomy of Arduino Uno .

Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies that enable them to communicate and exchange data with other devices and systems over the internet. - **Lack of standardization**: IoT devices use different protocols, platforms, and architectures, which makes it difficult to ensure compatibility and interoperability among them. Moreover, some IoT devices may not comply with the existing security standards and regulations, which can expose them to legal and ethical risks.
- **Limited physical security**: IoT devices are often small, portable, and easy to conceal, which makes them vulnerable to physical attacks, such as tampering, theft, or destruction. Viruses and malware can have various effects on IoT devices, such as stealing or deleting data, spying on users, disrupting operations, or launching attacks on other devices or systems. Antivirus and anti-malware software are needed to detect and remove viruses and malware from IoT devices and systems.
- **Legacy operating systems**: Operating systems are the software programs that manage the basic functions of IoT devices, such as memory, processing, communication, and security. However, some IoT devices use legacy or outdated operating systems, which may not have the latest security patches or updates, or may not be compatible with the current security standards or protocols. Legacy operating systems can expose IoT devices to various security vulnerabilities, such as buffer overflows, denial-of-service attacks, or remote code execution. Updating or replacing legacy operating systems is necessary to improve the security and performance of IoT devices.
- **Insecure web interface**: Web interface is the graphical user interface that allows users to interact with IoT devices or systems through a web browser. ### Communicating data with H/W units

- IoT devices are hardware units that can sense, process, and transmit data to other devices or applications over the internet.
- IoT devices can communicate data with other H/W units in different ways, depending on the type, distance, and purpose of the communication.
- Some of the common methods of communication are:

  - **Wired communication**: This involves using a physical cable or wire to connect the IoT device to a network or a gateway. This can provide a fast, reliable, and secure data transfer, but it also limits the mobility and scalability of the IoT device. Examples of wired communication protocols are serial, Ethernet, and USB.
  - **Wireless communication**: This involves using radio signals or waves to transmit data from the IoT device to another device or a gateway. This can provide more flexibility, mobility, and scalability for the IoT device, but it also introduces more challenges such as interference, latency, and security. Examples of wireless communication protocols are Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRaWAN, and 5G  .
  - **Unidirectional communication**: This involves sending data from the IoT device to another device or a gateway, without expecting any response or feedback. This can be useful for simple or periodic data collection or monitoring, but it also limits the functionality and interactivity of the IoT device. Examples of unidirectional communication scenarios are smart meters, weather stations, and asset trackers.
  - **Bidirectional communication**: This involves sending and receiving data between the IoT device and another device or a gateway. This can enable more complex or dynamic data processing and control, but it also requires more resources and coordination for the IoT device. Examples of bidirectional communication scenarios are smart home devices, wearable devices, and industrial robots.
  - **Human to Machine (H2M) communication**: This involves a human user providing input to the IoT device, such as speech, text, image, gesture, or touch. The IoT device then processes the input, performs some action, and responds back to the user by means of text, sound, light, or display. This can enhance the user experience and interaction with the IoT device, but it also requires more intelligence and capabilities for the IoT device. Examples of H2M communication scenarios are voice assistants, smart watches, and facial recognition systems.
  - **Machine to Machine (M2M) communication**: This involves two or more IoT devices exchanging data with each other, without any human intervention. The IoT devices can coordinate, cooperate, or compete with each other, depending on the application and the goal. This can enable more automation and efficiency for the IoT system, but it also requires more standardization and interoperability for the IoT devices. Examples of M2M communication scenarios are smart grid, autonomous vehicles, and swarm robotics.
