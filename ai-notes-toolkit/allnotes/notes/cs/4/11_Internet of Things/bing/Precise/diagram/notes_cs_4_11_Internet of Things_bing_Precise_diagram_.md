### M2M Communication

Machine to Machine communications, often termed M2M/IoT is going to be the next generation of Internet revolution connecting more and more devices on Internet. M2M communications refer to automated applications which involve machines or devices communicating through a network without human intervention.

- M2M is a general concept, a group of many different technologies, including IoT, while IoT is a specific subset of technologies that happen to rely on M2M.
- M2M communication comes in many different forms besides the communication between smart devices, and M2M communication is only one part of the Internet of Things.
- M2M is also the foundation for IoT.
- The first use-of M2M communication is credited to Theodore Paraskevakos, who invented this technology related to the transmission of data over telephone lines, the basis for modern-day caller-ID.
- IoT needs M2M, but M2M does not need IoT.
- Both terms relate to the communication of connected devices, but M2M systems are often isolated, stand-alone networked equipment.
- IoT systems take M2M to the next level, bringing together disparate systems into one large, connected ecosystem.
- M2M systems use point-to-point communications between machines, sensors and hardware over cellular or wired networks, while IoT systems rely on IP-based networks to send data collected from IoT-connected devices to gateways, the cloud or middleware platforms.

### IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization

IoT/M2M systems are designed to promote efficient communication and interoperability between devices. To achieve this, various organizations have proposed standardized architectures and protocols for these systems.

One such initiative is the oneM2M global initiative, launched in 2012 by ETSI and 13 other founding members. The oneM2M architecture divides IoT functions into three major domains: the application layer, the services layer, and the network layer .

- **Application layer:** This layer focuses on connectivity between devices and their applications.
- **Services layer:** This layer provides the necessary services to support the communication between devices and applications.
- **Network layer:** This layer is responsible for the actual transmission of data between devices.

Other organizations, such as the Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC), have also suggested open standards for sensors’ discovery, capabilities, quality, and other aspects with support to geographical information web support.

ETSI, for example, conducts its main IoT standardization activities at the radio layer in 3GPP (LTE-M, NB-IoT, and EC-GSM-IoT) and at the service layer in oneM2M.

These standardized architectures and protocols aim to promote interoperability and efficient communication between devices in IoT/M2M systems.



### Communication Technologies for IoT

1. **Cellular:** Cellular communication technologies such as 2G, 3G, 4G, and 5G provide wide area coverage and high data rates for IoT devices. These technologies are suitable for applications that require mobility and real-time data transmission.

2. **Wi-Fi:** Wi-Fi is a wireless local area network (WLAN) technology that provides high-speed data transfer over short distances. It is commonly used for IoT devices in home automation, smart buildings, and healthcare.

3. **Bluetooth:** Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology that is commonly used for IoT devices that require low power consumption and simple connectivity. It is suitable for applications such as wearables, smart home devices, and healthcare monitoring.

4. **Zigbee:** Zigbee is a wireless technology that operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band and is designed for low-power, low-data-rate applications. It is commonly used for IoT devices in home automation, smart buildings, and industrial control.

5. **Z-Wave:** Z-Wave is a wireless technology that operates in the sub-1 GHz frequency band and is designed for low-power, low-data-rate applications. It is commonly used for IoT devices in home automation and security systems.

6. **LoRaWAN:** LoRaWAN is a long-range, low-power wireless technology that is designed for IoT devices that require long battery life and long-range connectivity. It is suitable for applications such as smart agriculture, smart cities, and environmental monitoring.

7. **Sigfox:** Sigfox is a low-power, wide-area network (LPWAN) technology that provides long-range connectivity for IoT devices. It is suitable for applications that require low data rates and long battery life, such as asset tracking and remote monitoring.

8. **NB-IoT:** Narrowband IoT (NB-IoT) is a cellular technology that provides low-power, wide-area connectivity for IoT devices. It is suitable for applications that require long battery life and low data rates, such as smart metering and remote monitoring.

9. **Thread:** Thread is a wireless mesh networking technology that provides secure, low-power, and scalable connectivity for IoT devices. It is suitable for applications such as home automation, smart buildings, and healthcare.

These are some of the communication technologies used in the Internet of Things (IoT). Each technology has its own strengths and is suitable for different types of applications. It is important to choose the right communication technology for your IoT application based on its requirements.

### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology is a wireless communication technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects. It is commonly used in the Internet of Things (IoT) for hardware identification and tracking.

Some key points to note about RFID technology are:

1. RFID systems consist of a reader, an antenna, and a tag or transponder attached to the object being identified or tracked.
2. The reader sends out a radio signal to the tag, which responds with its unique identification number.
3. RFID tags can be passive, active, or semi-passive. Passive tags do not have their own power source and rely on the energy from the reader's signal to respond. Active tags have their own power source and can initiate communication with the reader. Semi-passive tags have a power source but only use it to power the tag's circuitry, not to initiate communication with the reader.
4. RFID technology can be used for a wide range of applications, including inventory management, asset tracking, supply chain management, and access control.
5. RFID technology has several advantages over traditional barcodes, including the ability to read multiple tags simultaneously, the ability to read tags without direct line-of-sight, and the ability to store more information on the tag.

## Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT

Embedded platforms are an essential component of the Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem. These platforms provide the hardware and software necessary to connect devices and sensors to the internet, allowing them to collect, process, and transmit data.

Some key points to consider when discussing embedded platforms for IoT include:

1. **Hardware:** Embedded platforms for IoT typically include microcontrollers or microprocessors, memory, and input/output interfaces. These components are designed to be small, low-power, and capable of operating in a wide range of environments.

2. **Software:** Embedded platforms for IoT often run real-time operating systems (RTOS) or other lightweight operating systems designed for resource-constrained environments. These operating systems provide the necessary software infrastructure to support IoT applications, including networking, security, and data management.

3. **Connectivity:** Embedded platforms for IoT must be able to connect to the internet, either through wired or wireless interfaces. Common wireless interfaces for IoT devices include Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular.

4. **Power:** Many IoT devices are battery-powered and must operate for extended periods of time without recharging. Embedded platforms for IoT must be designed with power efficiency in mind, using techniques such as low-power modes and energy harvesting to extend battery life.

5. **Security:** IoT devices are often deployed in unsecured environments and must be able to protect sensitive data and prevent unauthorized access. Embedded platforms for IoT must include security features such as encryption, secure boot, and secure key storage.

Overall, embedded platforms for IoT provide the foundation for building connected devices that can collect, process, and transmit data in real-time. These platforms are essential for enabling the development of new IoT applications and services.



### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing refers to the use of computer systems and software to perform dedicated functions within larger systems. These systems are often designed to perform a specific task or set of tasks, and are typically integrated into other devices or systems.

Here are some key points to consider when studying embedded computing basics:

1. **Embedded systems** are computer systems that are integrated into other devices or products, and are designed to perform specific functions.
2. **Microcontrollers** are small, low-cost computers that are often used in embedded systems. They typically have limited processing power and memory, but are highly efficient and can be programmed to perform a wide range of tasks.
3. **Real-time operating systems (RTOS)** are operating systems designed for use in embedded systems. They are optimized for fast, predictable response times, and are often used in applications where timing is critical.
4. **Programming languages** commonly used in embedded systems include C, C++, and assembly language. These languages are chosen for their efficiency and ability to interface directly with hardware.
5. **Hardware interfaces** are used to connect embedded systems to other devices or systems. Common interfaces include serial, USB, and Ethernet.
6. **Power consumption** is an important consideration in embedded systems, as many of these systems are battery-powered or have limited power supplies. Techniques such as power management and low-power design can help to reduce power consumption and extend battery life.

These are some of the basic concepts to consider when studying embedded computing for IoT. Understanding these concepts can help you to better understand the design and implementation of embedded systems in IoT applications.

### Wireless Medium Access Issues

Wireless medium access issues are a critical aspect of network and communication in IoT. These issues arise due to the shared nature of the wireless medium, where multiple devices compete for access to the same channel. Some of the key issues that need to be addressed in wireless medium access are:

1. **Collision Avoidance:** When multiple devices transmit data simultaneously, their signals can interfere with each other, resulting in a collision. Collision avoidance techniques are used to minimize the chances of such collisions.

2. **Fairness:** In a shared wireless medium, it is important to ensure that all devices have fair access to the channel. Fairness algorithms are used to prevent any single device from dominating the channel, and to ensure that all devices have an equal opportunity to transmit their data.

3. **Hidden Node Problem:** In a wireless network, two devices may be able to communicate with a common access point, but not with each other, due to distance or obstacles. This is known as the hidden node problem, and it can result in collisions and reduced network performance.

4. **Exposed Node Problem:** The exposed node problem occurs when a device is prevented from transmitting data, even though its transmission would not interfere with any ongoing communication. This can result in reduced network performance and inefficient use of the wireless medium.

These are some of the key wireless medium access issues that need to be addressed in the design and implementation of IoT networks. By addressing these issues, it is possible to improve the performance, reliability, and efficiency of wireless communication in IoT systems.

# Unit 7 - IoT Applications: Designing of Smart Street Lights in Smart City

Smart street lights are an integral part of a smart city. They are designed to improve energy efficiency, reduce maintenance costs, and enhance the safety and security of the city. Here are some key points to consider when designing smart street lights for a smart city:

1. **Sensors**: Smart street lights should be equipped with sensors that can detect the presence of pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles. This allows the lights to be dimmed or turned off when there is no activity, saving energy.

2. **Connectivity**: Smart street lights should be connected to a central control system that can monitor and control the lights remotely. This allows for real-time adjustments to be made based on changing conditions, such as weather or traffic patterns.

3. **LED Technology**: LED lights are more energy-efficient and have a longer lifespan than traditional street lights. They also provide better visibility and color rendering, making them a good choice for smart street lights.

4. **Maintenance**: Smart street lights should be designed with maintenance in mind. They should be easy to access and repair, and should be equipped with self-diagnostic capabilities that can alert the central control system to any issues.

5. **Integration**: Smart street lights should be integrated with other smart city systems, such as traffic management and public safety. This allows for a more coordinated and efficient response to events and emergencies.

6. **Data Collection**: Smart street lights can collect valuable data on traffic patterns, air quality, and other environmental factors. This data can be used to improve city planning and decision-making.

In summary, designing smart street lights for a smart city involves considering factors such as sensors, connectivity, LED technology, maintenance, integration, and data collection. By incorporating these elements, smart street lights can improve energy efficiency, reduce costs, and enhance the safety and security of the city.
