

## Unit 1 - Introduction to Data Analytics

Data analytics is the process of examining, cleaning, transforming, and modeling data to discover useful information, draw conclusions, and support decision-making. It involves the use of statistical, computational, and mathematical techniques to analyze data and extract insights.

Some key points to consider when studying data analytics include:

1. **Data collection and preparation**: This involves gathering data from various sources and preparing it for analysis by cleaning, transforming, and organizing it.

2. **Data analysis**: This involves the use of statistical and computational techniques to analyze data and extract insights. Some common techniques include regression analysis, clustering, and data visualization.

3. **Data interpretation**: This involves interpreting the results of the data analysis and drawing conclusions. It is important to consider the context of the data and the limitations of the analysis when interpreting the results.

4. **Data-driven decision making**: Data analytics can be used to support decision-making by providing insights and evidence to inform decisions.

Overall, data analytics is a powerful tool that can help organizations make better decisions, improve performance, and gain a competitive advantage. It is an important field of study for anyone interested in working with data and making data-driven decisions.



### Sources and nature of data

Data is a collection of facts, figures, and statistics that can be processed to produce information. Data can be collected from various sources and can be of different types. Here are some of the sources and nature of data:

1. **Primary data:** Primary data is the data that is collected directly from the source. It is original and first-hand information. Some of the methods of collecting primary data are surveys, interviews, and observations.

2. **Secondary data:** Secondary data is the data that has already been collected and processed by someone else. It is second-hand information. Some of the sources of secondary data are books, journals, and the internet.

3. **Quantitative data:** Quantitative data is the data that can be measured and expressed in numbers. It is numerical in nature. Some examples of quantitative data are height, weight, and temperature.

4. **Qualitative data:** Qualitative data is the data that cannot be measured and expressed in numbers. It is descriptive in nature. Some examples of qualitative data are color, taste, and texture.

5. **Structured data:** Structured data is the data that is organized in a specific format or schema. It is easy to search, analyze, and process. Some examples of structured data are spreadsheets and databases.

6. **Unstructured data:** Unstructured data is the data that is not organized in a specific format or schema. It is difficult to search, analyze, and process. Some examples of unstructured data are emails, videos, and images.

These are some of the sources and nature of data that are important to understand in the subject of Data Analytics. Understanding the sources and nature of data can help in selecting the appropriate methods for collecting, processing, and analyzing data.



### Classification of Data

Classification of data is the process of organizing data into categories or groups based on their characteristics or attributes. This is done to make the data easier to understand and analyze. There are several ways to classify data, including:

1. **Quantitative and Qualitative Data**: Quantitative data is numerical and can be measured, while qualitative data is non-numerical and is based on observations and descriptions.

2. **Discrete and Continuous Data**: Discrete data can only take on certain values, while continuous data can take on any value within a range.

3. **Nominal, Ordinal, Interval, and Ratio Data**: Nominal data is categorical and has no inherent order, while ordinal data is also categorical but has a specific order. Interval data is numerical and has a consistent scale, but no true zero point. Ratio data is also numerical, has a consistent scale, and has a true zero point.

4. **Cross-sectional and Time-series Data**: Cross-sectional data is collected at a single point in time, while time-series data is collected over a period of time.

### Semi-Structured Data

Semi-structured data refers to data that does not conform to a fixed schema or data model, but still contains some organizational properties, such as tags or markers, that separate data elements and enforce hierarchies of records and fields within the data.

Some common examples of semi-structured data include:
- XML (eXtensible Markup Language) documents
- JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) documents
- CSV (Comma-Separated Values) files

Semi-structured data is often used in situations where the data being stored or exchanged is not well-defined or is subject to change. This flexibility makes it a popular choice for data exchange and storage in many applications.

In the context of data analytics, semi-structured data can present some challenges, as it may require additional processing to extract relevant information and transform it into a structured format that can be analyzed. However, the flexibility of semi-structured data also provides opportunities for more nuanced and complex analysis, as it can capture and represent data that may not fit neatly into a fixed schema.

In summary, semi-structured data is a type of data that falls between structured and unstructured data, providing some organizational properties while still allowing for flexibility in the data model. It is commonly used in data exchange and storage, and presents both challenges and opportunities in the field of data analytics.

### Analysis vs Reporting

- **Reporting** is the process of organizing data into informational summaries in order to monitor how different areas of a business are performing.

- **Analysis** is the process of exploring data and reports in order to extract meaningful insights, which can be used to better understand and improve business performance.

- Reporting is focused on providing data in a structured manner, while analysis is focused on understanding the data and drawing conclusions from it.

- Reporting is often done on a regular basis, such as daily, weekly, or monthly, while analysis is done as needed to answer specific questions or solve problems.

- Reporting is often done using pre-defined templates and formats, while analysis may involve more ad-hoc exploration of data.

- Reporting is often done by operational staff, while analysis is often done by more specialized staff such as data analysts or business analysts.

- Reporting is often focused on what has happened, while analysis is focused on understanding why it happened and what can be done to improve performance.

- Reporting is often focused on providing information to others, while analysis is often focused on providing insights to support decision-making.

- Reporting and analysis are complementary processes, with reporting providing the data and information needed for analysis, and analysis providing the insights and recommendations needed to improve performance.

## Unit 2 - Data Analytics Lifecycle

The Data Analytics Lifecycle is a framework for conducting data analytics projects. It consists of several phases that guide the process of transforming raw data into valuable insights. The phases of the Data Analytics Lifecycle are:

1. **Discovery**: In this phase, the project team identifies the business problem or opportunity and determines the scope of the project. This involves understanding the business context, defining the objectives, and formulating the initial hypotheses.

2. **Data Preparation**: This phase involves collecting, cleaning, and transforming the data to make it ready for analysis. This includes tasks such as data integration, data quality assessment, and data transformation.

3. **Model Planning**: In this phase, the project team selects the appropriate analytical techniques and prepares the data for modeling. This involves tasks such as variable selection, data partitioning, and model selection.

4. **Model Building**: In this phase, the project team builds and tests the analytical models. This involves tasks such as model training, model validation, and model refinement.

5. **Communicating Results**: In this phase, the project team communicates the results of the analysis to the stakeholders. This involves tasks such as creating visualizations, preparing reports, and presenting the findings.

6. **Operationalizing**: In this phase, the project team implements the analytical models and integrates them into the business processes. This involves tasks such as model deployment, model monitoring, and model maintenance.

The Data Analytics Lifecycle provides a structured approach to conducting data analytics projects, ensuring that the project team follows a rigorous process and delivers valuable insights to the business.

### Data Preparation

Data preparation is the second phase of the Data Analytics Lifecycle and is a crucial step in any data analysis project. It involves cleaning, transforming, and organizing raw data into a format that can be easily analyzed. Here are some key points to consider when preparing data for analysis:

1. **Data Cleaning:** This involves identifying and correcting errors, inconsistencies, and inaccuracies in the data. Common data cleaning techniques include removing duplicate records, filling in missing values, and correcting data entry errors.

2. **Data Transformation:** This involves converting data from one format to another to make it more suitable for analysis. Common data transformation techniques include normalization, scaling, and encoding categorical variables.

3. **Data Integration:** This involves combining data from multiple sources to create a single, unified dataset. This can be done by merging datasets based on common identifiers or by appending datasets with similar variables.

4. **Data Reduction:** This involves reducing the size of the dataset by removing irrelevant or redundant variables. This can be done by selecting a subset of variables based on their relevance to the analysis or by applying dimensionality reduction techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA).

5. **Data Partitioning:** This involves dividing the dataset into smaller subsets for the purpose of training and testing machine learning models. This can be done by randomly splitting the dataset into training and test sets or by using techniques such as cross-validation.

In summary, data preparation is a critical step in the data analysis process that involves cleaning, transforming, integrating, reducing, and partitioning data to make it ready for analysis. Proper data preparation can greatly improve the accuracy and reliability of the analysis results.



### Model Planning

Model planning is a crucial step in the Data Analytics Lifecycle. It involves selecting the appropriate modeling technique to use for the analysis. Here are some key points to consider when planning a model for data analysis:

1. **Define the business objective:** The first step in model planning is to clearly define the business objective. This will help determine the type of analysis that needs to be performed and the type of model that will be most appropriate.

2. **Select the appropriate modeling technique:** There are many different modeling techniques that can be used for data analysis, including regression, decision trees, and neural networks. The appropriate technique will depend on the type of data being analyzed and the business objective.

3. **Prepare the data:** Before a model can be built, the data must be prepared. This involves cleaning the data, handling missing values, and transforming variables if necessary.

4. **Split the data:** The data should be split into a training set and a test set. The model will be built using the training set and then evaluated using the test set.

5. **Build the model:** Once the data has been prepared and split, the model can be built. This involves selecting the appropriate parameters and training the model using the training data.

6. **Evaluate the model:** After the model has been built, it should be evaluated using the test data. This will help determine how well the model is performing and whether any changes need to be made.

Model planning is an important step in the Data Analytics Lifecycle. By carefully selecting the appropriate modeling technique and preparing the data, a successful model can be built that will help achieve the business objective.



### Model Building

### Regression modeling for the notes of the Unit 3 - Data Analysis in the subject of Data Analytics

Regression modeling is a statistical technique used to analyze the relationship between two or more variables. It is commonly used in data analysis to make predictions, forecast trends, and identify patterns in data.

1. **Simple Linear Regression**: Simple linear regression is used to analyze the relationship between two continuous variables. It assumes a linear relationship between the independent and dependent variables.

2. **Multiple Linear Regression**: Multiple linear regression is used to analyze the relationship between one dependent variable and two or more independent variables. It assumes a linear relationship between the independent and dependent variables.

3. **Logistic Regression**: Logistic regression is used to analyze the relationship between a binary dependent variable and one or more independent variables. It assumes a logistic relationship between the independent and dependent variables.

4. **Polynomial Regression**: Polynomial regression is used to analyze the relationship between one dependent variable and one independent variable when the relationship is non-linear. It assumes a polynomial relationship between the independent and dependent variables.

5. **Ridge Regression**: Ridge regression is used to analyze the relationship between one dependent variable and multiple independent variables when multicollinearity is present. It adds a penalty term to the loss function to reduce the impact of multicollinearity.

6. **Lasso Regression**: Lasso regression is used to analyze the relationship between one dependent variable and multiple independent variables when feature selection is needed. It adds a penalty term to the loss function to reduce the impact of irrelevant features.

7. **Elastic Net Regression**: Elastic net regression is used to analyze the relationship between one dependent variable and multiple independent variables when both multicollinearity and feature selection are present. ### Stochastic Search Methods

Stochastic search methods are a class of optimization algorithms that use randomness to find the global optimum of a problem. These methods are particularly useful when the search space is large, complex, and poorly understood. Some common stochastic search methods include:

### Mining Frequent Itemsets

Mining frequent itemsets is a fundamental problem in data mining. It involves finding sets of items that frequently occur together in a given dataset. This information can be used for various purposes, such as market basket analysis, association rule mining, and clustering.

Here are some key points to remember when studying mining frequent itemsets:

1. **Support**: The support of an itemset is the proportion of transactions in the dataset that contain the itemset. An itemset is considered frequent if its support is greater than or equal to a specified minimum support threshold.

2. **Apriori Algorithm**: The Apriori algorithm is a popular algorithm for mining frequent itemsets. It uses a bottom-up approach, where it starts by finding frequent individual items and then iteratively generates larger itemsets by combining smaller frequent itemsets.

3. **Candidate Generation**: The Apriori algorithm generates candidate itemsets by taking the union of two frequent itemsets that share all but one item. It then prunes the candidate itemsets that are not frequent by checking their support.

4. **Association Rules**: Association rules are generated from frequent itemsets. An association rule is an implication of the form X -> Y, where X and Y are disjoint itemsets. The rule suggests that if a transaction contains X, it is likely to also contain Y.

5. **Confidence and Lift**: The confidence of an association rule is the proportion of transactions that contain X that also contain Y. The lift of an association rule is the ratio of the observed support of X and Y to the expected support if X and Y were independent. A high lift value indicates a strong association between X and Y.

### Apriori Algorithm

The Apriori algorithm is an influential algorithm for mining frequent itemsets for Boolean association rules. It was proposed by Agrawal and Srikant in 1994. The algorithm uses a bottom-up approach, where frequent subsets are extended one item at a time, and groups of candidates are tested against the data.

The key concept of the Apriori algorithm is its anti-monotonicity property, which states that all non-empty subsets of a frequent itemset must also be frequent. This property is used to prune the search space of candidate itemsets.

The Apriori algorithm consists of two steps:

1. Generate candidate itemsets: In this step, the algorithm generates all possible itemsets of a given size. For example, if the current size is 2, the algorithm generates all possible pairs of items.

2. Prune candidate itemsets: In this step, the algorithm removes all candidate itemsets that are not frequent. This is done by counting the support of each candidate itemset and comparing it to a minimum support threshold.

These two steps are repeated until no more frequent itemsets can be found. The final result is the set of all frequent itemsets.

The Apriori algorithm has several advantages:

- It is easy to understand and implement.
- It can handle large datasets.
- It can be parallelized for improved performance.

However, the Apriori algorithm also has some disadvantages:

- It can be slow for large datasets or low minimum support thresholds.
- It can generate a large number of candidate itemsets, which can be memory-intensive.

In summary, the Apriori algorithm is a widely used algorithm for mining frequent itemsets. It uses a bottom-up approach and the anti-monotonicity property to prune the search space of candidate itemsets. Despite its limitations, the Apriori algorithm remains an important tool in the field of data analytics.

### MapReduce

MapReduce is a programming model and an associated implementation for processing and generating large data sets. It is a framework for distributed computing on large datasets on clusters of computers. The framework is divided into two parts: Map and Reduce.

1. **Map**: The Map function takes an input pair and produces a set of intermediate key/value pairs. The MapReduce library groups together all intermediate values associated with the same intermediate key and passes them to the Reduce function.

2. **Reduce**: The Reduce function accepts an intermediate key and a set of values for that key. It merges together these values to form a possibly smaller set of values. The intermediate values are supplied to the user's reduce function via an iterator.

MapReduce allows for distributed processing of the map and reduction operations. The framework takes care of scheduling tasks, monitoring them and re-executing the failed tasks. The framework also manages the inter-node communication, data transfer and partitioning.

MapReduce is widely used for big data processing, such as data mining, data analysis, and machine learning. It is also used for processing large datasets for search engines, log analysis, and recommendation systems.

MapReduce is a powerful tool for processing large datasets, but it is not suitable for all types of data processing tasks. It is best suited for batch processing of large, unstructured datasets. It is not well suited for real-time processing or for processing small datasets.

MapReduce is a key component of the Apache Hadoop ecosystem, which is an open-source framework for distributed storage and processing of large datasets. Hadoop MapReduce is a specific implementation of the MapReduce programming model. Other implementations of MapReduce are also available, such as Apache Spark and Apache Flink.

### Sharding

Sharding is a type of database partitioning that separates large databases into smaller, faster, more easily managed parts called data shards . It involves splitting and distributing one logical data set across multiple databases that share nothing and can be deployed across multiple servers . To achieve sharding, the rows or columns of a larger database table are split into multiple smaller tables. Once a logical shard is stored on another node, it is known as a physical shard .

#### Advantages of Sharding
- Increased read/write throughput: By distributing the dataset across multiple shards, both read and write operations can be performed faster .
- Increased storage capacity: By increasing the number of shards, you can also increase overall total storage capacity .
- High availability: Shards can provide high availability .

#### Applications of Sharding
- Big Data Analytics: When you have terabytes of data, sharding means you don't have to warehouse data to do analytics on it. With up to 1000 shards in capacity, Oracle Sharding can turn a relational database into a warehouse-sized data store .

5. **Geospatial Visualization**: Geospatial visualization techniques are used to represent data that has a geographical component. These techniques can include the use of maps, globes, and other geographical representations to show the distribution of data over a particular area.

### Attribute and Data Types

In the context of data analytics, an attribute refers to a characteristic or feature of a data set. Attributes can be thought of as columns in a table, where each row represents an observation and each column represents an attribute of that observation.

Data types refer to the type of data that can be stored in an attribute. Common data types include:

1. **Numeric**: Numeric data types include integers and floating-point numbers. These data types are used to represent quantitative data, such as height, weight, and age.

2. **Categorical**: Categorical data types include nominal and ordinal data. Nominal data represents categories with no inherent order, such as gender or eye color. Ordinal data represents categories with an inherent order, such as education level or income bracket.

3. **Text**: Text data types include strings and characters. These data types are used to represent textual data, such as names, addresses, and descriptions.

4. **Date/Time**: Date and time data types are used to represent dates and times. These data types can be used to represent temporal data, such as the date of birth or the time of a transaction.

5. **Boolean**: Boolean data types represent true or false values. These data types can be used to represent binary data, such as whether a customer made a purchase or not.

It is important to correctly identify the data type of each attribute in a data set, as this will affect the choice of analysis methods and visualization techniques. For example, numeric data can be visualized using histograms and box plots, while categorical data can be visualized using bar charts and pie charts.



### Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with the collection, analysis, interpretation, presentation, and organization of data. It is used to summarize and describe the main features of a dataset, either through numerical calculations or graphical representations.

Here are some key points to remember about descriptive statistics:

1. Descriptive statistics is used to summarize and describe data, not to make inferences or predictions about a population.
2. Common measures of central tendency include the mean, median, and mode.
3. Measures of variability or dispersion include the range, variance, and standard deviation.
4. Graphical representations of data, such as histograms, box plots, and scatter plots, can also be used to describe data.
5. Descriptive statistics can be used to identify patterns and trends in data, but it cannot be used to make conclusions about cause and effect relationships.

Descriptive statistics is an important tool in data analysis and is often used in conjunction with inferential statistics to draw conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. It is a crucial part of the data analytics process and is covered in Unit 6 - Frame Works and Visualization in the subject of Data Analytics.



### Exploratory Data Analysis

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is an approach to analyzing data sets to summarize their main characteristics, often with visual methods. It is used to understand the data, discover patterns, relationships, or anomalies, and to formulate hypotheses that could lead to new data collection and experiments.

Here are some key points to remember when performing EDA:

1. EDA is an iterative process. It involves generating questions about the data, exploring the data to find answers, and then refining the questions based on the insights gained.

2. Visualization is a powerful tool in EDA. Graphical displays such as histograms, box plots, scatter plots, and heat maps can help reveal patterns and relationships in the data that may not be apparent from summary statistics alone.

3. EDA is not a one-size-fits-all approach. The methods used will depend on the nature of the data and the questions being asked. For example, different techniques may be needed to explore categorical data versus continuous data.

4. EDA can help identify outliers or anomalous data points. These may be errors in the data that need to be corrected, or they may be interesting observations that warrant further investigation.

5. EDA can also help identify missing or incomplete data. This can inform decisions about how to handle missing data, such as imputing values or excluding observations from the analysis.

6. EDA is not a substitute for formal statistical analysis. It is a preliminary step that can help guide the choice of appropriate statistical methods and models.

In summary, EDA is a crucial step in the data analysis process. It can help uncover insights and guide further analysis, ultimately leading to better decision-making.
