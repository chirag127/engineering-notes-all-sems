### Stages of State Formation in Ancient India

1. State formation is linked with the growth and spread of agriculture, trade, industry, and urbanization. In ancient India, this process began with the spread of agriculture along river valleys. The agricultural surplus generated led to trade, commerce, and urbanization. The first states (Janapadas) came about in these river valleys.

2. The pattern of state formation was replicated in the South and East of India.

3. In ancient India, the kingdom (rajya) was constituted of seven elements (sapta-prakrtayah) or seven limbs (sapta-anga). According to Manu-Smriti (1-2 century AD), these were: King (swamin), Minister (amatya), City (pura), Domain/territory (rastra), Treasury (kosa), Army (danda), and Ally (suhrd/mitra).

4. In the course of the evolution of the state in Indian history, monarchies gradually conquered the republics in ancient India. In ancient, medieval, and modern India until the British Raj, monarchies remained the mode of government without any revival of republicanism. Republican form could be revived only after India's Independence in 1947.

5. The establishment of the Delhi Sultanate in a.d. 1206 changed the political landscape of northern India, the strategically placed capital of Delhi becoming the new seat of central power. India's next imperial unification, under the Great Mughals, from Babur to Akbar (a.d. 1526–1605).

### Āshrama or the Stages of Life

Āshrama is a term used in Hinduism to describe the four stages of life. These stages are:

1. **Brahmacharya** (Student): This stage is focused on education and learning. During this stage, the individual is expected to remain celibate and focus on acquiring knowledge and developing character.

2. **Grihastha** (Householder): This stage is focused on building a family and fulfilling one's duties to society. During this stage, the individual is expected to marry, raise children, and contribute to the community.

3. **Vanaprastha** (Hermit or Forest-dweller): This stage is focused on spiritual pursuits and detachment from worldly concerns. During this stage, the individual is expected to gradually withdraw from society and focus on meditation and contemplation.

4. **Sannyasa** (Renunciate): This stage is focused on renunciation and the pursuit of spiritual liberation. During this stage, the individual is expected to renounce all worldly attachments and live a life of simplicity and spiritual devotion.

These stages are not strictly followed by all Hindus and may vary depending on individual circumstances and personal beliefs. However, they provide a general framework for understanding the progression of life in Hindu society.

### Harappan Script

- The Harappan Script, also known as the Indus Script, is a corpus of symbols produced by the Indus Valley Civilisation.
- Most inscriptions containing these symbols are extremely short, making it difficult to judge whether or not they constituted a writing system used to record the as-yet unidentified language(s) of the Indus Valley Civilisation.
- The script found in the Indus Valley from Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro is the oldest known script of India. Unfortunately, till now it has not been read satisfactorily. This has made the problem of the Indus Valley script even more intractable.
- The Harappan script has long defied attempts to read it, and therefore the language remains unknown.
- The Indus Script combined both word signs and symbols with phonetic value. This type of writing system is known as "logo-syllabic", where some symbols express ideas or words while others represent sounds. This view is based on the fact that roughly 400 signs have been identified, which makes it unlikely that the Indus Script was solely phonetic.



### Brahmi Script

- The Brahmi script is the earliest writing system developed in India after the Indus script .
- It is one of the most influential writing systems; all modern Indian scripts and several hundred scripts found in Southeast and East Asia are derived from Brahmi .
- The Brahmi script is mentioned in the ancient Indian texts of Hinduism, Jainism, and Buddhism, as well as their Chinese translations .
- For example, the 10th chapter of the Lalitavistara Sūtra (c. 200-300 CE), titled the Lipisala samdarshana parivarta, lists 64 lipi (scripts), with the Brahmi script starting the list .
- Brahmi appeared as a fully developed script in the third century BCE .
- Its descendants, the Brahmic scripts, continue to be used today across South and Southeast Asia .
- The Brahmi writing system, or script, appeared as a fully developed universal one in South Asia in the third century BCE .
- It is a forerunner of all writing systems that have found use in South Asia with the exception of the Indus script of the third millennium BCE, the Kharosthi script, which originated in what today is northwestern Pakistan in the fourth or possibly fifth century BCE, the Perso-Arabic Scripts of the medieval period, and the Latin scripts of the modern period .
- The Brahmi script was already divided into regional variants at the time of the earliest surviving epigraphy around the 3rd century BC .
- Cursives of the Brahmi script began to diversify further from around the 5th century AD and continued to give rise to new scripts throughout the Middle Ages .
- The Brāhmī alphabet is the ancestor of most of the 40 or so modern Indian alphabets, Khmer, and Tibetan .
- It is thought to have been modeled on the Aramaic or Phoenician alphabets and appeared in India sometime before 500 BC .
- Another theory is that Brāhmī developed from the Indus or Harappa script, which was used in the Indus valley until around 1300 BC .



### The Vedas

The Vedas are a large body of religious texts originating in ancient India. Composed in Vedic Sanskrit, the texts constitute the oldest layer of Sanskrit literature and the oldest scriptures of Hinduism. The Vedas date back to 1600 BC, and there is no specific date to validate the composition of the Hindu scriptures, as they were transmitted from generation to generation by oral tradition throughout the centuries.

The Vedas comprise a vast corpus of Sanskrit poetry, philosophical dialogue, myth, and ritual incantations developed and composed by Aryans over 3,500 years ago. Regarded by Hindus as the primary source of knowledge and the sacred foundation of their religion, the Vedas embody one of the world’s oldest surviving cultural traditions .

The word Veda means wisdom, knowledge or vision, and it serves to manifest the language of the gods in human speech. The laws of the Vedas have regulated the social, legal, domestic and religious customs of Hindus up to the present day. All the obligatory duties of Hindus at birth, marriage, death etc. are guided by Vedic rituals.

### Harappan Technologies

The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley Civilization, was known for its advancements in various fields of science and technology. Some of the key technologies used by the Harappan people include:

1. **Metallurgy**: The Harappan civilization is referred to as a Bronze Age civilization. They used copper, bronze, lead, and tin in their metallurgy. Copper and bronze vessels were among their finest products, formed by hammering sheets of metal. Casting of copper and bronze was understood, and figurines of men and animals were made by the lost-wax process.

2. **Standardized Weights and Measures**: The Harappan civilization developed the first accurate system of standardized weights and measures, some as accurate as to 1.6 mm.

3. **Seal Carving**: Harappans are known for seal carving — the cutting of patterns into the bottom face of a seal, a small, carved object used for stamping. They used these distinctive seals for the identification of property and to stamp clay on trade goods.

4. **Handicrafts**: The Harappan people also used new techniques in handicrafts, such as terracotta, metal, and stone to create sculpture, seals, pottery, and jewelry.

5. **Agriculture**: The Harappan civilization developed gradually from earlier Neolithic village cultures. It is believed that the better technology to exploit the fertile plains of the river Indus might have resulted in increased agricultural production. This led to the production of larger surplus to feed and maintain non-agricultural people such as artisans, administrators, etc. It also helped in the promotion of exchange or trading contacts with distant regions. It brought prosperity to the Harappan people and they were able to set up cities.

These are some of the key technologies used by the Harappan civilization. Their advancements in these fields have contributed significantly to the development of the civilization and its prosperity.

Martial arts are codified systems and traditions of combat practices, which are practised for a variety of reasons – self-defence, competition, physical health and fitness, entertainment, as well as mental, physical, and spiritual development. India has an ancient tradition in diverse martial arts.

- **Kalaripayattu**: Often known as the ‘Mother Of All Martial Arts’, this 3000-year old art form originating from Kerala, draws inspiration from the raw power, swift movements and sinuous strength of majestic animals – the lion, tiger, elephant, wild boar, snake and crocodile.

- **Khusti**: The Indian Wrestling is also a part of Indian Martial arts found throughout the India.

- **Thang-Ta**: The ancient Manipuri Martial Art, also known as Huyen Lallong, is Thang-Ta. Swords and spears are used in Manipuri Martial arts, a powerful yet gracefully complex technique.

- **Lathi**: Lathi is India’s oldest armed martial art. It is also regarded as one of the world’s earliest martial arts weapons.




### Fairs and Festivals

India is a land of diverse cultures and traditions, and this diversity is reflected in the numerous fairs and festivals celebrated across the country. These events are an integral part of Indian culture and provide a platform for people to come together and celebrate their shared heritage.

1. **Diwali**: Also known as the festival of lights, Diwali is one of the most popular festivals in India. It is celebrated to mark the victory of good over evil and is observed by lighting lamps and bursting firecrackers.

2. **Holi**: Holi is a festival of colors that marks the arrival of spring. People celebrate by smearing each other with colored powder and water.

3. **Dussehra**: Dussehra is a festival that celebrates the victory of Lord Rama over the demon king Ravana. It is observed by burning effigies of Ravana and organizing processions.

4. **Pongal**: Pongal is a harvest festival celebrated in the southern state of Tamil Nadu. It is a four-day festival that marks the end of the harvest season and the beginning of the new agricultural cycle.

5. **Onam**: Onam is a harvest festival celebrated in the southern state of Kerala. It is a ten-day festival that marks the homecoming of the mythical King Mahabali.

These are just a few examples of the many fairs and festivals celebrated in India. Each event is unique and provides a glimpse into the rich cultural heritage of the country. They are an important part of Indian society and play a significant role in preserving and promoting traditional art forms and customs.



### Current developments in Arts and Cultural Heritage in India

- Recently, three cities in India have joined the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities. These include two cities Thrissur and Nilambur in Kerala and Warangal in Telangana .
- India is actively sharing its art and cultural heritage with the rest of the world through Indian Festivals abroad .
- The National Mission on Cultural Mapping and Roadmap focuses on promoting ‘Our Culture Our Identity’ through media and events. It adopts a web-based integrated approach, aiming to target 6.40 lakh villages in 3 years .
- Art Deco Mumbai is in the process of cataloguing the city’s art deco structures and has so far identified 374 .
- A third of India’s 29 cultural World Heritage sites are in cities. These include the Taj Mahal, Humayun’s Tomb in Delhi and Jantar Mantar in Jaipur .
- India has one of the world’s largest collections of songs, music, dance, theatre, folk traditions, performing arts, rites and rituals, paintings and writings that are known, as the ‘Intangible Cultural Heritage’ (ICH) of humanity .
- Reliance Foundation is constantly reimagining ways to protect and promote Indian Arts, Culture & Heritage with the objective to preserve India’s rich heritage of art and culture as well as enhance avenues for livelihood of traditional artisans and crafts persons .
