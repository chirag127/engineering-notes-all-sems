## Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS

1. Virtual Reality (VR) - An artificial environment that is created with software and presented to the user in a very immersive way through headsets or multi-projected environments. The user experiences and interacts with this artificially created world.

2. Augmented Reality (AR) - An enhanced version of the real world where digital information is overlaid on the real world via devices like smart glasses or mobile AR apps. The user sees and interacts with the real world with additional digital information and media.

3. Mixed Reality (MR) - Combines the real and virtual worlds to produce new environments and visualizations where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time. MR incorporates elements of both AR and VR to create a sense of immersion and realism.

4. Virtual Environments - Simulated experiences which are made possible through hardware and software technologies. The user can navigate, interact with and manipulate a computer generated simulated environment. Data can be collected to study human behavior, reasoning and performance.

### Real-time computer graphics for the notes of the Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

1. Real-time computer graphics involves generating images in a fraction of a second, which is necessary for interactive applications such as virtual reality.
2. The key aspects of real-time computer graphics are:
- Fast algorithms: Algorithms must be efficient and optimized for speed.
- Limited computing resources: There are constraints on memory, processing power, and bandwidth.
- Trade-offs: Accurate physical modeling and high-quality images may need to be sacrificed for interactivity and performance.
3. Challenges in real-time computer graphics include:
- Vast amounts of data: The details in graphics data can be enormous. This data must be managed and processed efficiently.
- Complex computations: Techniques like shading, lighting, and transformations involve heavy computations. These must be optimized.
- Parallelism: Graphics processing can be parallelized across multiple cores/processors, but this introduces programming complexity.
4. Common techniques for real-time computer graphics include:
- Level of detail: Simpler models/textures are used for objects that are farther away.
- Culling: Off-screen objects are not drawn, thereby reducing workload.
- Shaders: Specialized programs that run on the GPU to handle shading, lighting, etc.
- Texture mapping: Images are "wrapped around" 3D models to add surface details.
- Shadows, reflections, etc.: Additional effects can be added, with associated performance costs.

### Flight simulation for the notes of the Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Flight simulation is a virtual recreation of the experience of flying an aircraft. It is used for training pilots.
- It provides a realistic virtual environment and simulated aircraft controls for the trainee pilot to learn how to handle different flying conditions and situations.
- The key components of a flight simulator are:
-- A motion system to provide the feeling of motion and forces as experienced in a real aircraft.
-- A visual system to provide an out-of-the-cockpit view for the trainee pilot. This could include multiple projection screens or VR headsets to simulate the view.
-- A control loading system to simulate the forces required to move the aircraft control surfaces like ailerons, elevators, rudder, etc. The controls are designed to "feel" like real aircraft controls.
-- A computing system to simulate the dynamics of flight and the responses to the trainee pilot's control inputs. It keeps track of the simulated aircraft state and generates responses to control inputs.
- The high fidelity of modern flight simulators allows pilots to train for complex real-world situations in a safe virtual environment. This enables them to gain experience without risk to passengers or equipment. Simulators save costs and time compared to real aircraft training.
- The technology and components used in flight simulators serve as a key example of virtual environments and their applications. The realistic simulations would not be possible without technological developments in computing, visuals, motion control, and control loading systems.

- Magnetic tracking: Uses magnetic fields to track the position and orientation of objects or body parts. Magnetic trackers are robust to occlusion but susceptible to magnetic interference.
- Optical tracking: Uses cameras to track the position and orientation of objects or body parts. Optical trackers do not suffer from magnetic interference but can have issues with occlusion.
- Ultrasonic tracking: Uses ultrasonic signals to measure distances and compute the position and orientation of objects. Ultrasonic trackers are typically less accurate than magnetic or optical trackers but are inexpensive and do not require a line of sight.

### Tracking Devices for the notes of the Unit 3 - 3D USER INTERFACE INPUT HARDWARE in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

1. Optical Tracking: Uses cameras to track the position and orientation of objects in the real world. markers or distinct patterns are placed on the objects to be tracked. 2. Magnetic Tracking: Uses magnetic fields to track the position and orientation of objects. Magnetic sensors are placed on the objects to detect the magnetic field and the changing magnetic field is used to determine the motion of the objects. Eg: Polhemus magnetic trackers

3. Mechanical Tracking: Uses physical linkages and joints to track the motion and determine the position and orientation. Eg: Data gloves that have fiber optic sensors to track finger motions.

4. Inertial Tracking: Uses accelerometers and gyroscopes to track the motion, velocity, orientation, and position of objects. The accelerometers measure acceleration and gyroscopes measure rotation to determine the movement. Eg: Motion tracking in smartphones, controllers

5. Ultrasonic Tracking: Uses high-frequency sound pulses to track the position of objects. The time taken for the sound pulses to reflect off the objects and return is used to calculate the distance which is then used to determine the position of the objects. Eg: Batman's sonar in movies

The tracking devices are essential inputs for 3D user interfaces to allow users to interact with the augmented or virtual environments. The selection of a appropriate tracking technology depends on the application requirements like accuracy, cost, latency, workspace size, etc.

It consists of fiber optic sensors, inertial measurement units, and flex sensors that are embedded into the fabric of the glove. It typically consists of inertial measurement units, flex sensors, and markers or sensors that can be tracked by external optical systems. The suit allows the user's full-body motion to be captured and relayed to a connected system. However, motion capture suits tend to be very expensive, need to be calibrated to each user, and can be intrusive and uncomfortable to wear.

- Position refers to the location of an object in a 3D space. It is defined by X, Y and Z coordinates.

### Bounding Volume

- Bounding volume is a volume that encloses a 3D object/model. It is used to determine if two objects are intersecting or overlapping.
- It is a key concept in collision detection which is important for simulations and games.
- The bounding volume can be either axis-aligned (AABB) or oriented (OBB).
- Axis-aligned bounding boxes (AABB) are commonly used as they are simple to calculate and test for intersection. However, they are not tight-fitting for most models and lead to more intersection tests than needed.
- Oriented bounding boxes (OBB) provide a tighter fit and more accurate overlap tests but are more complex to calculate and test.
- Hierarchical bounding volumes use a tree-like structure to enclose an object in volumes of different sizes at different levels of the hierarchy. This speeds up collision detection by testing for intersection at a coarse level first before proceeding to finer levels.
- Bounding spheres and bounding cylinders are other simple volume primitives used for collision detection. They are easier to test for intersection than AABBs/OBBs but are looser-fitting.

### Level of Details (LODs)

1. LOD0 - Simple shape representation: At this level, an object is represented as a simple shape like cube, sphere, cylinder etc. This level is used when the object is far away from the user.

3. LOD2 - Detailed mesh: At this level, the object is represented with more details using a detailed mesh. More features are clearly visible. This level is used when the object is closer to the user.
4. LOD3 - High resolution mesh: At this level, the object is represented with the highest level of details using a high resolution mesh. All the minor details are clearly visible. This level is used when the user is very close to the object.

The different LODs are used to optimize the performance. As the object moves away from the user, lower LOD is used. As the object moves closer to the user, higher LOD is used. This avoids rendering of unnecessary details and improves the performance. LODs are widely used in augmented reality and virtual reality applications.

### Design Guidelines - 3D Travel Tasks

3. Use landmarks or markers: Include memorable landmarks, markers, or other reference points along the path. These help give the user a sense of progress and orientation. However, be careful not to include too many reference points, which can also lead to confusion.

4. Control the speed: Give the user control over the speed and pace of the travel. Forcing the user to move at a fixed speed that is too fast or too slow can create a poor user experience. Allow the user to navigate at their own comfortable speed.

5. Limit distractions: Minimize additional objects, information, or tasks that could distract the user from the core travel task. Too many distractions can disrupt the user's focus and make the experience feel cluttered or disjointed. Keep the travel task as the central focus.

6. Provide feedback: Continually provide feedback to the user on their progress, location, and orientation. Feedback helps the user understand where they are in the journey and builds confidence that they are navigating correctly. ### Technology and features of augmented reality

1. Marker-based AR: Uses special markers or codes to trigger the AR experience. The device camera detects the marker and displays the AR elements. The markers can be images, QR codes, etc. The AR elements are aligned with the marker and placed in the real world.

3. Projection-based AR: Projects AR elements onto surfaces in the real world instead of displaying them on a device screen. Uses projectors and depth sensors to detect and track surfaces. The projections can serve as interfaces or to create illusion or ambiance.

No special markers are required but the core technology must be capable of robust detection and tracking of real-world features for accurate superimposition and alignment of AR elements.

### Augmented reality methods for the notes of the Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality

### Marker-less Tracking for Augmented Reality

- Marker-less tracking uses computer vision and image processing techniques to track objects, surfaces or people in the real world without the use of fiducial markers.
- Some of the popular marker-less tracking methods are:

1. Feature Tracking: Distinctive features like corners or edges of objects are tracked in consecutive frames to estimate pose or motion. Eg: Tracking corners of tables or doors.
2. Contour Tracking: The outer contours or shapes of objects are tracked. Eg: Tracking the shape of a car or a human body.
3. Model-based Tracking: A 3D model of the object is matched to the image/video frames and the pose is estimated. Eg: Tracking a known object like a toy car by matching its 3D model to the frames.
4. Learning-based Tracking: Machine learning techniques are used to train a system on large amounts of data to detect and track objects. Eg: Tracking humans using pose estimators trained on large datasets of human images/videos.

- Marker-less tracking is more flexible and enables tracking of unprepared real-world objects. However, it is more computationally expensive and prone to tracking errors/drift due to occlusion, clutter, varying lighting etc. Hence, a combination of marker-based and marker-less tracking is often used to get the best results for AR applications.
