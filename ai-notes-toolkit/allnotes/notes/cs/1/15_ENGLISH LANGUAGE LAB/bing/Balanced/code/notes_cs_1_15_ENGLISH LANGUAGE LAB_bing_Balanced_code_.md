- An English language lab is a facility where learners can practice and improve their language skills using computer software and audio-visual materials.
- The main objectives of an English language lab are to:
  - Enhance the learners' listening, speaking, reading and writing skills.
  - Provide authentic and interactive input for language acquisition.
  - Develop the learners' confidence and fluency in using English.
  - Expose the learners to different varieties and registers of English.
  - Foster the learners' autonomy and self-assessment.
- The main components of an English language lab are:
  - A teacher console that controls the activities and monitors the learners' progress.
  - A number of student stations that are equipped with headphones, microphones and keyboards.
  - A network that connects the teacher console and the student stations.
  - A software that provides the content and the feedback for the language exercises.
  - A projector and a screen that display the instructions and the multimedia materials.
- The main types of activities that can be done in an English language lab are:
  - Listening comprehension: The learners listen to audio or video clips and answer questions or complete tasks based on the input.
  - Pronunciation practice: The learners record their own voice and compare it with the model pronunciation or receive feedback from the software or the teacher.
  - Speaking practice: The learners engage in dialogues, role-plays, presentations or discussions with the software, the teacher or other learners.
  - Reading comprehension: The learners read texts and answer questions or complete tasks based on the input.
  - Writing practice: The learners write texts and receive feedback from the software, the teacher or other learners.
  - Grammar and vocabulary practice: The learners do exercises that test and reinforce their knowledge of grammar and vocabulary.
  - Language games: The learners play games that are designed to make language learning fun and motivating.

### 4. Presentation Skills for Technical Paper/Project Reports/ proposals based on proper Stress and Intonation Mechanics

- A technical presentation is a form of communication that aims to convey technical information to a specific audience, such as experts, managers, clients, or general public.
- A technical presentation should be clear, concise, accurate, and engaging, and should follow a logical structure that guides the audience through the main points and objectives of the paper, project, or proposal.
- A technical presentation should also use proper stress and intonation mechanics to enhance the delivery and impact of the message, and to avoid confusion or misunderstanding.
- Stress and intonation are the variations in loudness, pitch, and length of syllables and words in spoken language, which convey meaning, emphasis, attitude, and emotion.
- Stress and intonation mechanics can be divided into three main categories: word stress, sentence stress, and intonation patterns.

#### Word stress
- Word stress is the relative prominence given to certain syllables in a word, usually by making them louder, higher in pitch, or longer in duration.
- Word stress can affect the meaning and pronunciation of words, especially in English, where many words have different stress patterns depending on their part of speech or function in a sentence.
- For example, the word "record" can be a noun or a verb, but the stress is different: REcord (noun) vs. reCORD (verb).
- Word stress can also help the audience to identify and remember key terms and concepts in a technical presentation, by highlighting them with more stress.
- For example, in the sentence "The main objective of this project is to develop a new algorithm for image segmentation", the words "objective", "project", "algorithm", and "segmentation" are stressed, as they are the most important words for the topic.
- To stress a word, the speaker should increase the volume, pitch, and length of the stressed syllable, and reduce the volume, pitch, and length of the unstressed syllables.

#### Sentence stress
- Sentence stress is the relative prominence given to certain words in a sentence, usually by making them louder, higher in pitch, or longer in duration.
- Sentence stress can affect the meaning and structure of sentences, especially in English, where many words have different functions and roles depending on their position and context in a sentence.
- For example, the word "only" can modify different parts of a sentence, depending on where it is placed: "Only I saw him" vs. "I only saw him" vs. "I saw only him".
- Sentence stress can also help the audience to follow and understand the main ideas and arguments in a technical presentation, by highlighting them with more stress.
- For example, in the sentence "This method is faster, cheaper, and more accurate than the previous one", the words "faster", "cheaper", and "more accurate" are stressed, as they are the main points of comparison and contrast.
- To stress a word in a sentence, the speaker should increase the volume, pitch, and length of the stressed word, and reduce the volume, pitch, and length of the unstressed words.
