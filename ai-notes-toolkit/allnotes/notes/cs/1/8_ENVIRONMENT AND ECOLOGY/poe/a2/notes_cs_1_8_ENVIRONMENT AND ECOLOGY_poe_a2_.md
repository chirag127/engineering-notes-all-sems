## Unit 1 - Environment:

- Definition: Surrounding in which living organisms exist or operate.
- Types of Environment: Natural environment and Built environment.
- Components of environment: Atmosphere, Hydrosphere, Lithosphere, Biosphere.
- Segments of environment: Physical, Biological and Social environment.
- Scope and importance: Environment provides resources necessary for life. Pollution free environment is necessary for healthy living.
- Need for Public Awareness: To conserve natural resources and maintain ecological balance.

## Ecosystem:

- Definition: Community of organisms and their physical environment interacting as a system.
- Types of ecosystem: Natural ecosystem and Artificial ecosystem.
- Structure of ecosystem: Biotic and Abiotic components.
- Food Chain, Food Web, Ecological pyramid: To show flow of energy in an ecosystem.
- Balance Ecosystem: Where there is equilibrium between input and output of energy.

## Effects of Human Activities:

- Food, Shelter, Housing, Agriculture, Industry, Mining, Transportation, Economic and Social security activities impact environment in both positive and negative ways.
- Environmental Impact Assessment: To evaluate effects of activities on environment and take remedial measures.
- Sustainable Development: Meeting needs of present without compromising ability of future generations to meet their needs.

## Unit 2 - Natural Resources: Introduction, Classification

### Water Resources
- Availability: Freshwater available in rivers, lakes, glaciers, groundwater aquifers etc.
- Sources: Precipitation, surface runoff, groundwater recharge
- Quality aspects: pH, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, colour, odour, hardness, salinity, contaminants
- Water borne and water induced diseases: Cholera, dysentery, jaundice, typhoid etc. due to microbial contamination
- Fluoride and arsenic problems in drinking water: Excess fluoride causes fluorosis, arsenic causes arsenicosis which are hazardous to health

### Mineral Resources
- Material cycles: Carbon, nitrogen, sulfur cycles which are interconnected and essential for life
- Non-renewable minerals: Metals like iron, bauxite, copper etc. and non-metals like coal, petroleum, natural gas etc. which are being rapidly depleted due to human activities

### Energy Resources
- Conventional sources: Fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas), hydro and nuclear power
- Non-conventional sources: Solar, wind, tidal, geothermal, biomass which are renewable and eco-friendly but currently have limitations in harnessing energy from them

### Forest Resources
- Availability: Tropical rainforests have rich biodiversity but are being rapidly depleted; temperate forests have coniferous or deciduous trees
- Depletion of forests: Due to human activities like deforestation, mining, urbanization leading to loss of livelihood for tribes, reduced rainfall, soil erosion and loss of biodiversity
- Impact of forest depletion on society: Disruption of water cycles and increased flooding/droughts, increased air pollution and greenhouse gases leading to global warming, loss of valuable timber, medicinal plants and other resources

## Unit 3 - Pollution and their Effects; Public Health Aspects of Environmental; Water Pollution, Air Pollution, Soil Pollution, Noise Pollution, Solid waste management.

### Water Pollution

- discharge of untreated sewage and industrial waste into water bodies.
- effects: harmful chemicals enter food chain, diseases spread, loss of biodiversity.
- solutions: treat sewage and waste before disposing, curb dumping of waste, conserve water.

### Air Pollution

- emission of harmful gases and particles from vehicles and industries.
- effects: respiratory diseases, global warming, acid rain, reduced visibility.
- solutions: use of cleaner fuels, afforestation, public transport, emission controls.

### Soil Pollution

- excessive use of fertilizers and pesticides kills useful microorganisms.
- effects: loss of soil fertility, entry of chemicals into food chain.
- solutions: organic farming, waste management, bioremediation.

### Noise Pollution

- excessive noise from vehicles, machines, etc.
- effects: hearing loss, stress, sleep disturbance, reduced productivity.
- solutions: use of noise absorbing materials, minimise use of loud devices, traffic rules.

### Solid Waste Management

- excessive garbage generation and improper disposal.
- effects: spread of diseases, clogs drains and water bodies, loss of land space.
- solutions: segregate waste, composting, recycling, incineration, landfills.

## Unit 4 - Current Environmental Issues of Importance

### Global Warming
- Rise in average temperature of Earth's atmosphere and oceans.
- Caused due to greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide and methane trapping heat in the atmosphere.
- Leads to melting of glaciers, rise in sea level, changing weather patterns and more extreme weather events.

### Greenhouse Effect
- Certain gases in the atmosphere like carbon dioxide trap the heat from the sun and cause the temperature at the Earth's surface and lower atmosphere to rise.
- Natural greenhouse effect is essential to make the Earth's climate suitable for life. However, human activities like burning fossil fuels have caused enhanced greenhouse effect leading to global warming.

### Climate Change
- Long-term changes in average weather patterns across the globe.
- Can lead to both warming and cooling of the planet. Currently, climate change refers to the ongoing warming of the planet due to greenhouse effect.
- Effects include rising sea levels, melting glaciers, heavier precipitation, droughts, heatwaves, etc. which can have serious effects on human society and natural ecosystems.

## Unit 5 - Environmental Protection

### Environmental Protection Act 1986
- The Environment Protection Act 1986 is an important act passed by the Parliament of India to protect the environment.
- It provides the framework for waste management and pollution control.
- It emphasized on reducing pollution from industrial and municipal sources, protecting wildlife and biodiversity.
- It created authorities at central and state level to tackle environmental issues.

### Initiatives by Non Governmental Organizations (NGO’s)
- NGOs play an important role in creating environmental awareness and taking initiatives for conservation and protection of environment.
- Some important NGOs are:
-- Greenpeace India: It advocates for protecting forests, biodiversity and tackling climate change issues.
-- Centre for Science and Environment (CSE): It researches on environmental issues and runs campaigns to tackle pollution and create environmental awareness.
-- Wildlife Trust of India (WTI): It works towards conservation of wildlife and its habitats across India.
