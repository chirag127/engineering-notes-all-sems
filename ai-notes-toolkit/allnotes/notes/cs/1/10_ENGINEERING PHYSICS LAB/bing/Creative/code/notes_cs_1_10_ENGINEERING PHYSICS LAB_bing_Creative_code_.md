An experiment is a scientific procedure that aims to test a hypothesis, answer a question, or demonstrate a known fact. Experiments usually involve manipulating one or more variables and measuring their effects on other variables.

Some examples of experiments are:

- **The Milgram experiment**: This experiment was conducted by Stanley Milgram in the 1960s to study the obedience of people to authority figures. The participants were instructed to administer electric shocks to a learner (who was actually an actor) whenever they answered a question incorrectly. The shocks increased in intensity with each wrong answer, and the participants were urged to continue by the experimenter. The experiment revealed that most people were willing to inflict pain on another person if ordered by an authority figure.
- **The Stanford prison experiment**: This experiment was conducted by Philip Zimbardo in 1971 to study the effects of social roles on human behavior. The participants were randomly assigned to be either guards or prisoners in a simulated prison environment. The experiment was supposed to last for two weeks, but it was terminated after six days due to the extreme psychological distress and abuse that occurred. The experiment showed that people can quickly adopt the behaviors and attitudes of their assigned roles, regardless of their personality or morality.
- **The Pavlov's dog experiment**: This experiment was conducted by Ivan Pavlov in the 1890s to study the phenomenon of classical conditioning. Pavlov trained dogs to salivate at the sound of a bell by pairing it with food. He then tested whether the dogs would salivate at the bell alone, without the food. He found that they did, demonstrating that a neutral stimulus can become associated with a conditioned response through repeated pairing.
- **The Asch conformity experiment**: This experiment was conducted by Solomon Asch in the 1950s to study the influence of social pressure on individual judgment. The participants were asked to compare the lengths of different lines and give their answers aloud in a group. The group consisted of confederates who gave incorrect answers on purpose. The experiment measured how many participants would conform to the majority opinion, even when it was clearly wrong. The experiment found that about 75% of the participants conformed at least once, indicating that people tend to conform to social norms rather than trust their own perception.
- **The marshmallow experiment**: This experiment was conducted by Walter Mischel in the 1960s and 1970s to study the development of self-control and delayed gratification. The participants were children who were offered a choice between a small reward (such as a marshmallow) that they could have immediately, or a larger reward (such as two marshmallows) that they could have after waiting for some time. The experiment tracked how long the children could resist the temptation of the immediate reward, and how this ability correlated with their later outcomes in life. The experiment found that the children who could wait longer for the larger reward tended to have better academic, social, and health outcomes than those who could not.

##### Transmission Grating

A transmission grating is a type of diffraction grating that transmits light rather than reflecting it. Some features and applications of transmission gratings are:

- Transmission gratings have low polarization sensitivity, unlike reflection gratings, because the light is not reflected by a metallic coating.
- Transmission gratings can be used in compact, in-line configurations, because the light does not need to be folded back by a mirror.

- Transmission gratings can achieve high diffraction efficiency and low wavefront distortion by using precise lithographic fabrication techniques.

## Theory
The coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is a measure of its resistance to flow. It is defined as the ratio of the shear stress to the rate of shear strain in the liquid.

- The weight of the body, W = mg, acting downwards, where m is the mass of the body and g is the acceleration due to gravity.
- The upthrust or buoyant force, U = Vσg, acting upwards, where V is the volume of the body.
- The viscous drag force, F = 6πηrv, acting upwards, where v is the velocity of the body relative to the liquid.
