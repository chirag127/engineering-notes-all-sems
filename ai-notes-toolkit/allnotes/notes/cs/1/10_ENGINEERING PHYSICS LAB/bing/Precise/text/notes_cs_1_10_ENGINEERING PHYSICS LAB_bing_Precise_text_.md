##### 1. To determine the wavelength of sodium light by Newton’s ring experiment.

- Newton's ring experiment is a method to determine the wavelength of light using the phenomenon of interference.
- In this experiment, a plano-convex lens is placed on a flat glass plate, and a monochromatic light source, such as sodium light, is used to illuminate the setup.
- The light waves reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of the air film between the lens and the glass plate interfere with each other, producing a pattern of bright and dark rings known as Newton's rings.
- The radius of the rings can be measured using a traveling microscope, and the wavelength of the light can be calculated using the formula: `λ = (Dn^2 - Dn-1^2) / 4R`, where `λ` is the wavelength of the light, `Dn` is the diameter of the nth dark ring, `R` is the radius of curvature of the lens, and `n` is the order of the ring.
- This experiment is named after Sir Isaac Newton, who first described the phenomenon in his book "Opticks" in 1704.

5. **Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR):** This technique is used to determine the chemical composition of a material. It involves shining infrared light onto a sample and measuring the absorption of the light by the chemical bonds in the material.

These are just a few of the many experiments that can be used to analyze materials. The choice of experiment depends on the specific information that is needed about the material. #### Various Experiments

There are numerous experiments that have been conducted in various fields of science. Some of the most notable experiments include:

1. **The Double-Slit Experiment:** This experiment, first performed by Thomas Young in 1801, demonstrated the wave-like behavior of light. It involved shining a beam of light through two narrow slits and observing the resulting interference pattern on a screen behind the slits.

2. **The Michelson-Morley Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Albert Michelson and Edward Morley in 1887, was designed to detect the presence of the hypothetical "luminiferous ether" that was thought to permeate all space and serve as the medium through which light waves traveled. The experiment failed to detect any such ether, providing evidence for the theory of relativity.

3. **The Rutherford Gold Foil Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Ernest Rutherford in 1911, involved firing alpha particles at a thin sheet of gold foil. The results showed that most of the particles passed straight through the foil, while a small number were deflected at large angles. This led Rutherford to propose the existence of a small, dense nucleus at the center of the atom.

4. **The Stanford Prison Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Philip Zimbardo in 1971, involved assigning college students to play the roles of prisoners and guards in a simulated prison environment. The experiment was intended to study the psychological effects of perceived power, but had to be terminated early due to the abusive behavior of the "guards" and the distress of the "prisoners."

5. **The Milgram Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Stanley Milgram in 1961, involved instructing participants to administer electric shocks to a "learner" (who was actually a confederate of the experimenter) whenever the learner made a mistake on a memory task. The experiment was designed to study obedience to authority, and the results showed that a majority of participants were willing to administer what they believed to be potentially lethal shocks when instructed to do so by an authority figure.

These are just a few examples of the many experiments that have been conducted in the pursuit of scientific knowledge. Each experiment has contributed to our understanding of the world around us and has helped to shape the course of scientific inquiry.
