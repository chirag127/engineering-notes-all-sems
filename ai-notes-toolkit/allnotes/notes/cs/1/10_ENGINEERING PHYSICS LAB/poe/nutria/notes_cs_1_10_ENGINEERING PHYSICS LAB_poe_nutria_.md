3. The experiment consists of a convex lens placed in contact with a flat glass plate. 
4. A beam of monochromatic light (usually sodium light) is shone onto the lens and the reflected light is observed. 
5. When the lens is placed in contact with the flat surface, a series of concentric bright and dark rings are formed. 
6. The dark rings are called Newton's Rings, and the distance between two adjacent bright rings is called the wavelength of the light. 
7. The wavelength of the light can be calculated using the formula: 
$$\lambda = \frac{2\pi R}{n-1}$$ 
where $\lambda$ is the wavelength, R is the radius of the ring, and n is the number of the ring. 
8. The experiment can be used to determine the wavelength of any monochromatic light source.
