#### To determine the least count of Vernier calliper, vernier height gauge, micrometer (Screw gauge) and take different reading over given metallic pieces using these instruments.

- **Vernier caliper**: The least count of a Vernier caliper is determined by the difference between the value of one main scale division and the value of one Vernier scale division. The formula for calculating the least count of a Vernier caliper is given by: Least Count (LC) = (Value of 1 Main Scale Division) - (Value of 1 Vernier Scale Division).

- **Vernier height gauge**: The least count of a Vernier height gauge is determined in the same way as that of a Vernier caliper. The formula for calculating the least count of a Vernier height gauge is given by: Least Count (LC) = (Value of 1 Main Scale Division) - (Value of 1 Vernier Scale Division).

- **Micrometer (Screw gauge)**: The least count of a micrometer is determined by the pitch of the screw, which is the distance moved by the spindle per revolution. The formula for calculating the least count of a micrometer is given by: Least Count (LC) = (Pitch of the Screw) / (Number of Divisions on the Thimble).

To take different readings over given metallic pieces using these instruments, the following steps can be followed:

1. Place the metallic piece between the jaws of the instrument.
2. Adjust the position of the jaws until they are in contact with the metallic piece.
3. Read the main scale and the Vernier scale (for Vernier caliper and Vernier height gauge) or the thimble scale (for micrometer) to obtain the measurement.
4. Repeat the process for different metallic pieces and record the readings.

A lathe is a machine tool that rotates a workpiece about an axis of rotation to perform various operations such as cutting, sanding, knurling, drilling, deformation, facing, and turning. The lathe is one of the oldest and most important machine tools.

The main parts of a lathe machine are:

1. **Headstock:** The headstock is located on the left side of the bed and serves as a housing for the driving pulleys and back gears. It also contains the main spindle and the change gears.

2. **Tailstock:** The tailstock is located on the right side of the bed and is used to support the other end of the workpiece.

3. **Bed:** The bed is the main supporting structure of the lathe. It is a long, flat base that is mounted on legs or a cabinet.

4. **Carriage:** The carriage is a movable platform that supports the cutting tool and moves along the bed.

5. **Cross slide:** The cross slide is mounted on the carriage and moves perpendicular to the bed.

6. **Compound rest:** The compound rest is mounted on the cross slide and can be rotated to any angle.

7. **Tool post:** The tool post is mounted on the compound rest and holds the cutting tool.

8. **Lead screw:** The lead screw is a long threaded rod that runs the length of the bed and is used to move the carriage.

9. **Feed rod:** The feed rod is a long rod that runs parallel to the lead screw and is used to move the carriage.

The lathe can perform various operations such as:

- **Facing:** Facing is the process of machining the end of a workpiece to produce a flat surface.

- **Plane Turning:** Plane turning is the process of machining a cylindrical surface.

- **Step Turning:** Step turning is the process of machining a stepped cylindrical surface.

- **Taper Turning:** Taper turning is the process of machining a conical surface.

- **Threading:** Threading is the process of cutting a screw thread on a workpiece.

- **Knurling:** Knurling is the process of producing a rough surface on a workpiece.

- **Parting:** Parting is the process of cutting off a piece of the workpiece.

These are the basic operations that can be performed on a lathe machine. There are many other operations that can be performed on a lathe, depending on the accessories and attachments used. Some of these operations include drilling, boring, reaming, and tapping.

#### Study of Carpentry Tools, Equipment and different joints. Making of Cross Half lap joint, Half lap Dovetail joint and Mortise Tenon Joint

Carpentry is a skilled trade and a craft in which the primary work performed is the cutting, shaping and installation of building materials during the construction of buildings, ships, timber bridges, concrete formwork, etc. Carpenters traditionally worked with natural wood and did the rougher work such as framing, but today many other materials are also used and sometimes the finer trades of cabinetmaking and furniture building are considered carpentry.

Carpentry tools and equipment include:

- Cutting tools: hand saw, circular saw, jigsaw, miter saw
- Shaping tools: plane, chisel, router
- Fastening tools: hammer, nail gun, screwdriver, drill
- Clamping tools: bar clamp, C-clamp, pipe clamp

Different types of joints used in carpentry include:
- Butt joint: the simplest joint where two pieces of wood are butted together
- Lap joint: a joint where one piece of wood overlaps the other
- Dovetail joint: a joint where the ends of two pieces of wood are cut in a series of interlocking fingers
- Mortise and tenon joint: a joint where a tenon on one piece of wood fits into a mortise on the other

Cross Half Lap Joint:
- A cross half lap joint is a type of lap joint where two pieces of wood cross each other and half of the material is removed from each piece so that they fit together.
- To make a cross half lap joint, first mark the center of each piece of wood where they will cross.
- Use a saw to cut halfway through the thickness of each piece of wood at the center mark.
- Use a chisel to remove the waste material and create a flat surface.
- Fit the two pieces together so that they overlap at the center and are flush on both sides.

Half Lap Dovetail Joint:
- A half lap dovetail joint is a type of lap joint where one piece of wood overlaps the other and the ends are cut in a series of interlocking fingers.
- To make a half lap dovetail joint, first mark the center of each piece of wood where they will overlap.
- Use a saw to cut halfway through the thickness of each piece of wood at the center mark.
- Use a dovetail saw to cut the fingers on the ends of each piece of wood.
- Use a chisel to remove the waste material and create a flat surface.
- Fit the two pieces together so that the fingers interlock and the joint is flush on both sides.

Mortise and Tenon Joint:
- A mortise and tenon joint is a type of joint where a tenon on one piece of wood fits into a mortise on the other.
- To make a mortise and tenon joint, first mark the location of the mortise on one piece of wood and the location of the tenon on the other.
- Use a drill to remove most of the waste material from the mortise.
- Use a chisel to square up the sides of the mortise and create a flat bottom.
- Use a saw to cut the tenon to the correct size and shape.
- Fit the tenon into the mortise and make any necessary adjustments to ensure a tight fit.

3. Both moulding and casting can be used to produce complex shapes that would be difficult or impossible to produce using other manufacturing processes. They are commonly used to produce parts for machinery, tools, and other products.

3. **Select the appropriate material:** The material used for 3D printing will depend on the chosen 3D printing technology and the desired properties of the final product. Common materials used in 3D printing include plastics, resins, and metals.

5. **Start the printing process:** Once the 3D printer is prepared, the printing process can begin. The 3D printer will use the 3D model as a guide to build the product layer by layer.

6. **Post-processing:** After the printing process is complete, the product may require post-processing to achieve the desired finish. This may include removing support structures, sanding, or painting.

7. **Quality control:** Finally, the finished product should be inspected for quality and tested to ensure that it meets the desired specifications.

In summary, preparing a product using 3D printing involves designing a 3D model, choosing the right 3D printing technology and material, preparing the 3D printer, starting the printing process, post-processing, and quality control. The entire process can take around 3 hours, depending on the complexity of the product and the chosen 3D printing technology.

#### 4. CNC Fundamentals and Programming, By P. M. Agrawal, V. J. Patel, Charotar Publication

- CNC stands for Computer Numerical Control.
- CNC machines are automated machines that are controlled by a computer program.
- CNC machines can be used for a variety of manufacturing processes, including milling, drilling, and cutting.
- CNC programming involves creating a set of instructions for the machine to follow.
- These instructions are written in a language called G-code.
- G-code is a standardized language that is used to control CNC machines.
- CNC programming can be done manually, or it can be done using a computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) software.
- CAM software can generate G-code automatically based on a 3D model of the part to be manufactured.
- CNC machines can be programmed to perform complex operations with high precision and repeatability.
- CNC technology has revolutionized the manufacturing industry by allowing for faster and more efficient production.
