### 1 Introduction to Mechanical workshop material, tools and machines 3 Hrs

A mechanical workshop is a place where various materials, tools, and machines are used to manufacture, repair, or maintain mechanical systems. In this section, we will introduce some common materials, tools, and machines found in a mechanical workshop.

#### Materials
- **Metals**: Metals such as steel, aluminum, and copper are commonly used in mechanical workshops due to their strength, durability, and versatility.
- **Plastics**: Plastics are also commonly used in mechanical workshops due to their lightweight, low cost, and ease of fabrication.
- **Composites**: Composite materials, such as fiberglass and carbon fiber, are used in mechanical workshops for their high strength-to-weight ratio and corrosion resistance.

#### Tools
- **Hand tools**: Hand tools, such as hammers, screwdrivers, and wrenches, are used for manual tasks such as tightening bolts or driving nails.
- **Power tools**: Power tools, such as drills, saws, and grinders, are used for tasks that require more power or speed than can be achieved with hand tools.
- **Measuring tools**: Measuring tools, such as calipers, micrometers, and gauges, are used to measure dimensions and tolerances of mechanical parts.

#### Machines
- **Lathes**: Lathes are used to rotate a workpiece and remove material using cutting tools to create cylindrical shapes.
- **Milling machines**: Milling machines use rotary cutters to remove material from a workpiece to create a variety of shapes and features.
- **Drilling machines**: Drilling machines are used to create holes in a workpiece using a rotating drill bit.

These are just a few examples of the materials, tools, and machines that can be found in a mechanical workshop. A thorough understanding of these and other workshop equipment is essential for anyone working in the field of mechanical engineering.



#### To study layout, safety measures and different engineering materials (mild steel, medium carbon steel, high carbon steel, high speed steel and cast iron etc) used in workshop.

- **Layout:** The layout of a workshop refers to the arrangement of equipment, machinery, and workstations in a manner that maximizes efficiency, safety, and productivity. A well-designed layout takes into account the flow of materials and personnel, as well as the space and power requirements of the equipment.

- **Safety measures:** Safety measures in a workshop include the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) such as safety glasses, gloves, and hearing protection; proper training and supervision; and the implementation of safety procedures and protocols. Regular maintenance and inspection of equipment and machinery, as well as good housekeeping practices, can also help to prevent accidents and injuries.

- **Engineering materials:** Different engineering materials are used in a workshop depending on the specific application and requirements. Some common materials include:
  - **Mild steel:** A type of carbon steel with a low carbon content, mild steel is ductile, malleable, and easy to weld. It is commonly used for general-purpose applications such as construction and automobile manufacturing.
  - **Medium carbon steel:** With a higher carbon content than mild steel, medium carbon steel is stronger and harder but less ductile. It is often used for applications that require strength and toughness, such as shafts, gears, and machinery parts.
  - **High carbon steel:** High carbon steel has an even higher carbon content and is very hard and strong but brittle. It is commonly used for applications that require high strength and wear resistance, such as cutting tools and springs.
  - **High speed steel:** A type of tool steel, high speed steel is alloyed with elements such as tungsten, molybdenum, and cobalt to increase its hardness and heat resistance. It is commonly used for cutting tools such as drill bits and saw blades.
  - **Cast iron:** Cast iron is an alloy of iron, carbon, and silicon that is known for its excellent castability, wear resistance, and durability. It is commonly used for applications such as engine blocks, pipes, and machine frames.

#### To determine the least count of Vernier calliper, vernier height gauge, micrometer (Screw gauge) and take different reading over given metallic pieces using these instruments.

1. **Vernier Calliper**: The least count of a Vernier calliper is the difference between the value of one main scale division and the value of one Vernier scale division. To calculate the least count, divide the value of one main scale division by the total number of Vernier scale divisions.
2. **Vernier Height Gauge**: The least count of a Vernier height gauge is calculated in the same way as that of a Vernier calliper. Divide the value of one main scale division by the total number of Vernier scale divisions.
3. **Micrometer (Screw Gauge)**: The least count of a micrometer is the smallest distance that can be measured with the instrument. It is equal to the pitch of the screw, which is the distance between two adjacent threads on the screw.

To take different readings over given metallic pieces using these instruments, follow these steps:
1. Place the metallic piece between the jaws of the instrument.
2. Close the jaws gently until they touch the metallic piece.
3. Read the main scale and the Vernier scale or the thimble scale (in case of a micrometer) to obtain the measurement.
4. Repeat the process for different metallic pieces and record the readings.

#### Study of Carpentry Tools, Equipment and different joints. Making of Cross Half lap joint, Half lap Dovetail joint and Mortise Tenon Joint

Carpentry involves the use of various tools and equipment to create wooden structures and objects. Some common carpentry tools and equipment include:

1. **Saws**: Used to cut wood to the desired size and shape. There are different types of saws, such as hand saws, circular saws, and jigsaws.
2. **Hammers**: Used to drive nails into wood to join pieces together.
3. **Chisels**: Used to shape and carve wood.
4. **Planes**: Used to smooth and shape wood.
5. **Squares**: Used to ensure that angles are accurate and that pieces are square.
6. **Clamps**: Used to hold pieces of wood together while they are being worked on.

There are several different types of joints that can be used in carpentry, including the cross half lap joint, the half lap dovetail joint, and the mortise and tenon joint.

- **Cross Half Lap Joint**: This joint is created by removing half of the thickness of each piece of wood where they will intersect, and then fitting the two pieces together. This creates a strong joint that is often used in framing and other structural applications.
- **Half Lap Dovetail Joint**: This joint is similar to the cross half lap joint, but it includes a dovetail shape that helps to lock the two pieces together. This joint is often used in furniture making and other applications where strength and appearance are important.
- **Mortise and Tenon Joint**: This joint is created by cutting a mortise (a hole) into one piece of wood and a tenon (a protruding piece) on the other piece of wood. The tenon is then inserted into the mortise, creating a strong joint. This joint is often used in furniture making and other applications where strength is important.

To make these joints, the carpenter must carefully measure and mark the wood, and then use the appropriate tools to cut and shape the pieces. Once the pieces are prepared, they can be assembled and glued or nailed together to create the final joint. It is important to work carefully and accurately to ensure that the joints are strong and well-made.

#### Introduction to BI standards and reading of welding drawings

BI standards refer to the standards set by the British Institute of Standards for welding and fabrication. These standards provide guidelines for the design, fabrication, and inspection of welded joints.

Reading welding drawings is an essential skill for anyone involved in the welding and fabrication industry. Welding drawings provide detailed information about the dimensions, materials, and welding processes to be used in the fabrication of a component.

To practice reading welding drawings, it is important to understand the different types of joints and welding processes. Some common joints and welding processes include:

1. **Butt Joint:** A butt joint is a joint where two pieces of material are joined end to end. This type of joint is commonly used in welding and can be welded using various welding processes.

2. **Lap Joint:** A lap joint is a joint where two pieces of material overlap each other. This type of joint is commonly used in welding and can be welded using various welding processes.

3. **TIG Welding:** Tungsten Inert Gas (TIG) welding is a welding process that uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode to produce the weld. This process is commonly used for welding thin materials and produces high-quality welds.

4. **MIG Welding:** Metal Inert Gas (MIG) welding is a welding process that uses a consumable wire electrode to produce the weld. This process is commonly used for welding thicker materials and is faster than TIG welding.

By practicing these operations, you can improve your skills in reading welding drawings and understanding the different types of joints and welding processes.
