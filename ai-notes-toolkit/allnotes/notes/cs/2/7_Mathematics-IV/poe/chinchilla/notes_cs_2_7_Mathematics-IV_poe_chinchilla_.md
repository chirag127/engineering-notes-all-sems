### Linear and Non Linear Partial Equations of First Order

In the field of mathematics, partial differential equations (PDEs) play a crucial role in modeling various physical phenomena. In this module, we will focus on the first-order partial differential equations. 

#### Linear Partial Equations of First Order

Linear partial differential equations are those equations that can be expressed as a linear combination of partial derivatives of the dependent variable with respect to the independent variables. 

The general form of a linear partial differential equation of the first order is given as:

```
a(x,y)u_x + b(x,y)u_y = c(x,y)
```

where `u(x,y)` is the dependent variable, `a(x,y), b(x,y), c(x,y)` are the coefficients, and `u_x` and `u_y` are the partial derivatives of `u` with respect to `x` and `y` respectively.

The solution of a linear partial differential equation of the first order involves the method of characteristics. This method involves finding a set of curves (called characteristics) along which the differential equation reduces to an ordinary differential equation. 

#### Non-Linear Partial Equations of First Order

Non-linear partial differential equations are those equations that cannot be expressed as a linear combination of partial derivatives of the dependent variable with respect to the independent variables. 

The general form of a non-linear partial differential equation of the first order is given as:

```
F(x,y,u,u_x,u_y) = 0
```

where `u(x,y)` is the dependent variable, `u_x` and `u_y` are the partial derivatives of `u` with respect to `x` and `y` respectively, and `F` is a non-linear function.

Non-linear partial differential equations are generally more difficult to solve than linear partial differential equations. However, there are various methods available such as the method of characteristics, separation of variables, and numerical methods to solve non-linear partial differential equations.

In conclusion, understanding linear and non-linear partial differential equations of first order is essential in the field of mathematics and physics. The study of these equations helps in modeling various physical phenomena such as heat transfer, fluid flow, and electromagnetic fields.

#### Addition Law of Probability

The addition law of probability is used to determine the probability of the occurrence of either of two events. The formula for the addition law of probability is as follows:

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)

Where, 

- P(A or B) is the probability of A or B occurring.
- P(A) is the probability of event A occurring.
- P(B) is the probability of event B occurring.
- P(A and B) is the probability of both A and B occurring.

The addition law of probability can be extended to any number of events. If there are n events A1, A2, ..., An, then the probability of any one of them occurring is given by:

P(A1 or A2 or ... or An) = P(A1) + P(A2) + ... + P(An) - P(A1 and A2) - P(A1 and A3) - ... - P(An-1 and An) + P(A1 and A2 and ... and An)

#### Multiplication Law of Probability

The multiplication law of probability is used to determine the probability of the occurrence of two or more independent events. The formula for the multiplication law of probability is as follows:

P(A and B) = P(A) x P(B)

Where,

- P(A and B) is the probability of both A and B occurring.
- P(A) is the probability of event A occurring.

The multiplication law of probability can be extended to any number of independent events. If there are n independent events A1, A2, ..., An, then the probability of all of them occurring is given by:

### Baye’s Theorem

Baye’s theorem is an important concept in probability theory. This theorem is used to find out the probability of an event happening, given that another event has already happened. Baye’s theorem is named after Thomas Bayes, who first described it in the 18th century.

#### Formula

The formula for Baye’s theorem is:

P(A|B) = P(B|A)*P(A) / P(B)

where:

- P(A|B) is the probability of event A happening, given that event B has happened.
- P(B|A) is the probability of event B happening, given that event A has happened.
- P(A) is the probability of event A happening.
- P(B) is the probability of event B happening.

#### Steps to use Baye’s Theorem

To use Baye’s theorem, follow these steps:

1. Identify the events A and B.
2. Find the probability of event A happening, P(A).
3. Find the probability of event B happening, P(B).
4. Find the probability of event B happening, given that event A has happened, P(B|A).
5. Use Baye’s theorem formula to find the probability of event A happening, given that event B has happened, P(A|B).

   - Event B: The patient has a cough.

   - P(B) = 0.54 + 0.36
   - P(B) = 0.9

4. Find the probability of event B happening, given that event A has happened, P(B|A).
   - P(B|A) = P(cough|cancer)

### Testing a Hypothesis

Hypothesis testing is an important statistical technique that allows us to make decisions or draw conclusions about a population based on a sample. In this module, we will learn about the steps involved in testing a hypothesis and the different types of errors that can occur.

#### Steps Involved in Testing a Hypothesis

1. State the null and alternative hypothesis: The null hypothesis is the statement that we want to test. It is usually a statement of no difference or no effect. The alternative hypothesis is the statement that we want to accept if the null hypothesis is rejected.

2. Select the level of significance: The level of significance is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true. It is usually set at 0.05 or 0.01.

3. Determine the test statistic: The test statistic is a measure of how far the sample statistic is from the hypothesized value. It is calculated based on the sample data and the null hypothesis.

4. Determine the critical value: The critical value is the value of the test statistic that defines the rejection region. It is determined based on the level of significance and the degrees of freedom.

5. Make a decision: Compare the test statistic with the critical value. If the test statistic falls in the rejection region, reject the null hypothesis. If it falls in the acceptance region, accept the null hypothesis.

#### Types of Errors

There are two types of errors that can occur in hypothesis testing:

1. Type I error: Rejecting the null hypothesis when it is actually true. The probability of making a Type I error is equal to the level of significance.

2. Type II error: Accepting the null hypothesis when it is actually false. The probability of making a Type II error depends on the sample size, the level of significance, and the effect size.

#### Conclusion

Testing a hypothesis is an important statistical technique that allows us to make decisions or draw conclusions about a population based on a sample. It involves several steps, including stating the null and alternative hypothesis, selecting the level of significance, determining the test statistic and critical value, and making a decision. It is important to be aware of the different types of errors that can occur in hypothesis testing.
