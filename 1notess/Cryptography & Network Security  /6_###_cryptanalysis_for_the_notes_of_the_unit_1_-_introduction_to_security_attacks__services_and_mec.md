Cryptanalysis is the study of methods for obtaining the meaning of encrypted information without access to the key required to decrypt it. It is the practice of analyzing and breaking cryptographic systems. Substitution and transposition ciphers are classical encryption techniques that use substitution or transposition of letters to conceal the original message. Cryptanalysis is the art of breaking these ciphers to obtain the original message. Steganography is the practice of hiding information within other information, such as hiding a message within an image. Stream and block ciphers are modern encryption techniques that use a stream or block of data to encrypt the original message. Block ciphers use the principles of confusion and diffusion, as described by Shannon's theory, to encrypt data. The Fiestal structure is a specific structure used in block ciphers, such as the Data Encryption Standard (DES), which was widely used but has been replaced by stronger encryption methods. Differential cryptanalysis is a method of analyzing encryption systems to determine weaknesses in the system. Block cipher modes of operations describe how block ciphers are used to encrypt data, such as ECB, CBC, and CFB. Triple DES is a more secure version of DES that uses three keys to encrypt the data.
