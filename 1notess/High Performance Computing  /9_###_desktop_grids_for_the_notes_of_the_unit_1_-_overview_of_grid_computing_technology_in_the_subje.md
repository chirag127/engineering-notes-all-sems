### Desktop Grids for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject
Desktop Grids are a type of distributed computing system that leverage idle computing resources of personal computers to perform large-scale computational tasks. They are formed by connecting a large number of desktop computers through a network to create a virtual supercomputer. The main advantage of Desktop Grids is that they can provide a low-cost alternative to traditional supercomputing systems. The tasks are divided into smaller subtasks and distributed among the connected computers for parallel processing. The results are then combined to produce the final outcome. Desktop Grids are used in various scientific and engineering fields, such as climate modeling, protein folding, and drug discovery. They are also used for volunteer computing projects, where individuals can donate their idle computing resources to support scientific research.
