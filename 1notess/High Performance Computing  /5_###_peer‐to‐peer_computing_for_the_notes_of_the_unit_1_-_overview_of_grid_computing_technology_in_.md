### Peer‐to‐Peer Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject
Peer-to-Peer (P2P) computing is a decentralized computing model in which individuals or nodes in a network share resources and participate in the distribution of data and tasks. Unlike traditional client-server models, P2P networks have no central authority and rely on the cooperation of the peers to function. 

P2P networks can be used for a variety of applications, including file sharing, online gaming, and distributed computing. In P2P file sharing, for example, each peer can both share and receive files from other peers in the network. 

Advantages of P2P computing include increased scalability, reduced network congestion, and improved fault tolerance. However, P2P networks can also be vulnerable to security and privacy threats, as well as issues related to the distribution of resources and the management of the network. 

Overall, P2P computing represents an important development in the field of distributed computing and continues to be a subject of active research and development.
