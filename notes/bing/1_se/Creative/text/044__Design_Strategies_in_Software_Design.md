### Design Strategies in Software Design

- Design strategies are the approaches that are taken to design a software system that meets the requirements and specifications of the problem domain.
- Design strategies aim to achieve a balance between the quality attributes of the software, such as performance, reliability, usability, security, etc.
- Design strategies can be classified into three major categories: structured design, function-oriented design, and object-oriented design.
  - Structured design is a design strategy that focuses on the decomposition of the system into modules that have well-defined interfaces and responsibilities. Structured design follows a top-down approach, where the system is divided into sub-systems, and then into smaller components, until the desired level of abstraction is reached. Structured design is suitable for simple and stable systems that do not require frequent changes or extensions.
  - Function-oriented design is a design strategy that focuses on the identification and grouping of the functions or operations that the system performs. Function-oriented design follows a bottom-up approach, where the system is built from the basic functions, and then integrated into higher-level functions, until the desired functionality is achieved. Function-oriented design is suitable for complex and dynamic systems that require high performance and efficiency.
  - Object-oriented design is a design strategy that focuses on the identification and grouping of the objects or entities that the system interacts with. Object-oriented design follows a hybrid approach, where the system is modeled as a collection of objects that have attributes, behaviors, and relationships. Object-oriented design is suitable for modular and flexible systems that require reusability and extensibility.
