### Entity Relationship Diagrams in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

- Entity Relationship Diagrams (ERDs) are graphical representations of the data model of a software system.
- ERDs show the entities, attributes, relationships and constraints of the data that the software system needs to store and manipulate.
- ERDs are used in software engineering during the planning stages of the software project to analyze the requirements and design the database of the software system.
- ERDs are often used as the basis for data flow diagrams (DFDs) that show how the data flows between the components of the software system.
- ERDs are composed of the following elements:
  - Entities: An entity is a person, place, thing or concept that the software system needs to keep information about. For example, a student, a course, a book, etc. Entities are represented by rectangles in ERDs.
  - Attributes: An attribute is a property or characteristic of an entity that the software system needs to store. For example, a student entity may have attributes such as name, ID, email, etc. Attributes are represented by ovals connected to the entity they belong to in ERDs.
  - Relationships: A relationship is an association or link between two or more entities that the software system needs to capture. For example, a student entity may have a relationship with a course entity that indicates the student is enrolled in the course. Relationships are represented by diamonds or lines connecting the entities they involve in ERDs.
  - Constraints: A constraint is a rule or restriction that applies to the entities, attributes or relationships in the data model. For example, a constraint may specify that a student can only enroll in one course at a time, or that a book can only have one author. Constraints are represented by symbols or text annotations in ERDs.
