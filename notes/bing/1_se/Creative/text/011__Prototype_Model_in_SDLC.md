### Prototype Model in SDLC

- The prototype model is a software development life cycle (SDLC) model in which a prototype is built, tested, and then reworked as necessary until an acceptable prototype is finally achieved from which the complete system or product can be developed.
- A prototype is a working model of a software or system that displays the functionality of the product under development, but may not contain the exact logic or implementation details of the original software.
- The prototype model is used when the customers do not know the exact project requirements beforehand or when the requirements are complex and unclear.
- The prototype model involves the following phases  :
  - **Requirement gathering and analysis**: The basic requirements of the project are gathered from the customer and analyzed. The scope and feasibility of the project are also determined in this phase.
  - **Quick design**: A quick design of the prototype is created based on the requirements. The design does not have to be detailed or complete, but it should capture the essential features and functionality of the product.
  - **Build prototype**: The prototype is built according to the quick design. The prototype can be a paper-based model, a mock-up, a simulation, or a partial implementation of the software. The prototype should be easy to modify and refine.
  - **Customer evaluation**: The prototype is presented to the customer for feedback and evaluation. The customer can test the prototype and suggest changes or improvements. The customer can also validate if the prototype meets their expectations and requirements.
  - **Refining prototype**: Based on the customer feedback, the prototype is refined and improved. The changes are incorporated into the prototype and the cycle of building, testing, and evaluation is repeated until the customer is satisfied with the prototype.
  - **Engineer product**: Once the customer approves the prototype, the final product is engineered based on the prototype. The prototype serves as a basis for the design, development, testing, and deployment of the product.
- The advantages of the prototype model are  :
  - It reduces the risk of failure and misunderstanding of the requirements by involving the customer in the development process.
  - It improves the quality and usability of the product by incorporating the customer feedback and suggestions.
  - It facilitates better communication and collaboration between the developers and the customer.
  - It allows for early detection and correction of errors and defects.
  - It increases the customer satisfaction and confidence in the product.
- The disadvantages of the prototype model are  :
  - It can be time-consuming and costly to build and refine multiple prototypes.
  - It can lead to scope creep and unrealistic expectations from the customer if the requirements are not well-defined and controlled.
  - It can cause confusion and inconsistency between the prototype and the final product if the prototype is not properly documented and managed.
  - It can compromise the quality and performance of the product if the prototype is not properly tested and verified.
