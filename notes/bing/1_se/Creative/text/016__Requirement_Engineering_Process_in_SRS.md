### Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

Requirement engineering is the process of eliciting, analyzing, specifying, and validating the software requirements for a system. It is an essential and crucial activity in software development, as it lays the foundation for the design, implementation, testing, and maintenance of the software product. The main goal of requirement engineering is to produce a high-quality software requirements specification (SRS) document that describes the functional and non-functional requirements of the system, as well as the constraints, assumptions, and dependencies that affect the system.

The requirement engineering process consists of the following main activities:

- **Feasibility study**: This is the initial stage of the requirement engineering process, where the feasibility of the proposed system is assessed in terms of technical, economic, legal, operational, and schedule aspects. The feasibility study helps to determine whether the system is worth developing, and what are the possible risks and benefits involved. The feasibility study also defines the scope and objectives of the system, and identifies the main stakeholders and their needs.
- **Requirement elicitation**: This is the process of gathering the software requirements from various sources, such as the stakeholders, existing systems, documents, standards, regulations, etc. Requirement elicitation involves various techniques, such as interviews, questionnaires, surveys, brainstorming, observation, prototyping, etc. The aim of requirement elicitation is to collect as much information as possible about the system and its context, and to understand the problems and expectations of the users and customers.
- **Requirement analysis**: This is the process of analyzing the collected requirements and resolving any conflicts, ambiguities, inconsistencies, or gaps among them. Requirement analysis also involves prioritizing the requirements based on their importance, urgency, and dependency. Requirement analysis helps to refine and structure the requirements, and to identify the functional and non-functional requirements of the system. Requirement analysis also defines the acceptance criteria and the verification and validation methods for the requirements.
- **Requirement specification**: This is the process of documenting the software requirements in a formal and precise manner, using a standard notation or language. Requirement specification produces the software requirements specification (SRS) document, which is the main output of the requirement engineering process. The SRS document serves as a contract between the developers and the customers, and as a basis for the design, implementation, testing, and maintenance of the system. The SRS document should be clear, complete, consistent, verifiable, modifiable, and traceable.
- **Requirement validation**: This is the process of checking the quality and correctness of the software requirements, and ensuring that they meet the needs and expectations of the stakeholders. Requirement validation involves reviewing, inspecting, testing, and evaluating the software requirements, and identifying and resolving any errors, defects, or discrepancies in the SRS document. Requirement validation also involves obtaining feedback and approval from the stakeholders, and ensuring that the requirements are aligned with the project scope and objectives.
