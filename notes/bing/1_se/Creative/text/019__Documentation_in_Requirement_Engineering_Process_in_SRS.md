### Documentation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

- Documentation is the process of recording the requirements and specifications of a software system in a written form.
- Documentation is an essential part of the requirement engineering process, as it helps to communicate, verify, validate, and manage the requirements of the software system.
- Documentation also serves as a contract between the stakeholders and the developers, as it defines the scope, functionality, quality, and constraints of the software system.
- Documentation can be done at different levels of abstraction, such as user, system, and component level, depending on the intended audience and purpose of the document.
- Documentation can follow different formats, standards, and templates, such as IEEE 830, IEEE 29148, or Volere, to ensure consistency, completeness, and clarity of the requirements.
- Documentation can include different types of information, such as:

  - Functional requirements: describe what the software system should do, such as features, use cases, scenarios, and user stories.
  - Non-functional requirements: describe how the software system should behave, such as performance, reliability, security, usability, and maintainability.
  - Constraints: describe the limitations and assumptions of the software system, such as budget, schedule, resources, regulations, and standards.
  - Stakeholder needs: describe the expectations and goals of the stakeholders, such as customers, users, managers, and developers.
  - Glossary: define the terms and acronyms used in the document.
  - Models: illustrate the requirements using graphical or textual representations, such as diagrams, tables, charts, and equations.
  - Traceability: link the requirements to their sources, dependencies, and verification methods, such as interviews, surveys, prototypes, and tests.
