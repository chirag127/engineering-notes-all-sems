### Analysis in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

- Analysis is the second phase of the requirement engineering process, after elicitation.
- Analysis involves examining, evaluating, and refining the elicited requirements to ensure that they are consistent, complete, correct, and feasible.
- Analysis also involves resolving any conflicts or ambiguities among the stakeholders' needs and expectations, and prioritizing the requirements based on their importance and urgency.
- Analysis produces a set of validated and prioritized requirements that can be used as the basis for the design and development of the software system.
- Analysis can be performed using various techniques, such as:
  - Modeling: using graphical or textual representations to describe the structure, behavior, and interactions of the system and its components.
  - Prototyping: creating a simplified or partial version of the system to demonstrate its functionality and usability to the stakeholders and obtain their feedback.
  - Verification: checking that the requirements conform to the standards and specifications, and that they are free of errors and inconsistencies.
  - Validation: confirming that the requirements meet the stakeholders' needs and expectations, and that they are realistic and achievable.
  - Prioritization: assigning a level of importance and urgency to each requirement, based on the criteria and constraints of the project.
  - Negotiation: resolving any conflicts or trade-offs among the requirements and the stakeholders, and reaching a mutual agreement on the final set of requirements.
