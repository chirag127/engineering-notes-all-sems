### Software Configuration Management Activities

Software Configuration Management (SCM) is the process of identifying, controlling, and tracking the changes and versions of software artifacts. SCM aims to ensure the integrity, consistency, and quality of software products throughout the software development life cycle. SCM involves the following activities:

- **Configuration identification**: This activity involves defining and naming the software items and components that make up the software product, and establishing the relationships and dependencies among them. Configuration identification also involves defining the baselines, which are the approved versions of software items that serve as the basis for further development or delivery.
- **Configuration control**: This activity involves managing and controlling the changes and modifications to the software items and components, and ensuring that they are consistent with the requirements and specifications. Configuration control also involves evaluating and approving the change requests, implementing and testing the changes, and updating the baselines and documentation accordingly.
- **Configuration status accounting**: This activity involves recording and reporting the status and history of the software items and components, and the changes and modifications made to them. Configuration status accounting also involves tracking and auditing the configuration items and baselines, and providing visibility and traceability of the software configuration throughout the software development life cycle.
- **Configuration audit**: This activity involves verifying and validating that the software items and components conform to the requirements and specifications, and that the changes and modifications are properly implemented and documented. Configuration audit also involves checking and reviewing the compliance and quality of the software configuration, and identifying and resolving any discrepancies or issues.
- **Configuration management planning**: This activity involves defining and documenting the policies, procedures, standards, tools, and roles and responsibilities for performing the SCM activities. Configuration management planning also involves establishing and maintaining the SCM environment, which includes the hardware, software, and network resources required for SCM.
