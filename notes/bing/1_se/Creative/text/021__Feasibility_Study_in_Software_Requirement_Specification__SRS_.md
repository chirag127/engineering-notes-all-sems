### Feasibility Study in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

- A feasibility study is a study conducted to ascertain the viability of a structure or strategy.
- It is also one of the requirements of the engineering process.
- It is required when a client proposes a company to develop a product with a rough understanding of software functions and what functionality they necessitate from the software.
- The feasibility study aims to ascertain why developing software is appealing to users, adaptable to change, and compliant with applicable requirements .
- It delves into technical aspects of the project and product, such as compatibility, maintainability, efficiency, and integration capabilities.
- The feasibility study, especially professional software project feasibility analysis, takes place after specifying business requirements, that is, it is the second step of the requirements engineering process.
- Its justification is justified in order to analyze and answer some questions from the point of view of operational, technical, schedule, economic, and legal feasibility.
- The feasibility study helps to identify the risks involved in the project and to determine whether the project is worth pursuing or not.
- The feasibility study also helps to define the scope and objectives of the project, as well as the constraints and assumptions.
- The feasibility study can be documented in a Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document, which is a formal statement of the software requirements for the project.
- The SRS document serves as a basis for communication between the stakeholders, the developers, and the testers of the software.
- The SRS document should be clear, concise, consistent, complete, correct, and verifiable.
- The SRS document should also include the following sections:
  - Introduction: This section provides an overview of the purpose, scope, definitions, acronyms, abbreviations, references, and overview of the document.
  - General Description: This section provides a general description of the software, including the product perspective, product functions, user characteristics, general constraints, assumptions and dependencies, and requirements apportionment.
  - Specific Requirements: This section provides a detailed description of the functional and non-functional requirements of the software, such as performance, reliability, security, usability, maintainability, portability, etc. This section also includes the external interface requirements, such as user, hardware, software, and communication interfaces.
  - Appendices: This section provides any additional information that is relevant to the software requirements, such as data models, data dictionaries, glossaries, etc.
