## Unit 1 - Introduction to Software Engineering

- Software engineering is the application of engineering principles and practices to the development, maintenance, and evolution of software systems that meet the needs and expectations of stakeholders.
- Software engineering involves activities such as:
  - Requirements analysis: eliciting, specifying, and validating the functional and non-functional requirements of the software system.
  - Design: creating a high-level and low-level architecture of the software system that satisfies the requirements and constraints.
  - Implementation: writing, testing, and debugging the source code of the software system according to the design.
  - Verification and validation: ensuring that the software system meets the requirements and quality standards, and performs as expected.
  - Deployment: delivering, installing, and configuring the software system for the end-users or customers.
  - Maintenance: fixing errors, improving performance, adding features, and adapting the software system to changing needs and environments.
  - Evolution: managing the changes and updates to the software system over its lifetime.
- Software engineering also involves processes, methods, tools, standards, and best practices that support and guide the software development activities and ensure their quality and efficiency.
- Software engineering is a multidisciplinary field that requires knowledge and skills from various domains, such as computer science, mathematics, engineering, management, communication, and domain-specific expertise.
- Software engineering is a dynamic and evolving field that responds to the challenges and opportunities of new technologies, paradigms, and applications of software systems.
