#### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews) Static testing strategy

- Formal technical reviews (FTRs) or peer reviews are a type of static testing strategy that involves a structured and systematic examination of software artifacts by a team of qualified reviewers.
- The main objectives of FTRs are to find defects, improve quality, verify compliance with standards and specifications, and facilitate knowledge transfer among the team members.
- FTRs can be applied to various software artifacts, such as requirements, design, code, test cases, user manuals, etc.
- FTRs follow a predefined process that consists of the following steps:
  - Planning: The review leader selects the review team, assigns roles and responsibilities, defines the scope and objectives of the review, and schedules the review meeting.
  - Preparation: The review team members study the software artifact to be reviewed and identify potential issues, questions, and comments. They also prepare a checklist of criteria to evaluate the artifact.
  - Review meeting: The review team meets to discuss the software artifact and share their findings and opinions. The review leader moderates the meeting and ensures that the review objectives are met and the review rules are followed. The review scribe records the issues and action items identified during the meeting.
  - Rework: The author of the software artifact corrects the defects and implements the action items reported by the review team. The author also provides evidence of the corrections and updates the artifact accordingly.
  - Follow-up: The review leader verifies that the rework has been done satisfactorily and that the software artifact meets the quality standards and specifications. The review leader also evaluates the effectiveness and efficiency of the review process and provides feedback to the review team and the management.
