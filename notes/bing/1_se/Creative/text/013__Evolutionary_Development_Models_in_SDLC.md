### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

- Evolutionary Development Models are a type of Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) models that aim to deliver software products in successive versions, each with more features and functionality than the previous one.
- Evolutionary Development Models are based on the principle of feedback and adaptation, where the software is continuously refined and improved based on the customer's needs and expectations.
- Evolutionary Development Models are suitable for projects that have unclear or changing requirements, where the customer is involved throughout the development process, and where the software needs to be delivered quickly and incrementally.
- Evolutionary Development Models can be classified into two types: **Iterative** and **Incremental**.

  - **Iterative Development Model** is a type of Evolutionary Development Model where the software is developed in cycles, each cycle consisting of planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, and evaluation. Each cycle produces a working version of the software, which is then reviewed and modified based on the feedback from the customer and the developers. The cycle is repeated until the software meets the customer's satisfaction and quality standards.
  - **Incremental Development Model** is a type of Evolutionary Development Model where the software is developed in modules, each module adding some functionality to the software. Each module is developed using a mini-waterfall model, where the requirements, design, implementation, testing, and integration are done for each module separately. The modules are then integrated into the software, which is then delivered to the customer. The process is repeated until all the modules are completed and the software is fully functional.

- Evolutionary Development Models have some advantages and disadvantages over other SDLC models.

  - Advantages of Evolutionary Development Models include:

    - They allow for flexibility and adaptability to changing requirements and customer feedback.
    - They reduce the risk of delivering a software product that does not meet the customer's needs or expectations.
    - They enable faster delivery of software products and early detection of errors and defects.
    - They facilitate customer involvement and satisfaction throughout the development process.
    - They support object-oriented software development, where the software can be easily partitioned into units or objects.

  - Disadvantages of Evolutionary Development Models include:

    - They require more management and coordination among the developers and the customer.
    - They may result in scope creep, where the software becomes more complex and costly than initially planned.
    - They may compromise the quality and consistency of the software, as the software may not undergo rigorous testing and documentation.
    - They may not be suitable for large-scale or safety-critical software projects, where the requirements and specifications need to be clearly defined and verified.
