### Protocol stack for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

- A protocol stack is an implementation of a computer networking protocol suite or protocol family that defines the communication rules and formats for different network activities.
- A protocol stack consists of layers of protocols that work together to enable the exchange of data between applications or devices.
- Wireless networking protocols are the standards and specifications that govern the operation of wireless networks, such as wireless LANs, wireless sensor networks, and wireless personal area networks.
- Wireless networking protocols can be classified into different categories based on their functions, such as physical layer, data link layer, network layer, transport layer, and application layer protocols.
- Some of the common wireless networking protocols are:

  - **IEEE 802.11**: A family of standards that define the specifications for wireless LANs, such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, and 802.11ac. These standards specify the physical layer and data link layer protocols, such as modulation schemes, channel access methods, frame formats, and security mechanisms.
  - **Bluetooth**: A technology that enables short-range wireless communication between devices, such as mobile phones, laptops, headphones, and speakers. Bluetooth uses a protocol stack that consists of four layers: core protocols, cable replacement protocols, telephony control protocols, and adopted protocols. The core protocols include the radio, baseband, link manager, and logical link control and adaptation protocols.
  - **Wireless sensor network protocols**: A set of protocols that enable the communication and coordination of wireless sensor nodes, which are small devices that can sense, process, and transmit data in a network. Wireless sensor network protocols can be divided into three categories: data-centric protocols, hierarchical protocols, and location-based protocols. Some examples of wireless sensor network protocols are LEACH, PEGASIS, SPIN, and GAF.

- Some of the challenges and issues in wireless networking protocols are:

  - **MAC issues**: The medium access control (MAC) layer is responsible for coordinating the access of multiple devices to a shared wireless medium, such as radio frequency or infrared. MAC issues include how to avoid or resolve collisions, how to allocate bandwidth, how to conserve energy, and how to provide quality of service.
  - **Interference and noise**: Wireless signals are susceptible to interference and noise from other sources, such as other wireless devices, electrical appliances, and environmental factors. Interference and noise can degrade the quality and reliability of wireless communication, and cause errors, delays, and packet losses.
  - **Security and privacy**: Wireless networks are vulnerable to various attacks, such as eavesdropping, spoofing, jamming, and denial of service. Security and privacy issues include how to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of wireless data, and how to authenticate and authorize wireless devices and users.
  - **Mobility and scalability**: Wireless networks have to support the mobility and scalability of wireless devices and users, which means that they have to adapt to the changes in network topology, connectivity, and traffic. Mobility and scalability issues include how to maintain routing, addressing, and session management, and how to handle handoffs and roaming.
