## Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM:

- Mobile computing is a technology that allows transmission of data, voice and video via a computer or any other wireless enabled device without the use of any physical link or cables .
- Mobile computing systems are computing systems that may be easily moved physically and whose computing capabilities may be used while they are being moved. Examples are laptops, personal digital assistants (PDAs), and mobile phones.
- Mobile computing involves the following components:
  - Mobile devices: These are the portable devices that can communicate wirelessly with other devices or networks. They have limited processing power, memory, and battery life. They may have different operating systems, such as Android, iOS, Windows, etc.
  - Mobile communication: This is the process of exchanging data, voice and video between mobile devices or networks using wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, satellite, etc.
  - Mobile software: This is the application software that runs on mobile devices and provides various functionalities, such as web browsing, email, gaming, social networking, etc.
- Mobile computing has many advantages, such as :
  - Mobility: Users can access information and services from anywhere and anytime.
  - Convenience: Users can perform various tasks and activities on their mobile devices without the need of a desktop computer or a fixed location.
  - Productivity: Users can increase their efficiency and effectiveness by using mobile devices for work, education, entertainment, etc.
  - Connectivity: Users can communicate and collaborate with other users or networks using wireless technologies.
  - Cost: Users can reduce the cost of hardware, software, and maintenance by using mobile devices and wireless networks.
- Mobile computing also has some challenges and issues, such as :
  - Security: Mobile devices and wireless networks are vulnerable to various threats, such as unauthorized access, data theft, malware, viruses, etc. Users need to protect their data and devices using encryption, authentication, firewall, antivirus, etc.
  - Privacy: Mobile devices and wireless networks may collect and share personal or sensitive information of users, such as location, contacts, browsing history, etc. Users need to be aware of the privacy policies and settings of the applications and services they use.
  - Quality of service: Mobile devices and wireless networks may have limited bandwidth, latency, reliability, and availability. Users may experience poor performance, delays, interruptions, or disconnections while using mobile applications and services.
  - Compatibility: Mobile devices and wireless networks may have different standards, protocols, and formats. Users may face difficulties in interoperability, integration, and synchronization of data and devices.
  - Power consumption: Mobile devices and wireless networks consume a lot of battery power. Users need to charge their devices frequently or use power-saving modes or techniques.
- Wireless telephony is a technology that enables voice communication over wireless networks using mobile phones or other devices.
- Wireless telephony has evolved from analog to digital systems over the years. The main generations of wireless telephony are:
  - 1G: The first generation of wireless telephony that used analog signals and frequency division multiple access (FDMA) technique. It supported voice calls only and had low quality and security. Examples are AMPS, NMT, TACS, etc.
  - 2G: The second generation of wireless telephony that used digital signals and time division multiple access (TDMA) or code division multiple access (CDMA) technique. It supported voice and data services and had improved quality and security. Examples are GSM, CDMA, D-AMPS, etc.
  - 3G: The third generation of wireless telephony that used wideband CDMA (WCDMA) or CDMA2000 technique. It supported voice, data, and multimedia services and had high speed and capacity. Examples are UMTS, HSPA, EV-DO, etc.
  - 4G: The fourth generation of wireless telephony that used orthogonal frequency division multiple access (OFDMA) or single carrier FDMA (SC-FDMA) technique. It supported voice, data, and multimedia services and had very high speed and capacity. Examples are LTE, WiMAX, etc.
