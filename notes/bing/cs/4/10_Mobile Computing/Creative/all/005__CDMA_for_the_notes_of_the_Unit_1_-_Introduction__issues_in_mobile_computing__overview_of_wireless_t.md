### CDMA for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing

- CDMA stands for Code Division Multiple Access and is a spread spectrum multiple access technique  .
- Spread spectrum technique spreads bandwidth of data in a uniform manner for the same transmitted power.
- CDMA is the base on which access methods such as cdmaOne, CDMA2000, and WCDMA are built.
- CDMA allows numerous signals to occupy a single transmission channel, optimizing the use of available bandwidth.
- CDMA is a form of multiplexing, which means that it combines multiple signals into one signal for transmission and then separates them at the receiver.
- CDMA uses a special coding scheme called orthogonal codes to assign a unique code to each user in the same channel .
- The codes are mathematically independent of each other and have a low cross-correlation, which means that they do not interfere with each other .
- The codes are also called spreading codes or pseudo-noise (PN) codes, because they make the signal look like noise to an unauthorized receiver .
- The codes are modulated with the data signal using a process called code division modulation (CDM), which is a form of direct sequence spread spectrum (DSSS) modulation .
- The modulated signal is then transmitted over the channel along with other users' signals .
- At the receiver, the signal is demodulated using the same code as the transmitter, which allows the receiver to extract the data signal from the noise-like signal .
- The receiver also uses a process called code division demodulation (CDD), which is a form of correlation detection, to match the received signal with the code .
- The receiver can reject any signal that does not match the code, which reduces the interference from other users and noise sources .
- CDMA has several advantages over other multiple access techniques, such as:
  - Higher spectral efficiency, which means that it can support more users per unit of bandwidth  .
  - Higher security, because the codes are hard to decipher by unauthorized receivers  .
  - Higher resistance to multipath fading, which means that it can cope with the signal distortion caused by reflections and obstacles  .
  - Higher flexibility, because the codes can be dynamically assigned and changed according to the user's needs  .
- CDMA has some disadvantages, such as:
  - Higher complexity, because the codes require sophisticated generation and detection algorithms  .
  - Higher power consumption, because the transmitter has to spread the signal over a wider bandwidth  .
  - Higher interference, because the signals from different users can still overlap and cause cross-talk  .
- CDMA is used in various wireless communication systems, such as:
  - Cellular networks, such as cdmaOne, CDMA2000, and WCDMA, which are based on the IS-95, IS-2000, and UMTS standards respectively  .
  - Satellite networks, such as Globalstar and Iridium, which use CDMA to communicate with multiple satellites and ground stations  .
  - Wireless local area networks (WLANs), such as IEEE 802.11b, which use CDMA to share the same frequency band among multiple devices  .
  - Wireless personal area networks (WPANs), such as Bluetooth, which use CDMA to connect multiple devices in a short range  .
- A possible mnemonic to remember the main features of CDMA is:

  - C: Codes
  - D: Division
