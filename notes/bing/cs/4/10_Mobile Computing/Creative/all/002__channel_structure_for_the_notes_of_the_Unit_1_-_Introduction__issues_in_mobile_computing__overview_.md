### Channel structure for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing

- GSM stands for Global System for Mobile communication, and it is a standard for mobile communication that uses digital technology   .
- GSM is based on the cellular concept, which means that the network is divided into cells, each served by a base station. The cells can vary in size and shape depending on the terrain and traffic density  .
- GSM uses a combination of frequency division multiple access (FDMA) and time division multiple access (TDMA) to allocate channels to multiple users in a cell. FDMA divides the frequency band into 200 kHz channels, and TDMA divides each channel into eight 25 kHz time slots  .
- GSM operates on different frequency bands in different regions of the world. The most common bands are 900 MHz and 1800 MHz in Europe, Asia, Africa and Australia, and 850 MHz and 1900 MHz in North and South America  .
- The GSM network consists of four main components: the mobile station (MS), the base station subsystem (BSS), the network switching subsystem (NSS) and the operation and support subsystem (OSS) .
- The mobile station is the user's device that communicates with the network via radio waves. It consists of two parts: the mobile equipment (ME), which is the hardware and software of the device, and the subscriber identity module (SIM), which is a smart card that stores the user's identity and authentication information .
- The base station subsystem is the part of the network that connects the mobile stations to the core network. It consists of two elements: the base transceiver station (BTS), which is the radio equipment that transmits and receives signals in a cell, and the base station controller (BSC), which controls and manages the BTSs and the radio resources .
- The network switching subsystem is the core of the network that performs the switching and routing of calls and data between different cells and networks. It consists of several nodes, such as the mobile switching center (MSC), which is the main switch that connects the BSS to the public switched telephone network (PSTN) or other networks, the home location register (HLR), which is a database that stores the user's profile and location information, the visitor location register (VLR), which is a temporary database that stores the user's information when roaming in a different area, the authentication center (AUC), which is a security module that verifies the user's identity and encryption keys, and the equipment identity register (EIR), which is a database that stores the serial numbers of the mobile equipment .
- The operation and support subsystem is the part of the network that monitors and controls the performance and maintenance of the network. It consists of several functions, such as the operation and maintenance center (OMC), which is a central station that oversees the network elements and collects statistics and alarms, the network management center (NMC), which is a higher-level station that coordinates the OMCs and provides a global view of the network, and the billing center (BC), which is a module that collects and processes the billing data from the network .

- A possible mnemonic to remember the four main components of the GSM network is: **M**y **B**est **N**etwork **O**ffers **S**ervice (MS, BSS, NSS, OSS).
- A possible mnemonic to remember the frequency bands of GSM is: **N**ine **E**ighty **A**nd **N**ine **T**een **A**re **G**SM (900, 1800, 850, 1900).
