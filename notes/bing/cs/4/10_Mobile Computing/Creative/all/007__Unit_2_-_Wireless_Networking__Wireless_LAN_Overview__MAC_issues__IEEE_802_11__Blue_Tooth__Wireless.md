## Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

- Wireless networking is the use of wireless technologies to connect devices and networks without wires or cables.
- Wireless LAN (WLAN) is a type of wireless network that covers a local area, such as a home, office, or campus, using radio waves.
- WLANs are based on the IEEE 802.11 standard, which defines the medium access control (MAC) and physical layer (PHY) specifications for wireless communication.
- MAC issues are the challenges and solutions for coordinating the access of multiple wireless devices to the shared wireless medium, avoiding collisions, and ensuring fairness and efficiency.
- IEEE 802.11 is the most widely used WLAN standard, which has several variants and amendments to support different frequencies, data rates, modulation schemes, security features, and quality of service.
- Blue Tooth is a wireless technology that enables short-range communication between devices, such as phones, headsets, keyboards, mice, printers, etc.
- Blue Tooth uses a frequency-hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) technique to avoid interference and provide security and reliability.
- Blue Tooth is based on the IEEE 802.15.1 standard, which defines the MAC and PHY specifications for wireless personal area networks (WPANs).
- Wireless is a general term that refers to any type of communication that does not use wires or cables, such as cellular, satellite, radio, infrared, etc.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for this unit are:

- To remember the IEEE 802.11 standard, think of the word "WiFi" as an acronym for "Wireless Fidelity".
- To remember the IEEE 802.15.1 standard, think of the word "Blue Tooth" as an acronym for "Bluetooth Low Energy Technology".
- To remember the difference between WLAN and WPAN, think of the word "LAN" as an acronym for "Large Area Network" and the word "PAN" as an acronym for "Personal Area Network".
- To remember the frequency-hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) technique, think of a frog that hops from one lily pad to another in a pond. Each lily pad represents a frequency channel and the frog represents the signal.
