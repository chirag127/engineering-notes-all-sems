### Channel allocation in cellular systems

- Channel allocation means to allocate the available channels to the cells in a cellular system .
- Channels in a wireless communication system typically consist of time slots, frequency bands and/or CDMA pseudo noise sequences.
- Channel allocation strategies are designed to achieve efficient use of frequencies, time slots and bandwidth, while minimizing interference and maximizing quality of service .
- There are three main types of channel allocation schemes: fixed channel allocation (FCA), dynamic channel allocation (DCA) and hybrid channel allocation (HCA) .
- Fixed channel allocation (FCA) assigns a fixed number of channels to each cell, regardless of the traffic demand. The channels are reused in different cells according to a reuse pattern or reuse factor .
- Dynamic channel allocation (DCA) assigns channels to cells on demand, based on the traffic load and the interference conditions. The channels are not fixed to any cell and can be reused more flexibly .
- Hybrid channel allocation (HCA) combines FCA and DCA, by dividing the channels into two sets: one for FCA and one for DCA. The FCA set provides a minimum service level, while the DCA set adapts to the traffic variations and the interference situation .
- A simple mnemonic to remember the three types of channel allocation schemes is: **F**ixed, **D**ynamic and **H**ybrid.
- A simple learning trick to understand the trade-offs between FCA and DCA is to compare them to a restaurant reservation system. FCA is like reserving a fixed number of tables for each group of customers, regardless of the demand. This ensures a guaranteed service level, but may result in wasted resources or unsatisfied customers. DCA is like assigning tables to customers on a first-come, first-served basis, based on the availability and the size of the group. This optimizes the resource utilization, but may cause delays or interference.
- An example of a cellular system that uses FCA is GSM, which divides the frequency spectrum into 124 carrier frequencies, each with 8 time slots. Each cell is assigned a subset of these channels, according to a reuse pattern.
- An example of a cellular system that uses DCA is UMTS, which uses CDMA to spread the signals over a wide frequency band. Each cell can use the entire frequency band, but the number of users that can be supported depends on the interference level and the quality of service requirements.
