### QoS in Ad Hoc Networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Ad Hoc networks, localization, MAC issues, Routing protocols, global state routing (GSR), in the subject of Mobile Computing

- QoS stands for Quality of Service, which is the performance level of service offered by a network to the user.
- QoS is important for ad hoc networks, which are self-organizing networks of mobile nodes that communicate using wireless links without any fixed infrastructure or centralized control.
- QoS requirements arise at the application layer in the form of restrictions on values of certain QoS metrics, such as bandwidth, delay, jitter, packet loss, reliability, etc .
- QoS support in ad hoc networks is challenging due to the following factors  :
  - Dynamic topology: The network topology changes frequently and unpredictably due to node mobility, which affects the availability and stability of routes and links.
  - Limited resources: The network resources, such as bandwidth, energy, and memory, are scarce and shared among multiple nodes and flows, which leads to contention and congestion.
  - Interference: The wireless medium is prone to interference from neighboring nodes and external sources, which degrades the link quality and causes packet loss and errors.
  - Heterogeneity: The network may consist of nodes with different capabilities, such as transmission power, battery life, processing speed, etc, which creates asymmetry and diversity in the network.
- QoS support in ad hoc networks involves the following components  :
  - QoS model: This defines the QoS parameters, semantics, and guarantees that the network can offer to the user. Examples of QoS models are IntServ, DiffServ, and Best Effort.
  - QoS provisioning: This refers to the mechanisms and protocols that allocate and manage the network resources to meet the QoS requirements of the user. Examples of QoS provisioning are admission control, resource reservation, and traffic shaping.
  - QoS routing: This refers to the process of finding and maintaining routes that satisfy the QoS requirements of the user. Examples of QoS routing protocols are QoS-AODV, QoS-OLSR, and QoS-DSDV.
  - QoS monitoring: This refers to the process of measuring and reporting the QoS metrics of the network and detecting any QoS violations or degradation. Examples of QoS monitoring techniques are passive and active measurements, feedback, and probing.
- QoS in ad hoc networks is a trade-off between the QoS performance and the network overhead. Higher QoS performance may require more network overhead, such as signaling, control, and maintenance messages, which consume bandwidth, energy, and memory. Lower network overhead may result in lower QoS performance, such as longer delay, higher jitter, and lower reliability  .
- QoS in ad hoc networks is also a trade-off between the QoS optimization and the QoS adaptation. QoS optimization aims to find the optimal routes and resources that meet the QoS requirements of the user. QoS adaptation aims to adjust the QoS requirements and parameters according to the network conditions and user preferences. QoS optimization may be more suitable for applications that have strict and static QoS requirements, such as voice and video. QoS adaptation may be more suitable for applications that have flexible and dynamic QoS requirements, such as web browsing and email  .
