### GPRS for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing

- GPRS stands for **General Packet Radio Service**    .
- It is a **packet oriented** wireless data communication service for mobile communications on **2G and 3G** cellular communication systems   .
- It is **non-voice**, high speed packet switching technology intended for **GSM** networks   .
- It enables **moderately high-speed** data transfers using packet-based technologies  .
- It offers more data transmission options for GSM-based devices, as GSM networks at the time could only use **Short Message Service (SMS)**, for example, to transmit a small amount of data.
- It supports a wide range of **applications** such as web browsing, email, multimedia messaging, location-based services, online gaming, and IoT   .
- It uses **multiple time slots** in a GSM radio channel to transmit and receive data packets  .
- It can achieve a **theoretical maximum speed** of up to 171.2 kbps by using all eight time slots in a channel  .
- It can also support **multiple users** sharing the same channel by using **dynamic allocation** of time slots  .
- It has two main **classes** of devices: Class A and Class B  .
  - Class A devices can support **simultaneous** voice and data services  .
  - Class B devices can support **alternating** voice and data services  .
- It has three main **network elements**: Mobile Station (MS), Base Station Subsystem (BSS), and Serving GPRS Support Node (SGSN)  .
  - MS is the **mobile device** that supports GPRS  .
  - BSS is the **radio access network** that connects the MS to the SGSN  .
  - SGSN is the **gateway** that handles the packet routing, mobility management, and authentication of the MS  .
- It has four main **operational modes**: Idle, Ready, Standby, and Transfer  .
  - Idle mode is when the MS is **not attached** to the GPRS network  .
  - Ready mode is when the MS is **attached** to the GPRS network and has a **temporary logical link** with the SGSN  .
  - Standby mode is when the MS is **attached** to the GPRS network but has **no logical link** with the SGSN  .
  - Transfer mode is when the MS is **transmitting or receiving** data packets over the GPRS network  .
- It has some **advantages** such as:
  - Higher data rates than circuit-switched services   .
  - More efficient use of radio spectrum and network resources   .
  - Flexible and scalable network architecture   .
  - Seamless integration with existing GSM and IP networks   .
  - Support for a variety of applications
