The wireless devices are the end-user devices that communicate with the wireless access points (WAPs) using radio waves. The WAPs are the devices that connect the wireless devices to the wired network. The wired devices are the devices that communicate with the wireless devices through the WAPs using cables. The wireless devices, the WAPs, and the wired devices form a wireless local area network (WLAN). The WLAN can use different standards and protocols, such as IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, or Wi-Fi, to enable wireless communication. The wireless devices can also communicate with each other directly using peer-to-peer or ad-hoc modes. The wireless applications are the software programs that run on the wireless devices and provide various services and functions, such as web browsing, email, messaging, gaming, etc. The wireless applications can use different technologies and architectures, such as client-server, cloud computing, or mobile edge computing, to access and process data. The wireless applications can also use different security and privacy mechanisms, such as encryption, authentication, or authorization, to protect the data and the users. The wireless applications are the main focus of the subject of mobile computing, which studies the design, development, and evaluation of wireless applications and systems.
