### Channel structure

- A channel is a logical or physical communication path between two or more devices in a network.
- A channel structure defines how the channels are allocated and used in a cellular network, such as GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications).
- A channel structure consists of two main components: frequency channels and time slots.

#### Frequency channels

- A frequency channel is a portion of the radio spectrum that is assigned to a cellular operator for communication.
- A frequency channel has a fixed bandwidth and a center frequency.
- A frequency channel can be divided into sub-channels or carriers, each of which can carry one or more voice or data signals.
- The number of frequency channels available for a cellular network depends on the spectrum allocation and the channel spacing.
- For example, GSM uses a 25 MHz spectrum in the 900 MHz band, with a channel spacing of 200 kHz, resulting in 124 frequency channels .

#### Time slots

- A time slot is a fixed duration of time that is allocated to a user or a device for transmitting or receiving data on a frequency channel.
- A time slot can be divided into bursts or frames, each of which can carry a portion of a voice or data signal.
- The number of time slots available for a frequency channel depends on the multiplexing scheme and the modulation technique.
- For example, GSM uses a time division multiple access (TDMA) scheme, which divides each frequency channel into eight time slots, each of 0.577 ms duration .
- Each time slot can carry one voice or data call, resulting in eight calls per frequency channel.
