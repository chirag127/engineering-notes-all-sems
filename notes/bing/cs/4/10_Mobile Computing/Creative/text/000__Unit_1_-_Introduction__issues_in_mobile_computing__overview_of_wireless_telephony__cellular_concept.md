## Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM:

- Mobile computing is the use of portable devices that can access and process data over wireless networks. Mobile computing enables users to communicate, work, and access information anytime and anywhere.
- Some of the issues in mobile computing are:
  - Limited battery life and power consumption of mobile devices.
  - Limited bandwidth and reliability of wireless networks.
  - Security and privacy of data and communication over wireless networks.
  - Heterogeneity and interoperability of mobile devices and wireless networks.
  - Mobility and location management of mobile devices and users.
  - User interface and usability of mobile applications and services.
- Wireless telephony is the transmission of voice and data over wireless networks using radio waves. Wireless telephony can be classified into two types: cellular and cordless.
- Cellular telephony is a system of wireless communication that divides a geographical area into smaller regions called cells. Each cell is served by a base station that communicates with mobile stations (such as mobile phones) within its coverage area. Cellular telephony allows multiple users to share the same frequency spectrum by using frequency division multiple access (FDMA) and time division multiple access (TDMA) techniques.
- GSM (Global System for Mobile communication) is a standard developed by the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) to describe the protocols for second-generation digital cellular networks used by mobile devices such as mobile phones and tablets. GSM uses a variation of TDMA and is the most widely used of the three digital wireless telephony technologies: TDMA, GSM and code-division multiple access (CDMA).
- GSM has four main components: mobile stations, base station subsystems, network and switching subsystems, and operation and support subsystems. GSM provides services such as voice calls, text messages, data transmission, roaming, and encryption. GSM operates in four different frequency bands: 850 MHz, 900 MHz, 1800 MHz and 1900 MHz.
