### Disconnected operations

- Disconnected operations are a mode of working in mobile computing that allows users to execute applications when they are not connected to the network or when they choose to work offline .
- Disconnected operations can be voluntary or involuntary, depending on the user's intention and the network availability .
- Disconnected operations can improve the availability, performance, and energy efficiency of mobile applications, but they also introduce challenges such as data consistency, synchronization, and security .
- Disconnected operations can be supported by various techniques, such as:
  - Data replication: creating and maintaining multiple copies of data on different devices, such as servers, mobile clients, or proxies .
  - Data caching: storing frequently accessed or recently used data on the local device to reduce network traffic and latency .
  - Data hoarding: prefetching or preloading data that is likely to be needed in the future on the local device to avoid network dependency .
  - Mobile computation: transferring the execution of applications or parts of applications from one device to another, such as from a server to a mobile client or vice versa .
  - Adaptive clustering: grouping mobile devices that are geographically close or have similar interests or behaviors into clusters, and using cluster leaders or representatives to communicate with the network or other clusters .
- Disconnected operations require mechanisms to handle data conflicts, data reconciliation, and data updates when the devices reconnect to the network or to each other .
- Disconnected operations can be classified into different levels or modes, such as:
  - Fully connected: the device has full and continuous access to the network and the data sources.
  - Weakly connected: the device has intermittent or low-quality access to the network and the data sources, and may experience delays or errors.
  - Occasionally connected: the device has periodic or scheduled access to the network and the data sources, and may use data replication or caching techniques to work offline.
  - Disconnected: the device has no access to the network and the data sources, and may use data hoarding or mobile computation techniques to work offline.
