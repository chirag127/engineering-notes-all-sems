### Air-interface

- Air-interface is the communication link between the two stations in mobile or wireless communication.
- Air-interface involves both the physical and data link layers (layer 1 and 2) of the OSI model for a connection.
- Air-interface defines the frequency, channel bandwidth and modulation scheme for the radio transmission between mobile devices and the base station in a cellular network.
- Air-interface is also known as the radio interface or the access mode .
- Air-interface is the radio-frequency portion of the circuit between the cellular phone set or wireless modem and the active base station in cellular telephone communications.
- Air-interface changes periodically as a subscriber moves from one cell to another in the system.
- Air-interface in GSM is also known as UM interface, which is the interface between the mobile station (MS) and the base transceiver station (BTS).
- Air-interface in GSM uses a combination of frequency division multiple access (FDMA) and time division multiple access (TDMA) to allow multiple users to share the same frequency band.
- Air-interface in GSM uses Gaussian minimum shift keying (GMSK) as the modulation technique, which reduces the bandwidth and interference of the signal.
- Air-interface in GSM operates in different frequency bands depending on the region, such as 900 MHz, 1800 MHz, or 1900 MHz.
