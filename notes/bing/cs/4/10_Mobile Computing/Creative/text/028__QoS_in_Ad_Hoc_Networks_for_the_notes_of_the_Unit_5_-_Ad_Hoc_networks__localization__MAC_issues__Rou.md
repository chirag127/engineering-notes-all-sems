### QoS in Ad Hoc Networks

- QoS stands for Quality of Service, which is the performance level of service offered by a network to the user.
- QoS is important for ad hoc networks, which are self-organizing networks of mobile nodes that communicate without any fixed infrastructure or centralized control.
- QoS requirements arise at the application layer in the form of restrictions on values of certain QoS metrics, such as bandwidth, delay, jitter, packet loss, reliability, etc.
- QoS support in ad hoc networks is challenging due to the dynamic topology, limited resources, interference, mobility, and heterogeneity of the nodes and applications.
- QoS support in ad hoc networks can be achieved at different layers of the network protocol stack, such as MAC, routing, transport, and application.
- QoS support at the MAC layer involves mechanisms for channel access, scheduling, reservation, and prioritization of packets based on their QoS requirements.
- QoS support at the routing layer involves algorithms for finding and maintaining routes that satisfy the QoS requirements of the flows, as well as adapting to network changes and congestion.
- QoS support at the transport layer involves mechanisms for end-to-end flow control, congestion control, error control, and reliability, as well as adapting to the QoS feedback from the lower layers.
- QoS support at the application layer involves mechanisms for adapting the application behavior and parameters to the available QoS, such as adaptive coding, compression, scaling, etc.
- QoS support in ad hoc networks requires cross-layer design and coordination among different layers, as well as cooperation and fairness among the nodes.
