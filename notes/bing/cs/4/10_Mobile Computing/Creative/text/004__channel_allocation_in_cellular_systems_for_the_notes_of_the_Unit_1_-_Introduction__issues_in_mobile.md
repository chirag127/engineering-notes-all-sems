### Channel allocation in cellular systems

- Channel allocation is the process of assigning channels to cells in a cellular system to enable communication between users and base stations.
- Channels are the basic units of radio resources that can be used for transmitting and receiving signals over the wireless medium.
- Channels can be defined by different parameters, such as frequency, time, code, or spatial location, depending on the modulation and multiple access techniques used by the system.
- Channel allocation aims to maximize the system capacity, which is the number of users that can be supported by the system, while minimizing the interference, which is the unwanted signal that degrades the quality of communication.
- Channel allocation can be classified into three main categories :

  - Fixed channel allocation (FCA): Each cell is assigned a fixed number of channels that cannot be changed or shared with other cells. FCA is simple to implement but may result in inefficient use of channels and low system capacity.
  - Dynamic channel allocation (DCA): Channels are allocated to cells on demand, based on the traffic load and interference conditions. DCA can adapt to the variations in traffic and interference, but requires more complex coordination and signaling between cells and base stations.
  - Hybrid channel allocation (HCA): A combination of FCA and DCA, where some channels are fixed and some are dynamic. HCA can balance the trade-off between simplicity and adaptability, but may require more sophisticated algorithms and optimization techniques.

- Channel allocation is one of the key challenges in wireless and cellular networks, as it affects the performance, reliability, and scalability of the system. Channel allocation is also influenced by the cellular concept, which is the division of the service area into smaller regions called cells, each served by a base station .
- The cellular concept enables the reuse of channels across different cells, as long as the distance between the cells using the same channel is large enough to avoid interference. The reuse of channels increases the system capacity and allows more users to access the network.
- The cellular concept also introduces the need for handoff, which is the process of transferring the communication of a user from one cell to another, as the user moves across the service area. Handoff requires the coordination of channel allocation between the old and new cells, and may cause interruptions or delays in the communication.
