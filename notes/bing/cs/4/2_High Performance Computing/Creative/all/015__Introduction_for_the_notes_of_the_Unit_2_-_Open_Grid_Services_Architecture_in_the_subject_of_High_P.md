### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 2 - Open Grid Services Architecture in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Open Grid Services Architecture (OGSA) is a set of standards that extends Web services and service-oriented architecture to the grid computing environment for business and scientific use .
- OGSA was developed within the Open Grid Forum, which was called the Global Grid Forum (GGF) at the time, around 2002 to 2006.
- OGSA defines how information is shared and distributed among the components of large, heterogeneous grid systems; they apply to hardware, platforms and software.
- OGSA uses most of Web service technologies, notably WSDL and SOAP, but it aims to be largely agnostic in relation to the transport-level handling of data upon the grid.
- OGSA specifies a core set of interfaces, behaviors, resource models, and bindings that address key concerns in grid systems, such as security, discovery, monitoring, management, and virtualization.
- OGSA is based on the concept of grid services, which are stateful Web services that support the creation, naming, lifetime management, and notification of dynamic and transient service instances.
- OGSA is designed to be modular, extensible, and interoperable, allowing for the integration of existing and emerging technologies and standards.
