### Cluster Computer and its Architecture for the notes of the Unit 3 - Overview of Cluster Computing in the subject of High Performance Computing

- A cluster computer is a set of connected computers that work together as a single system    .
- The connected computers are called nodes, and they can be personal computers, workstations, servers, or supercomputers  .
- A cluster computer can be used to enhance the processing power, increase the resilience, or provide high availability of a system  .
- A cluster computer needs management nodes that coordinate the load sharing, detect node failure, and schedule its replacement.
- A cluster computer can be classified into different types based on its architecture, such as:
  - High-performance computing (HPC) clusters: These clusters are designed to provide high-speed computation for scientific or engineering applications  . They use fast interconnects, such as InfiniBand or Gigabit Ethernet, to communicate between nodes . They can also use specialized hardware, such as GPUs or FPGAs, to accelerate the computation . An example of an HPC cluster is the Summit supercomputer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory.
  - High-availability (HA) clusters: These clusters are designed to provide continuous operation for critical applications, such as databases or web servers  . They use redundant nodes, storage, and network devices to prevent single points of failure . They can also use failover mechanisms, such as heartbeat or quorum, to detect and recover from node failure . An example of an HA cluster is the Google File System (GFS) that supports the Google search engine.
  - Load-balancing clusters: These clusters are designed to distribute the workload among multiple nodes to improve the performance and scalability of an application  . They use load-balancing algorithms, such as round-robin or least-connection, to assign requests to the nodes . They can also use caching or replication techniques to reduce the network traffic and improve the response time . An example of a load-balancing cluster is the Apache HTTP Server that supports many web applications.
  - Grid computing clusters: These clusters are designed to share the resources of multiple geographically distributed clusters for large-scale applications, such as scientific research or data analysis  . They use grid middleware, such as Globus Toolkit or Condor, to coordinate the resource allocation, scheduling, and execution of the tasks . They can also use virtualization or cloud computing techniques to provide dynamic and flexible resource provisioning . An example of a grid computing cluster is the Large Hadron Collider Computing Grid (LHCb) that supports the particle physics experiments at CERN.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the types of cluster computer architectures is **HALL** (High-performance, High-availability, Load-balancing, Grid).
- A possible learning trick to understand the difference between the types of cluster computer architectures is to use an analogy of a restaurant:
  - A high-performance computing cluster is like a restaurant that has many chefs who work together to cook complex dishes for a few customers who order them.
  - A high-availability cluster is like a restaurant that has backup chefs, ingredients, and equipment to ensure that the customers always get their dishes without any delay or interruption.
  - A load-balancing cluster is like a restaurant that has many waiters who serve the dishes to many customers who order them, and can also store or make copies of the dishes to speed up the service.
  - A grid computing cluster is like a network of restaurants that share their chefs, ingredients, and equipment to cook and serve dishes for many customers who order them from different locations.
