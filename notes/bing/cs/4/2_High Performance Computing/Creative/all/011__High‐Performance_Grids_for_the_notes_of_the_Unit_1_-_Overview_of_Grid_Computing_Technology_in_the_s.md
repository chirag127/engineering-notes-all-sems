### High‐Performance Grids for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Grid computing is a form of distributed computing that involves coordinating and sharing computing resources across multiple locations, organizations, and domains.
- Grid computing is distinguished from conventional high-performance computing systems such as cluster computing in that grid computers have each node set to perform a different task/application.
- Grid computing can harness the power of an arbitrarily large collection of computing resources to meet the needs of compute intensive high performance computing (HPC) applications such as finite element model (FEM) simulations.
- Grid computing can also enable the sharing of data, software, and services among geographically dispersed users and organizations.
- Grid computing can be classified into three types based on the level of resource sharing and coordination:
  - Computational grid: A grid that focuses on providing high-performance computing power for CPU-intensive applications.
  - Data grid: A grid that focuses on providing high-performance data access and management for data-intensive applications.
  - Service grid: A grid that focuses on providing high-performance access to various services and applications.
- Grid computing can provide several benefits for HPC applications, such as  :
  - Scalability: Grid computing can scale up or down the computing resources according to the demand and availability of the applications.
  - Cost-effectiveness: Grid computing can reduce the cost of acquiring and maintaining dedicated supercomputers or clusters by utilizing existing or idle resources across multiple locations and domains.
  - Reliability: Grid computing can increase the reliability and availability of the applications by providing fault tolerance and load balancing mechanisms across the grid nodes.
  - Collaboration: Grid computing can facilitate the collaboration and sharing of data, software, and services among different users and organizations with common or complementary goals and interests.
- Grid computing can also pose several challenges for HPC applications, such as  :
  - Security: Grid computing can introduce security risks and vulnerabilities due to the heterogeneity and openness of the grid environment and the involvement of multiple parties and domains.
  - Performance: Grid computing can affect the performance and efficiency of the applications due to the network latency, bandwidth, and congestion issues and the overhead of coordination and communication among the grid nodes.
  - Complexity: Grid computing can increase the complexity and difficulty of developing, deploying, and managing the applications due to the diversity and dynamism of the grid resources and the lack of standardization and interoperability among the grid middleware and protocols.
  - Usability: Grid computing can require the users and developers to have a high level of technical knowledge and skills to use and access the grid resources and services and to deal with the grid challenges and issues.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the benefits and challenges of grid computing for HPC applications is:

  - **S**calability, **C**ost-effectiveness, **R**eliability, **C**ollaboration (**SCRC**) are the benefits of grid computing.
  - **S**ecurity, **P**erformance, **C**omplexity, **U**sability (**SPCU**) are the challenges of grid computing.
