### Peer‐to‐Peer Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Peer-to-peer (P2P) computing is a distributed application architecture that partitions tasks or workloads between peers.
- Peers are equally privileged, equipotent participants in the network. They are said to form a peer-to-peer network of nodes.
- In a P2P network, the peers are computer systems which are connected to each other via the Internet. Files can be shared directly between systems on the network without the need of a central server. In other words, each computer on a P2P network becomes a file server as well as a client.
- P2P computing can be used for various purposes, such as file sharing, content distribution, collaborative computing, social networking, and distributed computing.
- P2P computing has some advantages over the traditional client-server model, such as:
  - Scalability: P2P networks can handle a large number of nodes and requests without a single point of failure or bottleneck.
  - Fault tolerance: P2P networks can tolerate node failures and network disruptions without affecting the overall performance or availability.
  - Resource utilization: P2P networks can leverage the idle resources of the peers, such as CPU, memory, disk space, and bandwidth, to provide better service quality and efficiency.
  - Autonomy: P2P networks allow the peers to control their own resources and data, without relying on a central authority or intermediary.
- P2P computing also has some challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Security: P2P networks are vulnerable to various attacks, such as malware, spam, denial-of-service, and Sybil attacks, due to the lack of centralized authentication and authorization mechanisms.
  - Privacy: P2P networks expose the peers' identities and activities to other peers, which may compromise their anonymity and confidentiality.
  - Quality of service: P2P networks may suffer from low quality of service, such as latency, jitter, and packet loss, due to the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of the peers and the network.
  - Incentives: P2P networks may face the problem of free riding, where some peers consume more resources than they contribute, or cheating, where some peers provide false or malicious information, due to the lack of incentives or reputation systems.
- A mnemonic to remember the advantages and challenges of P2P computing is **SPARQ** (Scalability, Privacy, Autonomy, Resource utilization, Quality of service) and **SQIP** (Security, Quality of service, Incentives, Privacy). The common letters (Q and P) can be used to link the two acronyms. For example, Q can stand for Quality of service, which is both an advantage and a challenge, depending on the context. P can stand for Privacy, which is also a double-edged sword in P2P computing.
