### Types of Cloud for the notes of the Unit 5 - Overview of Cloud Computing in the subject of High Performance Computing

Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services over the internet, such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence. Cloud computing can be classified into two main types: deployment models and service models.

#### Deployment models

Deployment models refer to the location and management of the cloud's infrastructure. There are three main types of deployment models:

- **Public cloud**: The cloud infrastructure is owned and operated by a third-party cloud service provider, such as Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google Cloud Platform (GCP), or Microsoft Azure. The cloud service provider offers computing resources and services to the public over the internet, on a pay-as-you-go or subscription basis. Public clouds are suitable for applications that require scalability, elasticity, and cost-efficiency, such as web hosting, e-commerce, social media, and online gaming.

- **Private cloud**: The cloud infrastructure is owned and operated by a single organization, or a third-party service provider on behalf of the organization. The cloud services are accessible only to the authorized users of the organization, over a private network or the internet. Private clouds are suitable for applications that require high security, privacy, compliance, and performance, such as banking, healthcare, and government.

- **Hybrid cloud**: The cloud infrastructure is a combination of public and private clouds, connected by a secure network. The hybrid cloud allows the organization to leverage the benefits of both public and private clouds, depending on the needs and characteristics of the applications. For example, an organization can use the public cloud for non-sensitive workloads, such as web servers, and the private cloud for sensitive workloads, such as databases.

#### Service models

Service models refer to the level of abstraction and control that the cloud service provider offers to the cloud users. There are three main types of service models:

- **Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)**: The cloud service provider offers the basic computing infrastructure, such as servers, storage, networking, and virtualization. The cloud user can provision, configure, and manage the infrastructure according to their needs, and run any software or application on top of it. IaaS is suitable for applications that require low-level access and control over the infrastructure, such as high-performance computing, big data analytics, and web crawling.

- **Platform as a Service (PaaS)**: The cloud service provider offers the computing platform, such as operating system, middleware, runtime, and development tools. The cloud user can develop, deploy, and run their applications on the platform, without worrying about the underlying infrastructure. PaaS is suitable for applications that require rapid and agile development, such as web applications, mobile applications, and microservices.

- **Software as a Service (SaaS)**: The cloud service provider offers the software application, such as email, office suite, CRM, or ERP. The cloud user can access and use the application over the internet, without installing or maintaining it. SaaS is suitable for applications that require high availability, scalability, and convenience, such as collaboration, communication, and productivity.

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

Here are some mnemonics and learning tricks to help you remember the types of cloud computing:

- For the deployment models, you can use the acronym **PPH** (Public, Private, Hybrid), and associate it with the word **pH** (a measure of acidity or alkalinity). You can remember that public clouds are more **acidic**, meaning they are more exposed and shared with others, while private clouds are more **alkaline**, meaning they are more isolated and exclusive to the organization. Hybrid clouds are somewhere in between, depending on the mixture of public and private clouds.

- For the service models, you can use the acronym **SPI** (Software, Platform, Infrastructure), and associate it with the word **spy** (a person who secretly collects information). You can remember that SaaS is the most **secretive**, meaning it hides the most details from the cloud user, while IaaS is the least **secretive**, meaning it reveals the most details to the cloud user. PaaS is somewhere in between, depending on the level of abstraction and control.
