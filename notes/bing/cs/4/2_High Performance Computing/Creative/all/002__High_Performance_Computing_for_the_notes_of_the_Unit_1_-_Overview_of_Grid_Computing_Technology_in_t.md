### High Performance Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

- High Performance Computing (HPC) is the use of specialized hardware and software to achieve fast and efficient execution of complex and large-scale computational tasks.
- HPC systems typically consist of multiple processors, memory, storage, and network devices that work together to perform parallel and distributed computing.
- HPC applications can range from scientific simulations, data analysis, artificial intelligence, machine learning, image processing, cryptography, and more.
- Grid Computing is a form of HPC that involves the use of widely distributed and heterogeneous computer resources to reach a common goal .
- Grid Computing can be thought of as a distributed system with non-interactive workloads that involve many files.
- Grid Computing is different from conventional HPC systems such as cluster computing in that grid computers have each node set to perform a different task or application, and the nodes are not physically coupled or geographically co-located .
- Grid Computing enables the sharing and aggregation of resources across multiple domains, such as organizations, institutions, or regions .
- Grid Computing can provide benefits such as scalability, fault tolerance, load balancing, resource utilization, and cost efficiency .
- Grid Computing can also pose challenges such as security, interoperability, scheduling, coordination, and quality of service .
- Grid Computing can be used for various purposes, such as scientific research, engineering, business, education, health care, and social services  .
- Grid Computing can be classified into different types, such as computational grids, data grids, service grids, and knowledge grids, depending on the nature and function of the resources involved .
- Grid Computing can be implemented using different architectures, such as hierarchical, decentralized, or hybrid, depending on the level of control and coordination among the grid components .
- Grid Computing can be supported by different middleware, such as Globus Toolkit, Condor, BOINC, and gLite, that provide the necessary services and protocols for grid operation and management .

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for Grid Computing are:

- To remember the difference between grid computing and cluster computing, think of a grid as a **G**eographically dispersed and **R**esource heterogeneous **I**nterconnected **D**istributed system, while a cluster is a **C**o-located and **L**inked **U**niform **S**ystem of **T**ightly coupled **E**lements for **R**apid computation.
- To remember the benefits of grid computing, think of the acronym **SCALF** which stands for **S**calability, **C**ost efficiency, **A**vailability, **L**oad balancing, and **F**ault tolerance.
- To remember the challenges of grid computing, think of the acronym **SISSQ** which stands for **S**ecurity, **I**nteroperability, **S**cheduling, **S**ynchronization, and **Q**uality of service.
- To remember the types of grid computing, think of the acronym **CDSK** which stands for **C**omputational, **D**ata, **S**ervice, and **K**nowledge grids.
- To remember some examples of grid computing middleware, think of the acronym **GCBG** which stands for **G**lobus, **C**ondor, **B**OINC, and **g**Lite.
