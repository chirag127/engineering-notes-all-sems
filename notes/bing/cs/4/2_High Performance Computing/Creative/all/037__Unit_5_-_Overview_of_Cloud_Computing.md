## Unit 5 - Overview of Cloud Computing

- Cloud computing is a model for enabling **ubiquitous**, **convenient**, **on-demand** network access to a **shared pool** of **configurable** computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services) that can be **rapidly provisioned** and **released** with **minimal management effort** or **service provider interaction**.
- Cloud computing also refers to the **technology** that makes cloud work. This includes some form of **virtualized IT infrastructure**— servers, operating system software, networking, and other infrastructure that’s **abstracted**, using special software, so that it can be **pooled** and **divided** irrespective of physical hardware boundaries.
- Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services—including servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence—over the **Internet** (“the cloud”) to offer **faster innovation**, **flexible resources**, and **economies of scale**.

### Advantages of Cloud Computing

- Cloud computing offers many benefits to businesses and individuals, such as:
  - **Cost savings**: Cloud computing eliminates the need for buying, maintaining, and upgrading expensive hardware and software. Users only pay for the resources they use, and can scale up or down as needed.
  - **Performance**: Cloud computing provides access to high-performance computing resources that can handle large volumes of data and complex calculations. Cloud services are also constantly updated and improved by the service providers.
  - **Reliability**: Cloud computing ensures data backup and recovery, as well as fault tolerance and redundancy. Cloud services are also available from anywhere with an internet connection, and can support multiple devices and platforms.
  - **Security**: Cloud computing protects data and applications from unauthorized access, cyberattacks, and natural disasters. Cloud service providers use encryption, firewalls, authentication, and other security measures to safeguard their customers' data.
  - **Scalability**: Cloud computing allows users to easily adjust their computing resources according to their changing needs and demands. Cloud services can handle spikes in traffic and workload without compromising performance or availability.
  - **Innovation**: Cloud computing enables users to access the latest technologies and tools, such as artificial intelligence, machine learning, big data analytics, and internet of things. Cloud services also foster collaboration and creativity among users and developers.

### Disadvantages of Cloud Computing

- Cloud computing also has some drawbacks and challenges, such as:
  - **Dependency**: Cloud computing relies on the internet connection and the service provider's availability. Users may experience downtime, latency, or service disruptions due to network issues, technical glitches, or provider's maintenance.
  - **Security risks**: Cloud computing exposes users' data and applications to potential threats from hackers, malicious insiders, or third parties. Users may lose control over their data and privacy, and may face legal or regulatory compliance issues.
  - **Vendor lock-in**: Cloud computing may limit users' choices and flexibility in terms of switching or migrating to different cloud providers or platforms. Users may face compatibility, interoperability, or portability issues, and may incur high costs or risks in changing their cloud services.
  - **Limited customization**: Cloud computing may not meet users' specific or complex requirements or preferences. Users may have to adapt to the cloud provider's standards, policies, and features, and may not be able to modify or optimize their cloud services.

### Types of Cloud Computing

- Cloud computing can be classified into different types based on the **deployment model** and the **service model**.

#### Deployment Model

- The deployment model refers to the location and ownership of the cloud infrastructure and resources. There are four main types of deployment models:
  - **Public cloud**: The cloud infrastructure and resources are owned and operated by a third-party cloud service provider, and are available to the general public or a large group of customers over the internet. Examples of public cloud providers are Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP).
  - **Private cloud**: The cloud infrastructure and resources are owned and operated by a single organization, and are used exclusively by its members or customers. The private cloud can be located on-premise or off-premise, and can be managed by the organization or a third-party provider. Examples of private cloud providers are IBM Cloud, VMware, and Oracle Cloud.
