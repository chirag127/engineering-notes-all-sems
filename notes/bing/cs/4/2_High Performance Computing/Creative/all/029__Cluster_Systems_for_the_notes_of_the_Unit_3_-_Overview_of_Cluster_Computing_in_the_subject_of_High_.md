### Cluster Systems for the notes of the Unit 3 - Overview of Cluster Computing in the subject of High Performance Computing

- A cluster system is a collection of two or more individual computer systems that work together as a parallel unit to achieve high performance, availability, scalability and fault tolerance.
- Cluster systems are widely used in high performance computing (HPC) applications that require large-scale computation, data analysis, simulation, visualization and artificial intelligence.
- Cluster systems consist of the following components:
  - A cluster provisioner that ensures node homogeneity and manages the installation, configuration and maintenance of the cluster software and hardware.
  - Servers, often referred to as nodes, that provide the computing power and run the applications. Nodes can be homogeneous or heterogeneous in terms of CPU, memory, storage and accelerator (such as GPU or FPGA) capabilities.
  - A scheduler that queues up workloads against the cluster resources and allocates them to the nodes according to predefined policies and priorities.
  - A network for communication between nodes and external devices. The network can be based on different protocols and topologies, such as Ethernet, InfiniBand, Omni-Path or Torus, depending on the bandwidth, latency and reliability requirements of the applications.
  - A general-purpose storage solution used to store applications and user data. The storage can be local or remote, and can use different technologies, such as RAID, NAS, SAN or cloud.
  - A high-speed, low-latency clustered file system generally used for computational storage. The file system allows multiple nodes to access the same data concurrently and provides consistency, coherence and fault tolerance.
- Cluster systems have the following advantages:
  - They increase computer performance exponentially by sharing the workload among multiple nodes and exploiting parallelism.
  - They pass on a massive cost savings benefit over SMP and MPP-based computers by leveraging the hardware made for consumer and general business usage.
  - They are scalable and flexible, as new nodes can be added or removed easily to adjust to the changing workload and performance demands.
  - They are resilient and reliable, as they can tolerate node failures and continue to operate without interruption or data loss.
- Cluster systems have the following disadvantages:
  - They require complex software and hardware management and coordination to ensure node homogeneity, load balancing, fault tolerance and security.
  - They may suffer from performance degradation due to network congestion, communication overhead, synchronization issues and resource contention.
  - They may not be suitable for applications that are not parallelizable, have high inter-node communication requirements or have strict real-time constraints.
- Cluster systems have the following examples and applications:
  - Scientific computing, such as weather forecasting, climate modeling, molecular dynamics, fluid dynamics, quantum chemistry and astrophysics.
  - Engineering and design, such as computer-aided design, finite element analysis, computational fluid dynamics and structural analysis.
  - Data analytics and machine learning, such as big data processing, data mining, natural language processing, computer vision and deep learning.
  - Bioinformatics and genomics, such as genome sequencing, protein folding, drug discovery and disease diagnosis.
  - Entertainment and gaming, such as animation, rendering, virtual reality and online gaming.
