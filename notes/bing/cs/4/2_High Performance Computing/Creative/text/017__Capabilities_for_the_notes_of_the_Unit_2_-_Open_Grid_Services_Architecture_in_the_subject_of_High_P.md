### Capabilities

- Capabilities are the basic building blocks of Open Grid Services Architecture (OGSA).
- Capabilities define the functionality and behavior of a Grid service, such as security, data access, notification, management, etc.
- Capabilities are implemented as interfaces that a Grid service can expose to its clients.
- Capabilities can be composed to create complex Grid services that support various application scenarios.
- Capabilities can be standardized to ensure interoperability and portability of Grid services across different platforms and domains.
- Capabilities can be dynamically discovered and negotiated by Grid service clients and providers using service metadata and protocols.
- Capabilities can be extended and customized to meet specific requirements and preferences of Grid service users and providers.
- Capabilities can be categorized into core capabilities and domain-specific capabilities.
- Core capabilities are the common and essential capabilities that every Grid service should support, such as identity, lifetime, state, notification, etc.
- Domain-specific capabilities are the specialized and optional capabilities that are relevant to a particular application domain, such as data access, computation, workflow, etc.
