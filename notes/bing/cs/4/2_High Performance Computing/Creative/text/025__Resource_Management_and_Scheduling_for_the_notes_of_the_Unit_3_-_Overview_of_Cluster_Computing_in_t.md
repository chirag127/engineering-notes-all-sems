### Resource Management and Scheduling

- Resource management and scheduling (RMS) are critical tasks in cluster computing, which aim to optimize the performance and utilization of cluster resources .
- Cluster resources include processors, memory, disk, network, and other devices that are shared among multiple users and applications.
- RMS consists of four main functionalities:
  - Resource management: the process of monitoring, controlling, and maintaining the status and availability of cluster resources.
  - Job queuing: the process of receiving, storing, and prioritizing the jobs submitted by the users into the cluster system.
  - Job scheduling: the process of determining how resources are assigned to various jobs based on some criteria and policies.
  - Job execution: the process of dispatching the jobs to the assigned nodes and managing the job execution processes before returning the results to the users upon job completion.
- RMS faces several challenges in cluster computing, such as:
  - Heterogeneity: cluster resources may have different types, capacities, and performance characteristics, which require adaptive and flexible scheduling algorithms.
  - Scalability: cluster systems may consist of hundreds or thousands of nodes, which require efficient and scalable scheduling algorithms that can handle large-scale workloads and resource demands.
  - Fault-tolerance: cluster systems may experience failures of nodes, links, or applications, which require robust and reliable scheduling algorithms that can handle faults and recover from them.
  - Quality of service: cluster systems may have different service level agreements (SLAs) with the users, which require scheduling algorithms that can meet the quality of service (QoS) requirements such as response time, throughput, availability, etc.
- RMS can be classified into two main categories based on the level of scheduling:
  - Cluster-level scheduling: the process of allocating cluster resources to different users or applications based on some global policies and objectives.
  - Node-level scheduling: the process of allocating node resources to different tasks or processes within a user or application based on some local policies and objectives.
- RMS can also be classified into two main categories based on the time of scheduling:
  - Static scheduling: the process of allocating resources to jobs before their execution, based on some prior knowledge or estimation of the resource requirements and availability.
  - Dynamic scheduling: the process of allocating resources to jobs during their execution, based on the current or updated information of the resource requirements and availability.
- RMS can use different criteria and policies for scheduling jobs, such as:
  - First come first serve (FCFS): the process of scheduling jobs in the order of their arrival time, without considering their resource requirements or priorities.
  - Shortest job first (SJF): the process of scheduling jobs in the order of their expected execution time, with the aim of minimizing the average waiting time and response time.
  - Priority-based: the process of scheduling jobs based on their assigned priorities, which can be determined by some factors such as user preference, SLA, deadline, etc.
  - Fairness-based: the process of scheduling jobs based on some fairness metrics, such as proportional fairness, max-min fairness, etc., which aim to balance the resource allocation among different users or applications.
  - Load balancing: the process of scheduling jobs based on the current or expected load of the cluster nodes, with the aim of distributing the workload evenly and avoiding overloading or underutilizing the nodes.
  - Energy-aware: the process of scheduling jobs based on the energy consumption or efficiency of the cluster nodes, with the aim of reducing the energy cost or carbon footprint of the cluster system.
