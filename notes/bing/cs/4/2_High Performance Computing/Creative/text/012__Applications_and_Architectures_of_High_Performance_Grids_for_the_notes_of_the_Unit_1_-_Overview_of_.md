### Applications and Architectures of High Performance Grids

- A grid is a distributed system that enables the sharing, selection, and aggregation of heterogeneous and geographically dispersed resources for solving large-scale problems in science, engineering, and commerce  .
- A high performance grid is a grid that can harness the power of an arbitrarily large collection of computing resources to meet the needs of compute intensive high performance computing (HPC) applications such as finite element model (FEM) simulations, computational fluid dynamics (CFD), bioinformatics, and data mining  .
- Some of the applications that can benefit from high performance grids are:
  - Scientific simulations: such as climate modeling, earthquake simulation, astrophysics, and particle physics  .
  - Data-intensive applications: such as genomics, astronomy, and social network analysis  .
  - Collaborative applications: such as telemedicine, virtual reality, and e-learning  .
- Some of the challenges and requirements for high performance grids are:
  - Scalability: the ability to handle a large number of resources and users, and to adapt to dynamic changes in resource availability and demand  .
  - Interoperability: the ability to integrate heterogeneous resources and services from different domains and organizations, and to support various standards and protocols  .
  - Security: the ability to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the grid resources and data, and to enforce the policies and agreements of the grid stakeholders  .
  - Performance: the ability to provide efficient and reliable execution of the grid applications, and to optimize the utilization of the grid resources  .
- Some of the architectures and models for high performance grids are:
  - Layered architecture: a common way to structure the grid components and functionalities into different layers, such as the fabric layer, the connectivity layer, the resource layer, the collective layer, and the application layer  .
  - Service-oriented architecture: a paradigm that views the grid as a collection of loosely coupled and interoperable services that can be composed and orchestrated to achieve the grid goals  .
  - Peer-to-peer architecture: a decentralized and self-organizing approach that enables the grid nodes to cooperate and share resources without relying on a central authority or a fixed topology  .
  - Cloud architecture: a model that provides on-demand access to a pool of virtualized and scalable resources that can be provisioned and released according to the user needs  .
