### Desktop Grids

- Desktop grids are a type of grid computing that use the idle resources of geographically distributed personal computers (PCs) connected over the Internet .
- Desktop grids are also known as volunteer computing or public resource computing, as they depend on the general public to donate their computing power and storage space for scientific or humanitarian projects .
- Desktop grids are based on a simple architecture, where a central server distributes tasks to the participating PCs, which execute them and return the results to the server .
- Desktop grids can provide a large amount of computing power at a low cost, as they leverage the existing hardware and network infrastructure of the PCs .
- Desktop grids can also support a wide range of applications, such as data analysis, simulation, optimization, cryptography, and bioinformatics .
- Desktop grids face several challenges, such as heterogeneity, dynamism, scalability, security, reliability, and fault tolerance of the distributed system  .
- Desktop grids require efficient and robust middleware to manage the communication, coordination, scheduling, data management, load balancing, result certification, and fault tolerance of the system  .
- Some examples of desktop grid projects are SETI@home, Folding@home, BOINC, XtremWeb, and OurGrid  .
