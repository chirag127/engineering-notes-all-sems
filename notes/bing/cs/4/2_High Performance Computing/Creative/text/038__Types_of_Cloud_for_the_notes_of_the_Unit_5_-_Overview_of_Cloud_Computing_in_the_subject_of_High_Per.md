### Types of Cloud

Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services over the internet, such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence. Cloud computing can be classified into two main categories: deployment models and service models.

#### Deployment Models

Deployment models refer to the location and management of the cloud's infrastructure. There are four common types of deployment models:

- **Public cloud**: The cloud infrastructure is owned and operated by a third-party cloud service provider, such as Google, Amazon, or Microsoft. The cloud services are delivered over the internet and are available to anyone who wants to use them. The customers share the same infrastructure and resources, and pay only for what they use. Public clouds are ideal for applications that require scalability, elasticity, and cost-efficiency, such as web hosting, e-commerce, or social media.
- **Private cloud**: The cloud infrastructure is owned and operated by a single organization, or by a third-party service provider on behalf of the organization. The cloud services are delivered over a private network and are accessible only to the authorized users of the organization. The customers have more control and customization over the infrastructure and resources, and pay for the maintenance and operation of the cloud. Private clouds are ideal for applications that require security, compliance, or performance, such as banking, healthcare, or government.
- **Hybrid cloud**: The cloud infrastructure is a combination of public and private clouds, connected by a common technology that allows data and applications to move between them. The cloud services are delivered over both the internet and the private network, and are accessible to both the public and the private users. The customers can leverage the benefits of both public and private clouds, such as scalability, cost-efficiency, security, and flexibility, depending on their needs and preferences. Hybrid clouds are ideal for applications that require dynamic and complex workloads, such as big data, artificial intelligence, or disaster recovery.
- **Community cloud**: The cloud infrastructure is shared and operated by a group of organizations that have common goals, interests, or requirements. The cloud services are delivered over a community network and are accessible only to the members of the group. The customers can collaborate and cooperate on the infrastructure and resources, and share the costs and responsibilities of the cloud. Community clouds are ideal for applications that require specific standards, policies, or regulations, such as research, education, or non-profit.

#### Service Models

Service models refer to the types and levels of cloud services that are offered to the customers. There are three main types of service models:

- **Software as a Service (SaaS)**: The cloud service provider delivers software applications over the internet, such as email, office, or CRM. The customers can access the applications from any device and location, without installing or maintaining them. The customers pay for the subscription or usage of the applications, and the provider manages the infrastructure, platform, and security of the cloud. SaaS is ideal for applications that require low upfront costs, high availability, and frequent updates, such as collaboration, communication, or productivity.
- **Platform as a Service (PaaS)**: The cloud service provider delivers a platform over the internet, such as operating system, programming language, or database. The customers can use the platform to develop, test, deploy, and manage their own software applications, without worrying about the underlying infrastructure or middleware. The customers pay for the resources or tools they use on the platform, and the provider manages the infrastructure, scalability, and security of the cloud. PaaS is ideal for applications that require rapid development, innovation, and scalability, such as web, mobile, or social.
- **Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)**: The cloud service provider delivers infrastructure over the internet, such as servers, storage, networking, or virtualization. The customers can use the infrastructure to host, run, and manage their own software applications, with full control and flexibility over the configuration and operation. The customers pay for the resources they consume on the infrastructure, and the provider manages the maintenance and availability of the cloud. IaaS is ideal for applications that require high performance, customization, and control, such as gaming, analytics, or simulation.
