### Peer‐to‐Peer Computing

- Peer-to-peer (P2P) computing is a distributed application architecture that partitions tasks or workloads between peers .
- Peers are equally privileged, equipotent participants in the network. They are said to form a peer-to-peer network of nodes .
- In a P2P network, each computer can serve the function of both client and server, supplying and receiving files, with bandwidth and processing distributed among all members of the network .
- P2P computing can be used for the distribution of digital media files, such as music, videos, and software, as well as for other applications, such as distributed computing, file sharing, and instant messaging .
- P2P computing has some advantages over traditional client-server computing, such as:
  - Higher scalability, as the network can grow without a central point of failure or bottleneck .
  - Higher resilience, as the network can tolerate node failures and attacks without losing functionality .
  - Higher efficiency, as the network can utilize idle resources of peers, such as disk space, CPU power, and bandwidth .
  - Lower cost, as the network does not require dedicated servers or infrastructure .
- P2P computing also has some challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Security and privacy issues, as the network may expose peers to malicious or illegal content, viruses, or hackers .
  - Quality of service issues, as the network may suffer from low availability, reliability, or performance of peers or files .
  - Legal and ethical issues, as the network may enable or facilitate the infringement of intellectual property rights, censorship, or regulation .
