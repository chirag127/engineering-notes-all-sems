### Computational & Artificial Neuroscience

- Computational neuroscience is a field of study that seeks to understand how the brain works by using mathematical models, simulations, and computer simulations. It is an interdisciplinary field that involves expertise in biology, physics, mathematics, computer science, and engineering.
- Artificial neuroscience is a subfield of computational neuroscience that focuses on creating artificial neural networks that are inspired by the structure and function of the biological neural networks in the brain. Artificial neural networks are computational models that can learn from data and perform various tasks, such as classification, regression, clustering, reinforcement learning, etc.
- Computational neuroscience and artificial neuroscience have a mutual influence on each other. On one hand, computational neuroscience can provide insights and constraints for the design and evaluation of artificial neural networks. On the other hand, artificial neural networks can serve as hypotheses and tools for testing and exploring the mechanisms and principles of the brain.
- Some of the topics and applications of computational neuroscience and artificial neuroscience are:
  - Computational models of biological neurons and synapses, such as the Hodgkin-Huxley model, the integrate-and-fire model, the Hebbian learning rule, etc.
  - Computational models of neural circuits and systems, such as the visual system, the auditory system, the motor system, the memory system, etc.
  - Computational models of cognitive functions and behaviors, such as attention, decision making, learning, memory, emotion, etc.
  - Artificial neural networks architectures and algorithms, such as feedforward networks, recurrent networks, convolutional networks, deep networks, backpropagation, gradient descent, etc.
  - Artificial neural networks applications and challenges, such as computer vision, natural language processing, speech recognition, generative models, adversarial examples, explainability, etc.
