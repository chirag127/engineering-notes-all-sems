## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI), which is the study of how to create machines and software that can perform tasks that require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, etc.
- The main objectives of this unit are:
  - To understand the definition, history, and scope of AI.
  - To learn about the different types of AI systems, such as symbolic, sub-symbolic, and hybrid.
  - To explore the main applications and challenges of AI in various domains, such as gaming, robotics, healthcare, education, etc.
  - To appreciate the ethical, social, and legal implications of AI for human society and the environment.
- The main topics covered in this unit are:
  - What is AI? - This topic defines AI and its subfields, such as machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, etc. It also discusses the Turing test, which is a method to evaluate the intelligence of a machine by comparing it with a human.
  - History of AI - This topic traces the evolution of AI from its origins in the 1950s to the present day, highlighting the major milestones, achievements, and challenges along the way. It also introduces some of the pioneers and influential figures in the field of AI, such as Alan Turing, John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, etc.
  - Types of AI - This topic classifies AI systems into different categories based on their representation, reasoning, and learning methods, such as symbolic, sub-symbolic, and hybrid. It also explains the advantages and disadvantages of each type, and gives examples of existing or potential AI systems that belong to each category.
  - Applications of AI - This topic surveys the various domains and areas where AI can be applied to solve problems, enhance performance, or create new possibilities, such as gaming, robotics, healthcare, education, etc. It also illustrates some of the successful or promising AI systems that have been developed or are being developed for each domain, such as AlphaGo, Sophia, Watson, etc.
  - Challenges and Limitations of AI - This topic identifies and analyzes some of the main difficulties and obstacles that AI faces or may face in the future, such as computational complexity, scalability, uncertainty, explainability, etc. It also discusses some of the possible solutions or approaches to overcome or mitigate these challenges, such as heuristic search, probabilistic reasoning, neural networks, etc.
  - Ethical, Social, and Legal Issues of AI - This topic examines some of the ethical, social, and legal implications of AI for human society and the environment, such as privacy, security, accountability, responsibility, etc. It also explores some of the philosophical and moral questions that AI raises or may raise in the future, such as the nature of intelligence, consciousness, and free will, the rights and duties of machines and humans, the impact of AI on human dignity, values, and culture, etc.

- Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for this unit are:
  - To remember the definition of AI, you can use the acronym SMART, which stands for Systems that can Mimic, Adapt, Reason, and Think.
  - To remember the types of AI, you can use the acronym SSH, which stands for Symbolic, Sub-symbolic, and Hybrid.
  - To remember the applications of AI, you can use the acronym GRACE, which stands for Gaming, Robotics, Healthcare, Education, and other domains.
  - To remember the challenges and limitations of AI, you can use the acronym CUSE, which stands for Complexity, Uncertainty, Scalability, and Explainability.
  - To remember the ethical, social, and legal issues of AI, you can use the acronym PARIS, which stands for Privacy, Accountability, Responsibility, Impact, and Security.
