### Face Recognition for the notes of the Unit 5 - CASE STUDY AND APPLICATIONS in the subject of Deep Learning

- Face recognition is the problem of identifying or verifying faces in a photograph or a video.
- Face recognition is a process comprised of four steps: detection, alignment, feature extraction, and recognition.
- Detection is the task of locating faces in an image or a video, usually by using a bounding box or a landmark point.
- Alignment is the task of transforming the detected faces to a canonical pose and scale, usually by using geometric or deep learning methods.
- Feature extraction is the task of extracting high-level features from the aligned faces, usually by using deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs).
- Recognition is the task of comparing the extracted features with a database of known faces, usually by using a distance metric or a classifier.
- Deep learning models have achieved state-of-the-art performance for face recognition tasks, surpassing human performance.
- Deep learning models for face recognition can be divided into two categories: holistic and local.
- Holistic models use the whole face image as input and learn global features, such as DeepFace.
- Local models use patches or regions of the face image as input and learn local features, such as DeepID.
- Deep learning models for face recognition can also be divided into two categories: verification and identification.
- Verification models determine whether two face images belong to the same person or not, such as FaceNet.
- Identification models determine the identity of a face image from a set of known identities, such as VGGFace.
- Deep learning models for face recognition can benefit from various techniques, such as data augmentation, face normalization, loss functions, metric learning, and network architectures.
- Data augmentation is the process of generating more training data by applying transformations to the original data, such as rotation, scaling, cropping, flipping, and noise.
- Face normalization is the process of reducing the variations in face images caused by factors such as pose, illumination, expression, and occlusion, such as using a 3D face model or a generative adversarial network (GAN).
- Loss functions are the criteria used to measure the performance of the model and guide the optimization process, such as softmax, contrastive, triplet, and center loss.
- Metric learning is the process of learning a distance function that can measure the similarity or dissimilarity between face features, such as Euclidean, cosine, or angular distance.
- Network architectures are the structures of the deep learning models, such as the number and type of layers, the connections between layers, and the activation functions, such as AlexNet, VGG, ResNet, and Inception.
- Face recognition has various applications, such as security, surveillance, biometrics, social media, entertainment, and education.
