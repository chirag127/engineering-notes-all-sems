### History of Deep Learning for the notes of the Unit 2 - DEEP NETWORKS in the subject of Deep Learning

- Deep learning is a branch of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks to learn from data and perform tasks such as classification, regression, generation, etc.
- The term Deep Learning was introduced to the machine learning community by Rina Dechter in 1986, and to artificial neural networks by Igor Aizenberg and colleagues in 2000, in the context of Boolean threshold neurons.
- The history of deep learning can be traced back to 1943, when Walter Pitts and Warren McCulloch created a computer model based on the neural networks of the human brain. They used a combination of algorithms and mathematics they called “threshold logic” to mimic the thought process .
- In 1958, Frank Rosenblatt proposed the perceptron, a single-layer neural network that could learn to classify linearly separable patterns. However, the perceptron was limited by its inability to solve problems that were not linearly separable, such as the XOR problem.
- In 1965, Alexey Ivakhnenko and Valentin Lapa published the first general, working learning algorithm for supervised deep feedforward multilayer perceptrons (MLP), which they called Group Method of Data Handling (GMDH). They used principles of cybernetics and polynomial interpolation to train MLPs with arbitrary depth and complexity .
- In 1969, Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert published a book called Perceptrons, which analyzed the mathematical limitations of single-layer perceptrons and showed that they could not represent some simple functions, such as XOR. This book discouraged further research on neural networks for many years, until the emergence of the backpropagation algorithm.
- In 1974, Paul Werbos proposed the backpropagation algorithm, which is a method of training multilayer neural networks by adjusting the weights of the connections according to the error gradient. However, his work was largely ignored until the mid-1980s, when it was rediscovered by David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton, and Ronald Williams.
- In 1986, Geoffrey Hinton, David Rumelhart, and Ronald Williams published a paper called Learning representations by back-propagating errors, which popularized the backpropagation algorithm and demonstrated its effectiveness on various tasks, such as speech recognition, image recognition, and natural language processing. This paper sparked a resurgence of interest in neural networks and deep learning.
- In 1989, Yann LeCun, Leon Bottou, Yoshua Bengio, and Patrick Haffner developed a convolutional neural network (CNN) called LeNet-5, which was used to recognize handwritten digits on cheques. CNNs are a type of neural network that use convolutional layers to extract features from images, and are widely used for computer vision tasks today.
- In 1997, Sepp Hochreiter and Jürgen Schmidhuber proposed the long short-term memory (LSTM) network, which is a type of recurrent neural network (RNN) that can learn long-term dependencies in sequential data, such as text, speech, and video. LSTM networks are widely used for natural language processing, speech recognition, and machine translation tasks today.
- In 2006, Geoffrey Hinton, Simon Osindero, and Yee-Whye Teh introduced the concept of deep belief networks (DBNs), which are a type of generative model that can learn to represent complex data distributions using multiple layers of hidden units. They also proposed a greedy layer-wise pre-training method, which can initialize the weights of a deep neural network using unsupervised learning, and then fine-tune them using supervised learning. This method helped to overcome the problem of vanishing gradients, which occurs when the error signal becomes too small to propagate through many layers of a deep network.
- In 2012, Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton won the ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC), which is a competition to classify images into 1000 categories, using a deep CNN called AlexNet. AlexNet achieved a top-5 error rate of 15.3%, which was significantly lower than the previous best result of 26.2%. This breakthrough demonstrated the power of deep learning for computer vision, and inspired many researchers and companies to adopt deep learning techniques for various applications.
