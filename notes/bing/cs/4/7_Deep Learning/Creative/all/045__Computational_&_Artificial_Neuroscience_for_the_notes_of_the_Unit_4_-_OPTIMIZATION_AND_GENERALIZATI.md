### Computational & Artificial Neuroscience for the notes of the Unit 4 - OPTIMIZATION AND GENERALIZATION in the subject of Deep Learning

- Computational neuroscience is the study of brain function in terms of the information processing properties of the structures that make up the nervous system.
- Artificial neuroscience is the application of computational neuroscience methods and models to artificial systems, such as neural networks, computer vision, machine learning and artificial intelligence.
- Optimization and generalization are two key aspects of deep learning, which is a branch of artificial intelligence that uses brain-inspired architectures to learn from data.
- Optimization is the process of finding the best set of parameters for a neural network that minimizes a loss function, which measures the discrepancy between the network's output and the desired output.
- Generalization is the ability of a neural network to perform well on new and unseen data, not just on the data it was trained on.
- Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

  - Optimization in deep learning: Non-convex optimization for deep networks, stochastic optimization, gradient descent and its variants, momentum, adaptive learning rates, regularization, dropout, batch normalization, etc.
  - Generalization in neural networks: Overfitting and underfitting, bias-variance trade-off, cross-validation, model selection, early stopping, data augmentation, etc.
  - Spatial transformer networks: A module that allows a neural network to learn spatial transformations of the input, such as scaling, rotation, cropping, etc.
  - Recurrent networks, LSTM: A type of neural network that can process sequential data, such as text, speech, video, etc, by having a memory state that can store and update information over time. LSTM stands for long short-term memory, which is a variant of recurrent network that can learn long-term dependencies.
  - Recurrent neural network language models: A type of language model that uses a recurrent network to predict the next word in a sequence, based on the previous words and the context.
  - Word-level RNNs and deep reinforcement learning: A combination of word-level recurrent networks and reinforcement learning, which is a type of learning that involves an agent interacting with an environment and learning from rewards and penalties. This can be used to generate natural language responses, such as in chatbots, or to control agents in games, such as in AlphaGo.
  - Computational and artificial neuroscience: A deep learning framework for neuroscience, which argues that the three components of artificial neural networks (objective functions, learning rules and architectures) can also be used to model, engineer and optimize complex biological learning systems.

- Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for this unit are:

  - To remember the difference between optimization and generalization, think of OPTIMIZATION as OPTIMIZING the network's performance on the TRAINING data, and GENERALIZATION as GENERALIZING the network's performance to the TEST data.
  - To remember the difference between overfitting and underfitting, think of OVERFITTING as OVERLY FITTING the network to the training data, and UNDERFITTING as UNDERLY FITTING the network to the training data.
  - To remember the difference between bias and variance, think of BIAS as the tendency of the network to make systematic errors, and VARIANCE as the tendency of the network to make random errors.
  - To remember the difference between dropout and batch normalization, think of DROPOUT as DROPPING OUT some of the network's units randomly during training, and BATCH NORMALIZATION as NORMALIZING the network's inputs across different batches during training.
  - To remember the difference between LSTM and RNN, think of LSTM as a type of RNN that has a LONG memory, and RNN as a type of neural network that has a RECURRENT structure.
  - To remember the difference between reinforcement learning and supervised learning, think of REINFORCEMENT LEARNING as learning from REWARDS and PENALTIES, and SUPERVISED LEARNING as learning from LABELS and FEEDBACK.
  - To remember the difference between computational and artificial neuroscience, think of COMPUTATIONAL NEUROSCIENCE as using COMPUTATION to study the brain, and ARTIFICIAL NEUROSCIENCE as using the brain to study COMPUTATION.
