A consensus protocol enables all the parties of the blockchain network to come to a common agreement (consensus) on the present data state of the ledger. This promotes trust among unknown peers in a distributed computing environment .

In a permissioned blockchain, all the participating nodes are known and chosen. However, consensus is still required because we can’t assume that every node is trustworthy .

There are different types of consensus protocols for permissioned blockchains, such as:

- Proof of Work (PoW): The first consensus protocol used in the blockchain network was Proof of Work (PoW). It requires nodes to solve a complex mathematical puzzle to validate transactions and create new blocks. The node that solves the puzzle first broadcasts the solution to the network and receives a reward. PoW is used by Bitcoin and Ethereum, but it is not suitable for permissioned blockchains because it consumes a lot of energy and resources, and it is vulnerable to 51% attacks.
- Proof of Stake (PoS): Proof of Stake (PoS) is a consensus protocol that assigns nodes a stake based on their wealth or reputation in the network. The nodes with higher stakes have more chances to validate transactions and create new blocks. The nodes that validate transactions receive transaction fees as rewards, but they lose their stake if they act maliciously. PoS is more energy-efficient and scalable than PoW, but it may lead to centralization and oligarchy.
- Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS): Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS) is a consensus protocol that allows nodes to delegate their stake to a group of trusted validators, who are elected by the network. The validators are responsible for validating transactions and creating new blocks, and they share the rewards with their delegators. DPoS is faster and more democratic than PoS, but it may suffer from collusion and corruption.
- Transaction as Proof of Stake (TaPoS): Transaction as Proof of Stake (TaPoS) is a consensus protocol that prevents malicious activities, such as taking a transaction from one blockchain and fraudulently repeating it on another. This is known as “transaction replay.” Every transaction in the system must include the hash of the most recent block header. This way, the nodes can verify the validity and uniqueness of the transactions, and the network can achieve consensus without relying on a central authority. TaPoS is used by EOS and Steem .
- Delegated Byzantine Fault Tolerance (dBFT): Delegated Byzantine Fault Tolerance (dBFT) is a consensus protocol that uses a leader-based system to achieve consensus. The network elects a leader node, who proposes a new block to a group of validator nodes, who are also elected by the network. The validators vote on the proposed block, and if two-thirds of them agree, the block is added to the ledger. If the leader or some validators are dishonest, the network can replace them through a voting mechanism. dBFT is used by NEO and Ontology.
- Casper: Casper is a consensus protocol that combines PoS and dBFT to achieve consensus. It uses a PoS system to select validator nodes, who stake their tokens to participate in the consensus process. It also uses a dBFT system to allow the validators to vote on the proposed blocks, and to penalize the validators who act maliciously or inconsistently. Casper is designed to be used by Ethereum in the future.
- Proof of Importance (PoI): Proof of Importance (PoI) is a consensus protocol that assigns nodes a score based on their activity and contribution to the network. The nodes with higher scores have more chances to validate transactions and create new blocks. The nodes that validate transactions receive transaction fees as rewards, but they also have to pay fees to send transactions. PoI is used by NEM and Symbol.
- Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET): Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET) is a consensus protocol that uses a trusted execution environment (TEE) to achieve consensus.
