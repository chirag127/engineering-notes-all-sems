### KYC for the notes of the Unit 6 - Use case 1 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- KYC stands for Know Your Customer, which is a process of verifying the identity and background of customers who use financial services.
- KYC is a regulatory requirement for banks and other financial institutions to prevent money laundering, fraud, and terrorism financing.
- KYC involves collecting and verifying various documents and data from customers, such as identity proof, address proof, source of funds, business interests, and history.
- KYC is a costly and time-consuming process, which often leads to customer dissatisfaction, operational inefficiencies, and data duplication and inconsistency.
- Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that enables secure and transparent transactions among multiple parties without intermediaries.
- Blockchain can be used to improve KYC processes by creating a shared and immutable record of customer identity and data, which can be accessed and verified by authorized parties across different platforms and jurisdictions.
- Blockchain can reduce the cost and time of KYC processes by eliminating the need for repeated verification, manual data entry, and paper-based documentation.
- Blockchain can also enhance the security and privacy of KYC data by encrypting and hashing the data, and using smart contracts to enforce access and consent rules.
- Blockchain can also enable customers to have more control and ownership over their own data, and to share it selectively with trusted parties.
- Some of the use cases of blockchain for KYC are:

  - IBM Blockchain Trusted Identity: A decentralized platform for identification processes based on the blockchain and biometrics, which allows customers to create and manage their own digital identity and share it with service providers.
  - UAE KYC Blockchain Platform: A national KYC ecosystem launched by Dubai's Department of Economic Development and Dubai International Financial Centre, powered by Norbloc, which enables banks and government entities to exchange verified KYC data in real time.
  - uPort: An open identification system built on Ethereum, which allows users to create and manage their own identity and reputation, and to interact with decentralized applications and services.
  - Civic: A secure identity platform that leverages blockchain and biometrics to provide identity verification and authentication services for businesses and individuals.
  - SelfKey: A self-sovereign identity system that uses blockchain and smart contracts to enable users to create and manage their own identity and access various services and products.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the benefits of blockchain for KYC is:

  - **B**etter customer experience
  - **L**ower operational costs
  - **O**ptimized data quality
  - **C**ompliance with regulations
  - **K**eeping data secure and private
  - **C**ustomer empowerment and ownership

- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of blockchain for KYC is to imagine a digital passport that contains all your identity and background information, which is stored on a distributed network of computers that can verify and update it, and which you can use to access various financial services without having to provide the same information over and over again.
