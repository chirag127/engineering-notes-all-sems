### Visibility for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Blockchain Architecture Design

- Unit 7 of the course Blockchain Architecture Design and Use Cases covers the topic of Blockchain for Supply Chain Management.
- Use case 2 of this unit is about building a transparent supply chain using blockchain technology.
- Visibility for the notes of this use case means the ability to access and view the information stored on the blockchain ledger by different participants of the supply chain network.
- Visibility is an important feature of blockchain for supply chain management, as it can enhance trust, efficiency, and accountability among the stakeholders.
- Visibility can be achieved by using different design and architectural primitives of blockchain, such as:
  - Protocols: The rules and standards that govern the communication and interaction among the nodes of the blockchain network.
  - Security: The mechanisms that ensure the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of the data and transactions on the blockchain ledger.
  - Consensus: The process that ensures the agreement and validation of the transactions and blocks by the majority of the nodes of the blockchain network.
  - Permissions: The levels of access and control that different participants have over the data and transactions on the blockchain ledger.
  - Privacy: The degree of anonymity and confidentiality that the participants can maintain while using the blockchain ledger.
- A possible mnemonic to remember these five primitives is **PSPCP** (pronounced as "P-S-P-C-P"), which stands for **P**rotocols, **S**ecurity, **P**ermissions, **C**onsensus, and **P**rivacy.
- A possible learning trick to understand these primitives is to relate them to a real-world example of a supply chain network, such as the one shown below:

```
+--------+      +--------+      +--------+      +--------+      +--------+
| Farmer | ---> | Trader | ---> | Packer | ---> | Retailer| ---> | Consumer|
+--------+      +--------+      +--------+      +--------+      +--------+
```

- In this example, each participant of the supply chain network can be a node of the blockchain network, and each transaction of the goods can be recorded on the blockchain ledger.
- The protocols define how the nodes communicate and exchange information with each other, such as the format, frequency, and verification of the messages.
- The security ensures that the data and transactions on the ledger are protected from unauthorized access, modification, or deletion, such as by using encryption, digital signatures, and hashing.
- The consensus ensures that the nodes agree on the validity and order of the transactions and blocks on the ledger, such as by using proof-of-work, proof-of-stake, or other algorithms.
- The permissions define the roles and responsibilities of the nodes, such as who can create, read, update, or delete the data and transactions on the ledger.
- The privacy ensures that the nodes can maintain their identity and data confidentiality, such as by using pseudonyms, zero-knowledge proofs, or other techniques.
- By using these primitives, the blockchain ledger can provide visibility for the notes of the use case, such as:
  - The farmer can track the origin, quality, and quantity of the goods he produces and sells to the trader.
  - The trader can verify the authenticity and provenance of the goods he buys from the farmer and sells to the packer.
  - The packer can monitor the condition and status of the goods he receives from the trader and delivers to the retailer.
  - The retailer can access the history and details of the goods he receives from the packer and sells to the consumer.
  - The consumer can view the source, journey, and attributes of the goods he buys from the retailer.
- A possible advantage of using blockchain for this use case is that it can reduce the costs, risks, and delays associated with intermediaries, paperwork, and manual processes.
- A possible disadvantage of using blockchain for this use case is that it can introduce new challenges and complexities, such as scalability, interoperability, and governance.
- A possible example of an application of blockchain for this use case is the IBM Food Trust platform, which connects farmers, processors, distributors, and retailers to create a shared and transparent record of food origin, quality, and safety.
