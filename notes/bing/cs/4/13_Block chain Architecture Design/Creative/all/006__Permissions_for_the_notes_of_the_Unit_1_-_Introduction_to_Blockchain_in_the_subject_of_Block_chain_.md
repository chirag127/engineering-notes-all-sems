### Permissions for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Permissions are the rules that define who can access and perform actions on a blockchain network.
- Permissions can be classified into two types: permissionless and permissioned.
- Permissionless blockchains are open and public, meaning anyone can join and participate in the network without any restrictions or identity verification. Examples of permissionless blockchains are Bitcoin and Ethereum.
- Permissioned blockchains are closed and private, meaning only authorized users can join and access the network. The users need to identify themselves through certificates or other digital means and can only perform specific actions granted to them by the network administrators. Examples of permissioned blockchains are Hyperledger Fabric and Corda.
- Permissioned blockchains provide more security, privacy, scalability, and efficiency than permissionless blockchains, but they also sacrifice some degree of decentralization, transparency, and trustlessness.
- Permissioned blockchains are suitable for use cases that involve sensitive data, regulated industries, or complex business logic that require different roles and access levels for different participants. Examples of such use cases are supply chain management, healthcare, banking, and government services.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the difference between permissionless and permissioned blockchains is:

  - Permissionless: Anyone can join and play, no need to say who you are or what you do.
  - Permissioned: Only invited guests can join and stay, you need to show your ID and follow the rules.
