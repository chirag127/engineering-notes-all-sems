### Design goals for the notes of the Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Permissioned blockchains are a type of blockchain network that restricts the access and participation of nodes and users based on predefined rules and criteria .
- Permissioned blockchains are designed to benefit from the advantages of blockchain technology, such as transparency, immutability, and decentralization, without sacrificing the authority and control of a centralized system.
- Permissioned blockchains are suitable for enterprise and business applications that require compliance with regulations, privacy, and scalability .
- Some of the design goals for permissioned blockchains are:

  - **Security**: Permissioned blockchains should ensure the integrity and confidentiality of the data and transactions stored on the ledger. They should also prevent unauthorized access and malicious attacks from compromised nodes or external parties .
  - **Performance**: Permissioned blockchains should achieve high throughput and low latency for processing transactions and validating blocks. They should also optimize the resource consumption and network bandwidth of the nodes .
  - **Consensus**: Permissioned blockchains should use a suitable consensus protocol that ensures the agreement and consistency of the ledger among the nodes. The consensus protocol should also balance the trade-offs between security, performance, and decentralization .
  - **Governance**: Permissioned blockchains should have a clear and transparent governance model that defines the roles and responsibilities of the nodes and users, the rules and policies for joining and leaving the network, and the mechanisms for resolving disputes and conflicts .

- A possible mnemonic to remember the design goals for permissioned blockchains is **SPCG** (Security, Performance, Consensus, Governance).
- An example of a permissioned blockchain platform is Hyperledger Fabric, which is an open-source project under the Linux Foundation. Hyperledger Fabric supports modular and configurable design, pluggable consensus protocols, and smart contracts.
