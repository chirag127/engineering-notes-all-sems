### Blockchain in trade/supply chain for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Blockchain is a decentralized and distributed ledger technology that records and verifies transactions in a secure and transparent way  .
- Blockchain can transform the supply chain by enabling trust, transparency, traceability, and efficiency among the participants of the network   .
- Some of the benefits of blockchain in supply chain are  :
  - Improved efficiency: Blockchain can reduce the need for intermediaries, paperwork, and manual processes, and automate the data exchange and verification among the parties.
  - Enhanced security: Blockchain can protect the data from unauthorized access, tampering, and fraud, and ensure the integrity and authenticity of the transactions.
  - Increased visibility: Blockchain can provide real-time and accurate information about the location, status, and condition of the products and assets along the supply chain.
  - Reduced costs: Blockchain can lower the operational and transactional costs, and optimize the inventory and logistics management.
  - Better compliance: Blockchain can facilitate the compliance with regulations, standards, and contracts, and enable the verification of the origin, quality, and sustainability of the products.
- Some of the challenges of blockchain in supply chain are  :
  - Scalability: Blockchain may face issues with handling large volumes of transactions and data, and ensuring the performance and speed of the network.
  - Interoperability: Blockchain may need to integrate with existing systems and platforms, and ensure the compatibility and communication among different blockchain networks and protocols.
  - Governance: Blockchain may require a clear and agreed-upon set of rules and policies for the network, and a mechanism for resolving disputes and enforcing compliance.
  - Adoption: Blockchain may face barriers with the awareness, education, and acceptance of the technology, and the collaboration and coordination among the stakeholders.
- Some of the examples of blockchain in supply chain are   :
  - Walmart uses blockchain to track the provenance and quality of its food products, and to improve the food safety and recall processes.
  - Maersk and IBM have launched TradeLens, a blockchain platform that connects the participants of the global trade ecosystem, and provides end-to-end visibility and security of the shipments.
  - De Beers uses blockchain to trace the journey of its diamonds from the mine to the customer, and to ensure the ethical and conflict-free sourcing of the gems.
  - Starbucks uses blockchain to track the origin and journey of its coffee beans, and to reward the farmers and customers with digital tokens.
  - FedEx uses blockchain to improve the transparency and efficiency of its cross-border shipments, and to enable the verification and authentication of the documents and data.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the benefits of blockchain in supply chain is **STIVE** (Security, Transparency, Traceability, Efficiency, Visibility).
- A possible mnemonic to remember the challenges of blockchain in supply chain is **SIGA** (Scalability, Interoperability, Governance, Adoption).
