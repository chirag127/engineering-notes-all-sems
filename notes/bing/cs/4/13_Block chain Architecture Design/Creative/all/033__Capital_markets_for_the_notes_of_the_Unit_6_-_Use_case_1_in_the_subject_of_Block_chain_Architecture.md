### Capital markets for the notes of the Unit 6 - Use case 1 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Capital markets are the markets where securities such as stocks, bonds, derivatives and other financial instruments are traded.
- Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology (DLT) that enables peer-to-peer transactions without intermediaries, using cryptography and consensus mechanisms to ensure data integrity and security.
- Blockchain has the potential to transform various aspects of capital markets, such as issuance, trading, clearing, settlement, custody, asset servicing and post-trade services.
- Some of the benefits of blockchain in capital markets are:
  - Reduced costs and operational risks by eliminating or streamlining intermediaries, manual processes and reconciliation.
  - Increased efficiency and transparency by enabling real-time settlement, automated execution and reporting, and enhanced data quality and availability.
  - Improved liquidity and access by enabling fractional ownership, tokenization and digitalization of assets, and cross-border transactions.
  - Enhanced innovation and competition by enabling new business models, products and services, and market participants.
- Some of the challenges of blockchain in capital markets are:
  - Regulatory uncertainty and compliance issues, as different jurisdictions may have different rules and standards for blockchain-based activities and assets.
  - Scalability and performance issues, as blockchain networks may face limitations in terms of transaction throughput, latency and storage capacity.
  - Interoperability and integration issues, as different blockchain platforms may have different protocols, architectures and features, and may need to interact with legacy systems and infrastructures.
  - Security and privacy issues, as blockchain networks may be vulnerable to cyberattacks, data breaches and unauthorized access, and may need to balance data visibility and confidentiality.
- Some of the use cases of blockchain in capital markets are:
  - Issuance: Blockchain can enable the issuance of digital securities, such as equity tokens, debt tokens, asset-backed tokens and stablecoins, that represent ownership or claims on underlying assets or cash flows. Blockchain can also facilitate the issuance process by automating compliance, verification and disclosure requirements, and reducing the need for intermediaries and intermediation costs.
  - Trading: Blockchain can enable the trading of digital securities on decentralized exchanges (DEXs) or peer-to-peer platforms, that allow direct matching and execution of orders without intermediaries or central clearing parties. Blockchain can also enable the trading of traditional securities on centralized or hybrid platforms, that leverage blockchain for settlement, custody or asset servicing purposes.
  - Clearing and settlement: Blockchain can enable the clearing and settlement of securities transactions on a real-time or near-real-time basis, by synchronizing the transfer of ownership and payment on the same ledger, and using smart contracts to automate the execution and validation of transactions. Blockchain can also reduce the counterparty and settlement risks, and the capital and liquidity requirements, associated with the current delayed and fragmented settlement systems.
  - Custody: Blockchain can enable the custody of digital securities, by allowing the holders to store and manage their own private keys, or by using third-party custodians that provide secure and compliant storage solutions. Blockchain can also enable the custody of traditional securities, by using tokenization or digital representation of securities on the ledger, and by providing enhanced data and audit trails for the custodians and regulators.
  - Asset servicing: Blockchain can enable the asset servicing of securities, such as dividend payments, interest payments, corporate actions, voting rights and tax reporting, by using smart contracts to automate the distribution and verification of payments and events, and by providing transparent and timely information to the issuers, investors and regulators.
  - Post-trade services and infrastructure: Blockchain can enable the post-trade services and infrastructure of securities, such as reporting, analytics, risk management, compliance and audit, by providing standardized and granular data on the ledger, and by enabling data sharing and collaboration among the market participants and regulators.

- A mnemonic to remember the use cases of blockchain in capital markets is: **IT CAPS** (Issuance, Trading, Clearing and settlement, Custody, Asset servicing, Post-trade services and infrastructure).
