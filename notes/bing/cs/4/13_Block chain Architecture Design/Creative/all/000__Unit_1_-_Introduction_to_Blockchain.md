## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

- Blockchain is a type of shared database that stores data of any kind in blocks that are securely linked together via cryptographic hashes .
- Each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and transaction data (generally represented as a Merkle tree, where data nodes are represented by leaves).
- Blockchain is decentralized, distributed, and often public, meaning that it is not controlled by a single entity, it is maintained by a network of nodes, and it can be accessed and verified by anyone .
- Blockchain is immutable, transparent, and verifiable, meaning that the data stored on it cannot be altered retroactively, it can be seen by anyone, and it can be validated by mathematical proofs.
- Blockchain can be used to record information about cryptocurrency transactions, NFT ownership, DeFi smart contracts, supply chain tracking, digital identity, voting systems, and more .
- Blockchain has advantages such as increased security, efficiency, trust, and innovation, as well as disadvantages such as scalability issues, environmental impact, regulatory uncertainty, and technical complexity .

### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- To remember the four main characteristics of blockchain, you can use the acronym **DITV**: Decentralized, Immutable, Transparent, and Verifiable.
- To remember the difference between a blockchain and a typical database, you can use the analogy of a **book** and a **sheet of paper**: A book is made of pages that are bound together and cannot be easily changed, while a sheet of paper can be easily edited or torn. A book is also more durable and traceable than a sheet of paper.
- To remember the structure of a block, you can use the mnemonic **HAT**: Hash, Timestamp, and Transaction data.
