### Settlements for the notes of the Unit 6 - Use case 1 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Settlements are the processes of transferring assets or payments between parties after a trade or transaction has been executed.
- Blockchain technology can improve settlement processes substantially by enabling trustless, peer-to-peer, and instantaneous delivery versus payment (DVP) mechanisms.
- DVP is a settlement method that ensures that the delivery of an asset is directly linked to the payment for the asset, reducing the counterparty risk and the need for intermediaries.
- Blockchain architecture design for settlements depends on the type of blockchain platform, the consensus algorithm, the smart contract functionality, and the use case of the application   .
- Use case 1: A permissioned blockchain for cross-border payments
  - A permissioned blockchain is a proprietary network that defines and decides the participants and their roles, such as validators, endorsers, or clients.
  - A permissioned blockchain can provide faster, cheaper, and more secure cross-border payments than traditional methods that rely on intermediaries such as banks or payment processors.
  - A permissioned blockchain can use a consensus algorithm such as proof of authority (PoA) or practical Byzantine fault tolerance (PBFT) that requires less computational power and allows for higher scalability and finality than proof of work (PoW) or proof of stake (PoS).
  - A permissioned blockchain can use smart contracts to automate the execution of payment transactions based on predefined rules and conditions.
  - A permissioned blockchain can use a native or a stable digital currency to facilitate the exchange of value across different jurisdictions and currencies.
  - A permissioned blockchain can use cryptographic techniques such as zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) or homomorphic encryption to ensure the privacy and confidentiality of the payment data.
  - A permissioned blockchain can use interoperability protocols or bridges to connect with other blockchains or legacy systems to enable cross-chain or cross-platform transactions.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the key components of use case 1 is: **P**ermissioned, **P**ayments, **P**oA/PBFT, **S**mart contracts, **S**tablecoins, **Z**KPs, **I**nteroperability.
- A possible learning trick to understand use case 1 is to compare it with a traditional payment system and identify the differences and advantages of using a blockchain-based solution. For example, you can ask yourself questions such as:
  - How does a permissioned blockchain differ from a centralized or a public blockchain in terms of governance, access, and security?
  - How does a PoA or PBFT consensus algorithm differ from a PoW or PoS consensus algorithm in terms of speed, cost, and finality?
  - How does a smart contract differ from a manual or a scripted transaction in terms of automation, efficiency, and accuracy?
  - How does a stablecoin differ from a fiat currency or a volatile cryptocurrency in terms of stability, liquidity, and convertibility?
  - How does a ZKP or a homomorphic encryption differ from a plain or a hashed data in terms of privacy, confidentiality, and verifiability?
