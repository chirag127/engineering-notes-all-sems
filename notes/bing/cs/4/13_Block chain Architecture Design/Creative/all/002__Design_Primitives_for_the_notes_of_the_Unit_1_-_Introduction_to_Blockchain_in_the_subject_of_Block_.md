### Design Primitives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Design primitives are the basic building blocks of a blockchain system that define its functionality, performance, security, and scalability.
- According to , there are three main design primitives for blockchain: transaction design, consensus design, and block design.
- Transaction design refers to how the data is structured, validated, and processed on the blockchain. It includes aspects such as:
  - The format and content of the transactions, such as inputs, outputs, signatures, metadata, etc.
  - The rules and protocols for creating, verifying, and broadcasting transactions, such as scripting languages, transaction fees, nonce, etc.
  - The data structures and algorithms for storing and retrieving transactions, such as Merkle trees, hash pointers, etc.
- Consensus design refers to how the nodes in the network agree on the state and order of the transactions on the blockchain. It includes aspects such as:
  - The type and role of the nodes, such as full nodes, light nodes, miners, validators, etc.
  - The mechanism and criteria for selecting the leader or the proposer of the next block, such as proof-of-work, proof-of-stake, proof-of-authority, etc.
  - The protocol and rules for validating and appending the new block to the blockchain, such as longest chain rule, difficulty adjustment, forks, etc.
  - The incentives and rewards for participating in the consensus process, such as block rewards, transaction fees, penalties, etc.
- Block design refers to how the transactions are grouped, organized, and linked on the blockchain. It includes aspects such as:
  - The size and frequency of the blocks, such as block time, block limit, block interval, etc.
  - The format and content of the blocks, such as block header, block body, block hash, etc.
  - The structure and topology of the blockchain, such as linear chain, tree, DAG, etc.

- These design primitives can be combined and customized to create different types of blockchain systems, such as public, private, permissioned, permissionless, etc.
- These design primitives also affect the trade-offs and challenges of blockchain systems, such as scalability, security, privacy, interoperability, etc.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the three design primitives is: **T**ransactions are the **C**ore of the **B**lockchain. (TCB)
