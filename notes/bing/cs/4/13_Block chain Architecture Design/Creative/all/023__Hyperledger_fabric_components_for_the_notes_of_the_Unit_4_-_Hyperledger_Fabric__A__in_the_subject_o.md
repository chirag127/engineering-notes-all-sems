### Hyperledger fabric components for the notes of the Unit 4 - Hyperledger Fabric (A) in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Hyperledger Fabric is a distributed ledger technology (DLT) platform that allows for the development of applications or solutions with a modular architecture. Hyperledger Fabric has the following components:

- **Assets**: An asset is anything that has value, such as money, property, goods, etc. An asset has state and ownership, which are recorded on the ledger. Assets are represented in Hyperledger Fabric as a collection of key-value pairs.
- **Shared ledger**: The ledger records the state and ownership of assets, as well as the history of transactions that modify them. The ledger consists of two components:
  - **World state**: This is a database that describes the current state of the ledger at a given point in time. It is updated by applying transactions that are validated and committed by the network.
  - **Transaction log**: This is a record of all the transactions that have been executed on the ledger. It is used to verify the integrity and provenance of the world state.
- **Smart contract**: Hyperledger Fabric smart contracts are called chaincode. They are programs that define the business logic and rules for modifying the state of assets on the ledger. Chaincode is executed by peer nodes, which are the fundamental elements of the network. Chaincode can be written in various languages, such as Go, Java, or Node.js.
- **Peer nodes**: Peers are the nodes that host ledgers and chaincode, and participate in the transaction flow. Peers have different roles and responsibilities, such as:
  - **Endorsing peers**: These are peers that execute chaincode, verify the results, and endorse transactions by signing them. Endorsing peers are specified by an endorsement policy, which defines the number and identity of peers that must endorse a transaction before it can be committed.
  - **Committing peers**: These are peers that validate transactions and update the ledger. Committing peers check that the transactions have been endorsed by the required endorsing peers, and that they have not been tampered with. Committing peers also maintain the transaction log and the world state.
  - **Anchor peers**: These are peers that act as a bridge between different organizations in the network. Anchor peers communicate with other anchor peers to share information and updates about the network configuration and the ledger state.
- **Channel**: A channel is a private communication mechanism that allows a subset of organizations in the network to share a ledger and conduct transactions. A channel is defined by a configuration block that specifies the participating organizations, the anchor peers, the ordering service, and the policies that govern the channel. A channel provides isolation and confidentiality for the transactions and the ledger state.
- **Organizations**: An organization is a group of entities that share a common purpose and authority. An organization can have multiple peers, and can belong to multiple channels. An organization is identified by a unique name and a Membership Services Provider (MSP).
- **Membership Services Provider (MSP)**: An MSP is a component that defines the rules and policies for the identity, authentication, and authorization of the members of an organization. An MSP can use different mechanisms, such as certificates, signatures, or tokens, to verify the identity and credentials of the members. An MSP also defines the roles and privileges of the members, such as admin, peer, client, etc.
- **Ordering service**: The ordering service is a component that ensures the consistency and finality of the transactions on the ledger. The ordering service receives transactions from the endorsing peers, and batches them into blocks. The ordering service then delivers the blocks to the committing peers in a sequential and deterministic order. The ordering service can use different algorithms, such as Solo, Kafka, or Raft, to achieve consensus among the ordering nodes.
