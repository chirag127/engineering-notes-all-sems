### Blockchain in trade/supply chain for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Trade/supply chain is the process of moving goods from their origin to their destination, involving multiple parties such as producers, suppliers, transporters, distributors, retailers and consumers.
- Trade/supply chain faces many challenges such as lack of transparency, trust, efficiency, security and traceability, which can lead to delays, errors, fraud, disputes and losses.
- Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that can provide a shared, immutable and verifiable record of transactions among multiple parties, without the need for intermediaries or central authorities.
- Blockchain can offer many benefits for trade/supply chain, such as:
  - Transparency: Blockchain can enable real-time visibility and tracking of the movement and status of goods, documents and payments across the supply chain, reducing information asymmetry and increasing accountability.
  - Trust: Blockchain can ensure the authenticity and integrity of the data and documents exchanged among the parties, reducing the risk of tampering, duplication or falsification.
  - Efficiency: Blockchain can automate and streamline the processes and workflows of the supply chain, reducing manual intervention, paperwork, errors and costs.
  - Security: Blockchain can protect the data and documents from unauthorized access, modification or deletion, using encryption, digital signatures and consensus mechanisms.
  - Traceability: Blockchain can provide a complete and accurate history of the provenance and ownership of the goods, enabling product verification, quality assurance and recall management.
- Blockchain can be applied to various aspects of trade/supply chain, such as:
  - Trade finance: Blockchain can facilitate the issuance, verification and settlement of trade finance instruments, such as letters of credit, bills of lading and invoices, reducing the reliance on intermediaries and improving the liquidity and cash flow of the parties.
  - Smart contracts: Blockchain can enable the execution of self-enforcing agreements based on predefined rules and conditions, such as delivery confirmation, payment release and dispute resolution, enhancing the performance and compliance of the contracts.
  - Supply chain management: Blockchain can enable the coordination and collaboration of the supply chain actors, such as inventory management, demand forecasting, order fulfillment and quality control, improving the efficiency and responsiveness of the supply chain.
  - Supply chain analytics: Blockchain can provide valuable insights and intelligence from the data and documents collected and stored on the blockchain, such as market trends, consumer preferences, supplier performance and risk assessment, supporting the decision making and optimization of the supply chain.
