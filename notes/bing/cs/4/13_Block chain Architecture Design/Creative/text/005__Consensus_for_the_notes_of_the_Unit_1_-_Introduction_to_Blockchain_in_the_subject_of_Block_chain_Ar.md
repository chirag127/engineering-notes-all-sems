### Consensus for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Consensus is the process by which a group of peers – or nodes – on a network determine which blockchain transactions are valid and which are not.
- Consensus mechanisms are the methodologies used to achieve this agreement. They are sets of rules that help to protect networks from malicious behaviour and hacking attacks.
- Consensus mechanisms are essential for blockchain systems to achieve distributed agreement about the ledger's state. They ensure that all nodes have the same version of the ledger and that no conflicting transactions are accepted.
- Consensus mechanisms can be classified into different types based on various criteria, such as the level of participation, the resource consumption, the security guarantees, and the performance trade-offs.
- Some of the most common consensus mechanisms in blockchain are:
  - Proof of Work (PoW): This is the method used by Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies. It requires nodes to solve a cryptographic puzzle that is hard to compute but easy to verify. The node that solves the puzzle first gets to append a new block to the ledger and receive a reward. PoW is secure but consumes a lot of energy and has low scalability .
  - Proof of Stake (PoS): This is the method that Ethereum is planning to adopt. It assigns nodes a stake based on their amount of coins or tokens. The node with the highest stake gets to propose a new block and receive a reward. PoS is more energy-efficient and scalable than PoW, but it may introduce centralization and security risks .
  - Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS): This is a variation of PoS that allows nodes to delegate their stake to a group of validators. The validators are elected by the stakeholders and are responsible for proposing and validating new blocks. DPoS is faster and more democratic than PoS, but it may also suffer from centralization and corruption .
  - Proof of Authority (PoA): This is a method that relies on a set of trusted validators who have their identity and reputation at stake. The validators are pre-selected by the network and have the sole authority to create and validate new blocks. PoA is fast and efficient, but it sacrifices decentralization and censorship-resistance .
  - Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT): This is a method that can tolerate up to one-third of the nodes being faulty or malicious. It requires nodes to communicate and reach a consensus on each block through a series of rounds. BFT is robust and secure, but it has high communication overhead and limited scalability .
