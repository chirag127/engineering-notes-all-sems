### Digital Money to Distributed Ledgers

- Digital money is a form of electronic money that can be used to store, transfer, and exchange value digitally, without the need for physical cash or coins.
- Digital money can be issued by central banks, commercial banks, or other entities, and can be based on different technologies, such as smart cards, mobile wallets, or online platforms.
- Digital money can also be created and managed by decentralized networks of users, without the involvement of intermediaries or central authorities. This is the case of cryptocurrencies, such as Bitcoin, which use a technology called blockchain to record and verify transactions.
- Blockchain is a type of distributed ledger technology (DLT), which is a system of recording and sharing data among multiple participants in a network, using cryptographic methods to ensure its integrity and security.
- A distributed ledger is a database that is not stored or controlled by a single entity, but is replicated and synchronized across multiple nodes or locations. Each node has a copy of the ledger and can validate and update it independently, following a set of rules or protocols agreed by the network.
- A distributed ledger can record various types of information, such as transactions, contracts, assets, identities, or events, and can enable peer-to-peer exchange of value or data, without the need for intermediaries or trusted third parties.
- A distributed ledger can also be designed to have different levels of access, transparency, and governance, depending on the needs and preferences of the network participants. For example, some distributed ledgers are public and open to anyone, while others are private and restricted to a specific group of users.
- Distributed ledger technology has the potential to transform various sectors and industries, such as finance, trade, supply chain, health, education, and government, by improving efficiency, resilience, and reliability of data and processes, and by enabling new business models and services.
