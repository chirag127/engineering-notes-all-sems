## Unit 2 - Consensus

- Consensus is the process of reaching agreement among a group of participants on a common decision or action.
- Consensus is important for distributed systems, where multiple nodes need to coordinate their state and behavior in the presence of failures, network delays, and partial information.
- Consensus can be achieved by using various algorithms and protocols, such as Paxos, Raft, Byzantine Fault Tolerance, and Proof-of-Work.
- Consensus algorithms and protocols have different properties and trade-offs, such as safety, liveness, fault tolerance, performance, and scalability.
- Safety means that the nodes agree on the same value and do not violate any system invariants.
- Liveness means that the nodes eventually reach a decision and make progress.
- Fault tolerance means that the nodes can tolerate a certain number of failures or malicious behaviors without compromising safety or liveness.
- Performance means that the nodes can reach consensus quickly and efficiently, with low latency and overhead.
- Scalability means that the nodes can handle a large number of participants and transactions without degrading performance or reliability.
- Consensus is a fundamental and challenging problem in distributed systems, and it has many applications and implications, such as distributed databases, blockchain, leader election, replication, and coordination.
