### Provenance of goods

- Provenance of goods refers to the **chain of custody** of a product from the point of origin to the point of consumption .
- Provenance of goods is important for ensuring the **authenticity**, **quality**, and **sustainability** of products, as well as preventing **fraud** and **counterfeiting** .
- Blockchain technology can enable **traceability** and **transparency** of provenance of goods by providing a **decentralized**, **immutable**, and **verifiable** record of the product's history and transactions  .
- Blockchain technology can also facilitate **collaboration** and **coordination** among different stakeholders in the supply chain, such as manufacturers, distributors, retailers, and consumers, by creating a **shared** and **trusted** source of truth .
- Some of the use cases of blockchain for provenance of goods include:
  - Art: Blockchain can help verify the **ownership**, **originality**, and **value** of artworks, both digital and physical, by creating a **digital identity** and a **certificate of authenticity** for each piece.
  - Luxury goods: Blockchain can help ensure the **quality**, **origin**, and **ethical sourcing** of luxury goods, such as jewelry, precious metals and stones, wine collections, and limited collection apparel and accessories, by tracking the product's journey from the source to the end-user.
  - Land ownership: Blockchain can help establish the **legal rights** and **boundaries** of land ownership, as well as prevent **land grabbing** and **corruption**, by creating a **public** and **secure** ledger of land titles and transactions.
  - Supply chain information: Blockchain can help improve the **efficiency**, **visibility**, and **accountability** of supply chain operations, such as inventory management, quality control, logistics, and compliance, by providing real-time and accurate information on the location, condition, and status of goods.
