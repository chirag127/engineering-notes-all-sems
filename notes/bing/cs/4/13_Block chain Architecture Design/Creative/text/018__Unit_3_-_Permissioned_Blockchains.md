## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

- A permissioned blockchain is a distributed ledger that is not publicly accessible. It can only be accessed by users with permissions .
- Permissioned blockchains provide an additional level of security over typical blockchain systems like Bitcoin, as they require an access control layer. These blockchains are favored by entities who require security, identity, and role definition within the blockchain.
- Permissioned blockchains can be classified into two types: private and consortium .
  - A private blockchain is one where the participants are limited to a single organization or entity. The access rights and rules are defined by the central authority that controls the network. Examples of private blockchains are Hyperledger Fabric and Corda.
  - A consortium blockchain is one where the participants are limited to a group of organizations or entities that have agreed to cooperate on a common goal. The access rights and rules are defined by a consensus mechanism among the participants. Examples of consortium blockchains are R3 and Quorum.
- Permissioned blockchains have some advantages and disadvantages compared to permissionless blockchains.
  - Advantages:
    - Higher scalability and performance, as the number of nodes and transactions are limited and controlled.
    - Higher privacy and security, as the identity and role of the participants are verified and enforced.
    - Higher compliance and governance, as the rules and regulations are agreed upon and followed by the participants.
  - Disadvantages:
    - Lower decentralization and transparency, as the power and control are concentrated in a few entities or nodes.
    - Lower innovation and diversity, as the network is closed and restricted to a specific domain or purpose.
    - Lower interoperability and compatibility, as the network may not be compatible with other blockchains or systems.
