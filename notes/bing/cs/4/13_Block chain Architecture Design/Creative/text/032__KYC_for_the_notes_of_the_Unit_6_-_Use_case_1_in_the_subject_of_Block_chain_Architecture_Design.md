### KYC for the notes of the Unit 6 - Use case 1 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- KYC stands for Know Your Customer, which is a process of verifying the identity and credentials of customers who want to use certain services or products, such as banking, insurance, or cryptocurrency.
- KYC is important for preventing fraud, money laundering, terrorism financing, and other illegal activities that may harm the service providers or other customers.
- KYC usually involves collecting and verifying personal information, such as name, address, date of birth, nationality, identification documents, and biometric data, from the customers.
- KYC also involves checking the customers against various databases, such as sanctions lists, watchlists, blacklists, and whitelists, to ensure that they are not involved in any criminal or suspicious activities.
- KYC can be challenging and costly for service providers, especially in the context of global and digital transactions, where customers may be located in different jurisdictions and have different regulatory requirements.
- Block chain can offer a potential solution for KYC, by providing a decentralized, secure, and transparent platform for storing and sharing customer data among different service providers, regulators, and customers themselves.
- Block chain can enable a more efficient, accurate, and compliant KYC process, by reducing the duplication of efforts, enhancing the data quality and integrity, and facilitating the data access and auditability.
- Block chain can also empower the customers to have more control and ownership over their own data, by allowing them to create and manage their own digital identities, and to consent or revoke the access to their data by different service providers.
- Block chain can also enable a more inclusive and accessible KYC process, by lowering the barriers and costs for customers who may not have traditional identification documents or bank accounts, and by providing them with more opportunities and choices for using different services and products.
- Block chain can also create new challenges and risks for KYC, such as the scalability, interoperability, and standardization of different block chain platforms and protocols, the privacy and security of customer data, and the legal and regulatory compliance of block chain-based KYC solutions.
