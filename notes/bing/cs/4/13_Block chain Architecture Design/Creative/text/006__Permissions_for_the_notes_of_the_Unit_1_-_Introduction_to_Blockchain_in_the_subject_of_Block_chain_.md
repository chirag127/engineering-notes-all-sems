### Permissions for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Permissions are the rules that define who can access, modify, or validate the data stored in a blockchain network.
- Permissions can be classified into three types: public, private, and consortium.
- Public blockchains are open and permissionless, meaning anyone can join, read, write, or verify transactions on the network. Examples of public blockchains are Bitcoin and Ethereum.
- Private blockchains are closed and permissioned, meaning only authorized entities can join, read, write, or verify transactions on the network. Examples of private blockchains are Hyperledger Fabric and Corda.
- Consortium blockchains are hybrid and semi-permissioned, meaning a group of trusted entities can join, read, write, or verify transactions on the network, while others can only read or write. Examples of consortium blockchains are Quorum and R3.
- The choice of permissions depends on the use case, requirements, and trade-offs of the blockchain network. Some factors to consider are scalability, security, privacy, governance, and interoperability.
