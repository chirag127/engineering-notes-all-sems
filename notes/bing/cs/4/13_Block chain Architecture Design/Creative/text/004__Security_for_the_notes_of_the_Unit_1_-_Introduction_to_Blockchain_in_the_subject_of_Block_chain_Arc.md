### Security

- Security is one of the key features of blockchain technology that makes it suitable for various applications that require trust, transparency and immutability.
- Security in blockchain is achieved by using cryptographic techniques, such as hashing, digital signatures, encryption and consensus mechanisms, that ensure the integrity, authenticity, confidentiality and availability of the data stored in the blocks.
- Hashing is the process of transforming any input data into a fixed-length output, called a hash or a digest, that uniquely identifies the input data. Hashing is used to link the blocks in a blockchain, as each block contains the hash of the previous block, creating a chain of hashes that can be traced back to the genesis block. Hashing also ensures that the data in the blocks cannot be tampered with, as any modification would result in a different hash that would break the chain of hashes.
- Digital signatures are used to verify the identity and authority of the participants in a blockchain network, as well as to ensure the non-repudiation of the transactions. Digital signatures are based on public-key cryptography, which involves a pair of keys: a public key and a private key. The public key can be shared with anyone, while the private key is kept secret by the owner. The owner can use the private key to sign a message, and anyone can use the public key to verify the signature. Digital signatures are attached to the transactions in a blockchain, and they prove that the sender is the owner of the private key and that the message has not been altered.
- Encryption is the process of transforming data into an unreadable form, using a secret key, that can only be decrypted by the intended recipient, who has the corresponding key. Encryption is used to protect the confidentiality and privacy of the data in a blockchain, as well as to enable secure communication between the nodes in a blockchain network. Encryption can be symmetric or asymmetric, depending on whether the same key or different keys are used for encryption and decryption. Symmetric encryption is faster and simpler, but it requires a secure way of sharing the key. Asymmetric encryption, also known as public-key encryption, uses a pair of keys, similar to digital signatures, and it does not require a secure way of sharing the key, but it is slower and more complex.
- Consensus mechanisms are the rules and protocols that govern how the nodes in a blockchain network agree on the validity and order of the transactions and blocks. Consensus mechanisms are essential for maintaining the consistency and reliability of the blockchain, as well as for preventing malicious attacks, such as double-spending, Sybil attacks, or 51% attacks. Consensus mechanisms can be classified into two categories: proof-based and voting-based. Proof-based mechanisms, such as proof-of-work, proof-of-stake, proof-of-authority, or proof-of-burn, require the nodes to perform some work or stake some resources to participate in the consensus process. Voting-based mechanisms, such as Byzantine fault tolerance, delegated proof-of-stake, or practical Byzantine fault tolerance, require the nodes to vote on the validity and order of the transactions and blocks, based on some criteria or reputation.
