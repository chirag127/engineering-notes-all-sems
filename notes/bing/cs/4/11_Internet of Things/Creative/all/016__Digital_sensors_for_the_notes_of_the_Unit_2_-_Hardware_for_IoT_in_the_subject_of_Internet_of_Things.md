### Digital sensors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Digital sensors are sensors that produce a discrete output signal that can be directly processed by an IoT microcontroller.
- Digital sensors can be classified into two types: active and passive.
  - Active digital sensors are sensors that generate their own output signal without requiring an external power source. Examples of active digital sensors are photodiodes, thermocouples, and piezoelectric sensors.
  - Passive digital sensors are sensors that require an external power source to produce an output signal. Examples of passive digital sensors are resistive, capacitive, and inductive sensors.
- Digital sensors have some advantages over analog sensors, such as:
  - Higher accuracy and resolution, as there is no need for analog-to-digital conversion.
  - Easier interfacing and communication with IoT devices, as there is no need for signal conditioning or amplification.
  - Lower power consumption and noise, as there is no analog circuitry involved.
- Digital sensors also have some challenges, such as:
  - Higher cost and complexity, as they may require additional components or protocols to function.
  - Limited range and sensitivity, as they may have a fixed number of output levels or states.
  - Compatibility and interoperability issues, as they may use different standards or formats for data transmission.
- Digital sensors are widely used in IoT applications, such as:
  - Consumer devices, such as smart watches, fitness trackers, and wearable sensors.
  - Industry 4.0, such as smart factories, predictive maintenance, and industrial automation.
  - Medical applications, such as remote patient monitoring, telemedicine, and biosensors.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the types of digital sensors is: **APRIC** (Active, Passive, Resistive, Inductive, Capacitive).
- A possible learning trick to understand the difference between active and passive digital sensors is: **Active sensors act on their own, passive sensors need power to show.**
