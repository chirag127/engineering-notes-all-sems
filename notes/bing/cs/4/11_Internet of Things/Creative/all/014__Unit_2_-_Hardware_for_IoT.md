## Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT

- Hardware for IoT refers to the physical devices and components that enable the connectivity, communication, and functionality of IoT systems.
- Hardware for IoT can be classified into four main categories: sensors, microcontrollers, communication modules, and power sources.
- Sensors are devices that measure physical quantities such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, motion, etc. and convert them into electrical signals. Sensors are the most critical hardware in IoT applications and are used to gather information from the surroundings. Sensors can be analog or digital, active or passive, wired or wireless, etc. depending on their specifications and requirements. Some examples of sensors used in IoT are thermistors, photodiodes, accelerometers, gyroscopes, etc.
- Microcontrollers are small computers that execute a single task or program. They have a processor, memory, input/output ports, and sometimes other peripherals such as timers, analog-to-digital converters, etc. Microcontrollers are used to process the data from the sensors, control the actuators, and communicate with other devices or networks. Some examples of microcontrollers used in IoT are Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi, etc.
- Communication modules are devices that enable the transmission and reception of data between the IoT devices and the network. They can use different protocols and technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, LoRa, cellular, etc. depending on the range, bandwidth, power consumption, and security of the communication. Communication modules can be integrated into the microcontrollers or be separate modules that connect to them via serial, SPI, I2C, etc. Some examples of communication modules used in IoT are ESP8266, nRF24L01, SIM800L, etc.
- Power sources are devices that provide the electrical energy to the IoT devices. They can be batteries, solar panels, wind turbines, etc. depending on the availability, cost, and environmental impact of the power source. Power sources are important for the reliability and sustainability of the IoT systems, especially for those that operate in remote or harsh environments. Some examples of power sources used in IoT are AA batteries, LiPo batteries, solar cells, etc.

- Hardware for IoT has some challenges and limitations such as compatibility, scalability, security, cost, etc. that need to be addressed and overcome by the developers and users of the IoT systems.
- Hardware for IoT has some advantages and applications such as flexibility, customization, innovation, efficiency, etc. that make it suitable and beneficial for various domains and scenarios such as smart homes, smart cities, smart agriculture, smart healthcare, etc.
