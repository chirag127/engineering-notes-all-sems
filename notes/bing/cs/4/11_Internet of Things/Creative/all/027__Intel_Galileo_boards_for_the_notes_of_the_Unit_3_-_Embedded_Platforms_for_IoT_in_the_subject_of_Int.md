### Intel Galileo boards for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Intel Galileo is a development board based on Intel x86 architecture that is compatible with Arduino software and hardware.
- It is designed for the maker and education communities, and can be used for various IoT projects and applications .
- It has a 32-bit Intel Quark SoC X1000 processor, which is a low-power, single-core, Pentium-class CPU running at 400 MHz .
- It has 256 MB of DDR3 RAM and 8 MB of NOR flash memory, as well as a micro SD card slot for up to 32 GB of additional storage .
- It has 14 digital input/output pins, 6 of which can be used as PWM outputs, and 6 analog input pins .
- It has a USB host port, a USB client port, a 10/100 Mbps Ethernet port, a mini PCIe slot, and an RS-232 serial port .
- It supports various communication protocols, such as I2C, SPI, UART, and GPIO .
- It can be programmed using the Arduino IDE, the Intel XDK IoT Edition, or other compatible tools .
- It can run Linux-based operating systems, such as Yocto, which provide libraries for accessing the hardware features, such as MRAA and UPM .
- It can connect to the cloud and other devices using Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or cellular modules that can be plugged into the mini PCIe slot  .

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- To remember the processor name and speed, you can use the phrase **Quark at 400**.
- To remember the RAM and flash memory sizes, you can use the numbers **256** and **8**, which are both powers of 2.
- To remember the number of digital and analog pins, you can use the numbers **14** and **6**, which are both multiples of 2.
- To remember the communication protocols, you can use the acronym **ISUG** (I2C, SPI, UART, GPIO).
- To remember the Linux libraries, you can use the acronym **MUP** (MRAA, UPM).
