### E-health for the notes of the Unit 7 - IoT Applications in the subject of Internet of Things

- E-health is the use of digital technology to improve health and healthcare services.
- IoT is the network of physical devices, sensors, actuators, and software that can collect, process, and exchange data over the internet.
- IoT applications in e-health can be classified into three categories: personal health, clinical health, and public health.
- Personal health applications involve the use of smart wearables, implants, and ingestible electronics to monitor and manage the health and wellness of individuals. Examples are fitness trackers, smartwatches, glucose monitors, pacemakers, and pill cameras.
- Clinical health applications involve the use of IoT devices and systems to support the diagnosis, treatment, and care of patients in healthcare settings. Examples are remote physiological monitoring, telemedicine, smart beds, infusion pumps, and surgical robots.
- Public health applications involve the use of IoT devices and systems to enhance the prevention, surveillance, and response to public health issues and emergencies. Examples are environmental sensors, smart cities, disease outbreak detection, and disaster management.
- Some of the advantages of IoT applications in e-health are:
  - Improved access to healthcare services, especially for remote and underserved populations.
  - Enhanced quality and efficiency of healthcare delivery, reducing errors, costs, and waste.
  - Increased patient empowerment and engagement, enabling self-care and personalized medicine.
  - Better health outcomes and quality of life, preventing and managing chronic diseases and aging-related issues.
- Some of the challenges and risks of IoT applications in e-health are:
  - Privacy and security of sensitive health data, which can be vulnerable to cyberattacks, breaches, and misuse.
  - Safety and reliability of IoT devices and systems, which can malfunction, fail, or cause harm to users and patients.
  - Ethics and social implications of IoT applications in e-health, which can raise issues of consent, trust, accountability, and equity.
  - Interoperability and standardization of IoT devices and systems, which can vary in terms of protocols, formats, and quality.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the three categories of IoT applications in e-health is P-C-P: Personal, Clinical, and Public.
