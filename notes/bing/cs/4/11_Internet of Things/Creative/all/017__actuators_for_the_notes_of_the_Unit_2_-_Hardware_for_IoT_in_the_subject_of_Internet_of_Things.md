### Actuators for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- An actuator is a device that converts energy into motion. It does this by taking an electrical signal and combining it with an energy source.
- In an IoT system, the actuator can act on data collected by sensors to create an outcome as determined by the chosen settings of the user.
- For example, a smart thermostat can use a temperature sensor to detect the room temperature and send a signal to an actuator that controls the heating or cooling system.
- Some common types of actuators used in IoT are  :
  - Servo Motors: A servomotor is a rotary actuator or linear actuator that allows for precise control of angular or linear position, velocity and acceleration. Servo motors are widely used in robotics, automation, and CNC machines.
  - Stepper Motors: Stepper motors are DC motors that move in discrete steps. They can be controlled by pulses of current that determine the direction and speed of rotation. Stepper motors are used in applications that require precise positioning, such as 3D printers, scanners, and cameras.
  - DC Motors (Continuous Rotation Motors): DC (Direct Current) Motors are two wire (power & ground), continuous rotation motors. They can vary their speed and direction by changing the polarity and voltage of the current. DC motors are used in applications that require continuous rotation, such as fans, pumps, and wheels.
  - Linear Actuators: Linear actuators are devices that convert rotational motion into linear motion. They can be driven by electric motors, hydraulic pumps, pneumatic cylinders, or magnetic fields. Linear actuators are used in applications that require linear movement, such as valves, doors, and lifts.
  - Thermal/Magnetic Actuators: These are actuated by thermal or magnetic energy. Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs) or Magnetic Shape Memory Alloys (MSMAs) are materials that can change their shape when heated or magnetized. They can be used to create actuators that are lightweight, compact, and silent.
  - Mechanical Actuators: A mechanical actuator executes movement by converting rotary motion into linear motion. It can be composed of gears, screws, levers, springs, or cams. Mechanical actuators are used in applications that require high force, such as clamps, presses, and switches.
  - Soft Actuators: Soft actuators are made of flexible materials that can deform or bend when stimulated by electric, pneumatic, or chemical signals. They can mimic the movements of natural muscles and organs. Soft actuators are used in applications that require softness, adaptability, and biocompatibility, such as wearable devices, prosthetics, and soft robots.
  - Light Activated Actuators: Light activated actuators are materials that can change their shape, color, or transparency when exposed to light. They can be based on liquid crystals, polymers, or nanoparticles. Light activated actuators are used in applications that require optical modulation, such as displays, sensors, and switches.
- Some advantages of actuators in IoT are  :
  - They can enable physical interaction between the digital and physical worlds, such as controlling devices, manipulating objects, or creating feedback.
  - They can improve the efficiency, convenience, and safety of various processes and systems, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart factories, and smart healthcare.
  - They can create new possibilities and experiences for users, such as augmented reality, virtual reality, and haptic communication.
- Some disadvantages of actuators in IoT are  :
  - They can increase the complexity, cost, and power consumption of IoT devices and systems, as they require additional components, wiring, and batteries.
  - They can introduce new challenges and risks, such as mechanical failures, malfunctions, or cyberattacks, that can affect the performance, reliability, and security of IoT devices and systems.
  - They can raise ethical, social, and environmental concerns, such as privacy, autonomy, accountability, and sustainability, that need to be addressed by users, developers, and regulators.
