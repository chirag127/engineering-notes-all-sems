### Security Challenges for the notes of the Unit 6 - Challenges in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects or devices that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies to collect and exchange data with other devices and systems over the internet.
- IoT security is the practice of protecting IoT devices and networks from unauthorized access, manipulation, or harm.
- IoT security faces many challenges due to the diversity, complexity, and scale of IoT systems. Some of the major security challenges in IoT are:

  - **Lack of encryption**: Encryption is a technique to transform data into an unreadable form that can only be decrypted by authorized parties. Encryption is essential to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity of data transmitted or stored by IoT devices. However, many IoT devices do not use encryption or use weak encryption algorithms that can be easily broken by hackers. This can expose sensitive data to unauthorized access or modification, or allow attackers to impersonate legitimate devices or users.
  - **Insufficient testing and updating**: Testing and updating are important processes to identify and fix vulnerabilities or bugs in IoT devices and software. However, many IoT devices are not tested or updated regularly or adequately, leaving them exposed to known or new threats. Moreover, some IoT devices do not have a secure mechanism to receive and install updates, making them vulnerable to tampering or hijacking by attackers.
  - **Brute forcing and the risk of default passwords**: Brute forcing is a method of guessing passwords or other credentials by trying different combinations until finding the correct one. Many IoT devices use default or weak passwords that can be easily guessed by brute forcing tools. This can allow attackers to gain access to the devices or their data, or use them for malicious purposes. Therefore, it is important to change the default passwords and use strong and unique passwords for each IoT device and account.
  - **IoT malware and ransomware**: Malware is a malicious software that can infect, damage, or control IoT devices or networks. Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts the data or functionality of IoT devices or networks and demands a ransom for restoring them. IoT malware and ransomware can be spread by exploiting vulnerabilities, phishing, or social engineering. They can cause various harms, such as stealing data, disrupting services, extorting money, or launching attacks on other targets.
  - **IoT botnet aiming at cryptocurrency**: A botnet is a network of compromised IoT devices that are controlled by a central entity, usually a hacker or a criminal group. A botnet can be used to perform various malicious activities, such as spamming, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, or mining cryptocurrency. Cryptocurrency is a digital currency that is based on cryptography and blockchain technology. Mining cryptocurrency is a process of validating transactions and generating new units of the currency by solving complex mathematical problems. However, mining cryptocurrency requires a lot of computing power and energy, which can be costly. Therefore, some hackers or criminals use IoT botnets to mine cryptocurrency without the consent or knowledge of the device owners, consuming their resources and bandwidth, and potentially damaging their devices.
