### Sources of the IoT

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that can communicate and exchange data over the internet. The sources of the IoT are the devices, sensors, and systems that generate, collect, and transmit data. Some of the common sources of the IoT are:

- **Sensors**: Sensors are devices that can measure physical or environmental parameters, such as temperature, pressure, humidity, motion, light, sound, etc. Sensors can be embedded in various objects, such as machines, vehicles, buildings, appliances, wearables, etc. Sensors can send data to other devices or systems through wireless or wired connections. Sensors are one of the main sources of industrial IoT data, as they can monitor the performance and condition of assets, processes, and environments  .
- **Actuators**: Actuators are devices that can perform physical actions, such as turning on/off, opening/closing, moving, etc. Actuators can be controlled by other devices or systems through wireless or wired connections. Actuators can enable automation and remote control of various processes and functions. Actuators are often used in conjunction with sensors to create feedback loops and smart systems .
- **RFID tags and readers**: RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is a technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects. RFID tags are small chips that can store information and can be attached to various objects, such as products, assets, animals, etc. RFID readers are devices that can read the information from the tags and send it to other devices or systems. RFID can enable inventory management, asset tracking, authentication, security, etc. RFID is one of the technologies that can facilitate the role of IoT in various applications.
- **Smartphones and tablets**: Smartphones and tablets are devices that can access the internet and run various applications. Smartphones and tablets can also have various sensors, such as cameras, microphones, GPS, accelerometers, etc. Smartphones and tablets can act as sources of IoT data, as they can capture and send data about the user's location, activity, preferences, etc. Smartphones and tablets can also act as interfaces and controllers for other IoT devices and systems .
- **Wearables and biometrics**: Wearables and biometrics are devices that can be worn or implanted on the human body and can measure various physiological and behavioral parameters, such as heart rate, blood pressure, glucose level, sleep quality, mood, etc. Wearables and biometrics can send data to other devices or systems through wireless connections. Wearables and biometrics can enable health monitoring, fitness tracking, wellness management, etc. Wearables and biometrics are one of the sources of IoT data in the consumer and healthcare domains .
- **Smart appliances and devices**: Smart appliances and devices are devices that can connect to the internet and have various features and functions, such as remote control, automation, energy efficiency, etc. Smart appliances and devices can include various objects, such as TVs, refrigerators, thermostats, lights, speakers, etc. Smart appliances and devices can send data to other devices or systems through wireless or wired connections. Smart appliances and devices can enable smart home, smart office, smart entertainment, etc. Smart appliances and devices are one of the sources of IoT data in the commercial and residential domains  .
- **Machines and equipment**: Machines and equipment are devices that can perform various tasks and operations, such as manufacturing, transportation, agriculture, mining, etc. Machines and equipment can have various sensors and actuators that can measure and control their performance and condition. Machines and equipment can send data to other devices or systems through wireless or wired connections. Machines and equipment can enable industrial IoT, smart manufacturing, smart logistics, smart agriculture, etc. Machines and equipment are one of the main sources of IoT data in the industrial domain  .
