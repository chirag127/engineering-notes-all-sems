### ARM Cortex for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- ARM Cortex is a family of processors designed by ARM Holdings for various applications, including IoT and embedded systems.
- ARM Cortex processors are based on the Reduced Instruction Set Computer (RISC) architecture, which enables high performance, low power consumption, and code density.
- ARM Cortex processors are divided into three categories: Cortex-A, Cortex-R, and Cortex-M, each with different features and capabilities.

- Cortex-A processors are optimized for high-performance applications, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, and servers. They support multiple operating systems, such as Linux, Android, and Windows, and can run complex software stacks. Cortex-A processors have features such as memory management unit (MMU), cache, floating-point unit (FPU), and symmetric multiprocessing (SMP).
- Cortex-R processors are designed for real-time applications, such as automotive, industrial, and medical systems. They offer high reliability, safety, and responsiveness, and can handle critical tasks with tight deadlines. Cortex-R processors have features such as memory protection unit (MPU), lock-step mode, and error correction code (ECC).
- Cortex-M processors are targeted for low-cost and low-power applications, such as IoT devices, sensors, and microcontrollers. They are easy to program and integrate, and can run bare-metal or with a lightweight operating system, such as FreeRTOS, Zephyr, or Mbed OS. Cortex-M processors have features such as interrupt controller, debug and trace unit, and sleep modes.

- ARM also provides solutions for IoT and embedded development, such as Corstone subsystems and Virtual Hardware.

- Corstone subsystems are pre-integrated designs that combine Cortex-M or Cortex-A processors with essential components, such as memory, security, and peripherals. Corstone subsystems simplify and accelerate the development of IoT and embedded devices, and enable faster time to market and lower risk.
- Virtual Hardware is a cloud-based platform that enables developers to simulate and test IoT and embedded devices without physical hardware. Virtual Hardware supports various platforms, such as Raspberry Pi, Arduino, and STM32, and allows developers to use their preferred tools, such as Arm Development Studio, Keil MDK, or Visual Studio Code. Virtual Hardware reduces the cost and complexity of IoT and embedded development, and enables faster prototyping and debugging.

- One of the latest products from ARM is the Cortex-M85 processor, which is the highest performance Cortex-M processor to date. It offers a 25% increase in performance over the Cortex-M7 processor, and supports features such as DSP extensions, FPU, and TrustZone for Armv8-M. The Cortex-M85 processor is suitable for applications that require high performance and security, such as smart home, industrial, and automotive systems.
