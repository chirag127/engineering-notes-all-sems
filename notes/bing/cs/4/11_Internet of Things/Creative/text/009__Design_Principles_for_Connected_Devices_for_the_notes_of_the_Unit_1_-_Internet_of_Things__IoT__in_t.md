### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- Connected devices are physical objects that can communicate with other devices or systems through the internet or other networks.
- Connected devices can also be called smart devices, IoT devices, or embedded devices.
- Connected devices can have various functions, such as sensing, processing, actuating, displaying, or interacting with users or the environment.
- Connected devices can enable new applications and services that improve efficiency, convenience, safety, or quality of life.
- Connected devices can also pose challenges and risks, such as security, privacy, interoperability, or reliability.
- To design connected devices, some principles are:

  - **User-centric**: The device should meet the needs and expectations of the users, and provide a clear value proposition and user experience.
  - **Context-aware**: The device should adapt to the context of use, such as location, time, activity, or user preferences, and provide relevant information and functionality.
  - **Networked**: The device should leverage the connectivity and data from other devices or systems, and enable collaboration and coordination among them.
  - **Scalable**: The device should be able to handle increasing numbers of users, devices, or data, and maintain performance and quality of service.
  - **Secure**: The device should protect the data and functionality from unauthorized access or manipulation, and ensure the integrity and confidentiality of the communication.
  - **Reliable**: The device should function correctly and consistently, and handle errors or failures gracefully.
  - **Sustainable**: The device should minimize the environmental impact and resource consumption, and maximize the lifespan and recyclability.
