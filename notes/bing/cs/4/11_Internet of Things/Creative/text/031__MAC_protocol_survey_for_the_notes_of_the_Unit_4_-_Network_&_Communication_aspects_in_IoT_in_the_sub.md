### MAC protocol survey for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- MAC (Medium Access Control) protocols are responsible for coordinating the access of multiple devices to the shared wireless medium in IoT (Internet of Things) networks.
- MAC protocols can be classified into two main categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based MAC protocols allow devices to compete for the channel access, such as CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance) and ALOHA. These protocols are simple and flexible, but may suffer from collisions, hidden terminal problem, and low channel utilization.
- Contention-free MAC protocols assign the channel access to devices in a deterministic way, such as TDMA (Time Division Multiple Access), FDMA (Frequency Division Multiple Access), and CDMA (Code Division Multiple Access). These protocols can avoid collisions and improve channel utilization, but may incur overhead, synchronization, and scalability issues.
- MAC protocols for IoT should consider the following challenges and requirements: energy efficiency, scalability, reliability, latency, heterogeneity, mobility, and security.
- Some examples of MAC protocols for IoT are:

  - IEEE 802.11ah, also known as WiFi HaLow, is a scalable solution for medium-range communication in IoT. It leverages various innovative medium access techniques, such as restricted access window (RAW), target wake time (TWT), and group addressed transmission (GAT), to support a large number of devices with low power consumption and high reliability .
  - IEEE 802.15.4, also known as ZigBee, is a widely used standard for short-range communication in IoT. It supports both contention-based and contention-free MAC modes, such as slotted and unslotted CSMA/CA, beacon-enabled and non-beacon-enabled modes, and guaranteed time slots (GTS). It also provides security and mobility features.
  - LoRaWAN, also known as Long Range Wide Area Network, is a low-power wide-area network (LPWAN) technology for IoT. It uses a star-of-stars topology, where end devices communicate with gateways using LoRa modulation, and gateways forward the messages to a network server using IP-based protocols. It supports adaptive data rate (ADR), duty cycle control, and encryption.
  - BLE, also known as Bluetooth Low Energy, is a low-power short-range wireless technology for IoT. It uses a frequency hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) technique to avoid interference and enhance security. It supports two MAC modes: connection-oriented and connectionless. In the connection-oriented mode, devices establish a link and exchange data using time slots. In the connectionless mode, devices broadcast data using advertising channels.
