### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) is a technology that uses radio waves to passively identify a tagged object.
- An RFID system consists of a tiny radio transponder (tag), a radio receiver and transmitter (reader), and a database (server).
- The tag contains a unique identifier and possibly other information stored in its memory. The reader sends out an electromagnetic interrogation pulse and receives the tag's response. The server stores and processes the data from the tags and readers.
- RFID technology can be used for several applications, such as tracking items along a supply chain, keeping track of inventory, managing access control, identifying animals, and verifying documents  .
- RFID technology has some advantages over other identification methods, such as barcode or optical scanning. RFID tags can be read from a distance, without requiring a line of sight or physical contact. RFID tags can also store more data and be updated or rewritten .
- RFID technology also has some challenges and limitations, such as interference from other radio sources, security and privacy issues, high cost of tags and readers, and standardization and compatibility issues  .
