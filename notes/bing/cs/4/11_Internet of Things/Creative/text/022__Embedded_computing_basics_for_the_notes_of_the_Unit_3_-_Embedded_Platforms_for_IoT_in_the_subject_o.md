### Embedded computing basics

- Embedded computing is the process of designing and implementing computer systems that are integrated with physical devices, such as sensors, actuators, displays, and communication interfaces.
- Embedded computing systems are often constrained by factors such as power consumption, memory size, processing speed, and cost, which require careful optimization and trade-offs.
- Embedded computing systems are widely used in various domains, such as industrial automation, automotive, aerospace, medical, consumer electronics, and Internet of Things (IoT).
- Embedded computing systems typically consist of the following components:
  - Hardware: the physical devices that provide the computing capabilities, such as microcontrollers, microprocessors, system-on-chips (SoCs), field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs), and application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs).
  - Software: the programs that run on the hardware and provide the functionality and logic of the system, such as operating systems, drivers, libraries, middleware, and applications.
  - Firmware: the software that is stored in non-volatile memory and controls the low-level operations of the hardware, such as booting, initialization, and configuration.
  - Peripherals: the devices that are connected to the hardware and provide the input/output interfaces, such as sensors, actuators, displays, keyboards, mice, cameras, speakers, and network adapters.
- Embedded computing systems can be classified into different categories based on their characteristics, such as:
  - Stand-alone vs. networked: whether the system operates independently or communicates with other systems or the internet.
  - Real-time vs. non-real-time: whether the system has strict timing requirements and deadlines for its tasks or not.
  - General-purpose vs. domain-specific: whether the system can perform a variety of tasks or is optimized for a specific application or domain.
  - Single-core vs. multi-core: whether the system has one or more processing units that can execute tasks concurrently or in parallel.
  - Homogeneous vs. heterogeneous: whether the system has the same or different types of processing units, such as CPUs, GPUs, DSPs, etc.
