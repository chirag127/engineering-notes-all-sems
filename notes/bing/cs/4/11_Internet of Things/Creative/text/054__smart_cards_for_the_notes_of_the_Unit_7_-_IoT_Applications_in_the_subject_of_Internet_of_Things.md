### Smart Cards

- Smart cards are devices that store and process data using an embedded microchip and a wireless interface.
- Smart cards can be used for various applications such as identification, authentication, payment, access control, and data storage.
- Smart cards can communicate with other devices using contact or contactless methods. Contact methods require physical contact between the card and the reader, while contactless methods use radio frequency (RF) or near-field communication (NFC) technologies.
- Smart cards can be classified into three types based on their memory and processing capabilities: memory cards, microprocessor cards, and cryptographic cards.
- Memory cards have a fixed amount of memory and can only store data. They are typically used for prepaid services or loyalty programs.
- Microprocessor cards have a CPU, RAM, ROM, and I/O ports that enable them to perform computations and execute commands. They are typically used for secure transactions or personal identification.
- Cryptographic cards have a special chip that can perform cryptographic operations such as encryption, decryption, digital signature, and key generation. They are typically used for high-security applications such as electronic passports or digital certificates.
