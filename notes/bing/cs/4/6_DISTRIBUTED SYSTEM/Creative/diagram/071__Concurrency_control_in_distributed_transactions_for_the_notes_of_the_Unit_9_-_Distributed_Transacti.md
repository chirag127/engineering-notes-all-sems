### Concurrency control in distributed transactions

Concurrency control in distributed transactions is the process of ensuring that the ACID properties of transactions are preserved when they are executed concurrently in a distributed database system. A distributed database system consists of a set of data servers that host related data and communicate with each other to process transactions.

There are different methods of concurrency control in distributed transactions, such as locking-based, timestamp-based, and optimistic protocols. Each method has its own advantages and disadvantages in terms of performance, scalability, and complexity. A common challenge for all methods is to handle network failures, data conflicts, and deadlocks.
