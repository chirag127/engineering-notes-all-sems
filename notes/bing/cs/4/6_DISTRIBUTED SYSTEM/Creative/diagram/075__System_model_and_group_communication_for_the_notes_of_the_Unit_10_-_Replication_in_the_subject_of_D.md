A system model is a set of assumptions and properties that describe the behavior and characteristics of a distributed system. A system model can include aspects such as:

- The number and type of processes and nodes in the system
- The communication channels and network topology
- The failure modes and fault tolerance mechanisms
- The timing and synchronization assumptions
- The consistency and concurrency models

A group communication is a mechanism that allows one or more processes to send messages to a subset or all of the processes in a distributed system. A group communication can have different properties and guarantees, such as:

- The delivery order and reliability of messages
- The membership and view of the group
- The atomicity and agreement of operations
- The fault detection and recovery of processes

A replication is a technique that creates and maintains multiple copies of the same data or service across different nodes in a distributed system. A replication can have different goals and benefits, such as:

- The availability and performance of data or service
- The fault tolerance and resilience of data or service
- The scalability and load balancing of data or service
- The consistency and coherence of data or service
