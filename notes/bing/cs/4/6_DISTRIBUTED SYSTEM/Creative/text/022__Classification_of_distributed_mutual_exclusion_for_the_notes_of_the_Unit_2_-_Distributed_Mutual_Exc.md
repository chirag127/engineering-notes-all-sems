### Classification of distributed mutual exclusion

- Distributed mutual exclusion is the problem of ensuring that only one process in a distributed system can execute a critical section (CS) at a time, using only message passing as the communication mechanism.
- Distributed mutual exclusion algorithms can be classified into three basic approaches: token-based, non-token-based, and quorum-based.
- Token-based approach: A unique token is shared among the processes. A process can enter its CS only if it has the token. The token is passed from one process to another according to some protocol. This approach ensures mutual exclusion because the token is unique. Examples of token-based algorithms are: Ricart-Agrawala algorithm, Suzuki-Kasami algorithm, Raymond's algorithm, etc.
- Non-token-based approach: A process requests permission from other processes to enter its CS. A process can enter its CS only if it receives permission from all or a majority of other processes. This approach does not require a token, but may incur more message overhead. Examples of non-token-based algorithms are: Lamport's algorithm, Ricart-Agrawala algorithm, Maekawa's algorithm, etc.
- Quorum-based approach: A process requests permission from a subset of processes, called a quorum, to enter its CS. A process can enter its CS only if it receives permission from all the processes in its quorum. This approach reduces the message overhead by selecting smaller quorums, but may introduce conflicts or deadlocks. Examples of quorum-based algorithms are: Maekawa's algorithm, Tree-based algorithm, etc.
