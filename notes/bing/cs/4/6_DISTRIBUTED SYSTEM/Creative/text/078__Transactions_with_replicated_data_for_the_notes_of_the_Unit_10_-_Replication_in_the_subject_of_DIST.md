### Transactions with Replicated Data

- Transactions are a sequence of operations that read and write data in a database, and must satisfy the ACID properties (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, and Durability).
- Replication is the process of copying data and storing it in different locations, to improve availability, performance, and fault-tolerance of a distributed system.
- Transactions with replicated data involve executing transactions on multiple copies of the same data item, and ensuring that the copies remain consistent and synchronized with each other.
- There are two main approaches to transactions with replicated data: primary-copy and update-everywhere.
  - Primary-copy: Each data item has a designated primary copy, and all transactions that update that item must access the primary copy. The primary copy is responsible for propagating the updates to the other copies (replicas). This approach simplifies concurrency control and recovery, but introduces a single point of failure and a potential bottleneck for each data item.
  - Update-everywhere: Transactions can update any copy of a data item, and the updates are propagated to the other copies asynchronously or synchronously. This approach improves availability and performance, but complicates concurrency control and recovery, and may cause conflicts and inconsistencies among the copies.
- To ensure consistency and correctness of transactions with replicated data, some techniques are:
  - Quorum-based protocols: Transactions must obtain a quorum (a majority or a minimum number) of copies to read or write a data item. This ensures that at least one copy is up-to-date and that conflicting updates are detected and resolved.
  - Serializability: Transactions must execute in a way that is equivalent to some serial order of all transactions. This ensures that transactions do not interfere with each other and that the final result is the same as if they executed one at a time on a single copy of the data.
  - Two-phase commit: Transactions must go through two phases to commit: a prepare phase, where they ask all the copies involved to vote on whether they can commit, and a commit phase, where they inform all the copies of the final decision. This ensures that all the copies agree on the outcome of the transaction and that the transaction is durable.
