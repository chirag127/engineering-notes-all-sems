### Classification of Agreement Problem

An agreement problem is a problem where a set of processes in a distributed system need to reach a common decision based on their local inputs and messages exchanged with each other. Agreement problems are fundamental for achieving coordination, consistency, and fault tolerance in distributed systems. There are different types of agreement problems, depending on the assumptions and requirements of the system. Some of the well-known agreement problems are:

- **Byzantine agreement problem**: A single value, which is to be agreed on, is initialized by an arbitrary process and all non-faulty processes have to agree on that value. The system may have faulty processes that behave arbitrarily and may send conflicting or misleading messages to other processes. The goal is to ensure that all non-faulty processes agree on the same value, and that value is the initial value of some non-faulty process. This problem is also known as the **reliable broadcast problem** or the **oral messages problem**  .

- **Consensus problem**: Each process has an initial value, which is to be agreed on, and proposes it to other processes. All non-faulty processes have to agree on a single value, which must be one of the proposed values. The system may have faulty processes that crash and stop sending or receiving messages. The goal is to ensure that all non-faulty processes agree on the same value, and that value is one of the initial values of some non-faulty process. This problem is also known as the **atomic commitment problem** or the **written messages problem**  .

- **Interactive consistency problem**: Each process has an initial value, which is to be agreed on, and sends it to other processes. All non-faulty processes have to agree on a vector of values, one for each process, such that the value for each process is either its initial value or the default value. The system may have faulty processes that behave arbitrarily and may send conflicting or misleading messages to other processes. The goal is to ensure that all non-faulty processes agree on the same vector of values, and that vector satisfies the validity condition. This problem is also known as the **generalized Byzantine agreement problem** or the **Byzantine generals problem**  .

These problems are related to each other and can be solved using similar techniques, such as message passing, voting, quorums, and cryptography. However, they also have different levels of difficulty and impossibility results, depending on the number and type of faults, the synchrony and reliability of the communication, and the determinism and computability of the algorithms   .
