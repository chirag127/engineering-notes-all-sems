### Examples of Distributed Systems

A distributed system is a collection of independent computers that communicate and coordinate their actions by passing messages. The computers in a distributed system may be physically close or geographically dispersed, and they may be connected by different types of networks. Some examples of distributed systems are:

- **Telecommunication networks**: Telephone networks are an early example of a peer-to-peer network, where each node can initiate or receive calls. Cellular and telephone networks are forms and examples of distributed networks, where the nodes are mobile phones, base stations, switches, and routers .
- **Real-time systems**: Many industries use real-time systems distributed in various areas, locally and globally. For example, air traffic control systems, power grid systems, industrial control systems, and online gaming systems are all examples of real-time distributed systems, where the nodes are sensors, actuators, controllers, and servers .
- **Distributed database systems**: A distributed database has locations across multiple servers, physical locations, or both. The data may be replicated or partitioned, and the system may use different consistency models to ensure data integrity and availability. For example, Google's Bigtable, Amazon's Dynamo, and MongoDB are examples of distributed database systems, where the nodes are database servers, storage nodes, and clients .
- **Distributed computing systems**: A distributed computing system is a system that uses the idle resources of many computers to perform a large-scale computation or task. The system may use a centralized or decentralized architecture, and the nodes may be homogeneous or heterogeneous. For example, SETI@home, Folding@home, and Bitcoin are examples of distributed computing systems, where the nodes are volunteers' computers, servers, and miners .
- **Content delivery networks**: A content delivery network (CDN) is a system that distributes web content to users based on their geographic location, network conditions, and content origin. The system may use caching, load balancing, and routing techniques to improve the performance and availability of web services. For example, Akamai, Cloudflare, and Netflix are examples of content delivery networks, where the nodes are edge servers, origin servers, and clients .
