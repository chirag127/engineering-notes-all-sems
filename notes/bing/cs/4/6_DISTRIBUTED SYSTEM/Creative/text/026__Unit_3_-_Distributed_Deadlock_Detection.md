## Unit 3 - Distributed Deadlock Detection

- A deadlock is a condition where a set of processes request resources that are held by other processes in the set.
- Deadlocks can be handled by three strategies: deadlock prevention, deadlock avoidance, and deadlock detection.
- Deadlock prevention and avoidance are impractical in distributed systems, as they require global knowledge and coordination of the system.
- Deadlock detection is the best approach to handle deadlocks in distributed systems. It involves two steps: detecting the existence of deadlocks and resolving the detected deadlocks.
- Deadlock detection requires examining the status of the process-resource interactions for the presence of cyclic wait.
- There are three approaches to deadlock detection in distributed systems: centralized, distributed, and hierarchical.
- In the centralized approach, one node is designated as the deadlock detector and collects information from all other nodes about their resource allocation and requests. This node runs a deadlock detection algorithm on the global wait-for graph and initiates the resolution process if a deadlock is found. The advantages of this approach are simplicity and low message overhead, but the disadvantages are single point of failure and scalability issues.
- In the distributed approach, different nodes work together to detect deadlocks without a central coordinator. Each node maintains a local wait-for graph and exchanges information with other nodes about their resource allocation and requests. A deadlock detection algorithm is executed on the local graphs and the merged graphs to detect cycles. The advantages of this approach are fault tolerance and scalability, but the disadvantages are high message overhead and complexity.
- In the hierarchical approach, the nodes are organized into a tree structure and each node acts as a coordinator for its subtree. Each node maintains a local wait-for graph and a summary wait-for graph that represents the deadlock information of its subtree. The nodes exchange information with their parent and children nodes about their resource allocation and requests. A deadlock detection algorithm is executed on the local graphs and the summary graphs to detect cycles. The advantages of this approach are reduced message overhead and balanced workload, but the disadvantages are dependency on the tree structure and possible false deadlocks.
