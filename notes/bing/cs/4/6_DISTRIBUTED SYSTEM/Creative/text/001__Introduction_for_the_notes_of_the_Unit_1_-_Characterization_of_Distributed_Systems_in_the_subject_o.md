### Introduction

A distributed system is a system that consists of multiple computers or devices that are connected by a network and communicate with each other by passing messages. The computers or devices in a distributed system are called nodes or processes. The nodes or processes can be located at different physical locations and can have different hardware and software configurations. The main goal of a distributed system is to provide a single coherent view of the system to the users or applications, regardless of the underlying heterogeneity and distribution of the nodes or processes.

Some examples of distributed systems are:

- The Internet, which is a network of networks that connects millions of computers and devices across the world.
- The World Wide Web, which is a collection of web pages and resources that are hosted by different web servers and accessed by web browsers.
- Cloud computing, which is a model of providing computing resources and services on demand over the Internet.
- Peer-to-peer networks, which are networks of nodes that share resources and data without a central authority or server.
- Distributed databases, which are databases that are stored and managed by multiple nodes or processes that cooperate to provide a consistent and reliable view of the data.

Some of the main characteristics of distributed systems are:

- Scalability: The ability of a distributed system to handle increased workload or demand by adding more nodes or processes, without degrading the performance or functionality of the system.
- Reliability: The probability that a distributed system will function correctly and deliver the expected results, despite the failures or errors of some of its nodes or processes.
- Availability: The percentage of time that a distributed system is operational and able to provide its services or functions, despite the failures or errors of some of its nodes or processes.
- Efficiency: The measure of how well a distributed system utilizes its resources and minimizes its overhead, such as communication, synchronization, or coordination costs.
- Manageability: The ease of administering, monitoring, and maintaining a distributed system, such as configuring, updating, or repairing its nodes or processes.

Some of the main challenges of distributed systems are:

- Concurrency: The situation where multiple nodes or processes in a distributed system execute simultaneously and access or modify shared resources or data, which may lead to inconsistencies or conflicts.
- Coordination: The process of ensuring that the nodes or processes in a distributed system cooperate and synchronize their actions and states, which may require communication, agreement, or consensus protocols.
- Fault tolerance: The ability of a distributed system to continue functioning correctly and deliver the expected results, despite the failures or errors of some of its nodes or processes, which may require detection, recovery, or replication mechanisms.
- Security: The protection of a distributed system from unauthorized access, modification, or damage, which may require authentication, authorization, encryption, or auditing techniques.
