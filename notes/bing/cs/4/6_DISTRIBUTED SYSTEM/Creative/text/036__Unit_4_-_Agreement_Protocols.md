## Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols

- Agreement protocols are a class of distributed algorithms that allow a set of processes to reach a consensus on a common value, despite the possibility of failures or malicious behavior.
- Agreement protocols are useful for implementing fault-tolerant and secure distributed systems, such as distributed databases, replicated state machines, blockchain networks, and Byzantine fault-tolerant systems.
- Agreement protocols can be classified into different types based on the assumptions they make about the system model, such as the number and type of failures, the communication model, the synchrony assumptions, and the adversary model.
- Some of the common types of agreement protocols are:
  - **Consensus**: The processes have to agree on a single value proposed by one of them.
  - **Atomic broadcast**: The processes have to deliver the same set of messages in the same order.
  - **Byzantine agreement**: The processes have to agree on a single value proposed by one of them, even if some of them are faulty or malicious.
  - **Interactive consistency**: The processes have to agree on the values of all the processes, even if some of them are faulty or malicious.
  - **Leader election**: The processes have to elect a unique leader among them.
- Some of the common challenges and trade-offs in designing agreement protocols are:
  - **Fault tolerance**: The protocol should be able to tolerate a certain number of failures and still reach an agreement.
  - **Safety**: The protocol should ensure that the agreement is correct and consistent, i.e., the processes agree on a valid value and do not disagree.
  - **Liveness**: The protocol should ensure that the agreement is eventually reached, i.e., the processes do not get stuck or starve.
  - **Complexity**: The protocol should minimize the time, space, and communication costs required to reach an agreement.
  - **Security**: The protocol should be able to resist attacks from malicious processes or external adversaries, such as denial-of-service, impersonation, or corruption.
- Some of the common techniques and principles used in designing agreement protocols are:
  - **Quorums**: A quorum is a subset of processes that is large enough to ensure the agreement. For example, a majority quorum is a quorum that contains more than half of the processes. Quorums can be used to coordinate the processes and ensure consistency and fault tolerance.
  - **Broadcasts**: A broadcast is a communication primitive that allows a process to send a message to all the other processes. Broadcasts can be used to disseminate information and proposals among the processes. There are different types of broadcasts, such as reliable, ordered, or authenticated, that provide different guarantees and properties.
  - **Round-based protocols**: A round-based protocol is a protocol that proceeds in a sequence of rounds, where each round consists of a set of actions or messages exchanged by the processes. Round-based protocols can be used to synchronize the processes and ensure progress and termination.
  - **Randomization**: A randomization technique is a technique that uses random choices or values to break symmetry or avoid conflicts among the processes. Randomization can be used to improve the performance or the resilience of the protocol. For example, a randomized leader election protocol can elect a leader in expected constant time, while a deterministic protocol may require linear time.
