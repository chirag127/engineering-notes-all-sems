### Limitation of Distributed system for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

A distributed system is a collection of independent computers that communicate and cooperate with each other to achieve a common goal. Distributed systems have many advantages, such as scalability, fault tolerance, resource sharing, and performance. However, they also have some limitations that make them challenging to design, implement, and manage. Some of these limitations are:

- **Network issues**: Distributed systems rely on the network to exchange messages and data among the computers. However, the network can be unreliable, slow, congested, or even partitioned. These network issues can cause delays, errors, inconsistencies, or failures in the distributed system. For example, a message sent by one computer may be lost, duplicated, corrupted, or delivered out of order by the network. A network partition may isolate some computers from the rest of the system, making them unable to communicate or coordinate. To cope with these network issues, distributed systems need to use protocols and algorithms that can handle network faults, ensure reliable and ordered communication, and achieve consensus among the computers.

- **Concurrency**: Distributed systems involve multiple computers that execute concurrently and independently. This means that they can have different states, views, and actions at any given time. Concurrency can lead to conflicts, inconsistencies, or deadlocks in the distributed system. For example, two computers may try to access or modify the same shared resource at the same time, resulting in a race condition or a data inconsistency. A computer may wait for a message or a resource from another computer that is busy or unavailable, resulting in a deadlock or a livelock. To cope with concurrency, distributed systems need to use synchronization mechanisms, such as locks, semaphores, or atomic operations, to coordinate the access and modification of shared resources. They also need to use consistency models, such as sequential, causal, or eventual consistency, to define the expected behavior and guarantees of the distributed system.

- **Security**: Distributed systems involve multiple computers that may belong to different organizations, domains, or users. This means that they can have different levels of trust, authority, and access rights. Security is a major concern in distributed systems, as they can be vulnerable to various attacks, such as eavesdropping, spoofing, tampering, or denial of service. For example, an attacker may intercept, modify, or forge a message sent by one computer to another. An attacker may impersonate a legitimate computer or user to gain unauthorized access or privileges. An attacker may overload or disrupt a computer or the network to prevent it from functioning properly. To cope with security, distributed systems need to use cryptographic techniques, such as encryption, authentication, digital signatures, or certificates, to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity of the messages and data. They also need to use access control mechanisms, such as passwords, tokens, or firewalls, to restrict the access and privileges of the computers and users.
