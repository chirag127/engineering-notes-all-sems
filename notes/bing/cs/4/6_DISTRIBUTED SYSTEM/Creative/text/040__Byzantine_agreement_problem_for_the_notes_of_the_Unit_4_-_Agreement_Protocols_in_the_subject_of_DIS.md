### Byzantine agreement problem

- The Byzantine agreement problem is a fundamental challenge in fault-tolerant distributed computing, where a set of processes need to agree on a common value despite the presence of some faulty or malicious processes.
- The problem was first defined by Lamport et al. in 1982, inspired by a hypothetical scenario of several divisions of the Byzantine army camped outside an enemy city, each commanded by its own general . The generals can communicate with one another only by sending messages, and they must decide whether to attack or retreat. However, some of the generals may be traitors who try to prevent the loyal generals from reaching a consensus.
- The Byzantine agreement problem requires that every non-faulty process must agree on the same value, and that value must be the initial value of some non-faulty process. This is also known as interactive consistency or total agreement.
- The Byzantine agreement problem is impossible to solve deterministically in a synchronous system with n processes if more than ⌊ (n − 1) / 3 ⌋ processes are faulty . This is because any message sent by a faulty process can be arbitrarily corrupted, and any message received by a faulty process can be arbitrarily ignored or altered. Therefore, the faulty processes can create confusion and inconsistency among the non-faulty processes.
- The Byzantine agreement problem can be solved probabilistically in a synchronous system with n processes if less than n / 3 processes are faulty, using randomized algorithms that rely on coin tossing or cryptography. These algorithms can achieve agreement with high probability, but not with certainty.
- The Byzantine agreement problem can also be solved deterministically in an asynchronous system with n processes if less than n / 3 processes are faulty, using digital signatures or other cryptographic techniques. These techniques can ensure the authenticity and integrity of the messages, and prevent the faulty processes from forging or tampering with them.
- The Byzantine agreement problem is relevant for many applications in distributed systems, such as consensus protocols, fault-tolerant replication, distributed databases, blockchain, and peer-to-peer networks . Solving the Byzantine agreement problem can ensure the reliability, consistency, and security of these systems.
