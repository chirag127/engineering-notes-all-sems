## Unit 5 - Distributed Resource Management

- Distributed resource management (DRM) is an evolving discipline consisting of a set of software, hardware, network tools, procedures and policies for enabling distributed enterprise systems to operate effectively in production.
- DRM aims to optimize the utilization, performance, availability, and security of distributed resources, such as servers, storage, networks, applications, and data, across multiple locations and domains.
- DRM can be applied to various types of distributed systems, such as cloud computing, grid computing, edge computing, and distributed energy resources (DERs).
- Some of the benefits of DRM are:
  - Improved scalability and elasticity of distributed systems by dynamically allocating and deallocating resources according to the changing workload and demand.
  - Enhanced reliability and resilience of distributed systems by detecting and recovering from failures, faults, and attacks, and by providing backup and redundancy mechanisms.
  - Reduced operational costs and energy consumption of distributed systems by minimizing resource wastage and overprovisioning, and by implementing energy-efficient policies and algorithms.
  - Increased user satisfaction and quality of service (QoS) of distributed systems by meeting the service level agreements (SLAs), performance metrics, and user preferences and expectations.
- Some of the challenges of DRM are:
  - Complexity and heterogeneity of distributed systems, which involve multiple types of resources, platforms, protocols, and standards, and require interoperability and compatibility among them.
  - Uncertainty and dynamism of distributed systems, which are subject to unpredictable and fluctuating workload, demand, availability, and behavior, and require adaptability and flexibility to cope with them.
  - Security and privacy of distributed systems, which are exposed to various threats and risks, such as unauthorized access, data leakage, malicious attacks, and cyberattacks, and require protection and mitigation measures.
  - Coordination and collaboration of distributed systems, which involve multiple stakeholders, such as users, providers, brokers, and regulators, and require communication and negotiation among them.
- Some of the examples of DRM software are:
  - Altair Grid Engine, which is a leading distributed resource management system for optimizing workloads and resources in thousands of data centers, improving performance and boosting productivity and efficiency.
  - Veritone DERMS, which is a distributed energy resource management system that allows real-time communication and control across the batteries, solar panels, and other edge devices that normally lie behind-the-meter and outside grid operators’ direct control.
  - Awesense DERMS, which is a distributed energy resource management system that controls and coordinates a variety of distributed energy resources, such as solar, wind, and battery storage, and allows for better management of the grid and less electricity wastage.
