### System models for the notes of the Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- A system model is a set of assumptions and properties that describe the characteristics and behavior of a distributed system.
- A system model can include aspects such as communication, synchronization, failure, security, and performance.
- A system model can help to design, analyze, and implement distributed algorithms and protocols.
- Agreement protocols are a class of distributed algorithms that aim to achieve a common goal or decision among a set of processes in a network, despite the presence of failures or faults.
- Agreement protocols are essential for ensuring reliability, consistency, and fault tolerance in distributed systems.
- Agreement protocols can be classified into different types, such as:
  - Byzantine agreement: The processes have to agree on a single value, even if some of them are faulty and may behave arbitrarily or maliciously.
  - Consensus: The processes have to agree on a single value, which is proposed by one or more of them, even if some of them may fail or crash.
  - Interactive consistency: The processes have to agree on a vector of values, where each value corresponds to the initial state of a process, even if some of them are faulty and may lie about their states.
  - Leader election: The processes have to agree on a single process, which acts as a coordinator or a leader, even if some of them may fail or crash.
- Agreement protocols can be designed for different system models, depending on the assumptions and properties of the network, the communication, the processes, and the failures.
- Some of the common system models for agreement protocols are:
  - Synchronous system: The system has bounded delays for message transmission, message processing, and clock drifts. The processes can use timeouts and clocks to synchronize and detect failures.
  - Asynchronous system: The system has no bounds on the delays for message transmission, message processing, and clock drifts. The processes cannot use timeouts and clocks to synchronize and detect failures.
  - Partially synchronous system: The system is asynchronous for some unknown period of time, and then becomes synchronous. The processes have to cope with the uncertainty and the transition of the system behavior.
  - Reliable communication: The communication medium is error-free and delivers all messages correctly and in order.
  - Unreliable communication: The communication medium may lose, duplicate, reorder, or corrupt messages.
  - Fully connected: The processes can communicate with each other directly, without intermediaries.
  - Partially connected: The processes can communicate with each other only through some paths or neighbors, forming a network topology.
  - Crash failure: A faulty process stops executing and does not send or receive any messages.
  - Omission failure: A faulty process may fail to send or receive some messages, but otherwise behaves correctly.
  - Byzantine failure: A faulty process may behave arbitrarily or maliciously, sending or receiving incorrect, inconsistent, or conflicting messages.
