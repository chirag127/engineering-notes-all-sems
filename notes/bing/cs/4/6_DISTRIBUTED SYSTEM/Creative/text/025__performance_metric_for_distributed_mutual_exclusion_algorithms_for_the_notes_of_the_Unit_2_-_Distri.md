### Performance metric for distributed mutual exclusion algorithms

Distributed mutual exclusion algorithms are algorithms that ensure that only one process can access a shared resource or execute a critical section at a time in a distributed system. The performance of these algorithms can be evaluated by the following metrics :

- **Message complexity**: It is the number of messages that are required per critical section execution by a process. It indicates the communication overhead and network congestion caused by the algorithm. The lower the message complexity, the better the performance.
- **Synchronization delay**: It is the time elapsed between the moment a process leaves the critical section and the moment the next process enters the critical section. It indicates the degree of concurrency and fairness achieved by the algorithm. The lower the synchronization delay, the better the performance.
- **Response time**: It is the time elapsed between the moment a process requests to enter the critical section and the moment it actually enters the critical section. It indicates the waiting time and the service quality experienced by the process. The lower the response time, the better the performance.
- **Fault tolerance**: It is the ability of the algorithm to handle failures of processes or messages in the system. It indicates the reliability and robustness of the algorithm. The higher the fault tolerance, the better the performance.

Different algorithms may have different trade-offs among these metrics, depending on the design choices and assumptions they make. For example, some algorithms may use a central coordinator or a unique token to grant access to the critical section, while others may use a distributed voting or a logical clock mechanism. Some algorithms may require all processes to be aware of each other, while others may allow dynamic joining and leaving of processes. Some algorithms may guarantee mutual exclusion and deadlock-freedom, while others may also ensure starvation-freedom and ordering of requests. Therefore, it is important to compare and contrast different algorithms based on these metrics and the system requirements .
