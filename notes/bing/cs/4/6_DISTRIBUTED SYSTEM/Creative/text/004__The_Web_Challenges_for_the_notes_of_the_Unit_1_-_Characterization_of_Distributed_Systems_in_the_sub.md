### The Web Challenges for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- A distributed system is a collection of independent computers that appear to the users as a single coherent system.
- The web is an example of a distributed system that enables resource sharing and communication among millions of users and devices.
- The web challenges are the problems and difficulties that arise from the design, implementation and operation of distributed systems.
- Some of the major web challenges are  :

  - **Scalability**: The ability of a distributed system to handle increasing load and users without degrading the performance or functionality of the system. Scalability requires controlling the cost of physical and software resources, avoiding performance bottlenecks, and adapting to changes in the system size and demand.
  - **Heterogeneity**: The diversity and compatibility of the components of a distributed system, such as hardware, software, network, data, protocols, and users. Heterogeneity requires dealing with different platforms, languages, formats, standards, and interfaces, and enabling interoperability and integration among them.
  - **Openness**: The extent to which a distributed system can be extended, modified, or replaced by new components, without affecting the existing functionality or performance of the system. Openness requires following well-defined and widely accepted standards, interfaces, and protocols, and supporting modularity, reusability, and portability of components.
  - **Security**: The protection of a distributed system and its resources from unauthorized access, modification, or damage, by malicious or accidental actions. Security requires ensuring confidentiality, integrity, availability, and accountability of the system, and applying various techniques such as encryption, authentication, authorization, and auditing.
  - **Failure handling**: The detection, recovery, and tolerance of faults and errors that occur in a distributed system, due to hardware, software, network, or human failures. Failure handling requires designing reliable, resilient, and robust systems that can cope with partial failures, network partitions, and inconsistent states, and provide graceful degradation and fault masking.
  - **Concurrency**: The simultaneous execution and coordination of multiple processes or threads in a distributed system, that may access or modify shared resources or communicate with each other. Concurrency requires managing synchronization, consistency, and deadlock issues, and providing concurrency control mechanisms such as locks, transactions, and replication.
  - **Transparency**: The degree to which a distributed system hides its complexity and heterogeneity from the users and applications, and provides a consistent and uniform view of the system. Transparency requires masking various aspects of the system such as location, migration, replication, concurrency, failure, and access, and providing abstraction and simplicity to the users.
  - **Quality of service**: The level of performance, reliability, availability, and usability that a distributed system can provide to its users and applications, according to their requirements and expectations. Quality of service requires measuring, monitoring, and controlling various parameters of the system such as response time, throughput, bandwidth, latency, jitter, and error rate, and providing service level agreements and guarantees.
