### Total causal order for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Total causal order is a property of message delivery in distributed systems that ensures that all messages are delivered in the same order to all processes, regardless of their causal dependencies or concurrency.
- Total causal order is the strictest ordering in distributed systems; it establishes only one linearization, consistent with the causal ordering, among all the events that occur in the system, even those that occur concurrently. For that reason, the execution of the system is considered as synchronous.
- Total causal order can be achieved by using a total order broadcast protocol, which assigns a unique identifier to each message and delivers them according to a predefined order relation. Some examples of total order broadcast protocols are Lamport's logical clocks, vector clocks, and consensus algorithms .
- Total causal order has some advantages and disadvantages for distributed systems. Some of the advantages are :
  - It simplifies the design of distributed algorithms that require global consistency and agreement, such as distributed databases, replicated state machines, and fault-tolerant services.
  - It enables deterministic and reproducible execution of distributed systems, which facilitates debugging and testing.
  - It preserves the causal dependencies among messages, which is useful for maintaining the consistency of data and the correctness of operations.
- Some of the disadvantages are :
  - It imposes a high overhead on the communication and computation of the system, as each message needs to be assigned a unique identifier and delivered according to a global order, which may require additional messages and synchronization among processes.
  - It reduces the concurrency and scalability of the system, as each process has to wait for the delivery of all previous messages before delivering the next one, which may cause delays and bottlenecks.
  - It may violate the natural order of events in the system, as some messages that are not causally related may be delivered out of their original order, which may confuse the users or the applications.

- A mnemonic to remember the definition of total causal order is: **T**otal **C**ausal **O**rder = **T**he **C**omplete **O**rdering of messages in distributed systems.
- A learning trick to understand the concept of total causal order is to imagine a distributed system as a classroom of students who are sending messages to each other. If the teacher wants to ensure that all messages are delivered in the same order to all students, he or she can use a total order broadcast protocol, such as assigning a sequence number to each message and delivering them in ascending order of the sequence numbers. This way, the teacher can guarantee that all students receive the same messages in the same order, regardless of who sent them or when they were sent. However, this may also cause some problems, such as some messages being delayed or delivered out of their natural order, which may affect the communication and collaboration among the students.
