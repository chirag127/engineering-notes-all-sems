### Classification of distributed mutual exclusion for the notes of the Unit 2 - Distributed Mutual Exclusion in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Distributed mutual exclusion (DME) is the problem of ensuring that only one process in a distributed system can execute a critical section (CS) at any given time, using only message passing as the communication mechanism  .
- DME algorithms can be classified into three basic approaches :
  - Token-based approach: A unique token is shared among the sites. A site is allowed to enter its CS if it possesses the token. Mutual exclusion is ensured because the token is unique. Examples of token-based algorithms are Suzuki-Kasami's algorithm, Raymond's algorithm, and Maekawa's algorithm .
  - Non-token-based approach: A site requests permission from other sites to enter its CS. A site is allowed to enter its CS if it receives permission from all or a majority of the sites. Mutual exclusion is ensured by the voting mechanism. Examples of non-token-based algorithms are Ricart-Agrawala's algorithm, Lamport's algorithm, and Singhal's algorithm  .
  - Quorum-based approach: A site requests permission from a subset of sites (called a quorum) to enter its CS. A site is allowed to enter its CS if it receives permission from all the sites in the quorum. Mutual exclusion is ensured by the intersection property of quorums. Examples of quorum-based algorithms are S. Ghosh's algorithm, Agrawal-El Abbadi's algorithm, and Thomas's algorithm  .
- A possible mnemonic to remember the three approaches is **T**oken, **N**on-token, and **Q**uorum, or **TNQ**. A possible learning trick to remember the examples of each approach is to associate the first letter of each algorithm with the approach, such as **S**uzuki-Kasami, **R**aymond, and **M**aekawa for token-based; **R**icart-Agrawala, **L**amport, and **S**inghal for non-token-based; and **S**. Ghosh, **A**grawal-El Abbadi, and **T**homas for quorum-based. Alternatively, one can use acronyms such as **SRM**, **RLS**, and **SAT** to remember the examples of each approach.
