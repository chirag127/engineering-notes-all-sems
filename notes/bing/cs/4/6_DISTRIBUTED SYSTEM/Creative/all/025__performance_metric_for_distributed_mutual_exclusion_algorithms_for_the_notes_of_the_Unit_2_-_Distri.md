### Performance metric for distributed mutual exclusion algorithms for the notes of the Unit 2 - Distributed Mutual Exclusion in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Distributed mutual exclusion algorithms are algorithms that allow multiple processes in a distributed system to access a shared resource or a critical section without violating the mutual exclusion property, i.e., at most one process can be in the critical section at any time.
- The performance of distributed mutual exclusion algorithms is generally measured by the following four metrics :
  - **Message complexity**: It is the number of messages that are required per critical section execution by a process. It reflects the communication overhead and network bandwidth consumption of the algorithm. A lower message complexity is desirable.
  - **Synchronization delay**: After a process leaves the critical section, it is the time required before the next process enters the critical section. It reflects the degree of concurrency and fairness of the algorithm. A lower synchronization delay is desirable.
  - **Response time**: It is the time interval between when a process requests to enter the critical section and when it actually enters the critical section. It reflects the waiting time and latency of the algorithm. A lower response time is desirable.
  - **Throughput**: It is the number of critical section executions per unit time in the system. It reflects the efficiency and utilization of the shared resource. A higher throughput is desirable.
- Different types of distributed mutual exclusion algorithms have different trade-offs among these metrics, depending on the underlying assumptions, design choices, and implementation details. For example, centralized algorithms have low synchronization delay and response time, but high message complexity and single point of failure. Token-based algorithms have low message complexity and no single point of failure, but high synchronization delay and response time. Quorum-based algorithms have low message complexity and synchronization delay, but high response time and quorum maintenance overhead.  
- A simulation-based approach can be used to compare the performance of different distributed mutual exclusion algorithms under various scenarios and parameters, such as network size, network topology, network delay, request rate, critical section duration, etc. A simulation-based approach can provide empirical results and statistical analysis of the performance metrics, as well as insights into the behavior and characteristics of the algorithms.  
- A possible mnemonic to remember the four performance metrics is **MSTR** (Message complexity, Synchronization delay, Response time, Throughput), which sounds like **master**, as in mastering the distributed mutual exclusion problem. Alternatively, one can use **RSTM** (Response time, Synchronization delay, Throughput, Message complexity), which sounds like **rest** or **restroom**, as in resting or using the restroom while waiting for the critical section.
