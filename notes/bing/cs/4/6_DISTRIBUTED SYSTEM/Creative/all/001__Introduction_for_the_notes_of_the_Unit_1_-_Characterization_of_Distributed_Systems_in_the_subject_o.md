### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

A distributed system is a system that consists of multiple independent computers that communicate and coordinate their actions only by passing messages over a network. The computers in a distributed system may have different hardware, software, and operating systems, but they appear to the users and applications as a single coherent system. 

Some examples of distributed systems are:

- The Internet: a global network of interconnected computers and devices that exchange information and services.
- Cloud computing: a model of providing on-demand access to shared computing resources (such as servers, storage, databases, etc.) over the Internet.
- Peer-to-peer networks: a type of network where each node can act as both a client and a server, and share resources and data with other nodes without a central authority.
- Distributed databases: a type of database where the data is stored and accessed across multiple servers or locations, and can be replicated, partitioned, or distributed for performance, availability, or scalability reasons.

Some of the main characteristics of distributed systems are :

- Scalability: the ability of a system to handle increased workload or demand by adding more resources or nodes, without degrading the performance or quality of service.
- Reliability: the probability that a system will function correctly and deliver the expected results, without failures or errors, in a given period of time.
- Availability: the proportion of time that a system is operational and ready to perform its required functions, in a specific period of time.
- Efficiency: the ratio of the useful output of a system to the input or resources consumed by the system, such as time, space, energy, etc.
- Manageability: the ease and cost of operating, maintaining, and monitoring a system, such as configuration, deployment, troubleshooting, security, etc.

Some of the main challenges of distributed systems are:

- Concurrency: the situation where multiple components or processes execute simultaneously and may access or modify shared resources or data, which can lead to inconsistency, deadlock, or race conditions.
- Coordination: the process of synchronizing and ordering the actions and interactions of multiple components or processes, which can be difficult due to the lack of a global clock or a common state in a distributed system.
- Fault tolerance: the ability of a system to continue functioning correctly or gracefully degrade in the presence of failures or errors, such as hardware failures, network failures, software bugs, malicious attacks, etc.
- Security: the protection of a system and its data from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure, which can be challenging due to the heterogeneity, openness, and complexity of a distributed system.

A mnemonic to remember the characteristics of distributed systems is **SRAEM** (Scalability, Reliability, Availability, Efficiency, Manageability).

A mnemonic to remember the challenges of distributed systems is **CCFS** (Concurrency, Coordination, Fault tolerance, Security).
