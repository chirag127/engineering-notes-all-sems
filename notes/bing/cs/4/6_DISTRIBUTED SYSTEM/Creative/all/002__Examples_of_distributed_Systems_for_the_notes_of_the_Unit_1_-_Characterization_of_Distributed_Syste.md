### Examples of distributed Systems for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- A distributed system is a collection of independent computers that appear to the users as a single coherent system.
- Distributed systems are used to achieve scalability, reliability, availability, and performance goals that are difficult or impossible to achieve with a single computer.
- Some examples of distributed systems are:

  - **Telecommunication networks**: Telephone networks are an early example of a peer-to-peer network. Cellular and telephone networks are forms and examples of distributed networks . They allow users to communicate with each other over long distances using voice, text, or data. They consist of switches, routers, base stations, satellites, and other devices that connect and route calls and messages.
  - **Real-time systems**: Many industries use real-time systems distributed in various areas, locally and globally. For example, air traffic control systems, power grid systems, industrial control systems, and autonomous vehicles are all examples of real-time distributed systems. They require timely and accurate responses to events and changes in the environment. They often have strict deadlines and safety requirements.
  - **Distributed database systems**: A distributed database has locations across multiple servers, physical locations, or both. For example, Google's Bigtable, Amazon's DynamoDB, and MongoDB are all examples of distributed database systems. They allow users to store, query, and update large amounts of data across different nodes. They often provide features such as replication, partitioning, consistency, and fault tolerance.
  - **Content delivery networks**: A content delivery network (CDN) is a system that delivers web content to users based on their geographic location, the origin of the content, and the content delivery server. For example, Netflix, YouTube, and Akamai are all examples of content delivery networks. They improve the performance, availability, and security of web applications by caching and distributing content across multiple servers and locations.
  - **Distributed computing platforms**: A distributed computing platform is a system that enables parallel or distributed execution of applications or tasks on multiple computers. For example, Apache Hadoop, Apache Spark, and Google's MapReduce are all examples of distributed computing platforms. They allow users to process large amounts of data using frameworks such as batch processing, stream processing, or machine learning. They often provide features such as scheduling, resource management, fault tolerance, and data distribution.

- A mnemonic to remember some of the examples of distributed systems is **TRACD** (Telecommunication, Real-time, Database, Content delivery, Computing platform).
