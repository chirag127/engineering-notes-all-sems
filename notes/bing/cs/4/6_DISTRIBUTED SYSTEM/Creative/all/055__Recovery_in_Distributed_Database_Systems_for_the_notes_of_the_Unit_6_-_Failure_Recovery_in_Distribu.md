### Recovery in Distributed Database Systems

Recovery in distributed database systems is the process of restoring the database to a consistent and correct state after a failure. A failure can be a soft failure, such as a transaction abort, or a hard failure, such as a site crash or a network partition. Recovery in distributed database systems aims to maintain the atomicity and durability of distributed transactions, which are transactions that span multiple sites or nodes in the system. A database must guarantee that all statements in a transaction, distributed or non-distributed, either commit or roll back as a unit.

Some of the challenges and techniques for recovery in distributed database systems are:

- **Distributed commit protocols**: These are protocols that ensure that all sites involved in a distributed transaction agree on whether to commit or abort the transaction. The most common protocol is the two-phase commit (2PC) protocol, which consists of two phases: a prepare phase, where the coordinator asks all participants to vote on whether to commit or abort, and a commit phase, where the coordinator decides based on the votes and informs all participants. The 2PC protocol requires that each participant and the coordinator maintain a log of their actions and votes, and that they can recover from failures during the protocol execution. For example, if the coordinator fails after sending the commit decision to some participants, it must resend the decision to the remaining participants after recovery. Similarly, if a participant fails after receiving the commit decision, it must redo its local transaction after recovery.

- **Distributed undo and redo**: These are techniques that allow a site to undo or redo the effects of a local transaction that was part of a distributed transaction. Undo is used to roll back a transaction that was aborted by the distributed commit protocol, or that was faulty and caused the database to be inconsistent. Redo is used to reapply the effects of a transaction that was committed by the distributed commit protocol, but that was lost due to a site failure. Undo and redo operations are based on the log records of the local transactions, which contain the before and after images of the data items that were modified. Undo and redo operations must also take into account the dependencies among the local transactions, such as read-write and write-write conflicts, and ensure that they are executed in the correct order.

- **Distributed deadlock detection and resolution**: These are techniques that prevent or resolve situations where two or more transactions are waiting for each other to release some resources, such as locks or messages, and thus cannot proceed. Deadlocks can occur in distributed database systems due to the concurrent execution of distributed transactions that access data items at different sites. Deadlock detection and resolution algorithms must be able to detect cycles of waiting transactions across the sites, and choose one or more transactions to abort in order to break the cycle. Deadlock detection and resolution algorithms can be centralized, where a single site is responsible for detecting and resolving deadlocks, or distributed, where each site participates in the detection and resolution process.

- **Distributed backup and recovery**: These are techniques that allow a site to restore its data from a backup copy after a hard failure, such as a disk crash or a power outage. Backup and recovery techniques must ensure that the backup copy is consistent with the other sites in the system, and that it reflects the effects of the committed transactions. Backup and recovery techniques can be classified into two types: physical backup and recovery, where the entire database is copied to a secondary storage device, such as a tape or a disk, and logical backup and recovery, where only the changes to the database are recorded in a backup log. Physical backup and recovery techniques are faster and simpler, but require more storage space and bandwidth. Logical backup and recovery techniques are more efficient and flexible, but require more processing and coordination.

Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for recovery in distributed database systems are:

- To remember the phases of the 2PC protocol, think of the acronym PCP: Prepare, Commit, and Persist. Persist means that the participants and the coordinator must write their votes and decisions to their logs before sending or receiving messages.
- To remember the order of undo and redo operations, think of the acronym LIFO: Last In, First Out. Undo operations are executed in the reverse order of the log records, from the last to the first. Redo operations are executed in the same order of the log records, from the first to the last.
- To remember the types of backup and recovery techniques, think of the acronym PL: Physical and Logical. Physical backup and recovery techniques copy the whole database, while logical backup and recovery techniques copy only the changes to the database.
