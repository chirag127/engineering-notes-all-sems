### Classification of Agreement Problem for the notes of the Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Agreement problem is a fundamental issue in distributed systems, where a set of processes need to reach a common decision based on their local inputs and messages exchanged with each other.
- Agreement problem can have different versions depending on the system model, the failure assumptions, the communication primitives, and the desired properties of the solution.
- Some of the well-known agreement problems are:
  - Byzantine agreement problem: A single value, which is to be agreed on, is initialized by an arbitrary processor and all nonfaulty processors have to agree on that value. The value can be either a binary bit or a general value. The problem is named after the Byzantine generals problem, where a group of generals have to coordinate an attack or retreat based on their observations of the enemy and the messages they receive from each other. Some of the generals may be traitors and send misleading messages to confuse the others. The goal is to reach a consistent decision despite the presence of traitors.
  - Consensus problem: Each processor has an initial value, which can be either a binary bit or a general value, and all nonfaulty processors have to agree on a common value, which must be one of the initial values. The consensus problem is a generalization of the Byzantine agreement problem, where the value to be agreed on is not fixed by a single processor, but can be chosen from the inputs of all processors. The consensus problem is also related to the atomic commitment problem, where a set of transactions have to be either committed or aborted in a consistent manner.
  - Interactive consistency problem: Each processor has an initial value, which can be either a binary bit or a general value, and all nonfaulty processors have to agree on a vector of values, one for each processor, such that the value at the position of a nonfaulty processor is equal to its initial value, and the value at the position of a faulty processor can be arbitrary. The interactive consistency problem is a generalization of the consensus problem, where the output is not a single value, but a vector of values that reflects the inputs of all processors. The interactive consistency problem is also related to the reliable broadcast problem, where a message sent by a processor has to be delivered to all nonfaulty processors in a consistent manner.
- Some of the factors that affect the classification and the solvability of the agreement problems are:
  - The number of processors in the system and the number of faulty processors that can be tolerated. Typically, the system needs to have more than twice the number of faulty processors to achieve agreement in the presence of Byzantine faults, and more than half the number of faulty processors to achieve agreement in the presence of crash faults.
  - The type of faults that can occur in the system, such as crash faults, omission faults, timing faults, or Byzantine faults. The type of faults determines the assumptions that can be made about the behavior of the faulty processors and the messages they send or receive. Byzantine faults are the most general and the most difficult to handle, as they allow arbitrary deviations from the protocol specification.
  - The type of communication that is available in the system, such as synchronous or asynchronous, reliable or unreliable, authenticated or unauthenticated, broadcast or point-to-point, etc. The type of communication determines the assumptions that can be made about the timing and the delivery of the messages, and the ability to detect and prevent malicious messages. Synchronous communication is easier to handle than asynchronous communication, as it allows the use of timeouts and clocks to coordinate the actions of the processors. Reliable and authenticated communication is also easier to handle than unreliable and unauthenticated communication, as it reduces the uncertainty and the ambiguity of the messages.
  - The properties that are required from the solution, such as validity, agreement, termination, integrity, etc. The properties define the correctness criteria and the guarantees that the solution has to provide. Validity ensures that the output value is related to the input values in some way. Agreement ensures that all nonfaulty processors output the same value. Termination ensures that all nonfaulty processors eventually output a value. Integrity ensures that the output value is not influenced by the faulty processors in some way. Different properties may have different definitions and implications depending on the type of the problem and the system model.
