### Limitation of Distributed system for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- A distributed system is a collection of independent computers that appear to the users as a single coherent system.
- Distributed systems have many advantages, such as scalability, fault tolerance, resource sharing, and performance.
- However, distributed systems also face many limitations and challenges, such as:

  - **Heterogeneity**: The components of a distributed system may have different hardware, software, network, data formats, and protocols. This makes it difficult to design, implement, and manage a distributed system that can interoperate seamlessly and efficiently across different platforms and environments.
  - **Transparency**: The users and applications of a distributed system should not be aware of the details and complexities of the underlying system, such as the location, replication, migration, concurrency, and failure of the components. This requires hiding the distribution aspects from the users and providing a consistent and uniform interface to access the system resources and services.
  - **Concurrency**: The components of a distributed system may operate concurrently and independently, which may lead to conflicts, inconsistencies, and deadlocks. This requires coordination and synchronization mechanisms to ensure the correctness and consistency of the system state and behavior.
  - **Security**: The components of a distributed system may be exposed to various threats and attacks, such as unauthorized access, modification, deletion, or disclosure of data and resources, denial of service, and malicious code injection. This requires security mechanisms to protect the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and authenticity of the system and its users.
  - **Scalability**: The size and complexity of a distributed system may grow rapidly, which may affect the performance, reliability, and manageability of the system. This requires designing and implementing a distributed system that can adapt to the changing demands and requirements of the users and applications, and that can cope with the increasing load and traffic without degrading the quality of service.
  - **Failure handling**: The components of a distributed system may fail unexpectedly and unpredictably, which may cause partial or complete loss of functionality and data. This requires fault tolerance and recovery mechanisms to detect, isolate, mask, and resolve the failures, and to restore the normal operation of the system as soon as possible.
  - **Network issues**: The communication between the components of a distributed system may be affected by various network issues, such as latency, bandwidth, congestion, packet loss, and disconnection. This requires designing and implementing a distributed system that can tolerate and overcome the network limitations and uncertainties, and that can provide reliable and efficient communication and data transfer.
