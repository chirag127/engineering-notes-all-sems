### Distributed deadlocks for the notes of the Unit 9 - Distributed Transactions in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- A **distributed deadlock** is a situation where a set of processes in a distributed system are waiting for each other to release resources or messages, and none of them can proceed.
- Distributed deadlocks are similar to deadlocks in centralized systems, but they are harder to detect, avoid, and prevent, because the relevant information is scattered over multiple machines  .
- There are two types of distributed deadlocks, depending on the nature of the resources involved:
  - **Communication deadlocks**: These occur when processes are waiting for messages from each other, and no message is sent or received. For example, a process P1 sends a request to P2 and waits for a reply, while P2 sends a request to P1 and waits for a reply.
  - **Resource deadlocks**: These occur when processes are waiting for resources that are held by other processes, and no resource is released. For example, a process P1 holds a resource R1 and requests R2, while P2 holds R2 and requests R1.
- There are four conditions that are necessary for a deadlock to occur in a distributed system:
  - **Mutual exclusion**: The resources involved are non-sharable, meaning that only one process can use a resource at a time.
  - **Hold and wait**: The processes involved are holding some resources while waiting for other resources.
  - **No preemption**: The resources involved cannot be forcibly taken away from the processes that are holding them.
  - **Circular wait**: The processes involved form a circular chain of waiting, meaning that each process is waiting for a resource that is held by the next process in the chain.
- There are three main strategies to handle distributed deadlocks:
  - **Deadlock prevention**: This aims to ensure that at least one of the four conditions for deadlock is never satisfied. For example, by imposing a total ordering on the resources and requiring processes to request resources in that order, the circular wait condition can be avoided.
  - **Deadlock avoidance**: This aims to ensure that the system never enters a deadlock state by carefully allocating resources to processes. For example, by using a global banker's algorithm that considers the current and future resource requests of all processes, the system can avoid unsafe states that may lead to deadlock.
  - **Deadlock detection and recovery**: This aims to detect deadlock states after they occur and take actions to resolve them. For example, by constructing a global wait-for graph from local wait-for graphs at a deadlock detector or by using a distributed algorithm like edge chasing, the system can detect cycles of waiting and terminate or restart some processes to break the cycles .
- A possible mnemonic to remember the four conditions for deadlock is **M**y **H**orse **N**ever **C**hews **W**heat, where the first letter of each word corresponds to the first letter of each condition. A possible learning trick to remember the three strategies to handle distributed deadlocks is to think of them as three ways to deal with traffic jams: prevent them by following traffic rules, avoid them by choosing alternative routes, or detect and recover from them by calling for help.
