A trapped ion quantum computer is a system that uses ions (charged atoms) suspended in a magnetic or electric field as qubits (quantum bits) for information processing. It uses ion traps, which are devices that can contain a single ion or multiple ions in order to perform controlled operations on them.

The qubits are stored in stable electronic states of each ion, and quantum information can be transferred through the collective quantized motion of the ions, which acts as a quantum bus. The qubits can be manipulated by applying laser pulses or microwave fields to the ions, which induce transitions between the electronic states. The qubits can also be entangled by applying a two-qubit gate that couples the motion of the ions with their internal states.

The ion traps can be arranged in various configurations, such as linear, planar, or three-dimensional arrays, depending on the design and scalability of the quantum computer. The ion traps can also be connected by optical fibers or free-space optics, which allow the transfer of quantum information between different traps.
