The diagram illustrates the basic differences between classical computing, quantum computing, and quantum mechanics. Classical computing is the conventional form of computation that uses bits as the basic unit of information and follows Boolean logic and algebra. Quantum computing is a form of computation that uses qubits as the basic unit of information and follows quantum logic and algebra. Quantum mechanics is the branch of physics that describes the behavior of quantum systems, such as atoms, photons, electrons, etc. Quantum computing exploits the properties of quantum mechanics, such as superposition, entanglement, and uncertainty, to perform operations using quantum gates. Quantum mechanics requires complex mathematical tools, such as linear algebra, probability theory, etc., to explain nature.
