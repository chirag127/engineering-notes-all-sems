A harmonic oscillator quantum computer is a quantum system that uses harmonic oscillators as the basic units of information. A harmonic oscillator is a particle that is subject to a restoring force that is proportional to its displacement from an equilibrium position. The quantum harmonic oscillator is the quantum analog of the classical harmonic oscillator and is one of the most important model systems in quantum mechanics.

A harmonic oscillator quantum computer can encode a qudit, which is a quantum system that can exist in d orthogonal states, in a harmonic oscillator. The qudit states are labeled by the number of energy quanta or photons in the oscillator, denoted by |n>, where n = 0, 1, 2, ..., d-1. The generalized Pauli group for a qudit is generated by the number operator N and the phase operator P, which satisfy the commutation relation [N, P] = i mod d. The number operator N acts as a shift operator on the qudit states, such that N |n> = n |n> mod d. The phase operator P acts as a cyclic permutation operator on the qudit states, such that P |n> = |n+1> mod d.

A harmonic oscillator quantum computer can perform universal quantum computation by applying unitary transformations that belong to the generalized Clifford group, which is the normalizer of the generalized Pauli group. The generalized Clifford group can be generated by the following operations:

- Displacement operator D(a), which displaces the oscillator by a complex amplitude a. It acts as D(a) |n> = exp(-|a|^2/2 + a P) |n>.
- Fourier transform operator F, which performs a discrete Fourier transform on the qudit states. It acts as F |n> = (1/sqrt(d)) sum_{k=0}^{d-1} exp(2 pi i n k / d) |k>.
- Controlled-phase operator C, which applies a phase shift to the target oscillator conditioned on the state of the control oscillator. It acts as C |n> |m> = exp(2 pi i n m / d) |n> |m>.
