### Stabilizer codes for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Stabilizer codes are a class of quantum error-correcting codes that use the stabilizer formalism to encode and manipulate quantum information.
- The stabilizer formalism is a way of describing quantum states and operations using the language of group theory and linear algebra.
- A stabilizer group is a subgroup of the Pauli group that commutes with all its elements and contains the identity operator. The Pauli group is the set of all tensor products of Pauli matrices and the identity matrix, which act on single qubits or multi-qubit systems.
- A stabilizer state is a quantum state that is a simultaneous eigenstate of all the operators in a stabilizer group, with eigenvalue +1. A stabilizer state can be written as a linear combination of the basis states that are also eigenstates of the stabilizer group, with equal coefficients.
- A stabilizer code is a subspace of the Hilbert space that is spanned by a set of stabilizer states. A stabilizer code can correct errors that are in the normalizer of the stabilizer group, which is the set of operators that commute with the stabilizer group but are not in it.
- A stabilizer code can be specified by a set of generators of the stabilizer group, which are independent and mutually commuting operators that can be multiplied together to obtain any element of the stabilizer group. A stabilizer code can also be represented by a parity check matrix, which is a binary matrix that encodes the action of the generators on the basis states.
- A stabilizer code can be encoded and decoded using quantum circuits that implement the Clifford group, which is the normalizer of the Pauli group. The Clifford group consists of quantum operations that map Pauli operators to Pauli operators, such as the Hadamard, phase, and CNOT gates. The Clifford group preserves the stabilizer formalism and can be efficiently simulated on a classical computer.
