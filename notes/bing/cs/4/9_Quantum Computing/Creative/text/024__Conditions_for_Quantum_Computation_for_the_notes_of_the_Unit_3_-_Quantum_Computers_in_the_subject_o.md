### Conditions for Quantum Computation

Quantum computation is the process of using quantum systems, such as qubits, to perform operations that are impossible or inefficient for classical computers. Qubits are quantum bits that can exist in superposition of two states, such as 0 and 1, and can be manipulated by quantum gates. Quantum computation requires certain conditions to be met, such as:

- **A scalable physical system with well-characterized qubits**: The quantum computer must have a large number of qubits that can be individually controlled and measured, and that have well-defined and predictable properties. The qubits must also be compatible with the chosen physical implementation, such as superconducting circuits, trapped ions, or photons.
- **The ability to initialize the state of the qubits to a simple fiducial state**: The quantum computer must be able to prepare the qubits in a known and simple state, such as all 0s or all 1s, before performing any computation. This ensures that the qubits are not affected by any unwanted noise or interference from the environment or previous operations.
- **Long relevant decoherence times**: The quantum computer must be able to preserve the quantum state of the qubits for a sufficiently long time, compared to the duration of the computation. Decoherence is the process of losing quantum coherence due to interaction with the environment, which causes the qubits to behave like classical bits and lose their superposition and entanglement. The decoherence time depends on the physical system and the type of noise, and must be longer than the gate time and the measurement time.
- **A "universal" set of quantum gates**: The quantum computer must be able to perform any arbitrary quantum operation on the qubits, by applying a finite sequence of elementary quantum gates. A quantum gate is a unitary transformation that changes the state of one or more qubits, such as the NOT gate, the Hadamard gate, or the CNOT gate. A universal set of quantum gates is a minimal set of gates that can approximate any quantum operation to any desired accuracy, such as the Clifford+T set or the Solovay-Kitaev set.
- **A qubit-specific measurement capability**: The quantum computer must be able to measure the state of any individual qubit or a subset of qubits, without disturbing the state of the remaining qubits. Measurement is the process of obtaining classical information from the quantum system, such as the probability of finding a qubit in 0 or 1. Measurement also collapses the quantum state of the measured qubits, which means that they cannot be used for further computation. Therefore, measurement must be performed at the end of the computation, or selectively on ancillary qubits that are not part of the output.
