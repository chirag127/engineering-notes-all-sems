### Introduction

Quantum error correction (QEC) is a technique that aims to protect quantum information from the effects of noise and errors that occur in quantum systems. Quantum information is encoded in quantum states of physical systems, such as qubits, which are subject to decoherence, measurement errors, gate errors, and other sources of noise. These errors can cause the quantum information to be corrupted or lost, which can affect the performance and reliability of quantum computing and communication.

QEC is based on the idea of redundancy, which means using more physical qubits than logical qubits to encode the quantum information. By using a suitable QEC code, the logical qubits can be protected from a certain number of errors that affect the physical qubits. QEC codes are designed to allow the detection and correction of errors without disturbing the quantum information itself. This is achieved by performing syndrome measurements, which are multi-qubit measurements that reveal information about the errors without revealing information about the logical qubits.

QEC is essential for achieving fault-tolerant quantum computing, which means that quantum algorithms can be executed with high accuracy and efficiency even in the presence of noise and errors. Fault-tolerant quantum computing requires not only QEC codes, but also fault-tolerant quantum gates, fault-tolerant quantum preparation, and fault-tolerant quantum measurements. These are techniques that allow the manipulation and measurement of logical qubits without introducing additional errors.

QEC is a rich and active field of research, with many different types of QEC codes and methods.
