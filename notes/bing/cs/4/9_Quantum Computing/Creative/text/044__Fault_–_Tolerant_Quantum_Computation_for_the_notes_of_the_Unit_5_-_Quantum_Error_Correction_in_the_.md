### Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation

- Fault-tolerant quantum computation is the ability to perform quantum algorithms on a noisy quantum computer with a low probability of error.
- Fault-tolerance is essential for scalable quantum computing, as physical qubits are prone to errors due to decoherence, noise, and imperfect operations.
- Fault-tolerance can be achieved by using quantum error correction schemes, which encode logical qubits into physical qubits and apply error detection and correction procedures.
- Quantum error correction schemes require extra qubits, gates, and measurements, which introduce additional errors and overheads. Therefore, the physical error rate must be below a certain threshold for the logical error rate to be suppressed to arbitrarily low levels.
- This threshold is known as the quantum threshold theorem, and it depends on the error model, the error correction code, and the fault-tolerance protocol.
- The quantum threshold theorem implies that arbitrarily long quantum algorithms can be run efficiently on imperfect quantum hardware, as long as the hardware meets the accuracy threshold.
- Fault-tolerant quantum computation can be implemented using various techniques, such as gadgets, transversal gates, magic state distillation, and flag qubits, to protect quantum gates and measurements against correlated errors.
- Fault-tolerant quantum computation is an active area of research, and several experimental demonstrations have been performed using small-scale quantum devices.
