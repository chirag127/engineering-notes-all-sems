## Unit 3 - Quantum Computers

- A quantum computer is a computer that exploits quantum mechanical phenomena. At small scales, physical matter exhibits properties of both particles and waves, and quantum computing leverages this behavior using specialized hardware.
- Quantum computers use quantum bits or qubits as the basic unit of information. Unlike classical bits that can only be in one of two states (0 or 1), qubits can be in a superposition of both states simultaneously, meaning they can store more information and perform parallel computations.
- Quantum computers can also take advantage of another quantum phenomenon called entanglement, which is a correlation between two or more qubits that persists even when they are separated by large distances. Entangled qubits can be manipulated as a single unit, allowing for faster and more secure communication and computation.
- Quantum computers are expected to have applications in various fields such as cryptography, optimization, machine learning, simulation, and artificial intelligence. Some problems that are intractable for classical computers, such as factoring large numbers or finding optimal solutions for complex problems, could be solved by quantum computers in polynomial time or even exponentially faster.
- Quantum computers are still in the early stages of development and face many challenges such as scalability, error correction, and noise. However, several companies and research institutions are working on advancing the technology and making it more accessible and practical. IBM, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and Intel are some of the major players in the quantum computing industry.
