### Theory of Quantum Error –Correction

- Quantum error correction (QEC) is used in quantum computing to protect quantum information from errors due to decoherence and other quantum noise.
- QEC is theorised as essential to achieve fault tolerant quantum computing that can reduce the effects of noise on stored quantum information, faulty quantum gates, faulty quantum measurements, and faulty quantum communication.
- QEC is based on encoding states into larger Hilbert spaces subject to known interactions .
- QEC can be understood as a generalisation of classical error correction codes, where the quantum information is encoded in a set of quantum bits (qubits) that can be manipulated and measured.
- QEC codes can be classified into different types, such as stabilizer codes, which use ancilla qubits to protect the logical qubit, or topological error-correction codes, which use a lattice of qubits arranged on a surface such as a torus.
- QEC codes have necessary and sufficient conditions for the perfect recovery of an encoded state after its degradation by an interaction. The conditions depend only on the behavior of the logical states .
- QEC codes can be designed to correct different types of errors, such as bit-flip errors, phase-flip errors, or general errors that affect both the amplitude and the phase of the qubits.
- QEC codes can also be combined to form concatenated codes, where the qubits of one code are encoded by another code, to achieve higher levels of protection against errors.
- QEC protocols will play a central role in the realisation of quantum computing; the choice of error correction code will influence the full quantum computing stack, from the layout of qubits at the physical level to gate compilation strategies at the software level.
