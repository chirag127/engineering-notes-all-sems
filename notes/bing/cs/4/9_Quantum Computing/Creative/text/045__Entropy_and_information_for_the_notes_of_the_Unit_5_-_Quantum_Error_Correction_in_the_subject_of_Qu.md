### Entropy and information

- Entropy is a measure of the uncertainty or disorder of a system. It quantifies how much information is needed to describe the state of the system.
- Information is a measure of the reduction of uncertainty or disorder after observing or learning something new. It quantifies how much the state of the system can be specified or compressed.
- In quantum computing, entropy and information are important concepts for understanding the effects of noise, decoherence, and error correction on quantum states and operations.
- Some key points about entropy and information in quantum computing are:

  - The entropy of a quantum state is given by the von Neumann entropy, which is defined as $S(\rho) = -\text{Tr}(\rho \log \rho)$, where $\rho$ is the density matrix of the state. The von Neumann entropy is zero for pure states and positive for mixed states. It is a measure of the quantum uncertainty or entanglement of the state.
  - The information of a quantum measurement is given by the mutual information, which is defined as $I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) - S(AB)$, where $A$ and $B$ are two quantum systems, $S(A)$ and $S(B)$ are their entropies, and $S(AB)$ is the entropy of their joint state. The mutual information is zero for uncorrelated systems and positive for correlated or entangled systems. It is a measure of the quantum correlation or information gain between the systems after the measurement.
  - The entropy of a quantum channel is given by the quantum channel capacity, which is defined as the maximum amount of quantum information that can be reliably transmitted through the channel per use. The quantum channel capacity depends on the type of channel, the type of encoding and decoding, and the type of information (classical or quantum). It is a measure of the quantum communication or information processing power of the channel.
  - The information of a quantum error correction code is given by the quantum error correction threshold, which is defined as the maximum error rate of the channel that can be tolerated by the code while still preserving the quantum information. The quantum error correction threshold depends on the type of code, the type of error, and the type of recovery. It is a measure of the quantum robustness or information protection of the code.
