### Classical Noise and Markov Processes

- Classical noise is a random fluctuation or disturbance in a signal or a system that can affect its performance or accuracy. For example, thermal noise, shot noise, or channel noise.
- Markov processes are stochastic processes that have the property of memorylessness, meaning that the future state of the system depends only on the present state and not on the past history. For example, a coin toss, a random walk, or a Poisson process.
- In quantum information theory, classical noise and Markov processes can be used to model the effects of decoherence and dissipation on quantum systems that interact with their environment. For example, a qubit can undergo a bit flip or a phase flip due to noise, or lose its coherence due to entanglement with the environment.
- Quantum operations formalism is a mathematical framework that describes how quantum systems evolve under the influence of noise and measurements. It is based on the concept of completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) maps, which are linear operators that map density matrices to density matrices while preserving the positivity and the trace (total probability) of the quantum states.
- A special class of quantum operations are the quantum channels, which are CPTP maps that describe the evolution of a quantum system without any measurements. Quantum channels can be characterized by their Kraus operators, which are a set of matrices that satisfy certain conditions and can be used to express the action of the channel on any input state. For example, the amplitude damping channel, the depolarizing channel, or the dephasing channel.
- A quantum channel is said to be memoryless if the evolution of the quantum system is independent of its previous states, and depends only on the current state and the channel parameters. This is analogous to a classical Markov process, and such channels are also called Markovian channels. For example, the amplitude damping channel is a Markovian channel that models the spontaneous emission of a photon from an excited atom.
- A quantum channel is said to be non-Markovian if the evolution of the quantum system depends on its past states, and is influenced by the memory effects of the environment. This is analogous to a classical non-Markovian process, and such channels are also called non-Markovian channels. For example, the amplitude damping channel can become non-Markovian if the environment is finite and can reabsorb the emitted photon.
- Non-Markovian channels can exhibit quantum features that are absent in Markovian channels, such as quantum backflow, quantum memory, or quantum revival. These features can have implications for quantum information processing, such as quantum error correction, quantum metrology, or quantum cryptography.
