### Von Neumann

- Von Neumann was a mathematician and physicist who studied the problem of noise and errors in classical computation in the 1950s.
- He proposed the idea of using redundancy and majority voting to correct errors in classical bits.
- He also introduced the concept of a universal constructor, a machine that can replicate itself and other machines.
- His ideas inspired the development of quantum error correction (QEC), which is the use of quantum entanglement and measurement to protect quantum information from errors due to decoherence and other quantum noise.
- QEC is essential for achieving fault-tolerant quantum computing, which can reduce the effects of noise on stored quantum information, faulty quantum gates, faulty quantum measurements, and faulty quantum communication.
- QEC can be implemented using different types of codes, such as subspace codes, subsystem codes, and algebraic codes.
- Subspace codes are the most common type of QEC codes, which encode a logical qubit into a larger Hilbert space of physical qubits.
- Subsystem codes are a generalization of subspace codes, which encode a logical qubit into a subsystem of a larger Hilbert space of physical qubits.
- Algebraic codes are a further generalization of subsystem codes, which encode a logical qubit into an algebra of observables of a larger Hilbert space of physical qubits.
- QEC codes can be characterized by their distance, rate, and threshold.
- The distance of a QEC code is the minimum number of physical qubits that need to be corrupted to cause an error in the logical qubit.
- The rate of a QEC code is the ratio of the number of logical qubits to the number of physical qubits.
- The threshold of a QEC code is the maximum error rate per physical qubit that can be tolerated by the code.
- QEC codes can be classified into discrete and continuous codes, depending on the type of measurement used to detect and correct errors.
- Discrete QEC codes use projective von Neumann measurements on stabilizers to discretize the error syndromes into a finite set, and fast unitary gates are applied to recover the corrupted information .
- Continuous QEC codes use continuous measurements on observables to obtain continuous error syndromes, and feedback control is applied to correct the errors in real time.
