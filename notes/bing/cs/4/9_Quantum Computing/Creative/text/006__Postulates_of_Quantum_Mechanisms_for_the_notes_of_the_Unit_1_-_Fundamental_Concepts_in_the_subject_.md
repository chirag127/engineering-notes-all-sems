### Postulates of Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics is the branch of physics that describes the behavior of matter and energy at the smallest scales. Quantum mechanics is based on a set of postulates, or assumptions, that are not derived from any other theory, but are consistent with experimental observations. The postulates of quantum mechanics are:

- **Postulate 1**: The state of a quantum system is completely specified by a wave function, denoted by $\psi$, that assigns a complex number to each point in space and time. The wave function contains all the information about the system and its possible outcomes. The wave function must satisfy the Schrödinger equation, which is a differential equation that describes how the wave function evolves over time.

- **Postulate 2**: The physical observables of a quantum system, such as position, momentum, energy, spin, etc., are represented by operators, denoted by $\hat{O}$, that act on the wave function. The operators must be linear and Hermitian, which means that they satisfy the properties $\hat{O}(\alpha\psi_1 + \beta\psi_2) = \alpha\hat{O}\psi_1 + \beta\hat{O}\psi_2$ and $\hat{O}^\dagger = \hat{O}$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are complex numbers, $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ are wave functions, and $\hat{O}^\dagger$ is the adjoint of $\hat{O}$.

- **Postulate 3**: The only possible outcomes of a measurement of an observable $\hat{O}$ are the eigenvalues of the operator $\hat{O}$. The eigenvalues are the real numbers $\lambda$ that satisfy the equation $\hat{O}\psi = \lambda\psi$, where $\psi$ is an eigenfunction of $\hat{O}$. The eigenfunctions form a complete basis for the space of wave functions, which means that any wave function can be written as a linear combination of eigenfunctions.

- **Postulate 4**: The probability of obtaining a particular eigenvalue $\lambda$ as the result of a measurement of an observable $\hat{O}$ is given by the square of the absolute value of the inner product of the wave function $\psi$ and the corresponding eigenfunction $\phi$, i.e., $P(\lambda) = |\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2$. The inner product of two wave functions $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ is defined as $\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle = \int \psi_1^*(x)\psi_2(x) dx$, where $\psi^*(x)$ is the complex conjugate of $\psi(x)$. The inner product satisfies the properties $\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle = \langle\psi_2|\psi_1\rangle^*$ and $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$.

- **Postulate 5**: After a measurement of an observable $\hat{O}$, the state of the system collapses to the eigenfunction $\phi$ corresponding to the observed eigenvalue $\lambda$. This means that the wave function $\psi$ becomes $\phi$ and any subsequent measurement of $\hat{O}$ will yield the same result $\lambda$ with certainty. This postulate is also known as the projection postulate or the collapse of the wave function.
