### Global Perspectives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum computing is a new paradigm of computation that exploits the quantum mechanical properties of qubits, such as superposition and entanglement, to perform operations that are impossible or intractable for classical computers.
- Quantum computing has the potential to transform various fields and industries, such as cryptography, artificial intelligence, chemistry, physics, medicine, finance, and more, by enabling faster, more accurate, and more scalable solutions to complex problems.
- Quantum computing is also a highly competitive and rapidly evolving field, with multiple companies, governments, universities, and research institutions investing in the development and innovation of quantum hardware, software, algorithms, and applications.
- Quantum computing is not a distant or futuristic technology, but a reality that is already here and will continue to grow and mature in the next decade. Several milestones and breakthroughs have been achieved in recent years, such as the demonstration of quantum supremacy by Google in 2019, the launch of cloud-based quantum computing services by IBM, Microsoft, Amazon, and others, and the emergence of quantum start-ups and consortia.
- Quantum computing also poses significant challenges and risks, such as the threat to the security of current encryption systems, the ethical and social implications of quantum-enabled technologies, the need for a skilled and diverse quantum workforce, and the environmental impact of quantum devices and infrastructure.
- Quantum computing is a global phenomenon that requires collaboration and coordination among various stakeholders, such as policymakers, regulators, educators, researchers, developers, users, and consumers, to ensure its responsible and beneficial use for society.
