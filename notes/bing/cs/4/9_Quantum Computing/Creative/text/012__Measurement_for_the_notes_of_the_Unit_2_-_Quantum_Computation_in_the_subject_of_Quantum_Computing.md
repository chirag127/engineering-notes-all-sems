### Measurement

- Measurement is the process of extracting information from a quantum system, such as a qubit, by observing its state.
- Measurement is a probabilistic and irreversible operation that collapses the quantum state of the system to one of the possible outcomes, according to the Born rule.
- The Born rule states that the probability of measuring a quantum state |ψ⟩ in the basis state |φ⟩ is given by |⟨φ|ψ⟩|^2, where ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is the inner product of the two states.
- Measurement can be performed in any basis, but the most common one is the computational basis, which consists of the states |0⟩ and |1⟩. In this basis, the probability of measuring |0⟩ is |α|^2 and the probability of measuring |1⟩ is |β|^2, where |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ is the state of the qubit before measurement.
- Measurement can also be performed in other bases, such as the Hadamard basis, which consists of the states |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 and |-⟩ = (|0⟩ - |1⟩)/√2. In this basis, the probability of measuring |+⟩ is |⟨+|ψ⟩|^2 and the probability of measuring |-⟩ is |⟨-|ψ⟩|^2.
- Measurement can be generalized to multiple qubits by using the tensor product to form basis states. For example, the computational basis for two qubits consists of the states |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, and |11⟩, where |ij⟩ = |i⟩ ⊗ |j⟩. The probability of measuring a two-qubit state |ψ⟩ in the basis state |φ⟩ is given by |⟨φ|ψ⟩|^2, where ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is the inner product of the two states.
