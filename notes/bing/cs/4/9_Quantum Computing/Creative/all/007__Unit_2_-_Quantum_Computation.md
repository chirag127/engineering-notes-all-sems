## Unit 2 - Quantum Computation

- Quantum computation is a field of computer science that uses the principles of quantum physics to perform computations that are beyond the reach of classical computers.
- Quantum computation involves manipulating quantum bits, or qubits, which are physical systems that can exist in a superposition of two states, such as 0 and 1, at the same time.
- Quantum computation exploits two key phenomena of quantum physics: entanglement and interference.
  - Entanglement is the phenomenon where two or more qubits can share a quantum state and influence each other, even when they are physically separated.
  - Interference is the phenomenon where the probability of a qubit being in a certain state depends on the combination of the states of other qubits.
- Quantum computation can be performed using various physical platforms, such as superconducting circuits, trapped ions, photons, or atoms.
- Quantum computation can be described using quantum circuits, which are diagrams that show how qubits are initialized, manipulated, and measured using quantum gates, which are elementary operations that change the state of one or more qubits.
- Quantum computation can solve certain problems faster or more efficiently than classical computation, such as factoring large numbers, searching unsorted databases, simulating quantum systems, or optimizing combinatorial problems.
- Quantum computation also has some limitations, such as decoherence, noise, error correction, scalability, and algorithm design.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for quantum computation are:

- To remember the basic properties of quantum computation, use the acronym SEI: Superposition, Entanglement, Interference.
- To remember the names of some common quantum gates, use the mnemonic HAD CNOT: H is for Hadamard gate, A is for Pauli-X gate, D is for Pauli-Y gate, C is for Pauli-Z gate, N is for controlled-NOT gate, O is for Toffoli gate, T is for phase gate.
- To remember the difference between quantum and classical bits, use the analogy of a coin: A classical bit is like a coin that can only show heads or tails, while a qubit is like a coin that can show both heads and tails at the same time, until you look at it.
