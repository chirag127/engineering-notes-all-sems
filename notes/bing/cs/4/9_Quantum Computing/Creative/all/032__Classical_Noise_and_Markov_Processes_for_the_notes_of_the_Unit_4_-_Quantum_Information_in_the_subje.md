### Classical Noise and Markov Processes for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Classical noise is a type of disturbance that affects the state of a classical bit, such as a voltage or a magnetic field, by randomly flipping its value with some probability. For example, a bit that starts out in the state 0 or 1 may end up in the opposite state after some time due to noise.
- Markov processes are a class of stochastic processes that describe the evolution of a system that depends only on its current state and not on its past history. For example, a Markov chain is a sequence of random variables that follow a Markov process, where the probability of transitioning from one state to another is given by a transition matrix.
- Classical noise and Markov processes can be used to model the effects of noise on quantum systems, such as quantum bits (qubits) or quantum channels. For example, a qubit that starts out in a pure state may end up in a mixed state due to noise, and a quantum channel that transmits quantum information may introduce errors or decoherence due to noise.
- The formalism of quantum operations can be used to describe the effect of noise on quantum systems in a general way. A quantum operation is a map that takes an input density matrix and produces an output density matrix, satisfying certain conditions of linearity, trace preservation, and complete positivity. A quantum operation can be represented by a set of Kraus operators, which are matrices that satisfy certain conditions of orthogonality and normalization.
- The classical capacity of a quantum channel is a measure of how much classical information can be reliably transmitted through the channel per use. The classical capacity depends on the type and strength of the noise in the channel, as well as the encoding and decoding schemes used by the sender and receiver. For a channel with arbitrary Markovian correlated noise, which means that the noise depends on the current and previous inputs, the classical capacity can be expressed in terms of the communicating classes of the Markov chain that describes the noise.
- The filter function formalism is a method to perturbatively compute quantum processes in the presence of correlated classical noise, which means that the noise depends on the current and previous inputs as well as the current and previous outputs. The filter function is a function that modifies the ideal unitary evolution of a quantum system by taking into account the effects of noise. The filter function can be composed for a sequence of gates in terms of the filter functions of the individual gates .
- Quantum non-Markovian phenomena are those that involve memory effects or backflow of information from the environment to the system. For example, a quantum system that interacts with an environment may exhibit non-Markovian behavior if the environment retains some information about the system and influences its future evolution. Quantum non-Markovian phenomena can be characterized by various measures, such as the quantum Fisher information, the quantum mutual information, or the quantum discord.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for this topic are:

- To remember the conditions for a quantum operation, think of LTC: Linearity, Trace preservation, and Complete positivity.
- To remember the conditions for the Kraus operators, think of ON: Orthogonality and Normalization.
- To remember the difference between Markovian and non-Markovian noise, think of Markovian noise as having no Memory, and non-Markovian noise as having Memory.
