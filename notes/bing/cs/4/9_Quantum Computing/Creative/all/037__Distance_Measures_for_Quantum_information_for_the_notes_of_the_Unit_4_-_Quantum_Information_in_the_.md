### Distance Measures for Quantum Information

- Distance measures for quantum information are used to quantify the extent to which two quantum states behave in the same way or how well a protocol is performing.
- Distance measures are usually given by certain mathematical expressions, but they often have a simple operational meaning, such as the probability of distinguishing two states or the fidelity of a protocol.
- A distance measure is a function that takes two quantum states as inputs and outputs a non-negative real number that satisfies some basic properties, such as symmetry, identity of indiscernibles, and triangle inequality. Such a function is called a metric.
- Some examples of distance measures for quantum information are:

  - Trace distance: This is the quantum generalization of the Kolmogorov distance for classical probability distributions. It is defined as the half of the trace norm of the difference between two density matrices. It can be interpreted as the maximum probability of distinguishing two states by a single measurement.
  - Fidelity: This is a measure of the overlap or similarity between two quantum states. It is defined as the square root of the inner product of the square roots of the density matrices. It can be interpreted as the maximum probability of obtaining the same outcome when measuring two states in the same basis.
  - Quantum relative entropy: This is a measure of the information gain or loss when replacing one quantum state by another. It is defined as the trace of the product of the first density matrix and the logarithm of the ratio of the two density matrices. It can be interpreted as the difference between the von Neumann entropy of the first state and the conditional entropy of the first state given the second state.
  - Bures distance: This is a measure of the statistical distance between two quantum states. It is defined as the square root of the complement of the fidelity. It can be interpreted as the minimum angle between the two states in the Hilbert space.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering these distance measures are:

  - Trace distance: Think of tracing the outline of the difference between two shapes.
  - Fidelity: Think of being faithful or loyal to someone who is similar to you.
  - Quantum relative entropy: Think of how much information you lose or gain when you switch from one state to another.
  - Bures distance: Think of the word "bureaucracy" and how it creates distance between people.
