### Optical Photon Quantum Computer for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Optical photon quantum computer is a type of quantum computer that uses photons as information carriers and mainly uses linear optical elements to process quantum information.
- Linear optical elements are optical instruments that do not change the number of photons in a beam, such as mirrors, beam splitters, and phase shifters.
- Photon detectors and quantum memories are used to detect and store quantum information.
- Optical photon quantum computer can implement universal quantum computation, meaning that it can perform any quantum algorithm.
- Optical photon quantum computer has some advantages over other types of quantum computers, such as:
  - Photons are easy to manipulate and have low decoherence, meaning that they can preserve quantum information for a long time.
  - Photons can travel long distances and interact with other quantum systems, enabling quantum communication and networking.
  - Photons can be generated and detected using existing technologies, such as lasers and photodiodes.
- Optical photon quantum computer also has some challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Generating single photons on demand is difficult and inefficient, requiring complex and expensive sources.
  - Detecting single photons is probabilistic and noisy, leading to errors and losses.
  - Scaling up optical photon quantum computer requires a large number of optical elements and detectors, which increases the complexity and cost.
- Optical photon quantum computer can be implemented using different architectures and techniques, such as:
  - Linear optical quantum computing (LOQC), which relies on qubits each based on a single photon and uses interference and measurement to perform quantum gates.
  - Measurement-based quantum computing (MBQC), which uses a large entangled state of photons as a resource and performs quantum computation by measuring subsets of photons.
  - Programmable photonic circuit (PPC), which uses a reconfigurable array of optical elements and detectors to execute various quantum algorithms.
  - Quantum teleportation-based quantum computing (QTQC), which uses a laser to manipulate a single atom that can modify the state of photons via quantum teleportation.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the main components of optical photon quantum computer is: **P**hotons, **L**inear optical elements, **D**etectors, and **M**emories (PLDM).
- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of quantum teleportation is to imagine that Alice and Bob share a pair of entangled photons, and Alice wants to send her photon's state to Bob. She can do this by performing a joint measurement on her photon and another photon that she wants to teleport, and sending the measurement result to Bob. Bob can then use this information to apply a correction to his photon, and end up with the same state as Alice's photon. This way, Alice and Bob can transfer quantum information without sending the actual photon.
