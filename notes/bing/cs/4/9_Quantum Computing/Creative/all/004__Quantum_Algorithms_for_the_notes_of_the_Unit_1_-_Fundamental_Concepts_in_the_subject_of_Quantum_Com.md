### Quantum Algorithms for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

- A quantum algorithm is an algorithm that runs on a quantum computer, which is a machine that uses the principles of quantum mechanics to perform computations.
- A quantum computer consists of a classical computer that controls a quantum processor, which is a collection of quantum bits or qubits that can exist in superposition and entanglement of two or more states.
- A quantum algorithm is usually described by a quantum circuit, which is a sequence of quantum gates that act on the qubits and manipulate their states. A quantum gate is a unitary operation that preserves the norm of the quantum state vector.
- A quantum algorithm terminates with a measurement, which is a process that collapses the quantum state of the qubits to one of the basis states and returns a classical output. The output of a quantum algorithm is probabilistic, meaning that it may vary depending on the random outcome of the measurement.
- Quantum algorithms can be categorized by the main techniques or ideas that they use, such as:
  - Phase kick-back: a technique that transfers the phase of one qubit to another by using a controlled gate, such as the controlled-NOT gate. This technique is useful for implementing quantum logic and arithmetic operations, such as the Toffoli gate and the quantum adder.
  - Phase estimation: a technique that estimates the phase of an eigenstate of a unitary operator by using a quantum Fourier transform and a controlled unitary operation. This technique is useful for finding the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a matrix, such as the Hamiltonian of a quantum system.
  - Quantum Fourier transform: a quantum version of the discrete Fourier transform that maps a quantum state to its frequency domain representation. This technique is useful for performing fast arithmetic operations, such as multiplication and exponentiation, and for solving problems such as period finding and order finding.
  - Quantum walks: a quantum version of the random walk that explores a graph or a network by using quantum superposition and interference. This technique is useful for solving problems such as search and traversal, such as Grover's algorithm and the quantum PageRank algorithm.
  - Amplitude amplification: a technique that amplifies the probability of finding a desired state by using a Grover operator that consists of an oracle and a diffusion operator. This technique is useful for speeding up search and optimization problems, such as the unstructured search and the maximum cut problem.
  - Topological quantum field theory: a branch of mathematics that studies quantum systems that are invariant under continuous deformations of space and time. This technique is useful for designing quantum algorithms that are robust against noise and errors, such as the topological quantum error correction and the topological quantum computation.

- Some examples of quantum algorithms are:
  - Shor's algorithm: an algorithm that factors a large integer into its prime factors by using a quantum Fourier transform and a classical algorithm such as the continued fraction algorithm. This algorithm can break the security of some classical cryptographic schemes, such as the RSA encryption.
  - Grover's algorithm: an algorithm that finds a marked element in an unsorted database by using a Grover operator that iteratively applies an oracle and a diffusion operator. This algorithm can quadratically speed up the search problem, such as the satisfiability problem and the collision problem.
  - Deutsch-Jozsa algorithm: an algorithm that determines whether a function is constant or balanced by using a single query to the function and a Hadamard transform. This algorithm can exponentially speed up the function evaluation problem, such as the parity problem and the hidden subgroup problem.
  - Simon's algorithm: an algorithm that finds a hidden period in a function by using a quantum Fourier transform and a classical algorithm such as the Gaussian elimination. This algorithm can exponentially speed up the period finding problem, such as the discrete logarithm problem and the hidden linear function problem.
