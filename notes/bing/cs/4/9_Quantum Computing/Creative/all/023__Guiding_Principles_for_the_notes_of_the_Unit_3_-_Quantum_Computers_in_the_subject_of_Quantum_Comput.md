### Guiding Principles for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum computers are computers that exploit quantum mechanical phenomena, such as superposition and entanglement, to store and manipulate information in quantum bits (qubits) .
- Quantum computers can perform certain tasks faster and more efficiently than classical computers, such as factoring large numbers, simulating quantum systems, and searching unsorted databases .
- Quantum computers are composed of quantum processors, which consist of arrays of qubits that can be manipulated by applying quantum gates, which are operations that change the state of one or more qubits .
- Quantum computers are subject to noise and errors, which can affect the accuracy and reliability of the computation. Quantum error correction and fault tolerance are techniques to protect and correct the quantum information from noise and errors .
- Quantum computers are not yet widely available or scalable, but there are several platforms and architectures that are being developed and tested, such as superconducting qubits, trapped ions, photonic qubits, and topological qubits  .
- Quantum computers have potential applications in various fields, such as cryptography, optimization, machine learning, chemistry, physics, and biology  .

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the notes are:

- Qubits are like coins that can be in heads, tails, or both at the same time (superposition).
- Quantum gates are like flips or rotations that change the state of the coins (qubits).
- Quantum entanglement is like a magic trick that links two coins (qubits) together, so that measuring one reveals the state of the other.
- Quantum error correction is like a backup system that stores extra coins (qubits) to detect and fix errors in the original ones.
- Quantum computing platforms are like different types of coins (qubits) that have different properties and advantages.
