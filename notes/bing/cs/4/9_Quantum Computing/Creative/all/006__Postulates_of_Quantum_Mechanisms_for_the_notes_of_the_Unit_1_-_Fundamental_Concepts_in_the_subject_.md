### Postulates of Quantum Mechanisms for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

1. The state of a quantum system is completely described by a wave function, which is a complex-valued function of the system's degrees of freedom, such as position, momentum, spin, etc. The wave function contains all the information that can be known about the system, and its evolution is governed by the Schrödinger equation.
2. The wave function can be expressed as a linear combination of basis states, which are mutually orthogonal and normalized. The coefficients of the linear combination are called amplitudes, and they determine the probability of finding the system in a given basis state. The probability is given by the square of the absolute value of the amplitude, and the sum of the probabilities over all basis states is equal to one. This is known as the Born rule.
3. The only way to obtain information about a quantum system is to perform a measurement on it. A measurement is described by an observable, which is a Hermitian operator that acts on the wave function. The possible outcomes of a measurement are the eigenvalues of the observable, and the probability of obtaining a given outcome is given by the Born rule applied to the corresponding eigenvector of the observable. The measurement collapses the wave function to the eigenvector corresponding to the outcome, and this is known as the collapse postulate or the projection postulate.
4. If two quantum systems are prepared independently, their wave functions are tensor products of each other. This means that the state of the combined system is described by the product of the wave functions of the individual systems. However, if the systems interact with each other, their wave functions may become entangled, which means that they cannot be written as tensor products of each other. Entangled systems share quantum correlations that cannot be explained by classical physics, and they exhibit nonlocal behavior, such as quantum teleportation and quantum cryptography.
