## Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction

- Quantum error correction (QEC) is a technique to protect quantum information from decoherence and noise by encoding it into entangled states of multiple qubits.
- QEC is based on the idea of redundancy: by using more qubits than necessary to store the quantum information, one can detect and correct errors without disturbing the encoded information.
- QEC requires two main steps: error detection and error correction.
  - Error detection involves measuring some properties of the qubits, called syndromes, that reveal the presence and type of errors, but not the encoded information.
  - Error correction involves applying appropriate unitary operations or measurements to the qubits, depending on the syndromes, to restore the encoded information.
- QEC can be classified into two types: passive and active.
  - Passive QEC relies on encoding the quantum information into special states, called decoherence-free subspaces or noiseless subsystems, that are immune to certain types of errors.
  - Active QEC relies on performing periodic error detection and correction on the qubits, using special circuits, called quantum error-correcting codes, that are designed to correct certain types of errors.
- QEC is essential for building scalable and reliable quantum computers and communication systems, as it can increase the coherence time and fidelity of the qubits, and reduce the error rate of the quantum operations.
- QEC is also a rich source of fundamental insights into quantum mechanics, such as the quantum no-cloning theorem, the quantum Zeno effect, and the quantum threshold theorem.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for QEC are:

- Remember the acronym QEC: Quantum Error Correction.
- Remember the two types of QEC: Passive and Active.
- Remember the two steps of QEC: Error Detection and Error Correction.
- Remember the three properties of QEC: Coherence, Fidelity, and Error Rate.
- Remember the three examples of QEC: Decoherence-free Subspaces, Noiseless Subsystems, and Quantum Error-correcting Codes.
