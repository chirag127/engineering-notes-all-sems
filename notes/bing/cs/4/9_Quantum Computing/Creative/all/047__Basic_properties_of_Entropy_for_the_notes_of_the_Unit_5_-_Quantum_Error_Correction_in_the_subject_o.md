### Basic properties of Entropy for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Entropy is a measure of uncertainty or disorder in a quantum system. It quantifies how much information is needed to describe the state of the system or how much information is gained by measuring the system.
- There are different types of entropy for quantum systems, such as von Neumann entropy, Shannon entropy, Renyi entropy, and Tsallis entropy. Each type of entropy has its own definition, properties, and applications.
- Von Neumann entropy is the most commonly used entropy in quantum information theory. It is defined as S(ρ) = -tr(ρ log ρ), where ρ is the density matrix of the quantum system and tr is the trace operation. Von Neumann entropy satisfies the following basic properties :
  - Non-negativity: S(ρ) ≥ 0 for any ρ, and S(ρ) = 0 if and only if ρ is a pure state.
  - Concavity: S(ρ) is a concave function of ρ, meaning that S(∑i pi ρi) ≥ ∑i pi S(ρi) for any convex combination of density matrices ρi and probabilities pi.
  - Subadditivity: S(ρAB) ≤ S(ρA) + S(ρB) for any bipartite system AB, where ρA and ρB are the reduced density matrices of subsystems A and B, respectively. This property implies that the entropy of a composite system is less than or equal to the sum of the entropies of its parts.
  - Strong subadditivity: S(ρABC) + S(ρB) ≤ S(ρAB) + S(ρBC) for any tripartite system ABC. This property is a stronger version of subadditivity that involves three subsystems.
  - Continuity: S(ρ) is a continuous function of ρ, meaning that small changes in ρ result in small changes in S(ρ).
  - Invariance under unitary transformations: S(ρ) is invariant under unitary transformations, meaning that S(UρU†) = S(ρ) for any unitary matrix U. This property reflects the fact that entropy is a property of the state, not of the basis in which it is represented.
- Shannon entropy is the classical counterpart of von Neumann entropy. It is defined as H(X) = -∑i pi log pi, where X is a discrete random variable with probabilities pi. Shannon entropy satisfies similar properties as von Neumann entropy, such as non-negativity, concavity, subadditivity, and continuity. Shannon entropy can be used to measure the uncertainty of a quantum measurement outcome or the classical information content of a quantum state.
- Renyi entropy is a generalization of both von Neumann entropy and Shannon entropy. It is defined as Sα(ρ) = (1-α)^-1 log tr(ρ^α), where α is a positive parameter and ρ is a density matrix or a probability distribution. Renyi entropy reduces to von Neumann entropy when α = 1 and to Shannon entropy when α = 0. Renyi entropy satisfies some of the properties of von Neumann entropy, such as non-negativity, concavity, and invariance under unitary transformations, but not all of them, such as subadditivity and continuity. Renyi entropy can be used to measure the entanglement of quantum states or the distinguishability of quantum processes.
- Tsallis entropy is another generalization of both von Neumann entropy and Shannon entropy. It is defined as Sq(ρ) = (q-1)^-1 (1-tr(ρ^q)), where q is a positive parameter and ρ is a density matrix or a probability distribution. Tsallis entropy reduces to von Neumann entropy when q = 1 and to Shannon entropy when q = 0. Tsallis entropy satisfies some of the properties of von Neumann entropy, such as non-negativity, concavity, and invariance under unitary transformations, but not all of them, such as subadditivity and continuity. Tsallis entropy can be used to model non-equilibrium thermodynamics or non-extensive statistical mechanics .
