### Quantum Information for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum information is the information of the state of a quantum system. It is the basic entity of study in quantum information theory, and can be manipulated using quantum information processing techniques.
- Quantum information refers to both the technical definition in terms of Von Neumann entropy and the general computational term.
- Quantum information, like classical information, can be processed using digital computers, transmitted from one location to another, manipulated with algorithms, and analyzed with computer science and mathematics.
- Just like the basic unit of classical information is the bit, quantum information deals with qubits. A qubit is a two-state quantum system that can be in a superposition of both states simultaneously.
- Quantum information science aims to explore the nature of information at the quantum level, a world in which bits can be both zero and one at the same time and perfect copying is impossible. It covers both theoretical and experimental aspects of quantum physics, including the limits of what can be achieved with quantum information.
- Quantum information science has applications in various fields, such as quantum cryptography, quantum communication, quantum computation, quantum metrology, quantum sensing, and quantum simulation.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for quantum information are:

- To remember the definition of Von Neumann entropy, which is a measure of quantum information, use the acronym VINE: Von Neumann entropy = Information of a quantum system = Negative trace of the density matrix times the logarithm of the density matrix = Expectation value of the logarithm of the density matrix.
- To remember the difference between a bit and a qubit, use the rhyme: A bit is either zero or one, a qubit is both and none.
- To remember the basic postulates of quantum information theory, use the acronym QMIME: Quantum states are represented by vectors in a Hilbert space (Q); Measurements are described by operators on the Hilbert space (M); Information is conserved in a closed quantum system (I); Measurement outcomes are probabilistic and depend on the state (M); Evolution of a quantum system is governed by a unitary transformation (E).
