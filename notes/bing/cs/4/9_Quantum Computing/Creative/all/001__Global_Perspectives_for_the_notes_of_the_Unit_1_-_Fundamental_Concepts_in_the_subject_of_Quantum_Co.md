### Global Perspectives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum computing is a rapidly evolving field that promises to revolutionize various domains such as cryptography, artificial intelligence, optimization, simulation, and more.
- Quantum computing is based on the principles of quantum mechanics, which describe the behavior of subatomic particles such as electrons, photons, and atoms.
- Quantum computing uses quantum bits or qubits, which can exist in superposition of two states, 0 and 1, unlike classical bits that can only be either 0 or 1. This allows quantum computers to perform parallel computations and explore a larger solution space than classical computers.
- Quantum computing also exploits quantum entanglement, which is a phenomenon where two or more qubits can share a quantum state and influence each other, even when they are physically separated. This enables quantum computers to perform faster and more secure communication and computation.
- Quantum computing faces many challenges, such as maintaining coherence and avoiding decoherence of qubits, scaling up the number and quality of qubits, developing efficient quantum algorithms and error correction techniques, and overcoming hardware and software limitations.
- Quantum computing has attracted significant attention and investment from various stakeholders, such as governments, corporations, academic institutions, and start-ups. According to a report by Research and Markets, the global quantum computing market is expected to reach $9.56 billion by 2031, at a compound annual growth rate of 28.6% .
- Quantum computing has potential applications in various industries, such as finance, health care, energy, defense, aerospace, manufacturing, and more. Some of the use cases include cryptography, optimization, machine learning, simulation, quantum chemistry, and quantum metrology.
- Quantum computing also poses ethical, social, and economic implications, such as disrupting existing security systems, creating new opportunities and challenges for innovation and competitiveness, and impacting human rights, privacy, and democracy.
- Quantum computing requires a multidisciplinary and collaborative approach, involving physics, mathematics, computer science, engineering, and more. It also requires a global and inclusive perspective, involving diverse stakeholders, perspectives, and values.
- Quantum computing is an exciting and promising field that offers new possibilities and challenges for the future of technology and society. It is important to understand the fundamental concepts, the current state, and the future potential of quantum computing, as well as the implications and responsibilities that come with it.
