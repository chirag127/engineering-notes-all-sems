## Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts

This unit covers the basic concepts of computer science, such as data types, algorithms, abstraction, and complexity.

- Data types are the categories of values that can be stored and manipulated by a computer program. Examples of data types are integers, floating-point numbers, strings, booleans, lists, and dictionaries.
- Algorithms are the step-by-step instructions that describe how to solve a problem or perform a task. Algorithms can be expressed in natural language, pseudocode, flowcharts, or programming languages. Examples of algorithms are sorting, searching, encryption, and compression.
- Abstraction is the process of hiding the details or complexity of a system or problem and focusing on the essential features or properties. Abstraction helps to simplify, generalize, and modularize the design and implementation of a system or problem. Examples of abstraction are functions, classes, interfaces, and layers.
- Complexity is the measure of the difficulty or cost of solving a problem or performing a task. Complexity can be expressed in terms of time, space, or other resources. Complexity can be analyzed using mathematical models, such as asymptotic notation, recurrence relations, and decision trees. Examples of complexity are linear, logarithmic, polynomial, exponential, and NP-hard.
