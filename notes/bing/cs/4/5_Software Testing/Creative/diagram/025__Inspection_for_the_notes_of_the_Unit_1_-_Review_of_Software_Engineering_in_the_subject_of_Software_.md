### Inspection for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

Inspection is a type of static testing that involves a peer review of any work product by trained individuals who look for defects using a well-defined process . Inspection can be applied to various software artifacts, such as requirements, design, code, and test cases. The goal of inspection is to identify and correct defects early in the software development life cycle (SDLC), before they become more costly and difficult to fix .

- Planning: The inspection leader selects the work product to be inspected, the inspection team, the inspection date, and the inspection checklist .
- Preparation: The inspection team members study the work product and identify potential defects using the inspection checklist  .
- Inspection: The inspection team meets to discuss the work product and the defects found, and to reach a consensus on the severity and validity of the defects  .
- Reporting: The inspection leader records the inspection results, such as the number, type, and severity of defects, the inspection effort, and the inspection rate  .
- Follow-up: The inspection leader verifies that the defects have been corrected and that no new defects have been introduced  .
- Rework: The author of the work product fixes the defects and resubmits the work product for inspection if necessary  .

Inspection is a valuable technique for improving the quality of software products and reducing the cost and time of software development . Inspection can also enhance the communication and collaboration among the software engineers and stakeholders. Inspection can be integrated into any software development methodology, such as waterfall, agile, or spiral .
