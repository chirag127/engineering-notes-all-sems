### Levels of Testing

- Levels of testing are the different stages of the software testing process that aim to verify and validate the quality of the software product.
- Each level of testing has a specific scope, objective, and target audience.
- The main levels of testing are:

  - Unit testing: The testing of individual components or units of code, such as functions, classes, or modules. Unit testing is usually done by the developers using tools like JUnit, NUnit, or PyTest. The objective of unit testing is to ensure that the units work correctly and meet their specifications.
  - Integration testing: The testing of the interactions and interfaces between two or more units or components. Integration testing is usually done by the developers or testers using tools like TestNG, Cucumber, or Selenium. The objective of integration testing is to ensure that the units work together as expected and do not cause any errors or conflicts.
  - System testing: The testing of the entire software system as a whole, including its functionality, performance, reliability, security, and usability. System testing is usually done by the testers or quality assurance (QA) team using tools like LoadRunner, JMeter, or Postman. The objective of system testing is to ensure that the system meets the requirements and expectations of the stakeholders and end-users.
  - Acceptance testing: The testing of the software system by the intended users or customers in their real environment. Acceptance testing is usually done by the users or customers using tools like UserTesting, SurveyMonkey, or Google Forms. The objective of acceptance testing is to ensure that the system satisfies the needs and preferences of the users or customers and is ready for deployment or delivery.
