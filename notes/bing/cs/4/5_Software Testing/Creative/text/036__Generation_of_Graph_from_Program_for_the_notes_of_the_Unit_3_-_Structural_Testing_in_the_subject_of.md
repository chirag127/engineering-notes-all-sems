### Generation of Graph from Program

- A graph is a visual representation of the structure and flow of a program.
- A graph consists of nodes and edges, where nodes represent statements or blocks of code, and edges represent the possible transitions between nodes.
- A graph can be used to analyze the complexity, coverage, and dependencies of a program, and to design test cases based on various criteria.
- There are different types of graphs that can be generated from a program, such as control flow graph, data flow graph, call graph, etc.
- A control flow graph (CFG) shows the sequence of execution and branching of a program, and the conditions that affect the flow of control.
- A data flow graph (DFG) shows the definition and use of variables in a program, and the dependencies between them.
- A call graph shows the invocation and return of functions or procedures in a program, and the relationships between them.
- To generate a graph from a program, the following steps are usually followed:
  - Identify the nodes and edges of the graph based on the type of graph and the level of abstraction.
  - Label the nodes and edges with appropriate information, such as statement numbers, variable names, conditions, etc.
  - Draw the graph using a standard notation, such as boxes for nodes and arrows for edges.
  - Check the graph for completeness, correctness, and consistency with the program.
