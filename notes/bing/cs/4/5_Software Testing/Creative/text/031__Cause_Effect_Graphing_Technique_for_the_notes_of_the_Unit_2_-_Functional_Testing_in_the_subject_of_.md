### Cause Effect Graphing Technique

- Cause effect graphing technique is a black box testing technique that uses a graphical representation of the inputs and outputs of a system to generate test cases.
- The graph consists of nodes and edges, where nodes represent causes (inputs or conditions) and effects (outputs or actions), and edges represent the logical relationships between them.
- The graph can be converted into a decision table, which shows the combinations of causes and effects for each test case.
- The graph can also be simplified by applying some rules, such as eliminating redundant or contradictory causes and effects, or combining equivalent causes and effects.
- The advantages of cause effect graphing technique are:
  - It helps to identify the functional dependencies and interactions between the inputs and outputs of a system.
  - It helps to reduce the number of test cases by eliminating unnecessary or invalid combinations of inputs and outputs.
  - It helps to ensure the completeness and consistency of the test cases by covering all possible causes and effects.
  - It helps to document the test cases in a concise and structured way.
- The disadvantages of cause effect graphing technique are:
  - It requires a clear and precise specification of the system's functionality and behavior.
  - It may be difficult to construct and maintain the graph for complex systems with many inputs and outputs.
  - It may not be able to capture some aspects of the system, such as timing, performance, or usability.
