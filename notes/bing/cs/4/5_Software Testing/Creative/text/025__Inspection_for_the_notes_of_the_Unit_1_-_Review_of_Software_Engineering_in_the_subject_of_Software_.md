### Inspection for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Inspection is a type of **static testing** technique that relies on **visual examination** of software artifacts (such as code, design, requirements, etc.) to detect **defects** and **violations** of development standards.
- Inspection is also known as **peer review** or **Fagan inspection** (after Michael Fagan, the creator of a popular software inspection process).
- Inspection involves a **team** of trained individuals who follow a **well-defined process** to review the software artifacts and identify potential errors or areas for improvement .
- Inspection can help improve the **quality** of the software by finding and resolving faults **early** in the development process, reducing the cost and effort of later-stage testing and debugging.
- Inspection can also help improve the **productivity** and **skills** of the software developers by providing feedback, learning opportunities, and best practices.
- Inspection typically consists of the following **phases** :
  - **Planning**: The inspection leader selects the team members, assigns roles, schedules the meeting, and distributes the artifacts to be reviewed.
  - **Overview**: The inspection leader or the author of the artifact gives a brief presentation of the artifact's purpose, scope, and structure to the team members.
  - **Preparation**: The team members individually examine the artifact and identify defects or issues using checklists, guidelines, or tools.
  - **Inspection**: The team members meet and discuss the defects or issues found in the artifact, and record them in a report. The inspection leader moderates the meeting and ensures that the process is followed and the objectives are met.
  - **Rework**: The author of the artifact corrects the defects or issues identified in the inspection report and verifies that they are resolved.
  - **Follow-up**: The inspection leader checks that the rework is done and that no new defects are introduced. The inspection leader also evaluates the effectiveness of the inspection process and suggests improvements for future inspections.
