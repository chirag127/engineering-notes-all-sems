### Priority category for the notes of the Unit 4 - Regression Testing in the subject of Software Testing

- Regression testing is the process of retesting a software system after changes have been made to ensure that the changes have not introduced new defects or affected the existing functionality.
- Regression testing can be performed at different levels of testing, such as unit, integration, system, and acceptance testing.
- Regression testing can be done manually or automatically, depending on the availability of test cases, test tools, and resources.
- Regression testing can be classified into three priority categories based on the impact and frequency of the changes:

  - **High priority**: These are the test cases that cover the critical functionality of the system, the areas that are most affected by the changes, or the areas that have a high risk of failure. These test cases should be executed first and frequently during regression testing.
  - **Medium priority**: These are the test cases that cover the important functionality of the system, the areas that are moderately affected by the changes, or the areas that have a medium risk of failure. These test cases should be executed after the high priority test cases and periodically during regression testing.
  - **Low priority**: These are the test cases that cover the less important functionality of the system, the areas that are least affected by the changes, or the areas that have a low risk of failure. These test cases should be executed last and occasionally during regression testing.

- The priority category of the test cases can be determined by using various criteria, such as:

  - The severity and probability of the defects in the system
  - The business value and user expectations of the system
  - The complexity and stability of the system
  - The scope and extent of the changes in the system
  - The availability and reliability of the test data and test environment
  - The time and cost constraints of the testing process

- The priority category of the test cases can be updated or revised based on the feedback and results of the regression testing. The test cases that fail or reveal new defects can be assigned a higher priority, while the test cases that pass or show no defects can be assigned a lower priority. The test cases that are no longer relevant or applicable can be removed or replaced. The test cases that are missing or incomplete can be added or modified. The test cases that are redundant or overlapping can be merged or eliminated.
