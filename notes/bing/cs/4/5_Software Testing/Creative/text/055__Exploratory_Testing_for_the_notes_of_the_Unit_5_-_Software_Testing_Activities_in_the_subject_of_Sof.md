### Exploratory Testing

- Exploratory testing is a type of software testing that involves simultaneous learning, test design, and test execution.
- Exploratory testing is based on the tester's skills, creativity, and intuition, rather than on predefined test cases or scripts.
- Exploratory testing is often performed when there is limited time, documentation, or specification available, or when the tester wants to discover new risks or defects that may not be covered by formal testing methods.
- Exploratory testing is not random or ad hoc testing, but rather a structured and disciplined approach that follows a cycle of test planning, test execution, test evaluation, and test learning.
- Exploratory testing can be performed at any stage of the software development life cycle, and can be combined with other testing techniques, such as scripted testing, automated testing, or model-based testing.
- Exploratory testing can be performed by individual testers, pairs of testers, or teams of testers, depending on the context and the goals of the testing activity.
- Exploratory testing can be guided by heuristics, oracles, checklists, mind maps, test sessions, test charters, or other tools that help the tester to design and execute effective tests.
- Exploratory testing can be documented by using notes, screenshots, videos, logs, bug reports, test reports, or other artifacts that capture the tester's actions, observations, and findings.
- Exploratory testing can be evaluated by using metrics, such as test coverage, defect density, defect severity, test duration, test efficiency, or test effectiveness, depending on the context and the goals of the testing activity.
- Exploratory testing can be improved by using feedback, peer reviews, retrospectives, debriefings, or other methods that help the tester to learn from the testing experience and to identify areas for improvement.
