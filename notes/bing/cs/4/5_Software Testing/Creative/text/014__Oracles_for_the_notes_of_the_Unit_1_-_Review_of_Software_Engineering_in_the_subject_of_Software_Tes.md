### Oracles

- An oracle is a mechanism or principle that determines whether the observed behavior of a software system is correct or not.
- An oracle can be a human, a document, a test case, a specification, a model, a tool, or a combination of these.
- An oracle can be exact, heuristic, or probabilistic, depending on the level of certainty and precision it provides.
- An oracle can be explicit, implicit, or derived, depending on the source and availability of the information it uses.
- An oracle can be complete, sound, or consistent, depending on the coverage and quality of the judgments it makes.
- An oracle can be static, dynamic, or adaptive, depending on the time and manner of its evaluation.
- An oracle can be internal, external, or hybrid, depending on the location and perspective of its observation.
- An oracle can be deterministic, nondeterministic, or partially deterministic, depending on the variability and repeatability of its outcomes.
- An oracle can be stateful, stateless, or partially stateful, depending on the memory and history of its execution.
- An oracle can be synchronous, asynchronous, or partially synchronous, depending on the timing and coordination of its interaction.
