### Configuration Audits for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Configuration audits are an essential process in ensuring the quality and reliability of a software product. They provide an independent evaluation of the system's functionality, performance, and consistency with the relevant requirement specifications .
- Configuration audits are part of the software configuration management (SCM) process, which tracks and monitors changes to a software system's configuration metadata. SCM helps to maintain the integrity and traceability of the software product throughout its life cycle.
- Configuration audits are performed for all releases of the software product; however, audits of interim, internal releases may be less formal and rigorous, as defined by the project. Configuration audits are conducted by auditors who check that defined processes are being followed and that the SCM goals are satisfied.
- There are two types of configuration audits: the Functional Configuration Audit (FCA) and the Physical Configuration Audit (PCA).
  - FCA verifies that the software product satisfies the functional requirements and specifications. It ensures that the software product has the correct features, functions, and interfaces.
  - PCA verifies that the software product conforms to the physical requirements and specifications. It ensures that the software product has the correct structure, components, and documentation.
- Configuration audits can be performed at different levels of granularity, such as software components, subsystems, or the entire system. Configuration audits can also be performed at different stages of the software development process, such as design, coding, testing, or deployment.
- Configuration audits can provide several benefits, such as  :
  - Improving the quality and reliability of the software product by detecting and correcting errors, inconsistencies, and deviations.
  - Enhancing the visibility and transparency of the software development process by providing objective and verifiable evidence of the software product's status and progress.
  - Facilitating the communication and coordination among the stakeholders involved in the software development process by establishing a common understanding and agreement on the software product's configuration.
  - Supporting the change management and risk management activities by identifying and documenting the impacts and dependencies of the changes made to the software product's configuration.
  - Enabling the reuse and maintenance of the software product by providing a clear and accurate record of the software product's configuration history and evolution.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the types of configuration audits is **F**unctional **P**erformance **C**onsistency **A**udit, which can be abbreviated as FPCA. The first two letters of each word correspond to the types of configuration audits (FCA and PCA), and the last two letters of each word correspond to the aspects of the software product that are verified by the configuration audits (functionality, performance, and consistency).
