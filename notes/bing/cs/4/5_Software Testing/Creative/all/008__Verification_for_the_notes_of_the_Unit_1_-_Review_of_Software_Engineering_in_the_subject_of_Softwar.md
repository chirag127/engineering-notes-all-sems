### Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Verification is the process of checking or verifying the credentials, data or information to confirm their credibility and accuracy.
- In the field of software engineering, software verification is defined as the process of evaluating software product, to ensure that the development phase is being carried out accurately, to build the desired software product.
- Verification is the process of checking that a software achieves its goal without any bugs. It is the process to ensure whether the product that is developed is right or not. It verifies whether the developed product fulfills the requirements that we have.
- Verification is a static testing technique, which means that it does not involve executing the code. Verification operations include reviews, walk-throughs, and inspections.
- Verification is also known as quality control, which checks the quality of the software product during the development process.
- Verification answers the question: "Are we building the product right?".

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for verification are:

- Verification can be remembered as **V**erifying the **V**alidity of the software product.
- Verification can be associated with the word **Verify**, which means to check or confirm something.
- Verification can be related to the acronym **V**&**V**, which stands for verification and validation, two complementary processes in software testing.
- Verification can be contrasted with validation, which is the process of checking that the software meets the user's needs and expectations. Verification is done before validation, and validation is done after verification.
- Verification can be visualized as a process of checking the blueprint of a building, while validation is checking the actual building. Verification ensures that the blueprint is correct, while validation ensures that the building is functional and usable.
