### SRS Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- SRS stands for Software Requirement Specification, which is a document that describes the functional and non-functional requirements of a software system.
- SRS verification is the process of manually examining or reviewing the SRS document to ensure that it is complete, consistent, correct, unambiguous, testable, and traceable  .
- SRS verification is important because it can help to avoid faults in the software design and development, as well as to ensure that the software meets the expectations and needs of the customers and stakeholders .
- SRS verification can be done by using different methods, such as peer reviews, walkthroughs, inspections, checklists, etc  .
- Peer reviews are informal methods of verification, where a group of peers or colleagues review the SRS document and provide feedback and suggestions for improvement .
- Walkthroughs are semi-formal methods of verification, where the author of the SRS document presents and explains it to a group of reviewers, who ask questions and identify issues or errors .
- Inspections are formal methods of verification, where a group of trained inspectors follow a predefined process and use a checklist to systematically examine the SRS document and report any defects or deviations from the standards  .
- Checklists are tools that can help to verify the quality and completeness of the SRS document, by providing a list of questions or criteria that the SRS document should satisfy.
- Some examples of checklist items are:

  - Is the SRS document clear and concise?
  - Does the SRS document cover all the functional and non-functional requirements of the software system?
  - Are the requirements stated in measurable and testable terms?
  - Are the requirements consistent and compatible with each other and with the system environment?
  - Are the requirements traceable to the source of origin and to the design and test documents?
  - Are the requirements prioritized and categorized according to their importance and dependencies?
  - Are the requirements validated by the customers and stakeholders?
  - Are the assumptions, constraints, risks, and dependencies of the software system identified and documented?
  - Are the terms and acronyms used in the SRS document defined and explained?
  - Are the diagrams, tables, and figures used in the SRS document clear and accurate?
  - Are the references and sources used in the SRS document cited and acknowledged?

- SRS verification can be done at different stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC), such as before the design phase, after the design phase, or before the testing phase .
- SRS verification can be done by different roles or persons, such as the SRS document author, the project manager, the software developer, the software tester, the customer, or the stakeholder .
- SRS verification can be done by using different techniques, such as reading, questioning, cross-checking, comparing, etc.
- SRS verification can be done by using different tools, such as software tools, paper tools, or mental tools.
- SRS verification can be done by using different strategies, such as top-down, bottom-up, or hybrid.
- SRS verification can be done by using different standards, such as IEEE, ISO, CMMI, etc.

- A mnemonic to remember the quality characteristics of a good SRS document is **C3UT2**:

  - **C**omplete: The SRS document should cover all the requirements of the software system and not leave out any essential information.
  - **C**onsistent: The SRS document should not have any conflicting or contradictory requirements or statements.
  - **C**orrect: The SRS document should state the true and accurate requirements of the software system and not have any errors or mistakes.
