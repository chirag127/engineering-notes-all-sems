### SDLC for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- SDLC stands for Software Development Life Cycle. It is a process used by the software industry to design, develop and test high-quality software .
- The SDLC aims to produce a software that meets or exceeds customer expectations, reaches completion within time and cost estimates, and follows the standards and requirements of the software engineering discipline .
- The SDLC consists of several phases, each with its own activities, deliverables, and outcomes. The phases may vary depending on the type of software, the development model, and the organization's preferences  .
- The common phases of the SDLC are  :
  - Requirement analysis: This phase involves gathering and analyzing the needs and expectations of the stakeholders, such as the customers, users, developers, and managers. The output of this phase is a document that specifies the functional and non-functional requirements of the software  .
  - Planning: This phase involves defining the scope, objectives, schedule, budget, resources, risks, and quality standards of the software project. The output of this phase is a project plan that guides the development team throughout the SDLC  .
  - Software design: This phase involves creating the architectural and detailed design of the software, such as the modules, components, interfaces, data structures, algorithms, and protocols. The output of this phase is a design document that describes how the software will be implemented  .
  - Implementation: This phase involves coding, testing, debugging, and integrating the software components according to the design document. The output of this phase is a software product that is ready for testing  .
  - Testing: This phase involves verifying and validating the software product against the requirements and specifications. The testing activities include unit testing, integration testing, system testing, acceptance testing, and regression testing. The output of this phase is a software product that meets the quality criteria and satisfies the stakeholders  .
  - Deployment: This phase involves installing, configuring, and launching the software product in the target environment, such as the customer's site, the cloud, or the app store. The output of this phase is a software product that is available for use  .
  - Maintenance: This phase involves providing support, updates, enhancements, and bug fixes for the software product after its deployment. The output of this phase is a software product that remains functional, reliable, and secure over time  .
- The SDLC is not a linear or sequential process, but rather an iterative and incremental process that allows for feedback, changes, and improvements throughout the development cycle  .
- The SDLC can be implemented using different models, such as the waterfall model, the agile model, the spiral model, the V-model, the prototyping model, and the RAD model. Each model has its own advantages, disadvantages, and suitability for different types of software projects  .
- The SDLC is important for software testing because it ensures that the software product is tested at every stage of the development, from the requirements to the deployment. Testing helps to identify and correct errors, defects, and bugs in the software, and to ensure that the software meets the quality standards and the customer's needs  .
- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the SDLC are:
  - To remember the phases of the SDLC, use the acronym RAPID Testing: Requirement analysis, Planning, Implementation, Deployment, Testing.
  - To remember the types of testing in the testing phase, use the acronym U IS A RAT: Unit testing, Integration testing, System testing, Acceptance testing, Regression testing.
  - To remember the types of SDLC models, use the acronym WASP VR: Waterfall, Agile, Spiral, Prototyping, V-model, RAD.
