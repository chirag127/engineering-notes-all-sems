### Priority category for the notes of the Unit 4 - Regression Testing in the subject of Software Testing

- Regression testing is the process of retesting the modified parts of a software system and ensuring that no new defects have been introduced due to the changes.
- Regression testing can be classified into three priority categories based on the impact and likelihood of the changes affecting the existing functionality: high, medium and low.
- High priority regression testing is performed when the changes are critical or complex, and have a high risk of causing severe defects in the system. For example, changing the database structure, adding a new feature, or fixing a major bug.
- Medium priority regression testing is performed when the changes are moderate or simple, and have a medium risk of causing minor defects in the system. For example, changing the user interface, updating the documentation, or fixing a minor bug.
- Low priority regression testing is performed when the changes are trivial or cosmetic, and have a low risk of causing negligible defects in the system. For example, changing the color scheme, correcting the spelling, or fixing a typo.
- A mnemonic to remember the priority categories of regression testing is: **HML** (High, Medium, Low).
- A learning trick to understand the priority categories of regression testing is: **Think of the changes as a traffic light**. 
  - If the changes are red, they are high priority and need to be tested immediately and thoroughly, as they can cause major accidents or delays in the system. 
  - If the changes are yellow, they are medium priority and need to be tested soon and carefully, as they can cause minor incidents or slowdowns in the system. 
  - If the changes are green, they are low priority and need to be tested later and briefly, as they can cause negligible issues or improvements in the system.
