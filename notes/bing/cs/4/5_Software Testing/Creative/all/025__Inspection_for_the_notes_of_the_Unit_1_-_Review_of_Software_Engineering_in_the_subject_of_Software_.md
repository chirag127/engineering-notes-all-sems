### Inspection for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Inspection is a **peer review** technique that involves **examining** the software work products (such as requirements, design, code, test cases, etc.) to **detect and correct defects** .
- Inspection is a **static analysis** technique, which means it does not require executing the software or using any tools.
- Inspection is a **formal** and **structured** process that follows a well-defined procedure and roles . The main roles are:
  - **Author**: the person who created the work product to be inspected.
  - **Moderator**: the person who leads and facilitates the inspection process.
  - **Reviewer**: the person who examines the work product and identifies defects.
  - **Recorder**: the person who documents the defects and the inspection results.
- Inspection is a **cost-effective** and **quality-improving** technique that can help to find and fix defects early in the software development life cycle, before they become more expensive and difficult to correct.
- Inspection can also help to **improve communication** and **knowledge sharing** among the software development team members, as well as to **enforce standards** and **promote best practices**.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for inspection are:

- **I**nspection is a peer review technique that **I**nvolves examining the software work products.
- **S**tatic analysis means no execution or tools are required for **S**oftware inspection.
- **F**ormal and structured process with well-defined roles and procedure is the **F**eature of inspection.
- **C**ost-effective, quality-improving, communication-enhancing, standard-enforcing and best-promoting are the **C**haracteristics of inspection.
