### Part-of-Speech Tagging

- Part-of-speech (POS) tagging is the task of assigning a word or token in a text a grammatical category, such as noun, verb, adjective, adverb, etc.
- POS tagging is useful for many natural language processing applications, such as syntactic parsing, semantic analysis, information extraction, machine translation, text summarization, etc.
- POS tagging can be done at different levels of granularity, depending on the number and types of tags used. For example, a coarse-grained tagset may have only four tags: N (noun), V (verb), A (adjective), and R (adverb). A fine-grained tagset may have more than 40 tags, such as NN (singular noun), NNS (plural noun), VB (base form verb), VBD (past tense verb), JJ (adjective), RB (adverb), etc.
- POS tagging can be done using different methods, such as rule-based, statistical, or neural network-based. Rule-based methods use hand-crafted rules that rely on morphological, syntactic, and lexical cues to assign tags. Statistical methods use probabilistic models that learn from annotated corpora to assign tags. Neural network-based methods use deep learning architectures that learn from annotated corpora to assign tags.
- POS tagging can be evaluated using different metrics, such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Accuracy is the percentage of words that are correctly tagged. Precision is the percentage of words that are correctly tagged as a given tag. Recall is the percentage of words that are correctly tagged as a given tag out of all the words that should have that tag. F1-score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall.
