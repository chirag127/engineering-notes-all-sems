### Requirements for representation

- In natural language processing, semantics and pragmatics are two aspects of meaning that need to be represented and processed by computational models.
- Semantics is the study of the literal meaning of words and sentences, while pragmatics is the study of how context and situation affect meaning.
- To represent and process semantics and pragmatics, natural language processing systems need to have some requirements for representation, such as:
  - Expressiveness: the ability to capture the various types and levels of meaning in natural language, such as lexical, syntactic, semantic, pragmatic, and discourse meaning.
  - Formality: the ability to define the representation in a precise and unambiguous way, using a well-defined syntax and semantics, such as logic, algebra, or graph theory.
  - Computability: the ability to manipulate and reason with the representation using efficient and effective algorithms and data structures, such as parsing, inference, or search.
  - Interoperability: the ability to communicate and exchange the representation with other systems and components, using common standards and formats, such as XML, RDF, or JSON.
  - Learnability: the ability to acquire and update the representation from data and feedback, using machine learning and statistical methods, such as induction, classification, or clustering.
