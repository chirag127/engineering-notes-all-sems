### Word Senses

- Word senses are the different meanings that a word can have in different contexts or usages.
- For example, the word "bank" can have different senses depending on whether it is used as a noun or a verb, and whether it refers to a financial institution, a river shore, or a slope of land.
- Word senses are important for natural language processing because they affect the interpretation and understanding of natural language texts and utterances.
- Word sense disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying the correct sense of a word in a given context, using linguistic and/or external knowledge sources.
- WSD can be performed at different levels of granularity, such as coarse-grained (e.g., distinguishing between noun and verb senses) or fine-grained (e.g., distinguishing between specific senses within a part of speech).
- WSD can also be performed for different types of words, such as content words (e.g., nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) or function words (e.g., prepositions, conjunctions, determiners, pronouns).
- WSD can be applied to various natural language processing applications, such as machine translation, information retrieval, information extraction, text summarization, question answering, and sentiment analysis.
