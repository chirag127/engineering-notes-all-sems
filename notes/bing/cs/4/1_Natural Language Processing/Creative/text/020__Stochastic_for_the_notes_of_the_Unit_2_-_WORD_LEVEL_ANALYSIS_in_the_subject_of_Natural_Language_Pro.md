### Stochastic

- Stochastic is a term that refers to the randomness or uncertainty of a phenomenon or a process.
- In natural language processing, stochastic methods are often used to model the variability and unpredictability of natural languages.
- Stochastic methods can capture the statistical patterns and regularities of natural languages, as well as account for the exceptions and variations that occur in real data.
- Some examples of stochastic methods in natural language processing are:

  - **Stochastic grammars**: These are grammars that assign probabilities to the rules or productions of a formal grammar, such as a context-free grammar or a regular grammar. Stochastic grammars can be used to generate or parse natural language sentences based on their likelihood or plausibility.
  - **Stochastic finite-state automata**: These are finite-state automata that have probabilistic transitions between states. Stochastic finite-state automata can be used to model the morphology or phonology of natural languages, as well as to recognize or generate words or sequences of symbols.
  - **Stochastic language models**: These are models that assign probabilities to sequences of words or other units of natural languages, such as n-grams, sentences, or documents. Stochastic language models can be used to estimate the probability of a given text or to generate text that is coherent and fluent.
  - **Stochastic parsing**: This is the process of finding the most probable syntactic structure or parse tree for a given natural language sentence, based on a stochastic grammar and a stochastic language model. Stochastic parsing can be used to analyze the syntax or semantics of natural language sentences, as well as to resolve ambiguities or uncertainties that arise in natural language processing.
