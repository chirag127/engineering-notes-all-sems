## Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS

- Syntactic analysis is the process of analyzing the structure and meaning of natural language sentences or phrases.
- Syntactic analysis involves identifying the syntactic categories and functions of the words and phrases in a sentence, and determining how they are related to each other.
- Syntactic analysis can be performed at different levels of granularity, such as morphological, lexical, phrasal, and sentential.
- Syntactic analysis can be based on different formalisms or models, such as constituency, dependency, or feature structures.
- Syntactic analysis can be used for various applications, such as parsing, translation, summarization, question answering, and natural language generation.

Some key concepts and terms in syntactic analysis are:

- **Syntax**: The set of rules and principles that govern the structure and composition of sentences in a language.
- **Grammar**: A formal system that specifies the syntax of a language, usually consisting of a lexicon and a set of rules or constraints.
- **Lexicon**: A collection of words and their properties, such as part-of-speech, inflection, and meaning.
- **Part-of-speech (POS)**: A syntactic category that classifies words based on their function and form, such as noun, verb, adjective, or preposition.
- **Morphology**: The study of the internal structure and formation of words, such as affixes, stems, and roots.
- **Morpheme**: The smallest meaningful unit of a word, such as a prefix, suffix, or root.
- **Phrase**: A group of words that function as a syntactic unit, such as a noun phrase, verb phrase, or prepositional phrase.
- **Clause**: A group of words that contains a subject and a predicate, and can function as a sentence or a part of a sentence, such as a main clause, subordinate clause, or relative clause.
- **Sentence**: A group of words that expresses a complete thought, usually consisting of one or more clauses, and ending with a punctuation mark, such as a period, question mark, or exclamation mark.
- **Constituent**: A word or phrase that forms a syntactic unit within a larger structure, such as a sentence or a clause.
- **Head**: The word that determines the syntactic category and function of a phrase, such as a noun in a noun phrase, or a verb in a verb phrase.
- **Modifier**: A word or phrase that modifies or qualifies the meaning of another word or phrase, such as an adjective in a noun phrase, or an adverb in a verb phrase.
- **Argument**: A word or phrase that is required by the meaning and syntax of another word or phrase, such as a subject or object of a verb, or a complement of a preposition.
- **Adjunct**: A word or phrase that is optional and adds extra information to another word or phrase, such as an adverbial modifier of a verb, or a modifier of a noun.
- **Relation**: A syntactic or semantic link between two words or phrases, such as subject, object, modifier, or complement.
- **Parse tree**: A graphical representation of the syntactic structure and relations of a sentence, using nodes and branches to indicate constituents and their hierarchical organization.
- **Dependency graph**: A graphical representation of the syntactic structure and relations of a sentence, using nodes and arcs to indicate words and their dependencies or dependencies.
- **Feature structure**: A set of attribute-value pairs that encodes the syntactic and semantic properties of a word or phrase, such as number, gender, case, tense, aspect, or mood.
