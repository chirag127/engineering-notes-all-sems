### Articulatory Phonetics

- Articulatory phonetics is the branch of phonetics that studies how speech sounds are produced by the human vocal tract .
- Articulatory phonetics is concerned with the movements and positions of the vocal organs (articulators), such as the tongue, lips, jaw, vocal cords, etc., and how they affect the airflow and the acoustic properties of speech sounds .
- Articulatory phonetics can be divided into two main categories: segmental and suprasegmental.
  - Segmental phonetics deals with the individual speech sounds (phonemes) and their features, such as voicing, place and manner of articulation, etc.
  - Suprasegmental phonetics deals with the features that span over multiple speech sounds, such as stress, intonation, tone, etc.
- Articulatory phonetics can be used to describe and classify the speech sounds of any language, as well as to compare and contrast the sound systems of different languages .
- Articulatory phonetics can also be applied to speech synthesis, speech recognition, speech pathology, language teaching, and forensic phonetics .
