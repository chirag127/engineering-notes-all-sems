### Word Similarity using Thesaurus and Distributional methods

- Word similarity is the degree to which two words share a common meaning or are semantically related.
- Word similarity is an important task in natural language processing, as it can help with various applications such as information retrieval, text summarization, machine translation, word sense disambiguation, and more.
- There are two main approaches to measure word similarity: thesaurus-based methods and distributional methods .
- Thesaurus-based methods rely on manually curated lexical resources, such as WordNet, Roget's Thesaurus, or BabelNet, that group words into synonym sets or semantic classes.
- Thesaurus-based methods can capture fine-grained semantic distinctions and relations between words, such as synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy, meronymy, etc.
- Thesaurus-based methods have some limitations, such as being incomplete, inconsistent, domain-specific, and language-dependent.
- Distributional methods are based on the distributional hypothesis, which states that words that occur in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings.
- Distributional methods use large corpora of text to learn vector representations of words, also known as word embeddings, that capture their semantic and syntactic features.
- Distributional methods can capture both similarity and relatedness between words, where similarity refers to the degree of interchangeability or substitutability, and relatedness refers to the degree of association or co-occurrence.
- Distributional methods have some advantages, such as being unsupervised, scalable, domain-independent, and language-independent.
- Distributional methods also have some challenges, such as dealing with polysemy, homonymy, synonymy, and antonymy.
- Both thesaurus-based and distributional methods have their strengths and weaknesses, and can be combined or specialized for different tasks and domains  .
