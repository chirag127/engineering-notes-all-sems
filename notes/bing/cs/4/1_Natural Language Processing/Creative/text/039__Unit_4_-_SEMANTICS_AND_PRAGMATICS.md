## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

- Semantics is the study of meaning in language, especially the relationship between words and sentences and the situations they refer to.
- Pragmatics is the study of how language is used in context, especially the relationship between speakers and hearers and the assumptions they make about each other.
- Some of the topics covered in this unit are:
  - Meaning and reference: how words and sentences relate to the world and the things they denote or describe.
  - Sense and reference: how words and sentences have both a literal meaning (sense) and a contextual meaning (reference) depending on how they are used.
  - Lexical semantics: how words have different types of meaning, such as synonyms, antonyms, hyponyms, meronyms, etc.
  - Compositional semantics: how the meaning of a sentence is derived from the meaning of its parts and the way they are combined.
  - Truth conditions: how the truth or falsity of a sentence depends on the state of the world and the facts it describes.
  - Entailment and presupposition: how some sentences imply or presuppose the truth of other sentences, and how this affects the interpretation and communication of meaning.
  - Speech acts: how utterances perform different types of actions, such as asserting, questioning, requesting, promising, etc.
  - Implicature and inference: how speakers imply or suggest more than what they literally say, and how hearers infer or deduce the intended meaning from the context and the cooperative principle.
  - Politeness and face: how speakers use language to show respect, deference, or solidarity with their hearers, and how they avoid or mitigate face-threatening acts that might damage their self-image or social relationships.
  - Discourse and conversation: how language is used to structure and manage interactions between speakers and hearers, and how they follow rules and conventions of turn-taking, topic management, coherence, and relevance.
