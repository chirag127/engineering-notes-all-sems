### Ambiguity

- Ambiguity is the property of a sentence or phrase that can have more than one meaning or interpretation.
- Ambiguity can arise at different levels of language analysis, such as lexical, syntactic, semantic, pragmatic, etc.
- Ambiguity can cause problems for natural language processing systems, as they need to resolve the ambiguity to understand the intended meaning of the user or the text.
- Ambiguity can also be a source of creativity and humor in natural language, as it allows for multiple interpretations and associations.

#### Lexical Ambiguity

- Lexical ambiguity occurs when a word or phrase has more than one possible meaning in a given context.
- Lexical ambiguity can be caused by homonyms, polysemes, idioms, metaphors, etc.
- For example, the word "bank" can mean a financial institution, a river shore, a verb meaning to tilt or turn, etc.
- Lexical ambiguity can be resolved by using contextual clues, word sense disambiguation techniques, or external knowledge sources.

#### Syntactic Ambiguity

- Syntactic ambiguity occurs when a sentence or phrase has more than one possible structure or parse tree in a given grammar.
- Syntactic ambiguity can be caused by ambiguous grammatical rules, punctuation, word order, etc.
- For example, the sentence "I saw the man with the telescope" can have two possible structures: one where the man has the telescope, and one where the speaker saw the man using the telescope.
- Syntactic ambiguity can be resolved by using syntactic rules, preferences, constraints, or probabilistic models.
