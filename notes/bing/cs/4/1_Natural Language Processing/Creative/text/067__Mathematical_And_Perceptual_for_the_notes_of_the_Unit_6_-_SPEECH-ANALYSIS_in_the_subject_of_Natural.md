### Mathematical And Perceptual

- Speech is a complex signal that conveys information at different levels, such as phonetic, lexical, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic.
- Speech analysis is the process of extracting relevant features from speech signals for various applications, such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, speaker identification, emotion recognition, etc.
- Speech analysis can be performed in different domains, such as time, frequency, cepstral, and perceptual.
- Time domain analysis involves processing the speech signal as a sequence of samples or frames, and applying techniques such as zero-crossing rate, short-time energy, pitch detection, etc.
- Frequency domain analysis involves transforming the speech signal into a spectrum or a spectrogram, and applying techniques such as Fourier transform, filter banks, mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), etc.
- Cepstral domain analysis involves transforming the speech signal into a cepstrum or a cepstrogram, and applying techniques such as inverse Fourier transform, liftering, delta and delta-delta features, etc.
- Perceptual domain analysis involves modeling the speech signal based on the characteristics of the human auditory system, and applying techniques such as perceptual linear prediction (PLP), linear predictive coding (LPC), perceptual weighting, etc.
- Perceptual domain analysis aims to capture the salient features of speech that are relevant for human perception and cognition, and to reduce the effects of noise, channel distortion, and speaker variability.
- Perceptual domain analysis can be divided into two steps: perceptual filtering and perceptual feature extraction.
- Perceptual filtering involves applying a psychoacoustic model to the speech signal, such as the Bark scale, the critical band rate scale, or the equivalent rectangular bandwidth scale, to simulate the frequency resolution and masking effects of the human ear.
- Perceptual feature extraction involves applying a linear prediction model to the perceptually filtered speech signal, such as PLP or LPC, to estimate the spectral envelope and the formant frequencies of speech.
- Perceptual features can be further enhanced by applying perceptual weighting, which emphasizes the regions of the spectrum that are more important for speech intelligibility, such as the formant peaks and the valleys between them.
