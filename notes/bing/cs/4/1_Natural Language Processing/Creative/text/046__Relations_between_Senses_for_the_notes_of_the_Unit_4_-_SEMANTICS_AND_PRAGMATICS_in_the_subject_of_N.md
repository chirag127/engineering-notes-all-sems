### Relations between Senses

- In natural language, words and expressions can have different meanings or senses depending on the context and usage.
- For example, the word "bank" can mean a financial institution, a river shore, or a verb meaning to tilt or turn.
- The relations between senses can be classified into different types, such as synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy, meronymy, polysemy, homonymy, and metonymy.
- Synonymy is the relation between words or expressions that have the same or very similar meanings, such as "big" and "large", "happy" and "glad", or "sofa" and "couch".
- Antonymy is the relation between words or expressions that have opposite or contrasting meanings, such as "hot" and "cold", "up" and "down", or "love" and "hate".
- Hyponymy is the relation between words or expressions that have a subset-superset relation, such as "dog" and "animal", "rose" and "flower", or "red" and "color".
- Meronymy is the relation between words or expressions that have a part-whole relation, such as "finger" and "hand", "wheel" and "car", or "chapter" and "book".
- Polysemy is the relation between words or expressions that have multiple related meanings or senses, such as "bank" meaning a financial institution or a river shore, "head" meaning a body part or a leader, or "star" meaning a celestial object or a famous person.
- Homonymy is the relation between words or expressions that have multiple unrelated meanings or senses, such as "bat" meaning a flying mammal or a wooden club, "pen" meaning a writing instrument or an enclosure for animals, or "date" meaning a fruit or a social event.
- Metonymy is the relation between words or expressions that have a contiguity or association relation, such as "the White House" meaning the US president or the executive branch, "Hollywood" meaning the US film industry or the celebrities, or "the crown" meaning the monarchy or the royal authority.
