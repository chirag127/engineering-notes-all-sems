### Acoustic Phonetics

- Acoustic phonetics is the branch of phonetics that studies the physical properties of speech sounds as they are transmitted through the air.
- Acoustic phonetics analyzes speech sounds in terms of their frequency, amplitude, and duration, as well as other acoustic features such as formants, harmonics, and spectral envelope.
- Acoustic phonetics uses various tools and methods to measure and visualize speech sounds, such as spectrograms, waveforms, pitch tracks, and intensity contours.
- Acoustic phonetics can be used to describe and classify speech sounds, to compare different accents and dialects, to identify speakers and languages, to recognize speech and emotions, and to synthesize speech.
- Acoustic phonetics is based on the source-filter theory of speech production, which assumes that speech sounds are generated by a source of sound (such as the vocal cords or the airflow) and modified by a filter (such as the vocal tract or the mouth).
- Acoustic phonetics can be divided into three main subfields: segmental, suprasegmental, and prosodic.

  - Segmental acoustic phonetics deals with the acoustic properties of individual speech sounds, such as vowels and consonants, and how they are distinguished by the human ear.
  - Suprasegmental acoustic phonetics deals with the acoustic properties of larger units of speech, such as syllables, words, and phrases, and how they are affected by stress, tone, and intonation.
  - Prosodic acoustic phonetics deals with the acoustic properties of speech that convey meaning and emotion, such as pitch, loudness, tempo, and rhythm.
