### Word Sense Disambiguation

- Word sense disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying the correct meaning of a word in a given context, when the word has multiple possible meanings (polysemy).
- WSD is important for natural language processing applications such as machine translation, information retrieval, text summarization, question answering, etc. that require semantic understanding of the input text.
- WSD can be formulated as a classification problem, where the possible senses of a word are the classes, and the context is the input. The output is the most probable sense for the word in the context.
- WSD can be performed at different levels of granularity, such as coarse-grained (e.g. distinguishing between noun and verb senses) or fine-grained (e.g. distinguishing between specific senses within a part of speech).
- WSD can be approached in different ways, such as:

  - Knowledge-based methods: These methods rely on external sources of knowledge, such as dictionaries, thesauri, ontologies, etc. to define the senses of a word and the relations between them. They use various techniques, such as word definitions, synonyms, antonyms, hypernyms, hyponyms, etc. to compare the word with its context and select the most appropriate sense. Examples of knowledge-based methods are Lesk algorithm, vector space model, etc.
  - Supervised methods: These methods use annotated corpora, where each word is labeled with its correct sense, as training data. They use machine learning algorithms, such as decision trees, naive Bayes, support vector machines, neural networks, etc. to learn a model that can predict the sense of a word given its context. Examples of supervised methods are Senseval, SemCor, etc.
  - Unsupervised methods: These methods do not use any external knowledge or annotated data. They use statistical techniques, such as clustering, latent semantic analysis, topic models, etc. to discover the senses of a word from a large corpus of unannotated text. They assume that words that occur in similar contexts have similar meanings. Examples of unsupervised methods are Yarowsky algorithm, word embeddings, etc.
