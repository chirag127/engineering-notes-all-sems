### Transducers for lexicon and rules

- A transducer is a device that converts one form of energy or signal into another.
- In natural language processing (NLP), a transducer is a model that maps an input sequence (such as a word, a sentence, or a speech signal) to an output sequence (such as a part-of-speech tag, a syntactic parse, or a phonetic transcription).
- A transducer can be deterministic or probabilistic, depending on whether it assigns a single output or a distribution over possible outputs for a given input.
- A transducer can be finite-state or context-free, depending on whether it can handle only regular or also context-free languages.
- A transducer can be weighted or unweighted, depending on whether it assigns a numerical score or a binary value to each input-output pair.
- A transducer can be composed of two or more smaller transducers, such that the output of one transducer becomes the input of another.
- A lexicon is a collection of words and their properties, such as pronunciation, meaning, and grammatical category.
- A lexicon can be represented as a transducer that maps a word to its properties, or as a set of transducers that map a word to different properties.
- A rule is a statement that specifies how to transform an input sequence into an output sequence, or how to assign a property to an input sequence.
- A rule can be represented as a transducer that maps an input sequence to an output sequence, or as a weight that assigns a score to an input sequence.
- A rule can be context-sensitive or context-free, depending on whether it applies only in certain environments or universally.
- A rule can be ordered or unordered, depending on whether it has a priority over other rules or not.
- A rule can be recursive or non-recursive, depending on whether it can apply to its own output or not.
