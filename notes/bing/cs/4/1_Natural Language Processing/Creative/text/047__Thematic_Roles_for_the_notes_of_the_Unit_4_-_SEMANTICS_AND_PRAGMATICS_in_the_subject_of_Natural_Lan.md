### Thematic Roles

- Thematic roles are the semantic roles that the arguments of a predicate play in a sentence.
- They describe the relationship between the predicate and its arguments in terms of their meaning and function.
- For example, in the sentence "John ate an apple", John is the agent (the one who performs the action), and an apple is the patient (the one who undergoes the action).
- Thematic roles are also called theta roles, case roles, or semantic roles.
- Some of the common thematic roles are:

  - Agent: the initiator or doer of an action (e.g., John in "John ate an apple")
  - Patient: the entity that is affected by an action (e.g., an apple in "John ate an apple")
  - Theme: the entity that is moved or changes location or state (e.g., the book in "Mary gave the book to John")
  - Experiencer: the entity that perceives or feels something (e.g., Mary in "Mary saw a bird")
  - Instrument: the entity that is used to perform an action (e.g., a knife in "John cut the cake with a knife")
  - Source: the entity from which something moves or originates (e.g., New York in "John flew from New York to London")
  - Goal: the entity to which something moves or is directed (e.g., London in "John flew from New York to London")
  - Location: the entity where something is or happens (e.g., the park in "Mary walked in the park")
  - Beneficiary: the entity for whom something is done or intended (e.g., John in "Mary baked a cake for John")
  - Possessor: the entity that owns or has something (e.g., John in "John's car is red")
  - Cause: the entity that causes or triggers an event (e.g., the storm in "The storm caused a flood")

- Thematic roles are assigned by the predicate to its arguments based on the syntactic structure and the semantic properties of the predicate and its arguments.
- Different predicates may assign different thematic roles to the same argument depending on the context and the meaning of the predicate.
- For example, in "John broke the window" and "The window broke", the window is the patient in the first sentence and the theme in the second sentence.
- Thematic roles help to capture the meaning and the structure of a sentence and to resolve ambiguities and anaphora.
- For example, in "John saw the man with the telescope", the thematic roles can help to determine whether John or the man had the telescope.
