## Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS

- Word level analysis is the process of identifying and describing the structure and meaning of words in a language.
- Words are composed of smaller units called **morphemes**, which are the smallest meaningful units of language. Morphemes can be classified into two types: **roots** and **affixes**.
- Roots are the core of a word, which carry the main meaning and cannot be further divided. For example, in the word "unhappy", the root is "happy".
- Affixes are the additional units that modify the meaning or function of a root. Affixes can be further divided into two types: **prefixes** and **suffixes**.
- Prefixes are the affixes that attach to the beginning of a root. For example, in the word "unhappy", the prefix is "un-", which means "not".
- Suffixes are the affixes that attach to the end of a root. For example, in the word "happiness", the suffix is "-ness", which means "state or quality of".
- Some words can have more than one affix, such as "unhappiness", which has both a prefix and a suffix.
- The combination of a root and affixes is called a **stem**, which can form the base of a word. For example, in the word "unhappily", the stem is "unhappy", which is modified by the suffix "-ly" to form an adverb.
- The process of adding affixes to a root or a stem is called **morphological derivation**, which can create new words with different meanings or grammatical categories. For example, the noun "friend" can be derived into the verb "befriend" by adding the prefix "be-".
- The process of changing the form of a word to indicate its grammatical function in a sentence is called **morphological inflection**, which does not create new words but rather modifies existing ones. For example, the verb "walk" can be inflected into "walks", "walked", or "walking" to indicate its number, tense, or aspect.
- Morphological derivation and inflection are two types of **word formation processes**, which are the ways that new words are created or existing words are modified in a language. Other types of word formation processes include **compounding**, **blending**, **clipping**, **acronymy**, **backformation**, and **conversion**.
- Compounding is the process of combining two or more words to form a new word, such as "bookshelf" or "blackboard".
- Blending is the process of combining parts of two or more words to form a new word, such as "brunch" or "smog".
- Clipping is the process of reducing a word to a shorter form, such as "ad" or "phone".
- Acronymy is the process of forming a word from the initial letters of a phrase, such as "NASA" or "LOL".
- Backformation is the process of creating a new word by removing an affix from an existing word, such as "edit" from "editor" or "babysit" from "babysitter".
- Conversion is the process of changing the grammatical category of a word without changing its form, such as "book" from a noun to a verb or "green" from an adjective to a noun.
