### Grammar-based LM

- A grammar-based language model (LM) is a type of probabilistic model that assigns probabilities to sentences or sequences of words based on a set of rules or constraints that define the structure and syntax of a language.
- A grammar-based LM can be seen as a generative model that produces sentences by applying rules from a grammar, such as a context-free grammar (CFG) or a tree-adjoining grammar (TAG).
- A grammar-based LM can also be seen as a discriminative model that evaluates the likelihood of a sentence given a grammar, by computing the probability of the parse tree or derivation that corresponds to the sentence.
- A grammar-based LM can capture long-distance dependencies and complex syntactic phenomena that are difficult to model with n-gram LMs, which rely on local and fixed-length contexts.
- A grammar-based LM can also incorporate semantic and pragmatic information into the grammar rules, such as lexical preferences, selectional restrictions, and discourse relations, to improve the quality and coherence of the generated or evaluated sentences.
- A grammar-based LM can be estimated from a corpus of sentences and their corresponding parse trees or derivations, by counting the occurrences of the grammar rules and applying smoothing techniques to deal with data sparsity and unseen events.
- A grammar-based LM can be used for various natural language processing (NLP) tasks, such as speech recognition, machine translation, text summarization, natural language generation, and natural language understanding.
