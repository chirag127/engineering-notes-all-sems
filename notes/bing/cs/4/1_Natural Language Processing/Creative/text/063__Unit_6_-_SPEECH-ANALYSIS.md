## Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS

- Speech-analysis is the process of examining and interpreting the acoustic features of speech signals, such as pitch, intensity, duration, and spectral properties.
- Speech-analysis can be used for various purposes, such as speech recognition, speaker identification, emotion detection, accent classification, speech synthesis, and speech enhancement.
- Speech-analysis can be performed using different methods, such as waveform analysis, spectrogram analysis, linear predictive coding, cepstral analysis, and formant analysis.
- Waveform analysis is the simplest method of speech-analysis, which involves visualizing the speech signal as a function of time and amplitude. Waveform analysis can reveal the temporal structure of speech, such as pauses, syllables, and stress patterns.
- Spectrogram analysis is a method of speech-analysis that displays the speech signal as a function of time and frequency, showing how the energy of the signal is distributed across different frequency bands. Spectrogram analysis can reveal the spectral structure of speech, such as vowels, consonants, and harmonics.
- Linear predictive coding (LPC) is a method of speech-analysis that models the speech signal as a linear combination of past samples, using a set of coefficients called the LPC parameters. LPC can capture the vocal tract characteristics of speech, such as formants and resonances.
- Cepstral analysis is a method of speech-analysis that transforms the speech signal from the frequency domain to the cepstral domain, using a logarithmic function and an inverse Fourier transform. Cepstral analysis can separate the source and filter components of speech, such as the glottal excitation and the vocal tract shape.
- Formant analysis is a method of speech-analysis that identifies and measures the frequency and bandwidth of the prominent peaks in the speech spectrum, called the formants. Formants are related to the resonances of the vocal tract, and can be used to distinguish different vowel sounds.
