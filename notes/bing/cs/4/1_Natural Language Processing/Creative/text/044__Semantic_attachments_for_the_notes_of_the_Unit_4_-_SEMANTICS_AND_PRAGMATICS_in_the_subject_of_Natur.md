### Semantic attachments

- Semantic attachments are **rules** that associate **semantic representations** with **syntactic structures** in a natural language.
- Semantic attachments are used to **generate** or **interpret** the meaning of natural language sentences, based on their syntactic structure and lexical items.
- Semantic attachments can be **manual** or **automatic**, depending on how they are created and applied.
- Manual semantic attachments are **hand-crafted** by human experts, based on linguistic knowledge and domain knowledge. They are often used in **rule-based** natural language processing systems, such as parsers, generators, or translators.
- Automatic semantic attachments are **learned** from data, using machine learning techniques, such as supervised learning, unsupervised learning, or reinforcement learning. They are often used in **data-driven** natural language processing systems, such as neural networks, probabilistic models, or evolutionary algorithms.
- Semantic attachments can be **symbolic** or **subsymbolic**, depending on how they represent the meaning of natural language sentences.
- Symbolic semantic attachments use **logical forms**, such as first-order logic, lambda calculus, or semantic networks, to represent the meaning of natural language sentences. They are often used in **symbolic** natural language processing systems, such as logic-based systems, knowledge-based systems, or ontologies.
- Subsymbolic semantic attachments use **numerical vectors**, such as word embeddings, sentence embeddings, or neural activations, to represent the meaning of natural language sentences. They are often used in **subsymbolic** natural language processing systems, such as neural networks, vector space models, or distributional semantics.
