### LPC

- LPC stands for Linear Predictive Coding, which is a method of speech analysis and synthesis.
- LPC is based on the assumption that a speech signal can be modeled as the output of a linear system driven by an excitation signal.
- The linear system consists of an all-pole filter that represents the vocal tract, and the excitation signal can be either a periodic pulse train for voiced speech or a white noise for unvoiced speech.
- The goal of LPC is to estimate the filter coefficients and the excitation signal from the speech signal, and use them to reconstruct the speech signal or modify it for speech synthesis or coding purposes.
- The main steps of LPC are:

  - Pre-emphasis: This is a high-pass filtering operation that boosts the high-frequency components of the speech signal and reduces the dynamic range of the signal. This helps to improve the accuracy of the filter estimation and reduce the effects of noise and quantization errors.
  - Windowing: This is a process of dividing the speech signal into short segments or frames, typically of 10 to 30 ms duration, and applying a window function to each frame. The window function reduces the discontinuities at the frame boundaries and improves the spectral analysis of the signal. Common window functions are Hamming, Hanning, or Blackman windows.
  - Autocorrelation: This is a method of computing the correlation between the speech signal and its delayed versions. The autocorrelation function measures the similarity of the signal with itself as a function of the delay. The autocorrelation function is used to estimate the filter coefficients and the pitch period of the speech signal.
  - LPC analysis: This is the process of finding the filter coefficients that minimize the prediction error, which is the difference between the speech signal and the output of the linear system. The filter coefficients can be obtained by solving a system of linear equations derived from the autocorrelation function. The filter coefficients are also called the LPC coefficients or the reflection coefficients, and they represent the spectral envelope of the speech signal.
  - LPC synthesis: This is the process of generating a speech signal from the filter coefficients and the excitation signal. The excitation signal can be either the original one extracted from the speech signal, or a synthetic one generated from the pitch period and the voicing information. The speech signal is reconstructed by passing the excitation signal through the inverse of the linear system represented by the filter coefficients.
