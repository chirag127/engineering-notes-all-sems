### Grammar rules for English for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Grammar rules are the conventions that govern how words and phrases are arranged to form sentences in a language.
- Grammar rules can be divided into two categories: prescriptive and descriptive.
  - Prescriptive rules are the norms and standards that prescribe how a language should be used, such as avoiding split infinitives or ending sentences with prepositions.
  - Descriptive rules are the observations and generalizations that describe how a language is actually used, such as the fact that some speakers use double negatives or drop the subject in informal speech.
- Grammar rules can also be classified into different levels of analysis, such as morphology, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics.
  - Morphology is the study of the structure and formation of words, such as the rules for adding prefixes and suffixes or forming compound words.
  - Syntax is the study of the structure and formation of phrases and sentences, such as the rules for word order, agreement, and subordination.
  - Semantics is the study of the meaning and interpretation of words, phrases, and sentences, such as the rules for ambiguity, synonymy, and entailment.
  - Pragmatics is the study of the use and function of language in context, such as the rules for politeness, relevance, and implicature.
- Grammar rules can be represented using different formalisms and notations, such as phrase structure rules, dependency rules, or feature structures.
  - Phrase structure rules are the rules that specify how words can be grouped into constituents, such as noun phrases or verb phrases, and how these constituents can be combined to form sentences. For example, the rule S -> NP VP states that a sentence (S) consists of a noun phrase (NP) followed by a verb phrase (VP).
  - Dependency rules are the rules that specify how words depend on each other to form syntactic relations, such as subject, object, or modifier. For example, the rule NP -> N (mod) states that a noun phrase (NP) consists of a noun (N) and optionally a modifier (mod).
  - Feature structures are the rules that specify how words and phrases have certain properties or features, such as number, gender, or tense, and how these features constrain or agree with each other. For example, the rule NP[num=?n] -> Det[num=?n] N[num=?n] states that a noun phrase (NP) has a number feature (num) that matches the number feature of its determiner (Det) and noun (N).
- Grammar rules can be used for different purposes and applications, such as parsing, generation, or translation.
  - Parsing is the process of analyzing a given sentence and assigning a grammatical structure and meaning to it, such as a phrase structure tree or a dependency graph.
  - Generation is the process of producing a sentence that conforms to a given grammatical structure and meaning, such as a feature structure or a logical form.
  - Translation is the process of converting a sentence from one language to another while preserving its grammatical structure and meaning, such as using a bilingual grammar or a transfer rule.
