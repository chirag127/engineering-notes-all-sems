### Syntactic Parsing for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Syntactic parsing is the process of analyzing the strings of symbols in natural language conforming to the rules of formal grammar.
- Syntactic parsing assigns a semantic structure to text, such as a constituent or dependency tree, that represents the syntactic relations between words and phrases  .
- Syntactic parsing is also known as syntax analysis or parsing .
- Syntactic parsing is one of the important tasks in computational linguistics and natural language processing, and has been a subject of research since the mid-20th century with the advent of computers.
- Different theories of grammar propose different formalisms for describing the syntactic structure of sentences, such as context-free grammars, dependency grammars, lexical-functional grammars, etc.
- Syntactic parsing can be performed using various methods, such as top-down, bottom-up, chart-based, probabilistic, neural, etc .
- Syntactic parsing can be supervised, semi-supervised, or unsupervised, depending on the availability and quality of annotated data.
- Syntactic parsing can be useful for downstream tasks such as semantic parsing, relation extraction, machine translation, sentiment analysis, etc .

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for syntactic parsing are:

- Remember the acronym **SAP** for Syntactic Analysis or Parsing.
- Remember the phrase **"Syntax is the tree of meaning"** to recall that syntactic parsing assigns a semantic structure to text.
- Remember the analogy **"Syntactic parsing is like solving a puzzle"** to understand that syntactic parsing involves finding the best way to fit the words and phrases together according to the rules of grammar.
- Remember the rhyme **"Different grammars, different parsers, different tasks, different answers"** to remember that syntactic parsing can vary depending on the choice of grammar, parsing method, application, and evaluation.
