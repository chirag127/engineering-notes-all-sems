## Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing

Speech processing is the study of how humans produce, perceive, and understand speech, as well as how machines can recognize, synthesize, and manipulate speech signals. Speech processing involves three major levels of processing: production, perception, and analysis.

- Speech production is the process of generating speech sounds by the vocal tract and the lungs. Speech production involves three stages: conceptualization, formulation, and articulation. Conceptualization is the stage where the speaker decides what to say and selects the appropriate words and structures. Formulation is the stage where the speaker converts the words into a sequence of speech sounds, or phonemes, and assigns them stress and intonation patterns. Articulation is the stage where the speaker produces the speech sounds by coordinating the movements of the vocal cords, the tongue, the lips, and the jaw .

- Speech perception is the process of interpreting speech sounds by the auditory system and the brain. Speech perception involves three stages: segmentation, categorization, and comprehension. Segmentation is the stage where the listener divides the continuous speech signal into discrete units, such as syllables, words, and phrases. Categorization is the stage where the listener identifies the speech sounds and assigns them to phonetic categories, such as vowels, consonants, and tones. Comprehension is the stage where the listener extracts the meaning and the intention of the speaker from the speech signal.

- Speech analysis is the process of extracting information from speech signals by mathematical and computational methods. Speech analysis involves three stages: feature extraction, pattern comparison, and decision making. Feature extraction is the stage where the speech signal is transformed into a set of numerical values, or features, that represent its acoustic properties, such as frequency, amplitude, and duration. Pattern comparison is the stage where the features are compared with a set of reference patterns, or models, that represent the speech sounds or words of a given language or speaker. Decision making is the stage where the best matching pattern is selected and the corresponding speech sound or word is recognized .

Some of the applications of speech processing are:

- Speech recognition: the process of converting speech signals into text or commands.
- Speech synthesis: the process of converting text or commands into speech signals.
- Speech enhancement: the process of improving the quality of speech signals by reducing noise, distortion, or reverberation.
- Speech coding: the process of compressing speech signals for efficient transmission or storage.
- Speech translation: the process of converting speech signals from one language to another.
- Speaker recognition: the process of identifying or verifying the identity of a speaker from speech signals.
- Speech emotion recognition: the process of detecting or classifying the emotional state of a speaker from speech signals.
