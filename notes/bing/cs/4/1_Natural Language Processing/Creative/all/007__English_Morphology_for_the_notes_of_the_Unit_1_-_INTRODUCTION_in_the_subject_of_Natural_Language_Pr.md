### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Morphology is the branch of linguistics that studies the internal structure and formation of words in a language.
- Words are composed of smaller units called morphemes, which are the smallest meaningful units of language .
- Morphemes can be classified into two types: free and bound .
  - Free morphemes can stand alone as words, such as cat, dog, happy, etc.
  - Bound morphemes cannot stand alone as words, but must be attached to other morphemes, such as -s, -ed, -ing, un-, etc.
- Morphemes can also be classified into two types: roots and affixes .
  - Roots are the core of the word, carrying the main meaning and lexical category, such as cat, dog, happy, etc.
  - Affixes are the modifiers of the word, changing the meaning or grammatical function of the root, such as -s, -ed, -ing, un-, etc.
- Affixes can be further classified into four types: prefixes, suffixes, infixes and circumfixes .
  - Prefixes are attached to the beginning of the root, such as un-happy, re-do, etc.
  - Suffixes are attached to the end of the root, such as happy-ness, dog-s, etc.
  - Infixes are inserted into the middle of the root, such as fan-freaking-tastic, abso-bloody-lutely, etc. (Note: infixes are rare in English, but more common in other languages)
  - Circumfixes are attached to both the beginning and the end of the root, such as a-live, en-light-en, etc. (Note: circumfixes are also rare in English, but more common in other languages)
- The process of combining morphemes to form words is called word formation .
- There are four main types of word formation processes in English: derivation, compounding, conversion and blending .
  - Derivation is the process of adding affixes to a root to create a new word, such as happy-ness, un-happy, etc.
  - Compounding is the process of combining two or more roots to create a new word, such as dog-house, black-board, etc.
  - Conversion is the process of changing the lexical category of a word without changing its form, such as love (noun) -> love (verb), bottle (noun) -> bottle (verb), etc.
  - Blending is the process of combining parts of two words to create a new word, such as brunch (breakfast + lunch), smog (smoke + fog), etc.
- The study of morphology has implications for natural language processing, such as:
  - Morphological analysis: the task of identifying and segmenting the morphemes in a word, such as un-happy-ness -> un- + happy + -ness
  - Morphological generation: the task of producing a word from its morphemes, such as un- + happy + -ness -> un-happiness
  - Morphological parsing: the task of assigning a morphological structure to a word, such as un-happiness -> [un-[happy-ness]]
  - Morphological inflection: the task of producing the correct form of a word according to its grammatical context, such as dog (singular) -> dogs (plural), walk (present) -> walked (past), etc.
  - Morphological normalization: the task of reducing the variation in word forms to a standard form, such as dogs -> dog, walked -> walk, etc.
  - Morphological stemming: the task of removing the affixes from a word to obtain its root, such as un-happiness -> happy, dogs -> dog, etc.
  - Morphological lemmatization: the task of finding the lemma (dictionary form) of a word, such as un-happiness -> happiness, dogs -> dog, etc.

- To remember the types of morphemes, think of the acronym FABRIC: Free, Affix, Bound, Root, Infix, Circumfix.
