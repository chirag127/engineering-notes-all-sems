### Word Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Word senses are the different meanings that a word can have in different contexts or situations.
- For example, the word "bank" can have different senses depending on whether it is used to refer to a financial institution, a river shore, or a verb meaning to tilt or turn.
- Word sense disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying the correct sense of a word in a given context, using linguistic and/or external knowledge sources.
- WSD is important for natural language processing applications such as machine translation, information retrieval, text summarization, question answering, etc.
- There are different types of word senses, such as:
  - Lexical senses: the basic meanings of a word that are listed in a dictionary or a lexicon. For example, the word "bat" has two lexical senses: a flying mammal or a wooden club.
  - Metaphorical senses: the meanings of a word that are derived from a figurative or non-literal use of the word. For example, the word "bat" can have a metaphorical sense of a nocturnal person or a crazy person.
  - Idiomatic senses: the meanings of a word or a phrase that are specific to a language or a culture and cannot be inferred from the literal meanings of the individual words. For example, the phrase "bat an eye" has an idiomatic sense of showing no surprise or concern.
- There are different methods for representing and organizing word senses, such as:
  - Sense inventories: predefined sets of word senses that are created by lexicographers or linguists and are used as reference sources for WSD. For example, WordNet is a popular sense inventory that groups words into sets of synonyms called synsets and defines semantic relations among them.
  - Sense embeddings: numerical vectors that capture the semantic features of word senses and are learned from large corpora of text using machine learning techniques. For example, ELMo and BERT are models that generate sense embeddings for words in context.
  - Sense clusters: groups of word senses that are discovered from data using unsupervised or semi-supervised learning methods. For example, topic models and word sense induction algorithms can generate sense clusters based on the co-occurrence patterns of words in text.
- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for word senses are:
  - To remember the difference between lexical, metaphorical, and idiomatic senses, think of the acronym LMI: Lexical senses are Literal, Metaphorical senses are Figurative, and Idiomatic senses are Cultural.
  - To remember the difference between sense inventories, sense embeddings, and sense clusters, think of the acronym IEC: Sense Inventories are predefined and Explicit, Sense Embeddings are learned and Contextual, and Sense Clusters are discovered and Unsupervised.
  - To remember some examples of sense inventories, think of the acronym WOOF: WordNet, OntoNotes, Oxford English Dictionary, and FrameNet.
