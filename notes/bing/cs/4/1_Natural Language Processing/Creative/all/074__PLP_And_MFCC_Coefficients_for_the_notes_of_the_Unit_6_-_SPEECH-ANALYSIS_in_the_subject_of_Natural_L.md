### PLP And MFCC Coefficients for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- PLP (Perceptual Linear Prediction) and MFCC (Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients) are two commonly used feature extraction methods for speech recognition and analysis.
- Both methods aim to capture the spectral characteristics of the speech signal, but they differ in the way they model the human auditory system and the processing steps involved.
- PLP was proposed by Hynek Hermansky as a way to incorporate the perceptual aspects of speech into the feature extraction process. It is based on the assumption that the human ear acts as a filter bank that emphasizes certain frequency bands and suppresses others, depending on the loudness and pitch of the sound.
- MFCC was proposed by Steven Davis and Paul Mermelstein as a way to represent the speech signal in a compact and robust way. It is based on the assumption that the human ear perceives the frequency spectrum of the sound on a nonlinear scale, called the mel scale, that approximates the human perception of pitch.
- The main steps involved in PLP and MFCC feature extraction are:

  - Pre-emphasis: A high-pass filter is applied to the speech signal to boost the high-frequency components and reduce the effect of noise.
  - Framing and windowing: The speech signal is divided into short overlapping frames, typically 20-30 ms long, and each frame is multiplied by a window function, such as a Hamming window, to reduce the discontinuities at the edges.
  - Fourier transform: The discrete Fourier transform (DFT) is applied to each frame to obtain the frequency spectrum of the speech signal.
  - Filter bank analysis: A set of triangular filters, spaced according to the mel scale for MFCC and the Bark scale for PLP, are applied to the frequency spectrum to obtain the energy in each frequency band. The mel scale and the Bark scale are both nonlinear scales that approximate the human perception of pitch, but the Bark scale is more refined and takes into account the critical bandwidth of the human ear.
  - Logarithmic compression: The logarithm of the filter bank energies is taken to mimic the human perception of loudness, which is also logarithmic.
  - Cepstral analysis: The inverse DFT is applied to the log filter bank energies to obtain the cepstral coefficients, which are the features used for speech recognition and analysis. The cepstral coefficients represent the envelope of the frequency spectrum and are more robust to noise and speaker variations than the raw filter bank energies. For MFCC, the cepstral coefficients are obtained directly from the log filter bank energies. For PLP, an additional step of linear prediction analysis is performed to obtain the autoregressive coefficients, which are then converted to cepstral coefficients by a recursion formula.
  - Dimensionality reduction: Typically, only the lower-order cepstral coefficients are retained, as they contain most of the information relevant for speech recognition and analysis. The higher-order cepstral coefficients are discarded, as they represent the fine details of the frequency spectrum and are more susceptible to noise and speaker variations. A common practice is to retain 12-13 cepstral coefficients for MFCC and 10-12 cepstral coefficients for PLP.
  - Delta and delta-delta features: To capture the dynamic information of the speech signal, such as the rate of change and the acceleration of the cepstral coefficients, the first and second derivatives of the cepstral coefficients are computed and appended to the feature vector. This results in a feature vector of 36-39 dimensions for MFCC and 30-36 dimensions for PLP.

- The advantages and disadvantages of PLP and MFCC feature extraction methods are:

  - PLP is more perceptually motivated than MFCC, as it incorporates the effects of the human auditory system, such as the equal-loudness curve, the intensity-loudness power law, and the critical bandwidth. PLP also uses a more refined frequency scale, the Bark scale, than MFCC, which uses the mel scale. These factors make PLP more suitable for speech analysis and recognition in noisy and reverberant environments, as it can better suppress the irrelevant and redundant information in the speech signal.
  - MFCC is more computationally efficient than PLP, as it does not involve the linear prediction analysis step, which requires solving a set of linear equations. MFCC also has a simpler and more direct relationship between the cepstral coefficients and the filter bank energies, which makes it easier to interpret and manipulate. MFCC is more widely used and accepted than PLP
