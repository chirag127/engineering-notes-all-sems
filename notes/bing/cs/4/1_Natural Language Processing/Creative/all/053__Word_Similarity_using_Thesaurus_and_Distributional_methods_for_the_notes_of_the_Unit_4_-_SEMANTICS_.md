### Word Similarity using Thesaurus and Distributional methods for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Word similarity is the degree to which two words share a common meaning or are related in some way.
- Word similarity can be measured using different methods, such as thesaurus-based methods and distributional methods.
- Thesaurus-based methods use a manually created resource, such as a thesaurus or a wordnet, that groups words into categories or senses and provides relations between them, such as synonymy, antonymy, hypernymy, etc.
- Distributional methods use a corpus of text, such as a large collection of documents or web pages, to learn word representations based on the contexts in which they appear, such as word embeddings or word vectors.
- Thesaurus-based methods have the advantage of being more precise and reliable, as they are based on human knowledge and intuition, but they also have the disadvantage of being incomplete, inconsistent, and costly to create and maintain.
- Distributional methods have the advantage of being more scalable and adaptable, as they can learn from any text data, but they also have the disadvantage of being noisy, ambiguous, and sensitive to data quality and parameter choices.
- Word similarity can be used for various natural language processing tasks, such as information retrieval, text summarization, text classification, sentiment analysis, etc.
- Word similarity can also be specialized for different purposes, such as similarity or relatedness, depending on the task and the user's needs.
- Similarity is the degree to which two words have the same or similar meaning, such as car and automobile, or dog and canine.
- Relatedness is the degree to which two words are associated or connected in some way, such as car and road, or dog and bone.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the difference between similarity and relatedness is: Similar words are like siblings, related words are like cousins.
- Word similarity can also be measured in different contexts, such as the sentence or the document in which the words appear, to capture the dynamic and flexible nature of word meanings.
- A possible learning trick to understand the effect of context on word similarity is: Try to think of different sentences or documents that contain the same pair of words, and see how their similarity changes depending on the topic or the tone of the text. For example, the words apple and orange may have different similarity scores in a text about fruits, a text about colors, or a text about technology.
