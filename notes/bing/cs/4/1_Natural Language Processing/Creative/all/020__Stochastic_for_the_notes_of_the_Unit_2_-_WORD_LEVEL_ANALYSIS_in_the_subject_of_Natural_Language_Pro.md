### Stochastic for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Stochastic means involving randomness or probability. In natural language processing (NLP), stochastic methods are used to deal with the uncertainty and ambiguity of natural languages.
- Stochastic methods can be applied to different levels of language analysis, such as morphology, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics.
- Stochastic methods can be used to model the probability of a word, a phrase, a sentence, or a text given some context or prior knowledge. For example, a stochastic grammar is a grammar that assigns probabilities to grammar rules, and a stochastic language model is a model that assigns probabilities to sequences of words.
- Stochastic methods can also be used to infer the most likely or the best interpretation of a given word, phrase, sentence, or text given some evidence or observation. For example, a stochastic parser is a parser that uses probabilities to select the most likely syntactic structure of a sentence, and a stochastic semantic analyzer is a semantic analyzer that uses probabilities to select the most likely meaning of a word or a phrase.
- Stochastic methods are especially useful to resolve difficulties that arise because longer sentences are highly ambiguous when processed with realistic grammars, yielding thousands or millions of possible analyses. Stochastic methods can also handle the variability and diversity of natural languages, as well as the noise and errors in natural language data.
- Some examples of stochastic methods in NLP are:

  - Hidden Markov models (HMMs), which are probabilistic models that can capture the sequential dependencies of words or symbols.
  - N-gram models, which are models that use the frequencies of n consecutive words or symbols to estimate the probabilities of word sequences.
  - Bayesian networks, which are graphical models that can represent the conditional dependencies of random variables.
  - Neural networks, which are models that can learn complex nonlinear functions from data using layers of artificial neurons.
  - Statistical machine translation (SMT), which is a method that uses probabilities to translate text from one language to another.
  - Statistical parsing, which is a method that uses probabilities to parse sentences into syntactic structures.
  - Statistical semantic analysis, which is a method that uses probabilities to analyze the meaning of words or phrases.

- Some advantages of stochastic methods in NLP are:

  - They can handle uncertainty and ambiguity in natural languages.
  - They can learn from data and adapt to new domains or tasks.
  - They can leverage large amounts of data and computational resources.
  - They can achieve high performance and accuracy in many NLP applications.

- Some disadvantages of stochastic methods in NLP are:

  - They may require a lot of data and computational resources to train and run.
  - They may not capture the deeper or finer aspects of natural languages, such as logic, reasoning, or common sense.
  - They may not be interpretable or explainable, especially for complex models such as neural networks.
  - They may be biased or unethical, depending on the data and the objectives of the models.
