### Likelihood Distortions for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Likelihood distortions are the errors or inaccuracies that occur when estimating the probability of a speech signal given a word or a phoneme sequence.
- Likelihood distortions can affect the performance of speech recognition systems, especially when dealing with noisy or distorted speech signals.
- Some of the sources of likelihood distortions are:
  - Channel distortions: These are caused by the transmission medium or the recording device, such as background noise, reverberation, or microphone quality.
  - Speaker variability: These are caused by the differences among speakers, such as accent, dialect, gender, age, or emotional state.
  - Coarticulation: This is the phenomenon of adjacent sounds influencing each other, such as nasalization, assimilation, or elision.
  - Pronunciation variation: This is the variation in how a word or a phoneme is pronounced, such as stress, intonation, or regional variation.
- Some of the methods to reduce or compensate for likelihood distortions are:
  - Feature normalization: This is the process of transforming the speech features to a common scale or distribution, such as mean and variance normalization, cepstral mean subtraction, or z-score normalization.
  - Model adaptation: This is the process of adjusting the parameters of the acoustic model to better match the characteristics of the speech signal, such as maximum likelihood linear regression, maximum a posteriori adaptation, or speaker adaptive training.
  - Model combination: This is the process of combining the outputs of multiple acoustic models to improve the overall likelihood estimation, such as parallel model combination, log-linear interpolation, or product of experts.
  - Robust features: These are the features that are less sensitive to noise or distortion, such as perceptual linear prediction, mel-frequency cepstral coefficients, or auditory-inspired features.
  - Missing data techniques: These are the techniques that deal with the missing or unreliable features due to noise or distortion, such as marginalization, imputation, or masking.
