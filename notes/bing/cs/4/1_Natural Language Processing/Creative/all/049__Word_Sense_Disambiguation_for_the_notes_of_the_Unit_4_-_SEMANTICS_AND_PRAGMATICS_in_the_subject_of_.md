### Word Sense Disambiguation for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying the correct meaning or sense of a word in a given context, when the word has multiple possible meanings or senses.
- WSD is important for natural language processing applications such as machine translation, information retrieval, text summarization, question answering, etc. that require semantic understanding of the input text.
- WSD can be classified into two types: lexical and structural.
  - Lexical WSD is based on the individual words and their meanings, without considering the syntactic structure or the discourse context of the sentence. For example, the word "bank" can have different meanings depending on the domain (finance, geography, etc.).
  - Structural WSD is based on the syntactic structure and the discourse context of the sentence, and how they affect the meaning of the words. For example, the word "fly" can have different meanings depending on the part of speech (noun, verb, adjective, etc.).
- WSD can be performed using different methods, such as:
  - Dictionary-based methods: These methods use lexical resources, such as dictionaries, thesauri, ontologies, etc. to find the possible meanings or senses of a word, and then select the most appropriate one based on some criteria, such as frequency, similarity, etc. For example, the Lesk algorithm uses the definitions of the words in a dictionary to find the sense with the most overlap with the context words.
  - Corpus-based methods: These methods use annotated corpora, such as WordNet, SemCor, etc. to find the most probable sense of a word based on the statistical evidence from the corpus. For example, the Naive Bayes classifier uses the frequency of the word and its sense in the corpus to calculate the probability of each sense given the context words.
  - Knowledge-based methods: These methods use external knowledge sources, such as Wikipedia, DBpedia, etc. to find the most relevant sense of a word based on the semantic relations, such as hypernyms, hyponyms, synonyms, antonyms, etc. For example, the Babelfy algorithm uses the BabelNet knowledge graph to find the most coherent sense of a word based on the semantic similarity and connectivity with the other words in the context.
  - Supervised methods: These methods use machine learning techniques, such as decision trees, neural networks, etc. to learn a model from a labeled training data, and then use the model to predict the sense of a word in a new context. For example, the Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) uses a feedforward neural network with hidden layers to learn a nonlinear function that maps the context features to the sense labels.
  - Unsupervised methods: These methods do not use any labeled data, but instead use clustering techniques, such as k-means, hierarchical clustering, etc. to group the words into different senses based on their distributional similarity or co-occurrence patterns in a large unlabeled corpus. For example, the Chinese Whispers algorithm uses a graph-based clustering method to iteratively assign the words to the most frequent sense among their neighbors in the graph.
  - Semi-supervised methods: These methods use a combination of labeled and unlabeled data, and leverage the advantages of both supervised and unsupervised methods. For example, the Bootstrapping algorithm uses a small set of seed words with known senses to iteratively expand the sense inventory and the labeled data by finding the most confident unlabeled words that share the same sense with the seed words.
- Some of the challenges and limitations of WSD are:
  - Data sparsity: The lack of sufficient labeled data or lexical resources for some words, senses, domains, languages, etc. can affect the performance and the coverage of the WSD methods.
  - Sense granularity: The level of detail or specificity of the senses can vary depending on the lexical resource, the corpus, the application, etc. and can cause ambiguity or inconsistency in the WSD task.
  - Sense variability: The meaning or the usage of a word can change over time, across domains, genres, cultures, etc. and can require dynamic or adaptive WSD methods.
  - Evaluation: The evaluation of the WSD methods can be difficult or subjective, as there may not be a single correct or best sense for a word in a given context, and different annotators, lexical resources, corpora, etc. may have different criteria or judgments for the WSD task.
