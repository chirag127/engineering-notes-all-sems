### Word Classes for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Word classes, also known as parts of speech, are categories of words that share similar syntactic and semantic properties in a language.
- Word classes can be divided into two types: open and closed. Open classes are those that can be extended with new words, such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs. Closed classes are those that have a fixed set of words, such as pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, and determiners.
- Word classes are useful for natural language processing (NLP) because they can help to determine the structure and meaning of sentences, as well as to disambiguate words with multiple meanings.
- One of the common tasks in NLP is to assign word classes to each word in a text, which is called part-of-speech (POS) tagging. POS tagging can be done manually by human annotators, or automatically by machine learning models.
- There are different ways to define and label word classes, depending on the language and the level of granularity. For example, the Penn Treebank tagset defines 36 word classes for English, while the Universal Dependencies tagset defines 17 word classes for multiple languages.
- A mnemonic to remember the main word classes in English is **NAVAJOP**, which stands for:

  - **N**oun: a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea, such as *cat*, *Sydney*, *book*, or *love*.
  - **A**djective: a word that modifies a noun, such as *big*, *red*, *beautiful*, or *smart*.
  - **V**erb: a word that expresses an action or a state of being, such as *run*, *sing*, *be*, or *have*.
  - **A**dverb: a word that modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb, such as *quickly*, *very*, *well*, or *often*.
  - **J**unction: a word that connects words, phrases, or clauses, such as *and*, *but*, *or*, or *because*.
  - **O**ther: a word that does not belong to any of the above classes, such as *the*, *a*, *of*, or *to*.
  - **P**ronoun: a word that takes the place of a noun, such as *I*, *you*, *he*, *she*, or *it*.

- A learning trick to remember the word classes is to use examples of sentences that contain only one word class, such as:

  - Noun: *Sydney.*
  - Adjective: *Amazing.*
  - Verb: *Run.*
  - Adverb: *Quickly.*
  - Junction: *And.*
  - Other: *The.*
  - Pronoun: *I.*

- Another learning trick is to use examples of sentences that contain all the word classes, such as:

  - *I* (pronoun) *love* (verb) *the* (other) *big* (adjective) *and* (junction) *red* (adjective) *book* (noun) *very* (adverb) *much* (adverb).
  - *She* (pronoun) *is* (verb) *a* (other) *smart* (adjective) *and* (junction) *beautiful* (adjective) *girl* (noun) *who* (other) *studies* (verb) *well* (adverb) *often* (adverb).
