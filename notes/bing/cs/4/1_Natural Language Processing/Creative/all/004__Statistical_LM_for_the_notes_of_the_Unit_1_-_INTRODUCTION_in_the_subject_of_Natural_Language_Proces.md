### Statistical LM for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- A statistical language model (LM) is a probability distribution over sequences of words or symbols, such as sentences or texts.
- A statistical LM assigns a probability to a sequence of words or symbols, based on how likely it is to occur in a given language or domain.
- A statistical LM can be used for various natural language processing (NLP) tasks, such as speech recognition, machine translation, text summarization, text generation, etc.
- A statistical LM can be estimated from a large corpus of text, using various methods, such as counting, smoothing, interpolation, etc.
- A statistical LM can be evaluated using various metrics, such as perplexity, log-likelihood, cross-entropy, etc.

Some points to remember about statistical LM are:

- A population is the entire set of objects or individuals that we are interested in studying, such as all the sentences in a language or domain.
- A sample is a subset of the population that we can observe or measure, such as a corpus of text or a speech signal.
- A parameter is a numerical characteristic of the population, such as the probability of a word or a sequence of words in a language or domain.
- A statistic is a numerical characteristic of the sample, such as the frequency or relative frequency of a word or a sequence of words in a corpus of text or a speech signal.
- An inferential statistical analysis is the process of using the information in a sample to make inferences about the population from which the sample was drawn, such as estimating the parameters or testing hypotheses.
- A statistical model is an expression that attempts to explain patterns in the observed values of a response variable by relating the response variable to a set of predictor variables and parameters, such as y = mx + c for a linear model.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for statistical LM are:

- To remember the difference between a population and a sample, think of the phrase "a sample is a part of the population".
- To remember the difference between a parameter and a statistic, think of the phrase "a parameter is a property of the population, a statistic is a summary of the sample".
- To remember the difference between a probability and a frequency, think of the phrase "a probability is a measure of uncertainty, a frequency is a measure of occurrence".
- To remember the formula for relative frequency, think of the phrase "relative frequency is frequency divided by total".
- To remember the formula for perplexity, think of the phrase "perplexity is the inverse of the geometric mean of the probabilities".
