### Ambiguity for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Ambiguity is the property of a sentence or phrase that can have more than one meaning or interpretation.
- Ambiguity can arise at different levels of language processing, such as lexical, syntactic, semantic, pragmatic, etc.
- Syntactic ambiguity is the type of ambiguity that occurs when a sentence or phrase can have more than one syntactic structure or parse tree.
- Syntactic ambiguity can affect the meaning of a sentence or phrase, as different syntactic structures can imply different semantic relations among the words or constituents.
- Syntactic ambiguity can also affect the performance of natural language processing tasks, such as parsing, translation, summarization, etc., as different syntactic structures can lead to different outputs or results.
- Syntactic ambiguity can be resolved by using various methods, such as context, world knowledge, discourse, grammar rules, etc.
- Some examples of syntactic ambiguity are:

  - I saw the man with the telescope. (Who has the telescope? The speaker or the man?)
  - They are flying planes. (Are they pilots or birds?)
  - Visiting relatives can be boring. (Are the relatives visiting or being visited?)
  - The chicken is ready to eat. (Is the chicken alive or cooked?)
  - He likes her cooking. (Does he like the food she cooks or the act of her cooking?)

- A mnemonic to remember the types of ambiguity is: **L**exical, **S**yntactic, **S**emantic, **P**ragmatic. (LSSP)
- A learning trick to identify syntactic ambiguity is to try to draw different parse trees for the same sentence or phrase, and see if they have different meanings or interpretations.
