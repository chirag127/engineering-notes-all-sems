### Cepstral Distances for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Cepstral distances are a measure of similarity between two speech signals based on their cepstral coefficients.
- Cepstral coefficients are derived from the Fourier transform of the speech signal and represent the spectral envelope of the signal.
- Cepstral distances can be used for speech recognition, speaker identification, speech synthesis, and speech enhancement.
- Cepstral distances can be computed using different methods, such as Euclidean distance, Mahalanobis distance, cosine distance, or Kullback-Leibler divergence.
- Cepstral distances can be normalized or weighted to account for different factors, such as noise, channel distortion, or speaker variability.
- Cepstral distances can be compared using different criteria, such as minimum, average, or maximum distance, or using a threshold or a decision rule.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for cepstral distances are:

- Cepstral coefficients are like the "recipe" of the speech signal, and cepstral distances are like the "difference" between two recipes.
- Cepstral distances can be computed using different "metrics", such as Euclidean, Mahalanobis, cosine, or KL divergence.
- Cepstral distances can be normalized or weighted to account for different "factors", such as noise, channel, or speaker.
- Cepstral distances can be compared using different "criteria", such as minimum, average, or maximum distance, or using a threshold or a rule.
