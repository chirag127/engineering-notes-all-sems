### Multiple Time – Alignment Paths for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Multiple time – alignment paths are methods to align two or more speech signals in time domain, so that they have the same duration and the same number of frames.
- Time alignment is useful for many speech analysis applications, such as speech recognition, text to speech conversion, voice conversion, speaker identification, and speech enhancement.
- Time alignment can be achieved by using different techniques, such as dynamic time warping (DTW), linear time warping (LTW), or hidden Markov models (HMMs).
- Dynamic time warping (DTW) is a technique that finds the optimal alignment path between two speech signals by minimizing the distance between their corresponding frames. DTW can handle nonlinear distortions and variable speech rates, but it has a high computational complexity and requires a global optimization.
- Linear time warping (LTW) is a technique that finds the alignment path between two speech signals by using a linear transformation, such as scaling, shifting, or rotation. LTW can handle linear distortions and constant speech rates, but it cannot handle nonlinear distortions and variable speech rates. LTW has a lower computational complexity and requires a local optimization.
- Hidden Markov models (HMMs) are probabilistic models that represent the speech signals as sequences of states, and find the alignment path between them by using the Viterbi algorithm. HMMs can handle both linear and nonlinear distortions and variable speech rates, but they require a prior knowledge of the state transition probabilities and the observation probabilities. HMMs have a moderate computational complexity and require a global optimization.

- A mnemonic to remember the three techniques is: **D**on't **L**et **H**im **M**ove (DTW, LTW, HMM).
- A learning trick to understand the difference between the three techniques is to imagine that you are trying to align two maps of the same city, but one is distorted by some factors. DTW is like using a rubber band to stretch and shrink the maps until they match. LTW is like using scissors and glue to cut and paste the maps until they match. HMM is like using a dice and a ruler to assign probabilities and distances to the maps until they match.
