### LPC for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- LPC stands for Linear Predictive Coding, which is a method used mostly in audio signal processing and speech processing for representing the spectral envelope of a digital signal of speech in compressed form, using the information of a linear predictive model.
- LPC analyzes the speech signal by estimating the formants, which are the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract, removing their effects from the speech signal, and estimating the intensity and frequency of the remaining buzz, which is the source of the voiced sounds. The process of removing the formants is called inverse filtering, and the remaining signal after the subtraction of the filtered modeled signal is called the residue.
- LPC is the most widely used method in speech coding and speech synthesis, as it can achieve high compression rates and high quality of speech reconstruction. It is generally used for speech analysis and resynthesis, and it is also used as a form of voice compression by phone companies, such as in the GSM standard, for example.
- LPC is based on the assumption that the speech signal can be modeled as the output of a linear system with a known input and unknown coefficients. The input is either a periodic pulse train for voiced sounds, or a white noise for unvoiced sounds, and the coefficients are the reflection coefficients that describe the shape of the vocal tract. The goal of LPC is to find the optimal coefficients that minimize the prediction error between the actual speech signal and the modeled signal.
- LPC can be implemented in two steps: analysis and synthesis. In the analysis section, the reflection coefficients are extracted from the speech signal using an algorithm such as the Levinson-Durbin recursion, and the residual signal is computed by applying the inverse filter to the speech signal. In the synthesis section, the speech signal is reconstructed by applying the forward filter to the input signal, which is either the original residual signal or a modified one for speech modification purposes.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the steps of LPC is: **A**nalyze the **F**ormants and the **R**esidue, **S**ynthesize the **S**peech with the **F**ilter and the **I**nput.
- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of LPC is to use MATLAB or a similar software to perform LPC analysis and synthesis of a speech signal, and to visualize the spectral envelope, the formants, the residue, and the reconstructed speech signal. This can help to see the effects of LPC on the speech signal and to compare the original and the modified speech signals.
