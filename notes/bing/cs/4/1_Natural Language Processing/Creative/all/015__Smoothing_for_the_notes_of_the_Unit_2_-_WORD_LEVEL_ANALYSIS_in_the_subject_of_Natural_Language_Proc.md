### Smoothing for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Smoothing is the process of flattening a probability distribution implied by a language model so that all reasonable word sequences can occur with some probability.
- Smoothing techniques in NLP are used to address scenarios related to determining probability / likelihood estimate of a sequence of words (say, a sentence) occurring together when one or more words individually (unigram) or N-grams such as bigram (w i / w i − 1) or trigram (w i / w i − 1 w i − 2) in the given set have never occurred in the past.
- Smoothing is very important in natural language processing, as some words may have zero or close to zero probabilities such as the out-of-vocabulary words (words that do not exist in the vocabulary), but the same rare words may not have the same values in test data.
- Smoothing methods can be classified into two categories: interpolation and backoff.
- Interpolation methods combine the probabilities of different N-grams to estimate the probability of a word given its context. For example, the Jelinek-Mercer smoothing method uses a linear interpolation of unigram and bigram probabilities.
- Backoff methods use lower-order N-grams when higher-order N-grams have zero probability. For example, the Katz smoothing method uses unigram probabilities when bigram probabilities are zero.
- Some of the common smoothing techniques in NLP are :
  - Laplace smoothing: This method adds a small constant (usually 1) to the count of every N-gram, regardless of whether it has been seen or not. This ensures that no N-gram has zero probability, but it also overestimates the probability of rare N-grams.
  - Good-Turing smoothing: This method adjusts the count of N-grams based on how many N-grams have been seen exactly once (called singletons). The idea is that the probability of unseen N-grams is proportional to the number of singletons, and the probability of seen N-grams is reduced accordingly.
  - Kneser-Ney smoothing: This method is a refinement of the absolute discounting method, which subtracts a fixed amount (usually 0.75) from the count of every seen N-gram and distributes the remaining mass to the unseen N-grams based on the lower-order N-grams. The Kneser-Ney smoothing method modifies the lower-order N-grams by using the number of unique words that follow a given word (called continuation counts) instead of the raw counts. This captures the intuition that some words are more likely to appear in new contexts than others.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the smoothing techniques is: **L**et's **G**o **K**ite-flying, **N**o **J**oke, **K**eep **W**atching. This corresponds to the first letters of Laplace, Good-Turing, Kneser-Ney, Jelinek-Mercer, Katz, and Witten-Bell smoothing methods.
