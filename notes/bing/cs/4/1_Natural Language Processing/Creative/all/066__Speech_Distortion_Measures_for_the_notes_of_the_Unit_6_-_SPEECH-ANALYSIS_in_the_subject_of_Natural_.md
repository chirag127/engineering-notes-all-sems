### Speech Distortion Measures for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Speech distortion measures are numerical metrics that quantify the difference between a reference speech signal and a distorted speech signal.
- Speech distortion measures are useful for evaluating the performance of speech processing systems, such as speech enhancement, speech coding, speech recognition, speech synthesis, etc.
- Speech distortion measures can be classified into two categories: waveform-based measures and perceptual-based measures.
- Waveform-based measures compare the reference and distorted signals in the time or frequency domain, without considering the human auditory system. Examples of waveform-based measures are mean squared error (MSE), signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), segmental SNR (SSNR), log spectral distance (LSD), etc.
- Perceptual-based measures compare the reference and distorted signals in a perceptual domain, taking into account the human auditory system. Examples of perceptual-based measures are perceptual evaluation of speech quality (PESQ), perceptual objective listening quality assessment (POLQA), short-time objective intelligibility (STOI), etc.
- A good speech distortion measure should have the following properties:
  - High correlation with subjective quality or intelligibility ratings.
  - Low sensitivity to irrelevant factors, such as level, delay, phase, etc.
  - Low computational complexity and memory requirements.
  - Robustness to different types of distortions and speech signals.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the two categories of speech distortion measures is: **W**aveform-based measures **W**ork **W**ithout **W**orrying about the human ear, while **P**erceptual-based measures **P**erform **P**rocessing **P**rior to comparison.
