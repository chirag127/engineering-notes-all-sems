### Evaluating N-grams for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- N-grams are sequences of n words that are used to model the probability of a word given its previous words in a text.
- N-grams can be used for various tasks in natural language processing, such as language modeling, text generation, spelling correction, machine translation, speech recognition, etc.
- Evaluating n-grams means measuring how well they capture the statistical properties of natural language and how useful they are for the tasks they are applied to.
- There are different methods for evaluating n-grams, depending on the purpose and the data available. Some of the common methods are:

  - **Perplexity**: Perplexity is a measure of how well a probabilistic model predicts a sample. It is defined as the inverse of the average probability of the words in the sample, raised to the power of the sample size. A lower perplexity means a better model, as it assigns higher probabilities to the observed words. Perplexity can be used to compare different n-gram models or to tune the parameters of a model, such as the smoothing technique or the n-gram order.
  - **Intrinsic evaluation**: Intrinsic evaluation is a method of evaluating n-grams based on their internal properties, such as the size of the vocabulary, the number of unique n-grams, the entropy, the mutual information, etc. Intrinsic evaluation can be used to analyze the characteristics of n-grams and to identify their strengths and weaknesses. However, intrinsic evaluation does not directly measure how well n-grams perform on a specific task or application.
  - **Extrinsic evaluation**: Extrinsic evaluation is a method of evaluating n-grams based on their performance on a downstream task or application, such as text generation, machine translation, speech recognition, etc. Extrinsic evaluation can be used to measure the impact of n-grams on the quality and accuracy of the output. However, extrinsic evaluation can be costly and time-consuming, as it requires a large amount of data and a reliable evaluation metric for the task or application.

- Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for evaluating n-grams are:

  - **PINE**: PINE stands for Perplexity, Intrinsic, N-gram, Extrinsic. It can be used to remember the four main methods of evaluating n-grams and their order of preference. Perplexity is the most common and simple method, followed by intrinsic evaluation, which is more informative but less practical. N-gram is the basic unit of analysis, and extrinsic evaluation is the most relevant but also the most complex method.
  - **N-ICE**: N-ICE stands for N-gram, Intrinsic, Characteristics, Extrinsic. It can be used to remember the main aspects of each evaluation method. N-gram is the sequence of words that is evaluated, intrinsic evaluation is based on the internal properties of n-grams, characteristics are the features that describe n-grams, such as vocabulary size, entropy, etc., and extrinsic evaluation is based on the performance of n-grams on a task or application.
  - **SIP**: SIP stands for Smoothing, Interpolation, Perplexity. It can be used to remember the main techniques and metrics for improving and measuring n-gram models. Smoothing is a method of assigning non-zero probabilities to unseen n-grams, interpolation is a method of combining different n-gram models, and perplexity is a measure of how well a model predicts a sample.
