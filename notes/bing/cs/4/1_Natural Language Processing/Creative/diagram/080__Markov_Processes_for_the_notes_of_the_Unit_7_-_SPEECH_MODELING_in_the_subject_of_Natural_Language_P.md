A Markov process is a stochastic process that has the property of memorylessness, meaning that the future state of the system depends only on the current state and not on the past history. A Markov process can be represented by a state diagram, where each node is a possible state and each edge is a transition probability between states.

A hidden Markov model (HMM) is a special type of Markov process that has two layers: the hidden layer and the observable layer. The hidden layer consists of a sequence of states that follow a Markov process, but are not directly observable. The observable layer consists of a sequence of observations that are generated by the hidden states according to some emission probabilities. An HMM can be used to model the relationship between the acoustic features of speech and the underlying phonetic units.
