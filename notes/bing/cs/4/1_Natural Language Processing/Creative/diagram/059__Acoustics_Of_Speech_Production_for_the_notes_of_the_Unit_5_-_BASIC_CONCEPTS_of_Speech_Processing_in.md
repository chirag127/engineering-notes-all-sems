The acoustics of speech production is the study of how speech sounds are generated by the human vocal system and how they are modified by the shape and configuration of the vocal tract. A common model for speech production is the source-filter model, which assumes that speech sounds are produced by a source of sound energy (such as the vocal folds or the air flow) that is filtered by the resonances of the vocal tract (such as the tongue, the lips, the teeth, etc.). The source-filter model can be represented by the following diagram:

```
+-----------------+     +-----------------+     +-----------------+
| Sound source    |     | Vocal tract     |     | Sound radiation |
| (larynx, air)   | --> | (filter)        | --> | (acoustic wave) |
+-----------------+     +-----------------+     +-----------------+
```

The sound source can be either voiced (when the vocal folds vibrate) or unvoiced (when the air flow is turbulent). The vocal tract can be modeled as a series of tubes with different lengths and cross-sectional areas, which create resonances or formants in the speech spectrum. The sound radiation is the acoustic wave that propagates from the mouth and the nose to the listener's ear.

The acoustics of speech production can be analyzed using various methods, such as measuring the sound pressure level, the fundamental frequency, the formant frequencies, the spectral envelope, the harmonics-to-noise ratio, etc. These acoustic features can be used to characterize and classify different speech sounds, such as vowels, consonants, tones, etc. The acoustics of speech production can also be influenced by various factors, such as the speaker's age, gender, dialect, emotion, etc.
