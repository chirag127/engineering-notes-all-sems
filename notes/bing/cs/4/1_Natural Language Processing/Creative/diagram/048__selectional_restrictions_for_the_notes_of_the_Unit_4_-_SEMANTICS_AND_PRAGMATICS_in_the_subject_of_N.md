Selectional restrictions are the semantic restrictions that a word imposes on the environment in which it occurs. For example, a verb like eat requires that its subject refers to an animate entity and its object to something concrete. Selectional restrictions are part of the meanings of specific words; that is, they are linguistic in nature, rather than simply facts about the world. Selectional restrictions are a special type of presupposition, and they hold even in questions, negative statements, and other non-assertive environments.
