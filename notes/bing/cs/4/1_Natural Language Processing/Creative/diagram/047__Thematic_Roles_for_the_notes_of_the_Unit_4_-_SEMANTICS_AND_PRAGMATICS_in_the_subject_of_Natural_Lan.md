Thematic roles are the semantic relationships between a verb and its arguments in a sentence. They capture the commonalities and differences among the participants of various events or states described by verbs. For example, the agent is the entity that intentionally performs the action of the verb, while the theme is the entity that is affected by the action of the verb.

There are different inventories of thematic roles proposed by different theories of natural language processing, but some of the most common ones are:

- Agent: the entity that intentionally performs the action of the verb (e.g., John broke the window)
- Theme: the entity that is affected by the action of the verb (e.g., John broke the window)
- Experiencer: the entity that undergoes an emotion, a state of being, or a perception expressed by the verb (e.g., John loves Mary)
- Goal: the entity that is the destination or endpoint of the action of the verb (e.g., John gave the book to Mary)
- Source: the entity that is the origin or starting point of the action of the verb (e.g., John took the book from Mary)
- Instrument: the entity that is used to perform the action of the verb (e.g., John cut the bread with a knife)
- Beneficiary: the entity that benefits from the action of the verb (e.g., John baked a cake for Mary)
- Location: the entity that specifies the place where the action of the verb occurs (e.g., John lives in New York)
- Manner: the entity that specifies the way or mode of the action of the verb (e.g., John ran fast)
- Cause: the entity that causes or triggers the action of the verb (e.g., The storm caused the flood)
