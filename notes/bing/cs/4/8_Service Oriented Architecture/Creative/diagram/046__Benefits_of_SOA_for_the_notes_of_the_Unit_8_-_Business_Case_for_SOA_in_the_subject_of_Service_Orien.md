The diagram illustrates the basic architecture of a SOA, where each layer consists of a set of modular and independent services that communicate with each other through standard protocols. The benefits of SOA include  :

- Efficient and easy extension of business processes: SOA allows the reuse of existing services to create new or modified business processes, without having to rewrite or reconfigure the entire system. This reduces the development time and cost, and increases the agility and flexibility of the business.
- Unique and universally recognised communication architecture: SOA uses standard and interoperable protocols, such as SOAP, REST, and XML, to enable the communication between different services, regardless of the underlying platforms, languages, or technologies. This simplifies the integration and maintenance of the system, and reduces the dependency and coupling between the services.
- High speed in the circulation of information between systems: SOA enables the real-time and asynchronous exchange of data and messages between the services, without having to store or process them in a centralised location. This improves the performance and scalability of the system, and reduces the risk of data loss or corruption.
- Reduced cost of software management and upgrades: SOA allows the independent deployment and update of each service, without affecting the other services or the overall system. This reduces the downtime and the complexity of the software lifecycle management, and increases the reliability and availability of the system.
- Warehouse updates in real time: SOA enables the synchronisation and consistency of the data and information across different services and systems, such as inventory, order, and delivery. This enhances the accuracy and quality of the business operations, and increases the customer satisfaction and loyalty.
