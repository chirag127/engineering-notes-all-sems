A service-oriented architecture (SOA) is a software design that consists of reusable and interoperable services that communicate through standardized interfaces and protocols. A service is a self-contained unit of functionality that performs a specific business task. Services can be composed and orchestrated to create complex business processes and applications. SOA aims to achieve loose coupling, modularity, scalability, and agility in software development.

A microservices architecture (MSA) is a variant of SOA that focuses on building applications as a collection of small, independent, and fine-grained services that communicate through lightweight protocols. A microservice is a service that has a single responsibility and is aligned with a business domain. Microservices can be deployed and updated independently, and can use different technologies and platforms. MSA aims to achieve high cohesion, low coupling, resilience, and flexibility in software development.
