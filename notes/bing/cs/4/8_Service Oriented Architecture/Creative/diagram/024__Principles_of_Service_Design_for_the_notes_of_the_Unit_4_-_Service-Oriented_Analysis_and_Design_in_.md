The principles of service design are a set of guidelines that help to create services that are useful, usable, desirable, efficient and effective for the customers and the service providers. Based on the web search results, the following are some of the common principles of service design:

- User-centred: Design your services around your consumers’ needs and expectations. You need to know how consumers experience the service and what they value and prefer.
- Co-creative: Involve all the stakeholders, including customers, employees, managers, partners and suppliers, in the design process. Co-create solutions that benefit everyone and foster collaboration and innovation.
- Sequencing: Break down the service into a series of steps or stages that form the customer journey. Design each stage with attention to detail and consistency. Iterate and improve the service based on feedback and testing.
- Evidencing: Make the service tangible and visible to the customers and the service providers. Use visual communication and storytelling to convey the value and benefits of the service. Provide evidence of the service quality and outcomes.
- Holistic: Consider the whole service system and the context in which it operates. Align the service with the organizational vision, strategy and culture. Ensure the service is coherent and integrated across all the touchpoints and channels.
