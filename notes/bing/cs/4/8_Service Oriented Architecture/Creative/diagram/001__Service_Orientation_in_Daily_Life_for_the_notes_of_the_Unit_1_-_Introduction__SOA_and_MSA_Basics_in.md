Service orientation is the ability and desire to anticipate, recognize and meet others’ needs, sometimes even before those needs are articulated . Service-oriented people focus on providing satisfaction and making themselves available to others . They also recognize and act on their responsibilities to society, locally, nationally, and globally.

Some examples of service providers and consumers in daily life are:

- A doctor provides medical service to a patient who consumes the service.
- A teacher provides educational service to a student who consumes the service.
- A plumber provides plumbing service to a homeowner who consumes the service.

Service orientation in daily life involves:

- Understanding the needs and expectations of the service consumers.
- Delivering the service in a timely, efficient, and courteous manner.
- Seeking feedback and improvement from the service consumers.
- Respecting the diversity and dignity of the service consumers.
- Contributing to the social and environmental well-being of the service consumers.
