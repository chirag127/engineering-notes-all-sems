### SOA Strategy – Best Practices for the notes of the Unit 9 - SOA Best Practices in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

SOA (Service Oriented Architecture) is an architectural style that aims to achieve loose coupling, reusability, interoperability, and agility among different software components or services. SOA strategy is the process of planning, designing, implementing, and governing an SOA initiative within an organization. SOA best practices are the guidelines and recommendations that can help to achieve a successful SOA deployment and avoid common pitfalls and challenges.

Some of the SOA best practices are:

- **Establish a core architecture leadership team.** This team should consist of architects, developers, business analysts, and other stakeholders who can define the vision, goals, principles, and standards of the SOA initiative. The team should also oversee the governance, quality, and performance of the SOA services and ensure consistency and alignment with the business needs.

- **Get buy-in from management and other stakeholders.** SOA is not just a technical change, but also a cultural and organizational change. Therefore, it is important to communicate the benefits, challenges, and risks of SOA to the senior management and other stakeholders, and get their support and commitment. SOA should be aligned with the business strategy and objectives, and should demonstrate clear value and return on investment.

- **Start small, then evolve.** SOA is not a one-time project, but a continuous journey. Therefore, it is advisable to start with a small and manageable scope, such as a pilot project or a specific business domain, and then gradually expand and refine the SOA initiative based on the feedback, lessons learned, and changing requirements. This way, the SOA initiative can be tested and modified while rolling it out, and best practices can be identified and applied along the way .

- **Avoid \"death by governance.\"** Governance is the process of defining, enforcing, and monitoring the policies, rules, and standards that guide the design, development, deployment, and usage of SOA services. Governance is essential for ensuring the quality, security, reliability, and interoperability of SOA services, and for avoiding duplication, inconsistency, and complexity. However, governance should not be too rigid, bureaucratic, or restrictive, as it can stifle innovation, creativity, and agility. Governance should be flexible, adaptive, and collaborative, and should balance the needs of the service providers and consumers.

- **Communicate that \"governance is there to help.\"** Governance can sometimes be perceived as a burden, a constraint, or a threat by the service providers and consumers, especially if they are not involved or consulted in the governance process. Therefore, it is important to communicate the purpose, benefits, and expectations of governance to the SOA stakeholders, and to solicit their feedback and input. Governance should be seen as a facilitator, a supporter, and a partner, rather than a controller, a regulator, or a dictator.

- **Leverage standards and best practices.** SOA is based on the use of standards and best practices that enable the interoperability, reusability, and portability of SOA services across different platforms, technologies, and domains. Some of the common standards and best practices for SOA are: XML, SOAP, WSDL, UDDI, REST, WS-*, SOA patterns, SOA principles, SOA maturity models, etc. These standards and best practices should be adopted and followed as much as possible, unless there is a valid reason to deviate from them.

- **Focus on SOA reusability.** One of the main benefits and goals of SOA is to achieve reusability of SOA services, which can reduce development time and cost, improve quality and consistency, and enhance agility and flexibility. SOA reusability goes beyond traditional code reuse, as it involves reusing not only the functionality, but also the interface, the contract, the metadata, and the policies of SOA services.
