### Approach for Enterprise-wide SOA Implementation for the notes of the Unit 6 - SOA Governance and Implementation in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an integration architectural style and an enterprise-wide concept that enables existing applications to be exposed over loosely-coupled interfaces, each corresponding to a business function, that enables applications in one part of an extended enterprise to reuse functionality in other applications.
- SOA implementation requires a systematic and holistic approach that involves business users, IT, governance organizations, competency centers or centers of excellence, and cross-functional groupings.
- A typical SOA design approach leads to implementation of the enterprise services in a form of a specialized layer rationalizing existing enterprise functionality (applications) against "ideal" enterprise business model.
- Such architecture defines multiple layers in the SOA architecture:
  - Business Process Layer: This layer defines the business processes and workflows that orchestrate the services and provide the business logic.
  - Service Layer: This layer defines the services that encapsulate the business functionality and expose it over standard interfaces and protocols.
  - Integration Layer: This layer defines the integration mechanisms and technologies that enable the communication and interaction between the services and the underlying applications.
  - Application Layer: This layer defines the existing applications that provide the functionality and data for the services.
- One approach that contributes to an optimal SOA implementation is the use of an Enterprise Service Bus (ESB) to provide an infrastructural element to distributed Services on the network.
- An ESB is a middleware platform that provides the following capabilities:
  - Service mediation: The ESB acts as an intermediary between the service providers and consumers, providing routing, transformation, protocol conversion, and other functions to enable interoperability and loose coupling.
  - Service orchestration: The ESB supports the composition and coordination of services to implement complex business processes and workflows.
  - Service management: The ESB provides the monitoring, logging, auditing, security, and other functions to manage the service lifecycle and quality of service.
  - Service discovery: The ESB enables the registration, publication, and discovery of services through a service registry or repository.
- Another approach that contributes to an optimal SOA implementation is the incorporation of enterprise data into SOA, which involves the integration and alignment of the data sources, models, and services in the enterprise.
- Enterprise data integration (EDI) is the process of accessing, transforming, and delivering data from various sources to various targets, such as applications, databases, data warehouses, or data marts.
- Enterprise data alignment (EDA) is the process of ensuring that the data models and schemas used by the applications, services, and data sources are consistent and compatible with each other and with the enterprise business model.
- Enterprise data services (EDS) are the services that provide the access, manipulation, and delivery of data in the enterprise, such as data query, data update, data transformation, data validation, data synchronization, data replication, data federation, data virtualization, and data quality.
- A third approach that contributes to an optimal SOA implementation is the adoption of SOA governance, which is the set of policies, processes, roles, and responsibilities that guide, direct, and control how the enterprise's assets are utilized and enhanced to achieve its goals.
- SOA governance covers the following aspects:
  - SOA strategy: This aspect defines the vision, objectives, scope, and roadmap for SOA adoption and implementation in the enterprise, as well as the alignment of SOA with the business strategy and IT strategy.
  - SOA architecture: This aspect defines the principles, standards, guidelines, and best practices for designing, developing, deploying, and maintaining the SOA architecture and its components, such as services, processes, ESB, and EDS.
  - SOA portfolio: This aspect defines the processes and criteria for identifying, prioritizing, selecting, and funding the SOA projects and initiatives, as well as the methods and tools for managing the SOA portfolio and its dependencies and impacts.
