### Design of Business Process Services for the notes of the Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Service-Oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) is an approach to design SOA applications that are composed of reusable and interoperable services that implement business processes and functions .
- A SOAD approach in designing SOA applications requires the following key elements:
  - Process Model: Defining process and notation by orchestrating object-oriented analysis and design (OOAD), business process modeling (BPM) and enterprise architecture elements. Additional elements can also be defined if required.
  - Service Model: Identifying and defining services and service interfaces based on the process model and the business requirements. Services can be categorized into different types, such as business services, application services, infrastructure services, etc.
  - Service Contract: Specifying the details of the service interface, such as the operations, parameters, messages, policies, etc. Service contracts can be expressed using standards, such as WSDL, SOAP, REST, etc.
  - Service Implementation: Developing and deploying the service logic and behavior using the appropriate technology platform, such as .NET, J2EE, etc. Service implementation can also involve integrating existing legacy systems or applications.
  - Service Composition: Orchestrating and coordinating the service interactions and transactions to achieve the desired business outcome. Service composition can be achieved using standards, such as BPEL, WS-Coordination, WS-Transaction, etc.
- A SOAD process can be divided into two main phases: service-oriented analysis and service-oriented design  .
  - Service-Oriented Analysis (SOA) is the phase where the business requirements and the process model are analyzed to identify and define the services and service interfaces that are needed to support the business goals and objectives. Service-oriented analysis involves the following steps:
    - Define the business process and the business use cases that describe the business scenarios and the actors involved.
    - Identify the business entities and the data model that represent the information exchanged and manipulated by the business process.
    - Identify the business services and the service candidates that provide the functionality and the data required by the business process and the use cases. Service candidates are potential services that need to be further analyzed and refined.
    - Define the service context and the service boundaries that specify the scope and the granularity of the services and the service candidates. Service context refers to the business domain, the goals, the constraints, and the assumptions that influence the service design. Service boundaries refer to the level of abstraction, the level of reuse, and the level of autonomy of the services and the service candidates.
    - Model the service candidates and the service compositions using the appropriate notation and tools, such as UML, BPMN, etc. Service modeling is a sub-process that involves defining the service capabilities, the service contracts, the service dependencies, and the service compositions.
  - Service-Oriented Design (SOD) is the phase where the service model and the service contracts are refined and detailed to prepare for the service implementation and the service composition. Service-oriented design involves the following steps:
    - Refine the service model and the service contracts based on the design standards, the design principles, and the design patterns that are relevant for SOA. Design standards refer to the industry or the organization specific guidelines and best practices for SOA. Design principles refer to the fundamental concepts and rules that guide the SOA design, such as loose coupling, abstraction, reusability, etc. Design patterns refer to the proven and reusable solutions for common SOA design problems, such as service façade, service registry, service broker, etc.
    - Select the technology platform and the technology standards that are suitable for the service implementation and the service composition. Technology platform refers to the software environment that supports the development and the deployment of the services, such as .NET, J2EE, etc. Technology standards refer to the protocols and the formats that enable the communication and the interoperability of the services, such as WSDL, SOAP, REST, etc.
    - Design the service implementation and the service composition using the selected technology platform and the technology standards. Service implementation involves developing the service logic and the service behavior using the programming language and the framework of the technology platform, such as C#, Java, etc. Service composition involves orchestrating and coordinating the service interactions and the transactions using the language and the engine of the technology standards, such as BPEL, WS-Coordination, WS-Transaction,
