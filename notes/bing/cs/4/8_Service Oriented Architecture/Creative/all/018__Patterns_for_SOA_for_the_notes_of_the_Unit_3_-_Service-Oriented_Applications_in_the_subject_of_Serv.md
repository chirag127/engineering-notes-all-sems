### Patterns for SOA for the notes of the Unit 3 - Service-Oriented Applications in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Patterns for SOA are reusable solutions to common design problems that arise when building service-oriented applications.
- Patterns for SOA can help architects and developers to plan, implement, deploy, operate, and maintain complex systems that feature flexibility, availability, and scalability.
- Patterns for SOA can be classified into different categories, such as service design patterns, service composition patterns, service implementation patterns, service inventory patterns, and service governance patterns.
- Some examples of patterns for SOA are:

  - Agnostic Service: A service that implements logic that is common to multiple business problems and does not contain any logic specific to a single business problem. This pattern increases the reusability and interoperability of services.
  - Service Façade: A service that provides a simplified and standardized interface to a complex or heterogeneous set of services or systems. This pattern reduces the coupling and complexity of service consumers and providers.
  - Enterprise Service Bus (ESB): A middleware platform that provides a common communication channel and integration capabilities for services and systems. This pattern enables loose coupling, message routing, transformation, mediation, and orchestration of services.
  - Service Callback: A service that invokes another service and provides a callback address for the invoked service to send the response. This pattern supports asynchronous and event-driven communication between services.
  - Multiple Service Contracts: A service that exposes different contracts for different types of service consumers. This pattern allows the service to accommodate different levels of abstraction, granularity, and security requirements.
  - Authentication Broker: A service that centralizes the authentication logic and credentials for accessing other services. This pattern simplifies the security management and increases the security of service interactions.
  - Service Normalization: A design principle that advocates the creation of services with standardized contracts, interfaces, and data formats. This principle reduces the redundancy and inconsistency of services and improves the interoperability and maintainability of service inventory.
