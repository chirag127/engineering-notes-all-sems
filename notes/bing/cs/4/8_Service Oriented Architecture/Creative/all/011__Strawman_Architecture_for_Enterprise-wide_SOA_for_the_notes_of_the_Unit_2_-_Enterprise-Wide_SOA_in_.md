### Strawman Architecture for Enterprise-wide SOA for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Strawman Architecture is the initial architecture that serves as a starting point for developing the target architecture .
- It is a high-level and abstract representation of the enterprise-wide SOA that identifies the key components and their relationships.
- It is not a complete or detailed architecture, but rather a sketch or a blueprint that can be refined and elaborated as the project progresses.
- It helps to communicate the vision and scope of the SOA initiative, as well as to identify the gaps and risks that need to be addressed.
- It also serves as a reference for evaluating and selecting the appropriate technologies and standards for implementing the SOA solution .

- The Strawman Architecture for Enterprise-wide SOA consists of four layers:

  - Business Layer: This layer defines the business processes, services, and policies that govern the enterprise. It also specifies the business goals, objectives, and metrics that measure the performance and value of the SOA solution.
  - Service Layer: This layer defines the service contracts, interfaces, and implementations that provide the functionality and data required by the business layer. It also defines the service orchestration, composition, and mediation mechanisms that coordinate and integrate the services.
  - Integration Layer: This layer defines the integration infrastructure, protocols, and adapters that enable the communication and interoperability between the service layer and the legacy systems, applications, and data sources. It also defines the integration patterns, standards, and best practices that ensure the reliability, security, and scalability of the SOA solution.
  - Resource Layer: This layer defines the legacy systems, applications, and data sources that provide the underlying resources and capabilities for the service layer. It also defines the resource management, monitoring, and governance mechanisms that ensure the availability, performance, and quality of the SOA solution.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the four layers of the Strawman Architecture is **B-SIR** (Business, Service, Integration, Resource).
- A possible learning trick to understand the Strawman Architecture is to compare it with a house construction project:

  - The Business Layer is like the blueprint of the house that defines the layout, design, and functionality of the rooms and spaces.
  - The Service Layer is like the plumbing, wiring, and heating systems that provide the water, electricity, and temperature control for the house.
  - The Integration Layer is like the pipes, cables, and ducts that connect the plumbing, wiring, and heating systems with the external sources and networks.
  - The Resource Layer is like the water supply, power grid, and gas network that provide the resources and capabilities for the plumbing, wiring, and heating systems.
