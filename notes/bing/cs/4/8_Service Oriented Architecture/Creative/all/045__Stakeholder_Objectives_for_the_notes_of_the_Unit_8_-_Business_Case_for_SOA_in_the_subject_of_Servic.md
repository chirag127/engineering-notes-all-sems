### Stakeholder Objectives for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Stakeholders are the people or groups who have an interest or influence in the outcome of a project or initiative. In the context of SOA, stakeholders can be classified into two categories: business stakeholders and technical stakeholders .
- Business stakeholders are the ones who are concerned with the strategic goals, value propositions, customer satisfaction, and competitive advantage of the organization. They are the consumers of IT resources and services, and they expect them to be aligned with the business needs and requirements. Some examples of business stakeholders are business unit executives, managers, analysts, and end users.
- Technical stakeholders are the ones who are responsible for the design, development, deployment, and maintenance of the IT resources and services. They are the providers of IT resources and services, and they expect them to be reliable, scalable, secure, and interoperable. Some examples of technical stakeholders are IT executives, architects, developers, testers, and administrators.
- The objectives of the stakeholders for SOA are different and sometimes conflicting. Business stakeholders want to achieve agility, innovation, differentiation, and cost reduction by leveraging SOA as a way to reuse, integrate, and orchestrate existing and new IT assets. Technical stakeholders want to achieve quality, efficiency, standardization, and governance by leveraging SOA as a way to modularize, decouple, and abstract IT assets. Therefore, a business case for SOA should address both the business and technical objectives of the stakeholders, and demonstrate how SOA can deliver value to both .
- A possible mnemonic to remember the objectives of the stakeholders for SOA is:

  - Business stakeholders: AIDC (Agility, Innovation, Differentiation, Cost reduction)
  - Technical stakeholders: QESG (Quality, Efficiency, Standardization, Governance)

- A possible learning trick to remember the objectives of the stakeholders for SOA is:

  - Imagine a scenario where a business stakeholder wants to launch a new product or service in the market, and needs to integrate and orchestrate various IT assets to support it. The business stakeholder would want to achieve agility, innovation, differentiation, and cost reduction by using SOA as a solution. For example, the business stakeholder could reuse existing services, create new services, and compose them into a business process using a service bus or an orchestration engine. This would enable the business stakeholder to launch the new product or service faster, more creatively, more uniquely, and more cheaply than the competitors.
  - Imagine a scenario where a technical stakeholder wants to improve the quality, efficiency, standardization, and governance of the IT assets, and needs to design and develop them using SOA principles and best practices. The technical stakeholder would want to achieve quality, efficiency, standardization, and governance by using SOA as a solution. For example, the technical stakeholder could modularize the IT assets into services, decouple them using loose coupling and contracts, and abstract them using interfaces and policies. This would enable the technical stakeholder to improve the reliability, scalability, security, and interoperability of the IT assets, and to manage them using a service registry or a governance framework.
