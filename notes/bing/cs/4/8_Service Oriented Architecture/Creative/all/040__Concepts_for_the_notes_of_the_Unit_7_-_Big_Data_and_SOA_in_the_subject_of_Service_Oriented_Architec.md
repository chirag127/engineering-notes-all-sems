### Concepts for the notes of the Unit 7 - Big Data and SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Big Data is a term that refers to the large and complex datasets that are generated from various sources and require advanced techniques and technologies to store, process, and analyze.
- SOA (Service-Oriented Architecture) is a business-centric architectural approach that supports integrating business data and processes by creating reusable components of functionality, or services.
- Big Data and SOA can be combined to create data services, which are services that provide access to data sources, perform data manipulation and transformation, and expose data in a standardized and consistent way.
- Data services can enable SOA to leverage Big Data for various purposes, such as:
  - Improving business intelligence and analytics by providing timely and accurate data from multiple sources.
  - Enhancing customer experience and personalization by using data to understand customer behavior, preferences, and needs.
  - Optimizing business processes and operations by using data to monitor performance, identify bottlenecks, and automate tasks.
  - Innovating new products and services by using data to discover new opportunities, trends, and patterns.
- Data services can also benefit from SOA principles, such as:
  - Loose coupling, which means that data services are independent and can communicate with each other through standard interfaces and protocols.
  - Reusability, which means that data services can be shared and reused by multiple consumers and applications.
  - Abstraction, which means that data services hide the implementation details and complexity of the underlying data sources and provide a simple and consistent view of the data.
  - Discoverability, which means that data services can be easily found and accessed by using a service registry or a catalog.
- Some of the challenges and best practices of creating and using data services in SOA are:
  - Data quality, which means that data services should ensure that the data they provide is accurate, complete, consistent, and reliable.
  - Data security, which means that data services should protect the data they access and expose from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure.
  - Data governance, which means that data services should follow the policies and standards for data management, ownership, usage, and lifecycle.
  - Data integration, which means that data services should be able to handle heterogeneous and distributed data sources and formats.
  - Data performance, which means that data services should be able to handle large volumes and high velocities of data and provide fast and scalable data access.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the benefits of data services in SOA is **BICEP** (Business intelligence, Customer experience, Process optimization, and Product innovation).
- A possible mnemonic to remember the SOA principles for data services is **LARD** (Loose coupling, Abstraction, Reusability, and Discoverability).
- A possible mnemonic to remember the challenges and best practices of data services in SOA is **QSGIP** (Quality, Security, Governance, Integration, and Performance).
