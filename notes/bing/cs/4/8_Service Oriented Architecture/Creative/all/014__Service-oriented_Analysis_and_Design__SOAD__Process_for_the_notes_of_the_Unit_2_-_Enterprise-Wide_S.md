### Service-oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) Process for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Service-oriented analysis and design (SOAD) is a methodology that refers to the modeling and design for service-oriented architecture (SOA) applications.
- A SOAD approach in designing SOA applications requires the following key elements:
  - Identification of business processes and services that support them
  - Specification of service contracts and interfaces
  - Composition and orchestration of services into business processes
  - Implementation and deployment of services and processes
- SOAD aims to achieve the following benefits:
  - Reuse of existing services and processes
  - Loose coupling and interoperability of services and processes
  - Adaptability and flexibility of services and processes
  - Alignment of business and IT goals and requirements
- SOAD involves the following phases:
  - Service identification: This phase identifies the business processes and the services that support them. It also analyzes the commonality and variability of the services among different contexts and clients. It uses techniques such as business process modeling, use case modeling, and feature modeling.
  - Service specification: This phase specifies the service contracts and interfaces, which define the functionality, quality, and policies of the services. It also defines the service dependencies and collaborations. It uses techniques such as service contract modeling, service interface modeling, and service dependency modeling.
  - Service realization: This phase realizes the service contracts and interfaces by composing and orchestrating existing or new services into business processes. It also implements and deploys the services and processes. It uses techniques such as service composition modeling, service orchestration modeling, and service implementation modeling.
- SOAD can be applied to different types of services, such as business services, application services, and infrastructure services.
- SOAD can be supported by various tools and standards, such as Unified Modeling Language (UML), Business Process Modeling Notation (BPMN), Service Component Architecture (SCA), and Web Services Description Language (WSDL).

- A possible mnemonic to remember the phases of SOAD is **SIS** (Service Identification, Service Specification, Service Realization).
- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of SOAD is to compare it with object-oriented analysis and design (OOAD), which is a similar methodology for developing object-oriented applications. The main differences are:
  - SOAD focuses on modeling services and processes, while OOAD focuses on modeling objects and classes.
  - SOAD promotes loose coupling and interoperability between service providers and consumers, while OOAD promotes encapsulation and inheritance between objects and classes.
  - SOAD supports adaptability and flexibility of services and processes, while OOAD supports modularity and reusability of objects and classes.
  - SOAD aligns business and IT goals and requirements, while OOAD aligns system and user goals and requirements.
