### Object-oriented Analysis and Design (OOAD) Process for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Object-oriented analysis and design (OOAD) is a software engineering approach that models a system as a group of interacting objects.
- Each object represents some entity of interest in the system being modeled, and is characterized by its class, its state (data elements), and its behavior.
- OOAD applies the object-oriented paradigm throughout the entire software development life cycle, from analysis to design to implementation.
- OOAD uses visual modeling techniques, such as Unified Modeling Language (UML), to facilitate stakeholder communication and product quality .
- OOAD consists of two main activities: object-oriented analysis (OOA) and object-oriented design (OOD).
- OOA is the process of identifying and defining the objects, their attributes, methods, and relationships in the problem domain.
- OOD is the process of refining and expanding the OOA models to make them more suitable for implementation.
- OOAD follows an iterative and incremental process, where the requirements and solutions are developed and refined through multiple cycles of analysis, design, and feedback.
- OOAD can be applied to various types of systems, such as applications, business processes, or service-oriented architectures (SOA) .
- SOA is an architectural style that defines a system as a collection of loosely coupled services that communicate through standardized interfaces.
- OOAD can help design SOA systems by identifying the services, their inputs, outputs, and dependencies, and modeling them as objects and components.
- OOAD can also help ensure that the SOA system is modular, reusable, scalable, and maintainable.

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- One possible mnemonic to remember the steps of OOAD is **AID** (Analysis, Design, Implementation).
- Another possible mnemonic to remember the main concepts of OOAD is **COBRA** (Class, Object, Behavior, Relationship, Attribute).
- A learning trick to apply OOAD is to use the **CRC** (Class-Responsibility-Collaboration) cards technique, which is a brainstorming tool that helps identify and organize the classes, their responsibilities, and their collaborations.
- Another learning trick to apply OOAD is to use the **4+1** view model, which is a way of describing a software system from different perspectives: logical, development, process, physical, and scenarios.
