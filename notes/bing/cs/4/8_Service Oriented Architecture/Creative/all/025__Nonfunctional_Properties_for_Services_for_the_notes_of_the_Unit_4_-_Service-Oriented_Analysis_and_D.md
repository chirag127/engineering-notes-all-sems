### Nonfunctional Properties for Services for the notes of the Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Nonfunctional properties (NFPs) for a service are the qualities and features that are desirable by the service users, but are not directly related to the functional behavior of the service.
- NFPs can include aspects such as availability, performance, reliability, security, scalability, usability, maintainability, etc.
- NFPs are often hidden or transparent to service users, but they can affect the user satisfaction and the service value.
- NFPs can also influence the service selection, composition, and adaptation, as different services may have different NFPs for the same functionality.
- NFPs can be specified in service level agreements (SLAs), which are contracts between service providers and consumers that define the expected quality of service and the metrics for measuring and reporting it.
- NFPs can be classified into two categories: intrinsic and extrinsic.
  - Intrinsic NFPs are the properties that are inherent to the service implementation and execution, such as response time, throughput, availability, etc.
  - Extrinsic NFPs are the properties that depend on the context and the environment of the service, such as price, payment, trust, reputation, etc.
- NFPs can be described using formal or informal methods, such as natural language, UML diagrams, XML schemas, ontologies, etc.
- NFPs can be verified and validated using various techniques, such as testing, monitoring, simulation, analysis, etc.

Here is a mnemonic to remember the types of NFPs: **APRUSMS** (Availability, Performance, Reliability, Usability, Scalability, Maintainability, Security).

Here is an example of a table that compares the NFPs of two web services that provide weather information:

| NFP | Service A | Service B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability | 99.9% | 99.5% |
| Response time | 1 second | 2 seconds |
| Accuracy | 90% | 95% |
| Price | $0.01 per request | $0.02 per request |
| Reputation | 4.5/5 | 4/5 |

Based on this table, a service consumer can choose the service that best suits their needs and preferences. For example, if they value accuracy and price more than availability and response time, they may prefer Service B over Service A.
