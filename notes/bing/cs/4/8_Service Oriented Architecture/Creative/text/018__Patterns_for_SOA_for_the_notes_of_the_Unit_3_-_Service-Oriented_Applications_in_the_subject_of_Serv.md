### Patterns for SOA

- Patterns for SOA are reusable solutions to common design problems in service-oriented architecture (SOA).
- Patterns for SOA can help architects and developers plan, implement, deploy, operate, and maintain complex systems that feature flexibility, availability, and scalability.
- Patterns for SOA can be classified into different categories, such as:
  - **Service design patterns**: These patterns address the design of individual services, such as their granularity, interface, contract, logic, and composition.
  - **Service inventory design patterns**: These patterns address the design of collections of services that belong to the same domain or enterprise, such as their governance, standardization, interoperability, and quality of service.
  - **Service composition design patterns**: These patterns address the design of service compositions, such as their orchestration, choreography, transaction, and compensation.
  - **Service implementation patterns**: These patterns address the implementation of services using specific technologies, such as web services, REST, or microservices.
  - **Service security patterns**: These patterns address the security of services and service compositions, such as their authentication, authorization, encryption, and auditing.
  - **Service management patterns**: These patterns address the management of services and service compositions, such as their monitoring, logging, testing, and versioning.

- Some examples of patterns for SOA are:
  - **Agnostic service**: A service that implements logic that is common to multiple business problems and can be reused by different consumers.
  - **Service facade**: A service that provides a simplified and standardized interface to a complex or legacy system or a service composition.
  - **Enterprise service bus (ESB)**: A middleware platform that provides integration, routing, transformation, and mediation capabilities for services and service compositions.
  - **Service callback**: A service that invokes another service and provides a callback address for the invoked service to send the response asynchronously.
  - **Multiple service contracts**: A service that supports multiple contracts for different consumers, each with different levels of abstraction, granularity, and functionality.
  - **Authentication broker**: A service that centralizes the authentication of service consumers and issues security tokens that can be used by other services.
