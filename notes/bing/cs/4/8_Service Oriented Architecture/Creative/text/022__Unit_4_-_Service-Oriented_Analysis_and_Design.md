## Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design

- Service-oriented analysis and design (SOAD) is a methodology for developing software systems that are composed of loosely coupled, interoperable, and reusable services.
- SOAD aims to align the business and technical aspects of a system, by identifying the business processes, goals, and requirements, and then designing the services that support them.
- SOAD follows an iterative and incremental approach, where the system is developed in cycles of analysis, design, implementation, testing, and deployment.
- SOAD consists of the following phases:

  - **Service identification**: This phase involves identifying the potential services that can be part of the system, based on the business processes, goals, and requirements. The services should be cohesive, loosely coupled, reusable, and aligned with the business domain.
  - **Service specification**: This phase involves defining the interface and contract of each service, specifying the inputs, outputs, operations, and quality of service (QoS) attributes. The service specification should be platform-independent, technology-neutral, and standardized.
  - **Service realization**: This phase involves designing and implementing the service logic, using the appropriate technology and platform. The service realization should follow the service specification, and adhere to the principles of service-orientation, such as abstraction, autonomy, discoverability, reusability, and composability.
  - **Service testing**: This phase involves verifying and validating the functionality, performance, reliability, and security of each service, using various testing techniques and tools. The service testing should ensure that the service meets the expectations of the consumers and the providers, and conforms to the service contract.
  - **Service deployment**: This phase involves deploying the service to the production environment, where it can be accessed and consumed by the clients. The service deployment should consider the scalability, availability, and maintainability of the service, and follow the best practices for service governance and management.
