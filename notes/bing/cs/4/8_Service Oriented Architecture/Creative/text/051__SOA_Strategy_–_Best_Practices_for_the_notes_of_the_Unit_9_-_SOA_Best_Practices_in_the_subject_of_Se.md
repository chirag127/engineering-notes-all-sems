### SOA Strategy – Best Practices

- A SOA strategy is a plan that defines the vision, goals, principles, and roadmap for implementing and governing a service-oriented architecture (SOA) in an organization.
- A SOA strategy should align with the business strategy, IT strategy, and enterprise architecture of the organization, and should address the following aspects:
  - **Business value**: Identify the expected benefits and outcomes of SOA, such as improved agility, efficiency, quality, and innovation.
  - **Scope and prioritization**: Define the scope and boundaries of SOA, and prioritize the business domains, processes, and capabilities that will be service-enabled.
  - **Service portfolio**: Define the service inventory, service taxonomy, service lifecycle, and service ownership for the SOA.
  - **Service design and development**: Establish the standards, guidelines, and best practices for designing, developing, testing, and deploying services, such as service granularity, modularity, reusability, interoperability, security, and performance.
  - **Service governance**: Define the policies, roles, responsibilities, and processes for managing and controlling the SOA, such as service identification, specification, publication, discovery, consumption, monitoring, and evolution.
  - **Service infrastructure**: Define the technical architecture, platforms, tools, and frameworks that will support the SOA, such as service bus, registry, repository, orchestration, and mediation.
  - **Service culture**: Define the organizational structure, culture, and change management that will enable and sustain the SOA, such as service orientation, collaboration, communication, and education.
- A SOA strategy should be developed and executed in an iterative and incremental manner, following the principles of agile and lean methodologies, and should be continuously monitored and evaluated for its effectiveness and alignment with the changing business and IT needs.
