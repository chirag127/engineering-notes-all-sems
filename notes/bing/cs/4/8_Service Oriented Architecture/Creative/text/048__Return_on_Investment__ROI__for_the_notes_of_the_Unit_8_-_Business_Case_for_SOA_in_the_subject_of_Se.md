### Return on Investment (ROI) for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Return on Investment (ROI) is a measure of the profitability and efficiency of an investment, such as a Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) project.
- ROI is calculated by dividing the net benefits (benefits minus costs) of the investment by the total costs of the investment.
- ROI can be expressed as a percentage or a ratio.
- ROI can help to evaluate the feasibility and desirability of an SOA project, by comparing the expected benefits and costs of the project with the current situation or alternative solutions.
- ROI can also help to monitor and control the performance and progress of an SOA project, by comparing the actual benefits and costs of the project with the planned or expected ones.
- ROI can be influenced by various factors, such as the scope, duration, quality, risks, and assumptions of the SOA project, as well as the external environment and stakeholders' expectations.
- ROI can be estimated at different stages of the SOA project lifecycle, such as the initiation, planning, execution, and closure stages.
- ROI can be calculated using different methods, such as the payback period, the net present value, the internal rate of return, and the benefit-cost ratio.
- ROI can be presented using different formats, such as tables, charts, graphs, and dashboards.
- ROI can be communicated to different audiences, such as the project team, the management, the sponsors, the customers, and the suppliers, using different techniques, such as reports, presentations, meetings, and workshops.
