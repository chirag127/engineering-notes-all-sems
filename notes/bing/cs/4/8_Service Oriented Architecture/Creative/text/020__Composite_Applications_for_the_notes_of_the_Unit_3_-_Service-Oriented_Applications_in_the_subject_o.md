### Composite Applications

- A composite application is a software system that is composed of multiple independent and loosely coupled components or services that interact to provide a higher-level functionality.
- Composite applications can leverage the benefits of service-oriented architecture (SOA), such as reusability, interoperability, scalability, and agility.
- Composite applications can be designed and implemented using various technologies and standards, such as web services, RESTful services, microservices, enterprise service bus (ESB), service orchestration, service choreography, and business process management (BPM).
- Composite applications can be classified into different types based on the level of integration and coordination among the components or services, such as:
  - Data integration: The components or services exchange data through a common data model or format, such as XML or JSON.
  - Functional integration: The components or services invoke each other through a common interface or protocol, such as SOAP or HTTP.
  - Process integration: The components or services coordinate their activities through a common process model or language, such as BPEL or BPMN.
  - User interface integration: The components or services present a unified user interface to the end-users, such as a web portal or a dashboard.
- Composite applications can also be classified into different types based on the degree of coupling and autonomy among the components or services, such as:
  - Tightly coupled: The components or services depend on each other and have to be deployed and executed together, such as a monolithic application.
  - Loosely coupled: The components or services are independent and can be deployed and executed separately, such as a microservice-based application.
  - Decoupled: The components or services are unaware of each other and communicate through an intermediary, such as an ESB or a message broker.
- Composite applications can provide various advantages over traditional applications, such as:
  - Faster development and deployment: The components or services can be developed and deployed independently and in parallel, reducing the time and cost of software development life cycle (SDLC).
  - Easier maintenance and evolution: The components or services can be updated and replaced without affecting the whole system, improving the quality and reliability of the software.
  - Higher flexibility and adaptability: The components or services can be configured and composed dynamically based on the changing business needs and requirements, enhancing the business agility and innovation.
