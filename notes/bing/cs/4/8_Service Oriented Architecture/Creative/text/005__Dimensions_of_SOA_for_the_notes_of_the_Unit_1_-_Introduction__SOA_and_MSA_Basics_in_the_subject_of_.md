### Dimensions of SOA

- Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural approach in which applications make use of services available in the network.
- Services are self-contained, loosely coupled, reusable, and interoperable components that provide specific functionality and can be composed to create complex business processes.
- SOA has several dimensions that need to be considered when designing, developing, and testing SOA-based applications. They include:

  - **Service dimension**: This dimension focuses on the individual services that constitute the SOA application. It involves identifying, specifying, implementing, and testing the services according to the service contract, which defines the interface, behavior, and quality of service of the service. The service dimension also covers the aspects of service discovery, registration, and governance, which enable the service consumers to find and use the appropriate services.
  - **Process dimension**: This dimension focuses on the orchestration and choreography of services to create business processes that fulfill the business requirements. It involves defining, modeling, executing, and monitoring the business processes using standards such as Business Process Execution Language (BPEL) and Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN). The process dimension also covers the aspects of process optimization, exception handling, and transaction management, which ensure the reliability and efficiency of the business processes.
  - **Performance dimension**: This dimension focuses on the non-functional requirements of the SOA application, such as scalability, availability, security, and reliability. It involves measuring, analyzing, and improving the performance of the services and the business processes using tools and techniques such as load testing, stress testing, and performance monitoring. The performance dimension also covers the aspects of service level agreement (SLA), which defines the expected performance and quality of service of the service providers and the service consumers.
