### Standards and Guidelines for SOA

- Standards and guidelines are important for ensuring the quality, consistency, interoperability, and security of service-oriented architecture (SOA).
- Standards are formal specifications that define the rules, formats, protocols, and interfaces for service design, development, and communication. Standards are mandatory and enforceable by authorities or organizations.
- Guidelines are informal recommendations that suggest the best practices, principles, and methods for service-oriented architecture. Guidelines are optional and adaptable by service providers and consumers.
- Some examples of standards and guidelines for SOA are:

  - **Guiding Principles of SOA**: These are eight principles that describe the essential characteristics and goals of service-oriented architecture. They are:

    - Standardized service contract: Specified through one or more service description documents.
    - Loose coupling: Services are designed as self-contained components, maintain relationships that minimize dependencies on other services.
    - Abstraction: A service is completely defined by service contracts and description documents. They hide their logic, which is encapsulated within their implementation.
    - Reusability: Services are designed to be reused across different contexts and domains.
    - Autonomy: Services have control over their own logic and resources, and are not affected by external factors or changes.
    - Statelessness: Services minimize the retention of information specific to an activity, and manage their state information efficiently.
    - Discoverability: Services are supplemented with metadata that allows them to be effectively discovered and interpreted.
    - Composability: Services are designed to be composed into coordinated service compositions, and can participate in complex business processes.

  - **ISO/IEC 27001:2013**: This is an international standard that specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving an information security management system (ISMS) within an organization. It also includes the Statement of Applicability (SoA), which is a document that identifies the information security controls that are applicable to the organization's scope and objectives.

  - **SOA Continuing Professional Development (CPD) Requirement**: This is a requirement for SOA members to maintain and enhance their professional competence and ethical standards. SOA members can fulfill the SOA CPD requirement using one of the following five methods:

    - The Basic Requirement provisions of the SOA Continuing Professional Development (CPD) requirement found in Section B
    - The continuing education requirements of the U.S. Qualification Standard
    - The continuing professional development requirements of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries
    - The continuing professional development requirements of another International Actuarial Association (IAA) full member organization
    - The Alternative Compliance provisions of the SOA Continuing Professional Development (CPD) requirement found in Section C

  - **APA-Approved Standards and Guidelines**: These are pronouncements, statements, or declarations that suggest or recommend specific professional behavior, endeavor, or conduct for psychologists or for individuals or organizations that work with psychologists. They are aspirational in intent and provide guidance for ethical and professional practice.

  - **Guide to SOA Written Exams**: This is a document that provides information and guidance for candidates who are preparing for the SOA written exams. It covers the exam structure, format, content, grading, and resources. It also explains the syllabus, learning objectives, and core readings for each exam.
