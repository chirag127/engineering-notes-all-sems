## Unit 3 - Service-Oriented Applications

- A service-oriented application is an application that consists of multiple services that communicate with each other over a network.
- A service is a self-contained, reusable, and loosely coupled unit of functionality that provides a specific capability or value to its consumers.
- A service can be implemented using any technology, platform, or language, as long as it adheres to a well-defined interface and contract.
- A service can be invoked by other services or by clients using various protocols, such as HTTP, SOAP, REST, or messaging.
- A service can be composed of other services, forming a service composition or orchestration, which can provide higher-level functionality and value to its consumers.
- A service can be deployed, managed, and scaled independently of other services, enabling greater flexibility, agility, and availability.
- A service can be governed by policies and standards that define its quality of service, security, reliability, and interoperability.
- A service can be monitored and measured by metrics that indicate its performance, usage, and health.
- A service can be discovered and registered by a service registry or repository, which facilitates service discovery and reuse.
- A service can be designed and developed following a service-oriented architecture (SOA) approach, which is a set of principles and best practices that guide the creation and evolution of service-oriented applications.
- A service can be tested and verified using various techniques and tools, such as unit testing, integration testing, contract testing, and service virtualization.
- A service can be documented and described using various formats and standards, such as WSDL, Swagger, or OpenAPI.
