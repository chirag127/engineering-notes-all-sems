### Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture

- Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture is a set of guidelines and options for making architectural, design, and implementation decisions in the implementation of solutions based on Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) principles and techniques.
- SOA is an architectural style that facilitates the creation of flexible, re-usable assets for enabling end-to-end business solutions.
- Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture has nine layers representing nine key clusters of considerations and responsibilities that typically emerge in the process of designing an SOA solution or defining an enterprise architecture standard.
- The nine layers are:
  - Operational Systems Layer: This layer contains the existing systems and data sources that provide the functionality and information for the business processes.
  - Service Components Layer: This layer contains the service components that encapsulate the business logic and data access for the services. Service components can be implemented using various technologies and platforms, such as Java, .NET, or legacy systems.
  - Services Layer: This layer contains the services that expose the functionality and information of the service components to the consumers. Services are defined using standard interfaces and protocols, such as SOAP, REST, or JMS.
  - Business Process Layer: This layer contains the business processes that orchestrate the services to achieve the business goals. Business processes can be modeled using standard languages, such as BPMN or BPEL, and executed by a process engine.
  - Consumer Layer: This layer contains the consumers that invoke the services and participate in the business processes. Consumers can be human users or applications, such as web portals, mobile apps, or enterprise systems.
  - Integration Layer: This layer contains the integration components that enable the communication and mediation between the services and the consumers. Integration components can provide functions such as routing, transformation, security, or monitoring.
  - Quality of Service Layer: This layer contains the quality of service components that ensure the reliability, availability, performance, and scalability of the services and the business processes. Quality of service components can provide functions such as load balancing, caching, fault tolerance, or clustering.
  - Governance Layer: This layer contains the governance components that manage the lifecycle, policies, and standards of the services and the business processes. Governance components can provide functions such as registry, repository, auditing, or reporting.
  - Information Layer: This layer contains the information components that provide the common data model, semantics, and vocabulary for the services and the business processes. Information components can provide functions such as data mapping, validation, or enrichment.
- Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture applies to each of the components of the enterprise and describes aspects that they have in common. The architect would produce the Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture in parallel with the Enterprise Architecture, but as a separate set of artifacts.
