### Service Run-time Governance

- Service run-time governance is the process of monitoring and controlling the behavior and performance of services and service compositions during their execution.
- Service run-time governance involves the following aspects:
  - Service availability: ensuring that services are accessible and responsive to requests from service consumers.
  - Service reliability: ensuring that services deliver correct and consistent results to service consumers.
  - Service security: ensuring that services are protected from unauthorized access and malicious attacks, and that service consumers are authenticated and authorized to use services.
  - Service scalability: ensuring that services can handle varying and increasing workloads without compromising quality of service.
  - Service compliance: ensuring that services adhere to predefined policies and standards, and that service consumers respect the service contracts and service level agreements.
- Service run-time governance requires the use of various tools and mechanisms, such as:
  - Service registry and repository: a central location where service metadata, policies, contracts, and other artifacts are stored and managed.
  - Service bus: a communication infrastructure that enables service discovery, routing, mediation, and transformation.
  - Service monitor: a component that collects and analyzes service metrics, such as response time, throughput, availability, and errors.
  - Service auditor: a component that records and verifies service transactions, such as request and response messages, and service invocation details.
  - Service policy enforcement point: a component that intercepts and validates service requests and responses, and enforces service policies, such as authentication, authorization, encryption, and throttling.
  - Service policy decision point: a component that evaluates service policies and provides decisions to the service policy enforcement point.
  - Service orchestrator: a component that coordinates and controls the execution of service compositions, such as business processes and workflows.
  - Service adapter: a component that enables the integration of legacy systems and applications with services.
