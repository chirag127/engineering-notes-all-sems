### Service Oriented Architecture and Microservices Architecture

- Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is a software design approach that aims to create reusable and interoperable software components, or services, that can be combined to form complex applications.
- Microservices Architecture (MSA) is a software design approach that aims to create small, independent and loosely coupled services, each focusing on a single business capability or domain, that can communicate through lightweight protocols.
- Both SOA and MSA rely on services as the main building blocks of software systems, but they differ in the following aspects :
  - Scope: SOA has an enterprise-wide scope, while MSA has an application-specific scope. SOA services are designed to be shared and reused across different applications and business domains, while MSA services are designed to be specific and isolated to a single application or domain.
  - Size: SOA services are coarse-grained, meaning they have a large and complex functionality, while MSA services are fine-grained, meaning they have a small and simple functionality. SOA services typically have a large number of operations and data elements, while MSA services typically have a few operations and data elements.
  - Coupling: SOA services are loosely coupled, meaning they have minimal dependencies and interactions with other services, while MSA services are even more loosely coupled, meaning they have no dependencies and interactions with other services. SOA services may share some common data models and standards, while MSA services have their own data models and standards.
  - Communication: SOA services communicate through standardized and centralized protocols, such as SOAP and WS-*, while MSA services communicate through decentralized and lightweight protocols, such as REST and JSON. SOA services may use an Enterprise Service Bus (ESB) or a Service Registry to facilitate communication and discovery, while MSA services may use a Service Mesh or an API Gateway to facilitate communication and discovery.
  - Governance: SOA services are governed by a centralized and formal governance model, such as a Service Level Agreement (SLA) or a Service Contract, while MSA services are governed by a decentralized and informal governance model, such as a Service Level Objective (SLO) or a Service Level Indicator (SLI). SOA services are managed by a central authority or a governance board, while MSA services are managed by autonomous and self-organizing teams.
  - Deployment: SOA services are deployed as monolithic units, meaning they are packaged and deployed together as a single unit, while MSA services are deployed as independent units, meaning they are packaged and deployed separately as individual units. SOA services are deployed on a fixed and predefined infrastructure, while MSA services are deployed on a dynamic and scalable infrastructure. SOA services are deployed infrequently and synchronously, while MSA services are deployed frequently and asynchronously.
