### Steganography

- Steganography is the art or practice of concealing a message, image, or file within another message, image, or file  .
- Steganography can be used along with cryptography as an extra-secure method to protect data.
- Steganography can be applied to various types of media, such as images, audio, video, text, or network packets  .
- Steganography techniques can be classified into two categories: spatial domain and frequency domain.
  - Spatial domain techniques modify the least significant bits (LSB) of the pixels or bits of the cover media to embed the secret message.
  - Frequency domain techniques transform the cover media into a frequency representation using algorithms such as discrete cosine transform (DCT) or discrete wavelet transform (DWT) and then embed the secret message in the coefficients.
- Steganography can be used for various purposes, such as hiding sensitive information, watermarking, authentication, or covert communication .
- Steganography can also be used for malicious purposes, such as hiding malware, spyware, or cyber attacks .
- Steganography can be detected and analyzed by using steganalysis techniques, which aim to reveal the existence, location, and content of the hidden message .
- Steganalysis techniques can be classified into two categories: passive and active.
  - Passive steganalysis only observes the cover media and tries to find statistical anomalies or artifacts that indicate steganography.
  - Active steganalysis modifies the cover media and tries to extract or destroy the hidden message.
