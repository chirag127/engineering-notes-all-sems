### Introduction to group

- A group is a set of elements with a binary operation that satisfies four properties: closure, associativity, identity and inverse .
- A group is called abelian or commutative if the binary operation is also commutative, that is, for any two elements a and b in the group, a * b = b * a .
- A group is called cyclic if there exists an element g in the group such that every other element can be obtained by repeated application of the binary operation on g or its inverse .
- A group is called finite if it has a finite number of elements, otherwise it is called infinite .
- A group is a very general algebraic object and most cryptographic schemes use groups in some way  .
- In particular, Diffie–Hellman key exchange uses finite cyclic groups .
- Group-based cryptography refers mostly to cryptographic protocols that use infinite nonabelian groups such as a braid group .
- Groups are used to describe symmetries of objects in physics and chemistry, and to construct pseudorandom number generators, digital signatures, zero-knowledge proofs, and other cryptographic primitives .
