### Data Encryption Standard (DES)

- Data Encryption Standard (DES) is a **symmetric-key block cipher** that was published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in 1977 as a Federal Information Processing Standard (FIPS)   .
- DES was designed by IBM researchers in the early 1970s and was based on a **Feistel cipher** structure  .
- DES takes a **64-bit** plaintext block and encrypts it into a **64-bit** ciphertext block using a **56-bit** key   .
- DES performs **16 rounds** of encryption, each involving a series of steps such as **substitution**, **permutation**, **expansion**, **XOR** and **key generation**  .
- DES has been widely used for data encryption in various applications, such as banking, e-commerce, military and government  .
- DES has been proven to be **insecure** for modern applications, as it can be broken by **brute-force attacks** using powerful computers or specialized hardware   .
- DES has been replaced by more secure and efficient algorithms, such as **Advanced Encryption Standard (AES)**, which is also a symmetric-key block cipher published by NIST  .
- DES has been extended to **Triple DES (3DES)**, which applies DES three times with different keys, resulting in a **168-bit** effective key length  .
- DES has been influential in the advancement of cryptography, as it stimulated the development of new cryptanalytic techniques, such as **differential cryptanalysis** and **linear cryptanalysis**  .
- DES can operate in different **modes of operation**, such as **Electronic Codebook (ECB)**, **Cipher Block Chaining (CBC)**, **Cipher Feedback (CFB)** and **Output Feedback (OFB)**, which affect the security and performance of the encryption  .
