### Field

- A field is a mathematical structure that allows us to perform addition, subtraction, multiplication and division operations on its elements.
- A field has two operations, usually denoted by + and ×, that satisfy the following properties:
  - Closure: For any two elements a and b in the field, a + b and a × b are also in the field.
  - Associativity: For any three elements a, b and c in the field, (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) and (a × b) × c = a × (b × c).
  - Commutativity: For any two elements a and b in the field, a + b = b + a and a × b = b × a.
  - Distributivity: For any three elements a, b and c in the field, a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c).
  - Identity: There exist two distinct elements 0 and 1 in the field such that for any element a in the field, a + 0 = a and a × 1 = a.
  - Inverse: For any element a in the field, there exists an element -a in the field such that a + (-a) = 0. For any non-zero element a in the field, there exists an element a^-1 in the field such that a × a^-1 = 1.
  - Non-zero divisors: For any two non-zero elements a and b in the field, a × b ≠ 0.
- A field is a special case of a ring, which is a special case of a group. A group is a set with one operation that satisfies closure, associativity, identity and inverse. A ring is a set with two operations that satisfy closure, associativity, commutativity, distributivity and identity.
- Fields are important in cryptography because they provide a convenient and secure way to manipulate data and perform computations. Many cryptographic algorithms and protocols are based on the properties of fields, such as modular arithmetic, finite fields, elliptic curves, etc.
- Some examples of fields are the rational numbers, the real numbers, the complex numbers, and the finite fields of the form GF(p), where p is a prime number.
