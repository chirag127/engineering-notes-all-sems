### Discrete Logarithmic Problem for the notes of the Unit 2 - Introduction to group, field, finite field of the form GF(p), modular arithmetic, prime and relative prime numbers, Extended Euclidean Algorithm, Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) encryption and decryptionFermat

- The discrete logarithm problem is defined as: given a group G, a generator g of the group and an element h of G, to find the discrete logarithm to the base g of h in the group G.
- The discrete logarithm problem is not always hard. The hardness of finding discrete logarithms depends on the groups.
- Discrete logarithms are perhaps simplest to understand in the group Zp*, where p is a prime number. Let g be the generator of Zp*, then the discrete logarithm problem reduces to computing a, given (g, p, ga mod p) for a randomly chosen a < (p −1).
- The discrete logarithm problem is considered to be computationally intractable. That is, no efficient classical algorithm is known for computing discrete logarithms in general.
- A general algorithm for computing log b a in finite groups G is to raise b to larger and larger powers k until the desired a is found. This is called the **brute-force** or **exhaustive search** algorithm. It has a time complexity of O(|G|) and a space complexity of O(1).
- A more efficient algorithm for computing log b a in finite groups G is the **baby-step giant-step** algorithm. It has a time complexity of O(√|G|) and a space complexity of O(√|G|). It works by dividing the search space into two parts: the baby steps and the giant steps. The baby steps are the powers of b from 0 to √|G|, and the giant steps are the powers of b−√|G| from 0 to √|G|. The algorithm then tries to find a match between a baby step and a giant step, such that a = b^i * (b−√|G|)^j, where i and j are integers. If such a match is found, then log b a = i + j√|G|.
- A mnemonic for remembering the baby-step giant-step algorithm is: **B**aby steps are **B**ase powers, **G**iant steps are **G**roup inverses.
- A randomized algorithm for computing log b a in finite groups G is the **Pollard's rho** algorithm. It has a time complexity of O(√|G|) and a space complexity of O(1). It works by using a pseudo-random function to generate a sequence of elements in G, and then finding a collision in the sequence, such that x_i = x_2i, where x_i is the i-th element in the sequence. If such a collision is found, then log b a can be computed by solving a linear equation.
- A mnemonic for remembering the Pollard's rho algorithm is: **P**ollard's rho uses a **P**seudo-random function and finds a **C**ollision.
- The discrete logarithm problem is the basis of many cryptographic schemes, such as the Diffie-Hellman key exchange, the ElGamal encryption and signature, and the Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA).
