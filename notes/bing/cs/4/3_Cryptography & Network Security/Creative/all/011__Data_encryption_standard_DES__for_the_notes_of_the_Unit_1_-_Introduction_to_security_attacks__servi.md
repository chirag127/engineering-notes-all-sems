- Data encryption standard (DES) is a symmetric-key algorithm for the encryption and decryption of digital data .
- DES was developed by IBM in 1974 and adopted by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in 1977 as a federal standard for securing sensitive information .
- DES operates on 64-bit blocks of data, using a 56-bit key (with 8 parity bits) to produce a 64-bit ciphertext .
- DES is based on the two fundamental attributes of cryptography: substitution (also called confusion) and transposition (also called diffusion) .
- Substitution is the process of replacing one element of the plaintext with another, according to a predefined rule or table .
- Transposition is the process of rearranging the order of the elements of the plaintext, according to a predefined pattern or permutation .
- DES consists of 16 steps, each of which is called a round . Each round performs the steps of substitution and transposition, as well as key mixing and expansion .
- The overall structure of DES is called a Feistel cipher, which is a type of block cipher that splits the input block into two halves and applies a round function to one half, using a subkey derived from the main key, and then XORs the output with the other half .
- The round function of DES consists of four stages: expansion, substitution, permutation, and key mixing .
- Expansion is the process of increasing the size of the input from 32 bits to 48 bits, by duplicating some bits according to a predefined table .
- Substitution is the process of applying eight substitution boxes (S-boxes) to the 48-bit input, each of which takes 6 bits and produces 4 bits, according to a predefined table .
- Permutation is the process of rearranging the order of the 32-bit output of the S-boxes, according to a predefined table .
- Key mixing is the process of XORing the 32-bit output of the permutation with a 32-bit subkey derived from the main key .
- The subkeys are generated by a key schedule algorithm, which takes the 56-bit main key and produces 16 subkeys, each of 48 bits, by applying a series of shifts and permutations .
- The encryption process of DES starts with an initial permutation (IP) of the 64-bit plaintext, followed by 16 rounds of the round function, and ends with a final permutation (FP) of the 64-bit ciphertext, which is the inverse of the IP .
- The decryption process of DES is the reverse of the encryption process, using the same key but in reverse order .
- DES has a fixed block size of 64 bits, which means that it can only encrypt or decrypt 64 bits of data at a time . To encrypt or decrypt larger amounts of data, different modes of operation can be used, such as electronic codebook (ECB), cipher block chaining (CBC), cipher feedback (CFB), output feedback (OFB), or counter (CTR) .
- Each mode of operation has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the security and performance requirements of the application .
- DES has been widely used for securing sensitive information, such as banking transactions, military communications, and government documents .
