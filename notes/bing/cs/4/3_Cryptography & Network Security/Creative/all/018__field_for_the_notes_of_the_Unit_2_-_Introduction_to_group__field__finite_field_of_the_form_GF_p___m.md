- A field is a mathematical structure that allows us to perform addition, subtraction, multiplication and division operations on its elements.
- A field has the following properties :
  - Closure: For any two elements a and b in the field, a + b and a * b are also in the field.
  - Associativity: For any three elements a, b and c in the field, (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) and (a * b) * c = a * (b * c).
  - Commutativity: For any two elements a and b in the field, a + b = b + a and a * b = b * a.
  - Distributivity: For any three elements a, b and c in the field, a * (b + c) = (a * b) + (a * c).
  - Identity: There exist two distinct elements 0 and 1 in the field such that for any element a in the field, a + 0 = a and a * 1 = a.
  - Inverse: For any non-zero element a in the field, there exists an element a^-1 in the field such that a * a^-1 = 1. Similarly, for any element a in the field, there exists an element -a in the field such that a + (-a) = 0.
  - Non-zero divisors: For any two non-zero elements a and b in the field, a * b is also non-zero.
- A field is a special case of a ring, which is a special case of a group. A group is a set with one operation (usually denoted by *) that satisfies closure, associativity, identity and inverse properties. A ring is a set with two operations (usually denoted by + and *) that satisfies closure, associativity, commutativity, distributivity, identity and inverse (for + only) properties. A field is a ring that also satisfies inverse (for *) and non-zero divisors properties.
- Fields are important in cryptography because they provide a convenient and efficient way to perform arithmetic operations on data, such as encryption and decryption, without losing information or security .
- A finite field is a field that has a finite number of elements. A finite field of order p, where p is a prime number, is denoted by GF(p) or Z_p. In this field, the elements are the integers from 0 to p-1, and the operations are performed modulo p .
- Modular arithmetic is a system of arithmetic where numbers wrap around after reaching a certain value, called the modulus. For example, in modulo 12 arithmetic, 13 is equivalent to 1, 14 is equivalent to 2, and so on. Modular arithmetic is used to define the operations in finite fields .
- A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself. A relative prime number or a coprime number is a natural number that has no common positive divisors with another natural number other than 1. For example, 6 and 35 are coprime because their only common divisor is 1 .
- The Extended Euclidean Algorithm is an algorithm that computes the greatest common divisor (gcd) of two natural numbers a and b, and also finds two integers x and y such that ax + by = gcd(a, b). This algorithm is useful for finding the multiplicative inverse of an element in a finite field, as well as for solving linear congruences and Diophantine equations .
