### Evolution of Cloud Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction To Cloud Computing in the subject of Cloud Computing

- Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence over the Internet, often referred to as the cloud.
- The evolution of cloud computing can be traced back to the 1950s, when the concept of utility and grid computing emerged . Utility computing is the idea of providing computing resources as a metered service, similar to a public utility. Grid computing is the idea of connecting multiple computers to form a large, distributed system that can perform parallel tasks.
- In the 1960s, John McCarthy, the father of artificial intelligence, proposed that computing should be organized as a public utility. Joseph Carl Robnett Licklider, the founder of cloud computing, envisioned a global network of interconnected computers that would allow users to access data and programs from anywhere.
- In the 1970s, the first virtual machines were developed, which allowed multiple operating systems to run on a single physical machine. This enabled the creation of virtualized environments that could be shared by different users and applications .
- In the 1980s, the Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP) was standardized, which enabled the communication between different networks and devices. This laid the foundation for the World Wide Web, which was invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989 .
- In the 1990s, the term "cloud computing" was coined by Compaq, a computer company, to describe the delivery of computing services over the Internet. The first cloud service providers emerged, such as Salesforce, which offered software as a service (SaaS), and Amazon, which offered infrastructure as a service (IaaS) .
- In the 2000s, the cloud computing industry grew rapidly, with the introduction of new technologies and models, such as platform as a service (PaaS), which allowed developers to create and deploy applications on the cloud, and serverless computing, which removed the need for managing servers and infrastructure  .
- In the 2010s, the cloud computing market became more competitive and diverse, with the emergence of new players, such as Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud Platform, IBM Cloud, and Alibaba Cloud. The cloud also became more secure, scalable, and reliable, with the adoption of encryption, containers, microservices, and artificial intelligence  .
- In the 2020s, the cloud computing industry is expected to continue to evolve and innovate, with the trends of hybrid cloud, edge computing, quantum computing, and blockchain . Hybrid cloud is the combination of public and private cloud services, which offers more flexibility and efficiency. Edge computing is the processing of data closer to the source, which reduces latency and bandwidth. Quantum computing is the use of quantum mechanical phenomena, such as superposition and entanglement, to perform computations that are impossible or impractical for classical computers. Blockchain is a distributed ledger that records transactions in a secure and transparent way, which can enable new applications and business models.
