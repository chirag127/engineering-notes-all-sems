### Tools and Mechanisms for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cloud Enabling Technologies Service Oriented Architecture in the subject of Cloud Computing

- Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural style for building software applications that use services available in a network such as the web.
- A service is a self-contained unit of functionality that can be accessed and used by other software components through a set of strictly defined application program interfaces (APIs).
- Services are loosely coupled, meaning they can be reused, modified, and composed without affecting other services or applications.
- SOA promotes interoperability, scalability, reusability, and agility in software development and deployment.
- To implement SOA, you need the following tools and mechanisms :
  - **Service Architecture**: The design of the services, their interfaces, contracts, policies, and dependencies.
  - **Service Infrastructure**: The protocols and other tools that enable communication and interoperability between services and applications, such as SOAP, REST, XML, JSON, etc.
  - **Service Registry**: A repository that stores and publishes information about the available services, such as their location, description, functionality, and quality of service.
  - **Service Discovery**: A mechanism that allows service consumers to find and select the most suitable service providers based on their requirements and preferences.
  - **Service Composition**: A mechanism that allows service consumers to combine and orchestrate multiple services to create new functionality or applications, such as using BPEL, BPMN, or ESB.
  - **Service Governance**: A set of policies, standards, and processes that ensure the quality, security, reliability, and performance of the services and their interactions.
  - **Service Monitoring**: A mechanism that collects and analyzes data about the status, performance, and usage of the services and their interactions, such as using SLA, KPI, or BAM.
  - **Service Management**: A mechanism that controls and optimizes the lifecycle of the services and their interactions, such as using ITIL, COBIT, or CMMI.

- Some examples of SOA tools are:
  - **AmberPoint's SOA Management System**: A policy-based run-time governance software suite that provides service discovery, monitoring, management, security, and mediation capabilities.
  - **IBM's WebSphere Service Registry and Repository**: A service registry that enables service publication, discovery, and governance, as well as integration with other IBM products such as WebSphere Process Server and WebSphere ESB.
  - **Oracle's SOA Suite**: A comprehensive platform that offers service development, composition, orchestration, governance, and management tools, as well as support for various standards and protocols such as BPEL, BPMN, SOAP, REST, etc.
  - **Progress Software's Actional**: A service management and governance solution that provides service visibility, monitoring, security, and mediation capabilities, as well as integration with other Progress products such as Sonic ESB and Apama CEP.
  - **Software AG's webMethods**: A platform that offers service development, composition, orchestration, governance, and management tools, as well as support for various standards and protocols such as BPEL, BPMN, SOAP, REST, etc.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for SOA are:
  - **S**ervice-**O**riented **A**rchitecture: **S**oftware **O**rganized **A**round services.
  - **SOAP**: **S**imple **O**bject **A**ccess **P**rotocol: **S**ervice **O**riented **A**pplication **P**rotocol.
  - **REST**: **RE**presentational **S**tate **T**ransfer: **RE**source-**S**ervice **T**ransaction.
  - **BPEL**: **B**usiness **P**rocess **E**xecution **L**anguage: **B**uild **P**rocesses with **E**xisting **L**ogic.
  - **BPMN**: **B**usiness **P**rocess **M**odel and **N**otation: **B**e **P**recise with **M**odels and **N**ames.
  - **ESB**: **E**nterprise **S**ervice **B**us: **E**asy **S**ervice **B**ridge.
  - **SLA**: **S**ervice **L**evel **A**
