### Security Standards for the notes of the Unit 4 - Resource Management And Security In Cloud in the subject of Cloud Computing

- Security standards are guidelines and best practices that help organizations to protect their data and resources in the cloud from various threats and risks.
- Security standards can also help to ensure compliance with legal and regulatory requirements, as well as customer expectations and trust.
- Some of the security standards that are relevant for cloud computing are:

  - **ISO/IEC 27001/27002**: These are international standards for information security management systems (ISMS) that provide a framework for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving the security of an organization's information assets. ISO/IEC 27001 specifies the requirements for an ISMS, while ISO/IEC 27002 provides a code of practice with best practices and controls for implementing the ISMS. These standards can be applied to any type of organization, including cloud service providers and consumers.  
  - **ISO/IEC 27017**: This is an extension of ISO/IEC 27001/27002 that provides additional guidance and controls for cloud security. It covers aspects such as roles and responsibilities, risk assessment, asset management, access control, cryptography, operations security, incident management, business continuity, and compliance. It also defines cloud-specific terms and concepts, such as cloud service customer, cloud service provider, cloud service user, cloud service agreement, and cloud service level agreement. ISO/IEC 27017 can help organizations to reduce the risk of a security incident in the cloud and to achieve a consistent level of security across different cloud services.  
  - **ISO/IEC 27018**: This is another extension of ISO/IEC 27001/27002 that provides guidance and controls for protecting personal data in the cloud. It covers aspects such as consent, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, retention, disclosure, accountability, and audit. It also defines cloud-specific terms and concepts, such as personally identifiable information (PII), PII processor, PII controller, and PII principal. ISO/IEC 27018 can help organizations to comply with data protection laws and regulations, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and to enhance the privacy and trust of their customers and users.  
  - **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**: This is a European Union (EU) regulation that aims to harmonize and strengthen the data protection and privacy rights of individuals in the EU and the European Economic Area (EEA). It applies to any organization that processes personal data of individuals in the EU/EEA, regardless of where the organization or the data is located. It covers aspects such as data protection principles, data subject rights, data controller and processor obligations, data protection impact assessment, data breach notification, data protection officer, data protection by design and by default, and data transfer mechanisms. It also imposes strict penalties for non-compliance, such as fines up to 20 million euros or 4% of the annual global turnover, whichever is higher. GDPR can affect cloud computing in various ways, such as requiring cloud service providers and consumers to enter into data processing agreements, to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure data security and privacy, to conduct data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing activities, to notify data breaches to the relevant authorities and data subjects, to appoint data protection officers, to adhere to data protection by design and by default principles, and to use approved data transfer mechanisms, such as standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules, or adequacy decisions, when transferring data outside the EU/EEA.  
  - **System and Organization Controls (SOC) Reporting**: This is a framework for auditing and reporting on the internal controls of service organizations, such as cloud service providers, that affect the security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy of the information and systems they provide to their customers. There are three types of SOC reports: SOC 1, SOC 2, and SOC 3. SOC 1 reports on the controls related to financial reporting, SOC 2 reports on the controls related to the Trust Services Criteria (security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy), and SOC 3 reports on the same criteria as SOC 2 but in a more general and less detailed manner. SOC reports can help cloud service providers and consumers to assess and verify the effectiveness of the cloud service provider's controls and to demonstrate compliance with various standards and regulations
