A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It is formed by joining a p-type material, which has excess holes, and an n-type material, which has excess electrons. When the diode is connected to a voltage source, the p-type material becomes the anode and the n-type material becomes the cathode. The diode has two regions of operation: forward bias and reverse bias.

In forward bias, the anode is connected to the positive terminal of the voltage source and the cathode is connected to the negative terminal. The applied voltage reduces the potential barrier at the junction and allows the majority carriers (holes and electrons) to cross the junction and flow in the same direction. In reverse bias, the anode is connected to the negative terminal of the voltage source and the cathode is connected to the positive terminal. The applied voltage increases the potential barrier at the junction and prevents the majority carriers from crossing the junction. However, a small amount of current, called the reverse saturation current, flows due to the thermal generation of minority carriers (electrons in p-type and holes in n-type). The current remains almost constant with the applied voltage until a certain point, called the breakdown voltage, is reached. Beyond this point, the current increases rapidly with the voltage and the diode may be damaged. The reverse bias characteristic of the diode is shown in the graph below.

The static resistance of the diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the resulting current at a given point on the characteristic curve. It can be calculated by using Ohm's law: R = V/I. The static resistance of the diode varies with the applied voltage and the direction of bias. In forward bias, the static resistance is low and decreases with the increase in voltage. In reverse bias, the static resistance is high and increases with the increase in voltage.

The dynamic resistance of the diode is the ratio of the small change in voltage to the corresponding small change in current at a given operating point on the characteristic curve. It can be calculated by using the derivative of the diode equation: r = dV/dI. The dynamic resistance of the diode is useful for analyzing the small-signal behavior of the diode. In forward bias, the dynamic resistance is low and decreases with the increase in current. In reverse bias, the dynamic resistance is high and increases with the decrease in current.
