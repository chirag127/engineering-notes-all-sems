### 3. PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB.

a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB

1. Design the PCB layout using a software like Eagle or KiCad and export it as a PDF file.
2. Print the PCB design on a glossy paper using a laser printer. Make sure the scale is 1:1 and the print quality is high.
3. Cut out the PCB design from the paper and place it on a copper-clad board. Align the design with the copper side of the board and tape it securely.
4. Heat up an iron to the maximum temperature and press it firmly on the paper for about 10 minutes. Move the iron slowly and evenly over the entire paper to transfer the toner to the copper.
5. Peel off the paper carefully and check if the toner has transferred completely. If there are any gaps or defects, use a permanent marker to fill them in.
6. The PCB design is now printed on the copper board and ready for etching.

b. Etching & drilling of PCB

1. Prepare an etching solution by dissolving 2-3 teaspoons of ferric chloride powder in some water in a plastic box. Wear gloves and goggles to protect yourself from the chemicals.
2. Dip the PCB into the etching solution and wait for about 30 minutes. The solution will react with the unmasked copper and remove it from the board, leaving only the toner-covered traces.
3. Rinse the PCB with water and remove the toner with acetone or nail polish remover. The PCB is now etched and the traces are visible.
4. Drill holes on the PCB where the components will be inserted. Use a drill bit that matches the size of the component leads. Be careful not to damage the traces or the board.
5. The PCB is now ready for soldering the components.
