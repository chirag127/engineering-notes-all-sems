Part A PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB.

1. Design the PCB layout using a software like Eagle or KiCad and print it on a glossy paper using a laser printer. The layout should be mirrored and scaled to fit the PCB size.

2. Cut a piece of copper-clad board to the same size as the printed layout and clean it with a fine sandpaper or steel wool.

3. Place the printed layout on top of the copper board and align it carefully. Tape the edges of the paper to the board to prevent it from moving.

4. Heat up an iron to the maximum temperature and press it firmly on the paper for about 10 minutes. Make sure to cover the entire paper and apply even pressure.

5. Let the board cool down and then soak it in water for a few minutes. Peel off the paper gently and check if the toner has transferred to the copper. If not, repeat the ironing process.

6. The artwork and printing of a simple PCB is done. The toner acts as a resist for the etching process.

Part A PCB Lab: b. Etching & drilling of PCB.

1. Prepare an etching solution by dissolving 2-3 teaspoons of ferric chloride powder in some water in a plastic container. Wear gloves and goggles to protect yourself from the chemicals.

2. Dip the PCB into the etching solution and agitate it gently. The solution will react with the exposed copper and remove it from the board. This process may take 15 to 30 minutes depending on the concentration of the solution and the thickness of the copper.

3. Rinse the board with water and dry it with a paper towel. Remove the toner resist with acetone or nail polish remover and a cotton swab.

4. Drill holes for the components using a PCB drill or a hand drill with a small bit. Be careful not to damage the traces or the board.

5. The etching and drilling of PCB is done. The board is ready for soldering the components.
