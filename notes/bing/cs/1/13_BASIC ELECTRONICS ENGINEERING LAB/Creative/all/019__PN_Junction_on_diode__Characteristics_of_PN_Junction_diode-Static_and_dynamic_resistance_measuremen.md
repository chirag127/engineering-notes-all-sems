### PN Junction on diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode-Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph

- A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that consists of a p-region and an n-region separated by a depletion region where charge is stored.
- A PN junction diode has the ability to rectify electric current, create a potential barrier, and make use of its capacitance properties.
- A PN junction diode has various nonlinear current-voltage characteristics that depend on the biasing condition applied to the device.
- There are three biasing conditions for a PN junction diode: zero bias, forward bias, and reverse bias.
- Zero bias means that there is no external voltage applied to the PN junction diode. In this condition, the depletion region acts as a barrier that prevents the majority carriers (holes in p-region and electrons in n-region) from crossing the junction. However, some minority carriers (electrons in p-region and holes in n-region) can diffuse across the junction due to thermal energy. This creates a small current called the reverse saturation current, which is independent of the applied voltage.
- Forward bias means that a positive voltage is applied to the p-region and a negative voltage is applied to the n-region of the PN junction diode. In this condition, the depletion region becomes narrower and the potential barrier is reduced. This allows the majority carriers to overcome the barrier and flow across the junction. The current increases exponentially with the applied voltage until it reaches a saturation value. The forward bias characteristic is nonlinear and asymmetric.
- Reverse bias means that a negative voltage is applied to the p-region and a positive voltage is applied to the n-region of the PN junction diode. In this condition, the depletion region becomes wider and the potential barrier is increased. This prevents the majority carriers from crossing the junction. However, some minority carriers can still diffuse across the junction due to thermal energy, creating a small reverse current that is independent of the applied voltage. The reverse bias characteristic is linear and symmetric.
- The static resistance of a PN junction diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the resulting current at a given biasing condition. The static resistance can be calculated from the slope of the current-voltage graph at a given point. The static resistance is different for forward and reverse bias conditions. The static resistance is low for forward bias and high for reverse bias.
- The dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode is the ratio of the incremental change in voltage to the incremental change in current at a given biasing condition. The dynamic resistance can be calculated from the slope of the tangent to the current-voltage graph at a given point. The dynamic resistance is also different for forward and reverse bias conditions. The dynamic resistance is low for forward bias and high for reverse bias. The dynamic resistance is smaller than the static resistance at the same point.
- The dynamic resistance is more useful than the static resistance for analyzing the behavior of a PN junction diode in an AC circuit, where the applied voltage and current vary sinusoidally. The dynamic resistance determines the power dissipation and the efficiency of the PN junction diode in an AC circuit.
