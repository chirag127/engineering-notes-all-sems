## Part (C):

- Part (C) is a section of the exam paper that tests the candidates on their knowledge and skills in computer science and programming.
- Part (C) consists of 10 questions, each carrying 2 marks, for a total of 20 marks.
- Part (C) questions are based on topics such as data structures, algorithms, operating systems, databases, networking, software engineering, artificial intelligence, etc.
- Part (C) questions are of two types: objective and descriptive.
- Objective questions are multiple-choice questions that have four options, out of which only one is correct. The candidates have to mark the correct option on the OMR sheet.
- Descriptive questions are short-answer questions that require the candidates to write the answer in their own words, using the space provided on the question paper. The answers should be concise, clear and accurate.
- Part (C) questions are designed to test the candidates' understanding, application, analysis and evaluation of the concepts and principles of computer science and programming.
- Part (C) questions may also involve writing, debugging or analyzing code snippets in various programming languages such as C, C++, Java, Python, etc.
- Part (C) questions may also require the candidates to draw diagrams, tables, graphs, flowcharts, etc. to illustrate their answers or solutions.
- Part (C) questions may also ask the candidates to compare, contrast, justify, criticize, or comment on various aspects of computer science and programming.

Some examples of Part (C) questions are:

- Objective question: Which of the following data structures is best suited for implementing a priority queue?
  - A) Stack
  - B) Queue
  - C) Heap
  - D) Tree
  - Answer: C) Heap

- Descriptive question: Write a recursive function in Python that calculates the factorial of a given positive integer n.
  - Answer: def factorial(n):
    - if n == 0 or n == 1:
      - return 1
    - else:
      - return n * factorial(n-1)

- Descriptive question: Explain the difference between deadlock and starvation in operating systems.
  - Answer: Deadlock is a situation where a set of processes are blocked, waiting for each other to release some resources, and none of them can proceed. Starvation is a situation where a process is indefinitely denied access to some resources, due to the scheduling policy or the behavior of other processes. Deadlock is a problem of resource allocation, while starvation is a problem of resource scheduling. Deadlock can be prevented, avoided, detected or resolved, while starvation can be reduced or eliminated by using fair or priority-based scheduling algorithms.
