### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

- A P-N junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It is formed by joining a p-type semiconductor, which has an excess of holes, and an n-type semiconductor, which has an excess of electrons, together. The interface between the two regions is called the P-N junction.
- The P-N junction diode has two terminals: the anode, which is connected to the p-type region, and the cathode, which is connected to the n-type region. The diode can be biased in three ways: forward bias, reverse bias, and zero bias.
- Forward bias means that a positive voltage is applied to the anode and a negative voltage is applied to the cathode. This reduces the width of the depletion region, which is the region near the junction where there are no free charge carriers. The electric field across the depletion region opposes the diffusion of charge carriers from one region to the other. When the applied voltage is greater than the potential barrier of the junction, the electric field is overcome and the current starts to flow. The forward bias characteristic of the diode is nonlinear, meaning that the current is not proportional to the voltage.
- Reverse bias means that a negative voltage is applied to the anode and a positive voltage is applied to the cathode. This increases the width of the depletion region and the electric field across it. The charge carriers are pushed away from the junction and the current is blocked. The reverse bias characteristic of the diode is linear, meaning that the current is proportional to the voltage, but very small and negligible. The diode acts as an open circuit in reverse bias.
- Zero bias means that no external voltage is applied to the diode. The depletion region and the electric field are at their equilibrium state. The current is zero in zero bias.

- The resistance of a diode is the ratio of the voltage across it to the current through it. The resistance of a diode depends on the biasing condition and the operating point of the diode. There are three types of resistance that can be defined for a diode: static resistance, dynamic resistance, and reverse resistance.
- Static resistance is the resistance of the diode at a fixed operating point. It is calculated by dividing the voltage across the diode by the current through it at that point. The static resistance of a diode in forward bias is very low, while the static resistance of a diode in reverse bias is very high.
- Dynamic resistance is the resistance of the diode at a small change in voltage and current around an operating point. It is calculated by dividing the change in voltage by the change in current. The dynamic resistance of a diode in forward bias is the slope of the characteristic curve at that point. The dynamic resistance of a diode in reverse bias is the reciprocal of the slope of the characteristic curve at that point.
- Reverse resistance is the resistance of the diode in reverse bias when the breakdown voltage is reached. The breakdown voltage is the voltage at which the diode starts to conduct in reverse direction due to the avalanche or Zener effect. The reverse resistance of a diode is very low and depends on the type and doping of the semiconductor material.

- The static and dynamic resistance of a diode can be measured from the graph of the characteristic curve of the diode. The characteristic curve is a plot of the voltage across the diode versus the current through it for different biasing conditions.
