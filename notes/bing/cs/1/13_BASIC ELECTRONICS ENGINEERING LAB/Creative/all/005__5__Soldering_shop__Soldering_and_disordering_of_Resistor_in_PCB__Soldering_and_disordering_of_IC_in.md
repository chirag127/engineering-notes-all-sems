### 5. Soldering shop: Soldering and disordering of Resistor in PCB. Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB. Soldering and disordering of Capacitor in PCB.

Soldering is the process of joining two or more metal parts by melting and flowing a filler metal (solder) into the joint. Soldering is widely used in the fabrication and repair of printed circuit boards (PCBs), which are the backbone of electronic devices. Soldering can also be used to attach components such as resistors, capacitors, and integrated circuits (ICs) to the PCB.

Disordering is the opposite of soldering, which means removing the solder and separating the metal parts. Disordering is often necessary when repairing or replacing faulty components on a PCB. Disordering can also be used to salvage reusable components from old or damaged PCBs.

Soldering and disordering of resistor in PCB:

- A resistor is a passive electronic component that limits or regulates the flow of electric current in a circuit. Resistors are usually color-coded to indicate their resistance value in ohms. Resistors can be soldered and disorderd on a PCB using a soldering iron, a solder wire, a solder sucker, and a pair of tweezers.
- To solder a resistor on a PCB, follow these steps:
  - Clean the PCB and the resistor leads with a flux or a solvent to remove any dirt or oxidation.
  - Insert the resistor leads through the corresponding holes on the PCB. Bend the leads slightly to hold the resistor in place.
  - Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
  - Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the resistor lead and the PCB pad meet. Apply a small amount of solder to the joint and let it flow around the lead and the pad. Remove the soldering iron and let the joint cool down. Repeat for the other lead.
  - Cut off the excess leads with a wire cutter or a pair of scissors. Inspect the joint for any defects such as cold solder, solder bridges, or solder balls.
- To disorder a resistor from a PCB, follow these steps:
  - Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
  - Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the resistor lead and the PCB pad meet. Apply some suction with the solder sucker to remove the molten solder from the joint. Repeat for the other lead.
  - Gently pull out the resistor from the PCB with a pair of tweezers. Be careful not to damage the PCB or the resistor.
  - Clean the PCB and the resistor leads with a flux or a solvent to remove any residue or oxidation.

Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB:

- An IC is a complex electronic component that contains many transistors, resistors, capacitors, and other elements on a single chip. ICs are usually packaged in plastic or ceramic cases with metal pins that connect to the PCB. ICs can be soldered and disorderd on a PCB using a soldering iron, a solder wire, a solder sucker, a desoldering braid, and a pair of tweezers.
- To solder an IC on a PCB, follow these steps:
  - Clean the PCB and the IC pins with a flux or a solvent to remove any dirt or oxidation.
  - Align the IC with the corresponding socket or footprint on the PCB. Make sure the orientation of the IC matches the markings on the PCB. Gently press the IC into the socket or the footprint.
