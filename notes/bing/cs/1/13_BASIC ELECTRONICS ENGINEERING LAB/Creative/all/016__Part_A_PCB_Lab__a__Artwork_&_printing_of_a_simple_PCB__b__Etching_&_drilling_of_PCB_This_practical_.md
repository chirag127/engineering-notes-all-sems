### Part A PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB This practical is not possible by virtual lab. It will be conducted only in physical mode.

- A printed circuit board (PCB) is a thin board made of fiberglass, composite epoxy, or other laminate material, on which conductive tracks and components are mounted.
- PCBs are used to electrically connect and mechanically support electronic devices using soldering, conductive adhesives, or other methods.
- The process of making a PCB involves several steps, such as:
  - Designing the circuit layout using software tools or manual methods.
  - Transferring the circuit layout to a copper-clad board using artwork and printing techniques.
  - Removing the unwanted copper from the board using chemical etching or mechanical milling.
  - Drilling holes for mounting components and vias for connecting different layers of the board.
  - Soldering the components and wires to the board, or using other methods of attachment.
  - Testing and debugging the board for functionality and performance.

- The following are some of the details of the steps involved in making a simple PCB:

  - Artwork and printing of a simple PCB:
    - Artwork is the graphical representation of the circuit layout, which can be drawn by hand or using software tools.
    - Artwork can be printed on a transparent sheet or film, which is then used as a mask for transferring the layout to the copper-clad board.
    - Printing can be done using laser printers, inkjet printers, or photocopiers, depending on the quality and resolution required.
    - The printed artwork should be aligned and taped to the copper-clad board, which is then exposed to ultraviolet light or heat, depending on the type of board and film used.
    - The exposure causes the copper under the transparent areas of the artwork to harden, while the copper under the opaque areas remains soft and can be removed by etching.
    - The exposed board is then developed using a chemical solution, which removes the film and reveals the hardened copper pattern.

  - Etching and drilling of PCB:
    - Etching is the process of removing the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the desired circuit traces and pads.
    - Etching can be done using chemical solutions, such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate, which dissolve the copper, or using mechanical methods, such as milling or routing, which cut the copper away.
    - The board should be immersed in the etching solution or placed under the milling or routing machine, and monitored for the completion of the etching process.
    - The board should be rinsed and dried after etching, and inspected for any defects or errors in the circuit pattern.
    - Drilling is the process of making holes in the board for mounting components and vias.
    - Drilling can be done using manual or automated drills, depending on the accuracy and speed required.
    - The board should be marked or aligned with a template for the locations of the holes, and then drilled using the appropriate drill bits and speeds.
    - The board should be cleaned and deburred after drilling, and inspected for any damage or misalignment of the holes.

- Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for the Part A PCB Lab are:

  - To remember the steps of making a PCB, use the acronym DETER: Design, Expose, Develop, Etch, Rinse.
  - To remember the types of etching solutions, use the acronym FAME: Ferric chloride, Ammonium persulfate, Milling, Engraving.
  - To remember the types of printing methods, use the acronym LIP: Laser, Inkjet, Photocopy.
  - To remember the types of boards and films, use the acronym PEP: Positive, Negative, Photoresist.
  - To remember the types of holes in a PCB, use the acronym VMH: Vias, Mounting, Holes.
