### 3. PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

- A printed circuit board (PCB) is a board that electrically connects and mechanically supports electronic components using conductive tracks, pads, and other features.
- A simple PCB can be made by following these steps:

  1. **Artwork and printing**: This is the process of designing the layout of the PCB using a software tool (such as Eagle, KiCad, etc.) and printing it on a transparent sheet using a laser printer. The layout should include the component symbols, pads, tracks, and any other features that are needed for the circuit. The printed sheet is called the artwork or the mask.
  2. **Etching**: This is the process of removing the unwanted copper from the copper-clad board using a chemical solution (such as ferric chloride, ammonium persulfate, etc.). The artwork is placed on top of the board and exposed to ultraviolet light, which transfers the layout to the board. The board is then immersed in the etching solution, which dissolves the copper that is not covered by the layout. The board is then rinsed and dried, leaving only the desired copper traces on the board.
  3. **Drilling**: This is the process of making holes on the board for mounting the components and connecting the traces. The board is placed on a drilling machine and the holes are drilled according to the layout. The holes should be slightly larger than the component leads or the vias (the metal-plated holes that connect the traces on different layers of the board). The board is then cleaned and inspected for any defects.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the PCB lab are:

  - **A**rtwork and printing, **E**tching, and **D**rilling are the three main steps of making a simple PCB. Remember **AED** as the acronym for **A**utomated **E**xternal **D**efibrillator, a device that can save lives by delivering an electric shock to the heart.
  - The etching solution dissolves the **U**nwanted copper from the board. Remember **U** as the symbol for **U**ranium, a radioactive element that can dissolve in water and cause health problems.
  - The holes on the board should be slightly **L**arger than the component leads or the vias. Remember **L** as the symbol for **L**ithium, a metal that can expand when heated and cause explosions.
