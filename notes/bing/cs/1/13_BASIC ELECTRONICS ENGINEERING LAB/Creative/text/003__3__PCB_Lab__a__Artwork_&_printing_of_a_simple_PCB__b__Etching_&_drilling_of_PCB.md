### 3. PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB.

A printed circuit board (PCB) is a thin board made of fiberglass, composite epoxy, or other laminate material, on which electrical components are mounted and connected by copper traces. PCBs are used to create electronic circuits in a compact and reliable way.

The process of making a PCB involves the following steps:

- Artwork and printing: This is the design of the circuit layout on a computer software, such as Eagle, KiCad, or Altium. The design includes the placement of components, the routing of traces, and the dimensions of the board. The design is then printed on a special paper or film, called the artwork, which is used as a mask for the etching process. The artwork can be printed using a laser printer, an inkjet printer, or a photocopier. The artwork should have a high contrast between the traces and the background, and should be scaled to the actual size of the board.

- Etching and drilling: This is the removal of the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the traces and pads for the components. The board is coated with a thin layer of copper on both sides, and then immersed in a chemical solution, called the etchant, which dissolves the exposed copper. The artwork is placed on top of the board, and aligned with the copper layer. The artwork acts as a mask, protecting the copper traces from the etchant. The etching process can take from a few minutes to an hour, depending on the size and complexity of the board, and the concentration and temperature of the etchant. The etchant can be ferric chloride, ammonium persulfate, or sodium persulfate. The board should be agitated during the etching process, to ensure a uniform and complete removal of the copper. After the etching is done, the board is rinsed with water, and the artwork is peeled off. The board is then inspected for any defects, such as shorts, opens, or over-etching. The board is then drilled with a drill press or a CNC machine, to create holes for the components' leads. The holes should be aligned with the pads on the board, and should have a diameter slightly larger than the leads. The drilling process should be done carefully, to avoid damaging the board or the drill bits.

- Soldering and testing: This is the attachment of the components to the board, and the verification of the functionality of the circuit. The components are inserted through the holes on the board, and their leads are bent to secure them in place. The leads are then soldered to the pads on the board, using a soldering iron and a solder wire. The soldering process should be done with care, to avoid creating cold joints, bridges, or overheating the components. The solder should form a smooth and shiny cone around the lead and the pad, and should not touch any other traces or pads. The excess leads are then trimmed with a wire cutter, to prevent short circuits. The board is then tested with a multimeter, an oscilloscope, or a logic analyzer, to check for any errors or faults in the circuit. The board should perform according to the specifications and requirements of the design. The board is then cleaned with a solvent, such as isopropyl alcohol, to remove any flux residue or dirt. The board is then ready for use or further assembly.
