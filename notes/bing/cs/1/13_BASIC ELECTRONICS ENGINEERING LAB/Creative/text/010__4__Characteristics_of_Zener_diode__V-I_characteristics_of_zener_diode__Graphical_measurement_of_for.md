### 4. Characteristics of Zener diode: V-I characteristics of zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode that can operate in both forward and reverse bias modes, but has a very low breakdown voltage in reverse bias.
- The breakdown voltage of a Zener diode is called the Zener voltage (Vz) and it is the voltage at which the diode starts to conduct a large current in reverse bias.
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode are shown in the figure below. The forward characteristics are similar to a normal diode, but the reverse characteristics are different.

![V-I characteristics of Zener diode](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Zener_Diode_VI_Characteristic_Curve.svg/1200px-Zener_Diode_VI_Characteristic_Curve.svg.png)

- In the reverse bias region, the diode has a very high resistance until the applied voltage reaches the Zener voltage (Vz). At this point, the diode undergoes a process called Zener breakdown, where the electric field across the diode becomes so high that it causes the electrons to tunnel through the depletion region and create a large current flow. This current is called the Zener current (Iz) and it is independent of the applied voltage as long as it is above Vz.
- The Zener diode can maintain a constant voltage across its terminals as long as the applied voltage is higher than Vz and the current is within the maximum rating of the diode. This property makes the Zener diode useful as a voltage regulator or a reference voltage source in electronic circuits.
- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode can be measured graphically from the V-I characteristics. The forward resistance (Rf) is the slope of the forward characteristic curve at a given operating point. The reverse resistance (Rz) is the slope of the reverse characteristic curve at a given operating point. The figure below shows how to measure Rf and Rz graphically.

![Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance](https://i.imgur.com/0Xw5Z7p.png)

- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode are not constant, but depend on the operating conditions. The forward resistance decreases as the forward current increases, while the reverse resistance decreases as the reverse current increases. The reverse resistance also decreases as the temperature increases, which affects the stability of the Zener voltage. Therefore, the Zener diode should be operated within the specified limits of current and temperature to ensure reliable performance.
