##### 5. Measurement of power in 3- phase circuit by two-wattmeter method and determination of its power factor for star as well as delta connected load.

- The two-wattmeter method is a practical and convenient way of measuring the total power in a balanced or unbalanced three-phase circuit.
- The method involves connecting two wattmeters, one between any phase and the neutral (or the star point), and the other between the remaining two phases.
- The total power is then given by the sum of the readings of the two wattmeters.
- The power factor of the circuit can be determined by using the relation:

  `cos φ = (W1 - W2) / (W1 + W2)`

  where φ is the phase angle between the line voltage and the line current, W1 and W2 are the readings of the two wattmeters.

- For a star-connected load, the line voltage is equal to the phase voltage, and the line current is equal to the phase current times the square root of three. Therefore, the power factor can be written as:

  `cos φ = (Vp * Ip * cos θ1 - Vp * Ip * cos θ2) / (Vp * Ip * cos θ1 + Vp * Ip * cos θ2)`

  where θ1 and θ2 are the phase angles of the currents in the two wattmeters.

- For a delta-connected load, the line voltage is equal to the phase voltage times the square root of three, and the line current is equal to the phase current. Therefore, the power factor can be written as:

  `cos φ = (Vp * √3 * Ip * cos θ1 - Vp * √3 * Ip * cos θ2) / (Vp * √3 * Ip * cos θ1 + Vp * √3 * Ip * cos θ2)`

  where θ1 and θ2 are the phase angles of the currents in the two wattmeters.

- The two-wattmeter method can be used for any type of load, whether resistive, inductive, capacitive, or a combination of them. However, the method is not suitable for measuring very low power or power factor, as the errors due to the wattmeter resistance and inductance may become significant.
