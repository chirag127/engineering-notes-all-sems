##### 1. Verification of Kirchhoff’s laws

- Kirchhoff’s laws are two rules that relate the currents and voltages in a circuit. They are based on the conservation of charge and energy in a closed system.
- Kirchhoff’s current law (KCL) states that the algebraic sum of the currents entering a node (or a junction) is zero. This means that the total charge entering a node is equal to the total charge leaving the node.
- Kirchhoff’s voltage law (KVL) states that the algebraic sum of the voltages around a closed loop is zero. This means that the total energy gained by the charges in a loop is equal to the total energy lost by the charges in the same loop.
- To verify Kirchhoff’s laws experimentally, we need a circuit that has at least one node with more than two branches and at least one loop with more than one source of emf. We also need an ammeter to measure the currents and a voltmeter to measure the voltages in the circuit.
