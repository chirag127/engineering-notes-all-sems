## LIST OF EXPERIMENTS

- An experiment is a scientific procedure that is carried out to test a hypothesis, observe a phenomenon, or measure a variable.
- Experiments usually involve manipulating one or more independent variables and measuring their effects on one or more dependent variables.
- Experiments can be classified into different types based on their design, purpose, or outcome. Some common types of experiments are:

  - Controlled experiments: These are experiments where the researcher controls all the factors that could affect the outcome, except for the independent variable that is being tested. The researcher also uses a control group that does not receive the treatment or manipulation of the independent variable, and compares it with an experimental group that does. This allows the researcher to isolate the causal effect of the independent variable on the dependent variable.
  - Randomized experiments: These are experiments where the researcher randomly assigns the participants or units to the experimental or control groups, ensuring that each group is similar in all relevant aspects except for the independent variable. This reduces the possibility of confounding factors or selection bias that could affect the results.
  - Natural experiments: These are experiments where the researcher takes advantage of a natural occurrence or event that creates a situation where the independent variable is manipulated or varied without the researcher's intervention. The researcher then observes the effects of this natural manipulation on the dependent variable. This allows the researcher to study the causal effect of the independent variable in a real-world setting, but it may also introduce other sources of variation or error that are difficult to control or measure.
  - Quasi-experiments: These are experiments where the researcher does not have full control over the assignment of the participants or units to the experimental or control groups, or does not have a control group at all. The researcher may use other methods to try to create comparable groups, such as matching, pretest-posttest, or regression discontinuity. However, these methods may not eliminate all the confounding factors or selection bias that could affect the results, and the causal effect of the independent variable may not be as clear or valid as in a randomized or controlled experiment.
  - Field experiments: These are experiments that are conducted in a natural or realistic setting, rather than in a laboratory or artificial environment. The researcher may still manipulate the independent variable and measure the dependent variable, but the participants or units are not aware that they are part of an experiment, or they behave as they normally would in that setting. This increases the ecological validity or generalizability of the results, but it may also introduce other challenges or limitations, such as ethical issues, practical constraints, or external validity.
