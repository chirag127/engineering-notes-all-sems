##### 2. Measurement of power and power factor in a single phase ac series inductive circuit and study improvement of power factor using capacitor.

- A single phase ac series inductive circuit consists of a resistor, an inductor and a voltage source connected in series.
- The voltage and current in the circuit are not in phase due to the presence of inductance, which causes a phase difference between them.
- The power factor of the circuit is defined as the ratio of the true power to the apparent power, or the cosine of the phase angle between the voltage and current.
- The power factor can be measured using a wattmeter, which measures the true power, and a voltmeter and an ammeter, which measure the voltage and current respectively.
- The power factor can be improved by adding a capacitor in parallel with the inductor, which reduces the net reactance of the circuit and increases the phase angle between the voltage and current.
- The capacitor should be chosen such that the capacitive reactance is equal to the inductive reactance at the operating frequency, which results in a resonant condition and a power factor of unity.
