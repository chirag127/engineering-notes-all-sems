##### CO 4 Calculate efficiency of a single phase transformer and DC machine. K4.

- A single phase transformer is a device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through electromagnetic induction.
- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power, expressed as a percentage.
- The efficiency of a single phase transformer depends on the losses that occur in the transformer, which are mainly of two types: core losses and copper losses.
- Core losses are the losses due to the hysteresis and eddy currents in the magnetic core of the transformer. They are constant and independent of the load.
- Copper losses are the losses due to the resistance of the windings of the transformer. They are proportional to the square of the load current.
- The efficiency of a single phase transformer can be calculated by the following formula:

  η = Pout / Pin = (Pin - Ploss) / Pin = 1 - (Pcore + Pcopper) / Pin

  where η is the efficiency, Pout is the output power, Pin is the input power, Ploss is the total loss, Pcore is the core loss, and Pcopper is the copper loss.
- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is maximum when the core loss is equal to the copper loss. This condition is known as the maximum efficiency condition.
- The maximum efficiency of a single phase transformer can be calculated by the following formula:

  ηmax = 1 - √(Pcore / Pin)

  where ηmax is the maximum efficiency, Pcore is the core loss, and Pin is the input power.
