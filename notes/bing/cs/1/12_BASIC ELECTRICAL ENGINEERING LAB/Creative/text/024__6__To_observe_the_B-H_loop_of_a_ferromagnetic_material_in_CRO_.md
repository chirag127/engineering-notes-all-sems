##### 6. To observe the B-H loop of a ferromagnetic material in CRO.

- A B-H loop is a graphical representation of the relationship between the magnetic flux density (B) and the magnetic field intensity (H) of a ferromagnetic material.
- A ferromagnetic material is one that can be magnetized by an external magnetic field and retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
- A CRO (cathode ray oscilloscope) is an instrument that can display the variation of an electrical signal on a screen.
- To observe the B-H loop of a ferromagnetic material in CRO, the following steps are required:

  - Connect a primary coil of a transformer to an AC power supply and a secondary coil to a resistor and a CRO.
  - Insert a ferromagnetic core inside the primary coil and wrap another coil around it. This coil is called the search coil and it is connected to another CRO.
  - Adjust the CRO settings to display the voltage across the resistor (Vr) on the horizontal axis and the voltage across the search coil (Vs) on the vertical axis.
  - Vary the AC voltage and observe the shape of the curve on the CRO screen. This curve is the B-H loop of the ferromagnetic material.
  - The B-H loop shows the hysteresis behavior of the material, which means that the magnetization of the material depends on its previous history of exposure to the magnetic field.
  - The area enclosed by the B-H loop is proportional to the energy loss per cycle due to the magnetic hysteresis of the material.
  - The shape and size of the B-H loop depend on the type and quality of the ferromagnetic material. Some materials have narrow and symmetrical loops, while others have wide and asymmetrical loops.
