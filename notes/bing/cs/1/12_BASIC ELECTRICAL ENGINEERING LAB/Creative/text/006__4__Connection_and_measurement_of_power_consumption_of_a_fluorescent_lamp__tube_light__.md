##### 4. Connection and measurement of power consumption of a fluorescent lamp (tube light).

- A fluorescent lamp (or tube light) is a gas-discharge lamp that uses electricity to excite mercury vapor in a glass tube. The excited mercury atoms produce ultraviolet (UV) light, which is then converted into visible light by a phosphor coating on the inside of the tube.
- A fluorescent lamp requires a ballast to regulate the current and voltage in the circuit. The ballast can be either magnetic or electronic, depending on the type and design of the lamp. A magnetic ballast uses an iron core and a coil to create a magnetic field that limits the current. An electronic ballast uses a switching circuit to generate a high-frequency alternating current that drives the lamp.
- To connect and measure the power consumption of a fluorescent lamp, the following steps are required:

  - Connect the lamp to a suitable power source (such as a wall socket or a generator) using a plug and a wire. Make sure the power source matches the voltage and frequency rating of the lamp and the ballast.
  - Connect an ammeter in series with the lamp to measure the current flowing through the circuit. The ammeter should have a range that can accommodate the expected current value. For example, if the lamp is rated at 36 W and 230 V, the current should be around 0.16 A.
  - Connect a voltmeter in parallel with the lamp to measure the voltage across the lamp terminals. The voltmeter should have a range that can accommodate the expected voltage value. For example, if the lamp is rated at 230 V, the voltmeter should have a range of at least 250 V.
  - Turn on the power source and observe the readings on the ammeter and the voltmeter. Record the values and calculate the power consumption of the lamp using the formula P = VI, where P is the power in watts, V is the voltage in volts, and I is the current in amperes.
  - Turn off the power source and disconnect the lamp and the meters. Compare the calculated power consumption with the rated power of the lamp and the ballast. The calculated power consumption should be close to the rated power, but may differ slightly due to losses in the circuit and variations in the power source.
