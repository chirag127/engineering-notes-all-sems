##### 3. RLC series resonance

- An RLC series circuit consists of a resistor (R), an inductor (L), and a capacitor (C) connected in series with an alternating voltage source.
- The current in the circuit is the same for all the components, but the voltage across each component may vary depending on the frequency of the source.
- The impedance of the circuit is given by Z = R + j(XL - XC), where XL = ωL is the inductive reactance and XC = 1/ωC is the capacitive reactance.
- The phase angle of the circuit is given by θ = tan^-1 ((XL - XC)/R), where θ is the angle between the voltage and the current.
- The resonance condition occurs when the inductive reactance is equal to the capacitive reactance, i.e., XL = XC or ωL = 1/ωC. This implies that ω = 1/√(LC), which is the resonant frequency of the circuit.
- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is purely resistive, i.e., Z = R. The phase angle is zero, i.e., θ = 0. The current in the circuit is maximum, i.e., I = V/R, where V is the source voltage.
- At resonance, the voltage across the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude but opposite in phase, i.e., VL = -VC. The magnitude of the voltage across the inductor and the capacitor is given by VL = VC = QV, where Q = ωL/R is the quality factor of the circuit. The quality factor measures the sharpness of the resonance peak and the energy stored in the circuit.
- The power dissipated in the circuit is given by P = I^2R, where I is the current at resonance. The power factor of the circuit is given by PF = cos θ, which is equal to 1 at resonance. This means that the circuit is purely resistive and there is no reactive power.
