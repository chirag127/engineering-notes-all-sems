##### 7. Determination of (i) Voltage ratio (ii) polarity and (iii) efficiency by load test of a single phase Transformer

- The voltage ratio of a transformer is the ratio of the secondary voltage to the primary voltage. It is also equal to the ratio of the number of turns in the secondary winding to the number of turns in the primary winding, if the transformer is ideal and has no losses.
- The polarity of a transformer is the relative direction of the induced voltages in the primary and secondary windings. It can be determined by the dot convention, which assigns a dot to one terminal of each winding. If the current enters the dotted terminal of the primary winding, the voltage across the secondary winding will be positive at the dotted terminal and negative at the undotted terminal. The polarity can also be determined by a simple test using a battery and a voltmeter.
- The efficiency of a transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power. It is also equal to the ratio of the secondary voltage times the secondary current to the primary voltage times the primary current, if the transformer is ideal and has no losses. The efficiency can be determined by a load test, which involves connecting a resistive load to the secondary winding and measuring the input and output voltages and currents.

The following steps describe how to perform a load test of a single phase transformer to determine the voltage ratio, polarity and efficiency:

1. Connect the primary winding of the transformer to a variable AC voltage source and the secondary winding to a resistive load, as shown in the figure below. The load resistance should be chosen such that the secondary current does not exceed the rated value of the transformer. Also, connect a voltmeter across the primary and secondary windings and an ammeter in series with the primary and secondary circuits.

2. Adjust the AC voltage source to obtain the rated voltage across the primary winding. Note down the readings of the primary and secondary voltages (V1 and V2) and currents (I1 and I2).
3. To determine the voltage ratio, divide the secondary voltage by the primary voltage. The result should be close to the rated voltage ratio of the transformer. For example, if the rated voltage ratio is 10:1, then V2/V1 should be approximately 0.1.
4. To determine the polarity, connect a battery and a voltmeter in series with the secondary winding, as shown in the figure below. The battery should have a voltage lower than the secondary voltage. Observe the polarity of the voltmeter. If the voltmeter shows a positive reading, then the polarity of the transformer is the same as the dot convention. If the voltmeter shows a negative reading, then the polarity of the transformer is opposite to the dot convention.

5. To determine the efficiency, calculate the input power and the output power of the transformer. The input power is equal to the product of the primary voltage and the primary current. The output power is equal to the product of the secondary voltage and the secondary current. The efficiency is equal to the ratio of the output power to the input power, expressed as a percentage. For example, if the input power is 100 W and the output power is 95 W, then the efficiency is 95/100 x 100 = 95%.
