##### 1. Kirchhoff‟s laws

Kirchhoff's laws are a set of laws that quantify how current flows through a circuit and how voltage varies around a loop in a circuit. They are used to govern the conservation of charge and energy in standard electrical circuits . They were first described in 1845 by German physicist Gustav Kirchhoff.

There are two Kirchhoff's laws:

- **Kirchhoff's current law (KCL)**: This law, also called Kirchhoff's first law, or Kirchhoff's junction rule, states that, for any node (junction) in an electrical circuit, the sum of currents flowing into that node is equal to the sum of currents flowing out of that node; or equivalently: The algebraic sum of currents in a network of conductors meeting at a point is zero . This law is based on the principle of conservation of charge, which means that charge cannot be created or destroyed in a circuit.

- **Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL)**: This law, also called Kirchhoff's second law, or Kirchhoff's loop rule, states that, for any closed loop in an electrical circuit, the sum of voltages across each element of the loop is equal to zero; or equivalently: The algebraic sum of the products of the currents and the resistances in each of the conductors in any closed path in a network plus the algebraic sum of the electromotive forces in that path is zero . This law is based on the principle of conservation of energy, which means that energy cannot be created or destroyed in a circuit.

The following diagrams illustrate the application of Kirchhoff's laws to simple circuits:

![KCL diagram](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/physics/circuits-topic/circuits-resistance/a/ee-kirchhoffs-laws/a/kcl-1.png)

This diagram shows a node with four branches. The currents are labeled as I1, I2, I3, and I4. According to KCL, the sum of currents entering the node must equal the sum of currents leaving the node. Therefore, we can write:

I1 + I2 = I3 + I4

![KVL diagram](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/physics/circuits-topic/circuits-resistance/a/ee-kirchhoffs-laws/a/kvl-1.png)

This diagram shows a loop with three elements: a battery with voltage V, and two resistors with resistances R1 and R2. The current is labeled as I. According to KVL, the sum of voltages around the loop must equal zero. Therefore, we can write:

V - I*R1 - I*R2 = 0

Kirchhoff's laws are useful for analyzing complex circuits with multiple loops and nodes. They can be used to find the unknown currents and voltages in a circuit by applying a system of linear equations.
