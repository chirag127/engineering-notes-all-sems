- AC circuits are circuits that are powered by alternating current (AC) sources, such as generators, transformers, or power grids.
- AC sources produce sinusoidal voltages and currents that vary in magnitude and direction with time, according to the equation: v(t) = Vm sin(ωt + φ), where Vm is the peak voltage, ω is the angular frequency, t is the time, and φ is the phase angle.
- AC circuits can contain resistors, capacitors, inductors, or combinations of these elements, which affect the voltage and current in different ways.
- Resistors are elements that oppose the flow of current and dissipate electrical energy as heat. They have a constant resistance (R) that does not depend on the frequency or phase of the AC source. The voltage and current across a resistor are in phase, meaning they have the same frequency and phase angle.
- Capacitors are elements that store electrical energy in an electric field. They have a capacitance (C) that measures how much charge they can store per unit voltage. The voltage and current across a capacitor are out of phase by 90 degrees, meaning they have the same frequency but differ in phase angle by π/2 radians. The current leads the voltage by 90 degrees in a capacitor.
- Inductors are elements that store electrical energy in a magnetic field. They have an inductance (L) that measures how much voltage they can induce per unit change in current. The voltage and current across an inductor are out of phase by 90 degrees, meaning they have the same frequency but differ in phase angle by π/2 radians. The voltage leads the current by 90 degrees in an inductor.
- The behavior of AC circuits can be analyzed using complex numbers, phasors, and impedance. Complex numbers are numbers that have a real part and an imaginary part, such as a + jb, where a and b are real numbers and j is the imaginary unit, such that j^2 = -1. Phasors are complex numbers that represent sinusoidal voltages or currents as vectors, such as V = Vm∠φ, where Vm is the peak value and φ is the phase angle. Impedance is the complex ratio of voltage to current in an AC circuit, such as Z = V/I, where Z is the impedance, V is the phasor voltage, and I is the phasor current. Impedance can be expressed as Z = R + jX, where R is the resistance and X is the reactance, which measures the opposition to the change in voltage or current due to capacitance or inductance. The reactance of a capacitor is Xc = -1/(ωC), and the reactance of an inductor is Xl = ωL, where ω is the angular frequency and C and L are the capacitance and inductance, respectively.
- AC circuits can be connected to single phase AC supply or three phase AC supply. A single phase AC supply is a single sinusoidal voltage source that delivers power to a load. A three phase AC supply is a set of three sinusoidal voltage sources that are 120 degrees out of phase with each other and deliver power to a balanced or unbalanced load. A balanced load is a load that has the same impedance in each phase, while an unbalanced load is a load that has different impedances in each phase.
- The power in an AC circuit is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred from the source to the load. The power factor is the cosine of the power factor angle, and it measures how efficiently the power is delivered to the load. The power factor can range from 0 to 1, where 0 means no power is delivered and 1 means all the power is delivered.
