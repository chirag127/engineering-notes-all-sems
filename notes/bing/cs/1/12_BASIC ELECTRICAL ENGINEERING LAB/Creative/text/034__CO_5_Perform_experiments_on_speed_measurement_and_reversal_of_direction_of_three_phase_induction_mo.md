##### CO 5 Perform experiments on speed measurement and reversal of direction of three phase induction motor and Identify the type of DC and AC machines based on their construction. K4.

- Speed measurement of a three phase induction motor can be done by using a tachometer, a stroboscope, a frequency meter, or a speed sensor. The speed of the motor is proportional to the frequency of the supply voltage and the number of poles in the motor. The speed can be calculated by the formula:

  - N = 120f/P

  - where N is the speed in rpm, f is the frequency in Hz, and P is the number of poles.

- Reversal of direction of a three phase induction motor can be done by changing any two of the three lines feeding power to the motor. This reverses the direction of the rotating magnetic field within the motor, and thus the direction of the rotor. A reverse starter or a variable frequency drive can be used to control the direction and speed of the motor.

- DC and AC machines can be identified based on their construction as follows:

  - DC machines have a commutator and brushes to convert the alternating current in the armature to direct current in the external circuit. The commutator is a segmented ring that rotates with the armature and reverses the polarity of the current every half cycle. The brushes are stationary contacts that slide on the commutator and transfer the current to the load.

  - AC machines do not have a commutator or brushes. They have a stator and a rotor that are both wound with coils. The stator is the stationary part that produces a rotating magnetic field by the alternating current in the coils. The rotor is the rotating part that either induces a current in the coils by the magnetic field (induction motor) or produces a magnetic field by the current in the coils (synchronous motor). The current in the rotor is either supplied by slip rings (wound rotor) or induced by the stator (squirrel cage rotor).
