#### (A) Hardware based experiments

- Hardware based experiments are experiments that involve the use of physical devices, components, or systems to test a hypothesis, measure a phenomenon, or demonstrate a concept.
- Hardware based experiments can be classified into different types, such as:
  - Analog experiments: These experiments use analog signals, such as voltage, current, or resistance, to represent or manipulate information. Examples of analog experiments are measuring the frequency response of a filter circuit, or generating a sine wave using an oscillator.
  - Digital experiments: These experiments use digital signals, such as binary bits, to represent or manipulate information. Examples of digital experiments are implementing a logic gate using transistors, or encoding and decoding a message using a Hamming code.
  - Mixed-signal experiments: These experiments use both analog and digital signals, such as audio or video signals, to represent or manipulate information. Examples of mixed-signal experiments are modulating and demodulating a signal using amplitude modulation, or converting an analog signal to a digital signal using an analog-to-digital converter.
  - Embedded systems experiments: These experiments use microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, or other devices to control or interact with the physical world. Examples of embedded systems experiments are programming a robot to follow a line, or measuring the temperature and humidity using a sensor.
- Hardware based experiments can have different objectives, such as:
  - Verification: These experiments aim to verify the correctness or validity of a theory, model, or design. Examples of verification experiments are testing the functionality of a circuit, or comparing the experimental results with the theoretical predictions.
  - Exploration: These experiments aim to explore the properties, behavior, or performance of a system, device, or component. Examples of exploration experiments are measuring the characteristics of a diode, or observing the effects of noise on a signal.
  - Optimization: These experiments aim to optimize the parameters, settings, or configuration of a system, device, or component. Examples of optimization experiments are finding the optimal gain of an amplifier, or tuning the PID controller of a motor.
  - Innovation: These experiments aim to create, invent, or discover something new or novel. Examples of innovation experiments are designing a new circuit, or implementing a new algorithm.
