Kirchhoff's laws are two principles that govern the conservation of charge and energy in electrical circuits. They are:

- Kirchhoff's current law (KCL): The algebraic sum of currents entering and leaving a junction is zero. This means that the current is conserved at any point where three or more conductors meet.
- Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL): The algebraic sum of voltages around any closed loop is zero. This means that the energy is conserved when the current travels around a circuit.
