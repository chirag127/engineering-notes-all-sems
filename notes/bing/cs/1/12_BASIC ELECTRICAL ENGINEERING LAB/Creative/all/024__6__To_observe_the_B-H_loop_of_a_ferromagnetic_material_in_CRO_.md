##### 6. To observe the B-H loop of a ferromagnetic material in CRO.

- A B-H loop, or hysteresis loop, is a graphical representation of how the magnetization of a ferromagnetic material varies when subjected to a periodically reversing magnetic field .
- The B-H loop shows the nonlinear and irreversible relationship between the magnetic flux density (B) and the magnetic field intensity (H) of a ferromagnetic material .
- The shape and size of the B-H loop depend on the material properties, such as saturation magnetization, remanence, coercivity, and permeability.
- A CRO, or cathode ray oscilloscope, is an electronic device that can display the variation of an electrical signal on a screen. It can be used to measure the voltage, frequency, phase, and shape of a signal.
- To observe the B-H loop of a ferromagnetic material in CRO, the following steps are required:

  - Connect a primary coil of a transformer to an AC power supply, and a secondary coil to a resistor and a CRO. The secondary coil should have more turns than the primary coil to increase the voltage.
  - Insert a ferromagnetic core, such as iron, inside the primary coil. The core should have a rectangular shape to avoid eddy currents and hysteresis losses.
  - Adjust the CRO settings to display the voltage across the resistor (V) on the horizontal axis and the voltage across the secondary coil (E) on the vertical axis. The voltage V is proportional to the current I in the primary coil, and the voltage E is proportional to the magnetic flux Φ in the core. Therefore, the CRO will display the B-H loop of the core.
  - Vary the frequency and amplitude of the AC power supply to observe the changes in the shape and size of the B-H loop. A higher frequency or amplitude will result in a wider and taller loop, indicating more hysteresis losses and energy dissipation in the core.
  - Observe the characteristics of the B-H loop, such as the saturation points, the remanent magnetization, the coercive force, and the area enclosed by the loop. The saturation points are the maximum values of B and H, where the core cannot be further magnetized. The remanent magnetization is the value of B when H is zero, indicating the residual magnetism in the core. The coercive force is the value of H when B is zero, indicating the amount of reverse field required to demagnetize the core. The area enclosed by the loop is proportional to the energy lost per cycle in the core due to hysteresis.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the steps is: **C**onnect, **I**nsert, **A**djust, **V**ary, **O**bserve, or CIAVO.
