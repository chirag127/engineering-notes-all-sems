- The virtual lab link is for the simulation of **Determination of Young's Modulus of a Wire by Optical Lever Method**.
- Young's modulus is a measure of the stiffness of a material. It is defined as the ratio of stress to strain in a linear elastic material.
- The optical lever method is a technique to measure small displacements of a wire by using a light beam and a mirror attached to the wire.
- The simulation allows the user to perform the following steps:
  - Select the material of the wire from a list of options.
  - Adjust the length and diameter of the wire using sliders.
  - Apply a known weight to the wire and observe the change in its length.
  - Measure the angle of deflection of the light beam using a protractor.
  - Calculate the Young's modulus of the wire using the formula:

    `Y = (4L^3 * W * sin(theta))/(pi * d^4 * D)`

    where Y is the Young's modulus, L is the length of the wire, W is the weight, theta is the angle of deflection, d is the diameter of the wire, and D is the distance between the mirror and the screen.
