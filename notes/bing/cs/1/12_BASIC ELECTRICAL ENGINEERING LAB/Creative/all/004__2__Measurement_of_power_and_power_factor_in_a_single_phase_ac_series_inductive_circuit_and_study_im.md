##### 2. Measurement of power and power factor in a single phase ac series inductive circuit and study improvement of power factor using capacitor

- Power factor for a single-phase in an alternating current circuit is defined as a measure of energy efficiency. It is usually expressed as a number ranging from 0 to 1. It is the ratio of working power (or actual power) to apparent power.
- Working power is the power that is consumed by the resistive load in the circuit. Apparent power is the product of the rms values of voltage and current in the circuit. Apparent power is always greater than or equal to working power.
- Power factor can also be expressed as the cosine of the phase angle between voltage and current in the circuit. The phase angle depends on the reactance of the circuit, which is determined by the inductive and capacitive elements. The reactance causes the current to lag or lead the voltage in the circuit.
- A purely resistive circuit has zero reactance and zero phase angle, so the power factor is 1. This means that all the apparent power is converted into working power. A purely inductive or capacitive circuit has maximum reactance and 90 degrees phase angle, so the power factor is 0. This means that none of the apparent power is converted into working power.
- A series inductive circuit has a positive reactance and a lagging power factor. This means that the current lags behind the voltage in the circuit. A series capacitive circuit has a negative reactance and a leading power factor. This means that the current leads ahead of the voltage in the circuit.
- The power factor of a series inductive circuit can be improved by adding a capacitor in parallel to the circuit. The capacitor provides a negative reactance that cancels out some of the positive reactance of the inductor. This reduces the phase angle and increases the power factor. The capacitor also reduces the apparent power and the current in the circuit, which improves the efficiency and reduces the power losses.
- The power factor of a series inductive circuit can be calculated using the following formula:

    `power factor = cos φ = R/Z = W/VA`

    where R is the resistance, Z is the impedance, W is the working power, and VA is the apparent power of the circuit.
