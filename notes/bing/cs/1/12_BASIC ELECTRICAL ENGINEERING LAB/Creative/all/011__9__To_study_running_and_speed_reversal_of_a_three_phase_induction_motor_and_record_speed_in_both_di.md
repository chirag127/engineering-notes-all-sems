##### 9. To study running and speed reversal of a three phase induction motor and record speed in both directions.

- A three phase induction motor is a type of AC motor that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy by creating a rotating magnetic field in the stator and inducing a current in the rotor.
- The direction of rotation of a three phase induction motor depends on the phase sequence of the three phase supply. If the phase sequence is ABC, the motor rotates in clockwise direction. If the phase sequence is ACB, the motor rotates in anti-clockwise direction.
- To reverse the direction of rotation of a three phase induction motor, any two of the three supply lines can be interchanged. For example, if the phase sequence is ABC, swapping line A and line C will make the phase sequence ACB, and the motor will rotate in anti-clockwise direction.
- To study the running and speed reversal of a three phase induction motor, the following steps can be followed:
  - Connect the three phase induction motor to a three phase supply through a three phase switch and a wattmeter. The switch should have three positions: off, forward and reverse. In the forward position, the switch connects the supply lines as ABC. In the reverse position, the switch connects the supply lines as ACB.
  - Connect a tachometer to the motor shaft to measure the speed of rotation in rpm (revolutions per minute).
  - Turn on the switch to the forward position and observe the direction and speed of rotation of the motor. Record the wattmeter reading and the tachometer reading.
  - Turn off the switch and wait for the motor to stop completely.
  - Turn on the switch to the reverse position and observe the direction and speed of rotation of the motor. Record the wattmeter reading and the tachometer reading.
  - Compare the readings and note the change in direction and speed of the motor.
- The speed of a three phase induction motor is given by the formula: n = (120f/p)(1-s), where n is the speed in rpm, f is the frequency of the supply in Hz, p is the number of poles of the motor, and s is the slip. The slip is the difference between the synchronous speed and the actual speed of the motor, expressed as a fraction of the synchronous speed. The slip depends on the load and the torque of the motor.
- The speed of a three phase induction motor can be controlled by varying the frequency, the number of poles, or the slip. However, these methods have some limitations and drawbacks, such as reduced efficiency, increased losses, or complex circuitry. Therefore, other methods such as using variable frequency drives, soft starters, or vector control are preferred for speed control of three phase induction motors.
