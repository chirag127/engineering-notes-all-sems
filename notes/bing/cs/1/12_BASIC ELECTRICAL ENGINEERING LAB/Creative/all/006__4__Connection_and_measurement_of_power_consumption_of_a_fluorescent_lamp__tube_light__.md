##### 4. Connection and measurement of power consumption of a fluorescent lamp (tube light).

- A fluorescent lamp (tube light) is a type of electric light that uses a gas discharge to produce visible light. It consists of a glass tube filled with low-pressure mercury vapor and argon gas, and coated with a phosphor material that emits light when excited by ultraviolet radiation from the gas discharge.
- A fluorescent lamp requires a ballast to provide the necessary voltage and current to start and maintain the gas discharge. The ballast can be either magnetic or electronic, and it can be integrated in the lamp or separate from it.
- The power consumption of a fluorescent lamp depends on the wattage rating of the lamp and the ballast. The wattage rating of the lamp is the power dissipated in the gas discharge, and it does not include the power dissipated in the ballast. The power consumption of the ballast depends on its type, efficiency, and power factor.
- To measure the power consumption of a fluorescent lamp, a wattmeter can be used. A wattmeter is a device that measures the product of voltage and current, and accounts for the phase angle between them. The phase angle is the difference in time between the voltage and current waveforms, and it indicates the degree of power factor.
- The power factor is a measure of how efficiently the power is used by the load. It is the ratio of the real power (the power that does work) to the apparent power (the power that is supplied). A power factor of 1 means that the load is purely resistive and uses all the power supplied. A power factor of less than 1 means that the load is reactive and has some capacitance or inductance that causes the voltage and current to be out of phase.
- A fluorescent lamp with a magnetic ballast has a low power factor, typically around 0.5, because the ballast has a large inductance that causes the current to lag behind the voltage. A fluorescent lamp with an electronic ballast has a high power factor, typically around 0.95, because the ballast has a power factor correction circuit that reduces the phase angle.
- To connect and measure the power consumption of a fluorescent lamp, the following steps can be followed :

  1. Connect the fluorescent lamp with the appropriate ballast to the power supply. The ballast may have a starter switch that needs to be closed to initiate the gas discharge.
  2. Connect the wattmeter in series with the lamp and the power supply. The wattmeter has two terminals, one for the voltage and one for the current. The voltage terminal should be connected to the power supply, and the current terminal should be connected to the lamp.
  3. Turn on the power supply and observe the reading on the wattmeter. The reading indicates the power consumption of the lamp and the ballast in watts. The power consumption may vary slightly depending on the temperature and the age of the lamp.
  4. To calculate the power factor of the lamp and the ballast, divide the power consumption by the product of the voltage and the current. The voltage and the current can be measured by a voltmeter and an ammeter, respectively, or by a multimeter that can measure both. The power factor should be between 0 and 1, and it should be close to 1 for an electronic ballast and close to 0.5 for a magnetic ballast.
