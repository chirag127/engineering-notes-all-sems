##### 10. Demonstration of cut-out sections of machines: dc machine, three phase induction machine, single-phase induction machine and synchronous machine.

- The purpose of this demonstration is to study the various parts of cut section of dc machine, induction machine and synchronous machine and understand their construction and working principles.
- A cut-out section of a machine is a part of the machine that is cut or removed to expose the internal components and show how they are arranged and connected.
- The following are the main parts of the cut-out sections of the machines:

  - DC machine: A dc machine is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa by using the principle of electromagnetic induction. A dc machine consists of two main parts: a stator and a rotor. The stator is the stationary part of the machine that contains the field windings and the brushes. The rotor is the rotating part of the machine that contains the armature windings and the commutator. The commutator is a device that reverses the direction of the current in the armature windings as the rotor rotates, so that the torque is always in the same direction. The brushes are conductors that slide on the commutator and transfer the current between the stator and the rotor. A cut-out section of a dc machine shows the commutator-brush arrangement, the field and armature windings, and the air gap between the stator and the rotor.

  - Three phase induction machine: A three phase induction machine is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy by using the principle of electromagnetic induction. A three phase induction machine consists of two main parts: a stator and a rotor. The stator is the stationary part of the machine that contains the three phase stator windings that are connected to the three phase supply. The rotor is the rotating part of the machine that contains the rotor windings that are either wound or short-circuited. The most common type of rotor is the squirrel cage rotor, which consists of copper or aluminum bars that are short-circuited at both ends by end rings. A cut-out section of a three phase induction machine shows the stator and rotor windings, the air gap between the stator and the rotor, and the squirrel cage rotor.

  - Single-phase induction machine: A single-phase induction machine is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy by using the principle of electromagnetic induction. A single-phase induction machine consists of two main parts: a stator and a rotor. The stator is the stationary part of the machine that contains the single phase stator winding that is connected to the single phase supply. The rotor is the rotating part of the machine that contains the rotor winding that is either wound or short-circuited. The most common type of rotor is the squirrel cage rotor, which is similar to the three phase induction machine. However, a single-phase induction machine cannot start on its own, because the stator winding produces a pulsating magnetic field that does not create a rotating magnetic field. Therefore, a single-phase induction machine needs a starting mechanism, such as a capacitor, a shaded pole, or a split phase, to create a phase difference between the stator windings and produce a rotating magnetic field. A cut-out section of a single-phase induction machine shows the stator and rotor windings, the air gap between the stator and the rotor, the squirrel cage rotor, and the starting mechanism.

  - Synchronous machine: A synchronous machine is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa by using the principle of electromagnetic induction. A synchronous machine consists of two main parts: a stator and a rotor. The stator is the stationary part of the machine that contains the three phase stator windings that are connected to the three phase supply. The rotor is the rotating part of the machine that contains the rotor windings that are excited by a dc source. The rotor windings produce a constant magnetic field that is synchronized with the rotating magnetic field of the stator. A synchronous machine can operate as a motor or a generator, depending on the direction of the power flow. A cut-out section of a synchronous machine shows the stator and rotor windings, the air gap between the stator and the rotor, and the dc excitation in the rotor windings.
