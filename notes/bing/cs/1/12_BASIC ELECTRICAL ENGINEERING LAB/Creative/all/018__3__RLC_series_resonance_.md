##### 3. RLC series resonance.

- An RLC series circuit consists of a resistor (R), an inductor (L), and a capacitor (C) connected in series.
- The circuit has a resonant frequency (f_r) at which the impedance (Z) is purely resistive and the current (I) is maximum.
- The resonant frequency is given by the formula:

  `f_r = 1 / (2π√(LC))`

- At resonance, the inductive reactance (X_L) and the capacitive reactance (X_C) are equal and opposite, and cancel each other out.

  `X_L = X_C = 2πf_rL = 1 / (2πf_rC)`

- The impedance at resonance is equal to the resistance of the circuit.

  `Z = R`

- The voltage across each component is proportional to its impedance.

  `V_R = IR, V_L = IX_L, V_C = IX_C`

- The phasor diagram of the circuit at resonance shows that the voltage across the resistor is in phase with the current, while the voltage across the inductor is 90° ahead of the current and the voltage across the capacitor is 90° behind the current.

  ```
       V_L
        |
        |
        |     V_R
        |      |
        |      |
        |      |
  V_C --+------+-- I
        |      |
        |      |
        |      |
        |      |
        |      |
        +------+
  ```

- The quality factor (Q) of the circuit is a measure of how sharp the resonance is. It is defined as the ratio of the resonant frequency to the bandwidth (B), which is the range of frequencies for which the current is at least half of its maximum value.

  `Q = f_r / B`

- The quality factor is also equal to the ratio of the inductive reactance to the resistance at resonance.

  `Q = X_L / R = X_C / R`

- A higher quality factor means a higher selectivity and a lower energy loss in the circuit.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for RLC series resonance are:

  - Remember the formula for the resonant frequency as "one over two pi root LC".
  - Remember that at resonance, the impedance is equal to the resistance, and the reactances cancel out.
  - Remember that the quality factor is the ratio of the frequency to the bandwidth, or the ratio of the reactance to the resistance.
  - Remember that the voltage across the resistor is in phase with the current, while the voltage across the inductor leads the current by 90° and the voltage across the capacitor lags the current by 90°.
