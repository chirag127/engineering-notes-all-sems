- The virtual lab link is for an online simulation of a **series LCR circuit**.
- A series LCR circuit consists of a **resistor (R)**, a **capacitor (C)**, and an **inductor (L)** connected in series to an **alternating current (AC) source**.
- The simulation allows the user to **vary the values of R, C, L, and the frequency (f) of the AC source**, and observe the effects on the **voltage (V)**, the **current (I)**, the **phase angle (φ)**, and the **power factor (PF)** of the circuit.
- The simulation also shows the **phasor diagram** of the circuit, which is a graphical representation of the **phase difference** between the voltage and the current.
- The simulation can be used to study the following concepts and phenomena related to the series LCR circuit:
  - **Impedance (Z)**: The total opposition to the flow of current in the circuit, which depends on R, C, L, and f. It is given by the formula: Z = √(R^2 + (XL - XC)^2), where XL = 2πfL is the inductive reactance and XC = 1/(2πfC) is the capacitive reactance.
  - **Resonance**: The condition when the inductive reactance and the capacitive reactance are equal, i.e., XL = XC. In this case, the impedance is minimum and equal to R, and the current is maximum and in phase with the voltage. The resonance frequency is given by: f = 1/(2π√(LC)).
  - **Quality factor (Q)**: A measure of the sharpness of the resonance peak, which depends on R, C, and L. It is given by the formula: Q = 1/R √(L/C).
  - **Bandwidth (B)**: The range of frequencies for which the current is greater than or equal to 70.7% of its maximum value at resonance. It is given by the formula: B = R/L.
  - **Phase angle (φ)**: The angle by which the current lags or leads the voltage in the circuit, which depends on R, C, L, and f. It is given by the formula: φ = tan^(-1)((XL - XC)/R). If XL > XC, the current lags the voltage and φ is positive. If XL < XC, the current leads the voltage and φ is negative. If XL = XC, the current is in phase with the voltage and φ is zero.
  - **Power factor (PF)**: The ratio of the true power (P) to the apparent power (S) in the circuit, which depends on R, C, L, and f. It is given by the formula: PF = P/S = cos(φ). It is a measure of how efficiently the circuit uses the power supplied by the source. The higher the PF, the lower the power loss in the circuit. The PF is maximum and equal to 1 at resonance, when φ is zero.

  - To remember the formula for impedance, Z = √(R^2 + (XL - XC)^2), think of the Pythagorean theorem: Z is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with sides R and (XL - XC).
  - To remember the formula for resonance frequency, f = 1/(2π√(LC)), think of the inverse relationship between f and L and C: the higher the values of L and C, the lower the value of f, and vice versa.
  - To remember the formula for quality factor, Q = 1/R √(L/C), think of the inverse relationship between Q and R: the higher the value of R, the lower the value of Q, and vice versa.
