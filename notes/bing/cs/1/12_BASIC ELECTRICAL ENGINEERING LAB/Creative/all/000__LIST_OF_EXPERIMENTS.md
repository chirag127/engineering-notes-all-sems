## LIST OF EXPERIMENTS

- An experiment is a scientific procedure that is carried out to test a hypothesis, observe a phenomenon, or measure a variable.
- Experiments usually involve manipulating one or more independent variables and measuring their effects on one or more dependent variables.
- Experiments can be classified into different types based on their design, purpose, or outcome. Some common types of experiments are:

  - **Controlled experiments**: These are experiments where all the variables except the independent variable are kept constant or controlled. The independent variable is systematically varied and its effect on the dependent variable is observed. Controlled experiments allow for causal inference and eliminate confounding factors. For example, a controlled experiment to test the effect of fertilizer on plant growth would involve growing plants in identical conditions, except for the amount of fertilizer applied to each group.

  - **Randomized experiments**: These are experiments where the participants or units are randomly assigned to different groups or conditions. Randomization ensures that the groups are comparable in terms of all the variables except the independent variable. Randomized experiments are considered the gold standard for causal inference, as they eliminate selection bias and balance confounding factors. For example, a randomized experiment to test the effect of a new drug on blood pressure would involve randomly assigning patients to either receive the drug or a placebo, and measuring their blood pressure before and after the treatment.

  - **Natural experiments**: These are experiments where the independent variable is not manipulated by the experimenter, but by some natural or external factor. Natural experiments allow for causal inference in situations where controlled or randomized experiments are not feasible or ethical. For example, a natural experiment to test the effect of education on income would involve comparing the incomes of people who were exposed to different levels of schooling due to some policy change or historical event.

  - **Quasi-experiments**: These are experiments where the independent variable is manipulated by the experimenter, but the participants or units are not randomly assigned to different groups or conditions. Quasi-experiments are often used when randomization is not possible or practical, but they are subject to selection bias and confounding factors. For example, a quasi-experiment to test the effect of a training program on employee performance would involve comparing the performance of employees who voluntarily enrolled in the program with those who did not.

  - **Observational studies**: These are not experiments, but rather studies where the experimenter does not manipulate any variable, but only observes and measures the variables of interest. Observational studies can describe patterns, correlations, or associations, but they cannot establish causation. For example, an observational study to test the relationship between smoking and lung cancer would involve collecting data on the smoking habits and health outcomes of a large sample of people.
