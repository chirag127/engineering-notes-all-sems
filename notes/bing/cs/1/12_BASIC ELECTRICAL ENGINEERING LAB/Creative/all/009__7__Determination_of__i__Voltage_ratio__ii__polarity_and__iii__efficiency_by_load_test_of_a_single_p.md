##### 7. Determination of (i) Voltage ratio (ii) polarity and (iii) efficiency by load test of a single phase Transformer

- The voltage ratio of a single phase transformer is the ratio of the secondary voltage to the primary voltage. It is also equal to the ratio of the number of turns in the secondary winding to the number of turns in the primary winding, if the transformer is ideal.
- The polarity of a single phase transformer is the relative direction of the induced voltages in the primary and secondary windings. It can be determined by the dot convention, which assigns a dot to one terminal of each winding. The dots indicate that the voltages at those terminals have the same polarity at any instant.
- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power. It can be calculated by measuring the input voltage, current and power factor, and the output voltage, current and power factor, and applying the formula:

`Efficiency = (Output voltage x Output current x Output power factor) / (Input voltage x Input current x Input power factor)`

- To perform the load test of a single phase transformer, the following steps are followed:

  - Connect the primary winding of the transformer to a variable voltage source and a wattmeter, ammeter and voltmeter in series.
  - Connect the secondary winding of the transformer to a resistive load and a wattmeter, ammeter and voltmeter in series.
  - Adjust the input voltage to the rated value and note down the readings of all the meters.
  - Vary the load resistance and repeat the above step for different values of output current.
  - Plot the efficiency versus output power curve and find the maximum efficiency point.
