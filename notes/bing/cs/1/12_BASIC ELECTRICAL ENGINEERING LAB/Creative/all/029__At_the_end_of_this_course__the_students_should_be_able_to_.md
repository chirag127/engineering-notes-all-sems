#### At the end of this course, the students should be able to:

- Define and explain the basic concepts and principles of computer science, such as algorithms, data structures, abstraction, complexity, and recursion.
- Apply mathematical and logical reasoning to analyze and solve computational problems, such as proofs, induction, recurrence relations, and graph theory.
- Design, implement, test, and debug programs in various programming languages, such as Python, Java, C, and Assembly, using appropriate data structures and algorithms.
- Use software tools and environments, such as IDEs, debuggers, compilers, and version control systems, to develop and manage software projects.
- Evaluate the performance and efficiency of different algorithms and data structures, using asymptotic notation, empirical analysis, and experimental methods.
- Compare and contrast the advantages and disadvantages of different programming paradigms, such as imperative, functional, object-oriented, and declarative.
- Demonstrate the ability to work effectively in teams, communicate clearly and professionally, and adhere to ethical and professional standards in computer science.
