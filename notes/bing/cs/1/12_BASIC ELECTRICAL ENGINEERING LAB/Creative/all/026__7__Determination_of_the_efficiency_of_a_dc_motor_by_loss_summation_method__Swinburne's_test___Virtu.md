##### 7. Determination of the efficiency of a dc motor by loss summation method (Swinburne's test). Virtual lab link: http://em-iitr.vlabs.ac.in/exp5/index.php?section=Theory

- The efficiency of a dc motor is defined as the ratio of output (mechanical) power to input (electrical) power.
- The efficiency of a dc motor can be determined by direct or indirect methods.
- The direct method requires measuring the input power by a power analyzer and the output power by a torque meter and a tachometer.
- The indirect method, also known as the loss summation method, requires measuring the losses of the motor instead of the output power.
- The loss summation method is based on the principle that the input power is equal to the sum of the output power and the losses.
- The losses of a dc motor can be classified into two types: constant losses and variable losses.
- Constant losses are independent of the load and include core loss, friction and windage loss, and stray load loss.
- Variable losses are dependent on the load and include armature copper loss, field copper loss, and brush contact loss.
- The loss summation method can be applied to shunt and compound dc motors, but not to series dc motors.
- The loss summation method involves running the motor at no load and measuring the input power, which is equal to the constant losses.
- The loss summation method also involves running the motor at rated voltage and rated speed, and measuring the armature current and the field current, which are used to calculate the variable losses.

- The loss summation method has the advantages of convenience, economy, and accuracy, as it does not require loading the motor or measuring the output power.
- The loss summation method has the disadvantages of being applicable only to shunt and compound motors, and requiring the knowledge of the motor parameters and the losses.
