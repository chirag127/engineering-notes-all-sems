#### (A) Hardware based experiments

- Hardware based experiments are experiments that involve the use of physical devices, components, circuits, or systems to test a hypothesis, demonstrate a principle, or perform a measurement.
- Hardware based experiments can be classified into two types: analog and digital.
  - Analog experiments use continuous signals and components, such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, transistors, op-amps, etc. Analog experiments can be used to study topics such as filters, amplifiers, oscillators, modulators, etc.
  - Digital experiments use discrete signals and components, such as logic gates, flip-flops, counters, registers, microcontrollers, etc. Digital experiments can be used to study topics such as combinational and sequential logic, arithmetic circuits, memory devices, etc.
- Hardware based experiments require the following steps:
  - Design: The experiment should be designed based on the objective, the available components, the expected output, and the measurement methods. The design should be verified by simulation or calculation before implementation.
  - Implementation: The experiment should be implemented by assembling the components on a breadboard, a printed circuit board, or a prototyping board. The connections should be checked for correctness and continuity. The power supply should be connected and adjusted to the required voltage and current levels.
  - Testing: The experiment should be tested by applying the input signals and observing the output signals. The output signals should be measured by using instruments such as multimeters, oscilloscopes, logic analyzers, etc. The results should be recorded and compared with the expected values.
  - Analysis: The experiment should be analyzed by interpreting the results, identifying the sources of errors, and evaluating the performance. The analysis should be supported by graphs, tables, equations, etc. The analysis should also include suggestions for improvement or further exploration.
- Hardware based experiments have the following advantages:
  - They provide hands-on experience and practical skills in working with hardware devices and instruments.
  - They enhance the understanding of the theoretical concepts and principles by visualizing and verifying them.
  - They stimulate creativity and problem-solving abilities by requiring design and implementation solutions.
  - They foster teamwork and communication skills by involving collaboration and discussion among peers and instructors.
- Hardware based experiments have the following disadvantages:
  - They require more time, resources, and space than software based experiments.
  - They are more prone to errors, faults, and damages due to human or environmental factors.
  - They are more difficult to modify, debug, and document than software based experiments.
