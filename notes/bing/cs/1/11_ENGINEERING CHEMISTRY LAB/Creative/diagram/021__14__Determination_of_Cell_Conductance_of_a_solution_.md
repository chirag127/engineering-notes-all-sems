The conductance of a solution is the reciprocal of its resistance. Therefore, electrolytic conductivity of a solution can be measured by measuring the resistance of the solution. The resistance of an electrolytic solution is measured by using a special type of cell known as a conductivity cell and a Wheatstone bridge.

A conductivity cell is a device that consists of two electrodes of known area and distance, immersed in the solution whose conductance is to be measured. The cell constant, C, is the ratio of the distance between the electrodes, l, and the area of cross-section of the electrodes, a. The cell constant has units of m^-1^ and can be determined by measuring the resistance of a cell filled with a solution of known conductivity, such as KCl .
