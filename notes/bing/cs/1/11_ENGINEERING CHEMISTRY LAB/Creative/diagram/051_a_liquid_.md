A liquid is one of the three principal states of matter, intermediate between gas and crystalline solid. In a liquid, the particles are mostly touching but randomly arranged. They can move around each other and take the shape of their container. A liquid has a fixed volume but not a fixed shape.

A diagram of a liquid can be drawn using ASCII characters, such as dots, dashes, slashes, and brackets. Here is an example of a possible diagram of a liquid in a container:

```
  ___________
 /           \
| . . . . . . |
| . . . . . . |
| . . . . . . |
| . . . . . . |
| . . . . . . |
 \___________/
```

The dots represent the particles of the liquid, which are close together but not in a regular pattern. The slashes and brackets represent the container, which has a curved shape. The liquid conforms to the shape of the container and fills the bottom part of it. The space above the liquid is empty or filled with gas. This diagram is not to scale and does not show the actual size or number of particles in a liquid. It is a simplified representation to illustrate the concept of a liquid state of matter.
