One of the experiments to determine the surface tension of a given liquid is to use a paper clip and a cup of water. The paper clip can float on the surface of the water due to the surface tension, which is the tendency of the water molecules to stick together and form a thin film on the surface. The surface tension can be measured by the force required to break the film and make the paper clip sink.

To measure the surface tension, a needle or a pin can be used to gently push the paper clip down until it sinks. The force applied by the needle or the pin can be measured by a spring balance or a dynamometer. The surface tension can be calculated by dividing the force by the length of the paper clip. The formula is:

    Surface tension = Force / Length

The unit of surface tension is N/m (Newton per meter) or J/m^2 (Joule per square meter). The surface tension of water at 20°C is about 0.072 N/m. Different liquids have different surface tensions depending on their intermolecular forces. For example, mercury has a very high surface tension of 0.465 N/m, while ethanol has a low surface tension of 0.022 N/m.
