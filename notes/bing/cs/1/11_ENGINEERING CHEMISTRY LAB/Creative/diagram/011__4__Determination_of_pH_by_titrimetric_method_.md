The titrimetric method of pH determination is based on measuring the amount of a standard solution of known concentration that is required to react completely with a sample solution of unknown concentration. The point at which the reaction is complete is called the equivalence point or the end point, and it is usually indicated by a sudden change in the color of an indicator or the potential of a pH meter.

The procedure for the titrimetric method of pH determination is as follows:

1. Fill the burette with the standard solution of known concentration and record the initial volume.
2. Fill the Erlenmeyer flask with the sample solution of unknown concentration and add a few drops of the indicator or connect the pH meter to the flask.
3. Adjust the valve of the burette to allow the standard solution to flow slowly into the flask, while stirring the flask with the magnetic stirrer.
4. Observe the color change of the indicator or the potential reading of the pH meter as the standard solution is added to the flask.
5. Stop the flow of the standard solution when the indicator changes color or the pH meter reaches a constant value, indicating the equivalence point or the end point of the reaction.
6. Record the final volume of the standard solution in the burette and calculate the volume of the standard solution used in the titration.
7. Use the stoichiometry of the reaction and the known concentration of the standard solution to calculate the concentration of the sample solution.
8. Use the concentration of the sample solution and the dissociation constants of the acid or the base to calculate the pH of the sample solution. Alternatively, use the potential reading of the pH meter to directly determine the pH of the sample solution.
