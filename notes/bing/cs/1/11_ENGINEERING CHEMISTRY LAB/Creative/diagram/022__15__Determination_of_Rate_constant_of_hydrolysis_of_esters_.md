### 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters.

The hydrolysis of esters is a reversible reaction that can be catalyzed by either an acid or a base. In this experiment, we will use an acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of ethyl acetate as an example. The reaction is as follows:

CH3COOC2H5 + H2O <--> CH3COOH + C2H5OH

Ethyl acetate + Water <--> Acetic acid + Ethanol

The rate of the reaction depends on the concentrations of the reactants and the catalyst. The rate law can be written as:

Rate = k [CH3COOC2H5] [H2O] [H+]

where k is the rate constant, [CH3COOC2H5] is the concentration of ethyl acetate, [H2O] is the concentration of water, and [H+] is the concentration of the acid catalyst.

To determine the rate constant, we need to measure the change in concentration of one of the reactants or products over time. In this experiment, we will use a conductometric method to measure the change in concentration of acetic acid, which is a product of the hydrolysis. The conductance of a solution is proportional to the concentration of the ions in the solution. Since acetic acid is a weak acid, it will dissociate partially in water and produce acetate ions and hydrogen ions. The conductance of the solution will increase as more acetic acid is formed. By measuring the conductance of the solution at different time intervals, we can calculate the concentration of acetic acid and then use the stoichiometry of the reaction to find the concentration of ethyl acetate. The rate constant can then be calculated by using the rate law and the initial concentrations of the reactants and the catalyst.
