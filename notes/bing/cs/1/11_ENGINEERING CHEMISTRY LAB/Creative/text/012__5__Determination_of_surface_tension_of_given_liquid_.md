### 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid.

Surface tension is the property of a liquid that makes its surface behave like a stretched elastic membrane. It is caused by the cohesive forces between the molecules of the liquid. Surface tension is measured in units of force per unit length (N/m or dyn/cm).

There are different methods to determine the surface tension of a given liquid, such as:

- Drop number method using stalagmometer
- Drop weight method using drop weight apparatus
- Drip method using capillary tube
- Du Nouy ring method using tensiometer
- Wilhelmy plate method using tensiometer

Here, we will describe the drop number method using stalagmometer, which is a simple and inexpensive technique.

A stalagmometer is a glass tube with a bulb at one end and a fine nozzle at the other end. It is filled with the liquid whose surface tension is to be determined and then with water. The number of drops of the liquid and water falling from the nozzle are counted and recorded. The surface tension of the liquid is calculated using the formula:

rl = nw×dl rw nl×dw

where rl and rw are the surface tensions of the liquid and water respectively, nw and nl are the number of drops of water and liquid respectively, and dl and dw are the densities of the liquid and water respectively.

The surface tension of water at a given temperature can be obtained from a standard table or graph.

Some precautions to be taken while performing this experiment are:

- The stalagmometer should be clean and dry before filling it with the liquid.
- The nozzle of the stalagmometer should be free from any obstruction or air bubbles.
- The drops of the liquid and water should be allowed to fall freely without touching the nozzle or the container.
- The temperature of the liquid and water should be the same and constant throughout the experiment.
- The experiment should be repeated at least three times and the average value of the surface tension should be taken.
