### 5. To enable the students to understand about the synthesis of organic compounds such as adipic acid and paracetamol by conventional and green route.

- Adipic acid is an important industrial chemical that is used as a precursor for the production of nylon-6,6, a widely used synthetic polymer. The conventional route of synthesis of adipic acid involves the oxidation of benzene or cyclohexane with nitric acid, which generates large amounts of nitrous oxide (N2O), a potent greenhouse gas and an ozone-depleting substance. The green route of synthesis of adipic acid aims to reduce the environmental impact of the process by using renewable feedstocks, such as glucose, and environmentally benign oxidants, such as hydrogen peroxide. The green route of synthesis of adipic acid can be summarized as follows :

  - Glucose is converted to glucaric acid by oxidation with nitric acid or oxygen in the presence of a catalyst, such as manganese dioxide or gold nanoparticles.
  - Glucaric acid is dehydrated to 2,5-furandicarboxylic acid (FDCA) by heating with sulfuric acid or phosphoric acid.
  - FDCA is hydrogenated to adipic acid by using a hydrogen donor, such as formic acid, and a catalyst, such as ruthenium or palladium.

- Paracetamol is a widely used analgesic and antipyretic drug that is derived from coal tar or petroleum. The conventional route of synthesis of paracetamol involves the nitration of phenol to produce 4-nitrophenol, which is then reduced to 4-aminophenol by using sodium borohydride or iron and hydrochloric acid. The 4-aminophenol is then acetylated with acetic anhydride to yield paracetamol. The green route of synthesis of paracetamol aims to reduce the use of hazardous reagents and solvents by using renewable feedstocks, such as biomass, and environmentally benign catalysts, such as enzymes. The green route of synthesis of paracetamol can be summarized as follows:

  - Biomass, such as lignocellulose, is hydrolyzed to produce glucose and other monosaccharides by using enzymes, such as cellulases and hemicellulases.
  - Glucose is converted to 4-hydroxybenzoic acid by using a biocatalyst, such as a genetically modified microorganism or a plant cell culture, that expresses the enzymes involved in the shikimate pathway.
  - 4-hydroxybenzoic acid is decarboxylated to phenol by using a catalyst, such as copper or iron oxide, or by using a biocatalyst, such as a microorganism that expresses a decarboxylase enzyme.
  - Phenol is aminated to 4-aminophenol by using a catalyst, such as palladium or platinum, or by using a biocatalyst, such as a microorganism that expresses an aminase enzyme.
  - 4-aminophenol is acetylated to paracetamol by using a catalyst, such as zeolite or alumina, or by using a biocatalyst, such as an acetyltransferase enzyme.

- The advantages of the green route of synthesis of organic compounds over the conventional route are:

  - The green route uses renewable and abundant feedstocks, such as biomass, instead of fossil fuels, such as coal tar or petroleum, which are non-renewable and depleting resources.
  - The green route uses environmentally benign oxidants and reagents, such as hydrogen peroxide and enzymes, instead of hazardous and toxic chemicals, such as nitric acid and sodium borohydride, which pose health and safety risks and generate harmful by-products and wastes.
  - The green route uses mild and selective catalysts, such as gold nanoparticles and biocatalysts, instead of harsh and non-selective catalysts, such as manganese dioxide and sulfuric acid, which require high temperature and pressure and cause unwanted side reactions and low yields.
  - The green route reduces the energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions of the process by using ambient or low temperature and pressure and by avoiding the formation of nitrous oxide and carbon dioxide.
