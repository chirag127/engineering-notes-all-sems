### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample.

- Alkalinity is a measure of the acid-neutralizing capacity of a water sample. It is an important parameter to assess the quality of water and its suitability for various purposes.
- Alkalinity is mainly caused by the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium, sodium, and potassium in water. These substances can react with acids and buffer the pH of water.
- One common method to determine the alkalinity of a water sample is to titrate it with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, while monitoring the pH of the water with a pH meter or an indicator.
- The titration curve of alkalinity shows three regions: the first region is where the pH is above 8.3 and the alkalinity is due to hydroxides and carbonates; the second region is where the pH is between 4.5 and 8.3 and the alkalinity is due to bicarbonates and carbonates; and the third region is where the pH is below 4.5 and the alkalinity is due to bicarbonates only.
- The endpoint of the titration is usually taken as the pH where the second region ends and the third region begins, which is around 4.5. The volume of acid used to reach this endpoint is proportional to the total alkalinity of the water sample.
- The total alkalinity can be calculated by multiplying the volume of acid used by its normality and by a conversion factor of 50, which is the equivalent weight of carbon dioxide. The unit of alkalinity is usually expressed as mg/L as CaCO3, which is the amount of calcium carbonate that would have the same acid-neutralizing capacity as the water sample.
- The total alkalinity can also be divided into different components, such as carbonate alkalinity, bicarbonate alkalinity, and hydroxide alkalinity, depending on the pH range of the titration. These components can be calculated by using the following formulas:

  - Carbonate alkalinity = 2 x (Volume of acid used to reach pH 8.3) x (Normality of acid) x 50
  - Bicarbonate alkalinity = (Volume of acid used to reach pH 4.5 - Volume of acid used to reach pH 8.3) x (Normality of acid) x 50
  - Hydroxide alkalinity = (Volume of acid used to reach pH 10 - Volume of acid used to reach pH 8.3) x (Normality of acid) x 50

- The sum of these components should equal the total alkalinity of the water sample.
