### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

- Bleaching powder is a compound of calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)2) and calcium chloride (CaCl2) that is used as a disinfectant and a bleaching agent.
- The available chlorine in bleaching powder is the amount of chlorine that can be liberated from the compound when it reacts with water or an acid.
- The available chlorine can be determined by the iodometric method, which involves the following steps:

  - A known mass of bleaching powder is dissolved in distilled water by grinding and filtering.
  - A measured volume of this solution is taken in a conical flask and acidified with dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl).
  - An excess of potassium iodide (KI) solution is added to the flask, which reacts with the available chlorine and liberates iodine (I2) according to the equation:

    Ca(OCl)2 + 4KI + 4HCl → CaCl2 + 2KCl + 2I2 + 2H2O

  - The liberated iodine is then titrated with a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) using starch as an indicator. The starch turns blue in the presence of iodine and colorless when all the iodine is consumed by the thiosulfate according to the equation:

    2Na2S2O3 + I2 → Na2S4O6 + 2NaI

  - The volume of thiosulfate solution used is noted and the percentage of available chlorine in the bleaching powder is calculated using the formula:

    % available chlorine = (V x N x 35.5 x 100) / (W x 1000)

    where V is the volume of thiosulfate solution in ml, N is the normality of thiosulfate solution, W is the mass of bleaching powder in g, and 35.5 is the equivalent mass of chlorine.
