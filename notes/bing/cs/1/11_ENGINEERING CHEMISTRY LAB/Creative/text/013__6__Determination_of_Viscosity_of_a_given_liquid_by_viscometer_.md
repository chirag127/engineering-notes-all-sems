### 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer.

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow. It is defined as the ratio of the shear stress to the shear rate in a fluid.
- A viscometer is a device that measures the viscosity of a fluid by observing the flow of the fluid through a narrow tube or a capillary.
- The most common type of viscometer is the Ostwald viscometer, which consists of a U-shaped glass tube with two bulbs and a constant-diameter capillary connecting them. The lower bulb has a larger volume than the upper one and is connected to a reservoir of the fluid to be tested. The upper bulb has a mark to indicate the level of the fluid when the viscometer is filled.
- To determine the viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer, the following steps are followed:

  - The viscometer is cleaned and dried, and then filled with the liquid to be tested up to the mark on the upper bulb. The reservoir is also filled with the same liquid.
  - The viscometer is immersed in a water bath maintained at a constant temperature and allowed to reach thermal equilibrium.
  - The liquid is allowed to flow from the upper bulb to the lower bulb by opening a valve or a stopcock at the reservoir. The time taken for the liquid to flow from one mark to another mark on the capillary is noted using a stopwatch. This is the flow time of the liquid, t.
  - The same procedure is repeated for at least three times and the average flow time is calculated.
  - The viscometer is then emptied and rinsed with distilled water, and then filled with a standard liquid of known viscosity, such as water or glycerin, at the same temperature. The flow time of the standard liquid, t0, is measured in the same way as the test liquid.
  - The relative viscosity of the test liquid is calculated as the ratio of the flow time of the test liquid to the flow time of the standard liquid, i.e., ηr = t/t0.
  - The absolute viscosity of the test liquid is calculated by multiplying the relative viscosity with the viscosity of the standard liquid, i.e., η = ηr × η0.
  - The sources of error and the precautions to be taken in the experiment are:

    - The temperature of the water bath should be kept constant and uniform throughout the experiment, as the viscosity of liquids varies with temperature.
    - The viscometer should be free from air bubbles and dust particles, as they can affect the flow of the liquid and the accuracy of the measurements.
    - The liquid should be allowed to flow smoothly and steadily, without any jerks or vibrations, as they can alter the flow time and the viscosity.
    - The stopwatch should be started and stopped at the exact instant when the liquid level crosses the marks on the capillary, as any delay or error can affect the flow time and the viscosity.
