### 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method.

- Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions such as Ca2+ and Mg2+.
- The EDTA method involves titrating a water sample with a standard solution of EDTA in the presence of a suitable indicator that changes color at the end point of the titration.
- The indicator used for this method is Eriochrome Black T (EBT), which forms a wine-red complex with free Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions in water.
- As EDTA is added, it reacts with the metal ions and releases the EBT, which turns blue in the solution.
- The end point of the titration is reached when all the metal ions are complexed by EDTA and the solution turns from wine-red to blue.
- The hardness of water can be calculated from the volume of EDTA used and its concentration.

The procedure for the EDTA method is as follows:

- Pipette 50 mL of the water sample into a conical flask and add 2-3 drops of EBT indicator.
- Titrate the sample with 0.01 M EDTA solution from a burette until the color changes from wine-red to blue.
- Record the volume of EDTA used as V1.
- Repeat the titration with a blank (distilled water) and record the volume of EDTA used as V2.
- Calculate the hardness of water in ppm (parts per million) as:

Hardness (ppm) = (V1 - V2) x 0.01 M x 1000 / 50

- Alternatively, the hardness of water can be expressed in terms of CaCO3 equivalent as:

Hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) = (V1 - V2) x 0.01 M x 1000 x 50 / 50

- The hardness of water can be classified as soft, moderately hard, hard, or very hard based on the following ranges:

Soft: 0 - 75 mg/L as CaCO3

Moderately hard: 75 - 150 mg/L as CaCO3

Hard: 150 - 300 mg/L as CaCO3

Very hard: > 300 mg/L as CaCO3
