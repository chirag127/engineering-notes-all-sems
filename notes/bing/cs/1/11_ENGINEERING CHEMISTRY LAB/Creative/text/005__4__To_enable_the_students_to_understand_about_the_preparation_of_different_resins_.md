### 4. To enable the students to understand about the preparation of different resins.

- Resins are organic compounds that are derived from natural sources or synthesized artificially. They are usually solid or semi-solid substances that can be dissolved in solvents or melted by heat.
- Resins have various applications in industries such as paints, coatings, adhesives, plastics, rubber, cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, etc.
- There are different types of resins based on their origin, structure, properties, and uses. Some of the common types are:

  - Natural resins: These are obtained from plants or animals, such as rosin, shellac, amber, lacquer, etc. They are usually thermoplastic, meaning they soften when heated and harden when cooled.
  - Synthetic resins: These are produced by chemical reactions, such as polymerization, condensation, addition, etc. They are usually thermosetting, meaning they undergo irreversible curing when heated or exposed to catalysts. Some examples are phenolic resins, epoxy resins, polyurethane resins, etc.
  - Modified resins: These are obtained by modifying natural or synthetic resins with other substances, such as oils, alcohols, acids, etc. They are used to improve the properties or performance of the original resins. Some examples are alkyd resins, ester resins, silicone resins, etc.

- The preparation of different resins involves various steps, such as:

  - Selection of raw materials: The choice of raw materials depends on the type and purpose of the resin. For natural resins, the sources are plants or animals, such as pine trees, insects, etc. For synthetic resins, the monomers or precursors are organic or inorganic compounds, such as phenol, formaldehyde, ethylene, etc.
  - Extraction or synthesis: The raw materials are extracted or synthesized by different methods, such as distillation, extraction, precipitation, polymerization, condensation, addition, etc. The resulting products are crude resins that may contain impurities or by-products.
  - Purification: The crude resins are purified by various techniques, such as filtration, crystallization, washing, drying, etc. The purified resins are then ready for further processing or application.
  - Modification: The purified resins may be modified by adding or reacting with other substances, such as oils, alcohols, acids, etc. The modification may change the properties or performance of the resins, such as solubility, viscosity, hardness, flexibility, etc.
  - Formulation: The modified resins may be formulated with other ingredients, such as solvents, pigments, fillers, additives, etc. The formulation may affect the characteristics or functions of the resins, such as color, gloss, adhesion, durability, etc.
  - Application: The formulated resins may be applied to various substrates or products, such as wood, metal, plastic, paper, etc. The application may involve different methods, such as brushing, spraying, dipping, molding, casting, etc. The applied resins may undergo curing or drying to form a solid film or layer.
