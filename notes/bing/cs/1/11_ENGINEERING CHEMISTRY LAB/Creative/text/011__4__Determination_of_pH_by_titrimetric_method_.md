### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method.

- Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a solution of unknown concentration (titrand).
- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H+].
- Determination of pH by titrimetric method involves performing an acid-base titration, which is a type of titration in which an acid and a base react to form a salt and water.
- An acid-base titration can be used to find out the unknown concentration of an acid or a base by neutralizing it with a base or an acid of known concentration, respectively.
- An appropriate pH indicator is used in the titration chamber, which shows the pH of the solution by changing its color at certain pH values. Alternatively, a pH meter can be used to measure the pH of the solution directly.
- The point at which the acid and the base have reacted exactly in stoichiometric proportions is called the equivalence point. The volume of the titrant added at the equivalence point is called the titration volume.
- The pH at the equivalence point depends on the strength of the acid and the base involved in the titration. For a strong acid-strong base titration, the pH at the equivalence point is 7.00. For a weak acid-strong base titration, the pH at the equivalence point is greater than 7.00. For a strong acid-weak base titration, the pH at the equivalence point is less than 7.00. For a weak acid-weak base titration, the pH at the equivalence point is close to 7.00, but depends on the dissociation constants of the acid and the base.
- The pH at any point in the titration can be calculated by using a tabular format to determine the amounts of all the species present in the solution, and then using the equilibrium expressions to find the hydrogen ion concentration. Alternatively, the pH can be estimated by using a titration curve, which is a graph of pH versus titration volume. The titration curve shows the change in pH as the titrant is added to the titrand. The shape of the titration curve depends on the strength of the acid and the base involved in the titration.
