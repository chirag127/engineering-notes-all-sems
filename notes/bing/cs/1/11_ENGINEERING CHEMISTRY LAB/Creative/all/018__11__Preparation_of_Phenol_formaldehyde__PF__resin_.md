### 11. Preparation of Phenol formaldehyde (PF) resin.

- Phenol formaldehyde (PF) resin is a type of thermosetting polymer that is obtained by the reaction of phenol and formaldehyde.
- Phenol and formaldehyde can react in different ratios and under different conditions of temperature, pH, and catalyst to produce various types of PF resins with different properties and applications.
- The general reaction of phenol and formaldehyde is shown below:

```
  HO-C6H5 + H2C=O  <=>  HO-C6H4-CH2-OH  <=>  HO-C6H4-CH2-O-C6H4-OH + H2O
  phenol      formaldehyde     methylene glycol     bisphenol F     water
```

- The methylene glycol is an intermediate that can further react with more phenol or formaldehyde to form longer chains or cross-linked networks of PF resin.
- The reaction can be either acid-catalyzed or base-catalyzed, depending on the desired properties and applications of the PF resin.
- Acid-catalyzed PF resins are also known as novolacs, and they are formed by reacting phenol and formaldehyde in a molar ratio of 1:0.8 to 1:1.5, usually in the presence of hydrochloric acid or sulfuric acid as a catalyst. Novolacs are linear or branched polymers that are soluble in organic solvents and have low molecular weight and low reactivity. They require a curing agent, such as hexamethylenetetramine, to form cross-links and become thermoset. Novolacs are used for making molding compounds, adhesives, coatings, and laminates.
- Base-catalyzed PF resins are also known as resoles, and they are formed by reacting phenol and formaldehyde in a molar ratio of 1:1.5 to 1:3, usually in the presence of sodium hydroxide or ammonia as a catalyst. Resoles are highly reactive and can form cross-links by themselves upon heating or under acidic conditions. They have high molecular weight and are insoluble in organic solvents. They are used for making plywood, particleboard, insulation foam, and bakelite.
- Some of the advantages of PF resins are:
  - They have high thermal stability and resistance to heat, fire, and chemicals.
  - They have good mechanical strength and hardness.
  - They have low cost and wide availability of raw materials.
- Some of the disadvantages of PF resins are:
  - They have poor resistance to UV light and moisture, which can cause discoloration and degradation.
  - They have high curing temperature and time, which can limit their processing and application.
  - They have high formaldehyde emission, which can cause health and environmental problems.
- Some of the applications of PF resins are:
  - They are used for making electrical and electronic components, such as switches, sockets, plugs, and circuit boards.
  - They are used for making wood-based products, such as plywood, particleboard, fiberboard, and laminates.
  - They are used for making molding compounds, such as buttons, knobs, handles, and toys.
  - They are used for making adhesives, coatings, and binders for various industries, such as paper, textile, rubber, and leather.
