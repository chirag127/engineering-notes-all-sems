# ENGINEERING PHYSICS LAB KCS

- Engineering Physics is a program that combines engineering and physics to solve technological problems and advance scientific knowledge.
- Engineering Physics students have opportunities to explore a variety of lab experiences, such as Jayhawk Motorsports, aircraft design, renewable energy, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, etc. 
- Engineering Physics students can work in the private sector or in national laboratories at the forefront of technology, or pursue an advanced degree in engineering or physics.
- Engineering Physics students can choose from different concentrations, such as aerospace engineering, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, nuclear engineering, etc.
- Engineering Physics students can learn from faculty members who are experts in their fields, such as Kyle Smith, who studies electrochemical energy storage and conversion systems.
- Engineering Physics students can benefit from the resources and facilities of the Kennedy College of Sciences, which offers academic programs, research opportunities, and career services.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for Engineering Physics Lab KCS are:

- To remember the formula for the speed of sound in air, use the acronym **TACO**: **T**emperature, **A**ir, **C**onstant, **O**ne. The formula is: v = 331.5 + 0.6T, where v is the speed of sound in m/s, T is the temperature in °C, and 331.5 and 0.6 are constants.
- To remember the names of the four fundamental forces of nature, use the acronym **SWEG**: **S**trong, **W**eak, **E**lectromagnetic, **G**ravitational. The order of the letters also corresponds to the order of the strength of the forces, from strongest to weakest.
- To remember the difference between scalar and vector quantities, use the phrase **SAD VAM**: **S**calar quantities have only **A**mount, while **V**ector quantities have both **A**mount and **M**agnitude. For example, speed is a scalar quantity, while velocity is a vector quantity.
