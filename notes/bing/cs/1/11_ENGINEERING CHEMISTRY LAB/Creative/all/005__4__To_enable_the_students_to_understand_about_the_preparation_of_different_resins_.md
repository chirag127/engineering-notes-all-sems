### 4. To enable the students to understand about the preparation of different resins.

- Resins are organic substances that are insoluble in water, but soluble in organic solvents or alcohol. They are usually derived from plants, animals, or synthetic sources. Resins have various applications in industries such as paints, coatings, adhesives, plastics, rubber, electrical, jewelry, etc.
- There are different types of resins based on their origin, composition, properties, and uses. Some of the common types of resins are:

  - **Natural resins**: These are resins that are obtained from natural sources such as plants or animals. They are usually formed by the secretion or exudation of certain substances from living organisms. For example, rosin, shellac, amber, copal, etc. Natural resins are usually brittle, hard, and have a characteristic odor. They are used for varnishes, lacquers, incense, etc.
  - **Synthetic resins**: These are resins that are artificially prepared by chemical reactions or polymerization of monomers or oligomers. They are usually more stable, durable, and versatile than natural resins. For example, epoxy, polyester, polyurethane, phenolic, etc. Synthetic resins are used for resin casting, resin impregnation, resin coating, resin molding, etc.
  - **Modified resins**: These are resins that are obtained by modifying natural or synthetic resins by adding or removing certain functional groups or substituents. They are usually designed to improve the properties or performance of the original resins. For example, alkyd, silicone, acrylic, etc. Modified resins are used for paints, sealants, adhesives, etc.

- The preparation of different resins depends on the type, source, and purpose of the resin. Some of the general methods of preparing resins are:

  - **Extraction**: This is the method of obtaining natural resins from plant or animal sources by using solvents, heat, or mechanical pressure. The resinous material is dissolved in a suitable solvent and then filtered, purified, and dried. For example, rosin is extracted from pine trees by tapping the trunk and collecting the resin, shellac is extracted from the secretions of lac insects by boiling them in alcohol, etc.
  - **Polymerization**: This is the method of synthesizing synthetic or modified resins by joining monomers or oligomers together by chemical bonds. The polymerization can be initiated by heat, light, catalysts, or other agents. The polymerization can be either addition or condensation, depending on the type of monomers and the by-products. For example, epoxy resin is prepared by polymerizing epichlorohydrin and bisphenol A, polyester resin is prepared by polymerizing glycol and dicarboxylic acid, etc.
  - **Modification**: This is the method of altering the structure or properties of natural or synthetic resins by introducing or removing certain functional groups or substituents. The modification can be done by chemical reactions, physical treatments, or blending with other resins. For example, alkyd resin is prepared by modifying polyester resin with fatty acids, silicone resin is prepared by modifying phenolic resin with silanes, etc.

- Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for the preparation of different resins are:

  - **REPS**: This stands for Resin Extraction Polymerization Modification Synthesis, which are the four main methods of preparing resins.
  - **NAP**: This stands for Natural Alkyd Phenolic, which are the three types of resins that are derived from plants or animals.
  - **EPP**: This stands for Epoxy Polyester Polyurethane, which are the three types of resins that are prepared by polymerization of monomers or oligomers.
  - **SAM**: This stands for Silicone Acrylic Modified, which are the three types of resins that are obtained by modifying other resins.
