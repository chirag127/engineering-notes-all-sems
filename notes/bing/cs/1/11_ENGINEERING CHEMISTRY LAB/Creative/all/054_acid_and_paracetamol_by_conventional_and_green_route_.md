# Acid and Paracetamol by Conventional and Green Route

## Conventional Route

- The conventional route for the synthesis of paracetamol (also known as acetaminophen) from phenol involves three steps: nitration, reduction and acetylation.
- In the first step, phenol is nitrated with nitric acid to form p-nitrophenol, which is separated from the unwanted o-nitrophenol by steam distillation.
- In the second step, p-nitrophenol is reduced with tin and hydrochloric acid to form p-aminophenol, which is purified by recrystallization.
- In the third step, p-aminophenol is acetylated with acetic anhydride to form paracetamol, which is isolated by filtration and recrystallization.

## Green Route

- The green route for the synthesis of paracetamol from phenol involves two steps: direct amidation and microwave irradiation.
- In the first step, phenol is reacted with acetic anhydride and sodium acetate in the presence of a catalyst, such as zeolite, to form paracetamol and acetic acid. This step eliminates the need for nitration and reduction, and avoids the formation of by-products and waste.
- In the second step, the reaction mixture is heated in a microwave oven for a few minutes to complete the reaction and enhance the yield. This step reduces the energy consumption and the reaction time.

## Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

- To remember the conventional route, one can use the acronym NRA: Nitration, Reduction, Acetylation.
- To remember the green route, one can use the acronym DAM: Direct Amidation, Microwave.
- To remember the difference between the two routes, one can use the phrase: Green route is DAM good, conventional route is NRA bad.
