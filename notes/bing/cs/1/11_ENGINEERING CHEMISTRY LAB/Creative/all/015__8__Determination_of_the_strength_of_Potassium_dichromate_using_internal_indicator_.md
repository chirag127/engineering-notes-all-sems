### 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator.

- Potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7) is a primary standard substance that can be used to determine the concentration of other solutions by redox titration.
- In this experiment, the strength of potassium dichromate solution is determined by titrating it against a known volume of ferrous ammonium sulphate (FAS) solution, which is a secondary standard substance.
- The reaction between potassium dichromate and FAS is as follows:

  Cr2O7<sup>2-</sup> + 6 Fe<sup>2+</sup> + 14 H<sup>+</sup> → 2 Cr<sup>3+</sup> + 6 Fe<sup>3+</sup> + 7 H2O

- The endpoint of the titration is detected by using an internal indicator, such as diphenylamine, which changes color from colorless to blue when the FAS is completely oxidized by the potassium dichromate.
- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:

  - Prepare a standard solution of potassium dichromate by dissolving a known mass of the salt in distilled water and transferring it to a volumetric flask of known volume. Calculate the normality of the solution using the formula:

    N = mass of K2Cr2O7 / (molar mass of K2Cr2O7 x volume of solution)

  - Pipette out a known volume of FAS solution into a conical flask and add a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid and phosphoric acid to provide the acidic medium for the reaction and to prevent the precipitation of iron hydroxides.
  - Add a few drops of diphenylamine indicator to the flask and swirl to mix well.
  - Fill a burette with the potassium dichromate solution and note the initial reading.
  - Titrate the FAS solution with the potassium dichromate solution until a faint blue color appears in the flask, indicating the endpoint of the titration. Note the final reading of the burette.
  - Repeat the titration for two more times and take the average of the readings.
  - Calculate the normality of the FAS solution using the formula:

    N1V1 = N2V2

    where N1 and V1 are the normality and volume of the FAS solution, and N2 and V2 are the normality and volume of the potassium dichromate solution.

  - Calculate the percentage of iron in the FAS solution using the formula:

    % Fe = (N1 x V1 x equivalent mass of Fe x 100) / (mass of FAS taken)

    where equivalent mass of Fe = atomic mass of Fe / 2

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for this experiment are:

  - Remember the color change of the indicator as **Diphenylamine is Blue**.
  - Remember the reaction between potassium dichromate and FAS as **Crunchy Fries** (Cr2O7<sup>2-</sup> + 6 Fe<sup>2+</sup>).
  - Remember the normality formula of potassium dichromate as **N = mass / (molar mass x volume)**.
