### 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a solution.

- Cell conductance is a measure of how well a solution can conduct electric current. It depends on the concentration, type and mobility of the ions present in the solution.
- Cell conductance is related to cell resistance by the equation: `G = 1/R`, where `G` is the cell conductance, and `R` is the cell resistance.
- Cell conductance is also related to the specific conductance (or conductivity) of the solution by the equation: `G = K * C`, where `K` is the specific conductance, and `C` is the cell constant.
- The cell constant is a geometrical factor that depends on the shape, size and distance of the electrodes in the cell. It can be calculated by the equation: `C = l/A`, where `l` is the distance between the electrodes, and `A` is the area of cross-section of the electrodes.
- The cell constant can also be determined experimentally by measuring the cell conductance of a solution with a known specific conductance, such as 0.0200 M KCl. The equation can be rearranged as: `C = G/K`.
- To determine the cell conductance of a solution, the following steps are followed:
  - Prepare the solution of known concentration and temperature, and fill the conductivity cell with it.
  - Connect the conductivity cell to a conductance meter or a Wheatstone bridge circuit, and measure the cell resistance or conductance.
  - Calculate the cell conductance by using the equation: `G = 1/R`, or use the value obtained from the conductance meter.
  - Calculate the cell constant by using the equation: `C = G/K`, where `K` is the known specific conductance of the solution.
  - Rinse the conductivity cell with distilled water, and fill it with the solution whose cell conductance is to be determined.
  - Repeat the steps 2 and 3 to measure the cell conductance of the unknown solution.
  - Calculate the specific conductance of the unknown solution by using the equation: `K = G/C`, where `C` is the cell constant obtained from the previous step.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the equations is: `Gee, I Rock! Kay, See?` (`G = 1/R`, `K = G/C`).
- A possible learning trick to remember the cell constant is: `The cell constant is like a cell phone number. It is unique to each cell, and it helps you connect with the solution.`
