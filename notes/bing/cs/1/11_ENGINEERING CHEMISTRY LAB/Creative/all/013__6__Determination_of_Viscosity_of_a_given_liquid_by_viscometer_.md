### 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer.

- Viscosity is the measure of the internal friction or resistance of a fluid to flow. It is defined as the shear stress required to produce a unit rate of shear strain in a fluid. Viscosity depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature and pressure of the fluid.
- A viscometer is an instrument that measures the viscosity of a fluid by observing its flow through a narrow tube or a capillary. There are different types of viscometers, such as Ostwald viscometer, Ubbelohde viscometer, Cannon-Fenske viscometer, etc. Each viscometer has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the accuracy, range and application of the measurement.
- The basic principle of a viscometer is to measure the time taken by a known volume of fluid to flow through a capillary of known dimensions under a constant pressure difference. The viscosity of the fluid can be calculated by using the following formula:

  $$\eta = k \frac{t}{\rho}$$

  where $\eta$ is the viscosity, $k$ is the viscometer constant, $t$ is the flow time, and $\rho$ is the density of the fluid.

- The viscometer constant $k$ can be determined by using a standard fluid of known viscosity, such as water or glycerin, and measuring its flow time through the viscometer. Alternatively, $k$ can be calculated by using the following formula:

  $$k = \frac{\pi r^4 \Delta P}{8 V L}$$

  where $r$ is the radius of the capillary, $\Delta P$ is the pressure difference, $V$ is the volume of the fluid, and $L$ is the length of the capillary.

- The procedure for determining the viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer is as follows:

  - Clean the viscometer thoroughly and dry it with a gentle stream of air.
  - Fill the viscometer with the liquid to be tested and immerse it in a water bath at a constant temperature.
  - Allow the liquid to reach the thermal equilibrium with the water bath.
  - Adjust the liquid level in the viscometer such that the upper meniscus is slightly above the upper mark on the capillary.
  - Open the valve or release the suction bulb to allow the liquid to flow through the capillary.
  - Start the stopwatch when the lower meniscus passes the upper mark and stop it when it passes the lower mark.
  - Record the flow time and repeat the measurement at least three times to obtain an average value.
  - Calculate the viscosity of the liquid by using the formula $\eta = k \frac{t}{\rho}$, where $k$ is the viscometer constant and $\rho$ is the density of the liquid.
  - Report the viscosity with the appropriate units and the temperature of the measurement.

- Some possible sources of error and uncertainty in the viscosity measurement are:

  - Air bubbles or impurities in the liquid or the viscometer.
  - Variation in the temperature or the pressure of the water bath or the liquid.
  - Parallax or human error in reading the meniscus or the stopwatch.
  - Calibration or manufacturing error in the viscometer or the stopwatch.

- Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the viscosity measurement are:

  - Viscosity is the measure of how sticky or thick a fluid is. A high viscosity fluid flows slowly, while a low viscosity fluid flows quickly. You can remember this by using the words "viscous" and "quick".
  - A viscometer measures the viscosity of a fluid by observing its flow through a narrow tube. You can remember this by using the word "viscometer" and the phrase "viscosity meter".
  - The formula for viscosity is $\eta = k \frac{t}{\rho}$, where $\eta$ is the viscosity, $k$ is the viscometer constant, $t$ is the flow time, and $\rho$ is the density of the fluid. You can remember this by using the acronym "VKTDR" or the phrase "Viscosity equals K times Time divided by Density".
