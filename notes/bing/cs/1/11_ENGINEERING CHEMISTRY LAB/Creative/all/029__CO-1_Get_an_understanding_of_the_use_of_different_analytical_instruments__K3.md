### CO-1 Get an understanding of the use of different analytical instruments. K3

- Analytical instruments are devices that are used to measure, analyze, or monitor physical, chemical, or biological properties of samples, substances, or systems.  
- Analytical instruments can be classified into different categories based on their principles, applications, or techniques. Some common categories are:
  - Spectroscopy: The study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter. Examples of spectroscopic instruments are UV-Vis spectrophotometer, infrared spectrometer, atomic absorption spectrometer, etc.
  - Chromatography: The separation of a mixture into its components based on their affinity to a stationary phase and a mobile phase. Examples of chromatographic instruments are gas chromatograph, liquid chromatograph, ion chromatograph, etc.
  - Mass spectrometry: The determination of the mass-to-charge ratio of ions generated from a sample. Examples of mass spectrometric instruments are quadrupole mass spectrometer, time-of-flight mass spectrometer, etc.
  - Electrochemistry: The study of the relationship between electrical and chemical phenomena. Examples of electrochemical instruments are potentiometer, pH meter, conductivity meter, etc.
  - Thermal analysis: The measurement of physical or chemical changes in a sample as a function of temperature or time. Examples of thermal analysis instruments are differential scanning calorimeter, thermogravimetric analyzer, etc.
  - Microscopy: The use of a microscope to observe objects that are too small to be seen by the naked eye. Examples of microscopy instruments are optical microscope, scanning electron microscope, atomic force microscope, etc.
- Analytical instruments are used for various purposes, such as:
  - Quality control: To ensure that the products or processes meet the specifications or standards.
  - Research and development: To explore new phenomena, discover new materials, or improve existing methods or technologies.
  - Diagnosis and treatment: To detect and identify diseases, disorders, or abnormalities, or to provide therapeutic interventions.
  - Environmental monitoring: To assess the impact of human activities or natural events on the environment or the health of living organisms.
  - Forensic analysis: To provide evidence or information for legal or criminal investigations.
