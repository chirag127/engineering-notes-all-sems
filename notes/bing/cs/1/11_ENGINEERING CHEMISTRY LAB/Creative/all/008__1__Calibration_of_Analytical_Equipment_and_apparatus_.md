### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and apparatus.

- Calibration is the process of comparing a reading on one piece of equipment or system, with another piece of equipment that has been calibrated and referenced to a known set of parameters .
- The purpose of calibration is to evaluate and adjust the precision and accuracy of measurement equipment, and to ensure that the equipment is traceable to a recognized standard  .
- Calibration is important for maintaining the quality and reliability of analytical results, as well as for complying with regulatory requirements and good laboratory practices .
- Calibration of analytical equipment and apparatus involves the following steps :
  - Selecting a suitable reference standard or instrument that has a higher accuracy and stability than the equipment to be calibrated.
  - Establishing a calibration procedure that specifies the frequency, method, conditions, and acceptance criteria for calibration.
  - Performing the calibration by applying a known input to the equipment and recording the output response.
  - Comparing the output response with the expected value from the reference standard or instrument, and calculating the error or deviation.
  - Adjusting the equipment if the error or deviation exceeds the acceptance criteria, or documenting the error or deviation if adjustment is not possible or required.
  - Issuing a calibration certificate or report that records the details and results of the calibration, and the traceability of the reference standard or instrument.
- Some examples of analytical equipment and apparatus that require calibration are balances, pipettes, thermometers, pH meters, spectrophotometers, chromatographs, and titrators  .
