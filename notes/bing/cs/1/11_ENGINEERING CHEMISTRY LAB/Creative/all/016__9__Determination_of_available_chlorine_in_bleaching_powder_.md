### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

- Bleaching powder is a compound of calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)2) and calcium chloride (CaCl2) that is used as a disinfectant and a bleaching agent.
- The available chlorine in bleaching powder is the amount of chlorine that can be liberated from the compound when it reacts with an acid or a reducing agent.
- The available chlorine can be determined by the iodometric method, which involves the following steps:

  1. Weigh about 0.5 to 1 g of bleaching powder and dissolve it in 1 litre of distilled water by grinding. This is the stock solution of bleaching powder.
  2. Pipette 25 ml of the stock solution into a conical flask and add 5 to 7 ml of potassium iodide (KI) solution and 20 ml of dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl). This will liberate iodine (I2) from the KI according to the reaction:

     Ca(OCl)2 + 2KI + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + 2KCl + I2 + H2O

  3. Titrate the liberated iodine with a standard sodium thiosulphate (Na2S2O3) solution using starch as an indicator. The starch will turn blue in the presence of iodine and colourless when all the iodine is consumed by the Na2S2O3 according to the reaction:

     I2 + 2Na2S2O3 -> 2NaI + Na2S4O6

  4. Note the volume of Na2S2O3 solution used in the titration and calculate the available chlorine in the bleaching powder using the formula:

     Available chlorine (%) = (V x N x 35.5 x 100) / (W x 1000)

     where V is the volume of Na2S2O3 solution in ml, N is the normality of Na2S2O3 solution, W is the weight of bleaching powder in g, and 35.5 is the equivalent weight of chlorine.

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for available chlorine is:

  **V**ery **N**ice **C**hlorine **W**orks **W**ell

  V x N x C / W x W

  where C is 35.5, the equivalent weight of chlorine.
