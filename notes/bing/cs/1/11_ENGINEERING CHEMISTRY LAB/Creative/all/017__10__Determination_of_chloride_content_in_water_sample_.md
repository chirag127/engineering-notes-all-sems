### 10. Determination of chloride content in water sample.

- Chloride ions are one of the major inorganic anions in water and wastewater. They can cause corrosion, salinity, and affect the taste and odour of water. Therefore, it is important to determine the chloride content in water samples for quality control and monitoring purposes.
- One of the common methods for determining the chloride content in water samples is the **Mohr method**, which is based on the precipitation and titration of chloride ions with silver nitrate solution using potassium chromate as an indicator.
- The principle of the Mohr method is that when water containing chloride ions is titrated with silver nitrate solution, chloride ions are precipitated as white silver chloride according to the following reaction:

  $$\text{Cl}^- + \text{Ag}^+ \rightarrow \text{AgCl} \downarrow$$

- Potassium chromate is used as an indicator, which supplies chromate ions that react with excess silver ions to form a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate, indicating the end point of the titration:

  $$\text{CrO}_4^{2-} + 2\text{Ag}^+ \rightarrow \text{Ag}_2\text{CrO}_4 \downarrow$$

- The amount of chloride ions in the water sample can be calculated from the volume and concentration of the silver nitrate solution used in the titration.

- The procedure for the Mohr method is as follows:

  1. Take 50 mL of the water sample (V mL) and dilute to 100 mL with distilled water in a volumetric flask.
  2. If the sample is highly coloured, add 3 mL of aluminium hydroxide and shake well, allow to settle, filter, wash and collect the filtrate.
  3. Bring the sample pH to 7-8 by adding acid or alkali if necessary. This is to prevent the precipitation of other anions such as carbonate, phosphate, or sulphide by silver ions.
  4. Add 1 mL of potassium chromate indicator to the sample solution in a conical flask.
  5. Titrate the solution against standard silver nitrate solution (0.01 M or 0.02 M) from a burette until a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate is obtained. Record the volume of silver nitrate solution used (V1 mL).
  6. Repeat the titration with another aliquot of the sample solution and record the average volume of silver nitrate solution used (V2 mL).

- The calculation of the chloride content in the water sample is as follows:

  - The concentration of chloride ions in the sample solution (C) can be calculated from the following formula:

    $$C = \frac{V_1 \times N}{V}$$

    where V1 is the volume of silver nitrate solution used in mL, N is the normality of silver nitrate solution in eq/L, and V is the volume of the sample solution taken for titration in mL.

  - The concentration of chloride ions in the original water sample (C1) can be calculated from the following formula:

    $$C_1 = C \times \frac{100}{V}$$

    where C is the concentration of chloride ions in the sample solution in mg/L, and V is the volume of the water sample taken for dilution in mL.

  - The chloride content in the original water sample (W) can be calculated from the following formula:

    $$W = C_1 \times V$$

    where C1 is the concentration of chloride ions in the original water sample in mg/L, and V is the volume of the original water sample in L.

- Some of the advantages of the Mohr method are:

  - It is simple, rapid, and accurate.
  - It does not require expensive or sophisticated equipment or reagents.
  - It can be used for a wide range of chloride concentrations in water samples.

  - It is not suitable for samples that contain interfering substances such as bromide, iodide, cyanide, thiocyanate, or nitrite, which also react with silver ions and cause errors in the titration.
