### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness and alkalinity are two important parameters of water quality that indicate the concentration of dissolved minerals and bases in water, respectively.
- Hardness is mainly caused by the presence of calcium and magnesium ions, which form insoluble precipitates with soap and interfere with the action of detergents. Hardness can also cause scaling and corrosion in pipes and boilers.
- Alkalinity is the measure of the water's ability to neutralize acids, which is important for maintaining a stable pH and preventing acid rain damage. Alkalinity is mainly contributed by bicarbonate, carbonate and hydroxide ions, which are derived from the dissolution of carbon dioxide and geological minerals in water.
- The units of hardness and alkalinity are usually expressed as milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) equivalent, which is the common reference compound for both parameters.
- The methods of measuring hardness and alkalinity are based on titration with standard solutions of acids or bases, using indicators or pH meters to detect the end points. The end points depend on the type and concentration of the ions present in the water sample.
- The common methods of measuring hardness are:
  - EDTA titration: A complexometric titration using ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) as the titrant and Eriochrome Black T as the indicator. The end point is marked by a color change from wine red to blue. This method measures the total hardness, which is the sum of calcium and magnesium hardness.
  - Soap titration: A direct titration using a standard soap solution as the titrant and observing the formation of a stable lather as the end point. This method measures the calcium hardness only, as magnesium does not react with soap.
- The common methods of measuring alkalinity are:
  - Phenolphthalein titration: A simple acid-base titration using a standard acid solution (usually sulfuric acid) as the titrant and phenolphthalein as the indicator. The end point is marked by a color change from pink to colorless. This method measures the phenolphthalein alkalinity, which is the sum of hydroxide and half of carbonate alkalinity.
  - Methyl orange titration: A simple acid-base titration using a standard acid solution (usually sulfuric acid) as the titrant and methyl orange as the indicator. The end point is marked by a color change from yellow to orange. This method measures the methyl orange alkalinity, which is the sum of hydroxide, carbonate and bicarbonate alkalinity.
- The total alkalinity can be calculated by adding the phenolphthalein and methyl orange alkalinity, or by subtracting the phenolphthalein alkalinity from twice the methyl orange alkalinity. The individual contributions of hydroxide, carbonate and bicarbonate alkalinity can be determined by using the pH and the titration data.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the methods of measuring hardness and alkalinity is: **E**DTA and **S**oap for **H**ardness, **P**henolphthalein and **M**ethyl orange for **A**lkalinity.
