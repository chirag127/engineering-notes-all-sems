### 2. To enable the students to understand about the analysis of chloride content, hardness, alkalinity of water.

- Chloride content is a measure of the amount of chloride ions (Cl-) in water. Chloride ions are derived from sources such as salt, rocks, soil, and human activities. Chloride content can affect the taste, corrosion, and biological activity of water.
- Hardness is a measure of the amount of calcium (Ca2+) and magnesium (Mg2+) ions in water. Hardness can affect the formation of scale, soap scum, and foam in water. Hardness can also affect the efficiency of water heaters, boilers, and pipes.
- Alkalinity is a measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids. Alkalinity is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonate (HCO3-), carbonate (CO32-), and hydroxide (OH-) ions in water. Alkalinity can affect the pH, buffering capacity, and biological activity of water.

- The analysis of chloride content, hardness, and alkalinity of water can be done by various methods, such as titration, conductivity, pH, and colorimetry. Some of the common methods are:

  - Mohr method: This is a titration method for determining chloride content in water. It involves adding a standard solution of silver nitrate (AgNO3) to the water sample until a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate (Ag2CrO4) forms. The end point is indicated by a color change from yellow to reddish-brown. The amount of silver nitrate used is proportional to the chloride content in the water sample. This method is suitable for neutral or slightly alkaline water with a pH range of 6.5-9.0.

  - EDTA method: This is a titration method for determining hardness in water. It involves adding a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) to the water sample until all the calcium and magnesium ions are complexed by the EDTA. The end point is indicated by a color change from red to blue using a metal indicator such as Eriochrome Black T. The amount of EDTA used is proportional to the hardness in the water sample. This method can determine the total hardness, as well as the individual concentrations of calcium and magnesium, by adjusting the pH of the water sample. This method is suitable for water with a pH range of 7-10.

  - Phenolphthalein method: This is a titration method for determining alkalinity in water. It involves adding a standard solution of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) to the water sample until the pink color of phenolphthalein indicator disappears. The end point is indicated by a color change from pink to colorless. The amount of sulfuric acid used is proportional to the phenolphthalein alkalinity in the water sample, which is mainly due to the presence of hydroxide and carbonate ions. This method is suitable for water with a pH range of 8.3-10.

  - Conductivity method: This is a non-titration method for determining chloride content, hardness, and alkalinity in water. It involves measuring the electrical conductivity of the water sample using a conductivity meter. The conductivity of water is influenced by the presence of dissolved ions, such as chloride, calcium, magnesium, bicarbonate, and carbonate. The higher the concentration of these ions, the higher the conductivity of water. The conductivity method can provide a quick and easy estimation of the water quality parameters, but it is less accurate and precise than the titration methods. This method is suitable for water with a wide range of pH and salinity.

  - pH method: This is a non-titration method for determining alkalinity in water. It involves measuring the pH of the water sample using a pH meter or a pH indicator. The pH of water is influenced by the presence of acidic and basic ions, such as hydrogen, hydroxide, bicarbonate, and carbonate. The higher the concentration of these ions, the higher the alkalinity of water. The pH method can provide a quick and easy estimation of the alkalinity of water, but it is less accurate and precise than the titration methods. This method is suitable for water with a wide range of pH and salinity.
