### Course Outcomes Bloom’s

- Course outcomes are statements that describe what students should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course.
- Bloom’s taxonomy is a framework for classifying educational objectives into six levels of cognitive complexity: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Course outcomes can be written using Bloom’s verbs to indicate the level of cognitive skill required for each outcome.
- For example, a course outcome for a mathematics course could be: “Apply the rules of differentiation and integration to solve problems involving functions of one variable.”
- The verb “apply” indicates that the outcome is at the third level of Bloom’s taxonomy, which requires students to use learned concepts or procedures in new situations.
- Writing course outcomes using Bloom’s verbs can help instructors design appropriate assessments and activities that align with the intended learning outcomes of the course.
- It can also help students understand the expectations and goals of the course, and monitor their own progress and learning.
- Some examples of Bloom’s verbs for each level of the taxonomy are:

  - Remember: define, list, recall, identify, recognize, etc.
  - Understand: explain, describe, summarize, illustrate, interpret, etc.
  - Apply: use, demonstrate, solve, calculate, perform, etc.
  - Analyze: compare, contrast, classify, examine, differentiate, etc.
  - Evaluate: judge, critique, assess, justify, argue, etc.
  - Create: design, construct, produce, compose, synthesize, etc.

- A mnemonic device to remember the six levels of Bloom’s taxonomy is: RUA2EC (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create).
