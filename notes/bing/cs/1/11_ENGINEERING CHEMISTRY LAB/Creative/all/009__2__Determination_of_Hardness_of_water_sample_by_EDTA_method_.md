### 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method.

- Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. Hard water can cause scaling, corrosion, and reduced efficiency of boilers, heaters, and pipes.
- EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions, such as Ca2+ and Mg2+, in water. EDTA can be used to titrate the hardness of water by reacting with the metal ions and changing the color of an indicator.
- The indicator used for EDTA titration is Eriochrome Black T, which is a metallochromic dye that can change color depending on the metal ion present. Eriochrome Black T forms a wine-red complex with Ca2+ and Mg2+ at pH 10, but turns blue when all the metal ions are complexed by EDTA.
- The procedure for determination of hardness of water by EDTA method is as follows   :
  - Take a sample volume of 20 ml (V ml) of water in a conical flask.
  - Dilute the sample to 40 ml by adding 20 ml of distilled water.
  - Add 1 ml of ammonia buffer to adjust the pH to 10 ± 0.1. The buffer consists of a mixture of ammonium chloride and ammonia solution.
  - Add 1 or 2 drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator solution. The solution should turn wine-red due to the presence of Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions.
  - Titrate the sample with a standard solution of EDTA of known concentration (C mg/ml) from a burette. Swirl the flask constantly and observe the color change.
  - Stop the titration when the color changes from wine-red to blue. This is the end point of the titration, indicating that all the metal ions have been complexed by EDTA.
  - Record the volume of EDTA solution used (B ml).
- The calculation of hardness of water by EDTA method is based on the following equation  :
  - C * B = V * H
  - Where C is the concentration of EDTA solution in mg/ml, B is the volume of EDTA solution used in ml, V is the volume of water sample taken in ml, and H is the hardness of water in mg/ml as CaCO3.
  - The hardness of water can be expressed in different units, such as ppm, grains per gallon, or degrees. To convert from mg/ml to ppm, multiply by 1000. To convert from mg/ml to grains per gallon, multiply by 17.12. To convert from mg/ml to degrees, multiply by 5.6 for German degrees, 7.0 for French degrees, or 10 for English degrees.
- A mnemonic to remember the color change of Eriochrome Black T indicator is **R**ed **B**efore **B**lue. Another mnemonic to remember the equation for hardness calculation is **C**alculating **B**y **V**ery **H**ard method. These mnemonics are easy to remember because they use the first letters of the words and rhyme.
