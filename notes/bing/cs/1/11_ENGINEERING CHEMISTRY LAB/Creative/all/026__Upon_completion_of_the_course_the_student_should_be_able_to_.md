### Upon completion of the course the student should be able to:

- Define the basic concepts and terminology of computer science, such as algorithms, data structures, abstraction, complexity, and programming languages.
- Apply the principles of algorithm design and analysis to solve various computational problems, such as sorting, searching, encryption, compression, and graph algorithms.
- Implement and test algorithms using a high-level programming language, such as Python, Java, or C++.
- Compare and contrast different data structures, such as arrays, lists, stacks, queues, trees, graphs, and hash tables, and choose the appropriate ones for different applications.
- Evaluate the trade-offs between time and space complexity of different algorithms and data structures, and use asymptotic notation to express the worst-case and average-case performance of algorithms.
- Demonstrate the use of abstraction and modularity to design and implement large-scale software systems, such as object-oriented programming, classes, inheritance, polymorphism, and interfaces.
- Explain the basic concepts and mechanisms of operating systems, such as processes, threads, synchronization, memory management, file systems, and I/O devices.
- Understand the principles and techniques of computer networks, such as the Internet, TCP/IP, routing, switching, DNS, HTTP, and network security.
- Appreciate the ethical, social, and professional issues and responsibilities of computer scientists in the context of computing and society.
