### 7. Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator.

- Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a light green crystalline salt with the formula (NH4)2Fe(SO4)2.6H2O. It contains two different cations, Fe2+ and NH4+, and is used as a reducing agent in redox titrations.
- Potassium dichromate, K2Cr2O7, is a bright orange compound that acts as an oxidizing agent in redox titrations. It can oxidize Fe2+ to Fe3+ in acidic medium, according to the following equation:

  K2Cr2O7 + 6Fe2+ + 14H+ -> 2Cr3+ + 6Fe3+ + 7H2O

- Potassium ferricyanide, K3[Fe(CN)6], is a red compound that is used as an external indicator in redox titrations involving Fe2+ and K2Cr2O7. It forms a blue precipitate of Prussian blue with Fe2+, but not with Fe3+, according to the following equation:

  K3[Fe(CN)6] + 3Fe2+ -> 3K+ + Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3

- The procedure for determining the strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator is as follows:

  - Prepare a standard solution of K2Cr2O7 of known concentration, such as 0.1 N, by dissolving a weighed amount of the salt in distilled water and making up the volume in a volumetric flask.
  - Pipette out a known volume of the standard K2Cr2O7 solution, such as 10 mL, into a conical flask and add some dilute sulfuric acid to make the solution acidic.
  - Fill a burette with the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution of unknown concentration and note the initial reading.
  - Add the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution from the burette to the conical flask containing the K2Cr2O7 solution, while stirring the mixture with a glass rod.
  - When the color of the solution changes from orange to green, stop the titration and note the final reading of the burette. This is the end point of the titration, as all the Fe2+ has been oxidized to Fe3+ by K2Cr2O7.
  - Repeat the titration with fresh aliquots of the K2Cr2O7 solution until consistent readings are obtained.
  - To confirm the end point, take a drop of the solution from the conical flask and place it on a spot plate. Add a drop of potassium ferricyanide solution to the same spot. If a blue color appears, it means that some Fe2+ is still present and the titration is incomplete. If no color change occurs, it means that all the Fe2+ has been oxidized and the titration is complete.
  - Calculate the average volume of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution used in the titration and use the following formula to find its strength:

    Strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution = (Volume of K2Cr2O7 solution x Normality of K2Cr2O7 solution x Equivalent weight of ferrous ammonium sulfate) / (Volume of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution)

- A possible mnemonic to remember the formula is:

  Strength = (V K x N K x E F) / (V F)

  where V stands for volume, N stands for normality, E stands for equivalent weight, K stands for potassium dichromate, and F stands for ferrous ammonium sulfate.
