### 3. To enable the students to understand about the measure of pH, surface tension and viscosity of a liquid.

- pH is a measure of how acidic or basic a solution is. It is defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration in moles per liter. The pH scale ranges from 0 to 14, where 7 is neutral, lower than 7 is acidic, and higher than 7 is basic. The pH of a solution can be measured using indicators, such as litmus paper, or pH meters, which are electronic devices that measure the voltage difference between two electrodes immersed in the solution.
- Surface tension is a measure of how strongly the molecules of a liquid are attracted to each other at the surface. It is the force per unit length that must be applied to the surface to break it. Surface tension causes liquids to form spherical droplets, minimize their surface area, and resist external forces. The surface tension of a liquid depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature, and the presence of surfactants, which are substances that reduce the surface tension. Surface tension can be measured using methods such as the drop weight method, the ring method, or the pendant drop method.
- Viscosity is a measure of how resistant a liquid is to flow. It is the ratio of the shear stress to the shear rate in a fluid. The shear stress is the force per unit area that is applied parallel to the surface of the fluid, and the shear rate is the rate of change of the fluid's velocity in the direction perpendicular to the surface. The viscosity of a liquid depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature, pressure, and the shape and size of the molecules. Viscosity can be measured using methods such as the capillary tube method, the rotating cylinder method, or the falling ball method.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the pH scale, one can use the phrase "Please Have No Acid Base Attacks", where the first letter of each word corresponds to the pH value from 0 to 7.
- To remember the factors that affect the surface tension, one can use the acronym FITS, where F stands for forces, I stands for intermolecular, T stands for temperature, and S stands for surfactants.
- To remember the factors that affect the viscosity, one can use the acronym TIPS, where T stands for temperature, I stands for intermolecular, P stands for pressure, and S stands for shape and size.
