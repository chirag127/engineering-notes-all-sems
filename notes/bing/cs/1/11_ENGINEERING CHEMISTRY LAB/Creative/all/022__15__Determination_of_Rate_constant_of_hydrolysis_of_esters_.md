### 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters.

- Hydrolysis of esters is the reverse reaction of esterification, in which an ester reacts with water to produce a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.
- Hydrolysis of esters can be catalyzed by either an acid or a base. In this experiment, we will focus on the acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of an ester, which follows the pseudo-first order kinetics.
- Pseudo-first order kinetics means that the rate of the reaction depends on the concentration of one reactant, while the other reactants are present in large excess and their concentrations are constant.
- The general equation for the acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of an ester is:

  `R-CO-OR' + H2O --H+--> R-COOH + R'-OH`

  where R and R' are alkyl groups.

- The rate law for this reaction is:

  `Rate = k[ester][H2O][H+]`

  where k is the rate constant, and [ester], [H2O], and [H+] are the concentrations of the ester, water, and acid, respectively.

- Since water and acid are present in large excess, their concentrations are constant and can be combined with the rate constant to give a new constant, k':

  `Rate = k'[ester]`

  where k' = k[H2O][H+]

- The rate constant k' can be determined experimentally by measuring the concentration of the ester at different times and plotting the natural logarithm of the concentration versus time. The slope of the plot is equal to -k'.
- A common ester used for this experiment is methyl acetate (CH3-CO-OCH3), which hydrolyzes to acetic acid (CH3-COOH) and methanol (CH3-OH) in the presence of hydrochloric acid (HCl) as the catalyst.
- The equation for this reaction is:

  `CH3-CO-OCH3 + H2O --HCl--> CH3-COOH + CH3-OH`

- The procedure for this experiment is as follows:

  1. Prepare a solution of 0.04 M methyl acetate and 0.1 M HCl in a conical flask.
  2. Place the flask in a water bath at a constant temperature (e.g. 25°C).
  3. Start a stopwatch and take a small sample of the solution at regular intervals (e.g. every 5 minutes) using a pipette.
  4. Transfer the sample to a titration flask and titrate it with a standard solution of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) using phenolphthalein as the indicator.
  5. Record the volume of NaOH required to reach the end point of the titration.
  6. Repeat steps 3 to 5 until the reaction is complete (e.g. after 30 minutes).
  7. Calculate the concentration of methyl acetate at each time interval using the following formula:

     `[CH3-CO-OCH3] = ([HCl] - [NaOH]) / 2`

     where [HCl] is the initial concentration of HCl, and [NaOH] is the concentration of NaOH used in the titration.

  8. Plot the natural logarithm of the concentration of methyl acetate versus time and find the slope of the plot, which is equal to -k'.
  9. Report the value of k' with the appropriate units and significant figures.
