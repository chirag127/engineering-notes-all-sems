There are many possible ways to draw a diagram for a list of experiments, depending on the type and purpose of the experiments. One possible way is to use a table to organize the experiments by their name, category, description, and outcome. For example:

## List of Experiments

| Name | Category | Description | Outcome |
|------|----------|-------------|---------|
| Harry Harlow's experiments with baby monkeys and wire and cloth surrogate mothers | Psychology | Studied the effects of maternal deprivation and attachment on infant monkeys | Showed that monkeys preferred the cloth mother over the wire mother, even if the wire mother had food, and that maternal deprivation had long-term negative consequences on the monkeys' social and emotional development |
| Stanley Milgram's experiments on human obedience | Psychology | Tested how far people would go in obeying an authority figure who instructed them to administer electric shocks to a learner | Found that most people (65%) would obey the authority figure and administer the highest level of shock (450 volts), even if they heard the learner scream and protest |
| Walter Mischel's marshmallow experiment | Psychology | Measured the ability of children to delay gratification by offering them a choice between one marshmallow now or two marshmallows later | Found that children who waited longer for the two marshmallows tended to have better life outcomes, such as higher academic achievement, lower obesity, and better coping skills |
| KSTAR nuclear fusion experiment | Physics | Achieved a sustained nuclear fusion reaction at a temperature of 100 million degrees Celsius for 30 seconds | Demonstrated the feasibility of nuclear fusion as a source of clean and unlimited energy, and set a new world record for fusion plasma operation |
| Science Buddies experiments | Science education | Provided hundreds of fun and easy science experiments for kids to do at home or school | Encouraged curiosity and exploration of various scientific topics, such as kitchen chemistry, DIY mini drones, and slime making |
