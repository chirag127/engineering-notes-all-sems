A polarimeter is a scientific instrument used to measure the angle of rotation caused by passing polarized light through an optically active substance, such as cane sugar solution. The basic components of a polarimeter are a light source, a polarizer, a sample tube, an analyzer and a detector. The following diagram illustrates the basic architecture of a polarimeter using ASCII characters:

```
    Light source
        |
        |
        v
    +---------+   Unpolarized light
    |Polarizer|------------------------+
    +---------+                        |
                                      |
                                      v
    +---------+   Linearly polarized light
    |Sample   |------------------------+
    |tube     |                        |
    +---------+                        |
                                      |
                                      v
    +---------+   Optically rotated light
    |Analyzer |------------------------+
    +---------+                        |
                                      |
                                      v
    +---------+   Detector
    |Detector |<-----------------------+
    +---------+
```

The light source emits unpolarized light, which is then transmitted through a fixed polarizer that has thin slits in it. Only a certain orientation of light can pass through the slits and enter the sample tube. As light is passed through the sample tube containing the cane sugar solution, it is rotated at a unique angle depending on the concentration and optical activity of the solution. As the user rotates the analyzer, which is another polarizer, the light passing through the sample tube has a maximum intensity at a specific angle. This angle is called the angle of rotation and can be measured by the detector, which is usually a photometer or a human eye. The specific rotation of the cane sugar solution can be calculated using the formula:

```
[α] = α / (l * c)
```

where [α] is the specific rotation in degrees, α is the observed angle of rotation in degrees, l is the length of the sample tube in decimeters, and c is the concentration of the solution in grams per milliliter.
