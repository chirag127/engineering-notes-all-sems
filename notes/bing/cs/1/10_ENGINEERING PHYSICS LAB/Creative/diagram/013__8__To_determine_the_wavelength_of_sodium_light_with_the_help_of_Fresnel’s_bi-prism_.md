The Fresnel's bi-prism experiment is a method to determine the wavelength of sodium light by using the interference of two coherent sources. The bi-prism consists of two thin prisms joined at their bases to form an isosceles triangle. A single slit is placed in front of a sodium lamp, and the light passes through a convex lens to focus on the bi-prism. The light is then refracted by the two prisms, creating two virtual sources that act as a double slit. The interference pattern is observed on a screen placed at some distance from the bi-prism. The wavelength of sodium light can be calculated by using the formula:

lambda = (D * d) / (b * m)

where lambda is the wavelength, D is the distance between the screen and the bi-prism, d is the distance between the two virtual sources, b is the distance between the slit and the bi-prism, and m is the fringe order.
