##### determine its hysteresis loss.

Hysteresis loss is the energy dissipated as heat in a magnetic material due to the reversal of its magnetization when subjected to an alternating magnetic field. It is also known as eddy current loss or magnetic loss.

To determine the hysteresis loss of a magnetic material, the following steps can be followed:

- Measure the area of the hysteresis loop of the material on a B-H curve, where B is the magnetic flux density and H is the magnetic field intensity. The area of the loop represents the energy loss per cycle of magnetization.
- Multiply the area of the loop by the frequency of the alternating magnetic field, which is the number of cycles per second. This gives the energy loss per second or the power loss due to hysteresis.
- Divide the power loss by the volume of the material to get the hysteresis loss per unit volume.

Alternatively, the hysteresis loss can be calculated using the following formula:

- P = ηB<sub>m</sub><sup>n</sup>fV
- Where P is the power loss, η is the hysteresis coefficient, B<sub>m</sub> is the maximum value of the magnetic flux density, n is the Steinmetz exponent, f is the frequency of the alternating magnetic field, and V is the volume of the material.

The hysteresis coefficient and the Steinmetz exponent are constants that depend on the type and quality of the magnetic material. They can be determined experimentally or obtained from tables.
