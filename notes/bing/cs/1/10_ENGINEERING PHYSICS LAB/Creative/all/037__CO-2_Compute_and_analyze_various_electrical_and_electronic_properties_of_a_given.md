#### CO-2 Compute and analyze various electrical and electronic properties of a given

- Electrical properties are the characteristics of a material that describe how it responds to an electric field or a current, such as resistance, capacitance, inductance, etc.   
- Electronic properties are the characteristics of a material that describe how its electrons behave under an electric field or a current, such as band gap, carrier concentration, mobility, etc.    
- To compute and analyze various electrical and electronic properties of a given material, device, or circuit, one needs to use appropriate mathematical models, equations, and tools, such as Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws, Thevenin's theorem, Norton's theorem, etc. for electrical properties, and band theory, Fermi-Dirac statistics, drift-diffusion equation, etc. for electronic properties. 
- Some examples of electrical and electronic properties and their applications are:

  - Resistance: the measure of how much a material or a device opposes the flow of electric current. Resistance is important for controlling the amount of current and voltage in a circuit, and for designing resistors, potentiometers, thermistors, etc. 
  - Capacitance: the measure of how much electric charge a material or a device can store when subjected to an electric potential difference. Capacitance is important for storing and releasing electric energy, and for designing capacitors, filters, oscillators, etc. 
  - Inductance: the measure of how much a material or a device can generate an electromotive force when the current through it changes. Inductance is important for creating magnetic fields, and for designing inductors, transformers, solenoids, etc. 
  - Band gap: the measure of how much energy is required to excite an electron from the valence band to the conduction band in a material. Band gap is important for determining the electrical conductivity, optical absorption, and emission of a material, and for designing semiconductors, solar cells, LEDs, lasers, etc. 
  - Carrier concentration: the measure of how many free electrons or holes are available for conduction in a material. Carrier concentration is important for determining the electrical conductivity, carrier mobility, and doping level of a material, and for designing semiconductors, transistors, diodes, etc. 
  - Mobility: the measure of how fast an electron or a hole can move under an electric field in a material. Mobility is important for determining the electrical conductivity, drift velocity, and diffusion coefficient of a material, and for designing semiconductors, sensors, detectors, etc.
