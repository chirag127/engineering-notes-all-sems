##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

- The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or liberated at an electrode by one coulomb of electric charge during electrolysis.
- The ECE of copper can be determined by using Faraday's first law of electrolysis, which states that the mass of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to the charge passed through the electrolyte.
- The ECE of copper can be calculated by the formula:

  ECE of copper = mass of copper deposited / charge passed

- To determine the ECE of copper experimentally, the following steps can be followed:

  - Set up an electrolytic cell with a copper sulfate solution as the electrolyte and two copper electrodes as the anode and the cathode.
  - Connect the electrodes to a direct current source and a variable resistor to control the current.
  - Connect an ammeter in series and a voltmeter in parallel to measure the current and the voltage respectively.
  - Clean and dry the cathode and weigh it accurately using a balance.
  - Switch on the current and allow the electrolysis to proceed for a fixed time, say 10 minutes.
  - Switch off the current and disconnect the electrodes.
  - Wash and dry the cathode and weigh it again using the same balance.
  - Calculate the mass of copper deposited on the cathode by subtracting the initial mass from the final mass.
  - Calculate the charge passed through the electrolyte by multiplying the current by the time.
  - Calculate the ECE of copper by dividing the mass of copper deposited by the charge passed.

- The ECE of copper can also be obtained from the electrochemical equivalent table, which gives the ECE values of some elements in kg/C or g/C. The ECE of copper is 3.295×10^(-7) kg/C or 0.3295 mg/C according to the table.

  **C**opper **E**CE is **C**ool and **E**asy: 3.295×10^(-7) kg/C

- The ECE of copper is useful for applications such as electroplating, electrowinning, and electrorefining, where copper is deposited or extracted from a solution using electric current.
