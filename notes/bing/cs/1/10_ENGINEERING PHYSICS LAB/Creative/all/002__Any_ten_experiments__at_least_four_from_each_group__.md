### Any ten experiments (at least four from each group)

Here are some possible experiments that can be performed in a physics laboratory, grouped by topics. Note that this is not an exhaustive list and there may be other experiments that are suitable for your syllabus and level of study.

#### Mechanics

- **Measuring the acceleration due to gravity using a simple pendulum.** This experiment involves measuring the time period of a pendulum of different lengths and plotting a graph of T^2^ vs L, where T is the time period and L is the length. The slope of the graph gives the value of g/4π^2^, from which g can be calculated. A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for the time period of a simple pendulum is **TIPPLE**: T = 2π √(L/g).
- **Verifying Hooke's law and finding the spring constant of a spring.** This experiment involves measuring the extension of a spring when different weights are attached to it and plotting a graph of F vs x, where F is the force and x is the extension. The slope of the graph gives the value of the spring constant k, and the intercept gives the initial tension in the spring. A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for Hooke's law is **FLEX**: F = kx.
- **Studying the conservation of momentum and energy in a collision.** This experiment involves using an air track or a smooth surface to make two carts or trolleys collide with each other and measuring their velocities before and after the collision. Depending on the type of collision (elastic or inelastic), the total momentum and kinetic energy of the system are conserved or not. A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for momentum is **MUV**: p = mv, where p is the momentum, m is the mass and v is the velocity.
- **Determining the coefficient of friction between two surfaces.** This experiment involves placing a block of wood or a similar object on an inclined plane and measuring the angle of inclination at which the block starts to slide down. The coefficient of friction can be calculated from the formula tan θ = μ, where θ is the angle of inclination and μ is the coefficient of friction. A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for friction is **FRICTION**: F = μN, where F is the frictional force and N is the normal force.

#### Electricity and Magnetism

- **Measuring the resistance of a wire using a meter bridge.** This experiment involves using a meter bridge, which is a device that consists of a wire of uniform cross-section and a sliding contact that can move along the wire. By connecting a battery, a galvanometer and a known resistance in a circuit, the sliding contact can be adjusted until the galvanometer shows zero deflection, indicating a balanced condition. The resistance of the wire can be calculated from the formula R = (l/L)R_0_, where R is the resistance of the wire, l is the length of the wire from one end to the sliding contact, L is the total length of the wire and R_0_ is the known resistance.
- **Studying the magnetic field of a bar magnet using a compass.** This experiment involves placing a bar magnet on a sheet of paper and tracing its outline. Then, a compass is placed near one end of the magnet and its needle is aligned with the magnetic field of the magnet. The direction of the needle is marked on the paper and the compass is moved along the field line until it reaches the other end of the magnet. The process is repeated for different points around the magnet until a complete map of the magnetic field is obtained. The magnetic field lines show the direction and strength of the magnetic field of the magnet.
- **Verifying Ohm's law and finding the resistance of a resistor.** This experiment involves connecting a battery, a resistor, an ammeter and a voltmeter in a circuit and measuring the current and the potential difference across the resistor for different values of the battery voltage. A graph of V vs I is plotted, where V is the potential difference and I is the current. The slope of the graph gives the value of the resistance R, and the intercept gives the internal resistance of the battery. A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for Ohm's law is **VIR**: V = IR, where V is the potential difference, I is the current and R is the resistance.
