##### 2. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a

- Hall effect is the phenomenon of generation of a transverse electric field (Hall field) in a current-carrying conductor when it is placed in a magnetic field perpendicular to the current direction .
- Hall effect can be used to determine the type, density and mobility of charge carriers in a material, especially semiconductors   .
- Hall coefficient is a measure of the strength of the Hall field induced by a given magnetic field and current density. It is defined as R_H = E_H / (j_x B_z), where E_H is the Hall field, j_x is the current density, and B_z is the magnetic field .
- Hall coefficient is related to the carrier density and mobility by the following equations :
  - For electrons (n-type): R_H = 1 / (n e) = \mu / \sigma, where n is the electron density, e is the electron charge, \mu is the electron mobility, and \sigma is the electrical conductivity.
  - For holes (p-type): R_H = -1 / (p e) = -\mu / \sigma, where p is the hole density, e is the hole charge, \mu is the hole mobility, and \sigma is the electrical conductivity.
- The sign of the Hall coefficient indicates the type of charge carriers in the material. A positive sign means that the majority carriers are holes (p-type), and a negative sign means that the majority carriers are electrons (n-type)  .
- The magnitude of the Hall coefficient indicates the density of charge carriers in the material. A large magnitude means that the carrier density is low, and a small magnitude means that the carrier density is high .
- The mobility of charge carriers is a measure of how easily they can move in the material under the influence of an electric field. It is defined as \mu = v_d / E, where v_d is the drift velocity of the carriers, and E is the electric field .
- The mobility of charge carriers is affected by the scattering mechanisms in the material, such as impurities, defects, phonons, etc. The mobility decreases with increasing temperature, as the scattering rate increases .
- To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a material, the following steps are required  :
  - Prepare a thin rectangular sample of the material with four electrical contacts at the corners (van der Pauw configuration).
  - Connect a constant current source to two opposite contacts, and measure the voltage drop across the other two contacts. This gives the resistivity of the material.
  - Apply a magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the sample, and measure the Hall voltage across the same two contacts as before. This gives the Hall coefficient of the material.
  - Repeat the measurements for different values of current and magnetic field, and plot the Hall voltage versus the magnetic field. The slope of the plot gives the Hall coefficient of the material.
  - Calculate the carrier density and mobility of the material using the equations given above, and the resistivity and Hall coefficient values obtained from the measurements.
