#### CO-1 Apply the principle of interference and diffraction to find the wavelength of

- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves, resulting in a new wave pattern that has regions of constructive and destructive interference .
- Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of waves around obstacles or apertures, resulting in the formation of secondary waves that interfere with each other .
- The principle of superposition states that when two or more waves overlap in space, the resultant displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of the displacements of the individual waves at that point .
- To find the wavelength of a wave using interference and diffraction, we can use the following methods:
  - Double-slit interference: When a monochromatic light source passes through two narrow slits that are close together, it produces an interference pattern on a screen. The distance between the bright fringes (maxima) or the dark fringes (minima) depends on the wavelength of the light, the distance between the slits, and the distance from the slits to the screen. The formula for the fringe spacing is:

    `y = (lambda * D) / d`

    where y is the fringe spacing, lambda is the wavelength, D is the distance from the slits to the screen, and d is the distance between the slits. By measuring y, D, and d, we can find lambda.

  - Single-slit diffraction: When a monochromatic light source passes through a single narrow slit, it produces a diffraction pattern on a screen. The pattern consists of a bright central maximum and a series of dark and bright fringes on either side. The angular position of the first dark fringe (minima) depends on the wavelength of the light and the width of the slit. The formula for the angular position is:

    `sin(theta) = lambda / a`

    where theta is the angle of the first dark fringe, lambda is the wavelength, and a is the width of the slit. By measuring theta and a, we can find lambda.

  - Diffraction grating: A diffraction grating is a device that consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits that act as sources of coherent light. When a monochromatic light source passes through a diffraction grating, it produces an interference pattern on a screen. The angular position of the bright fringes (maxima) depends on the wavelength of the light, the distance between the slits, and the order of the fringe. The formula for the angular position is:

    `d * sin(theta) = n * lambda`

    where d is the distance between the slits, theta is the angle of the nth bright fringe, n is the order of the fringe, and lambda is the wavelength. By measuring d, theta, and n, we can find lambda.

- A mnemonic to remember the formulas for the fringe spacing, the angular position of the first dark fringe, and the angular position of the nth bright fringe is:

  `Yield = Lambda * Distance / distance`

  `Sine = Lambda / Aperture`

  `Distance * Sine = n * Lambda`

  where the capital letters denote the variables in the formulas, and the lowercase letters denote the words in the mnemonic.
