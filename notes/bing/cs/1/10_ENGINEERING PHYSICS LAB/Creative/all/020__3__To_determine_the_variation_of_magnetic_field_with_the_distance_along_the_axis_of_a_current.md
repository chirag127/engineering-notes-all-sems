##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current

- The magnetic field due to a current carrying wire depends on the distance from the wire and the direction of the current.
- If the wire is of a circular cross-section of radius a, then the magnetic field inside the wire is given by the formula:

  B = μ0Ir/2πa2

  where B is the magnetic field, μ0 is the permeability of free space, I is the current, r is the distance from the axis of the wire, and a is the radius of the wire.

- The magnetic field outside the wire is given by the formula:

  B = μ0I/2πr

  where B is the magnetic field, μ0 is the permeability of free space, I is the current, and r is the distance from the axis of the wire.

- The variation of magnetic field with distance from the axis of the wire is shown in the following graph:

  ```

  ```

- The magnetic field is directly proportional to the current and inversely proportional to the distance from the wire.
- The magnetic field is zero at the axis of the wire and reaches a maximum value at the surface of the wire.
- The magnetic field decreases as 1/r outside the wire.

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for the magnetic field inside the wire is:

  BIRDS: B = μ0Ir/2πa2

  where B is the magnetic field, I is the current, R is the distance from the axis, D is the diameter of the wire, and S is the square.

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for the magnetic field outside the wire is:

  BIR: B = μ0I/2πr

  where B is the magnetic field, I is the current, and R is the distance from the axis.
