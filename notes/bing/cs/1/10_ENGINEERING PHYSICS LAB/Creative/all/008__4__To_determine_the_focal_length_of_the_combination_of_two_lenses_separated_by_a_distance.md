##### 4. To determine the focal length of the combination of two lenses separated by a distance

- The focal length of the combination of two lenses separated by a distance is the distance from the second lens to the focal point of the combined lenses. This is also called the back focal length (BFL).
- The BFL can be calculated using the formula :

  `BFL = f2 * (d - f1) / (d - (f1 + f2))`

  where f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the first and second lens, respectively, and d is the distance between them.

- If the lenses are in contact, then d = 0 and the formula simplifies to:

  `1 / f = 1 / f1 + 1 / f2`

  where f is the focal length of the combination.

- If the distance between the lenses is equal to the sum of their focal lengths, then d = f1 + f2 and the formula becomes:

  `1 / f = 0`

  which means the combined focal length is infinite. This is called an afocal system, and it makes the refracted light rays parallel to each other.

- An example of a combination of two lenses separated by a distance is a simple optical telescope, which consists of a convex objective lens and a concave eyepiece lens. The objective lens has a large focal length and collects light from a distant object. The eyepiece lens has a small focal length and magnifies the image formed by the objective lens. The distance between the lenses is adjusted to make the system afocal, so that the final image is at infinity and can be seen clearly by the eye.
