#### Monochromatic and Polychromatic Light

- Monochromatic light is light that has only one wavelength or frequency. It is pure and single-colored. Examples of monochromatic light sources are lasers, LEDs, and sodium lamps.
- Polychromatic light is light that has more than one wavelength or frequency. It is mixed and multi-colored. Examples of polychromatic light sources are white light, sunlight, and incandescent lamps.
- Monochromatic light is useful for applications that require high coherence, such as interferometry, holography, and spectroscopy. Polychromatic light is useful for applications that require a broad spectrum, such as photography, colorimetry, and solar energy.
- A monochromator is a device that can separate polychromatic light into its constituent wavelengths using dispersion or diffraction. A prism or a grating can act as a monochromator. A monochromator can select a narrow band of wavelengths from a polychromatic light source.
- A mnemonic to remember the difference between monochromatic and polychromatic light is: Mono means one, poly means many. Monochromatic light has one color, polychromatic light has many colors.
