##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

- A hysteresis curve, also known as a B-H curve, is a graph that shows the relationship between the magnetic flux density (B) and the magnetic field intensity (H) of a material.
- A transformer is a device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another by using electromagnetic induction. A transformer consists of a primary coil and a secondary coil that are wound around a common core, which is usually made of a ferromagnetic material.
- To draw a hysteresis curve of a specimen in the form of a transformer, we need to measure the input current and output voltage of the transformer, and use the following formulas to calculate the values of B and H:

  - B = μ_0 * N * I / l
  - H = V / (N * A * f)

  - where μ_0 is the permeability of free space, N is the number of turns of the coil, I is the current, l is the length of the core, V is the voltage, A is the cross-sectional area of the core, and f is the frequency of the input current.

- The input current should be an alternating current (AC) that changes its direction periodically. The output voltage will also be an AC that follows the input current with some phase difference.
- As the input current changes, the magnetic field intensity (H) also changes, and induces a magnetic flux density (B) in the core. The B-H curve shows how B varies with H for one cycle of the input current.
- The B-H curve has a loop shape, which means that the magnetic flux density (B) does not return to zero when the magnetic field intensity (H) returns to zero. This is because the ferromagnetic material has some residual magnetism, or remanence, that remains even after the external field is removed. The remanence is represented by the point B_r on the B-H curve.
- To remove the remanence, a reverse magnetic field intensity (H) is needed, which is called the coercivity, or H_c. The coercivity is represented by the point H_c on the B-H curve.
- The area of the B-H curve represents the energy loss that occurs in the core of the transformer due to the repeated magnetization and demagnetization of the material. This energy loss is called the hysteresis loss, and it causes the core to heat up. The hysteresis loss increases with the area of the B-H curve.
- Different materials have different shapes of B-H curves, depending on their magnetic properties. Soft magnetic materials, such as iron, have a thin B-H curve, which means they have low remanence and coercivity, and low hysteresis loss. They are suitable for applications that require frequent switching of polarity, such as transformers and motor windings. Hard magnetic materials, such as steel, have a wide B-H curve, which means they have high remanence and coercivity, and high hysteresis loss. They are suitable for applications that require permanent magnetism, such as magnets and speakers.
