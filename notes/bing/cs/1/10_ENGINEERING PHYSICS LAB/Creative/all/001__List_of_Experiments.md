## List of Experiments

- A list of experiments is a collection of scientific or educational activities that are designed to test a hypothesis, demonstrate a principle, or explore a phenomenon.
- There are many types and categories of experiments, depending on the field of study, the purpose, and the method of conducting them.
- Some examples of experiments are:

  - List experiments: A type of survey technique that aims to measure sensitive or socially undesirable attitudes or behaviors by presenting respondents with a list of items, some of which are related to the topic of interest, and asking them to report how many items in the list apply to them. This way, the respondents do not have to reveal their individual answers, but the researcher can estimate the prevalence of the sensitive attribute by comparing the average responses of two randomly assigned groups: one that receives the list with the sensitive item and one that does not.
  - Harry Harlow's experiments with baby monkeys and wire and cloth surrogate mothers: A series of experiments conducted by the American psychologist Harry Harlow from 1957 to 1974, in which he studied the effects of maternal deprivation and social isolation on the emotional and cognitive development of rhesus monkeys. He found that monkeys raised with wire or cloth "mothers" that provided food but no comfort or warmth showed signs of anxiety, depression, and abnormal behaviors, while monkeys raised with real or surrogate mothers that offered both food and comfort developed normally.
  - Stanley Milgram's experiments on human obedience: A series of experiments conducted by the American social psychologist Stanley Milgram in 1963, in which he tested the willingness of ordinary people to obey an authority figure who instructed them to administer electric shocks to a learner (who was actually an actor) for every wrong answer in a memory test. He found that most of the participants (65%) continued to deliver the shocks up to the highest level (450 volts), even when the learner screamed, begged, or fell silent, indicating the powerful influence of authority and social pressure on human behavior.
  - Walter Mischel's marshmallow experiment: A series of experiments conducted by the American psychologist Walter Mischel and his colleagues from the late 1960s to the 1980s, in which they tested the ability of preschool children to delay gratification by offering them a choice between a small reward (such as a marshmallow) that they could have immediately or a larger reward (such as two marshmallows) that they could have after waiting for some time (usually 15 minutes). They found that the children who were able to wait longer for the larger reward showed better outcomes later in life, such as higher academic achievement, better self-control, and lower rates of obesity and substance abuse.
  - Science experiments for kids: A variety of fun and easy experiments that can be done at home or in school, using everyday materials and simple instructions, to explore various topics in science, such as chemistry, physics, biology, engineering, and more. Some examples are: making a volcano with baking soda and vinegar, creating a rainbow with a glass of water and a flashlight, building a mini drone with a paper cup and a motor, and growing crystals with salt and water .
