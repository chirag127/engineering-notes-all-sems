##### 9. To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid.

- The coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is a measure of its resistance to flow. It is defined as the ratio of the shearing stress to the velocity gradient of the fluid .
- The SI unit of coefficient of viscosity is pascal-second (Pa s), which is equivalent to newton-second per square meter (N s/m^2^) or kilogram per meter per second (kg/m s)  .
- The coefficient of viscosity of liquids decreases with the increase in temperature, while the coefficient of viscosity of gases increases with the increase in temperature  .
- One of the methods to determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid is to use a capillary tube viscometer, also known as an Ostwald viscometer or a U-tube viscometer. It consists of a U-shaped glass tube with two bulbs and a narrow capillary section. The liquid is drawn into the tube by suction and allowed to flow under gravity. The time taken for the liquid to flow between two marked points on the tube is measured. The coefficient of viscosity of the liquid is proportional to the product of the density, the length and the time of flow, and inversely proportional to the pressure difference and the radius of the capillary .
- The formula for the coefficient of viscosity of the liquid using the capillary tube viscometer is given by:

  `η = (ρghπr^4^)/(8l∆t)`

  where η is the coefficient of viscosity, ρ is the density of the liquid, g is the acceleration due to gravity, h is the height difference between the two bulbs, r is the radius of the capillary, l is the length of the capillary, and ∆t is the time of flow .

- A possible mnemonic to remember the formula is:

  `Viscosity = (Rho Gravity Height Pie Radius^4^)/(Eight Length Delta Time)`

  where the first letter of each word corresponds to the symbol of the variable in the formula.

- Some examples of the coefficient of viscosity of different liquids at 20 °C are:

  | Liquid | Coefficient of viscosity (Pa s) |
  |--------|---------------------------------|
  | Water  | 0.001                           |
  | Glycerol | 1.49                          |
  | Honey  | 10                              |
  | Tar    | 1000                            |

- Some applications of the coefficient of viscosity of liquids are:

  - It affects the flow rate of liquids through pipes, valves, pumps, etc.
  - It influences the lubrication of moving parts in machines, engines, bearings, etc.
  - It determines the mixing and stirring of liquids in chemical and biological processes.
  - It affects the heat transfer and thermal conductivity of liquids.
