##### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

- Stefan’s law states that the total amount of heat energy radiated per second per unit area of a perfect black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
- Mathematically, Stefan’s law can be written as:

  `E = σT^4`

  where E is the emissive power, T is the absolute temperature, and σ is the Stefan’s constant.

- To verify Stefan’s law by electric method, we need the following apparatus:

  - A Leslie’s cube, which is a hollow metal cube with four different surfaces (blackened, white, polished, and rough) and a small hole at the top to fill it with hot water.
  - A sensitive galvanometer, which is a device that measures small electric currents by the deflection of a magnetic needle.
  - A thermopile, which is a device that converts heat into electric voltage by connecting several thermocouples in series.
  - A thermometer, which is a device that measures temperature.
  - A rheostat, which is a device that controls the electric resistance in a circuit.
  - A battery, which is a device that provides electric power.
  - A key, which is a device that closes or opens an electric circuit.
  - A water bath, which is a container that maintains a constant temperature of water.

- The experimental setup is as follows:

  - Fill the Leslie’s cube with hot water and place it in the water bath to keep it at a constant temperature.
  - Connect the thermopile to the galvanometer and the battery through the rheostat and the key.
  - Adjust the rheostat to make the galvanometer reading zero when the thermopile is away from the Leslie’s cube.
  - Bring the thermopile near one of the surfaces of the Leslie’s cube and note the deflection of the galvanometer.
  - Repeat the same for the other three surfaces of the Leslie’s cube and note the corresponding deflections of the galvanometer.
  - Measure the temperature of the water in the Leslie’s cube with the thermometer.

- The observations are as follows:

  - The galvanometer deflection is maximum for the blackened surface and minimum for the polished surface of the Leslie’s cube.
  - The galvanometer deflection is proportional to the emissive power of the surface of the Leslie’s cube.
  - The emissive power of the surface of the Leslie’s cube is proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.

- The conclusion is as follows:

  - The experiment verifies Stefan’s law by electric method.
  - The experiment also shows that the blackened surface is the best emitter and absorber of heat, while the polished surface is the worst emitter and absorber of heat.
