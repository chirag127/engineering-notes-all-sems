##### 10. To determine the value of acceleration due to gravity (g) using compound pendulum.

- A compound pendulum is a rigid body that can oscillate about a horizontal axis passing through it.
- The time period of a compound pendulum depends on the length of the pendulum, the mass distribution of the pendulum, and the acceleration due to gravity.
- The acceleration due to gravity can be determined by measuring the time period of a compound pendulum for different values of the distance between the axis of oscillation and the center of mass of the pendulum.
- The formula for the time period of a compound pendulum is:

  T = 2π √(I/mgd)

  where T is the time period, I is the moment of inertia of the pendulum about the axis of oscillation, m is the mass of the pendulum, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and d is the distance between the axis of oscillation and the center of mass of the pendulum.

- To determine the value of g using a compound pendulum, the following steps are followed:

  1. Set up the compound pendulum by suspending a rigid body (such as a metal rod or a wooden plank) from a fixed horizontal axis (such as a knife edge or a pin) and ensure that the pendulum can swing freely in a vertical plane.
  2. Measure the mass (m) and the length (L) of the pendulum, and locate the center of mass (C) of the pendulum by balancing it on a horizontal support.
  3. Measure the distance (d) between the axis of oscillation (O) and the center of mass (C) of the pendulum.
  4. Displace the pendulum slightly from its equilibrium position and release it gently. Start a stopwatch when the pendulum passes through its mean position and count the number of oscillations (n) in a given time interval (t). Repeat this for at least 10 oscillations and calculate the average time period (T) of one oscillation using the formula:

     T = t/n

  5. Repeat steps 3 and 4 for different values of d by changing the position of the axis of oscillation along the length of the pendulum. Record the values of d and T in a table.
  6. Plot a graph of T^2 versus d and draw the best fit straight line. The slope of the line is equal to 4π^2I/mg, where I is the moment of inertia of the pendulum about the center of mass. The intercept of the line is equal to 4π^2k^2/mg, where k is the radius of gyration of the pendulum about the center of mass.
  7. Calculate the value of g using the formula:

     g = 4π^2I/m(slope - intercept)

  8. Compare the value of g obtained from the experiment with the standard value of g at the location of the experiment and calculate the percentage error.

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for the time period of a compound pendulum is:

  Time = Two times pi times root of (Inertia over mass times gravity times distance)

  T = 2π √(I/mgd)
