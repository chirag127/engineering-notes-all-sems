##### Carrying coil and estimate the radius of the coil

- A carrying coil is a coil of wire that carries an electric current and produces a magnetic field around it.
- The magnetic field of a carrying coil depends on the current, the number of turns, and the shape and size of the coil.
- One way to estimate the radius of a carrying coil is to use the formula for the magnetic field at the center of a circular loop of wire:

  `B = μ₀I/2r`

  where B is the magnetic field, μ₀ is the magnetic constant, I is the current, and r is the radius of the loop.

- To estimate the radius of a carrying coil, we can measure the magnetic field at the center of the coil using a device such as a Hall probe, and then rearrange the formula to solve for r:

  `r = μ₀I/2B`

- For example, if we measure the magnetic field at the center of a carrying coil to be 0.01 T, and we know that the current is 2 A, then we can estimate the radius of the coil as:

  `r = (4π × 10⁻⁷ N/A²)(2 A)/(2)(0.01 T)`

  `r = 0.0126 m`

  `r ≈ 1.3 cm`

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for the magnetic field at the center of a circular loop of wire is:

  `Big = Micro I over 2 radius`

  where Big stands for B, Micro stands for μ₀, I stands for I, and radius stands for r.
