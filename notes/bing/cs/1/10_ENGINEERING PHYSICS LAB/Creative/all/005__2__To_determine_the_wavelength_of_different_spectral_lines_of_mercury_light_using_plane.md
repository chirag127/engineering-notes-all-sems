##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- The aim of this experiment is to determine the wavelengths of the prominent lines of mercury by a plane transmission diffraction grating, hence to find the chromatic resolving power and the dispersive power of the grating.
- The apparatus required are spectrometer, plane transmission grating, sodium vapour lamp, mercury vapour lamp and spirit level .
- The theory behind this experiment is based on the diffraction grating equation, which relates the wavelength of the incident light, the angle of diffraction, the order of the spectrum and the grating constant .
- The grating constant is the reciprocal of the number of lines per unit length on the grating. It can be determined by using a sodium vapour lamp, which emits a yellow light of known wavelength (589.3 nm) .
- The procedure of the experiment is as follows :
  - Set up the spectrometer and adjust the collimator, the telescope and the prism table to make them horizontal and perpendicular to each other.
  - Place the plane transmission grating on the prism table and align it with the collimator axis.
  - Illuminate the grating with the sodium vapour lamp and observe the spectrum through the telescope. Adjust the grating position and the slit width to get a clear and sharp spectrum.
  - Locate the zero order spectrum (the undeviated beam) and the first order spectrum (the diffracted beam) on both sides of the zero order. Measure the angle of deviation for the first order spectrum using the vernier scale.
  - Repeat the same procedure for the mercury vapour lamp, which emits several spectral lines of different colours and wavelengths. Identify the spectral lines by their colours and measure the angle of deviation for each line in the first order spectrum.
  - Record the readings and calculate the wavelength of each spectral line using the diffraction grating equation.
  - Calculate the chromatic resolving power and the dispersive power of the grating for each spectral line using the formulas given in the reference.
- The precautions and sources of error in this experiment are :
  - The grating should be free from dust and scratches and should be handled carefully.
  - The slit width should be adjusted to get a narrow and bright spectrum.
  - The vernier scale should be read with parallax error.
  - The spectral lines may overlap or be distorted due to the quality of the grating or the lamp.
  - The temperature and pressure of the vapour lamps may affect the wavelength of the emitted light.
