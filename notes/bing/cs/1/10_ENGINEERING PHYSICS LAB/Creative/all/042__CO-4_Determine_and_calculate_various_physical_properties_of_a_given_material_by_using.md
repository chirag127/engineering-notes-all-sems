#### CO-4 Determine and calculate various physical properties of a given material by using

- Physical properties are the traits a material has before it is used, such as colour, hardness, density, etc.  
- Physical properties can be observed or measured without changing the composition of the material. 
- Physical properties can be classified into two categories: intensive and extensive properties. 
- Intensive properties are those that do not depend on the amount of material, such as temperature, specific heat, melting point, etc. 
- Extensive properties are those that depend on the amount of material, such as mass, volume, heat capacity, etc. 
- Some physical properties can be calculated by using mathematical formulas or equations, such as density, specific gravity, modulus of elasticity, etc. 
- Density of a material is the mass per unit volume. It can be calculated by dividing the mass by the volume. 
- Specific gravity of a material is the ratio of its density to the density of a reference substance, usually water. It can be calculated by dividing the density of the material by the density of water. 
- Modulus of elasticity of a material is the ratio of stress to strain when the material is subjected to a small deformation. It can be calculated by dividing the applied stress by the resulting strain. 
- Some physical properties can be determined by using experimental methods or instruments, such as hardness, brittleness, coefficient of restitution, etc. 
- Hardness of a material is the resistance to indentation or scratching. It can be determined by using a hardness tester, such as a Brinell, Rockwell, or Vickers tester, which measures the depth or area of the indentation made by a standard indenter under a known load. 
- Brittleness of a material is the tendency to break or shatter without significant deformation when under stress. It can be determined by using a fracture test, such as a Charpy or Izod test, which measures the energy absorbed by a standard specimen when it is struck by a pendulum. 
- Coefficient of restitution of a material is the ratio of the relative velocity of two colliding bodies after impact to the relative velocity before impact. It can be determined by using a ballistic pendulum, which measures the change in momentum of a projectile after hitting a target.
