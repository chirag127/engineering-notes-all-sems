##### 3. To determine the specific rotation of cane sugar solution using polarimeter.

- A polarimeter is an instrument that measures the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light passing through an optically active substance, such as cane sugar solution.
- An optically active substance is one that can rotate the plane of polarization of light, either clockwise (dextrorotatory) or anticlockwise (levorotatory).
- The angle of rotation depends on the concentration, length, temperature and wavelength of the solution.
- The specific rotation of an optically active substance is defined as the angle of rotation produced by a column of the solution one centimeter long and one gram per cubic centimeter in concentration.
- The specific rotation of cane sugar solution can be calculated by the formula:

  `S = θ / LC`

  where `S` is the specific rotation, `θ` is the angle of rotation in degrees, `L` is the length of the tube in decimeters, and `C` is the concentration of the solution in grams per cubic centimeter.

- To measure the specific rotation of cane sugar solution using polarimeter, the following steps are followed:

  1. Prepare a cane sugar solution of known concentration and fill a polarimeter tube of known length with it.
  2. Place the tube between the polarizer and the analyzer of the polarimeter and adjust the zero reading.
  3. Rotate the analyzer until the field of view is equally bright on both sides of the biquartz plate (or any other device that splits the light into two colors).
  4. Note the reading of the analyzer, which is the angle of rotation of the plane-polarized light by the cane sugar solution.
  5. Repeat the experiment with different concentrations and lengths of the tube and record the readings.
  6. Plot a graph of angle of rotation versus concentration and length of the tube and find the slope of the graph, which is the specific rotation of the cane sugar solution.

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for specific rotation is:

  `Silly = Theta / Love * Concentration`

  where `Silly` stands for specific rotation, `Theta` stands for angle of rotation, `Love` stands for length of the tube, and `Concentration` stands for concentration of the solution.
