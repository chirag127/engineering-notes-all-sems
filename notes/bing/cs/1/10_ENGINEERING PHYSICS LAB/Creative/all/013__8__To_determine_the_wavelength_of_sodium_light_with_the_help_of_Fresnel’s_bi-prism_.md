##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

- Fresnel's bi-prism is an optical device that produces two coherent sources of light by the refraction of a single wavefront.
- The bi-prism consists of two thin prisms joined at their bases to form an isosceles triangle with a very small angle (about 1 degree).
- When a narrow beam of monochromatic light (such as sodium light) passes through the bi-prism, it is split into two diverging beams that appear to originate from two virtual sources S1 and S2 behind the prism.
- The distance between the virtual sources is given by d = 2a tan(alpha), where a is the distance from the slit to the prism and alpha is the refracting angle of the prism.
- The two beams interfere in the region beyond the prism and form a pattern of alternate bright and dark fringes on a screen placed at a distance D from the slit.
- The fringe width (or fringe spacing) is given by beta = lambda D / d, where lambda is the wavelength of the light.
- To determine the wavelength of sodium light, the following steps are followed:

  - A sodium lamp is placed in front of a narrow slit and a convex lens is used to focus the light on the bi-prism.
  - A screen is placed at a suitable distance from the bi-prism and the position of the slit, the lens and the screen are adjusted to obtain clear and sharp fringes.
  - The distance D between the slit and the screen is measured using a meter scale.
  - The distance d between the virtual sources is measured by moving a thin wire across the fringes and counting the number of fringes that pass by the wire in a known distance x. The distance d is given by d = nx / N, where n is the number of fringes and N is the total number of fringes on the screen.
  - The wavelength of sodium light is calculated using the formula beta = lambda D / d, where beta is the fringe width, which can be measured by dividing the distance x by the number of fringes n.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for fringe width is: **B**eta is **L**ambda times **D**istance over **d**istance. (BLD/d)
- A possible learning trick to remember the formula for the distance between the virtual sources is: **d** is **2a** times the **tan** of the **alpha** angle. (d = 2a tan(alpha))
