##### And verify the formula for the focal length of combination of lenses.

- A combination of lenses is a system of two or more lenses that are placed close to each other, such that the image formed by one lens acts as the object for the next lens.
- The focal length of a combination of lenses is the distance from the last lens to the final image, when the object is placed at infinity.
- The formula for the focal length of a combination of lenses depends on whether the lenses are in contact or separated by some distance.

- If the lenses are in contact, the formula is:

  `1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 + ... + 1/fn`

  where f is the focal length of the combination, and f1, f2, ..., fn are the focal lengths of the individual lenses.

- If the lenses are separated by some distance d, the formula is:

  `1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 + ... + 1/fn - d/(f1f2)`

  where d is the distance between the first and the second lens, and the other terms are the same as before.

- To verify the formula for the focal length of a combination of lenses, we can use the lens equation and the magnification equation for each lens, and then eliminate the intermediate variables.

- The lens equation for L1 is:

  `1/u1 + 1/v1 = 1/f1`

  where u1 is the object distance and v1 is the image distance for L1.

- The lens equation for L2 is:

  `1/u2 + 1/v2 = 1/f2`

  where u2 is the object distance and v2 is the image distance for L2.

- The magnification equation for L1 is:

  `m1 = -v1/u1`

  where m1 is the magnification for L1.

- The magnification equation for L2 is:

  `m2 = -v2/u2`

  where m2 is the magnification for L2.

- The total magnification for the combination is:

  `m = m1 * m2`
