##### 1. To determine the wavelength of sodium light by Newton’s ring experiment.

- Newton's ring experiment is a method of measuring the wavelength of light using the phenomenon of interference of light.
- When a plano-convex lens of long focal length is placed over a glass plate, a thin air film of varying thickness is formed between them. The light reflected from the upper and lower surfaces of the air film interferes to produce a pattern of bright and dark rings, called Newton's rings.
- The radius of the nth bright ring is given by the formula:

  `r_n = sqrt[(R * n * lambda) / 2]`

  where R is the radius of curvature of the lens, n is the order of the ring, and lambda is the wavelength of light.

- The radius of the nth dark ring is given by the formula:

  `r_n = sqrt[(R * (2n - 1) * lambda) / 4]`

- To determine the wavelength of sodium light, we can use a monochromatic source of sodium light and measure the radii of the rings using a travelling microscope. Then, we can plot a graph of r^2 versus n and find the slope of the graph, which is proportional to the wavelength of light. Alternatively, we can use a known wavelength of light, such as green light, and compare the radii of the rings with those of sodium light to find the ratio of the wavelengths.
