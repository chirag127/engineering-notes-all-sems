##### 3. To determine the specific rotation of cane sugar solution using polarimeter.

- A polarimeter is an instrument that measures the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light by an optically active substance, such as cane sugar solution.
- The specific rotation of a substance is defined as the angle of rotation per unit length of the substance per unit concentration of the solution.
- The specific rotation of cane sugar solution can be determined by the following formula:

$$[\alpha] = \frac{\alpha}{l \times c}$$

where $[\alpha]$ is the specific rotation, $\alpha$ is the observed angle of rotation, $l$ is the length of the polarimeter tube, and $c$ is the concentration of the solution.

- To perform the experiment, the following steps are followed:

  - Prepare a standard cane sugar solution of known concentration and fill it in a polarimeter tube of known length.
  - Place the polarimeter tube in the polarimeter and adjust the analyzer and the vernier scale to obtain a clear field of view.
  - Rotate the analyzer until the two halves of the field are equally bright and note the reading of the vernier scale. This is the initial reading.
  - Remove the polarimeter tube and replace it with another tube containing pure water. Repeat the same procedure and note the reading of the vernier scale. This is the final reading.
  - Calculate the angle of rotation by subtracting the final reading from the initial reading.
  - Calculate the specific rotation of cane sugar solution by using the formula given above.
  - Repeat the experiment with different concentrations of cane sugar solution and record the observations.
  - Plot a graph of specific rotation versus concentration and verify the linear relationship between them.
