##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

- The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or liberated at an electrode by one coulomb of electric charge.
- The ECE of copper can be determined by using an ammeter and a copper voltameter, which is a device that measures the amount of electric current passing through a solution of copper sulfate using copper electrodes.
- The procedure of the experiment is as follows :
  - Connect a battery, an ammeter, a rheostat, a key and a copper voltameter in series.
  - Clean and dry the copper electrodes of the voltameter and weigh them separately. Record the initial masses of the anode (W1) and the cathode (W2).
  - Fill the voltameter with a solution of copper sulfate and immerse the electrodes in it. Make sure the electrodes do not touch each other or the walls of the voltameter.
  - Close the key and adjust the rheostat to obtain a steady current in the circuit. Note the initial reading of the ammeter (I) and the stopwatch (t).
  - After a certain time interval (say 10 minutes), open the key and stop the stopwatch. Note the final reading of the ammeter (I) and the stopwatch (t).
  - Remove the electrodes from the voltameter and wash them with distilled water. Dry them with a filter paper and weigh them again. Record the final masses of the anode (W1') and the cathode (W2').
  - Repeat the experiment for different values of current and time.

- The calculation of the ECE of copper is as follows :
  - The mass of copper deposited at the cathode is given by: m = W2' - W2
  - The mass of copper liberated at the anode is given by: m' = W1 - W1'
  - The average current passing through the circuit is given by: I = (I + I') / 2
  - The time interval for which the current flows is given by: t = t - t
  - The charge passing through the circuit is given by: Q = I x t
  - The ECE of copper is given by: z = m / Q
  - The ECE of copper can also be calculated from the anode by: z' = m' / Q
  - The average ECE of copper is given by: z = (z + z') / 2
- The discussion of the experiment is as follows:
  - The ECE of copper is a constant value that depends on the atomic mass and the valency of copper.
  - The ECE of copper can be calculated from either the cathode or the anode, as the mass of copper deposited or liberated is equal to the mass of copper transferred by the electric charge.
  - The ECE of copper can be verified by comparing it with the theoretical value, which is given by: z = E / F, where E is the chemical equivalent of copper and F is the Faraday constant
