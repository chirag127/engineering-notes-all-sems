#### CO CO Statement Bloom’s

- CO stands for Course Outcomes, which are the specific and measurable learning objectives that students are expected to achieve by the end of a course.
- CO statements should be written using action verbs that reflect the level of cognitive skills required by the students to demonstrate their learning.
- Bloom’s taxonomy is a framework that classifies different levels of cognitive skills from lower-order to higher-order thinking. It consists of six categories: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, and Create.
- Bloom’s taxonomy can be used as a guide to write CO statements that are aligned with the intended learning outcomes of the course. Each category of Bloom’s taxonomy has a list of verbs that can be used to write CO statements.
- For example, if the course outcome is to make students able to design a simple circuit, the CO statement could be written as: “By the end of this course, students will be able to create a schematic diagram of a simple circuit using appropriate symbols and components.”
- The verb “create” indicates that the students need to use higher-order thinking skills to synthesize and generate new ideas from existing knowledge. This CO statement is aligned with the Create category of Bloom’s taxonomy.
- Writing CO statements using Bloom’s taxonomy can help teachers to:
  - Clarify the expectations and standards of the course for the students and themselves.
  - Design appropriate assessment methods and criteria that match the level of cognitive skills required by the CO statements.
  - Plan and deliver effective instruction that supports the students’ learning and progression through the levels of Bloom’s taxonomy.
  - Evaluate and improve the quality and effectiveness of the course based on the students’ achievement of the CO statements.
