##### 6. To study the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit.

- A series LCR circuit consists of a resistor (R), an inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) connected in series to an alternating voltage source (V).
- The current (I) in the circuit is the same for all the components, but the voltages across them may differ in magnitude and phase.
- The voltage across the resistor (VR) is in phase with the current, the voltage across the inductor (VL) leads the current by 90 degrees, and the voltage across the capacitor (VC) lags the current by 90 degrees.
- The total voltage across the circuit (VT) is the phasor sum of VR, VL and VC, and the total impedance (Z) of the circuit is the ratio of VT and I.
- The impedance of the circuit depends on the frequency (f) of the alternating voltage source, and the values of R, L and C. It is given by the formula:

  Z = R + j(wL - 1/wC)

  where j is the imaginary unit, and w = 2πf is the angular frequency.

- The impedance of the circuit has a real part (R) and an imaginary part (wL - 1/wC), which are also called the resistance and the reactance of the circuit, respectively.
- The reactance of the circuit can be positive or negative, depending on whether the inductive reactance (wL) is greater or smaller than the capacitive reactance (1/wC).
- The resonance condition of a series LCR circuit is when the reactance of the circuit is zero, or when the inductive reactance is equal to the capacitive reactance. This means that:

  wL - 1/wC = 0

  or

  wL = 1/wC

  or

  w = 1/sqrt(LC)

- The frequency at which the resonance condition is satisfied is called the resonant frequency (fr) of the circuit, and it is given by:

  fr = 1/2πsqrt(LC)

- At the resonant frequency, the impedance of the circuit is equal to the resistance (Z = R), and the current in the circuit is maximum (I = V/R).
- At the resonant frequency, the voltages across the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude but opposite in phase, so they cancel out. This means that the total voltage across the circuit is equal to the voltage across the resistor (VT = VR).
- The resonance condition of a series LCR circuit can be verified experimentally by measuring the current and the voltage across the circuit for different frequencies, and finding the frequency at which the current is maximum and the voltage is minimum. Alternatively, the resonance condition can be verified by measuring the phase difference between the current and the voltage across the circuit, and finding the frequency at which the phase difference is zero.
