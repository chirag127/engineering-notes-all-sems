##### 8. To calibrate the given ammeter and voltmeter by potentiometer.

- The aim of this experiment is to compare the readings of an ammeter and a voltmeter with the true values of current and voltage obtained by a potentiometer, and to find the correction factors for the instruments.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the fact that a potentiometer can measure the potential difference between two points in a circuit without drawing any current from it, and hence can give the true value of the voltage. Similarly, a potentiometer can measure the current in a circuit by measuring the voltage drop across a known resistance, and hence can give the true value of the current.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are a potentiometer, a battery, a rheostat, a key, a galvanometer, a jockey, a standard cell, an ammeter, a voltmeter, a resistance box, and connecting wires.

![circuit diagram](https://i.imgur.com/0z6nZ8M.png)

- The procedure for this experiment is as follows:

  - Connect the potentiometer, the battery, the rheostat, the key, and the galvanometer as shown in the circuit diagram. Adjust the rheostat to obtain a steady deflection of the galvanometer when the jockey touches the end of the potentiometer wire.
  - Connect the standard cell in series with the galvanometer and the jockey. Find the balancing point on the potentiometer wire by sliding the jockey until the galvanometer shows zero deflection. Note the length of the potentiometer wire from the positive terminal of the battery to the balancing point. This length is proportional to the potential difference of the standard cell.
  - Connect the given voltmeter in parallel with the standard cell. Note the reading of the voltmeter. Repeat the above steps for different values of the rheostat and obtain a set of readings of the potentiometer length and the voltmeter reading. Calculate the ratio of the potentiometer length to the voltmeter reading for each set of readings. This ratio is the correction factor for the voltmeter.
  - Connect the given ammeter in series with the resistance box and the battery. Adjust the resistance box to obtain a suitable current in the circuit. Note the reading of the ammeter. Connect the potentiometer, the galvanometer, and the jockey in parallel with the resistance box. Find the balancing point on the potentiometer wire by sliding the jockey until the galvanometer shows zero deflection. Note the length of the potentiometer wire from the positive terminal of the battery to the balancing point. This length is proportional to the voltage drop across the resistance box, which is equal to the product of the current and the resistance. Calculate the current by dividing the voltage drop by the resistance. Repeat the above steps for different values of the resistance box and obtain a set of readings of the potentiometer length, the ammeter reading, and the calculated current. Calculate the ratio of the calculated current to the ammeter reading for each set of readings. This ratio is the correction factor for the ammeter.
  - Plot a graph of the correction factor versus the voltmeter reading for the voltmeter calibration, and a graph of the correction factor versus the ammeter reading for the ammeter calibration. Draw the best fit lines for the graphs and find their slopes and intercepts. The slope of the graph gives the percentage error of the instrument, and the intercept gives the zero error of the instrument.
