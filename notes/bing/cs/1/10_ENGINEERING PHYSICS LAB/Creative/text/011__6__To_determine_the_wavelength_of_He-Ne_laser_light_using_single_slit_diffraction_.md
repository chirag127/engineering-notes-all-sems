##### 6. To determine the wavelength of He-Ne laser light using single slit diffraction.

- The aim of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of a He-Ne laser light source using the phenomenon of single slit diffraction.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which states that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the resultant wave at any point is the superposition of these wavelets. When a monochromatic light beam passes through a narrow slit, it diffracts and forms a pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen. The angular position of the fringes depends on the wavelength of the light and the width of the slit.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are a He-Ne laser, a single slit, a screen, a meter scale, and a protractor.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:

  - Set up the apparatus as shown in the figure below, with the laser, the slit, and the screen aligned on a horizontal line. Adjust the distance between the slit and the screen to be about 1 m. Make sure the laser beam is perpendicular to the slit and the screen.

  - Turn on the laser and observe the diffraction pattern on the screen. You should see a central bright fringe and several alternating bright and dark fringes on either side of it.

  - Measure the width of the slit using a meter scale. This is the value of a.

  - Measure the distance between the slit and the screen using a meter scale. This is the value of D.

  - Measure the angular position of the first bright fringe on either side of the central fringe using a protractor. This is the value of θ.

  - Repeat the measurements for different values of a and D, and record the data in a table.

- The formula for calculating the wavelength of the light is given by:

  - λ = a sin θ / m

  - where λ is the wavelength, a is the width of the slit, θ is the angular position of the fringe, and m is the order of the fringe. For the first bright fringe, m = 1.

- The analysis of this experiment is as follows:

  - Calculate the wavelength of the light for each set of measurements using the formula.

  - Find the average value of the wavelength and the standard deviation.

  - Compare the experimental value of the wavelength with the theoretical value of 632.8 nm for He-Ne laser light.

  - Discuss the sources of error and uncertainty in this experiment, such as alignment, measurement, and diffraction effects.

  - Draw a graph of sin θ versus a / m, and verify that it is a straight line passing through the origin. The slope of this line is equal to the wavelength of the light.
