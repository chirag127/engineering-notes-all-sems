##### Determine its hysteresis loss.

- Hysteresis loss is the energy dissipated as heat in a ferromagnetic material when it is subjected to a changing magnetic field.
- It is caused by the irreversible movement of the magnetic domains inside the material, which align and reverse their direction according to the external field.
- The hysteresis loss per cycle is proportional to the area enclosed by the hysteresis loop of the material, which represents the relationship between the magnetic flux density and the magnetic field strength.
- To determine the hysteresis loss of a material, the following steps can be followed:
  - Apply an alternating current (AC) to a coil wound around the material, which creates an alternating magnetic field.
  - Measure the voltage and current of the coil, and calculate the power input to the coil.
  - Measure the magnetic flux density and the magnetic field strength of the material, and plot the hysteresis loop.
  - Calculate the area of the hysteresis loop using numerical integration or graphical methods.
  - Multiply the area of the hysteresis loop by the frequency of the AC, which gives the hysteresis loss per unit volume of the material.
  - Multiply the hysteresis loss per unit volume by the volume of the material, which gives the total hysteresis loss of the material.
