##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

- The aim of this experiment is to draw the hysteresis curve of a magnetic material (specimen) that is used as the core of a transformer and to calculate its hysteresis loss.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are: a transformer with a primary and a secondary winding, an AC source, an ammeter, a voltmeter, a wattmeter, a rheostat, and a CRO (cathode ray oscilloscope).
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Connect the primary winding of the transformer to the AC source through the rheostat, the ammeter, and the wattmeter. The rheostat is used to control the current in the primary winding.
  - Connect the secondary winding of the transformer to the CRO through the voltmeter. The CRO is used to display the voltage and the current waveforms of the secondary winding.
  - Adjust the rheostat to obtain a suitable current in the primary winding. Note down the readings of the ammeter, the voltmeter, and the wattmeter.
  - Observe the CRO screen and adjust the time base and the voltage sensitivity to obtain a clear and stable Lissajous figure. The Lissajous figure represents the relation between the voltage and the current in the secondary winding, which is proportional to the magnetic flux density (B) and the magnetic field intensity (H) in the core, respectively.
  - Trace the Lissajous figure on a graph paper and label the axes as B and H. The shape of the Lissajous figure is an ellipse, which is the hysteresis curve of the specimen.
  - Repeat the above steps for different values of current in the primary winding and obtain different hysteresis curves for different values of magnetic field intensity in the core.
  - Calculate the area of each hysteresis curve by using the formula: A = pi * a * b, where a and b are the semi-major and semi-minor axes of the ellipse, respectively. The area of the hysteresis curve is proportional to the energy dissipated as heat in the core per cycle, which is the hysteresis loss.
  - Plot a graph of hysteresis loss versus magnetic field intensity and observe the relation between them.
- The observations and results of this experiment are as follows:
  - The hysteresis curve of the specimen shows the magnetic properties of the material, such as the coercivity, the retentivity, the saturation magnetization, and the remanence.
  - The hysteresis loss of the specimen depends on the area of the hysteresis curve, which increases with the increase in the magnetic field intensity.
  - The hysteresis loss of the specimen also depends on the frequency of the AC source, which determines the number of cycles per second. The higher the frequency, the higher the hysteresis loss.
  - The hysteresis loss of the specimen can be reduced by using a material with a narrow hysteresis loop, such as soft iron or silicon steel, which have low coercivity and low retentivity.
