##### Using Carey Foster's Bridge

- Carey Foster's bridge is an electrical circuit used to measure the medium value of resistance or resistances which are nearly equal in their value  .
- The construction of Carey Foster's bridge circuit is similar to the Wheatstone bridge which consists of four arms in which four resistances are connected .
- The difference between the Wheatstone bridge and the Carey Foster bridge is that one of the arms of the Carey Foster bridge consists of a uniform resistance wire with a sliding contact  .
- The sliding contact can be moved along the resistance wire to obtain a balanced condition, which is indicated by a null deflection in a galvanometer connected between the two junctions of the bridge  .
- The balanced condition of the bridge is given by the equation:

  `P/Q = (R + x)/(S + l - x)`

  where P, Q, R and S are the four resistances, l is the length of the resistance wire, and x is the distance of the sliding contact from one end of the wire  .
- The advantage of the Carey Foster bridge is that it can measure small differences between two large resistances with high accuracy, by interchanging the positions of the resistances and taking the average of the two readings of x  .
- The applications of the Carey Foster bridge include:

  - Determining the resistance per unit length of the resistance wire.
  - Determining an unknown low resistance by comparing it with a known standard resistance.
  - Determining the specific resistance of a material by measuring its length, cross-sectional area and resistance.
  - Calibrating an ammeter or a voltmeter by comparing it with a standard cell and a potentiometer.
