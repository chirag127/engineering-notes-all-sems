##### 9. To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid.

- The coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is a measure of its resistance to flow. It depends on the intermolecular forces and the temperature of the liquid.
- One method to determine the coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is to use a **viscometer**, which is a device that measures the rate of flow of a liquid through a narrow tube or a capillary.
- The basic principle of a viscometer is that the pressure difference between the two ends of the tube or the capillary is proportional to the rate of flow of the liquid and the coefficient of viscosity of the liquid.
- The formula for the pressure difference is given by **Poiseuille's law**, which states that:

$$\Delta P = \frac{8\pi\eta LQ}{r^4}$$

where $\Delta P$ is the pressure difference, $\eta$ is the coefficient of viscosity, $L$ is the length of the tube or the capillary, $Q$ is the rate of flow of the liquid, and $r$ is the radius of the tube or the capillary.

- To use a viscometer, the following steps are followed:

  - A known volume of the liquid is filled in a reservoir attached to the tube or the capillary.
  - The liquid is allowed to flow through the tube or the capillary under the influence of gravity.
  - The time taken by the liquid to flow from one mark to another mark on the tube or the capillary is measured using a stopwatch.
  - The rate of flow of the liquid is calculated by dividing the volume of the liquid by the time taken.
  - The pressure difference is calculated by multiplying the density of the liquid by the acceleration due to gravity and the height difference between the two marks.
  - The coefficient of viscosity is calculated by rearranging Poiseuille's law and substituting the values of the pressure difference, the length, the rate of flow, and the radius of the tube or the capillary.

- The coefficient of viscosity of a liquid can be compared with that of another liquid by using the same viscometer and repeating the steps with the other liquid. The liquid with a higher coefficient of viscosity will have a lower rate of flow and a higher pressure difference for the same volume of liquid.
