### Any ten experiments (at least four from each group)

Here are some examples of experiments that can be performed in different fields of science. The experiments are grouped into three categories: physics, chemistry, and biology.

Physics:

- Measuring the speed of sound using a tuning fork and a resonance tube. This experiment demonstrates the relationship between the frequency of a sound wave and the length of the tube that produces a standing wave.
- Investigating the laws of reflection and refraction using a ray box and a glass block. This experiment shows how light rays change direction when they encounter different media.
- Determining the focal length of a convex lens using a candle and a screen. This experiment illustrates the principle of image formation by a converging lens.
- Exploring the properties of magnets and magnetic fields using a compass and iron filings. This experiment reveals the shape and direction of the magnetic field around a magnet and how it interacts with other magnets.

Chemistry:

- Testing the acidity or alkalinity of different substances using litmus paper and universal indicator. This experiment introduces the concept of pH and the use of indicators to measure it.
- Performing a titration to find the concentration of an acid or a base using a burette, a pipette, and a suitable indicator. This experiment demonstrates the technique of titration and the stoichiometry of acid-base reactions.
- Making a voltaic cell using two metal electrodes and a salt bridge. This experiment shows how electricity can be generated from a spontaneous redox reaction.
- Separating a mixture of sand, salt, and iron filings using a magnet, filtration, and evaporation. This experiment illustrates the methods of physical separation and the differences between pure substances and mixtures.

Biology:

- Observing the structure of plant and animal cells using a microscope and prepared slides. This experiment helps to identify the main organelles and features of eukaryotic cells.
- Investigating the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis using a water plant and a light source. This experiment measures the amount of oxygen produced by the plant as an indicator of photosynthesis.
- Testing for the presence of starch, sugar, protein, and fat in different food samples using iodine, Benedict's solution, Biuret reagent, and ethanol. This experiment demonstrates the use of chemical tests to identify the main types of biomolecules.
- Studying the inheritance of a single trait using a monohybrid cross and Punnett squares. This experiment applies the principles of Mendelian genetics to predict and analyze the genotypes and phenotypes of offspring.
