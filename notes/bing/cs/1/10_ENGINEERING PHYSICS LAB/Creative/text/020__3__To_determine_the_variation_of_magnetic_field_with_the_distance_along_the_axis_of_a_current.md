##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current

- The magnetic field produced by a current-carrying circular coil can be measured at different points along its axis using a magnetic compass or a Hall probe.
- The magnetic field at the center of the coil is given by the formula:

  B = (μ₀NI)/(2R)

  where B is the magnetic field, μ₀ is the permeability of free space, N is the number of turns of the coil, I is the current in the coil, and R is the radius of the coil.
- The magnetic field at any point on the axis of the coil is given by the formula:

  B = (μ₀NI)/(2(R² + x²)^(3/2))

  where x is the distance from the center of the coil along the axis.
- The variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of the coil can be plotted on a graph by taking x as the independent variable and B as the dependent variable.
- The graph will show that the magnetic field is maximum at the center of the coil and decreases as the distance from the center increases. The graph will also show that the magnetic field approaches zero as the distance becomes very large compared to the radius of the coil.
