##### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

- Stefan’s law states that the total radiant energy emitted per unit time per unit area of a black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature, i.e., E = σT^4, where σ is the Stefan’s constant.
- To verify this law by electric method, we use an apparatus consisting of a cylindrical vessel with a small hole at the top, a heating coil inside the vessel, a thermocouple to measure the temperature of the coil, a voltmeter and an ammeter to measure the voltage and current supplied to the coil, and a photodiode to measure the intensity of radiation emitted from the hole.
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Connect the circuit as shown in the figure below.
  - Switch on the power supply and adjust the rheostat to obtain a suitable current in the coil.
  - Note the readings of the voltmeter, ammeter, and thermocouple, and calculate the temperature of the coil using the calibration curve of the thermocouple.
  - Note the reading of the photodiode, which is proportional to the intensity of radiation emitted from the hole.
  - Repeat the above steps for different values of current and obtain a set of readings of temperature and intensity.
  - Plot a graph of intensity versus temperature to the power four, i.e., I vs T^4.
  - The graph should be a straight line passing through the origin, which verifies Stefan’s law.
