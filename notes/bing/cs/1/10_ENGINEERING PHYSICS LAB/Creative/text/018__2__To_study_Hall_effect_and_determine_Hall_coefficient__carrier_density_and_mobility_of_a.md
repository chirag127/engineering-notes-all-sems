##### 2. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a

- Hall effect is the development of a transverse electric field in a solid material when it carries an electric current and is placed in a magnetic field that is perpendicular to the current.
- Hall effect can be used to measure the magnetic field strength, the type and density of charge carriers, and their mobility in a material.
- Hall effect is based on the concept of Lorentz force, which states that the charges in a conductor experience forces due to both electric and magnetic fields.
- To study Hall effect, a thin rectangular sample of the material is connected to a constant current source and placed between two poles of a magnet. The magnetic field is perpendicular to the current direction and the sample plane.
- Due to the magnetic field, the charge carriers in the sample experience a force that pushes them to one side of the sample, creating a potential difference across the sample width. This potential difference is called the Hall voltage and can be measured by a voltmeter.
- The Hall coefficient, R_H, is defined as the ratio of the Hall voltage to the product of the current, the magnetic field, and the sample thickness. It has the units of m^3/C and depends on the type and density of charge carriers in the material.
- The carrier density, n, is the number of charge carriers per unit volume in the material. It can be calculated from the Hall coefficient as n = 1/R_H e, where e is the elementary charge.
- The mobility, μ, is the measure of how easily the charge carriers can move in the material under the influence of an electric field. It can be calculated from the Hall coefficient and the resistivity, ρ, of the material as μ = R_H/ρ. It has the units of m^2/Vs and depends on the scattering of charge carriers by impurities, defects, and phonons in the material.
- To determine the Hall coefficient, carrier density, and mobility of a material, the following steps are required:
  - Prepare a thin rectangular sample of the material with known dimensions and connect it to a constant current source and a voltmeter.
  - Place the sample between two poles of a magnet and adjust the magnetic field strength using a gaussmeter.
  - Measure the Hall voltage for different values of the current and the magnetic field and record the data in a table.
  - Plot the Hall voltage versus the product of the current and the magnetic field and find the slope of the linear fit. This slope is equal to the Hall coefficient of the material.
  - Calculate the carrier density and the mobility of the material using the formulas given above and the resistivity of the material, which can be measured by a four-probe method or a Wheatstone bridge.
  - Compare the results with the theoretical values and discuss the sources of error and uncertainty in the experiment.
