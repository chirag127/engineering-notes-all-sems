##### Transmission Grating

- A transmission grating is a type of diffraction grating that transmits light through a periodic array of slits or grooves.
- The transmitted light is diffracted into different directions according to the wavelength and the grating spacing.
- The angular dispersion of the diffracted light is given by the grating equation: $$d(\sin\theta_m - \sin\theta_i) = m\lambda$$ where $d$ is the grating spacing, $\theta_i$ is the angle of incidence, $\theta_m$ is the angle of diffraction, $m$ is the diffraction order, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light.
- Transmission gratings can be classified into two types: surface relief gratings and volume Bragg gratings.
- Surface relief gratings are made by etching or ruling a pattern of slits or grooves on a transparent substrate. They have a thin layer of coating to enhance the transmission efficiency and reduce the scattering losses.
- Volume Bragg gratings are made by creating a periodic modulation of the refractive index inside a transparent material. They have a high diffraction efficiency and a narrow spectral bandwidth.
- Transmission gratings have some advantages over reflection gratings, such as low polarization sensitivity, compact and in-line configuration, and high thermal stability. They are widely used in spectroscopy, laser beam shaping, optical communication, and optical metrology.
