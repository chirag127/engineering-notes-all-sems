##### Given semiconductor material using Hall effect setup

The Hall effect is a phenomenon that occurs when a current-carrying conductor or a semiconductor is placed in a perpendicular magnetic field, resulting in a voltage difference across the conductor or the semiconductor. This voltage difference is called the Hall voltage and it is proportional to the product of the current, the magnetic field and the Hall coefficient. The Hall coefficient depends on the type, number and mobility of the charge carriers in the material. Therefore, the Hall effect can be used to determine the properties of a given semiconductor material, such as the carrier type, concentration and mobility.

The Hall effect setup for a semiconductor material consists of the following components:

- A thin rectangular sample of the semiconductor material with four electrical contacts, two on the opposite sides for applying a current and two on the adjacent sides for measuring the Hall voltage.
- A constant current source to provide a steady current through the sample.
- A voltmeter to measure the Hall voltage across the sample.
- A pair of magnets to generate a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the sample.
- A gaussmeter to measure the magnetic field strength.

The procedure for the Hall effect experiment is as follows:

- Connect the current source to the two opposite contacts of the sample and the voltmeter to the two adjacent contacts of the sample.
- Place the sample between the magnets and adjust the distance between them to obtain a desired magnetic field strength. Measure the magnetic field strength with the gaussmeter.
- Turn on the current source and measure the Hall voltage with the voltmeter. Note the polarity of the Hall voltage.
- Repeat the above steps for different values of the current and the magnetic field strength.

The observation and analysis of the Hall effect experiment are as follows:

- The Hall voltage is proportional to the product of the current, the magnetic field strength and the Hall coefficient, as given by the equation:

  V<sub>H</sub> = R<sub>H</sub>IB/d

  where V<sub>H</sub> is the Hall voltage, R<sub>H</sub> is the Hall coefficient, I is the current, B is the magnetic field strength and d is the thickness of the sample.

- The Hall coefficient can be calculated from the slope of the plot of the Hall voltage versus the product of the current and the magnetic field strength.

- The sign of the Hall coefficient indicates the type of the charge carriers in the material. A positive Hall coefficient means that the charge carriers are positive (holes) and a negative Hall coefficient means that the charge carriers are negative (electrons).

- The carrier concentration can be calculated from the Hall coefficient using the relation:

  R<sub>H</sub> = 1/ne

  where n is the carrier concentration and e is the elementary charge.

- The carrier mobility can be calculated from the Hall coefficient and the electrical resistivity of the material using the relation:

  R<sub>H</sub> = μρ/e

  where μ is the carrier mobility and ρ is the electrical resistivity.
