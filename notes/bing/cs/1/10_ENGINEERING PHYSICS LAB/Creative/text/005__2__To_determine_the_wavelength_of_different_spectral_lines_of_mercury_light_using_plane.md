##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane transmission grating.

- Aim: To determine the wavelengths of the prominent lines of mercury by a plane transmission diffraction grating, hence to find the chromatic resolving power and the dispersive power of the grating.
- Apparatus: Spectrometer, plane transmission grating, mercury vapour lamp, spirit level, reading lens, etc.
- Theory: When a beam of monochromatic light is incident on a plane transmission grating, it is diffracted into different directions. The angle of diffraction $\theta$ is related to the wavelength $\lambda$ of the light and the grating constant $d$ (the number of lines per unit length) by the equation:

$$d(\sin i + \sin \theta) = n\lambda$$

where $i$ is the angle of incidence and $n$ is the order of diffraction. For normal incidence ($i=0$), the equation simplifies to:

$$d\sin \theta = n\lambda$$

By measuring the angle of diffraction $\theta$ for different spectral lines of mercury and knowing the grating constant $d$, the wavelengths $\lambda$ can be calculated.

The chromatic resolving power of a grating is defined as the ratio of the wavelength to the minimum difference in wavelength that can be resolved by the grating. It is given by:

$$R = \frac{\lambda}{\Delta \lambda} = nN$$

where $N$ is the total number of lines on the grating and $\Delta \lambda$ is the minimum difference in wavelength that can be resolved by the grating.

The dispersive power of a grating is defined as the rate of change of angle of diffraction with respect to wavelength. It is given by:

$$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = \frac{n}{d\cos \theta}$$

- Procedure:
  - Level the spectrometer table using the spirit level and adjust the collimator slit to get a fine parallel beam of light.
  - Mount the grating on the prism table and adjust it to make it perpendicular to the collimator axis.
  - Illuminate the grating with the mercury vapour lamp and observe the spectrum through the telescope.
  - Rotate the telescope to the left and right to locate the different spectral lines of mercury in different orders of diffraction.
  - Record the readings of the vernier scales for each spectral line and each order of diffraction.
  - Repeat the observations for at least three different positions of the grating.
  - Calculate the mean value of the angle of diffraction $\theta$ for each spectral line and each order of diffraction.
  - Calculate the wavelength $\lambda$ of each spectral line using the grating equation and compare it with the standard values.
  - Calculate the chromatic resolving power and the dispersive power of the grating for each spectral line and each order of diffraction.

- Precautions:
  - The grating should be free from dust and scratches and should be handled carefully.
  - The collimator slit should be narrow and parallel and should be adjusted for minimum deviation.
  - The telescope should be focused on the cross-wire and the spectral lines.
  - The readings of the vernier scales should be taken with care and without parallax error.
  - The mercury vapour lamp should be allowed to warm up for some time before taking the observations.
