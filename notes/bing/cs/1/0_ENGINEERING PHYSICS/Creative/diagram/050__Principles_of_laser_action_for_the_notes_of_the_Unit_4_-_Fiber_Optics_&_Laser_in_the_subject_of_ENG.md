The basic principle of laser action is the amplification of light by stimulated emission of radiation. This means that an atom or molecule in an excited state can be induced to emit a photon of the same frequency and phase as an incoming photon, resulting in a coherent and intense beam of light.

To achieve laser action, three conditions are required:

1. A suitable medium that can emit light of a specific wavelength. This can be a gas, a liquid, a solid, or a semiconductor.
2. A population inversion, which means that more atoms or molecules are in the excited state than in the ground state. This can be achieved by pumping energy into the medium, such as by an electric current, a flash lamp, or another laser.
3. A resonant cavity, which is a pair of mirrors that reflect the light back and forth, allowing it to gain intensity and coherence. One of the mirrors is partially transparent, allowing some of the light to escape as the laser beam.
