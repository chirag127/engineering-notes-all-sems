Newton's rings is a phenomenon in which an interference pattern is created by the reflection of light between two surfaces, typically a spherical surface and an adjacent touching flat surface. It is named after Isaac Newton, who investigated the effect in 1666.

The following diagram illustrates the basic setup of Newton's rings experiment:

```
            O  <--- Convex lens
            |

            V
    -----------------  <--- Flat glass surface
            |
            |
            V
    Microscope
```

The light from the source passes through the monochromatic filter and the collimating lens, and falls on the convex lens placed on the flat glass surface. The light is reflected from both the surfaces, and the reflected rays interfere with each other, producing a series of concentric, alternating bright and dark rings centered at the point of contact between the two surfaces. The radius of the rings depends on the wavelength of the light, the angle of incidence, and the radius of curvature of the convex lens. The microscope is used to observe the rings.

Newton's rings have various applications in optics, such as:

- Measuring the wavelength of light
- Measuring the refractive index of liquids
- Testing the quality of optical surfaces
- Measuring the thickness of thin films
- Measuring the pressure of gases
