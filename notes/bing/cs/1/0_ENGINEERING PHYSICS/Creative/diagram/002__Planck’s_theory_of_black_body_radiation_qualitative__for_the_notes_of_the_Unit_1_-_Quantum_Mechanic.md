### Planck’s theory of black body radiation(qualitative)

According to Planck's theory, a black body is a hypothetical body that completely absorbs all radiant energy falling upon it, reaches some equilibrium temperature, and then reemits that energy as quickly as it absorbs it. The energy of the emitted radiation depends on the frequency of the electromagnetic waves and the temperature of the black body. Planck assumed that the energy of the radiation is quantized, meaning that it can only take discrete values that are multiples of a fundamental unit called a quantum. The quantum of energy for a given frequency is proportional to the frequency itself, and the constant of proportionality is called Planck's constant.

The curves represent the spectral energy distribution of the black body radiation for different temperatures. The higher the temperature, the higher the peak of the curve and the lower the frequency at which the peak occurs. This means that the black body emits more energy and shifts to higher frequencies as the temperature increases. The curves also approach zero as the frequency approaches zero or infinity, meaning that the black body emits no energy at these extreme frequencies.

The diagram also shows the Wien's displacement law and the Stefan-Boltzmann law, which are two important consequences of Planck's theory. The Wien's displacement law states that the frequency at which the peak of the curve occurs is inversely proportional to the temperature of the black body. The Stefan-Boltzmann law states that the total energy emitted by the black body per unit area and per unit time is proportional to the fourth power of the temperature. These laws are represented by the following equations:

```
Wien's displacement law: f_max = b / T

Stefan-Boltzmann law: E = sigma * T^4
```

where f_max is the frequency at which the peak occurs, b is a constant called Wien's displacement constant, T is the temperature of the black body, E is the total energy emitted, and sigma is a constant called Stefan-Boltzmann constant.
