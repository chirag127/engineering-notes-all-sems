Skin depth is the distance over which the magnitude of the electric or magnetic field is reduced by a factor of 1/e (about 37%) of the value at the surface of a conductor. It depends on the frequency of the current and the electrical and magnetic properties of the conductor. The incident wave is the electromagnetic wave that hits the conductor. The reflected wave is the part of the incident wave that bounces back from the conductor. The transmitted wave is the part of the incident wave that penetrates the conductor. The skin depth δ is the distance over which the transmitted wave amplitude decreases by a factor of 1/e. The skin depth is smaller for higher frequencies and higher conductivities. This means that high-frequency currents tend to flow near the surface of a conductor, while low-frequency currents can penetrate deeper into the conductor. This phenomenon is called the skin effect.
