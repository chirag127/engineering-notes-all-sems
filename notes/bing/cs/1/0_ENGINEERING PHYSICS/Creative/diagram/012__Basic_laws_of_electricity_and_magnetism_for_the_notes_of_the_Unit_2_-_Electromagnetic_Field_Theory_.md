### Basic laws of electricity and magnetism

The basic laws of electricity and magnetism are:

- Coulomb's law: The force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The force is along the line joining the charges and is attractive if the charges have opposite signs and repulsive if they have the same sign.

  ```
  +q1      +q2
   o-------o
    \     /
     \   /
      \ /
       X
      / \
     /   \
    /     \
   o-------o
  -q3      -q4
  ```

  In the above diagram, the charges q1 and q2 repel each other, while the charges q3 and q4 attract each other. The force between q1 and q2 is given by:

  ```
  F12 = k*q1*q2/r12^2
  ```

  where k is the Coulomb's constant and r12 is the distance between q1 and q2.

- Gauss's law: The electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the net charge enclosed by the surface divided by the permittivity of free space. The electric flux is the product of the electric field and the area vector of the surface.

  In the above diagram, the electric field lines due to the charges q and -q are shown. The electric flux through the closed surface S is given by:

  ```
  ΦE = ∫S E.dA = q/ε0
  ```

  where E is the electric field, dA is the area vector, and ε0 is the permittivity of free space.

- Ampere's circuital law: The line integral of the magnetic field around any closed loop is equal to the permeability of free space times the net current enclosed by the loop. The magnetic field is generated by moving charges or currents.

  ```
  ∫L B.dl = μ0*I

  where B is the magnetic field, dl is the line element, and μ0 is the permeability of free space.

- Faraday's law of induction: The electromotive force (emf) induced in a loop of wire is equal to the negative rate of change of the magnetic flux through the loop. The magnetic flux is the product of the magnetic field and the area vector of the loop. The emf causes a current to flow in the loop, which opposes the change in the magnetic flux.

  ```
   N

  In the above diagram, the magnetic field B is constant and points into the page. The loop of wire is moving to the right with a velocity v. The magnetic flux through the loop is given by:

  ```
  ΦB = B*A*cosθ
  ```

  where A is the area of the loop and θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the area vector. The emf induced in the loop is given by:

  ```
  ε = -dΦB/dt = -B*dA/dt*cosθ = -B*v*L*cosθ
  ```

  where L is the length of the loop. The emf causes a current to flow in the loop, which generates a magnetic force that opposes the motion of the loop.
