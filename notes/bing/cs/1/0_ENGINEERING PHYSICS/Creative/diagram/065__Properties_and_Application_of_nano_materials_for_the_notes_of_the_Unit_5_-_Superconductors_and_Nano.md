### Properties and Application of nano materials

Nanomaterials are materials with a particle size of less than 100 nm in at least one of their dimensions. They exhibit unique optical, magnetic, electrical, and other properties that are different from their bulk counterparts . Nanomaterials can be classified into nanostructured and nanoparticle materials. Nanostructured materials have a regular pattern or arrangement of their atoms or molecules, while nanoparticle materials have a random distribution of their constituents.

Some of the properties of nanomaterials are:

- High surface area to volume ratio: Nanomaterials have a large fraction of atoms or molecules exposed on their surface, which enhances their reactivity, catalytic activity, adsorption, and sensitivity .
- Quantum confinement: Nanomaterials have discrete energy levels due to the confinement of electrons in a small space, which affects their optical, electrical, and magnetic properties . For example, quantum dots are nanomaterials that emit different colors of light depending on their size.
- Surface plasmon resonance: Nanomaterials can interact with electromagnetic waves and produce collective oscillations of electrons on their surface, which results in enhanced scattering, absorption, and emission of light . For example, gold nanoparticles have a characteristic red color due to surface plasmon resonance.

Some of the applications of nanomaterials are:

- Electronics: Nanomaterials can be used to fabricate nanoscale devices, such as transistors, sensors, memory, and displays, that have improved performance, speed, and efficiency . For example, carbon nanotubes are nanomaterials that have high electrical conductivity and mechanical strength, and can be used as nanowires, field-effect transistors, and interconnects.
- Medicine: Nanomaterials can be used to deliver drugs, genes, or imaging agents to specific targets in the body, as well as to diagnose, treat, or prevent diseases . For example, magnetic nanoparticles can be used to enhance the contrast of magnetic resonance imaging, or to deliver heat or drugs to cancer cells by applying an external magnetic field.
- Environment: Nanomaterials can be used to remediate, monitor, or prevent environmental pollution, as well as to produce clean and renewable energy sources  . For example, titanium dioxide nanoparticles can be used to degrade organic pollutants by photocatalysis, or to produce hydrogen by water splitting.

```

    |                   |
    |  Nanomaterial     |
    |                   |
    +-------------------+
    |                   |
    |  Nanoparticle     |
    |                   |
    +-------------------+
    |                   |
    |  Atom/Molecule    |
    |                   |
    +-------------------+
```

The diagram shows that a nanomaterial is composed of nanoparticles, which are in turn composed of atoms or molecules. The size of the nanomaterial is less than 100 nm, while the size of the nanoparticle is typically between 1 and 100 nm. The size of the atom or molecule is on the order of 0.1 nm. The properties of the nanomaterial depend on the size, shape, and composition of the nanoparticles, as well as the interactions between them.
