A diffraction grating is an optical plate that divides or disperses white light, which is composed of seven different colours, each with its own different wavelength. A diffraction grating pattern is an interference pattern consisting of maximums and minimums when light is diffracted.

The diagram shows the path difference between rays of light scattered from adjacent slits at the same local position on each slit of a diffraction grating. The path difference is given by:

`d sin θ = m λ`

where `d` is the distance between the slits, `θ` is the angle of diffraction, `m` is the order of the fringe, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light.

The bright fringes occur when the path difference is an integer multiple of the wavelength, i.e. `m` is an integer. The dark fringes occur when the path difference is a half-integer multiple of the wavelength, i.e. `m` is a half-integer.

The diffraction grating can be used to measure the wavelength of light by observing the angle of diffraction for a given order of the fringe. Alternatively, it can be used to separate the colours of white light by observing the different angles of diffraction for different wavelengths. The shorter the wavelength, the smaller the angle of diffraction. Therefore, violet light is diffracted the least and red light is diffracted the most.
