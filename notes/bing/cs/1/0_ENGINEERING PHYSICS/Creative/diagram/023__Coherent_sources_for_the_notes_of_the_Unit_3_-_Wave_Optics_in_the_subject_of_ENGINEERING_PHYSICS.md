Coherent sources are sources that emit waves with the same frequency, wavelength and constant phase difference. Coherent sources produce interference patterns when the waves superimpose and the positions of maxima and minima are fixed. Coherent sources can be obtained by splitting a single source of light into two beams using a device such as a beam splitter, a prism, a mirror or a thin film.

S is the original source of light, which is split into four coherent sources (S1, S2, S3 and S4) by the beam splitter. The beam splitter is a device that reflects and transmits a fraction of the incident light. The four coherent sources can produce interference patterns when they overlap.
