Einstein’s Coefficients are the probabilities of absorption, spontaneous emission and stimulated emission of light by an atom or molecule. They are denoted by A 21, B 12 and B 21 respectively.

The diagram shows two energy levels E 1 and E 2 of an atom, where E 2 > E 1. The energy difference between the levels is E 2 - E 1 = hν, where h is the Planck’s constant and ν is the frequency of the light.

The transitions between the levels are as follows:

1. Absorption: An atom in the lower energy level E 1 can absorb a photon of energy hν and jump to the higher energy level E 2. The probability of this transition per unit time is B 12 E, where E is the energy density of the radiation field.

2. Spontaneous emission: An atom in the higher energy level E 2 can spontaneously emit a photon of energy hν and fall to the lower energy level E 1. The probability of this transition per unit time is A 21, which is independent of the radiation field.

3. Stimulated emission: An atom in the higher energy level E 2 can be stimulated by a photon of energy hν and emit another photon of the same energy and phase, and fall to the lower energy level E 1. The probability of this transition per unit time is B 21 E, where E is the energy density of the radiation field.
