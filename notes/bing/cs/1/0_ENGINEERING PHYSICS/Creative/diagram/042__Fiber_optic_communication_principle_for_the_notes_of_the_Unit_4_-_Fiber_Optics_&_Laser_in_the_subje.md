The basic architecture of a fiber optic communication system consists of three main components: a transmitter, a fiber optic cable, and a receiver. The transmitter converts an electrical signal to an infrared light signal. The light signal is modulated to carry information. The fiber optic cable consists of a core, a cladding, and a coating. The core is the medium through which the light signal travels. The cladding is a layer of material that surrounds the core and reflects the light back into the core by total internal reflection. The coating is a protective layer that covers the cladding and prevents damage to the fiber. The receiver converts the light signal back to an electrical signal and demodulates the information.

The fiber optic communication principle has many advantages over conventional copper wire communication, such as higher bandwidth, lower attenuation, immunity to electromagnetic interference, and security.
