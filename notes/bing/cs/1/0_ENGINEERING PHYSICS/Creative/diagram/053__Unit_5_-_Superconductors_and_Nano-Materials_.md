## Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials:

Superconductors are materials that exhibit zero electrical resistance below a certain critical temperature (Tc). They also expel magnetic fields from their interior, a phenomenon known as the Meissner effect. Superconductors can be classified into two types: Type I and Type II. Type I superconductors have a single critical magnetic field (Hc) above which they lose their superconductivity. Type II superconductors have two critical magnetic fields (Hc1 and Hc2) and can sustain a mixed state of superconductivity and normal conductivity between them. High temperature superconductors are a class of materials that have Tc above 77 K, the boiling point of liquid nitrogen. They have potential applications in power transmission, magnetic levitation, and quantum computing.

Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the range of 1 to 100 nanometers. They exhibit novel physical and chemical properties due to their small size and large surface area. Nanomaterials can be classified into three types: zero-dimensional, one-dimensional, and two-dimensional. Zero-dimensional nanomaterials are nanoparticles that have no length, width, or height. They include quantum dots, nanocrystals, and fullerenes. One-dimensional nanomaterials are nanowires or nanotubes that have only one dimension. They include carbon nanotubes, silicon nanowires, and nanorods. Two-dimensional nanomaterials are nanosheets or nanofilms that have only two dimensions. They include graphene, boron nitride, and transition metal dichalcogenides.
