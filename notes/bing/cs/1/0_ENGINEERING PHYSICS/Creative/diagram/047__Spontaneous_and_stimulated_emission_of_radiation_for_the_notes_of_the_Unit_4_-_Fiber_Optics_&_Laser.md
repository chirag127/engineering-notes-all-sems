The following is a detailed ASCII diagram for spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS.

Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom in an excited state jumps back to the ground state by emitting a photon of energy without the aid of external agency. The direction and phase of the emitted photon are random and the radiation is incoherent.

Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom in an excited state is forced to jump to the ground state by emitting a photon of energy when it interacts with another photon of the same frequency. The emitted photon has the same frequency, phase, polarization, and direction of propagation as the incident photon. The radiation is coherent and amplifies the incident photon.

The diagram shows two atoms, A and B, in an excited state E2 and a photon of frequency v incident on atom A. The photon stimulates atom A to emit another photon of the same frequency and characteristics, resulting in two photons traveling in the same direction. Atom B undergoes spontaneous emission and emits a photon of frequency v in a random direction.
