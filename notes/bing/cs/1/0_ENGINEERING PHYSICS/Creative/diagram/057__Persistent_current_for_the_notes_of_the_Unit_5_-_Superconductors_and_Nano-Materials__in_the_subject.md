### Persistent current in superconductors

A persistent current is a perpetual electric current that does not require an external power source. Such a current is possible in superconductors, which have zero resistance and can maintain the current indefinitely. A persistent current can be induced in a superconducting ring by applying a magnetic field and then removing it. The current will produce a magnetic flux that opposes the change in the external field, according to Lenz's law. This is an example of the Meissner effect, which is the expulsion of magnetic fields from a superconductor.

The diagram shows the four steps of inducing a persistent current in a superconducting ring:

1. The ring has no magnetic field and no current.
2. A magnetic field B is applied perpendicular to the plane of the ring. This induces a current I in the ring, in the clockwise direction, according to Faraday's law of induction. The current produces a magnetic flux that is opposite to the external field, according to Lenz's law.
3. The magnetic field B is removed. The current I persists in the ring, since there is no resistance to dissipate it. The current maintains a magnetic flux that is outward from the plane of the ring, according to the right-hand rule.
4. The ring has a persistent current and a persistent magnetic flux, even without an external field or power source. This is a manifestation of the Meissner effect and the quantum nature of superconductivity.
