Fibre Optics: Principle and construction of optical fiber

An optical fiber is a thin, flexible, transparent fiber that carries light signals from one end to the other. It works on the principle of total internal reflection, which means that the light is completely reflected back into the fiber when it hits the boundary between two different materials with different refractive indices.

The basic structure of an optical fiber consists of three layers: the core, the cladding, and the protective coating. The core is the innermost layer of the fiber, where the light travels. The cladding is the outer layer of the fiber, which has a lower refractive index than the core, and acts as a barrier to prevent the light from escaping the fiber. The protective coating is a layer of plastic or other material that covers the cladding and protects the fiber from damage and environmental factors.
