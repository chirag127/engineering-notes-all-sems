The acceptance angle of an optical fiber is the maximum angle of a ray (against the fiber axis) hitting the fiber core which allows the incident light to be guided by the core . It depends on the refractive indices of the core, the cladding, and the surrounding medium . The numerical aperture (NA) is a parameter that characterizes the acceptance angle of an optical fiber. It is defined as:

NA = n0 sin θa

where n0 is the refractive index of the surrounding medium, and θa is the acceptance angle.

The angle θa is the acceptance angle, and the angle θc is the critical angle for total internal reflection at the core-cladding interface. The relation between these angles is given by Snell's law:

n0 sin θa = n1 sin θc

where n1 is the refractive index of the core. The critical angle θc can also be expressed in terms of the refractive indices of the core and the cladding (n2) as:

sin θc = n2 / n1

Combining these equations, we get the formula for the numerical aperture as:

NA = n0 sin θa = n1 sin θc = sqrt(n1^2 - n2^2)

This formula shows that the numerical aperture and the acceptance angle depend only on the refractive indices of the core, the cladding, and the surrounding medium, and not on the core diameter or the wavelength of light.
