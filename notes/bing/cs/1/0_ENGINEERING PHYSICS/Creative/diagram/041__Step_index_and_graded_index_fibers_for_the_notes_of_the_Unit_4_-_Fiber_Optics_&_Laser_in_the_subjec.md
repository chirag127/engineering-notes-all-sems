Step index and graded index fibers are two types of optical fibers that differ in the way the refractive index of the core varies with the radial distance from the center of the fiber. The refractive index is a measure of how much the light bends when it enters a medium.

In a step index fiber, the core has a uniform refractive index that is higher than the cladding, which also has a uniform refractive index. This creates a sharp boundary between the core and the cladding, and the light propagates in a zigzag manner by total internal reflection. Step index fibers can be either single mode or multimode, depending on the core diameter and the wavelength of the light. Single mode fibers have a very thin core that allows only one mode of light to propagate, while multimode fibers have a thicker core that allows multiple modes of light to propagate.

In a graded index fiber, the core has a varying refractive index that decreases gradually from the center to the edge of the core. This creates a smooth transition between the core and the cladding, and the light propagates in a helical manner by continuous refraction. Graded index fibers are only multimode, and they have a higher bandwidth than step index fibers because they reduce the modal dispersion, which is the spreading of the light pulses due to the different path lengths of the modes.
