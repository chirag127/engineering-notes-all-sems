### Absent spectra for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Absent spectra or missing order spectra are the phenomenon of some interference fringes disappearing in the thin-film interference pattern. This happens when the phase difference between the reflected rays from the upper and lower surfaces of the film is equal to an odd multiple of pi, resulting in destructive interference.

The path difference between the two reflected rays is given by:

`delta = 2nt cos(theta)`

where theta is the angle of refraction inside the film.

The phase difference between the two reflected rays is given by:

`phi = (2pi/lambda) delta`

where lambda is the wavelength of light in air.

The condition for destructive interference is:

`phi = (2k + 1) pi`

where k is an integer.

Substituting the expressions for delta and phi, we get:

`(2pi/lambda) 2nt cos(theta) = (2k + 1) pi`

Simplifying, we get:

`2nt cos(theta) = (k + 1/2) lambda`

This equation gives the condition for absent spectra or missing order spectra. It depends on the thickness of the film, the refractive index of the film, the angle of refraction inside the film, and the wavelength of light in air. For a given film and incident angle, some wavelengths of light will satisfy this condition and will be absent from the interference pattern, while others will not and will be present. This explains why thin films produce colorful effects when illuminated by white light.
