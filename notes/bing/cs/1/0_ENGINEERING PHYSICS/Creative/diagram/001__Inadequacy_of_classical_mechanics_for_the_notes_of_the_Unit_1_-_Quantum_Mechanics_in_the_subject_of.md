Classical mechanics is based on the assumption that physical phenomena can be explained by deterministic laws of motion and force. However, classical mechanics fails to account for some phenomena at the atomic and subatomic scales, such as:

- Stability of atoms: According to classical theory, electrons revolving around the nucleus in circular or elliptical orbits are accelerating and should emit electromagnetic radiation, losing energy and eventually falling into the nucleus. This would make atoms unstable and collapse. Quantum mechanics solves this problem by introducing the concept of quantized energy levels, where electrons can only occupy discrete orbits with fixed energies and do not radiate while in these orbits.

- Blackbody radiation: A blackbody is an idealized object that absorbs and emits all frequencies of electromagnetic radiation. According to classical theory, the intensity of the emitted radiation should increase with frequency, leading to an infinite energy output at high frequencies. This is known as the ultraviolet catastrophe. Quantum mechanics solves this problem by introducing the concept of photons, discrete packets of energy that are proportional to the frequency of the radiation. This limits the energy output at high frequencies and agrees with experimental observations.

- Photoelectric effect: The photoelectric effect is the phenomenon of electrons being ejected from a metal surface when exposed to light. According to classical theory, the energy of the ejected electrons should depend on the intensity of the light, and there should be a delay between the exposure and the emission. However, experiments showed that the energy of the ejected electrons depends only on the frequency of the light, and that the emission is instantaneous. Quantum mechanics solves this problem by introducing the concept of photons, discrete packets of energy that are proportional to the frequency of the light. This explains why only light above a certain threshold frequency can eject electrons, and why the emission is instantaneous.

- Hydrogen emission spectrum: The hydrogen emission spectrum is the set of discrete wavelengths of light emitted by hydrogen atoms when they transition from higher to lower energy levels. According to classical theory, the emission spectrum should be continuous, as any frequency of light can be emitted by the accelerating electrons. Quantum mechanics solves this problem by introducing the concept of quantized energy levels, where electrons can only occupy discrete orbits with fixed energies and can only emit or absorb photons with specific energies corresponding to the difference between the energy levels.
