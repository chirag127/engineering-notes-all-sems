### Fibre Optics: Principle and construction of optical fiber for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Fibre optics is the science of transmitting data, voice, and images by the passage of light through thin, transparent fibers.
- Optical fibers are flexible, transparent fibers made by drawing glass or plastic to a diameter slightly thicker than that of a human hair.
- Optical fibers work on the principle of total internal reflection of light, which means that no part of the light signal gets refracted or transmitted to the second medium, but instead, all of the signal is retained inside the fiber .
- The condition for total internal reflection is that the light must travel from an optically denser medium to an optically rarer medium, and the angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle for the boundary.
- The critical angle is the angle of incidence for which the angle of refraction is 90 degrees. It can be calculated by using Snell's law: sin c = n2 / n1, where c is the critical angle, n1 is the refractive index of the denser medium, and n2 is the refractive index of the rarer medium.
- The construction of an optical fiber consists of three main parts: the core, the cladding, and the protective layer .
- The core is the innermost part of the fiber, where the light signal travels. It is made of glass or plastic with a high refractive index (n1).
- The cladding is the outer layer of the fiber, which surrounds the core. It is made of glass or plastic with a lower refractive index (n2) than the core. The cladding acts as a boundary for the light signal and ensures total internal reflection at the core-cladding interface.
- The protective layer is the outermost layer of the fiber, which covers the cladding. It is made of a material that provides mechanical strength and protection from environmental factors, such as moisture, dust, or abrasion. The protective layer can be a plastic coating, a metal sheath, or a buffer tube.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

  - To remember the condition for total internal reflection, think of DART: Denser to Rarer, and angle greater than critical angle, Total internal reflection.
  - To remember the formula for the critical angle, think of SIN: Snell's law, Inverse, n2 over n1.
  - To remember the order of the layers of an optical fiber, think of CAP: Core, Cladding, Protective layer.
