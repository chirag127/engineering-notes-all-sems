### Temperature dependence of critical field for the notes of the Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials: in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- The critical field of a superconductor is the maximum magnetic field that the material can withstand without losing its superconductivity.
- The critical field depends on the temperature of the superconductor, and generally decreases as the temperature increases.
- The temperature dependence of the critical field can be described approximately by the following formula:

$$
B_c(T) = B_c(0) \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2 \right]
$$

where $B_c(0)$ is the critical field at absolute zero temperature, and $T_c$ is the critical temperature of the superconductor.

- The formula can be derived from the thermodynamic relation between the free energy of the superconducting and normal states, and the assumption that the superconducting energy gap is proportional to the critical temperature.
- The formula is valid for low-temperature superconductors (LTS), which have a single energy gap and a single critical field. For high-temperature superconductors (HTS), which have multiple energy gaps and multiple critical fields, the formula is not applicable.
- The critical field of a superconductor can also be affected by other factors, such as the shape, size, orientation, and purity of the material, the presence of defects, impurities, and external stress, and the type of magnetic field (static or dynamic).
- The critical field can be measured experimentally by applying a magnetic field to a superconducting sample and monitoring its resistance or magnetization. The critical field is the value of the magnetic field at which the resistance or magnetization becomes nonzero.

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for the temperature dependence of the critical field is:

**B**e **C**ool, **T**emperature **T**akes **C**are

where B stands for the critical field, C stands for the constant $B_c(0)$, T stands for the temperature, and Tc stands for the critical temperature. The formula can be read as:

The critical field is equal to the constant times one minus the square of the ratio of the temperature to the critical temperature.

- An example of applying the formula is:

Given that the critical field of a superconductor at absolute zero is 0.1 T, and its critical temperature is 10 K, find the critical field at 5 K.

Using the formula, we have:

$$
B_c(5) = B_c(0) \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{5}{10}\right)^2 \right]
$$

Simplifying, we get:

$$
B_c(5) = 0.1 \times (1 - 0.25)
$$

Therefore, the critical field at 5 K is 0.075 T.
