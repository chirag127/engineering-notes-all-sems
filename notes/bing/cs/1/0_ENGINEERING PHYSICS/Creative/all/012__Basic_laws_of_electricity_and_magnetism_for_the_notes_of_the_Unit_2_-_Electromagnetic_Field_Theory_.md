### Basic laws of electricity and magnetism for the notes of the Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Electricity and magnetism are two aspects of one common phenomenon: electromagnetism. Electromagnetism is the interaction between electric charges and magnetic fields. The basic laws of electricity and magnetism are:

- **Coulomb's law**: This law states that the force of attraction or repulsion between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The force acts along the line joining the charges. The mathematical expression of Coulomb's law is:

  `F = k q1 q2 / r^2`

  where F is the force, k is a constant, q1 and q2 are the charges, and r is the distance.

- **Gauss's law**: This law states that the net electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the total charge enclosed by the surface divided by the permittivity of free space. The electric flux is the measure of the number of electric field lines passing through the surface. The mathematical expression of Gauss's law is:

  `Phi = Q / epsilon_0`

  where Phi is the electric flux, Q is the charge, and epsilon_0 is the permittivity of free space.

- **Ampere's law**: This law states that the line integral of the magnetic field around any closed loop is equal to the product of the permeability of free space and the net current passing through the loop. The magnetic field is the measure of the force exerted by a magnet on a moving charge. The mathematical expression of Ampere's law is:

  `int B dot dl = mu_0 I`

  where B is the magnetic field, dl is the differential element of the loop, mu_0 is the permeability of free space, and I is the current.

- **Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction**: This law states that the induced electromotive force (emf) in a loop of wire is equal to the negative rate of change of magnetic flux through the loop. The emf is the measure of the potential difference that drives the current in the loop. The magnetic flux is the measure of the number of magnetic field lines passing through the loop. The mathematical expression of Faraday's law is:

  `emf = - dPhi / dt`

  where emf is the induced emf, Phi is the magnetic flux, and t is the time.

- **Maxwell's correction to Ampere's law**: This correction states that the line integral of the magnetic field around any closed loop is not only equal to the product of the permeability of free space and the net current passing through the loop, but also to the product of the permeability of free space and the displacement current. The displacement current is the measure of the rate of change of electric flux through the loop. The mathematical expression of Maxwell's correction is:

  `int B dot dl = mu_0 (I + epsilon_0 dPhi / dt)`

  where B, dl, mu_0, I, epsilon_0, Phi, and t have the same meanings as before.

These laws can be used to explain various phenomena such as electric fields, electric potentials, capacitors, resistors, currents, magnetic fields, magnetic forces, inductors, transformers, generators, motors, electromagnetic waves, and light. Some mnemonics and learning tricks to remember these laws are:

- For Coulomb's law, remember that **like charges repel and unlike charges attract**. You can use the acronym **LURA** to memorize this.
- For Gauss's law, remember that **electric flux is proportional to charge**. You can use the acronym **EFPC** to memorize this.
- For Ampere's law, remember that **magnetic field is proportional to current**. You can use the acronym **MBPC** to memorize this.
- For Faraday's law, remember that **emf is proportional to change in magnetic flux**. You can use the acronym **EMPCMF** to memorize this.
- For Maxwell's correction, remember that **displacement current is proportional to change in electric flux**. You can use the acronym **DPCCEF** to memorize this.
