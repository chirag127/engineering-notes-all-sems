### Newton’s Rings and its applications for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Newton's rings is a phenomenon in which an interference pattern is created by the reflection of light between two surfaces, typically a spherical surface and an adjacent touching flat surface.
- It is named after Isaac Newton, who investigated the effect in 1666.
- The phenomenon can be explained based on the wave theory of light. An air film of varying thickness is formed between the lens and the glass sheet. When a ray falls on the surface of the lens, it is reflected as well as refracted.
- The reflected rays from the upper and lower surfaces of the air film interfere with each other and produce bright and dark rings.
- The condition for constructive interference is given by:

    `2nt = (m + 1/2)λ`

    where n is the refractive index of air, t is the thickness of the air film, m is the order of the ring, and λ is the wavelength of light.

- The condition for destructive interference is given by:

    `2nt = mλ`

    where the symbols have the same meaning as above.

- The radius of the mth ring is given by:

    `rm = √(Rmλ)`

    where R is the radius of curvature of the lens.

- The diameter of the mth ring is given by:

    `Dm = 2rm = 2√(Rmλ)`

    where the symbols have the same meaning as above.

- The applications of Newton's rings are:

    - To determine the wavelength of monochromatic light.
    - To measure the refractive index of a thin film.
    - To test the quality of optical surfaces.
    - To measure the thermal expansion of solids.
    - To study the properties of liquids.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for the radius of the mth ring is:

    `Romeo and Juliet met at the root of a rainbow`

    where Romeo stands for radius, Juliet stands for order, met stands for multiplication, and root stands for square root. Rainbow stands for the product of radius of curvature and wavelength.
