## Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also called physical optics, is a branch of optics that deals with the behavior of light and its wave characteristics .
- Wave optics is useful for studying phenomena such as polarization, diffraction, interference and other occurrences where ray approximation of geometric optics cannot be done .
- Some of the important concepts and formulas in wave optics are:

  - Huygens' principle: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets .
  - Superposition principle: When two or more waves overlap in a medium, the resultant displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of the displacements due to each wave .
  - Interference: The phenomenon of superposition of two or more coherent waves to produce a resultant wave of varying intensity .
  - Young's double slit experiment: An experiment that demonstrates the interference of light waves from two coherent sources and produces a pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen .
  - Fringe width: The distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes in an interference pattern .
  - Fringe width formula: $$\beta = \frac{\lambda D}{d}$$ where $\beta$ is the fringe width, $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light, $D$ is the distance between the slits and the screen, and $d$ is the distance between the slits .
  - Path difference: The difference in the distances traveled by two waves from their sources to a point on the screen .
  - Phase difference: The difference in the phases of two waves at a point on the screen .
  - Phase difference formula: $$\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x$$ where $\Delta \phi$ is the phase difference, $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light, and $\Delta x$ is the path difference .
  - Condition for constructive interference: The path difference or the phase difference between two waves is an integral multiple of the wavelength or $2\pi$, respectively .
  - Condition for destructive interference: The path difference or the phase difference between two waves is an odd multiple of half the wavelength or $\pi$, respectively .
  - Diffraction: The phenomenon of bending of light waves around the edges of an obstacle or an aperture .
  - Fraunhofer diffraction: The diffraction of light when the source and the screen are effectively at infinite distances from the obstacle or the aperture .
  - Single slit diffraction: The diffraction of light when it passes through a narrow slit and produces a pattern of a central bright maximum and several secondary maxima and minima on a screen .
  - Angular width of central maximum: The angle subtended by the central bright maximum at the center of the slit in a single slit diffraction pattern .
  - Angular width of central maximum formula: $$\theta = \frac{2\lambda}{a}$$ where $\theta$ is the angular width of central maximum, $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light, and $a$ is the width of the slit .
  - Condition for minima in single slit diffraction: The path difference or the phase difference between two waves from the opposite edges of the slit is an odd multiple of half the wavelength or $\pi$, respectively .
  - Condition for minima in single slit diffraction formula: $$a \sin \theta = n \lambda$$ where $a$ is the width of the slit, $\theta$ is the angle of diffraction, $n$ is an odd integer, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light .
