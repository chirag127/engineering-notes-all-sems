### Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature for the notes of the Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that consists of electric and magnetic fields that oscillate sinusoidally in planes perpendicular to the direction of propagation of the wave.
- In a vacuum, electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light, commonly denoted c, which is approximately 3 x 10^8 m/s   .
- The electric and magnetic fields of a plane electromagnetic wave are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of energy and wave propagation, forming a transverse wave     .
- The electric and magnetic fields of a plane electromagnetic wave have the same frequency and wavelength, and are in phase with each other.
- The electric and magnetic fields of a plane electromagnetic wave obey the following relations:

  - E = cB, where E is the electric field amplitude, B is the magnetic field amplitude, and c is the speed of light.
  - E/B = c, where E/B is the ratio of the electric and magnetic field amplitudes, and c is the speed of light.
  - E x B = cS, where E x B is the cross product of the electric and magnetic fields, c is the speed of light, and S is the Poynting vector, which represents the direction and magnitude of the energy flux of the wave.

- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can be demonstrated by using polarizers, which are devices that allow only certain orientations of electric or magnetic fields to pass through them. By placing two polarizers at right angles to each other, the intensity of the transmitted electromagnetic wave can be reduced to zero, showing that the electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular to the direction of propagation of the wave.
- A mnemonic to remember the transverse nature of electromagnetic waves is **EBIT**:

  - **E**lectric field is perpendicular to the **B** field
  - **B**oth fields are perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation (**I**)
  - **T**ransverse wave

- An example of a plane electromagnetic wave in vacuum is a light wave, which is a type of electromagnetic radiation that has a frequency range from about 4 x 10^14 Hz to 8 x 10^14 Hz and a wavelength range from about 380 nm to 750 nm. Light waves can be polarized by using polarizers, filters, or reflection from certain surfaces.
