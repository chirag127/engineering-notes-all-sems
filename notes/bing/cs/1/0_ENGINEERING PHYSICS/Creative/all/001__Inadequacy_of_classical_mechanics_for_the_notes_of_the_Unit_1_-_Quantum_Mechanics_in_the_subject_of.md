### Inadequacy of classical mechanics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Quantum Mechanics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Classical mechanics is the branch of physics that deals with the motion of macroscopic objects under the influence of forces. It is based on Newton's laws of motion and the principle of conservation of energy and momentum. Classical mechanics can explain many phenomena in the physical world, such as the motion of planets, stars, cars, projectiles, etc.

However, classical mechanics fails to describe the behavior of microscopic objects, such as atoms, electrons, photons, etc. These objects exhibit phenomena that cannot be explained by classical mechanics, such as the stability of atoms, the spectra of black body radiation, the photoelectric effect, the Compton effect, the wave-particle duality, etc. These phenomena require a new theory of physics, called quantum mechanics, which is based on the concept of quantization of energy and matter.

Some of the main inadequacies of classical mechanics and how quantum mechanics solves them are:

- **Stability of atom**: According to classical theory, electrons revolving around the nucleus in circular or elliptic orbits are accelerating. Therefore, they should emit electromagnetic radiation and lose energy. This would cause them to spiral into the nucleus and the atom would collapse. However, this does not happen in reality. Quantum mechanics explains that electrons can only occupy certain discrete energy levels in the atom, and they do not radiate energy when they are in a stationary state. The lowest energy level is called the ground state, and the electron cannot fall below this level. Therefore, the atom is stable.
- **Spectra of black body radiation**: A black body is an ideal object that absorbs and emits all frequencies of electromagnetic radiation. According to classical theory, the energy of the radiation emitted by a black body should be proportional to the frequency of the radiation. This means that the black body should emit more energy at higher frequencies than at lower frequencies. However, this contradicts the experimental observation, which shows that the black body emits a peak amount of energy at a certain frequency, and then the energy decreases rapidly at higher frequencies. This is known as the ultraviolet catastrophe. Quantum mechanics explains that the energy of the radiation emitted by a black body is quantized, and it can only take discrete values that are multiples of a constant called Planck's constant. This means that the black body can only emit a certain amount of energy at a certain frequency, and the energy cannot exceed a maximum value. Therefore, the ultraviolet catastrophe is avoided.
- **Photoelectric effect**: The photoelectric effect is the phenomenon of emission of electrons from a metal surface when it is exposed to light. According to classical theory, the number of electrons emitted and their kinetic energy should depend only on the intensity of the light, not its frequency. However, this contradicts the experimental observation, which shows that the number of electrons emitted depends on the frequency of the light, and there is a minimum frequency, called the threshold frequency, below which no electrons are emitted, regardless of the intensity of the light. Also, the kinetic energy of the electrons depends on the frequency of the light, not its intensity. Quantum mechanics explains that the light is composed of discrete packets of energy, called photons, and the energy of each photon is proportional to its frequency. When a photon hits a metal surface, it can transfer its energy to an electron, if the energy is greater than or equal to the work function of the metal, which is the minimum energy required to free an electron from the metal. The excess energy of the photon becomes the kinetic energy of the electron. Therefore, the photoelectric effect depends on the frequency of the light, not its intensity.
- **Compton effect**: The Compton effect is the phenomenon of scattering of X-rays by electrons. According to classical theory, the wavelength of the scattered X-rays should be the same as the wavelength of the incident X-rays, and the scattering angle should depend only on the angle of incidence. However, this contradicts the experimental observation, which shows that the wavelength of the scattered X-rays increases, and the increase depends on the scattering angle. Quantum mechanics explains that the X-rays are composed of photons, and the photons can collide with electrons and transfer some of their energy and momentum to them. This causes the photons to lose some of their energy and increase their wavelength. The amount of energy and momentum transferred depends on the scattering angle. Therefore, the Compton effect depends on the scattering angle, not the angle of incidence.
