### Persistent current for the notes of the Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials: in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Persistent current refers to a perpetual electric current, not requiring an external power source.
- Such a current is impossible in normal electrical devices, since all commonly-used conductors have a non-zero resistance, and this resistance would rapidly dissipate any such current as heat.
- However, in superconductors and some mesoscopic devices, persistent currents are possible and observed due to quantum effects.
- In resistive materials, persistent currents can appear in microscopic samples due to size effects.
- Persistent currents are widely used in the form of superconducting magnets.
- The formula for the persistent current in a superconducting ring is given by:

    I = I_0 sin(2πΦ/Φ_0)

    where I_0 is the maximum current, Φ is the magnetic flux through the ring, and Φ_0 is the flux quantum.

- The persistent current in a superconducting ring is periodic with respect to the magnetic flux, and has a period of Φ_0.
- The persistent current in a superconducting ring is independent of the temperature, as long as it is below the critical temperature of the superconductor.
- The persistent current in a superconducting ring can be induced by placing the ring in a magnetic field above its critical temperature, and then cooling it below its critical temperature.
- The persistent current in a superconducting ring can also be induced by applying an external current to the ring below its critical temperature, and then removing the external current.
- The persistent current in a superconducting ring can be destroyed by applying a magnetic field above the critical field of the superconductor, or by heating the ring above its critical temperature.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the formula for the persistent current is:

    I see a sine wave with two pies and a flux capacitor.

    I = I_0 sin(2πΦ/Φ_0)

- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of persistent current is to imagine a superconducting ring as a frictionless roller coaster track, and the electrons as the cars. The electrons can keep moving around the track without any loss of energy, unless they encounter a strong enough force (such as a magnetic field or heat) that can stop them. The magnetic flux through the ring can be thought of as the height of the track, which affects the speed and direction of the electrons. The flux quantum is the minimum change in the height of the track that can cause the electrons to reverse their direction.
