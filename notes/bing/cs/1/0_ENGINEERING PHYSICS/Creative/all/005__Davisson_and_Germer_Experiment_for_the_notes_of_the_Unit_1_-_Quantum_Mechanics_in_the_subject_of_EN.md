### Davisson and Germer Experiment for the notes of the Unit 1 - Quantum Mechanics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- The Davisson and Germer experiment was a 1923-27 experiment by Clinton Davisson and Lester Germer at Western Electric (later Bell Labs), in which electrons, scattered by the surface of a crystal of nickel metal, displayed a diffraction pattern.
- The experiment confirmed the de Broglie hypothesis that matter has wave-like behavior and established the wave-particle duality hypothesis which was a fundamental step in quantum theory.
- The experiment was based on the assumption that waves reflected from two distinct atomic layers of a Ni crystal will have a fixed phase difference and will interfere either constructively or destructively, producing a diffraction pattern .
- The experiment consisted of a vacuum chamber with an electron gun that emitted a beam of electrons towards a nickel target. The target could be rotated to change the angle of incidence of the electrons. A Faraday cup was used to measure the intensity of the scattered electrons at different angles .
- The experiment was initially performed to study the surface of nickel, but due to an accidental heating of the target, the surface became polycrystalline and showed a diffraction pattern. The experiment was repeated with a monocrystalline target and the same pattern was observed .
- The experiment showed that the intensity of the scattered electrons had a peak at a certain angle, which corresponded to the Bragg condition for X-ray diffraction. This implied that the electrons behaved like waves with a wavelength given by the de Broglie relation .
- The experiment verified the de Broglie equation for the first time and provided experimental evidence for the wave nature of electrons .

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- One possible mnemonic to remember the names of the experimenters is **D**avisson and **G**ermer = **D**iffraction of **G**amma rays (electrons are also called gamma rays).
- Another possible mnemonic to remember the de Broglie relation is **L**ambda = **h** over **p** = **L**ong hair over **p**ants (lambda is the wavelength, h is the Planck constant, and p is the momentum).
- A possible learning trick to understand the diffraction pattern is to imagine the electrons as water waves hitting a row of pebbles. The waves will scatter in different directions and interfere with each other, creating a pattern of bright and dark spots. The angle of the brightest spot depends on the distance between the pebbles and the wavelength of the waves.
