### Fiber optic communication principle for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Fiber optic communication is a method of transmitting information from one place to another by sending pulses of infrared light through an optical fiber.
- The light is a form of carrier wave that is modulated to carry information, such as digital data, voice, or images.
- The optical fiber is a thin, flexible, transparent strand of glass or plastic that can guide light along its length by the principle of total internal reflection .
- Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from a denser medium (such as glass) to a less dense medium (such as air) at an angle greater than the critical angle, which depends on the refractive indices of the media .
- The optical fiber consists of three basic concentric elements: the core, the cladding, and the coating.
- The core is the central region of the fiber where the light is guided. It has a higher refractive index than the surrounding cladding, which is a thin layer of glass or plastic that prevents the light from escaping the core.
- The coating is a protective layer of plastic or polymer that covers the cladding and provides mechanical strength and environmental protection to the fiber.
- The diameter of the core ranges from a few micrometers to a few hundred micrometers, depending on the type and application of the fiber.
- There are two main types of optical fibers: single-mode and multimode.
- Single-mode fibers have a very small core diameter (about 10 micrometers) and can carry only one mode of light, which means that the light travels in a straight line along the fiber with minimal dispersion and attenuation.
- Single-mode fibers are used for long-distance and high-bandwidth applications, such as telecommunications and internet.
- Multimode fibers have a larger core diameter (about 50 to 1000 micrometers) and can carry multiple modes of light, which means that the light travels in different paths along the fiber with different speeds and angles.
- Multimode fibers are used for short-distance and low-bandwidth applications, such as local area networks and medical imaging.
- The advantages of fiber optic communication include: high data transmission rate, low signal loss, immunity to electromagnetic interference, security, flexibility, and durability .
- The disadvantages of fiber optic communication include: high initial cost, need for skilled technicians, susceptibility to physical damage, and difficulty in splicing and connecting .
- The applications of fiber optic communication include: telephone, internet, cable television, medical, defense, government, industrial, and commercial sectors .

- A possible mnemonic to remember the principle of fiber optic communication is: **F**iber **O**ptic **C**ommunication **T**ransmits **I**nfrared **L**ight **T**hrough **T**otal **I**nternal **R**eflection.
- A possible learning trick to understand the difference between single-mode and multimode fibers is: single-mode fibers are like highways with only one lane, while multimode fibers are like roads with multiple lanes. The highways can carry more traffic faster and farther, but they are more expensive and harder to build. The roads can carry less traffic slower and shorter, but they are cheaper and easier to build.
