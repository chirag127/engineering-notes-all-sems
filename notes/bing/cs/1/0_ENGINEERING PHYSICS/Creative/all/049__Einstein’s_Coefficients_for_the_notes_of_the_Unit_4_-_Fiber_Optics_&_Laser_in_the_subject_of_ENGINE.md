### Einstein’s Coefficients for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Einstein’s coefficients are mathematical quantities that measure the probability of absorption or emission of light by an atom or molecule.
- There are three types of Einstein’s coefficients: A, B, and C. They are related to the rate of spontaneous emission, stimulated emission, and absorption of light, respectively .
- The coefficients are determined by the atomic system and do not depend on the state of the gas or the radiation field.
- The coefficients can be derived using Planck’s theory of radiation and Boltzmann’s distribution law under thermal equilibrium.
- The derivation is based on the following assumptions:
  - There are two energy levels E1 and E2 (E2 > E1) in the atom, and the number of atoms per unit volume in each level is N1 and N2, respectively.
  - The atoms are in a radiation field of photons of energy E2 - E1 = hv, where h is Planck’s constant and v is the frequency of light.
  - The energy density of the radiation field is E, and it is constant throughout the space.
  - The atoms can undergo three processes: spontaneous emission, stimulated emission, and absorption of photons.
  - The processes are independent of each other and do not affect the energy density of the radiation field.
- The derivation is as follows:
  - The rate of change of N1 due to absorption is proportional to N1 and E, and the proportionality constant is C12. Therefore, dN1/dt = -C12 N1 E.
  - The rate of change of N1 due to stimulated emission is proportional to N2 and E, and the proportionality constant is C21. Therefore, dN1/dt = C21 N2 E.
  - The rate of change of N1 due to spontaneous emission is proportional to N2, and the proportionality constant is A21. Therefore, dN1/dt = A21 N2.
  - The total rate of change of N1 is the sum of the above three terms. Therefore, dN1/dt = -C12 N1 E + C21 N2 E + A21 N2.
  - Similarly, the total rate of change of N2 is the negative of the above expression. Therefore, dN2/dt = C12 N1 E - C21 N2 E - A21 N2.
  - At thermal equilibrium, the rate of change of N1 and N2 are both zero. Therefore, -C12 N1 E + C21 N2 E + A21 N2 = 0 and C12 N1 E - C21 N2 E - A21 N2 = 0.
  - Solving these equations simultaneously, we get C12 N1 E = C21 N2 E + A21 N2 and C12 N1 = C21 N2.
  - Using Boltzmann’s distribution law, we have N2/N1 = exp(-hv/kT), where k is Boltzmann’s constant and T is the absolute temperature.
  - Substituting this into the above equations, we get C12 E = C21 E exp(-hv/kT) + A21 exp(-hv/kT) and C12 = C21 exp(-hv/kT).
  - Dividing the first equation by the second equation, we get E = A21/C12 + E exp(-hv/kT).
  - Using Planck’s formula for the energy density of the radiation field, we have E = (8πhv3/c3)/(exp(hv/kT) - 1), where c is the speed of light.
  - Equating the above two expressions for E, we get A21/C12 + E exp(-hv/kT) = (8πhv3/c3)/(exp(hv/kT) - 1).

  - These are the relations between Einstein’s coefficients.
