### Introduction to diffraction for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of a wave around the edges of an opening or an obstacle in its path .
- Diffraction occurs with all types of waves, such as sound, light, X-rays, and even matter waves .
- Diffraction is one of the evidences that supports the wave nature of light, along with interference and polarization .
- Diffraction can be explained by the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which states that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the resultant wave at any point is the superposition of these wavelets .
- The amount of diffraction depends on the ratio of the size of the opening or obstacle to the wavelength of the wave. The smaller the ratio, the more the diffraction. The effect of diffraction is only noticeable when the ratio is of the same order of magnitude .
- Diffraction can be classified into two types: Fresnel diffraction and Fraunhofer diffraction. Fresnel diffraction occurs when the source and the screen are at finite distances from the opening or obstacle, and the wavefronts are curved. Fraunhofer diffraction occurs when the source and the screen are at infinite distances from the opening or obstacle, and the wavefronts are plane .
- Some examples of diffraction are: the spreading of light when it passes through a narrow slit, the formation of rainbow by the droplets of water in the air, the diffraction grating used to split light into its component colors, and the electron diffraction used to study the structure of crystals   .

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- To remember the definition of diffraction, use the acronym **BOW**:

  - **B**ending of a wave
  - **O**pening or obstacle
  - **W**avefront

- To remember the types of diffraction, use the acronym **FF**:

  - **F**resnel diffraction: **F**inite distances
  - **F**raunhofer diffraction: in**F**inite distances

- To remember the condition for diffraction, use the phrase **small gap, big diffraction**:

  - The smaller the gap, the bigger the diffraction. The gap should be comparable to the wavelength of the wave.
