### de-Broglie concept of matter waves for the notes of the Unit 1 - Quantum Mechanics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- The de-Broglie concept of matter waves is based on the idea that **all matter exhibits wave-like properties** when it is in motion .
- This concept was proposed by Louis de Broglie in 1924 as a generalization of the wave-particle duality of light .
- According to de Broglie, the wavelength of a matter wave is inversely proportional to its momentum, and is given by the equation:

  \[\lambda = \frac{h}{p}\]

  where \(\lambda\) is the wavelength, \(h\) is the Planck's constant, and \(p\) is the momentum of the particle .

- The de-Broglie concept of matter waves can explain some of the quantum phenomena observed in the microscopic world, such as the **interference and diffraction** of electrons .
- It can also provide a rationale for the **quantization of the angular momentum** of electrons in the Bohr's model of the atom, by assuming that the electrons orbit the nucleus as **standing waves** .
- The de-Broglie concept of matter waves was experimentally verified by the **Davisson-Germer experiment** in 1927, which showed that electrons scattered by a crystal exhibit a diffraction pattern similar to that of X-rays .

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the de-Broglie concept of matter waves are:

- To remember the equation \(\lambda = \frac{h}{p}\), one can use the phrase "**Lamda is high over pee**".
- To remember the relation between the wavelength and the momentum, one can use the analogy of a **car and a truck**. A car has a smaller mass and a higher speed than a truck, so it has a higher momentum and a smaller wavelength. A truck has a larger mass and a lower speed than a car, so it has a lower momentum and a larger wavelength.
- To remember the concept of standing waves, one can use the analogy of a **guitar string**. A guitar string can vibrate in different modes, each with a different frequency and a different number of nodes (points of zero displacement). The lowest frequency mode is called the **fundamental frequency**, and it has only two nodes at the ends of the string. The higher frequency modes are called the **harmonics**, and they have more nodes along the string. The frequency of a mode is proportional to the number of nodes, and the wavelength of a mode is inversely proportional to the number of nodes. Similarly, an electron orbiting the nucleus can have different energy levels, each with a different frequency and a different number of nodes in the wave function. The lowest energy level is called the **ground state**, and it has only two nodes at the opposite sides of the nucleus. The higher energy levels are called the **excited states**, and they have more nodes along the orbit. The energy of a level is proportional to the frequency, and the wavelength of a level is inversely proportional to the number of nodes.
