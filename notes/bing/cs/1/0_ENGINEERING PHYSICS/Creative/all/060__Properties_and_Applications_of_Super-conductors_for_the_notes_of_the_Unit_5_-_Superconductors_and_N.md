### Properties and Applications of Super-conductors for the notes of the Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials: in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- A superconductor is an element or metallic alloy which, when cooled below a certain threshold temperature, the material dramatically loses all electrical resistance.
- In principle, superconductors can allow electrical current to flow without any energy loss (although, in practice, an ideal superconductor is very hard to produce).
- The threshold temperature below which a material becomes superconducting is called the critical temperature (Tc).
- Another important property of a superconductor is its critical magnetic field (Bc), which is the maximum applied magnetic field at a temperature T that will allow a material to remain superconducting.
- Superconductors also exhibit the Meissner effect, which is the expulsion of magnetic fields from the interior of the superconductor as it transitions to the superconducting state.
- Superconductors can be classified into two types: Type I and Type II.
  - Type I superconductors are usually pure metals or simple alloys that have a single critical magnetic field (Bc) and a sharp transition to the superconducting state.
  - Type II superconductors are usually complex alloys or compounds that have two critical magnetic fields (Bc1 and Bc2) and a gradual transition to the superconducting state.
  - Type II superconductors can also form vortices of magnetic flux that penetrate the superconductor in the region between Bc1 and Bc2, creating a mixed state of superconductivity and normal conductivity.
- Superconductors have a wide variety of applications in various fields, such as   :
  - Particle accelerators, generators, and electric motors that use powerful electromagnets made of superconducting coils .
  - Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) machines that use superconducting magnets to create high-resolution images of the human body .
  - Maglev trains that use superconducting magnets to levitate the trains off the track and reduce friction, resulting in high-speed and energy-efficient transportation .
  - Computing and data storage devices that use superconducting circuits and memory cells to achieve faster and more reliable performance .
  - Power transmission and distribution systems that use superconducting cables to transmit electricity over long distances with minimal loss .
  - Medical and scientific instruments that use superconducting sensors and detectors to measure extremely small signals and phenomena .
