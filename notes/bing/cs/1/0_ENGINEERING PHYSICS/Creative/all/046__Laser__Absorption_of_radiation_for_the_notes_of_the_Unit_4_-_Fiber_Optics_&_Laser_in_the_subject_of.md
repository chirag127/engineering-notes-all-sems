### Laser: Absorption of radiation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Absorption of radiation is the process by which an atom or a molecule absorbs a photon of electromagnetic radiation and transitions to a higher energy state.
- Absorption of radiation is one of the three processes that determine the population distribution of atoms or molecules among different energy levels in a laser system. The other two processes are spontaneous emission and stimulated emission.
- Absorption of radiation can be described by the following equation:

  `dN_2/dt = B_12 ρ(ν) N_1 - B_21 ρ(ν) N_2`

  where `N_1` and `N_2` are the number densities of atoms or molecules in the lower and higher energy levels respectively, `B_12` and `B_21` are the Einstein coefficients for absorption and induced emission respectively, `ρ(ν)` is the spectral energy density of the radiation field, and `ν` is the frequency of the radiation.

- Absorption of radiation depends on the following factors:

  - The energy difference between the lower and higher energy levels, which determines the frequency of the radiation that can be absorbed.
  - The spectral energy density of the radiation field, which determines the number of photons available for absorption.
  - The transition probability or cross-section of the absorption process, which determines the likelihood of absorption occurring for a given photon and atom or molecule.
  - The population distribution of atoms or molecules among different energy levels, which determines the availability of absorbers and emitters.

- Absorption of radiation can be classified into two types: homogeneous and inhomogeneous.

  - Homogeneous absorption occurs when all the atoms or molecules have the same energy levels and transition probabilities, and the radiation field has a uniform spectral energy density. In this case, the absorption coefficient is constant and independent of the frequency of the radiation.
  - Inhomogeneous absorption occurs when there is a variation in the energy levels, transition probabilities, or spectral energy density of the atoms, molecules, or radiation field. In this case, the absorption coefficient is a function of the frequency of the radiation and may exhibit peaks or dips at certain frequencies.

- Absorption of radiation plays an important role in the operation of a laser system. It determines the threshold condition for laser action, the gain or loss of the laser medium, and the spectral characteristics of the laser output.

  - The threshold condition for laser action is the minimum pumping rate or input power required to achieve a population inversion between the lower and higher energy levels of the laser medium. This condition depends on the balance between the absorption and emission processes in the laser medium. If the absorption rate is higher than the emission rate, the population inversion cannot be maintained and the laser action cannot occur. If the absorption rate is lower than the emission rate, the population inversion can be achieved and the laser action can occur.
  - The gain or loss of the laser medium is the net rate of change of the photon number or energy in the laser cavity due to the absorption and emission processes in the laser medium. If the gain is positive, the photon number or energy increases and the laser output is amplified. If the gain is negative, the photon number or energy decreases and the laser output is attenuated.
  - The spectral characteristics of the laser output are the frequency, wavelength, bandwidth, and shape of the laser output spectrum. These characteristics depend on the spectral properties of the absorption and emission processes in the laser medium. If the absorption and emission processes are homogeneous, the laser output spectrum is narrow and monochromatic. If the absorption and emission processes are inhomogeneous, the laser output spectrum is broad and polychromatic.

- A mnemonic to remember the factors that affect the absorption of radiation is:

  `FESTA`

  where `F` stands for frequency, `E` stands for energy difference, `S` stands for spectral energy density, `T` stands for transition probability, and `A` stands for population distribution.
