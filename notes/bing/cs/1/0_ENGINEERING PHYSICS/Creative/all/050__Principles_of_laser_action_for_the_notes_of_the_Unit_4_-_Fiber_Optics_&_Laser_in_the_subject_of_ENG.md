### Principles of laser action for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Laser is an acronym for **Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**.
- Laser action is the process of producing a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by stimulating the atoms or molecules of a medium to emit photons in a synchronized manner.
- The basic principles of laser action are :
  - **Spontaneous emission**: This is the natural process of an excited atom or molecule returning to a lower energy level and emitting a photon of a random wavelength, phase, and direction.
  - **Stimulated emission**: This is the process of an excited atom or molecule being stimulated by an incoming photon of the same wavelength and phase as the emitted photon, resulting in the emission of two identical photons in the same direction. This is the key mechanism for amplifying light in a laser.
  - **Stimulated absorption**: This is the process of an atom or molecule in a lower energy level absorbing an incoming photon of the same wavelength and phase as the transition to a higher energy level, resulting in the atom or molecule being excited. This is the opposite of stimulated emission and reduces the intensity of light in a medium.
  - **Non-radiative decay**: This is the process of an excited atom or molecule losing energy by other means than emitting a photon, such as collisions, vibrations, or heat. This is an undesirable process for laser action as it reduces the population of excited atoms or molecules available for stimulated emission.
  - **Population inversion**: This is the condition in which the number of atoms or molecules in an excited energy level is greater than the number of atoms or molecules in a lower energy level. This is necessary for laser action as it ensures that stimulated emission dominates over stimulated absorption in a medium.
  - **Amplification of light**: This is the result of multiple cycles of stimulated emission in a medium, leading to an exponential increase in the number of photons of the same wavelength, phase, and direction. This is achieved by using a resonant cavity or an optical feedback system that reflects the photons back and forth in the medium, allowing them to stimulate more emissions.
- The methods of pumping action are the techniques used to create a population inversion in a medium by supplying energy to the atoms or molecules. Some common methods are:
  - **Optical pumping**: This is the method of using a light source, such as a flash lamp or another laser, to excite the atoms or molecules of a medium to a higher energy level. The light source must have a wavelength that matches the absorption spectrum of the medium.
  - **Electrical pumping**: This is the method of using an electric current or a discharge to excite the atoms or molecules of a medium to a higher energy level. The electric current or discharge must have a voltage that matches the energy gap of the medium.
  - **Chemical pumping**: This is the method of using a chemical reaction to excite the atoms or molecules of a medium to a higher energy level. The chemical reaction must produce a product that has a higher energy level than the reactants.
  - **Nuclear pumping**: This is the method of using a nuclear reaction to excite the atoms or molecules of a medium to a higher energy level. The nuclear reaction must produce a product that has a higher energy level than the reactants and emit gamma rays or neutrons that can be absorbed by the medium.
- A mnemonic to remember the basic principles of laser action is **SASANAP**, which stands for:
  - **S**pontaneous emission
  - **A**mplification of light
  - **S**timulated emission
  - **A**bsorption
  - **N**on-radiative decay
  - **P**opulation inversion
- A learning trick to understand the difference between spontaneous emission and stimulated emission is to imagine a classroom of students. Spontaneous emission is like a student randomly raising their hand and answering a question, while stimulated emission is like a student raising their hand and answering the same question as another student who just answered it. The teacher is like the photon that stimulates the emission, and the answer is like the emitted photon. The more students answer the same question, the more amplified the answer becomes.
