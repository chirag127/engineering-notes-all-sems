### Displacement current for the notes of the Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Displacement current is a phenomenon in electromagnetism that explains how a changing electric field can produce a magnetic field .
- Displacement current is not a real current of moving charges, but a mathematical term that accounts for the rate of change of the electric displacement field.
- The electric displacement field D is related to the electric field E by the equation D = εE, where ε is the permittivity of the medium.
- The displacement current density Jd is defined as the partial derivative of D with respect to time, i.e., Jd = ∂D/∂t.
- The displacement current density has the same units and dimensions as the conduction current density J, which is the current of moving charges per unit area.
- The displacement current density is added to the conduction current density in Ampere's circuital law, which relates the magnetic field B to the total current density Jt = J + Jd .
- The modified Ampere's law is one of the four Maxwell's equations that describe the fundamental laws of electromagnetism  .
- The displacement current allows the existence of electromagnetic waves, which are self-sustaining oscillations of electric and magnetic fields that propagate in free space or in a medium  .
- The displacement current also explains the phenomenon of electromagnetic induction, which is the generation of an electromotive force (emf) in a conductor due to a changing magnetic flux .

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the displacement current are:

- To remember the definition of displacement current density, use the acronym DID: Displacement current density = ∂D/∂t.
- To remember the modified Ampere's law, use the phrase "Ampere's law is JAD": ∮B⋅dl = μ0(J + Jd)⋅dA, where μ0 is the permeability of free space, dl is an infinitesimal element of a closed loop, and dA is an infinitesimal element of a surface bounded by the loop.
- To remember the relation between the electric displacement field and the electric field, use the word DEE: D = εE.
