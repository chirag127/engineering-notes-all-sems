### Necessity of extended sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Wave optics is the branch of optics that considers the behavior of light when it exhibits wave characteristics, particularly when it interacts with small objects.
- Interference is one of the phenomena that demonstrates the wave nature of light. It is the superposition of two or more coherent waves, resulting in a new wave pattern.
- Coherent sources are two sources of light that have a constant phase difference between them. They are necessary for producing a stable interference pattern.
- An extended source is a source of angular size greater than the resolution of the instrument used to observe it. It is said to be resolved when the instrument can distinguish its different parts.
- An extended source is necessary for observing interference in thin films, such as soap bubbles or oil films. This is because a narrow source of light would only produce interference for a small part of the film, and the eye would not be able to see the whole pattern.
- To illustrate this, consider a thin film and a narrow source of light at S (fig.a). For each incident ray E1, E2, we get a pair of parallel interfering rays. The incident ray produces a reflected ray R1 and a transmitted ray T1. The transmitted ray T1 undergoes a second reflection at the lower surface of the film and produces a ray R2. The rays R1 and R2 interfere at the point P on the screen. Similarly, for another incident ray E3, we get another pair of interfering rays R3 and R4. However, these rays do not interfere with R1 and R2, because they are not coherent with them. Therefore, the eye can only see the interference due to one pair of rays at a time, and the whole film appears dark.

fig.a: Interference in thin film with a narrow source

- To overcome this problem, an extended source is used, such as a broad slit or a lens. This way, the rays from different parts of the source can interfere with each other at different points on the screen, and the eye can see the whole film illuminated with different colors (fig.b).

```
         R2 T2  R4 T4
S2  E2          E4
 \ / \         / \
  R5  T5      R7  T7
   \ /  \     /  \ /
    R    \   /    S
         R6 T6  R8 T8
```
fig.b: Interference in thin film with an extended source

- A mnemonic to remember the necessity of extended sources for interference in thin films is: **Narrow source, no show. Extended source, rainbow.**
