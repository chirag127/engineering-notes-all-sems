### Solid state Laser (Ruby laser) and Gas Laser (He-Ne laser) for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- A solid state laser is a laser that uses a gain medium that is a solid, rather than a liquid as in dye lasers or a gas as in gas lasers.
- A ruby laser is a solid state laser that was developed by Maiman in 1960 using ruby as an active medium. Ruby is a crystal of aluminium oxide, in which some of the aluminium ions are replaced by chromium ions.
- A gas laser is a laser that uses a gas as the active medium. Gas lasers can use pure gases or gas mixtures as the lasing material.
- A He-Ne laser is a gas laser that uses a mixture of helium and neon as the active medium. It was the first continuous wave laser and the first laser to operate on the principle of converting electrical energy to a laser light output.

#### Construction and working of ruby laser

- A ruby laser consists of a ruby rod that acts as the active medium, a flash lamp that provides the optical pumping, and a resonant cavity that consists of two mirrors at the ends of the rod .
- The ruby rod is cut and polished into a cylinder with flat and parallel ends that are coated with silver to act as mirrors . One of the mirrors is fully reflective and the other is partially reflective, allowing some light to escape as the laser output .
- The flash lamp is a helical tube that surrounds the ruby rod and produces a bright flash of white light when a high voltage pulse is applied to it . The flash lamp provides the energy to excite the chromium ions in the ruby rod from the ground state to the metastable state .
- The resonant cavity is formed by the two mirrors at the ends of the ruby rod. The cavity allows the light to bounce back and forth between the mirrors, stimulating the emission of more photons from the excited chromium ions .
- When the flash lamp is triggered, the ruby rod absorbs the light and the chromium ions are excited to the metastable state, where they can stay for a long time without decaying . This creates a population inversion, which is a condition where more atoms are in the excited state than in the lower state .
- When a photon of the right wavelength passes through the ruby rod, it can stimulate the emission of another photon from an excited chromium ion, resulting in two identical photons traveling in the same direction . This is called stimulated emission, which is the process that produces the laser light .
- The stimulated emission can occur at two wavelengths: 694.3 nm (red) and 692.9 nm (deep red) . The red wavelength is more favored because it has a lower energy and a longer lifetime than the deep red wavelength .
- The photons that are produced by the stimulated emission can reflect back and forth between the mirrors, creating a positive feedback loop that amplifies the light intensity . Some of the photons can escape through the partially reflective mirror as the laser output .
- The ruby laser is a pulsed laser, meaning that it can only produce short bursts of laser light when the flash lamp is triggered . The duration of the laser pulse depends on the energy of the flash lamp and the length of the ruby rod .
