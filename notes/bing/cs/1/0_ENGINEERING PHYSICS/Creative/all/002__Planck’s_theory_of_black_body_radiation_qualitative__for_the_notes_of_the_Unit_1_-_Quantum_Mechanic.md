### Planck’s theory of black body radiation(qualitative) for the notes of the Unit 1 - Quantum Mechanics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- A black body is a hypothetical body that completely absorbs all radiant energy falling upon it, reaches some equilibrium temperature, and then reemits that energy as quickly as it absorbs it.
- The spectrum of the radiation emitted by a black body depends only on its temperature, not on its shape, size, or material.
- The classical theory of electromagnetism failed to explain the spectral distribution of black body radiation, as it predicted an infinite amount of energy at high frequencies (short wavelengths), which contradicted the experimental observations. This problem was known as the ultraviolet catastrophe.
- In 1900, Max Planck proposed a new theory to explain the black body radiation, based on the assumption that the energy of electromagnetic waves is quantized, i.e., it can only take discrete values that are multiples of a fundamental unit of energy .
- Planck assumed that the black body contains many oscillators (atoms or molecules) that can emit or absorb radiation of different frequencies. He also assumed that the oscillators can only exchange energy with the radiation field in discrete units called quanta, whose energy is proportional to the frequency of the radiation .
- Planck derived a mathematical formula that describes the spectral density of the radiation emitted by a black body at a given temperature, which is known as Planck's law. Planck's law agrees with the experimental data and avoids the ultraviolet catastrophe, as it predicts that the energy of the radiation decreases exponentially at high frequencies .
- Planck's law can be written as:

  $$B(\lambda,T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}}-1}$$

  where $B(\lambda,T)$ is the spectral radiance of the black body at wavelength $\lambda$ and temperature $T$, $h$ is Planck's constant, $c$ is the speed of light, and $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant.
- Planck's theory of black body radiation was one of the first evidences of the quantum nature of light and matter, and it paved the way for the development of quantum mechanics.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for Planck's theory of black body radiation are:

- To remember the formula of Planck's law, you can use the acronym **BELL**:

  $$B(\lambda,T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}}-1}$$

  where **B** stands for black body, **E** stands for exponential, **L** stands for lambda, and **L** stands for light.
- To remember the relation between the frequency and the energy of a quantum of radiation, you can use the phrase **FEE**:

  $$E = hf$$

  where **F** stands for frequency, **E** stands for energy, and **E** stands for equal.
- To remember the shape of the black body radiation curve, you can use the analogy of a **mountain**:

  - The peak of the curve corresponds to the most probable wavelength of the radiation, which is inversely proportional to the temperature of the black body. This is known as Wien's displacement law.
  - The area under the curve corresponds to the total energy emitted by the black body, which is proportional to the fourth power of the temperature. This is known as Stefan-Boltzmann law.
  - The curve shifts to the left (shorter wavelengths) and becomes taller (higher energy) as the temperature increases. This is known as Wien's distribution law.
