## Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser

- Fiber optics are thin strands of glass or plastic that can transmit light signals over long distances by using the principle of total internal reflection.
- Laser is an acronym for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation. It is a device that produces a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by exciting atoms or molecules to a higher energy level and then stimulating them to emit photons in phase.
- Fiber lasers are a type of solid-state lasers that use a doped optical fiber as the gain medium. The fiber core is usually made of silica glass and contains atoms of rare-earth elements, such as erbium or ytterbium, that provide the lasing action.
- Fiber lasers have many advantages over other types of lasers, such as high efficiency, compact size, low maintenance, good beam quality, and wavelength tunability.
- Fiber lasers have many applications in various fields, such as industrial cutting and welding, medical surgery and imaging, telecommunications, sensing, and research.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for this unit are:

- To remember the acronym LASER, think of "Light Amplified by Stimulated Emission of Radiation".
- To remember the difference between erbium and ytterbium, think of "Er-bium is for Er-bium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFAs), which are used for optical communication, while Yt-terbium is for Yt-terbium-doped fiber lasers (YDFLs), which are used for high-power applications".
- To remember the principle of total internal reflection, think of "The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle for the light to be totally reflected inside the fiber".
- To remember the difference between single mode and multimode fibers, think of "Single mode fibers have a small core diameter and can carry only one mode of light, while multimode fibers have a large core diameter and can carry multiple modes of light".
