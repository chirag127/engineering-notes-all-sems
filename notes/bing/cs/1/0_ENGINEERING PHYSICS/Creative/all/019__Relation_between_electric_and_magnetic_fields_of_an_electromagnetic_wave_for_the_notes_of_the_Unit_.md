### Relation between electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave for the notes of the Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- An electromagnetic wave is a type of wave that consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields, which radiate outward from the source at the speed of light.
- The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation of the wave .
- The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave have the same frequency and wavelength, and are in phase with each other.
- The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave obey the following relations :

    - The magnitude of the electric field is proportional to the magnitude of the magnetic field, and the constant of proportionality is the speed of light: `E = cB`, where `E` is the electric field, `B` is the magnetic field, and `c` is the speed of light.
    - The direction of the electric field is given by the cross product of the direction of propagation and the direction of the magnetic field: `E = k x B`, where `k` is the unit vector in the direction of propagation.
    - The direction of the magnetic field is given by the cross product of the direction of the electric field and the direction of propagation: `B = E x k`.

- The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave are related by Maxwell's equations, which describe how a changing electric field generates a magnetic field, and how a changing magnetic field generates an electric field .
- The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave carry energy and momentum, which can be transferred to matter when the wave interacts with it.
- The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave can be polarized, which means that they can have a preferred orientation in space.
- Some examples of electromagnetic waves are radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the relation between electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave is: **E**lectric field is **c**ool and **c**rosses both **B** and **k**, **B**ut **B** is **E**xtra and **E**xcludes **k**.
