### Numerical aperture for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Numerical aperture (NA) is a measure of the ability of an optical fiber to collect or confine the incident light ray inside it. It is among the most basic property of optical fiber. Numerical aperture is abbreviated as NA and shows the efficiency with which light is collected inside the fiber in order to get propagated.
- Numerical aperture is defined as the sine of half of the angle of fiber's light acceptance cone. i.e. NA = sin θa where θa is called acceptance cone angle. The acceptance cone angle is the maximum angle at which the incident light can enter the fiber and still be guided by the core.
- Numerical aperture can also be expressed in terms of the refractive indices of the core and the cladding of the fiber. If n1 is the refractive index of the core and n2 is the refractive index of the cladding, then NA = √(n1^2 - n2^2) . This expression assumes that the incident light is in air (n0 = 1).
- Numerical aperture is an important parameter for optical fiber communication, as it determines the bandwidth and the coupling efficiency of the fiber. A higher NA means a larger acceptance cone angle, which allows more light to enter the fiber and increases the bandwidth. However, a higher NA also means a larger divergence of the light beam exiting the fiber, which reduces the coupling efficiency with other optical devices. Therefore, there is a trade-off between NA and coupling efficiency.
- Numerical aperture can be measured experimentally by using a light source, a detector, and a fiber. The light source is placed at a distance d from the fiber end, and the detector is moved along a circle of radius r around the fiber axis. The spot size of the beam at the detector is recorded as a function of the angle θ between the fiber axis and the detector. The NA can be calculated from the relation NA = r/d sin θ .
- A mnemonic to remember the expression for NA in terms of refractive indices is: NA = "n1 minus n2" (n1 - n2) . This is easy to remember because n1 is always larger than n2 for a fiber, and the difference between them is proportional to the NA. However, this mnemonic is only valid for small NA values, as it neglects the square root and the square terms in the actual expression. For larger NA values, a more accurate mnemonic is: NA = "n1 square minus n2 square" (n1^2 - n2^2) . This is also easy to remember because it is similar to the Pythagorean theorem.
