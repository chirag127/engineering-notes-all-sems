### Step index and graded index fibers for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Step index fiber and graded index fiber are two types of multimode fibers that can transmit multiple modes of light simultaneously.
- A mode is a path that a light ray follows within the fiber. The number of modes depends on the core diameter, the wavelength of light, and the refractive index difference between the core and the cladding.
- The core is the central region of the fiber where the light is confined, and the cladding is the outer layer that surrounds the core and has a lower refractive index to prevent the light from escaping.
- The refractive index is a measure of how much the light bends when it enters a medium. A higher refractive index means that the light bends more and travels slower.

#### Step index fiber

- A step index fiber has a core with a constant refractive index and a sharp transition to the cladding at the core-cladding interface .
- The light rays in a step index fiber can be divided into two types: meridional rays and skew rays.
- Meridional rays are those that cross the fiber axis at each reflection, and skew rays are those that never cross the fiber axis.
- Meridional rays can be further classified into axial rays, which travel along the fiber axis, and non-axial rays, which travel at an angle to the fiber axis.
- Non-axial rays have different path lengths and travel times, depending on their angle of incidence. This causes modal dispersion, which is the spreading of the light pulse due to the different arrival times of the modes  .
- Modal dispersion limits the bandwidth and the distance of the step index fiber, as the pulse becomes distorted and overlaps with the adjacent pulses.
- Step index fiber is cheaper and easier to manufacture than graded index fiber, but it cannot offer higher bandwidth nor longer distances  .
- Step index fiber is mainly used for short-distance applications, such as local area networks (LANs), security systems, and industrial sensors.

#### Graded index fiber

- A graded index fiber has a core with a varying refractive index that decreases gradually from the center to the periphery, and a smooth transition to the cladding at the core-cladding interface .
- The light rays in a graded index fiber are mostly non-axial, and they follow a sinusoidal path within the core.
- The varying refractive index compensates for the different path lengths and travel times of the modes, as the rays that travel closer to the axis experience a higher refractive index and slower speed, and the rays that travel farther from the axis experience a lower refractive index and faster speed  .
- This reduces modal dispersion, as the modes tend to arrive at the same time at the output end of the fiber  .
- Graded index fiber offers superior transmission quality, higher bandwidth, and longer distance than step index fiber  .
- Graded index fiber is more expensive and complex to manufacture than step index fiber, but it is preferred for modern communication applications, such as telephony, cable television, and internet  .
- Graded index fiber is also used for laser power transmission, where it can provide a better output beam profile than step index fiber.
