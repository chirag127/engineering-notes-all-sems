### Spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Spontaneous emission and stimulated emission are two types of processes that involve the emission of photons by atoms or molecules in an excited state.
- Spontaneous emission takes place without interaction with other photons, and the direction and phase are random . It is a natural and irreversible process that occurs after a certain lifetime of the excited state, typically around 10^-8^ seconds. The emitted photons are incoherent and have a broad spectrum.
- Stimulated emission takes place when the excited atom or molecule interacts with another photon that has the same energy as the transition between the excited state and a lower state . It is an induced and reversible process that occurs when the population of the excited state is high enough, which is called population inversion. The emitted photon has the same frequency, phase, polarization, and direction of propagation as the incident photon. The emitted photons are coherent and have a narrow spectrum.
- Spontaneous emission and stimulated emission are the basis of laser operation. A laser is a device that produces a beam of coherent and monochromatic light by amplifying the stimulated emission of photons. A laser consists of three main components: an active medium, a pumping source, and an optical resonator.
- The active medium is the material that provides the atoms or molecules that undergo the emission processes. It can be a gas, a liquid, or a solid. The active medium has at least three energy levels: a ground state, an excited state, and a metastable state. The metastable state is a long-lived state that allows the population inversion to be achieved.
- The pumping source is the external energy source that excites the atoms or molecules in the active medium from the ground state to the excited state. It can be an electric current, a flash lamp, or another laser. The pumping source creates a non-equilibrium situation in which the population of the excited state is higher than the population of the lower states.
- The optical resonator is the structure that confines and reflects the photons in the active medium. It consists of two mirrors, one of which is partially transparent. The optical resonator provides positive feedback for the stimulated emission process, as the photons that are emitted in the direction of the mirrors are reflected back and forth, stimulating more emission. The partially transparent mirror allows some of the photons to escape as the laser output.
- A mnemonic to remember the difference between spontaneous emission and stimulated emission is: **S**pontaneous emission is **S**elf-induced and **S**cattered, while **S**timulated emission is **S**timulated by another photon and **S**ame as the incident photon.
