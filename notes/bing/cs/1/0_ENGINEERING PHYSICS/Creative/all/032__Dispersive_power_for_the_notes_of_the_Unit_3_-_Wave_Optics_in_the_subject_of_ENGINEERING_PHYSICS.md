### Dispersive power for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Dispersive power is a measure of how much a medium separates different wavelengths of light when they pass through it.
- Dispersive power is defined as the ratio of the angular dispersion of two wavelengths to their mean deviation from the undeviated ray.
- Mathematically, dispersive power is given by:

$$
\omega = \frac{\delta \theta}{\Delta \theta} = \frac{\theta_2 - \theta_1}{\theta_m - \theta_0}
$$

where $\delta \theta$ is the angular dispersion, $\Delta \theta$ is the mean deviation, $\theta_2$ and $\theta_1$ are the angles of deviation of the two wavelengths, $\theta_m$ is the angle of deviation of the mean wavelength, and $\theta_0$ is the angle of deviation of the undeviated ray.

- Dispersive power depends only on the material of the medium and not on its shape or size. It also depends on the choice of the two wavelengths used to calculate it.
- Dispersive power is related to the refractive index of the medium and its variation with wavelength. A medium with a higher refractive index and a greater variation with wavelength will have a higher dispersive power.
- Dispersive power is important for understanding the phenomenon of chromatic aberration, which is the distortion of images due to the dispersion of light by lenses or prisms. Chromatic aberration can be reduced by using achromatic lenses or prisms, which are combinations of materials with different dispersive powers that cancel out each other's dispersion effects.
- Dispersive power can also be used to study the dispersion of visible light in optical instruments, such as spectrometers, which use prisms or gratings to separate light into its constituent wavelengths for analysis.

- A mnemonic to remember the formula for dispersive power is:

$$
\omega = \frac{\delta \theta}{\Delta \theta} \sim \frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda}
$$

where $\Delta \lambda$ is the difference between the two wavelengths and $\lambda$ is the mean wavelength. This mnemonic helps to recall that dispersive power is proportional to the relative difference in wavelengths.
