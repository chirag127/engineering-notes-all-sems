### Interference in uniform and wedge shaped thin films for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves of the same frequency and same polarization, resulting in a new wave pattern.
- Interference can be observed in thin films of transparent materials, such as soap bubbles, oil films, or air films between glass plates.
- Thin films have two surfaces, one in contact with air and the other with the material of the film. When light is incident on a thin film, some of it is reflected from the first surface and some of it is transmitted and then reflected from the second surface. The two reflected waves can interfere with each other, depending on the thickness of the film, the wavelength of light, and the refractive indices of the media involved.
- The path difference between the two reflected waves is given by:

  $$\Delta = 2t\cos r$$

  where $t$ is the thickness of the film, $r$ is the angle of refraction in the film, and $\cos r$ is the correction factor for the oblique incidence of light.

- The phase difference between the two reflected waves is given by:

  $$\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta + \delta$$

  where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light in the film, $\Delta$ is the path difference, and $\delta$ is the phase change due to reflection.

- The phase change due to reflection depends on whether the light is reflected from a medium of higher or lower refractive index than the incident medium. If the light is reflected from a medium of higher refractive index, there is a phase change of $\pi$ radians or 180 degrees. If the light is reflected from a medium of lower refractive index, there is no phase change.

- The condition for constructive interference is:

  $$\phi = 2n\pi$$

  where $n$ is an integer.

- The condition for destructive interference is:

  $$\phi = (2n + 1)\pi$$

  where $n$ is an integer.

- A uniform thin film is a film of constant thickness. The interference pattern produced by a uniform thin film is a set of concentric bright and dark rings, called Newton's rings. The radius of the $n$th bright ring is given by:

  $$R_n = \sqrt{n\lambda R}$$

  where $R$ is the radius of curvature of the lens or the mirror used to form the film, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light in air.

- A wedge shaped thin film is a film of varying thickness, such as a film formed by placing a thin piece of paper between two glass plates. The interference pattern produced by a wedge shaped thin film is a set of straight bright and dark fringes, parallel to the edge of the wedge. The fringe width is given by:

  $$\beta = \frac{\lambda}{2\alpha}$$

  where $\alpha$ is the angle of the wedge, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light in air.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for interference in thin films are:

  - To remember the phase change due to reflection, use the acronym **FILM**: **F**rom **I**ncident **L**ower to **M**edium higher, phase change of $\pi$.
  - To remember the condition for constructive and destructive interference, use the acronym **BAND**: **B**right for **A**dding **N**othing, **D**ark for adding $\pi$.
  - To remember the formula for the radius of the $n$th bright ring, use the acronym **RAN**: **R**adius equals **A**verage of **N**umbers. The average of numbers is the arithmetic mean of $n$ and $\lambda R$.
  - To remember the formula for the fringe width of a wedge shaped film, use the acronym **WAL**: **W**idth equals **A**ngle inverse times **L**ambda. The angle inverse is the reciprocal of the angle of the wedge.
