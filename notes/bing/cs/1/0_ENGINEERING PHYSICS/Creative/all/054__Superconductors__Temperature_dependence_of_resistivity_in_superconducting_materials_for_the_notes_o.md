### Superconductors: Temperature dependence of resistivity in superconducting materials for the notes of the Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials: in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Superconductors are materials that have **zero electrical resistance** below a certain **critical temperature** (Tc)  .
- This means that superconductors can carry electric current without any energy loss or heat generation.
- Superconductivity is a **quantum phenomenon** that involves the formation of **Cooper pairs** of electrons that move in a coherent way and avoid scattering by impurities or lattice vibrations .
- The critical temperature of a superconductor depends on its **chemical composition**, **crystal structure**, **pressure**, and **magnetic field** .
- The highest Tc achieved so far is about **288 K** (15 °C) in a hydrogen-rich compound under high pressure .
- Most superconductors have very low Tc values, ranging from a few **millikelvins** to a few **kelvins** .
- There are two types of superconductors: **conventional** and **unconventional** .
- Conventional superconductors are mostly metals or alloys that can be explained by the **BCS theory**, which describes the interaction between electrons and phonons (lattice vibrations) .
- Unconventional superconductors are mostly ceramics or organic compounds that have higher Tc values but cannot be explained by the BCS theory .
- The most studied unconventional superconductors are the **cuprates**, which have a layered structure and can reach Tc values above **100 K** .
- The temperature dependence of resistivity in superconducting materials can be represented by the following graph :

![Temperature dependence of resistivity in superconducting materials](https://semesters.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/temperature-dependence-of-resistivity-in-superconducting-materials.png)

- The graph shows that the resistivity of a superconductor is **constant** at high temperatures, where the material behaves as a **normal conductor**.
- As the temperature is lowered, the resistivity starts to **decrease** gradually, indicating the onset of **superconducting transition**.
- At the critical temperature (Tc), the resistivity drops to **zero**, indicating the complete establishment of **superconductivity**.
- Below Tc, the resistivity remains **zero**, as long as the material is not exposed to a **critical magnetic field** or a **critical current** that can destroy the superconducting state .
- The graph also shows that different superconducting materials have different Tc values, depending on their properties .

  - **S**uperconductors are **S**uper **S**mooth at **S**ub-zero temperatures.
  - **Z**ero resistance means **Z**ero energy loss in **Z**uperconductors.
