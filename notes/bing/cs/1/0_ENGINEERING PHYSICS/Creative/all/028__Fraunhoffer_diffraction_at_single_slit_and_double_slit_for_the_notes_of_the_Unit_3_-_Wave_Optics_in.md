### Fraunhoffer diffraction at single slit and double slit for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Fraunhoffer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when plane waves are incident on a diffracting object, and the diffraction pattern is viewed at a sufficiently long distance from the object (in the far-field region), or at the focal plane of a lens that focuses the diffracted light .
- Fraunhoffer diffraction can be used to analyze the shape and size of the diffracting object, such as a slit, a grating, or an aperture, by measuring the intensity and angular distribution of the diffracted light.
- Fraunhoffer diffraction at a single slit is the simplest case of diffraction, where a narrow slit of width `d` is illuminated by a monochromatic light of wavelength `λ`. The slit acts as a source of secondary waves that interfere with each other to form a diffraction pattern on a screen placed at a large distance from the slit  .
- The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright fringe, flanked by alternating dark and bright fringes of decreasing intensity. The angular position of the nth dark fringe is given by the formula:

```math
\sin\theta_n = \frac{n\lambda}{d}
```

where `n` is an integer, `λ` is the wavelength of the light, and `d` is the width of the slit  .
- The angular width of the central bright fringe is twice that of the other bright fringes, and is given by the formula:

```math
\sin\theta_0 = \frac{\lambda}{d}
```

where `λ` is the wavelength of the light, and `d` is the width of the slit  .
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern is given by the formula:

```math
I(\theta) = I_0 \left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2
```

where `I_0` is the intensity of the incident light, `θ` is the angle from the center of the slit, and `β` is given by:

```math
\beta = \frac{\pi d \sin\theta}{\lambda}
```

where `d` is the width of the slit, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light  .
- Fraunhoffer diffraction at a double slit is a more complex case of diffraction, where two narrow slits of width `a` are separated by a distance `b`, and are illuminated by a monochromatic light of wavelength `λ`. The slits act as sources of secondary waves that interfere with each other to form a diffraction pattern on a screen placed at a large distance from the slits .
- The diffraction pattern consists of a series of bright and dark fringes, modulated by a single slit envelope. The angular position of the nth bright fringe is given by the formula:

```math
\sin\theta_n = \frac{n\lambda}{b}
```

where `n` is an integer, `λ` is the wavelength of the light, and `b` is the distance between the slits .
- The angular width of the bright fringes is given by the formula:

```math
\sin\theta_0 = \frac{\lambda}{a}
```

where `λ` is the wavelength of the light, and `a` is the width of the slits .
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern is given by the formula:

```math
I(\theta) = I_0 \left(\frac{\sin\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^2 \cos^2\beta
```

```math
\alpha = \frac{\pi a \sin\theta}{\lambda}
```
