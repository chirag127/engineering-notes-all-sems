### Fiber optic communication principle

- Fiber optic communication is a method of transmitting information from one place to another by sending pulses of infrared light through an optical fiber.
- The light is a form of carrier wave that is modulated to carry information, such as voice, data, or images.
- The optical fiber is a thin, flexible, transparent strand of glass or plastic that can guide light along its length .
- The optical fiber consists of three basic concentric elements: the core, the cladding, and the coating.
- The core is the central part of the fiber that carries the light signal. It has a higher refractive index than the cladding, which is the outer layer that surrounds the core and reflects the light back into the core.
- The coating is a protective layer that covers the cladding and prevents damage and moisture from affecting the fiber.
- The principle of fiber optic communication is based on total internal reflection, which occurs when light travels from a denser medium to a less dense medium and is reflected back at the interface if the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle .
- The critical angle is the minimum angle of incidence for which total internal reflection occurs. It depends on the refractive indices of the two media.
- In an optical fiber, the core acts as the denser medium and the cladding acts as the less dense medium. The light rays entering the core at a small angle are reflected back and forth by the cladding and travel along the fiber with minimal loss of intensity.
- The advantages of fiber optic communication include:
  - Higher bandwidth and data transmission rate than copper wires or radio waves .
  - Lower attenuation and interference than copper wires or radio waves .
  - Smaller size and weight than copper wires or radio waves .
  - Higher security and reliability than copper wires or radio waves .
  - Lower cost and maintenance than copper wires or radio waves .
- Fiber optic communication is used by telecommunications companies to transmit telephone signals, Internet communication, and cable television signals. It is also used in other industries, such as medical, defense, government, industrial, and commercial .
