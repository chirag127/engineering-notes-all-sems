### Superconductors: Temperature dependence of resistivity in superconducting materials

- Superconductors are materials that have zero electrical resistance below a certain critical temperature (Tc)  .
- The critical temperature varies for different materials, but generally is below 20 K (-253 °C) for conventional superconductors, such as metals and alloys .
- Some ceramic materials, such as cuprates, can exhibit superconductivity at higher temperatures, up to 135 K (-138 °C), and are called high-temperature superconductors .
- The resistivity of materials depends on the temperature, and can be expressed by the equation: ρt = ρ0 [1 + α (T – T0)], where ρt is the resistivity at temperature T, ρ0 is the resistivity at a reference temperature T0, and α is the temperature coefficient of resistivity .
- For superconductors, the resistivity drops abruptly to zero when the temperature is lowered below Tc, as shown in the figure below  .

- The phenomenon of superconductivity is explained by the BCS theory, which states that below Tc, the electrons in the material form pairs called Cooper pairs, which can move without resistance through the lattice of ions  .
- Another characteristic of superconductors is the Meissner effect, which is the expulsion of magnetic fields from the material when it becomes superconducting . This effect can cause a magnet to levitate above a superconducting material, as shown in the figure below .

![Figure: Meissner effect and magnetic levitation](https://phys.libretexts.org/@api/deki/files/1356/Meissner.png)

- Superconductors have many applications in fields such as medicine, transportation, energy, and communication, due to their properties of zero resistance, high current density, and strong magnetic fields  .
