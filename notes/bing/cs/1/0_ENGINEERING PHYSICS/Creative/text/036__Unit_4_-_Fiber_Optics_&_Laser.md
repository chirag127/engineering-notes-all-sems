## Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser

- Fiber optics are thin strands of glass or plastic that can transmit light signals over long distances with minimal loss or distortion.
- Fiber optics are used for various applications such as telecommunications, data transmission, medical imaging, sensing, and illumination.
- Fiber optics have several advantages over conventional copper wires, such as higher bandwidth, lower attenuation, immunity to electromagnetic interference, and lighter weight.
- Fiber optics consist of three main components: the core, the cladding, and the coating. The core is the central part of the fiber that carries the light signal. The cladding is the outer layer of the fiber that surrounds the core and reflects the light back into the core by total internal reflection. The coating is the protective layer that covers the cladding and prevents damage or contamination of the fiber.
- Fiber optics can be classified into two types: single-mode and multi-mode. Single-mode fibers have a very thin core (about 10 micrometers) and can carry only one mode of light, which means that the light travels in a straight line along the fiber axis. Single-mode fibers have lower attenuation and higher bandwidth than multi-mode fibers, but they are more expensive and require more precise alignment of the light source and the fiber. Multi-mode fibers have a thicker core (about 50 to 100 micrometers) and can carry multiple modes of light, which means that the light can travel in different paths within the fiber. Multi-mode fibers have higher attenuation and lower bandwidth than single-mode fibers, but they are cheaper and easier to connect than single-mode fibers.

- Lasers are devices that produce coherent and monochromatic beams of light by stimulating the emission of photons from atoms or molecules.
- Lasers are used for various applications such as cutting, welding, drilling, engraving, printing, scanning, surgery, communication, measurement, and entertainment.
- Lasers have several advantages over conventional light sources, such as higher intensity, higher directionality, higher coherence, and higher purity of color.
- Lasers consist of three main components: the active medium, the pump source, and the optical resonator. The active medium is the material that emits the laser light when excited by the pump source. The active medium can be a gas, a liquid, a solid, or a fiber. The pump source is the energy source that provides the necessary excitation to the active medium. The pump source can be an electric current, a flash lamp, a chemical reaction, or another laser. The optical resonator is the structure that reflects the light back and forth between two mirrors, one of which is partially transparent, and amplifies the light by stimulated emission.
- Lasers can be classified into four types: continuous-wave, pulsed, mode-locked, and Q-switched. Continuous-wave lasers produce a constant beam of light with a fixed wavelength and power. Pulsed lasers produce a series of short bursts of light with a fixed wavelength and variable power. Mode-locked lasers produce a train of ultra-short pulses of light with a fixed wavelength and a very high peak power. Q-switched lasers produce a single pulse of light with a fixed wavelength and a very high peak power.
