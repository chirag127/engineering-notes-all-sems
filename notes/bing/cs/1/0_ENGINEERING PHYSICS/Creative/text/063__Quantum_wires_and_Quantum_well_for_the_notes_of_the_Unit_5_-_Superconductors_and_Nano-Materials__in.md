### Quantum wires and Quantum well

- Quantum wires and quantum wells are examples of **nanostructures** that have one or more dimensions on the order of **nanometers** (10^-9^ m).
- Quantum wires and quantum wells are made of **semiconductor materials** that have different **band gaps**. A band gap is the energy difference between the highest occupied and the lowest unoccupied electronic states in a solid.
- Quantum wires and quantum wells are created by **embedding** a lower band gap material inside a higher band gap material, forming a **heterostructure**. This creates a **potential barrier** that confines the electrons in the lower band gap material to a certain region.
- Quantum wires and quantum wells exhibit **quantum confinement effects**, which means that the electrons can only occupy certain discrete energy levels, depending on the size and shape of the nanostructure. This affects the **electronic, optical, and transport properties** of the nanostructure, such as the current, voltage, resistance, capacitance, luminescence, absorption, etc.
- Quantum wells are **two-dimensional** (2D) nanostructures, where the electrons are confined in one direction, but free to move in the other two directions. Quantum wells have a **rectangular** cross-section and a **planar** geometry. Quantum wells are used for making **lasers**, **light-emitting diodes**, **photodetectors**, **solar cells**, etc.
- Quantum wires are **one-dimensional** (1D) nanostructures, where the electrons are confined in two directions, but free to move in one direction. Quantum wires have a **cylindrical** cross-section and a **linear** geometry. Quantum wires are used for making **nanoelectronic devices**, such as **transistors**, **switches**, **logic gates**, **interconnects**, etc.
