### Temperature dependence of critical field

- The critical field is the external magnetic field that can destroy the superconducting state of a material.
- The critical field depends on the temperature of the material, as well as its intrinsic properties.
- The critical field decreases as the temperature increases, and becomes zero at the critical temperature, which is the highest temperature at which the material can exhibit superconductivity.
- The temperature dependence of the critical field can be expressed by the empirical formula:

$$H_c(T) = H_c(0) \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{T}{T_c} \right)^2 \right]$$

- Where $H_c(T)$ is the critical field at temperature $T$, $H_c(0)$ is the critical field at absolute zero, and $T_c$ is the critical temperature of the material.
- The formula is valid for low-temperature superconductors, which have a critical temperature below 30 K.
- For high-temperature superconductors, which have a critical temperature above 30 K, the formula is not accurate, and more complex models are needed to describe the temperature dependence of the critical field.
