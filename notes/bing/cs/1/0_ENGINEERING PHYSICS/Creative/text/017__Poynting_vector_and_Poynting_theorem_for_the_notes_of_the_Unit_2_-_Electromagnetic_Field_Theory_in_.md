### Poynting vector and Poynting theorem

- Poynting vector is a vector quantity that represents the directional energy flux (the energy transfer per unit area per unit time) of an electromagnetic field.
- Poynting vector is defined as the cross product of the electric field vector E and the magnetic field vector H, that is, **S = E x H** .
- Poynting vector is named after its discoverer, J.H. Poynting, who derived it in 1884 .
- Poynting vector has the units of watts per square meter (W/m^2^) and is a measure of the power density of the electromagnetic field.
- Poynting vector is used in conjunction with Poynting's theorem, which is the continuity equation expressing the conservation of electromagnetic energy.
- Poynting's theorem states that the net electromagnetic power flowing into a region of space may be either dissipated, or used to change the energy stored in electric and magnetic fields within that region.
- Poynting's theorem can be written as **∇ · S = - ∂u/∂t - J · E**, where S is the Poynting vector, u is the energy density of the electromagnetic field, J is the current density, and E is the electric field .
- Poynting's theorem can be derived from Maxwell's equations and the Lorentz force law, and it is valid in any medium, linear or nonlinear, isotropic or anisotropic, homogeneous or inhomogeneous .
- Poynting's theorem implies that the electromagnetic field carries both energy and momentum, and that the energy and momentum can be transferred to matter by the action of the electric and magnetic forces.
