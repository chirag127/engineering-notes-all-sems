### Spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation

- Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state decays to a lower energy state by emitting a photon of energy without the aid of external agency.
- Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state decays to a lower energy state by emitting a photon of energy when it interacts with another photon of the same frequency, phase, polarization, and direction .
- The properties of photons generated by spontaneous and stimulated emission are quite different:
  - Spontaneous emission takes place randomly and the emitted photons are incoherent, i.e., they have different frequencies, phases, polarizations, and directions .
  - Stimulated emission takes place in a non-random manner and the emitted photons are coherent, i.e., they have the same frequency, phase, polarization, and direction as the incident photons .
- Spontaneous and stimulated emission are important processes for the operation of lasers:
  - Spontaneous emission provides the initial photons for the laser action.
  - Stimulated emission amplifies the photons and produces a coherent beam of light.
- The rate of spontaneous and stimulated emission depends on the population of the excited state and the intensity of the incident radiation.
- Einstein coefficient relations describe the relation between the rate of spontaneous and stimulated emission and the rate of absorption of radiation by the atoms or molecules.
