### Population inversion

- Population inversion is a condition in which more atoms or molecules of a system are in higher, excited states than in lower, unexcited states.
- Population inversion is necessary for laser or maser action to occur, as it allows stimulated emission to dominate over absorption .
- Normally, a system of atoms is in thermal equilibrium and follows the Boltzmann distribution, which means that there are always more atoms in low energy states than in higher ones  .
- To achieve population inversion, a system must be supplied with energy from an external source, such as an electric field, a flash lamp, or a chemical reaction .
- The energy input must be sufficient to overcome the natural tendency of the system to relax to the lower energy states through spontaneous emission or collisions .
- There are different methods to create population inversion, such as three-level, four-level, or cascade pumping schemes, depending on the number and arrangement of the energy levels involved .
- Population inversion is a non-equilibrium and unstable situation, and it can only be maintained for a short time before the system reaches a new equilibrium state .
