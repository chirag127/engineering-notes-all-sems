### Skin depth

- Skin depth is a measure of how deep an electromagnetic wave can penetrate into a conducting material.
- Skin depth depends on the frequency of the wave, the conductivity of the material, and the permeability of the material.
- Skin depth is defined as the distance at which the amplitude of the wave decreases to 1/e (about 37%) of its original value.
- Skin depth can be calculated by the formula:

$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega \mu \sigma}}$$

where $\delta$ is the skin depth, $\omega$ is the angular frequency of the wave, $\mu$ is the permeability of the material, and $\sigma$ is the conductivity of the material.
- Skin depth is inversely proportional to the square root of the frequency, the permeability, and the conductivity. This means that higher frequency waves, more magnetic materials, and more conductive materials have smaller skin depths.
- Skin depth is important for applications such as radio transmission, microwave ovens, induction heating, and electromagnetic shielding.
