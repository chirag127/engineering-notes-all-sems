### Einstein’s Coefficients

- Einstein’s coefficients are mathematical quantities that describe the probability of absorption or emission of light by an atom or molecule.
- There are three types of Einstein’s coefficients: A, B and C.
- The Einstein A coefficient is related to the rate of spontaneous emission of light, which is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state emitting a photon and returning to a lower energy state without any external influence.
- The Einstein B coefficient is related to the absorption and stimulated emission of light, which are the processes of an atom or molecule in a lower energy state absorbing a photon and jumping to a higher energy state, or an atom or molecule in an excited state emitting a photon and returning to a lower energy state due to the presence of an external radiation field.
- The Einstein C coefficient is related to the induced emission of light, which is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state emitting a photon and returning to a lower energy state due to the presence of an external electric field.
- The Einstein coefficients are fixed probabilities per time associated with each atom or molecule, and do not depend on the state of the gas of which the atoms or molecules are a part.
- The Einstein coefficients can be derived from the quantum theory of radiation and matter, and they satisfy certain relations that ensure the consistency of thermodynamics and statistics.
- The Einstein coefficients are important for understanding the spectral properties of atoms and molecules, and they are widely used in the fields of atomic physics, molecular physics, spectroscopy, astrophysics, and laser physics .
