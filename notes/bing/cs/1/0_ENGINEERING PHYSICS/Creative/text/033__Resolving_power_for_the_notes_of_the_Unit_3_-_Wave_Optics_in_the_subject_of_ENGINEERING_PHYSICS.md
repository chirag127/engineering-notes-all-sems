### Resolving power

- Resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to distinguish between two closely spaced objects or sources of light.
- Resolving power depends on the resolution of the instrument, which is the minimum distance between two points that can be seen as separate by the observer or the detector.
- Resolving power is inversely proportional to the wavelength of light and directly proportional to the diameter of the aperture or the objective lens of the instrument.
- Resolving power can be expressed by different formulas for different optical instruments, such as telescope, microscope, prism and grating.
- Resolving power of a telescope is given by: R = d / (1.22 λ), where d is the diameter of the objective lens, λ is the wavelength of light and 1.22 is a constant factor.
- Resolving power of a microscope is given by: R = 2 n sin θ / λ, where n is the refractive index of the medium, θ is the half-angle of the cone of light entering the objective lens and λ is the wavelength of light.
- Resolving power of a prism is given by: R = λ / (dλ), where λ is the wavelength of the spectral line, dλ is the difference between the wavelengths of two adjacent spectral lines and d is the dispersive power of the prism.
- Resolving power of a grating is given by: R = N m, where N is the total number of lines on the grating, m is the order of diffraction and λ is the wavelength of light.
