### High temperature superconductors

- High temperature superconductors are materials that behave as superconductors at temperatures above 77 K (−196.2 °C; −321.1 °F), the boiling point of liquid nitrogen.
- Superconductors are materials that have zero electrical resistance and can carry electric current without any loss of energy.
- Superconductivity is a quantum phenomenon that arises from the formation of Cooper pairs, which are pairs of electrons that move in sync and avoid scattering by the lattice vibrations or impurities in the material.
- The main class of high temperature superconductors is copper oxides combined with other metals, especially the rare-earth barium copper oxides (REBCOs) such as yttrium barium copper oxide (YBCO).
- The second class of high temperature superconductors is the iron-based compounds, such as iron pnictides and iron chalcogenides.
- The mechanism of high temperature superconductivity is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve strong electron-electron interactions, spin fluctuations, and unconventional pairing symmetries.
- The highest critical temperature (Tc) achieved so far in a high temperature superconductor is 138 K (−135 °C; −211 °F) in a mercury-based cuprate under high pressure.
- In 2023, scientists claimed to have discovered a room-temperature superconductor, a hydrogen-sulfur-carbon compound that exhibited superconductivity at 287 K (14 °C; 57 °F) under 267 gigapascals of pressure  .
- Room-temperature superconductivity, if confirmed and reproducible, would have enormous implications for energy transmission, transportation, computing, and other applications .
- However, the claim of room-temperature superconductivity is still controversial and has not been independently verified by other researchers .
