### Meissner effect

- The Meissner effect is the phenomenon of expulsion of magnetic flux from a superconducting material when it is cooled below its critical temperature.
- The Meissner effect is a consequence of the perfect diamagnetism of superconductors, which means that they have zero magnetic susceptibility and oppose any external magnetic field.
- The Meissner effect can be explained by the London theory of superconductivity, which states that the superconducting electrons form a thin layer near the surface of the material that cancels out the external magnetic field inside the bulk of the material.
- The Meissner effect is a characteristic property of superconductors and distinguishes them from normal conductors, which can only partially exclude magnetic fields due to eddy currents.
- The Meissner effect can be observed by levitating a magnet above a superconducting material or by measuring the magnetic flux through a superconducting ring.
