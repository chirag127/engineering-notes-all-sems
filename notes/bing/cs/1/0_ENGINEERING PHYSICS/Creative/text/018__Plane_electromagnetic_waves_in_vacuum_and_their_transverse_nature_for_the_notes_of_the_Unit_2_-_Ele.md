### Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a given plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields that are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave can be represented by the following equations:

$$\vec{E} = \vec{E}_0 \cos(\omega t - kx) \hat{j}$$

$$\vec{B} = \vec{B}_0 \cos(\omega t - kx) \hat{k}$$

- Here, $\vec{E}_0$ and $\vec{B}_0$ are the amplitudes of the electric and magnetic fields, $\omega$ is the angular frequency, $k$ is the wave number, $x$ is the position along the direction of propagation, and $\hat{j}$ and $\hat{k}$ are the unit vectors along the $y$ and $z$ axes, respectively.
- The speed of a plane electromagnetic wave in vacuum is given by:

$$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_0 \mu_0}}$$

- Here, $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space and $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space. The value of $c$ is approximately $3 \times 10^8$ m/s.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves means that the electric and magnetic fields are always perpendicular to the direction of propagation and to each other. This can be shown by applying Gauss's law for electric and magnetic fields to a plane electromagnetic wave.
- Gauss's law for electric fields states that the net electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the net electric charge enclosed by the surface divided by $\epsilon_0$. For a plane electromagnetic wave, there is no electric charge enclosed by any surface, so the net electric flux must be zero. This implies that the electric field must have zero component along the direction of propagation, otherwise there would be a net flux through a cylindrical surface with its axis along the direction of propagation. Therefore, the electric field must be perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- Gauss's law for magnetic fields states that the net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero. For a plane electromagnetic wave, this implies that the magnetic field must have zero component along the direction of propagation, otherwise there would be a net flux through a cylindrical surface with its axis along the direction of propagation. Therefore, the magnetic field must be perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- Since the electric and magnetic fields are both perpendicular to the direction of propagation, they must also be perpendicular to each other, otherwise they would have a non-zero component along the direction of propagation. Therefore, the electric and magnetic fields are transverse to the direction of propagation and to each other.
