## Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials:

- Superconductors are materials that have zero electrical resistance and perfect diamagnetism when cooled below a certain critical temperature.
- Superconductivity is a quantum phenomenon that involves the formation of Cooper pairs of electrons that can move through the material without scattering or losing energy.
- Superconductors have many applications, such as magnetic levitation, MRI, particle accelerators, quantum computing, and power transmission.
- There are two types of superconductors: conventional and unconventional. Conventional superconductors are explained by the BCS theory, which states that the Cooper pairs are formed by the attraction between electrons mediated by phonons (vibrations of the lattice). Unconventional superconductors are not fully understood, but they involve other mechanisms of pairing, such as spin fluctuations or electron-electron interactions.
- The critical temperature of a superconductor depends on various factors, such as the material composition, structure, doping, pressure, and magnetic field. The highest critical temperature achieved so far is 138 K for a hydrogen sulfide compound under high pressure.
- Nano-materials are materials that have at least one dimension in the nanometer range (1-100 nm). They exhibit novel physical, chemical, and biological properties that differ from their bulk counterparts due to the quantum confinement, surface effects, and self-assembly phenomena.
- Nano-materials have many applications, such as nanoelectronics, nanomedicine, nanocatalysis, nanosensors, and nanocomposites.
- There are various methods to synthesize nano-materials, such as top-down and bottom-up approaches. Top-down methods involve breaking down larger materials into smaller ones, such as lithography, milling, or etching. Bottom-up methods involve building up nano-materials from smaller units, such as chemical vapor deposition, sol-gel, or self-assembly.
- Some examples of nano-materials are carbon nanotubes, graphene, quantum dots, nanoparticles, nanowires, and nanofibers.
