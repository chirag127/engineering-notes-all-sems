### Displacement current

- Displacement current is a phenomenon in electromagnetism that is analogous to an ordinary electric current .
- Displacement current is defined as the rate of change of the electric displacement field D with respect to time.
- Displacement current density Jd is given by the equation Jd = ∂D/∂t, where ∂ is the partial derivative operator.
- Displacement current density has the same units as electric current density, and it is a source of the magnetic field just as actual current is.
- Displacement current was introduced by James Clerk Maxwell in his famous set of equations that describe the laws of electromagnetism .
- Maxwell added the displacement current term to Ampere's circuital law, which relates the magnetic field around a closed loop to the electric current passing through the loop .
- Maxwell's modified version of Ampere's law is given by the equation ∇ × H = J + ∂D/∂t, where ∇ × is the curl operator, H is the magnetic field intensity, and J is the conduction current density .
- Through the displacement current, a time-varying electric flux may be a source of the magnetic field. In other words, the electric and magnetic fields are coupled when the electric flux is time-varying.
- Displacement current is essential for the existence of electromagnetic waves, which are propagating oscillations of electric and magnetic fields .
- Displacement current allows the continuity of the magnetic field across a capacitor, where there is no conduction current  .
