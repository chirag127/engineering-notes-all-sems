### de-Broglie concept of matter waves

- According to the de-Broglie concept of matter waves, the matter has **dual nature**. It means when the matter is moving it shows the **wave properties** (like interference, diffraction etc.) and when it is in the state of rest then it shows **particle properties**.
- This concept was proposed by **Louis de Broglie** in 1924, inspired by the **photon theory** of Albert Einstein, which established that an electromagnetic wave can behave like a particle of light when interacting with matter .
- de Broglie suggested that the **wavelength** of the matter wave is inversely proportional to its **momentum**, and he derived the following equation:

    \[\lambda = \frac{h}{p}\]

    where \(\lambda\) is the wavelength, \(h\) is the Planck's constant, and \(p\) is the momentum of the particle .

- de Broglie also applied the concept of matter waves to the structure of the atom. He postulated that electrons orbit the nucleus as **standing waves** (stationary waves). Standing waves are formed as a result of interference between two waves of equal frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions.
- de Broglie's hypothesis of matter waves was experimentally verified by **Davisson and Germer** in 1927, who observed the diffraction of electrons by a nickel crystal. The wavelength of the electrons matched the value predicted by de Broglie's equation .
- de Broglie's concept of matter waves has important implications for the **quantum mechanics** of microscopic particles, such as electrons, protons, neutrons, atoms and molecules. It shows that these particles have both wave-like and particle-like properties, and that their behavior cannot be fully described by classical physics .
