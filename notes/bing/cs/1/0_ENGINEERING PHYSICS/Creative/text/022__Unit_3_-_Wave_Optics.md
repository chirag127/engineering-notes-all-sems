## Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also called physical optics, is a branch of optics that deals with the behavior of light and its wave characteristics.
- Wave optics is useful for studying phenomena such as polarization, diffraction, interference and other occurrences where ray approximation of geometric optics cannot be done .
- Some of the important concepts and formulas in wave optics are:

  - Huygens' principle: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets that spread out in all directions with the same speed as the wave. The new wavefront is the envelope of the secondary wavelets.
  - Superposition principle: When two or more waves overlap in a medium, the resultant displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of the displacements due to each wave.
  - Interference: The phenomenon of superposition of two or more coherent waves to produce a resultant wave of varying intensity.
  - Young's double slit experiment: An experiment that demonstrates the interference of light waves from two coherent sources (slits) separated by a small distance. The interference pattern consists of alternate bright and dark fringes on a screen.
  - Path difference: The difference in the distances traveled by two waves from their sources to a point on the screen.
  - Phase difference: The difference in the phases of two waves at a point on the screen.
  - Condition for constructive interference: The path difference or the phase difference between the two waves is an integral multiple of the wavelength or 2π radians, respectively.
  - Condition for destructive interference: The path difference or the phase difference between the two waves is an odd multiple of half the wavelength or π radians, respectively.
  - Fringe width: The distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes on the screen.
  - Fringe width formula: β = λD/d, where λ is the wavelength of light, D is the distance between the slits and the screen, and d is the distance between the slits.
  - Diffraction: The phenomenon of bending of light waves around the edges of an obstacle or an aperture.
  - Single slit diffraction: The diffraction of light waves from a single narrow slit. The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright maximum and several secondary maxima and minima on either side.
  - Condition for minima in single slit diffraction: a sin θ = nλ, where a is the width of the slit, θ is the angle of diffraction, n is an integer, and λ is the wavelength of light.
  - Polarization: The phenomenon of restricting the vibrations of light waves to a particular plane.
  - Methods of polarization: Reflection, refraction, scattering, and selective absorption.
  - Brewster's law: The law that states that the angle of incidence for which the reflected light is completely polarized is equal to the polarizing angle, and the sum of the polarizing angle and the angle of refraction is 90°.
  - Brewster's law formula: tan ip = n, where ip is the polarizing angle and n is the refractive index of the medium.
  - Malus' law: The law that states that the intensity of polarized light transmitted through an analyzer is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle between the planes of polarization of the polarizer and the analyzer.
  - Malus' law formula: I = I0 cos2 θ, where I is the transmitted intensity, I0 is the incident intensity, and θ is the angle between the planes of polarization.
