### Fraunhofer diffraction at single slit and double slit

- Fraunhofer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when plane waves are incident on a diffracting object, and the diffraction pattern is observed at a sufficiently long distance from the object or at the focal plane of a lens .
- Fraunhofer diffraction is also known as far-field diffraction, because the distance from the object to the observation point is much larger than the wavelength of the incident light .
- Fraunhofer diffraction can be analyzed using the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which states that every point on a wavefront acts as a secondary source of spherical waves, and the resulting wave at any point is the superposition of these secondary waves .
- Fraunhofer diffraction can be used to study the shape and size of the diffracting object, such as a slit, a grating, a circular aperture, etc. .

#### Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit

- A single slit is a narrow opening of width d that allows light to pass through .
- When a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength λ is incident on a single slit, the light is diffracted and forms a pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen or a lens .
- The bright fringes are called maxima, and the dark fringes are called minima .
- The central bright fringe is the widest and the brightest, and the intensity of the fringes decreases as they move away from the center .
- The angular position of the nth bright fringe is given by :

  `d sin θ_n = n λ`,

  where n is an integer.

- The angular position of the nth dark fringe is given by :

  `d sin θ_n = (n + 1/2) λ`,

  where n is an integer.

- The angular width of the central bright fringe is given by :

  `β = 2 θ_1 = 2 sin^(-1) (λ / d)`.

- The intensity of the bright fringes is given by :

  `I = I_0 (sin (π d sin θ / λ) / (π d sin θ / λ))^2`,

  where I_0 is the intensity of the incident light.

#### Fraunhofer diffraction at a double slit

- A double slit is a pair of narrow openings of width a that are separated by a distance d .
- When a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength λ is incident on a double slit, the light is diffracted and forms an interference pattern of bright and dark fringes on a screen or a lens .
- The bright fringes are called maxima, and the dark fringes are called minima .
- The interference pattern is modulated by the diffraction pattern of a single slit of width d .
- The angular position of the nth bright fringe is given by :

  `d sin θ_n = n λ`,

  where n is an integer.

- The angular position of the nth dark fringe is given by :

  `d sin θ_n = (n + 1/2) λ`,

  where n is an integer.

- The angular width of the central bright fringe is given by :

  `β = 2 θ_1 = 2 sin^(-1) (λ / d)`.

- The intensity of the bright fringes is given by :

  `I = I_0 (sin (π d sin θ / λ) / (π d sin θ / λ))^2 (cos (π a sin θ / λ))^2`,

  where I_0 is the intensity of the incident light.

- The visibility of the interference pattern is given by :

  `V = (I_max - I_min) / (I_max + I_min) = (sin (π a sin θ / λ) / (π a sin θ / λ))^2`,

  where I_max and I_min are the maximum and minimum intensities, respectively.
