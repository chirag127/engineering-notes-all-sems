### Newton’s Rings and its applications

Newton’s rings are a phenomenon of interference of light that occurs when a plano-convex lens is placed on a flat glass plate, forming a thin air film between them. The thickness of the air film varies from zero at the point of contact to some value depending on the curvature of the lens. When a monochromatic light source is placed above the lens, an interference pattern of bright and dark rings is observed on the glass plate. The radius of the rings depends on the wavelength of the light and the thickness of the air film.

Some of the applications of Newton’s rings are:

- For testing the uniformity of a polished surface by studying the interference pattern the surface makes when placed in contact with a perfectly flat glass surface . If the surface is not uniform, the rings will be distorted or irregular.
- For controlling the thickness of paint that is used on posters. Too much paint would exceed total weight requirements, and too little paint would not cover the poster adequately. By observing the interference pattern of the paint film on a flat surface, the optimal thickness can be determined.
- For measuring the refractive index of liquids by placing a drop of the liquid between the lens and the glass plate. The refractive index can be calculated from the change in the radius of the rings due to the presence of the liquid.
- For determining the wavelength of monochromatic light sources by measuring the radius of the rings and using the formula:

$$r_n^2 = R(n\lambda + \frac{\lambda}{2})$$

where $r_n$ is the radius of the $n$th ring, $R$ is the radius of curvature of the lens, $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light, and the factor $\frac{\lambda}{2}$ accounts for the phase change of $\pi$ due to reflection at the lens surface.
