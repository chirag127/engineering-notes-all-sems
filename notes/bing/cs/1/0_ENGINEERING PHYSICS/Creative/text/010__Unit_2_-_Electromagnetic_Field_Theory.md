## Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory

- Electromagnetic field theory is the study of the interaction between electric and magnetic fields, and the sources and effects of these fields.
- Electromagnetic fields are produced by electric charges, either stationary or moving, and can exert forces on other charges and magnetic materials.
- Electromagnetic fields can also be influenced by the presence and motion of matter, which can have electric and magnetic properties.
- Electromagnetic fields can propagate through space as electromagnetic waves, which carry energy and information, and can interact with matter and other waves.
- Electromagnetic field theory is based on the following fundamental concepts and laws:
  - Coulomb's law: The electric force between two point charges is proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
  - Gauss's law: The electric flux through a closed surface is equal to the net charge enclosed by the surface divided by the permittivity of free space.
  - Ampere's law: The magnetic flux through a closed loop is equal to the product of the permeability of free space and the net current passing through the loop.
  - Faraday's law: The electromotive force induced in a loop is equal to the negative rate of change of the magnetic flux through the loop.
  - Biot-Savart law: The magnetic field at a point due to a current element is proportional to the product of the current and the length of the element, and inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the element to the point.
  - Lorentz force law: The force on a charge moving in an electric and magnetic field is equal to the product of the charge and the sum of the electric field and the cross product of the velocity and the magnetic field.
  - Maxwell's equations: A set of four partial differential equations that relate the electric and magnetic fields to their sources and effects, and describe how they propagate as electromagnetic waves. They are:
    - Gauss's law for electricity: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$
    - Gauss's law for magnetism: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$
    - Faraday's law of induction: $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$
    - Ampere's law with Maxwell's correction: $\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$
  - Where $\mathbf{E}$ is the electric field, $\mathbf{B}$ is the magnetic field, $\rho$ is the charge density, $\mathbf{J}$ is the current density, $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space, and $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space.
- Electromagnetic field theory has many applications in physics, engineering, and technology, such as electric circuits, antennas, motors, generators, transformers, lasers, optical fibers, radar, radio, television, wireless communication, and medical imaging.
