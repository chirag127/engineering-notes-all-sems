### Necessity of extended sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Wave optics, also known as physical optics, is the branch of physics that studies the interference, diffraction, polarization, and other phenomena of light waves.
- Wave optics is based on the principle of superposition, which states that when two or more waves overlap, the resulting wave is the sum of the individual waves.
- Wave optics can explain many phenomena that cannot be explained by ray optics, such as the colors of thin films, the diffraction patterns of slits and gratings, and the polarization of light by reflection and transmission.
- Wave optics requires the use of extended sources, which are sources that emit light waves over a finite area, rather than point sources, which are idealized sources that emit light waves from a single point.
- The use of extended sources is necessary for wave optics because point sources do not produce interference or diffraction effects, since the light waves from a point source are coherent, meaning they have the same frequency, phase, and direction.
- Extended sources, on the other hand, produce interference and diffraction effects, since the light waves from different parts of the source are incoherent, meaning they have different frequencies, phases, and directions.
- Extended sources can be either monochromatic, meaning they emit light of a single wavelength, or polychromatic, meaning they emit light of a range of wavelengths.
- Monochromatic extended sources can produce interference and diffraction patterns that depend on the wavelength of the light and the geometry of the source and the obstacle.
- Polychromatic extended sources can produce interference and diffraction patterns that depend on the spectrum of the light and the dispersion of the medium.
- Extended sources can be either coherent, meaning they emit light waves that have a fixed phase relationship, or incoherent, meaning they emit light waves that have a random phase relationship.
- Coherent extended sources can produce stable interference and diffraction patterns that do not change with time.
- Incoherent extended sources can produce transient interference and diffraction patterns that change with time.
- Coherent extended sources can be obtained by using lasers, which are devices that produce intense and narrow beams of monochromatic and coherent light, or by using optical devices, such as mirrors, lenses, and filters, that modify the light waves from an incoherent source.
- Incoherent extended sources can be obtained by using natural sources, such as the sun, stars, and flames, or by using artificial sources, such as light bulbs, LEDs, and screens, that emit light waves with a broad spectrum and a random phase.
