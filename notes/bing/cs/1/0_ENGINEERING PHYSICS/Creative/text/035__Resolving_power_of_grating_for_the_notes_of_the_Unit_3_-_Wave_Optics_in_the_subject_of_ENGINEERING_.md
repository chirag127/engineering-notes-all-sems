### Resolving power of grating

- Resolving power of grating is the ability of a grating to separate two spectral lines of very close wavelengths.
- The resolving power of grating is given by the formula:

  $$R = \frac{\lambda}{\Delta \lambda} = Nm$$

  where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the incident light, $\Delta \lambda$ is the minimum difference in wavelength that can be resolved, $N$ is the total number of slits in the grating, and $m$ is the order of diffraction.
- The resolving power of grating depends on the number of slits and the order of diffraction. It does not depend on the slit width or the grating constant.
- The resolving power of grating is maximum when the incident light is normal to the grating and the spectral lines are observed at the center of the diffraction pattern.
- The resolving power of grating is higher than that of a prism or a single slit, because the grating produces a large number of interference maxima in a narrow angular range.
