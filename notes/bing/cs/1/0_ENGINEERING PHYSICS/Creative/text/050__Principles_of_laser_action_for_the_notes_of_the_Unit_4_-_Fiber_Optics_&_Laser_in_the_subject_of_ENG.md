### Principles of laser action

- Laser is an acronym for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.
- Laser action is the process of producing a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by stimulating atoms or molecules to emit photons of the same frequency, phase, and direction.
- Laser action requires three basic conditions:
  - A suitable medium that can be excited to higher energy levels and emit photons when returning to lower levels. This medium can be solid, liquid, gas, or plasma.
  - A mechanism of pumping or excitation that provides energy to the medium and creates a population inversion, which means more atoms or molecules are in the higher energy level than in the lower one.
  - A resonant cavity or optical feedback that reflects the emitted photons back and forth through the medium, causing more stimulated emission and amplification of the light.
- Laser action can be explained by the following steps :
  - The medium is pumped by an external source of energy, such as electric current, flash lamp, or another laser, and some atoms or molecules are excited to higher energy levels.
  - Some of the excited atoms or molecules spontaneously emit photons and return to lower energy levels. This is called spontaneous emission and it produces light of random frequency, phase, and direction.
  - Some of the photons produced by spontaneous emission encounter other excited atoms or molecules and stimulate them to emit photons of the same frequency, phase, and direction. This is called stimulated emission and it produces coherent light.
  - The photons produced by stimulated emission travel through the medium and are reflected by the mirrors at the ends of the resonant cavity. This creates a positive feedback loop that increases the intensity of the light and causes more stimulated emission.
  - One of the mirrors is partially transparent and allows some of the amplified light to escape as the laser beam. The other mirror is fully reflective and keeps the light inside the cavity. The shape and size of the cavity determine the mode and divergence of the laser beam.
