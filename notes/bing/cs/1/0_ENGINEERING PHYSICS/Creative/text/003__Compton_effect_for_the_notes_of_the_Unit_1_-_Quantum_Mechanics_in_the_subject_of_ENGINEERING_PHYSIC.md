### Compton effect

- Compton effect is the phenomenon of **inelastic scattering** of high-energy photons (such as X-rays or gamma rays) by **free electrons**  .
- In this process, the photon transfers some of its energy to the electron and changes its direction, resulting in a **decrease in frequency** or an **increase in wavelength** of the photon  .
- The change in wavelength of the photon is called the **Compton shift** and it depends only on the **scattering angle** and not on the material or the initial wavelength  .
- The Compton effect was discovered by **Arthur Holly Compton** in 1923 and it provided experimental evidence for the **particle nature** of electromagnetic radiation  .
- The Compton effect can be explained by applying the **conservation of energy** and **conservation of momentum** to the collision between the photon and the electron  .
- The Compton effect can be expressed by the following formula  :

\[\lambda' - \lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c}(1 - \cos \theta)\]

where

- \(\lambda'\) is the **wavelength of the scattered photon**
- \(\lambda\) is the **wavelength of the incident photon**
- \(h\) is the **Planck's constant**
- \(m_e\) is the **rest mass of the electron**
- \(c\) is the **speed of light**
- \(\theta\) is the **scattering angle** of the photon

- The Compton effect has applications in **nuclear physics**, **astrophysics**, **medical imaging**, and **material analysis**  .
