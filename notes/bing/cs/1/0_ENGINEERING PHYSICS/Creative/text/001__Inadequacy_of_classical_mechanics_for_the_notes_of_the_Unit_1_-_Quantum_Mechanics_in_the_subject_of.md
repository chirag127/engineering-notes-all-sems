### Inadequacy of classical mechanics

- Classical mechanics is the branch of physics that deals with the motion of macroscopic objects under the influence of forces.
- Classical mechanics can explain correctly the motion of celestial bodies, such as planets and stars, and microscopic terrestrial bodies, such as bullets and cars, that move with non-relativistic speeds (much less than the speed of light).
- However, classical mechanics fails to explain certain phenomena observed in nature, especially at the atomic and subatomic scales, where the objects have very small masses and very high speeds.
- Some of the phenomena that classical mechanics cannot explain are:

  - The stability of atoms: According to classical mechanics, an electron orbiting a nucleus should lose energy due to electromagnetic radiation and eventually fall into the nucleus, making the atom unstable. However, atoms are observed to be stable and have discrete energy levels.
  - The spectra of black body radiation: A black body is an ideal object that absorbs and emits all frequencies of electromagnetic radiation. According to classical mechanics, the energy of the radiation should be proportional to the frequency, and the black body should emit infinite energy at high frequencies. However, experiments show that the energy of the radiation is quantized, and the black body emits less energy at high frequencies than predicted by classical mechanics.
  - The photoelectric effect: The photoelectric effect is the phenomenon of emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of a certain frequency is incident on it. According to classical mechanics, the energy of the emitted electrons should depend on the intensity of the light, and the emission should occur for any frequency of light. However, experiments show that the energy of the emitted electrons depends on the frequency of the light, and the emission occurs only for light above a certain threshold frequency.
  - The particle nature of light: According to classical mechanics, light is a wave that can be described by Maxwell's equations. However, experiments show that light also behaves like a particle, called a photon, that has zero rest mass, no electric charge, and carries energy and momentum that depend on its frequency. Photons can interact with other particles, such as electrons, and travel with the speed of light in vacuum, but with different speeds in different media.

- These phenomena indicate that classical mechanics is inadequate to describe the nature of matter and energy at the microscopic level, and a new theory is needed to account for the quantum effects. This new theory is called quantum mechanics, and it is based on the principles of uncertainty, superposition, and probability. Quantum mechanics provides a more accurate and complete description of the physical world than classical mechanics.
