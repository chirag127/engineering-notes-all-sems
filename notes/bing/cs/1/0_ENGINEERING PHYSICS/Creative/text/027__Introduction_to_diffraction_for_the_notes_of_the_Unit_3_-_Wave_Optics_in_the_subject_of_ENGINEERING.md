### Introduction to diffraction

- Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of light waves around the edges of an obstacle or an aperture.
- Diffraction occurs when the size of the obstacle or the aperture is comparable to the wavelength of light.
- Diffraction is a consequence of the wave nature of light and the Huygens-Fresnel principle.
- Diffraction can be classified into two types: Fresnel diffraction and Fraunhofer diffraction.
- Fresnel diffraction occurs when the source of light and the screen are at finite distances from the obstacle or the aperture.
- Fraunhofer diffraction occurs when the source of light and the screen are at infinite distances from the obstacle or the aperture, or when a converging lens is used to focus the diffracted light on the screen.
- The intensity distribution of the diffracted light on the screen depends on the shape and size of the obstacle or the aperture, and the wavelength of light.
- Some examples of diffraction are the formation of fringes in a single slit experiment, the diffraction grating, the diffraction of X-rays by crystals, and the rainbow.
