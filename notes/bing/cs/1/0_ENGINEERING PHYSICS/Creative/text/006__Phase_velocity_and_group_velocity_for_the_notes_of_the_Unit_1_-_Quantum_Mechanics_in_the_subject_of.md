### Phase velocity and group velocity

- In quantum mechanics, a wave packet is a superposition of plane waves with different wavelengths and frequencies.
- A wave packet can be used to describe the state of a particle with a certain position and momentum.
- The phase velocity of a wave packet is the speed at which a single plane wave component travels.
- The group velocity of a wave packet is the speed at which the envelope of the wave packet travels.
- The phase velocity and the group velocity are related by the dispersion relation of the medium, which depends on the frequency and the wavelength of the wave.
- For a non-dispersive medium, the phase velocity and the group velocity are equal and constant, and equal to the speed of light in vacuum.
- For a dispersive medium, the phase velocity and the group velocity are different and vary with the frequency and the wavelength of the wave.
- The phase velocity can be greater than the speed of light in vacuum, but this does not violate causality, because it does not carry any information or energy.
- The group velocity can never exceed the speed of light in vacuum, because it is related to the propagation of information and energy.
- The phase velocity and the group velocity can also be negative, which means that the wave travels in the opposite direction of its phase or group velocity vector. This can happen in some anomalous dispersion regimes or in negative index materials.
