Autonomous Driving: Implement a simulation of an autonomous car that can navigate a virtual environment while avoiding obstacles and reaching a destination. Technologies: Python, OpenCV, TensorFlow, ROS (Robot Operating System).

Autonomous driving is a complex and challenging domain that involves many technologies and stakeholders. Some of the main challenges and solutions are:

- Balancing the need for high-resolution mapping vs improved vision systems. Imaging and photonics solutions cannot yet be trusted to perform the necessary recognition and range finding required for self-driving vehicles independently and in all lighting conditions. Therefore, high-resolution maps that provide detailed information about the road geometry, traffic signs, lane markings, etc. are needed to complement the vision systems. However, creating and updating such maps is costly and time-consuming, and requires reliable communication between vehicles and infrastructure. A possible solution is to use machine learning techniques to generate dynamic maps from sensor data collected by vehicles.
- Scaling up LIDAR scanner solutions. LIDAR (Light Detection And Ranging) is a technology that uses laser beams to measure distances and create 3D representations of the environment. It is widely used in autonomous driving to detect obstacles, pedestrians, other vehicles, etc. However, LIDAR scanners are expensive, bulky, power-hungry, and prone to interference from other sources of light or electromagnetic waves. A possible solution is to develop cheaper, smaller, more efficient, and more robust LIDAR sensors that can be integrated into vehicle design.
- Where will processors go? Autonomous driving requires high-performance computing power to process large amounts of data from multiple sensors and run complex algorithms for perception, planning, control, etc. However, current processors are limited by size, cost, power consumption, heat dissipation, reliability,
and security issues. A possible solution is to use specialized processors that are optimized for specific tasks or applications (such as neural network processing), or distributed computing architectures that leverage cloud computing or edge computing resources.
