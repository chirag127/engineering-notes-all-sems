Autonomous driving is a complex and challenging domain that requires integrating various technologies and ensuring safety, comfort, and intelligence on the road. Some of the main challenges and solutions of autonomous driving are:

- Balancing the need for high-resolution mapping vs improved vision systems. Imaging and photonics solutions cannot yet be trusted to perform the necessary recognition and range finding required for self-driving vehicles independently and in all lighting conditions. Therefore, high-resolution maps are needed to provide accurate localization and contextual information for autonomous vehicles. However, creating and updating such maps is costly and time-consuming. One possible solution is to use blockchain technology to enable collaborative mapping among connected autonomous vehicles (CAVs) and other stakeholders.
- Scaling up LIDAR scanner solutions. LIDAR (Light Detection And Ranging) is a key sensor technology for autonomous driving that provides 3D information about the surrounding environment. However, LIDAR scanners are expensive, bulky, and prone to interference from other sources of light or electromagnetic waves. Some possible solutions are to develop cheaper and smaller LIDAR sensors, use alternative technologies such as radar or camera-based systems, or combine multiple sensors to enhance robustness .
- Where will processors go? Autonomous driving requires high-performance computing power to process large amounts of data from various sensors and run complex software algorithms for perception, planning, control, and learning. However, current processors are limited by power consumption, heat dissipation, bandwidth, latency, reliability, security, and cost issues . Some possible solutions are to optimize processor architectures for specific tasks or applications (such as neural network processing), use edge computing or cloud computing platforms to offload some computations from onboard processors, or design dedicated hardware accelerators for autonomous driving.
- Mitigating risk. Autonomous driving poses significant legal, ethical, social, and technical challenges regarding liability, accountability,
trustworthiness
