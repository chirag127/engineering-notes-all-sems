Some of the challenges and solutions of Augmented Reality are:

- Legal concerns: As AR technology advances faster than legal systems can cope with, regulators and lawmakers are left playing catchup. XR is no exception. There are issues such as intellectual property rights, content regulation, liability, and jurisdiction that need to be addressed.
- Moral questions: This conundrum on what should and shouldn't be allowed in a virtual environment isn't just a legal question. It also raises ethical and moral dilemmas, such as how to deal with violence, harassment, addiction, and identity in XR.
- Access for the few, not the many: The cost of manufacturing and acquiring AR devices is still high for many consumers and businesses. There is also a lack of diversity and inclusion in the XR industry and market, which may limit the potential of AR to reach a wider audience .
- Privacy and security: AR technology collects a lot of data from users' environments, behaviors, preferences, and biometrics. This data can be used for personalization, analytics, advertising, or malicious purposes. Users need to be aware of how their data is collected, stored, shared, and protected by AR providers and platforms .
- Health concerns: AR technology can cause physical and psychological effects on users' health. Some of these effects include eye strain, nausea, headaches, motion sickness, fatigue, addiction, isolation . Users need to be mindful of their usage habits and take breaks when needed.

To create an AR application that can display digital objects in the real world and interact with them using Unity (a cross-platform game engine), you will need to use one or more of these technologies:

- ARKit: A framework that allows developers to create AR experiences for iOS devices using features such as face tracking,
plane detection,
image recognition,
and object occlusion.
- ARCore: A platform that enables developers to build AR applications for Android devices using features such as environmental understanding,
light estimation,
and cloud anchors.
- Vuforia: A software development kit that supports various types of AR experiences across multiple platforms using features such as image targets,
model targets,
ground plane,
and area targets.
- C#: A general-purpose programming language that is widely used for developing games with Unity.
