### Analysis vs Reporting in Big Data

- Analysis and reporting are two essential aspects of Big Data that help to derive insights and value from large and complex datasets.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, transforming, modeling, and interpreting data to answer specific questions, test hypotheses, or discover patterns and trends.
- Reporting is the process of presenting, visualizing, and communicating data and analysis results to inform decision-making, monitor performance, or share knowledge.
- Analysis and reporting can be performed using various tools, techniques, and frameworks, such as SQL, Python, R, Spark, Hadoop, Tableau, Power BI, etc.
- Analysis and reporting can have different goals, audiences, and formats, depending on the context and the use case.
- Some examples of analysis and reporting in Big Data are:

  - Business intelligence (BI): BI is the process of using data to support strategic and operational decisions, such as identifying market opportunities, optimizing processes, or improving customer satisfaction. BI involves both analysis and reporting, as it requires collecting, processing, and analyzing data, and then presenting and delivering the insights to the relevant stakeholders, such as managers, executives, or customers.
  - Data science: Data science is the interdisciplinary field that applies scientific methods, algorithms, and systems to extract knowledge and insights from data. Data science involves both analysis and reporting, as it requires applying advanced techniques, such as machine learning, artificial intelligence, or natural language processing, to solve complex problems, and then communicating and visualizing the findings and recommendations to the end-users, such as researchers, engineers, or policymakers.
  - Data journalism: Data journalism is the practice of using data to enhance, inform, or create news stories, such as investigative reports, interactive features, or data-driven narratives. Data journalism involves both analysis and reporting, as it requires finding, verifying, and analyzing data, and then storytelling and publishing the stories to the public, such as readers, viewers, or listeners.
