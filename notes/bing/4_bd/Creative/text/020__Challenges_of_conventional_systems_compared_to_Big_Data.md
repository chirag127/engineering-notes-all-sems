### Challenges of conventional systems compared to Big Data

- Conventional systems are designed to work with structured data, which have a fixed schema and format. Big data, on the other hand, can be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured, and can vary in size, velocity, and variety. This makes it difficult for conventional systems to store, process, and analyze big data efficiently and effectively  .
- Conventional systems rely on relational databases, which have limitations in scalability, performance, and flexibility. Big data requires distributed and parallel processing, which can handle large volumes of data across multiple nodes. Big data also requires different types of data storage, such as NoSQL databases, Hadoop Distributed File System (HDFS), or cloud storage, which can handle different types of data and queries  .
- Conventional systems have difficulty in maintaining the accuracy and confidentiality of the data, as the quality of the data is high and the cost of storing such massive quantity of data is expensive. Big data, on the other hand, can leverage techniques such as data cleansing, data compression, data encryption, and data anonymization, which can improve the data quality and security, and reduce the storage cost .
- Conventional systems have limited analytical capabilities, as they can only perform predefined and structured queries on the data. Big data, on the other hand, can use advanced analytical tools and techniques, such as machine learning, data mining, natural language processing, and sentiment analysis, which can extract valuable insights and patterns from the data, and support complex and dynamic queries  .
- Conventional systems are less flexible and adaptable, as they require predefined data models and schemas, and are difficult to change or update. Big data, on the other hand, can be more flexible and adaptable, as it can handle different types of data sources and formats, and can be easily integrated and transformed  .
