### Analysis vs Reporting in Big Data

- Analysis and reporting are two essential tasks in big data that help to derive insights and value from large and complex datasets.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, transforming, modeling, and interpreting data to answer specific questions or test hypotheses. Analysis can be descriptive, predictive, or prescriptive, depending on the goal and the type of data.
- Reporting is the process of presenting and communicating the results of analysis to various stakeholders, such as managers, customers, or regulators. Reporting can be done in different formats, such as dashboards, charts, tables, or narratives, depending on the audience and the purpose.
- Analysis and reporting are interrelated and complementary, but they have some key differences:

  - Analysis is more exploratory and iterative, while reporting is more structured and standardized.
  - Analysis requires more technical skills and tools, such as programming languages, statistical methods, and machine learning algorithms, while reporting requires more business skills and tools, such as data visualization, storytelling, and report design.
  - Analysis is more focused on finding patterns, trends, and relationships in data, while reporting is more focused on summarizing, explaining, and recommending actions based on data.
  - Analysis is more suitable for complex and dynamic problems, where the data and the questions are constantly changing, while reporting is more suitable for simple and stable problems, where the data and the questions are well-defined and consistent.

- A simple mnemonic to remember the difference between analysis and reporting is:

  - Analysis is about asking **why** and **how** data behaves the way it does, while reporting is about telling **what** and **so what** data means and implies.
