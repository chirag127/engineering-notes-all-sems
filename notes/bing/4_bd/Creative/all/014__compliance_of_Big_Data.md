#### Compliance of Big Data

- Compliance of big data refers to the process of ensuring that the data collected, processed, and stored by an organization meets the standards and regulations of the relevant authorities and stakeholders.
- Compliance of big data is important for several reasons, such as:
  - Protecting the privacy and security of the data subjects and owners.
  - Preventing and detecting fraud, money laundering, and other criminal activities.
  - Enhancing the trust and reputation of the organization among its clients and partners.
  - Avoiding legal penalties and fines for non-compliance.
- Compliance of big data involves several challenges, such as:
  - Managing the volume, velocity, and variety of data from different sources and formats.
  - Balancing the trade-off between data quality and data quantity.
  - Adapting to the changing and evolving regulations and standards across different jurisdictions and domains.
  - Implementing effective and efficient data governance and risk management practices.
- Compliance of big data can be achieved by following some best practices, such as:
  - Adopting a data-centric approach that focuses on the data lifecycle and the data value chain.
  - Implementing data security measures, such as encryption, anonymization, and access control, to protect the data from unauthorized access and misuse.
  - Applying data transparency principles, such as informing the data subjects and owners about the purpose, scope, and duration of data collection, processing, and storage, and providing them with the right to access, correct, and delete their data.
  - Applying data minimization techniques, such as collecting and retaining only the data that is necessary and relevant for the intended use, and deleting the data when it is no longer needed.
  - Leveraging data analytics and predictive models to identify and mitigate data risks and compliance issues.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the key aspects of compliance of big data is:

  - **C**ollect data with consent and purpose
  - **O**bserve data quality and quantity
  - **M**onitor data flow and lifecycle
  - **P**rotect data with security and privacy
  - **L**earn from data with analytics and insights
  - **I**nform data subjects and owners with transparency
  - **A**dapt to data regulations and standards
  - **N**arrow down data with minimization
  - **C**orrect data with access and control
  - **E**rase data with retention and deletion
