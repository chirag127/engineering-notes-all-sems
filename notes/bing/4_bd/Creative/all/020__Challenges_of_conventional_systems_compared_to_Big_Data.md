### Challenges of conventional systems compared to Big Data

Conventional systems are the traditional data storage and processing systems that are based on relational databases and structured data. They are designed to handle small to medium-sized data sets that are consistent, homogeneous, and predictable. Big Data, on the other hand, is the term used to describe large, complex, and diverse data sets that are generated at high velocity and variety. Big Data poses several challenges to conventional systems, such as:

- **Data volume**: Big Data is characterized by its massive scale, which can range from terabytes to petabytes and beyond. Conventional systems have limited storage capacity and scalability, which makes it difficult and expensive to store and manage such large amounts of data. Big Data requires distributed and parallel systems that can store and process data across multiple nodes and clusters.
- **Data velocity**: Big Data is generated at high speed and frequency, which can be in real-time or near-real-time. Conventional systems have limited processing power and throughput, which makes it challenging and time-consuming to ingest, analyze, and act on such fast-moving data. Big Data requires streaming and batch processing systems that can handle data in motion and at rest.
- **Data variety**: Big Data is composed of different types and formats of data, which can be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured. Conventional systems have rigid data models and schemas, which makes it hard and complex to integrate and query such heterogeneous and diverse data. Big Data requires flexible and schema-less systems that can handle data in any shape and form.
- **Data veracity**: Big Data is often noisy, incomplete, inconsistent, and inaccurate, which can affect the quality and reliability of the data. Conventional systems have strict data quality and integrity rules, which makes it impossible and impractical to deal with such uncertain and dirty data. Big Data requires robust and tolerant systems that can handle data in any condition and quality.
- **Data value**: Big Data is potentially valuable and useful, but it is not always easy to extract and exploit the insights and knowledge from the data. Conventional systems have limited analytical capabilities and tools, which makes it difficult and costly to perform complex and advanced analytics on such large and complex data. Big Data requires sophisticated and intelligent systems that can perform descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analytics on the data.

A possible mnemonic to remember these challenges is **5 Vs of Big Data**: Volume, Velocity, Variety, Veracity, and Value.
