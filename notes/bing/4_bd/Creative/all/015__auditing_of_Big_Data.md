#### Auditing of Big Data

- Big data is the term used to describe large and complex data sets that are generated from various sources and require advanced techniques and technologies to process, analyze and derive value from them .
- Auditing of big data involves applying audit principles and methods to assess the quality, reliability, security and usefulness of big data and the systems that produce and consume them  .
- Auditing of big data can help auditors to:
  - Expand the scope and depth of their audit projects by using larger and more diverse data populations .
  - Enhance their audit evidence and findings by using data analytics and artificial intelligence to uncover patterns, anomalies, risks and opportunities  .
  - Provide more value-added and forward-looking recommendations to the stakeholders by using big data to identify trends, benchmarks, best practices and areas for improvement  .
- Auditing of big data also poses many challenges and risks for auditors, such as:
  - Knowing what data is available, relevant and reliable for the audit objectives and scope  .
  - Accessing and obtaining data that might be sensitive, confidential, proprietary or regulated by laws and policies  .
  - Combining and integrating data from different sources, systems and formats, such as structured data (e.g., transactions, records, tables) and unstructured data (e.g., emails, texts, images, videos)  .
  - Applying appropriate data quality and governance controls to ensure the accuracy, completeness, validity and integrity of the data and the audit results  .
  - Developing and maintaining the skills, tools and techniques to perform data analysis, visualization and interpretation in a timely and effective manner   .
  - Communicating and reporting the audit findings and recommendations in a clear, concise and actionable way to the stakeholders   .
- Auditing of big data requires auditors to adopt a risk-based and data-driven approach that involves the following steps  :
  - Planning: Define the audit objectives, scope, criteria and methodology; identify the data sources, requirements and availability; assess the data risks and opportunities; and design the data analysis plan and procedures.
  - Execution: Obtain and prepare the data for analysis; perform the data analysis using appropriate tools and techniques; validate and document the data quality and audit evidence; and identify and report the audit findings and issues.
  - Reporting: Analyze and interpret the audit findings and issues; formulate and prioritize the audit recommendations; communicate and discuss the audit results and implications with the stakeholders; and prepare and deliver the audit report and follow-up actions.
- Auditing of big data can benefit from some mnemonics and learning tricks, such as:
  - The 5 Vs of big data: Volume, Variety, Velocity, Veracity and Value  . These are the key characteristics and challenges of big data that auditors need to consider and address in their audit projects.
  - The 4 Cs of data analysis: Collect, Clean, Compute and Communicate. These are the main steps and tasks involved in performing data analysis for audit purposes.
  - The 3 As of data quality: Accuracy, Availability and Applicability. These are the main dimensions and criteria of data quality that auditors need to assess and ensure in their audit data and results.
