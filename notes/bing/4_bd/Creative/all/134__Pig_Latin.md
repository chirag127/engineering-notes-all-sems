#### Pig Latin

- Pig Latin is a language game or argot in which words in English are altered, usually by adding a suffix or by moving the onset or initial consonant or consonant cluster of a word to the end of the word and adding a vocalic syllable to create such a suffix.
- The objective is to conceal the meaning of the words from others not familiar with the rules.
- The reference to Latin is a deliberate misnomer, as it is simply a form of jargon, used only for its English connotations as a strange and foreign-sounding language.
- Some examples of Pig Latin are:

  - "Pig" becomes "igpay"
  - "Latin" becomes "atinlay"
  - "Banana" becomes "ananabay"
  - "Happy" becomes "appyhay"
  - "Duck" becomes "uckday"
  - "Hello" becomes "ellohay"

- The usual rules for changing standard English into Pig Latin are as follows:

  - For words that begin with consonant sounds, all letters before the initial vowel are placed at the end of the word sequence. Then, "ay" is added, as in the following examples:

    - "Pig" becomes "igpay"
    - "Trash" becomes "ashtray"
    - "Glove" becomes "oveglay"
    - "Smile" becomes "ilesmay"

  - For words that begin with vowel sounds or silent letter, one just adds "way" or "yay" to the end (or just "ay"). Examples are:

    - "Eat" becomes "eatway" or "eatay"
    - "Omelet" becomes "omeletway" or "omeletay"
    - "Are" becomes "areway" or "areay"
    - "Honest" becomes "onesthay"

  - For words that end in "y", such as "happy", the "y" is left alone and not counted as a vowel. Examples are:

    - "Happy" becomes "appyhay"
    - "Silly" becomes "illysay"
    - "Candy" becomes "andycay"

  - For words that contain a "qu" at the beginning, the "qu" is treated as a single consonant and moved to the end. Examples are:

    - "Question" becomes "estionquay"
    - "Squash" becomes "ashsquay"
    - "Quail" becomes "ailquay"

  - For words that contain a "qu" after the first consonant or consonant cluster, the "u" is treated as part of the cluster and moved to the end. Examples are:

    - "Squeal" becomes "ealsquay"
    - "Square" becomes "aresquay"
    - "Squirt" becomes "irtsquay"

- Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for Pig Latin are:

  - To remember the rule for words that begin with consonant sounds, think of the phrase "consonant to the end, add ay".
  - To remember the rule for words that begin with vowel sounds or silent letter, think of the phrase "vowel or silent, add way or yay".
  - To remember the rule for words that end in "y", think of the phrase "y is not a vowel, leave it alone".
  - To remember the rule for words that contain a "qu", think of the phrase "qu is one sound, move it around".
