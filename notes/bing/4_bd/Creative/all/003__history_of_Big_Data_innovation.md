### History of Big Data Innovation

- The concept of big data is not new, but the term itself was coined by Roger Mougalas from O'Reilly Media in 2005, a year after they created the term Web 2.0 .
- Big data refers to a large set of data that is almost impossible to manage and process using traditional business intelligence tools. It is characterized by the 3Vs: volume, velocity, and variety .
- The origins of large data sets go back to the 1960s and 1970s, when the first data centers and the relational database model were developed .
- Some of the milestones in the history of big data innovation are:

  - 1965: The US Government plans the world's first data center to store 742 million tax returns and 175 million sets of fingerprints on magnetic tape .
  - 1970: IBM mathematician Edgar F. Codd presents his framework for a "relational database", which allows data to be stored and accessed in a structured and efficient way .
  - 1976: The Cray-1 supercomputer is introduced, capable of processing 160 million floating-point operations per second (FLOPS).
  - 1989: Tim Berners-Lee invents the World Wide Web, which enables the sharing and linking of information across the internet.
  - 1995: The Apache web server is released, which becomes the most popular platform for hosting websites and web applications.
  - 1997: Michael Lesk publishes an article estimating that the amount of information on the internet is about 100 terabytes, and that it doubles every year.
  - 1999: Google introduces the PageRank algorithm, which uses the link structure of the web to rank the relevance of web pages.
  - 2001: Doug Laney, an analyst at Gartner, defines the 3Vs of big data: volume, velocity, and variety.
  - 2003: Google develops the MapReduce framework, which allows parallel processing of large data sets across multiple servers.
  - 2004: Google publishes a paper on the Google File System, a distributed file system designed to handle large amounts of data.
  - 2005: Yahoo creates Hadoop, an open-source software framework that implements MapReduce and the Google File System.
  - 2006: Amazon launches its cloud computing platform, Amazon Web Services, which provides on-demand access to computing resources and storage.
  - 2008: The term "big data" becomes popular after a Wired article by Chris Anderson titled "The End of Theory: The Data Deluge Makes the Scientific Method Obsolete".
  - 2009: Google releases TensorFlow, an open-source software library for machine learning and artificial intelligence.
  - 2010: Facebook launches the Open Graph protocol, which allows third-party applications to access and integrate data from the social network.
  - 2011: IBM's Watson computer defeats human champions in the quiz show Jeopardy!, demonstrating the power of natural language processing and big data analytics.
  - 2012: The Obama campaign uses big data to target and mobilize voters, winning the US presidential election.
  - 2013: The NSA's PRISM program is revealed, exposing the mass surveillance of internet and phone data by the US government.
  - 2014: Alibaba, the Chinese e-commerce giant, sets a record for the largest initial public offering (IPO) in history, raising $25 billion. Alibaba's success is attributed to its use of big data to understand and serve its customers.
  - 2015: The European Union adopts the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which aims to protect the privacy and rights of individuals in the digital age.
  - 2016: AlphaGo, a computer program developed by Google's DeepMind, defeats the world champion of Go, a complex board game, using deep learning and big data.
  - 2017: China announces its plan to become the world leader in artificial intelligence by 2030, investing heavily in big data and machine learning research and development.
