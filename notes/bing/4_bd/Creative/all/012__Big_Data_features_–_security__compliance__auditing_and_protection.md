### Big Data features – security, compliance, auditing and protection

- Security: The process of ensuring that only authorized users can access the data and resources, and preventing unauthorized access, modification, or deletion of data.
  - Security challenges in Big Data include:
    - Data volume: The large amount of data makes it difficult to apply traditional security mechanisms such as encryption, access control, or firewall.
    - Data variety: The heterogeneous and unstructured nature of data makes it hard to classify and protect data according to its sensitivity or value.
    - Data velocity: The high speed and frequency of data generation and processing requires real-time security solutions that can handle dynamic and streaming data.
    - Data veracity: The uncertainty and inconsistency of data quality and provenance poses risks of data tampering, manipulation, or falsification.
  - Security solutions for Big Data include:
    - Data encryption: The process of transforming data into an unreadable form using a secret key, so that only authorized parties can decrypt and access the data.
    - Data masking: The process of hiding or replacing sensitive data elements with fictitious or anonymized data, so that the data can be used for analysis or testing without revealing the original data.
    - Data tokenization: The process of replacing sensitive data elements with non-sensitive tokens that have no meaning or value, but can be mapped back to the original data using a secure lookup table.
    - Data access control: The process of defining and enforcing policies and rules that specify who can access what data and resources, and under what conditions.
    - Data auditing: The process of monitoring and recording the activities and events related to data access, usage, and modification, and generating reports and alerts for security analysis and compliance purposes.
    - Data protection: The process of safeguarding data from loss, corruption, or theft, by using techniques such as backup, replication, recovery, or erasure.

- Compliance: The process of ensuring that the data and resources are managed and used in accordance with the applicable laws, regulations, standards, and policies.
  - Compliance challenges in Big Data include:
    - Data governance: The lack of clear and consistent rules and responsibilities for data ownership, stewardship, quality, and lifecycle management.
    - Data privacy: The risk of violating the rights and preferences of data subjects regarding the collection, processing, and sharing of their personal or sensitive data.
    - Data ethics: The potential of using data for malicious, discriminatory, or harmful purposes, or without the consent or awareness of data subjects or stakeholders.
  - Compliance solutions for Big Data include:
    - Data classification: The process of categorizing data according to its sensitivity, value, or regulatory requirements, and applying appropriate security and privacy measures accordingly.
    - Data retention: The process of determining and implementing the optimal duration and location for storing data, based on its business value and legal obligations.
    - Data deletion: The process of permanently removing data from the storage media, when it is no longer needed or required, or when requested by data subjects or authorities.
    - Data anonymization: The process of removing or modifying any identifying or linkable information from data, so that the data cannot be traced back to the data subjects or sources.
    - Data consent: The process of obtaining and managing the permission and preferences of data subjects or stakeholders for the collection, processing, and sharing of their data.

- Auditing: The process of verifying and validating that the data and resources are secure, compliant, and reliable, and that the data processing and analysis are accurate, consistent, and trustworthy.
  - Auditing challenges in Big Data include:
    - Data provenance: The difficulty of tracking and documenting the origin, history, and lineage of data, especially when data is derived from multiple and diverse sources, or when data is transformed or aggregated.
    - Data quality: The uncertainty and inconsistency of data accuracy, completeness, timeliness, and relevance, especially when data is heterogeneous and unstructured, or when data is dynamic and streaming.
    - Data integrity: The vulnerability of data to unauthorized or unintended modification, deletion, or corruption, especially when data is distributed and replicated across multiple nodes or locations.
    - Data trust: The lack of confidence and credibility of data and its analysis, especially when data is incomplete, inaccurate, or biased, or when data is processed or presented in a misleading or deceptive way.
