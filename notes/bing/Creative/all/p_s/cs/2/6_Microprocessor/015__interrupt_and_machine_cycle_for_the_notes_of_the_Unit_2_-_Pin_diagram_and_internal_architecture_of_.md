### Interrupt and Machine Cycle

- An **interrupt** is a signal from an external device that requests the attention of the microprocessor. It causes the microprocessor to temporarily suspend the current program and execute a subroutine to handle the interrupt. Interrupts can be used for data transfer, device control, error handling, etc.
- The 8085 microprocessor supports five types of interrupts: **INTR, TRAP, RST 7.5, RST 6.5, and RST 5.5**. Each interrupt has a different priority and a different way of being acknowledged by the microprocessor .
- The **INTR** interrupt is a maskable, vectored, and edge-triggered interrupt. It can be enabled or disabled by the EI and DI instructions. It is acknowledged by the INTA' signal, which causes the microprocessor to read an 8-bit instruction from the external device. This instruction is usually a CALL or RST instruction that jumps to the interrupt service routine (ISR) .
- The **TRAP** interrupt is a non-maskable, vectored, and level-triggered interrupt. It has the highest priority among the interrupts and cannot be disabled by any instruction. It is acknowledged by the TRAP' signal, which causes the microprocessor to execute a RST 4 instruction that jumps to the ISR at address 0024H .
- The **RST 7.5, RST 6.5, and RST 5.5** interrupts are maskable, vectored, and edge-triggered interrupts. They can be enabled or disabled by the SIM and RIM instructions. They are acknowledged by the RST 7.5', RST 6.5', and RST 5.5' signals, which cause the microprocessor to execute a RST 7, RST 6, and RST 5 instruction respectively. These instructions jump to the ISR at address 003CH, 0034H, and 002CH respectively .
- The priority order of the interrupts is: **TRAP > RST 7.5 > RST 6.5 > RST 5.5 > INTR** .
- A **machine cycle** is the basic operation performed by the microprocessor. It consists of one or more **T-states**, which are the subdivisions of the clock cycle. Each T-state lasts for one clock period .
- The 8085 microprocessor has five types of machine cycles: **Opcode Fetch, Memory Read, Memory Write, I/O Read, and I/O Write**. Each machine cycle has a different number of T-states and a different function .
- The **Opcode Fetch** machine cycle is used to fetch the opcode of an instruction from the memory. It consists of four T-states: T1, T2, T3, and T4. In T1, the address of the instruction is placed on the address bus and the ALE signal is activated. In T2, the RD' signal is activated and the opcode is read from the memory. In T3, the opcode is placed on the data bus and the RD' signal is deactivated. In T4, the opcode is transferred to the instruction register and the ALE signal is deactivated .
- The **Memory Read** machine cycle is used to read data from the memory. It consists of three T-states: T1, T2, and T3. In T1, the address of the data is placed on the address bus and the ALE signal is activated. In T2, the RD' signal is activated and the data is read from the memory. In T3, the data is placed on the data bus and the RD' and ALE signals are deactivated .
- The **Memory Write** machine cycle is used to write data to the memory. It consists of three T-states: T1, T2, and T3. In T1, the address of the data is placed on the address bus and the ALE signal is activated. In T2, the data is placed on the data bus and the WR' signal is activated. In T3, the data is written to the memory and the WR' and ALE signals are deactivated .
