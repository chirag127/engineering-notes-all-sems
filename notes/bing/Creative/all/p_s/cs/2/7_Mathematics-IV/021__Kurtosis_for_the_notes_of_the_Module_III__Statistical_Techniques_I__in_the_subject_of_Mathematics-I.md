### Kurtosis

- Kurtosis is a measure of the **tailedness** of a distribution, i.e., how often **outliers** occur .
- Outliers are values that are far away from the mean or the center of the distribution.
- Kurtosis can be used to describe the shape of a distribution, whether it is **peaked** or **flat**, and whether it has **thick** or **thin** tails .
- A distribution with high kurtosis has a sharp peak and heavy tails, meaning that it has more outliers than a normal distribution  .
- A distribution with low kurtosis has a flat peak and light tails, meaning that it has fewer outliers than a normal distribution  .
- A normal distribution has a kurtosis of 3, which is also called **mesokurtic**  .

- Kurtosis can also be expressed as **excess kurtosis**, which is the difference between the kurtosis of a distribution and the kurtosis of a normal distribution, i.e., kurtosis - 3  .
- Excess kurtosis can be positive, negative, or zero :
  - Positive excess kurtosis (> 3) indicates that the distribution is **leptokurtic**, meaning that it is more peaked and has thicker tails than a normal distribution  .
  - Negative excess kurtosis (< 3) indicates that the distribution is **platykurtic**, meaning that it is less peaked and has thinner tails than a normal distribution  .
  - Zero excess kurtosis (= 3) indicates that the distribution is **mesokurtic**, meaning that it has the same shape as a normal distribution  .
- Kurtosis can be used to assess the **risk** of a distribution, as higher kurtosis implies more extreme values and more uncertainty .
- Kurtosis can also be used to compare different distributions and test for normality .
- Kurtosis can be influenced by the sample size, the outliers, and the scale of measurement .
- Kurtosis is not the same as skewness, which is a measure of the **asymmetry** of a distribution.
