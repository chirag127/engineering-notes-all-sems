## Module III: Statistical Techniques I:

- This module covers some of the basic and advanced statistical techniques that are useful for analyzing data and making inferences.
- The topics covered in this module are:

  - **Measures of central tendency**: These are numerical values that summarize the center or average of a data set, such as mean, median, and mode.
  - **Moments**: These are numerical values that describe the shape or spread of a data set, such as variance, standard deviation, skewness, and kurtosis.
  - **Moment generating function (MGF)**: This is a function that can be used to find the moments of a probability distribution, as well as other properties such as the characteristic function and the cumulant generating function.
  - **Curve fitting**: This is a process of finding a mathematical function that best fits a given set of data points, using various methods such as the method of least squares, the method of maximum likelihood, or the method of moments.
  - **Method of least squares**: This is a method of curve fitting that minimizes the sum of the squared errors between the observed data points and the fitted function, resulting in a linear or nonlinear regression model.
  - **Fitting of straight lines**: This is a special case of the method of least squares, where the fitted function is a linear equation of the form y = a + bx, and the parameters a and b are estimated using the normal equations.
  - **Fitting of polynomials**: This is another special case of the method of least squares, where the fitted function is a polynomial equation of the form y = a0 + a1x + a2x^2 + ... + anx^n, and the parameters a0, a1, ..., an are estimated using the normal equations or the Vandermonde matrix.
  - **Fitting of exponential and logarithmic functions**: These are two more special cases of the method of least squares, where the fitted function is either an exponential equation of the form y = aebx or a logarithmic equation of the form y = a + b ln x, and the parameters a and b are estimated using the linearization technique or the nonlinear least squares method.

- The objectives of this module are:

  - To understand the concepts and applications of the measures of central tendency and the moments.
  - To learn how to use the moment generating function to find the moments and other properties of a probability distribution.
  - To know the principles and procedures of curve fitting and the method of least squares.
  - To be able to fit straight lines, polynomials, exponential and logarithmic functions to a given set of data points, and to evaluate the goodness of fit and the confidence intervals of the parameters.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the order of the measures of central tendency, use the acronym **MMM** (Mean, Median, Mode).
- To remember the formula for the variance, use the phrase **"mean of squares minus square of mean"**.
- To remember the formula for the skewness, use the phrase **"cube of deviation over cube of standard deviation"**.
- To remember the formula for the kurtosis, use the phrase **"quartic of deviation over quartic of standard deviation"**.
- To remember the definition of the moment generating function, use the phrase **"expected value of e to the tx"**.
- To remember the normal equations for fitting a straight line, use the acronym **SSE** (Sum of Squares of Errors) and the phrase **"SSE is minimized when the partial derivatives are zero"**.
- To remember the normal equations for fitting a polynomial, use the acronym **VDM** (Vandermonde Matrix) and the phrase **"VDM times the coefficient vector equals the data vector"**.
- To remember the linearization technique for fitting an exponential or logarithmic function, use the phrase **"take the log of both sides and fit a straight line"**.
