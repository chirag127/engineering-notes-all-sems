## Module IV: Statistical Techniques II

Statistical techniques are methods of collecting, analyzing, and interpreting data to make informed decisions, test hypotheses, and draw conclusions. Statistical techniques can be applied to various fields of study, such as business, engineering, social sciences, natural sciences, and humanities. Some of the common statistical techniques are:

- **Mean**: The mean, or average, is the sum of a list of numbers divided by the number of items in the list. The mean is a measure of central tendency that indicates the typical value of a data set. For example, the mean of the list {2, 4, 6, 8} is (2 + 4 + 6 + 8) / 4 = 5.
- **Standard deviation**: The standard deviation (SD) is a measure of dispersion that indicates how much the data points vary from the mean. A low standard deviation means that the data points are close to the mean, while a high standard deviation means that the data points are spread out. For example, the standard deviation of the list {2, 4, 6, 8} is 2.236, which means that the data points are relatively close to the mean of 5.
- **Regression**: Regression is a statistical technique that models the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables. Regression can be used to predict, explain, or test causal hypotheses. For example, a regression model can be used to estimate the effect of advertising spending on sales revenue, or to test whether smoking causes lung cancer.
- **Hypothesis testing**: Hypothesis testing is a statistical technique that evaluates a claim or a statement about a population parameter based on a sample of data. Hypothesis testing involves setting up a null hypothesis (a default assumption) and an alternative hypothesis (a competing claim), and then using a test statistic and a significance level to determine whether to reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis. For example, a hypothesis test can be used to determine whether the mean height of men is different from the mean height of women, or whether a new drug is more effective than a placebo.
- **Correlation**: Correlation is a statistical technique that measures the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two variables. Correlation can range from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative relationship, 0 indicates no relationship, and 1 indicates a perfect positive relationship. Correlation does not imply causation, meaning that a high correlation does not necessarily mean that one variable causes the other. For example, a correlation can be used to measure the association between income and education, or between temperature and ice cream sales.
- **ANOVA**: ANOVA, or analysis of variance, is a statistical technique that compares the means of two or more groups of data. ANOVA can be used to test whether there is a significant difference among the group means, or whether the observed variation in the data is due to random chance. For example, ANOVA can be used to compare the test scores of students from different schools, or the blood pressure of patients who received different treatments.
- **Chi-square test**: Chi-square test is a statistical technique that tests the goodness of fit between an observed frequency distribution and an expected frequency distribution. Chi-square test can be used to test whether a categorical variable follows a certain probability distribution, or whether two categorical variables are independent of each other. For example, chi-square test can be used to test whether a die is fair, or whether gender and political preference are related.
