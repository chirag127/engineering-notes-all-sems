## Module I: Partial Differential Equations

- A partial differential equation (PDE) is an equation that relates a function of several variables to its partial derivatives.
- A partial derivative of a function of several variables is the rate of change of the function with respect to one of the variables, while keeping the others constant.
- PDEs are used to model various phenomena in physics, engineering, biology, and other fields, such as heat, sound, fluid flow, electromagnetism, etc.
- The general form of a PDE is:

$$
F(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n, u, u_{x_1}, u_{x_2}, \dots, u_{x_n}, u_{x_1 x_1}, u_{x_1 x_2}, \dots, u_{x_n x_n}, \dots) = 0
$$

where $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ are the independent variables, $u$ is the dependent variable, and $u_{x_i}$, $u_{x_i x_j}$, etc. are the partial derivatives of $u$ with respect to $x_i$, $x_j$, etc.

- The order of a PDE is the highest order of the partial derivatives that appear in the equation. For example, the equation

$$
u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0
$$

is a second-order PDE, because the highest order of the partial derivatives is two.

- The degree of a PDE is the power of the highest order derivative in the equation, when the equation is written as a polynomial in the derivatives. For example, the equation

$$
u_{xx}^2 + u_{yy}^2 = 1
$$

is a second-order PDE of degree two, because the highest order derivative is squared.

- The linearity of a PDE depends on whether the equation is linear or nonlinear in the dependent variable and its derivatives. A PDE is linear if it can be written as a sum of terms, each of which is a constant or a product of a constant and a derivative of $u$. For example, the equation

$$
u_{xx} + u_{yy} + u = 0
$$

is a linear PDE, because each term is either a constant or a product of a constant and a derivative of $u$. A PDE is nonlinear if it contains any term that is not linear, such as a product of derivatives, a power of a derivative, a function of a derivative, etc. For example, the equation

$$
u_{xx} + u_{yy} + u^2 = 0
$$

is a nonlinear PDE, because it contains a term that is a power of $u$.

- The solution of a PDE is a function that satisfies the equation and any given boundary or initial conditions. The solution may be unique, non-unique, or nonexistent, depending on the equation and the conditions. The solution may also be explicit, implicit, or numerical, depending on the method of finding the solution.

- Some common types of PDEs are:

  - The transport equation:

  $$
  u_t + c u_x = 0
  $$

  where $c$ is a constant. This equation models the propagation of a wave or a signal with constant speed $c$.

  - The heat equation:

  $$
  u_t = k u_{xx}
  $$

  where $k$ is a constant. This equation models the diffusion of heat in a one-dimensional rod with thermal conductivity $k$.

  - The wave equation:

  $$
  u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}
  $$

  where $c$ is a constant. This equation models the vibration of a one-dimensional string with tension $c^2$.

  $$
  u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0
  $$

Mnemonics are memory tricks that can help you remember new information by connecting it to something you already know. There are different types of mnemonics, such as rhymes, acronyms, diagrams, or images. For example, you can use the acronym HOMES to remember the names of the Great Lakes: Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, and Superior.

Some tips for using mnemonics effectively are  :

- Choose the right mnemonic for your situation. For example, if you want to learn how to spell a word, you can use a spelling mnemonic, such as "there is a rat in separate".
- Practice the mnemonic several times to help you remember it. You can also repeat it to others or write it down.
- Use visual and/or auditory cues to make the mnemonic more vivid and memorable. For example, you can imagine a picture of a rat in the word separate or say it out loud with emphasis on the "rat" sound.
- Use humor, exaggeration, or emotion to make the mnemonic more interesting and fun. For example, you can use a silly rhyme, such as "My very eager mother just served us nine pizzas" to remember the order of the planets in the solar system (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto).
- Review the mnemonic periodically to keep it fresh in your memory. You can also test yourself or quiz others on the information.
