### Lagrange's Equations

- Lagrange's equations are a class of partial differential equations of the first order that have the form Pp + Qq = R, where P, Q and R are functions of x, y, z and p = u_x and q = u_y are the partial derivatives of u with respect to x and y respectively .
- Lagrange's equations are also known as quasi-linear equations because they are linear in the partial derivatives p and q, but not necessarily in u .
- Lagrange's equations can be used to model various physical phenomena, such as minimal surfaces, wave propagation, heat conduction, fluid dynamics and optics .
- Lagrange's equations can be solved by using the method of characteristics, which involves finding curves along which the equation becomes an ordinary differential equation .
- The general solution of Lagrange's equation can be expressed as a function of two arbitrary functions of one variable, which are determined by the initial or boundary conditions .
- An example of Lagrange's equation is xyp + yzq = zx, which can be solved by using the method of characteristics as follows:

  - The characteristic equations are:

    - dx/P = dy/Q = dz/R = xyp/y^2z = yzq/x^2z = zx/xy^2

  - Solving for x, y and z in terms of a parameter t, we get:

    - x = c_1e^t
    - y = c_2e^t
    - z = c_3e^{-2t}

  - Eliminating t, we get the characteristic curves:

    - x^2z = c_1c_3
    - y^2z = c_2c_3
    - xy = c_1c_2

  - The general solution of the equation is:

    - u = f(x^2z, y^2z) + g(xy)

  - where f and g are arbitrary functions of one variable.
