### Discrete and Continuous Probability Distribution

- A probability distribution is a function that describes all possible values of a random variable as well as the associated probabilities.
- A random variable is a variable whose value is determined by the outcome of a random experiment.
- A probability distribution may be either discrete or continuous, depending on the type of the random variable.
- A discrete random variable is a random variable that has countable or finite values, such as a list of non-negative integers, or a set of categories.
- A continuous random variable is a random variable that can assume any value on a continuum, such as a real number, or a measurement.
- A discrete probability distribution is a probability distribution of a discrete random variable, while a continuous probability distribution is a probability distribution of a continuous random variable.
- The two types of distributions differ in several ways, such as:

  - A discrete probability distribution assigns a probability to each possible value of the discrete random variable, while a continuous probability distribution assigns a probability to each interval of values of the continuous random variable.
  - A discrete probability distribution can be represented by a table, a graph, or a formula, while a continuous probability distribution can be represented by a curve, or a formula.
  - A discrete probability distribution has a finite or countable number of possible values, while a continuous probability distribution has an infinite or uncountable number of possible values.
  - A discrete probability distribution has a probability mass function (PMF) that gives the probability of each possible value of the discrete random variable, while a continuous probability distribution has a probability density function (PDF) that gives the probability of an interval of values of the continuous random variable.
  - A discrete probability distribution has a cumulative distribution function (CDF) that gives the probability of the discrete random variable being less than or equal to a given value, while a continuous probability distribution has a CDF that gives the probability of the continuous random variable being less than a given value.

- Some examples of discrete probability distributions are:

  - Binomial distribution: A discrete probability distribution that models the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, each with a constant probability of success.
  - Poisson distribution: A discrete probability distribution that models the number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space, given a constant average rate of occurrence.
  - Bernoulli distribution: A discrete probability distribution that models a single trial with two possible outcomes, success or failure, each with a fixed probability.
  - Geometric distribution: A discrete probability distribution that models the number of trials until the first success in a sequence of independent trials, each with a constant probability of success.
  - Uniform distribution: A discrete probability distribution that assigns equal probability to each possible value of the discrete random variable.

- Some examples of continuous probability distributions are:

  - Normal distribution: A continuous probability distribution that models the distribution of many natural phenomena, such as heights, weights, IQ scores, etc. It has a bell-shaped curve that is symmetric about the mean, and is determined by two parameters: the mean and the standard deviation.
  - Exponential distribution: A continuous probability distribution that models the time between events in a Poisson process, such as the time between arrivals of customers, or the time between failures of a machine. It has a decreasing curve that is skewed to the right, and is determined by one parameter: the rate of occurrence.
  - Uniform distribution: A continuous probability distribution that assigns equal probability to each interval of values of the continuous random variable. It has a rectangular shape that is determined by two parameters: the lower bound and the upper bound.
  - Beta distribution: A continuous probability distribution that models the distribution of probabilities, such as the probability of success in a binomial experiment. It has a flexible shape that can be symmetric, skewed, or bimodal, and is determined by two parameters: the shape parameters alpha and beta.
  - Gamma distribution: A continuous probability distribution that models the distribution of waiting times, such as the time until the nth event in a Poisson process. It has a shape that can be increasing, decreasing, or bell-shaped, and is determined by two parameters: the shape parameter k and the scale parameter theta.
