### Measures of central tendency for the notes of the Module III: Statistical Techniques I: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

- Measures of central tendency are numerical values that summarize the main characteristics of a data set.
- They are also called measures of location, central values, or summary statistics.
- The most common measures of central tendency are the mean, the median, and the mode.
- The mean is the sum of all the data values divided by the number of data values. It is also called the arithmetic average or simply the average. The mean is sensitive to outliers, which are extreme values that deviate from the rest of the data.
- The median is the middle value of a data set when it is arranged in ascending or descending order. If the data set has an odd number of values, the median is the middle value. If the data set has an even number of values, the median is the average of the middle two values. The median is not affected by outliers, so it is a more robust measure of central tendency than the mean.
- The mode is the most frequently occurring value in a data set. There can be more than one mode in a data set, or no mode at all. The mode is useful for describing categorical or nominal data, such as colors, names, or labels. The mode is also not affected by outliers, but it may not be very informative if the data set has many different values with similar frequencies.
- The choice of the best measure of central tendency depends on the type and distribution of the data, and the purpose of the analysis. Some general guidelines are:

  - For symmetric and unimodal data, the mean, the median, and the mode are all close to each other, and any of them can be used as a measure of central tendency.
  - For skewed data, the mean is pulled towards the tail of the distribution, so the median is usually a better measure of central tendency than the mean. The mode is often located at the peak of the distribution, which may or may not be close to the median.
  - For bimodal or multimodal data, the mean and the median may not reflect the true center of the data, so the mode or the modes may be more appropriate measures of central tendency. However, the mode may not be unique, and it may not capture the variability of the data.
  - For ordinal data, such as ratings, ranks, or grades, the median is the most suitable measure of central tendency, as it preserves the order of the data values. The mean and the mode may not be meaningful for ordinal data, as they do not take into account the relative distances between the data values.
  - For nominal data, such as names, colors, or labels, the mode is the only valid measure of central tendency, as it indicates the most common category or value. The mean and the median are not applicable for nominal data, as they require numerical values that can be added or ordered.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the order of the mean, the median, and the mode in a skewed distribution, use the acronym MMM: Mean is in the Middle of the Median and the Mode.
- To remember the formula for the mean, use the phrase "Sum over n": The mean is the sum of all the data values divided by the number of data values, or x̄ = Σx/n.
- To remember how to find the median, use the phrase "Middle or Mean": The median is the middle value of the data set when it is ordered, or the mean of the middle two values if the data set has an even number of values.
- To remember how to find the mode, use the phrase "Most or Modes": The mode is the most frequently occurring value in the data set, or the values that occur the most if the data set has more than one mode.
