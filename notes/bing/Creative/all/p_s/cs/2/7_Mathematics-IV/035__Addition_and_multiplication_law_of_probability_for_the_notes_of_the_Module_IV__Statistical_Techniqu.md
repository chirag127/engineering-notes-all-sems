### Addition and multiplication law of probability

- The addition and multiplication laws of probability are two basic rules that help us to calculate the probability of combined events.
- The addition law of probability states that the probability of the union of two events A and B is equal to the sum of the probabilities of A and B minus the probability of the intersection of A and B. Symbolically, P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B).
- The multiplication law of probability states that the probability of the intersection of two events A and B is equal to the product of the probability of A and the conditional probability of B given A. Symbolically, P(A ∩ B) = P(A) * P(B | A).
- The addition and multiplication laws of probability can be extended to more than two events by using the principles of inclusion-exclusion and independence.
- The addition and multiplication laws of probability can be used to solve various problems involving probability distributions, such as binomial, Poisson, normal, etc.
- The addition and multiplication laws of probability can also be used to derive other important results in probability theory, such as Bayes' theorem, the law of total probability, the law of large numbers, etc.

Some examples of applying the addition and multiplication laws of probability are:

- Example 1: Suppose a fair die is rolled twice. What is the probability of getting a sum of 7 or a double?
  - Solution: Let A be the event of getting a sum of 7 and B be the event of getting a double. Then P(A) = 6/36, P(B) = 6/36, and P(A ∩ B) = 1/36. Using the addition law of probability, we get P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B) = 6/36 + 6/36 - 1/36 = 11/36.
- Example 2: Suppose a card is drawn from a standard deck of 52 cards. What is the probability of getting a king or a spade?
  - Solution: Let A be the event of getting a king and B be the event of getting a spade. Then P(A) = 4/52, P(B) = 13/52, and P(A ∩ B) = 1/52. Using the addition law of probability, we get P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B) = 4/52 + 13/52 - 1/52 = 16/52 = 4/13.
- Example 3: Suppose a coin is tossed three times. What is the probability of getting exactly two heads?
  - Solution: Let A be the event of getting exactly two heads. Then A can occur in three ways: HHT, HTH, or THH. Each of these outcomes has a probability of 1/8. Using the multiplication law of probability, we get P(A) = P(HHT) + P(HTH) + P(THH) = 1/8 + 1/8 + 1/8 = 3/8.
