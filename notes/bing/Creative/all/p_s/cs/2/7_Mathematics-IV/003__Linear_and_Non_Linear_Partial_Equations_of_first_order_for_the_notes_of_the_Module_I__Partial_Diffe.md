### Linear and Non Linear Partial Equations of first order

- Partial differential equations (PDEs) are equations that involve partial derivatives of an unknown function of two or more variables.
- First-order PDEs are those in which the highest partial derivatives of the unknown function are of the first order. They can be both linear and non-linear.
- A linear PDE is one that is linear in the unknown function and its partial derivatives, i.e., it has the form
  ```
  a(x,y)u_x + b(x,y)u_y + c(x,y)u = f(x,y)
  ```
  where `u` is the unknown function, `u_x` and `u_y` are its partial derivatives with respect to `x` and `y`, and `a`, `b`, `c`, and `f` are given functions of `x` and `y`.
- A non-linear PDE is one that is not linear in the unknown function and its partial derivatives, i.e., it has the form
  ```
  F(x,y,u,u_x,u_y) = 0
  ```
  where `F` is a given function of `x`, `y`, `u`, `u_x`, and `u_y`.
- Linear PDEs can be solved by various methods, such as separation of variables, Fourier series, Laplace transform, etc. Non-linear PDEs are usually more difficult to solve and often require special techniques, such as the method of characteristics, similarity solutions, etc.
- Linear and non-linear PDEs have many applications in physics, engineering, biology, and other fields. Some examples of linear PDEs are the heat equation, the wave equation, and the Laplace equation. Some examples of non-linear PDEs are the Burgers equation, the Korteweg-de Vries equation, and the Navier-Stokes equation.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the general form of a linear PDE, you can use the acronym `ABC-FU`, which stands for `a(x,y)u_x + b(x,y)u_y + c(x,y)u = f(x,y)`.
- To remember the general form of a non-linear PDE, you can use the acronym `FUN`, which stands for `F(x,y,u,u_x,u_y) = 0`.
- To remember the names of some common linear PDEs, you can use the following phrases:
  - `Heat the Wave and Lap the Lace` for the heat equation, the wave equation, and the Laplace equation.
  - `Poisson is a Fish with a Source` for the Poisson equation, which is a generalization of the Laplace equation with a source term `f(x,y)`.
- To remember the names of some common non-linear PDEs, you can use the following phrases:
  - `Burger King fries are Korteweg-de Vries` for the Burgers equation and the Korteweg-de Vries equation, which are both models of nonlinear waves.
  - `Navier-Stokes is a Nasty Bloke` for the Navier-Stokes equation, which is a model of fluid dynamics.
