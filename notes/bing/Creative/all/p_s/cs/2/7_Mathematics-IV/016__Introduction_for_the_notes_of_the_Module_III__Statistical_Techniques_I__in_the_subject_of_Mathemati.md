### Introduction

- Statistical techniques are methods of collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data to make decisions or draw conclusions based on evidence.
- Statistical techniques are widely used in various fields such as business, economics, engineering, medicine, social sciences, and natural sciences.
- Statistical techniques can be classified into two main branches: descriptive statistics and inferential statistics.
  - Descriptive statistics summarize and display the main features of a data set using numerical measures, tables, and graphs.
  - Inferential statistics use sample data to make generalizations or predictions about a population or a phenomenon of interest.
- Some common statistical techniques are:
  - Mean: the average of a set of numbers.
  - Standard deviation: a measure of how much the data values vary from the mean.
  - Hypothesis testing: a procedure to test whether a certain claim or assumption is true for a population based on sample data.
  - Regression: a method of modeling the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables.
  - Sample size determination: a method of deciding how many observations are needed to achieve a desired level of accuracy and confidence in a statistical analysis.
  - Analysis of variance: a technique of comparing the means of two or more groups of data to see if they are significantly different.
- Statistical techniques are important because they help us to :
  - Describe and explore the data in a meaningful way.
  - Identify patterns, trends, and outliers in the data.
  - Test hypotheses and answer research questions.
  - Draw valid and reliable conclusions from the data.
  - Support decision making and problem solving based on evidence.
