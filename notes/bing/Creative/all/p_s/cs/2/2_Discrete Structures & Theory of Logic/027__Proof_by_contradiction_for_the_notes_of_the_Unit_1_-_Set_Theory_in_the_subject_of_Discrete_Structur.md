### Proof by contradiction

- Proof by contradiction is a common proof technique that is based on a very simple principle: something that leads to a contradiction cannot be true, and if so, the opposite must be true .
- It is also known as indirect proof or the method of reductio ad absurdum.
- The general steps of proof by contradiction are as follows:
  - Assume the negation of the statement to be proved, i.e., assume that the statement is false.
  - Use logical reasoning and known facts to derive a contradiction from the assumption, i.e., show that the assumption leads to something that does not make sense or violates a known truth.
  - Conclude that the assumption is false, and therefore the statement to be proved is true.
- Proof by contradiction is often used when the direct method of proof is not applicable or too difficult. It can also be used to prove the existence or non-existence of something by showing that the opposite assumption leads to absurd consequences.
- Proof by contradiction is a powerful and elegant method of proof, but it should be used with caution and clarity. It is important to state the assumption and the contradiction clearly, and to avoid circular reasoning or hidden assumptions.

#### Example 1: Prove that √2 is irrational.

- Assume that √2 is rational, i.e., √2 = p/q for some integers p and q.
- Without loss of generality, assume that p and q are coprime, i.e., they have no common factors other than 1.
- Squaring both sides, we get 2 = p^2/q^2, or 2q^2 = p^2.
- This implies that p^2 is even, and therefore p is even, i.e., p = 2k for some integer k.
- Substituting p = 2k into 2q^2 = p^2, we get 2q^2 = 4k^2, or q^2 = 2k^2.
- This implies that q^2 is even, and therefore q is even, i.e., q = 2l for some integer l.
- But this contradicts the assumption that p and q are coprime, since they both have 2 as a common factor.
- Therefore, the assumption that √2 is rational is false, and √2 is irrational.

#### Example 2: Prove that there is no largest prime number.

- Assume that there is a largest prime number, call it p.
- Consider the number n = p! + 1, where p! is the factorial of p, i.e., the product of all positive integers up to p.
- Note that n is larger than p, since p! is divisible by p and n is one more than p!.
- By the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, n must have a prime factor, call it q.
- Since q is a factor of n, it must also be a factor of n - p!, i.e., q divides p! + 1 - p! = 1.
- But the only positive integer that divides 1 is 1 itself, which is not a prime number.
- Therefore, q does not exist, which contradicts the fact that n has a prime factor.
- Therefore, the assumption that there is a largest prime number is false, and there is no largest prime number.
