### Lagrange's theorem

- Lagrange's theorem is one of the central theorems of abstract algebra. It states that in group theory, for any finite group say G, the order of subgroup H of group G divides the order of G. The order of the group represents the number of elements  .
- Mathematically, if G is a finite group and H is a subgroup of G, then |H| divides |G|, where |H| and |G| denote the number of elements in H and G respectively .
- Lagrange's theorem can be proved using the concept of cosets. A coset of H in G is a subset of G that is obtained by multiplying all the elements of H by a fixed element of G. For example, if g is an element of G, then the left coset of H by g is gH = {gh : h in H}, and the right coset of H by g is Hg = {hg : h in H} .
- The key properties of cosets are:

  - Every element of G belongs to exactly one left coset of H and exactly one right coset of H .
  - All left cosets of H have the same size as H, and all right cosets of H have the same size as H .
  - The number of left cosets of H is equal to the number of right cosets of H, and this number is called the index of H in G, denoted by [G : H] .

- Using these properties, we can prove Lagrange's theorem as follows:

  - Let G be a finite group and H be a subgroup of G. Then, by the first property, G can be partitioned into disjoint left cosets of H, say g1H, g2H, ..., gkH, where k is the index of H in G. Similarly, G can be partitioned into disjoint right cosets of H, say Hg1, Hg2, ..., Hk .
  - By the second property, each left coset of H has the same size as H, and each right coset of H has the same size as H. Therefore, |G| = k|H|, where k is the index of H in G .
  - By the third property, k = [G : H] is the number of left cosets of H, which is also the number of right cosets of H. Therefore, |G| = [G : H]|H| .
  - Since |G| and |H| are positive integers, it follows that |H| divides |G|, which is Lagrange's theorem .
