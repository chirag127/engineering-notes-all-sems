### Normal Subgroups

- A normal subgroup of a group is a subgroup that is invariant under conjugation by members of the group. In other words, a subgroup H of a group G is normal in G if and only if for all x in G and h in H, xhx<sup>-1</sup> belongs to H. The usual notation for this relation is H ⊴ G.
- Normal subgroups are important because they allow us to define factor groups, which are groups obtained by "collapsing" a normal subgroup to a single element. Factor groups have many applications in algebra and beyond.
- Some properties of normal subgroups are:
  - Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal, since xhx<sup>-1</sup> = h for any x and h in an abelian group.
  - The trivial subgroup {e} and the whole group G are always normal subgroups of G, for any group G.
  - If H and K are normal subgroups of G, then so are H ∩ K and HK (the subgroup generated by H and K).
  - If H is a normal subgroup of G and K is a subgroup of G, then H ∩ K is a normal subgroup of K and K/H is a normal subgroup of G/H (the factor group of G by H).
  - If H is a normal subgroup of G and g is an element of G, then gHg<sup>-1</sup> (the conjugate of H by g) is also a normal subgroup of G, and is isomorphic to H.
  - If H is a normal subgroup of G and N is a normal subgroup of H, then N is a normal subgroup of G if and only if N is invariant under conjugation by G, that is, gNg<sup>-1</sup> = N for all g in G.
- Some examples of normal subgroups are:
  - The center of a group, which is the set of elements that commute with every element of the group, is a normal subgroup of the group.
  - The commutator subgroup of a group, which is the subgroup generated by all the commutators of the group, that is, elements of the form [x, y] = xyx<sup>-1</sup>y<sup>-1</sup>, is a normal subgroup of the group.
  - The kernel of a homomorphism, which is the set of elements that are mapped to the identity by the homomorphism, is a normal subgroup of the domain of the homomorphism.
  - The alternating group A<sub>n</sub>, which is the subgroup of the symmetric group S<sub>n</sub> consisting of all the even permutations, is a normal subgroup of S<sub>n</sub> for any n ≥ 2.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the definition of a normal subgroup, you can use the acronym NICHE: Normal Invariant under Conjugation by g in gHg<sup>-1</sup>.
- To remember some properties of normal subgroups, you can use the acronym NICK: Normal Intersections, Conjugates, and Kernels.
- To remember some examples of normal subgroups, you can use the acronym CACK: Center, Alternating group, Commutator subgroup, and Kernel.
