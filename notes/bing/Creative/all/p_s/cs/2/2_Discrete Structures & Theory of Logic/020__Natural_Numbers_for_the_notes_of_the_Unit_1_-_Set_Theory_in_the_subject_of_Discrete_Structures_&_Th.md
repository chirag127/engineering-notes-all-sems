### Natural Numbers

- Natural numbers are the positive integers, including numbers from 1 to infinity .
- Natural numbers are also called counting numbers, as they are used to count and measure things .
- Natural numbers are a subset of the set of whole numbers, which also include zero .
- Natural numbers are denoted by the symbol **N** and can be written as:

  N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}

- Natural numbers have the following properties:

  - Closure property: The sum or product of any two natural numbers is always a natural number. For example, 3 + 5 = 8 and 4 × 6 = 24 are both natural numbers.
  - Commutative property: The order of addition or multiplication of any two natural numbers does not change the result. For example, 3 + 5 = 5 + 3 and 4 × 6 = 6 × 4.
  - Associative property: The grouping of addition or multiplication of any three natural numbers does not change the result. For example, (3 + 5) + 7 = 3 + (5 + 7) and (4 × 6) × 2 = 4 × (6 × 2).
  - Distributive property: The multiplication of a natural number by the sum or difference of two natural numbers is equal to the sum or difference of the products of the natural number with each of the two natural numbers. For example, 4 × (3 + 5) = (4 × 3) + (4 × 5) and 4 × (3 - 5) = (4 × 3) - (4 × 5).
  - Identity property: The sum of any natural number and zero is the same natural number. The product of any natural number and one is the same natural number. For example, 3 + 0 = 3 and 4 × 1 = 4.
  - Additive inverse property: There is no natural number that can be added to another natural number to get zero as the sum. For example, there is no natural number x such that 3 + x = 0.
  - Multiplicative inverse property: There is no natural number that can be multiplied by another natural number to get one as the product, except one itself. For example, there is no natural number x such that 4 × x = 1, except x = 1.

  - Cardinality: The number of elements in a set is called its cardinality, and it is a natural number. For example, the cardinality of the set {a, b, c} is 3.
  - Ordinality: The position of an element in a sequence or an ordered set is called its ordinality, and it is a natural number. For example, the ordinality of the element b in the sequence (a, b, c) is 2.
  - Divisibility: A natural number is divisible by another natural number if the quotient is also a natural number and the remainder is zero. For example, 12 is divisible by 3, but not by 5.
  - Factors: A natural number that divides another natural number is called a factor of that natural number. For example, 3 and 4 are factors of 12, but 5 is not.
  - Multiples: A natural number that is divisible by another natural number is called a multiple of that natural number. For example, 12 and 24 are multiples of 3, but 13 is not.
