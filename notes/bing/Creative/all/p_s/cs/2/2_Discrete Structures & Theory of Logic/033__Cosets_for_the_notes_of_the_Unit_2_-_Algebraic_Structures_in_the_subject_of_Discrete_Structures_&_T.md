### Cosets

- A coset is a subset of a mathematical group that consists of all the products obtained by multiplying a fixed element of the group by each of the elements of a given subgroup, either on the right or on the left.
- For example, if G is a group, H is a subgroup of G, and g is an element of G, then the left coset of H with respect to g is the set gH = {gh : h ∈ H}, and the right coset of H with respect to g is the set Hg = {hg : h ∈ H}.
- The number of left cosets of H in G is equal to the number of right cosets of H in G, and this number is called the index of H in G, denoted by [G : H].
- The index of H in G is equal to the quotient of the order of G and the order of H, that is, [G : H] = |G| / |H|.
- The left cosets of H in G form a partition of G, that is, they are disjoint and their union is G. The same is true for the right cosets of H in G.
- Two left cosets gH and g'H are either equal or disjoint, and the same is true for two right cosets Hg and Hg'. A criterion for equality of cosets is gH = g'H if and only if g^-1g' ∈ H, and Hg = Hg' if and only if gg'^-1 ∈ H.
- A subgroup H of G is called normal if its left cosets are the same as its right cosets, that is, gH = Hg for all g ∈ G. A normal subgroup is also denoted by H ⊲ G.
- If H is a normal subgroup of G, then the set of all cosets of H in G, denoted by G / H, forms a group under the operation of coset multiplication, defined by (gH)(g'H) = (gg')H. This group is called the quotient group or factor group of G by H.
- The quotient group G / H has the same order as the index of H in G, that is, |G / H| = [G : H].
- The quotient group G / H is isomorphic to a subgroup of the group of permutations of the cosets of H in G, called the symmetric group on [G : H] letters, denoted by S_[G : H]_. This is a consequence of the Cayley's theorem and the first isomorphism theorem.
