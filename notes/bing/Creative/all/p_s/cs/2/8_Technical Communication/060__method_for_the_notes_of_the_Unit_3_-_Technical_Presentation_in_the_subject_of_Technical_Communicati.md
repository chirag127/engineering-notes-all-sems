### Method for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that aims to inform, persuade, motivate or entertain a specific audience on a technical topic.
- A technical presentation requires careful planning, preparation, practice and delivery to achieve its purpose and objectives.
- A technical presentation should have the following characteristics:
  - It should be well adapted to the audience's needs, interests, expectations and background knowledge.
  - It should convey the required information in a clear, concise, accurate and logical manner.
  - It should use appropriate visuals, such as graphs, charts, diagrams, images, videos, etc., to support and enhance the verbal message.
  - It should have a clear structure, with an introduction, a body and a conclusion, and use transitions to connect the main points.
  - It should have a strong opening and closing, to capture the audience's attention and leave a lasting impression.
  - It should use effective verbal and non-verbal communication skills, such as voice, tone, pace, volume, eye contact, gestures, posture, etc., to engage the audience and convey confidence and credibility.
- A technical presentation can have different purposes, such as to inform or educate, to persuade, to motivate or inspire, or to entertain the audience.
- A technical presentation can use different methods, such as:
  - Lecture: A formal and authoritative presentation that delivers information or instruction on a topic.
  - Demonstration: A practical and interactive presentation that shows how to do something or how something works.
  - Case study: A detailed and analytical presentation that examines a specific problem, situation or example and its solution or outcome.
  - Panel discussion: A collaborative and conversational presentation that involves a group of experts or representatives who share their views or opinions on a topic and answer questions from the audience or a moderator.
  - Workshop: A participatory and experiential presentation that involves the audience in learning or applying a skill, technique or concept through activities, exercises or simulations.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for technical presentation are:

- To remember the characteristics of a good technical presentation, use the acronym **CLEAR**: Clear, Logical, Engaging, Accurate, Relevant.
- To remember the structure of a technical presentation, use the rhyme **Tell them what you're going to tell them, tell them, then tell them what you told them**. This means that you should have an introduction, a body and a conclusion, and summarize the main points at the beginning and the end of your presentation.
- To remember the different purposes of a technical presentation, use the acronym **IPME**: Inform, Persuade, Motivate, Entertain.
- To remember the different methods of a technical presentation, use the acronym **LCDPW**: Lecture, Demonstration, Case study, Panel discussion, Workshop.
- To remember the effective verbal and non-verbal communication skills for a technical presentation, use the acronym **VOICE**: Volume, Pace, Tone, Eye contact, Gestures, Posture.
