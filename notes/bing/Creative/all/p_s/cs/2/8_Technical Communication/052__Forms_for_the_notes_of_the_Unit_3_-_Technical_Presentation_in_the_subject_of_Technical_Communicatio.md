### Forms for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- Notes are a useful tool for preparing and delivering a technical presentation.
- Notes can help the presenter to remember the main points, elaborate on the details, and avoid reading from the slides.
- Notes can also help the presenter to anticipate questions, address potential objections, and provide additional information or examples.
- There are different forms of notes that can be used for a technical presentation, depending on the preference and purpose of the presenter.
- Some common forms of notes are:

  - **Speaker notes**: These are notes that are added to the slides in a presentation software, such as PowerPoint or Google Slides. They are visible only to the presenter and not to the audience. They can be printed out or viewed on a separate screen or device. Speaker notes can include keywords, phrases, sentences, or paragraphs that summarize or expand on the content of the slides. They can also include cues for transitions, timing, or delivery. Speaker notes can help the presenter to stay on track, avoid repetition, and maintain eye contact with the audience.  

  - **Outline notes**: These are notes that are written or typed in a separate document, such as a Word file or a notepad. They are organized in a hierarchical or bullet-point format, following the structure of the presentation. They can include the main points, subpoints, supporting details, and references for each slide or section. Outline notes can help the presenter to organize the information, recall the key ideas, and provide evidence or examples. They can also include reminders for questions, activities, or feedback. Outline notes can be used as a reference or a backup in case of technical issues.  

  - **Index cards**: These are notes that are written or printed on small pieces of paper, such as 3x5 or 4x6 cards. They are arranged in a sequential or logical order, corresponding to the slides or sections of the presentation. They can include the main points, keywords, facts, figures, or quotes for each slide or section. Index cards can help the presenter to memorize the information, emphasize the important points, and avoid reading from the slides. They can also include prompts for gestures, pauses, or emphasis. Index cards can be held in the hand or placed on a stand or a table.  

  - **Mind maps**: These are notes that are drawn or created in a graphical or diagrammatic form, such as a tree, a web, or a network. They are centered on a main topic or theme, and branch out into subtopics, concepts, or connections. They can include the main points, keywords, images, symbols, or colors for each slide or section. Mind maps can help the presenter to visualize the information, generate ideas, and show relationships. They can also include cues for creativity, storytelling, or humor. Mind maps can be sketched on a paper or a board, or created using a software or an app.  

- The choice of the form of notes depends on the preference and purpose of the presenter, as well as the context and audience of the presentation.
- Some factors to consider when choosing the form of notes are:

  - **Level of preparation**: The presenter should choose a form of notes that matches their level of preparation and confidence. For example, speaker notes or outline notes can be useful for a presenter who is less familiar with the topic or the audience, while index cards or mind maps can be useful for a presenter who is more comfortable with the content and the delivery.  

  - **Type of presentation**: The presenter should choose a form of notes that suits the type and style of the presentation. For example, speaker notes or outline notes can be useful for a formal or technical presentation that requires accuracy and clarity, while index cards or mind maps can be useful for an informal or creative presentation that allows flexibility and spontaneity.  

  - **Size of audience**: The presenter should choose a form of notes that adapts to the size and expectations of the audience. For example, speaker notes or outline notes can be useful for a large or diverse audience that needs more information and explanation, while index cards or mind maps can be useful for a small or familiar audience that prefers more interaction and engagement.
