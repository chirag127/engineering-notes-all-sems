Here are some examples of quiz questions for the unit 3 - technical presentation:

- Which of the following is a myth about presenting?
  - A) You need to memorize your entire speech
  - B) You need to use complex language and jargon
  - C) You need to avoid eye contact with the audience
  - D) None of the above
  - Answer: D
- What are the three main parts of a technical presentation?
  - A) Introduction, body, and conclusion
  - B) Problem, solution, and recommendation
  - C) Background, methods, and results
  - D) All of the above
  - Answer: D
- What are some of the benefits of using visual aids in a technical presentation?
  - A) They can enhance the clarity and impact of your message
  - B) They can attract and maintain the attention of your audience
  - C) They can reinforce and supplement your verbal communication
  - D) All of the above
  - Answer: D
- What are some of the tips for using presentation software effectively?
  - A) Choose a simple and consistent design for your slides
  - B) Use bullet points, images, charts, and graphs to illustrate your points
  - C) Avoid using too much text, animation, sound, and color
  - D) All of the above
  - Answer: D
- How can you improve your verbal and nonverbal communication skills in a technical presentation?
  - A) Practice your speech aloud and record yourself
  - B) Use appropriate tone, volume, pace, and pronunciation
  - C) Use gestures, facial expressions, and eye contact to convey your emotions and confidence
  - D) All of the above
  - Answer: D

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the three main parts of a technical presentation, you can use the acronym PIE: Problem, Introduction, and Evaluation.
- To remember the steps of planning a technical presentation, you can use the acronym SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
- To remember the types of visual aids, you can use the acronym PICT: Pictures, Icons, Charts, and Tables.
- To remember the tips for using presentation software, you can use the acronym KISS: Keep It Simple and Straightforward.
- To remember the elements of verbal and nonverbal communication, you can use the acronym VENOM: Voice, Eye contact, Nodding, Orientation, and Movement.
