### Style in Technical Communication

- Style in technical communication is the way one speaks or writes to convey technical information.
- Style in technical communication depends on the audience, the communicative context, and the purpose of communication.
- Style in technical communication is (almost always) concise, precise, direct, and well organized .
- Style in technical communication requires using meaningful, precise language that avoids ambiguity, jargon, and unnecessary words.
- Style in technical communication also involves using appropriate voice and tone that suit the situation and the relationship with the reader .
- Style in technical communication can vary from formal to informal, depending on the degree of formality, objectivity, and personalization required .
- Style in technical communication can be improved by following some guidelines and best practices, such as :
  - Use active voice instead of passive voice, unless there is a specific reason to do otherwise.
  - Use simple and clear sentences that avoid complex structures and modifiers.
  - Use consistent terminology and formatting throughout the document.
  - Use headings, lists, tables, and graphics to organize and present information effectively.
  - Use positive and polite language that avoids negative or harsh expressions.
  - Use inclusive and respectful language that avoids stereotypes, biases, and assumptions.
  - Use plain language that avoids technical jargon, acronyms, and abbreviations, unless they are well-defined and widely understood by the audience.
  - Use appropriate punctuation, grammar, spelling, and capitalization to ensure clarity and accuracy.
  - Use feedback and revision to refine and improve the style and quality of the document.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of style in technical communication are:

- To remember the four main characteristics of style in technical communication (concise, precise, direct, and well organized), you can use the acronym CPDW (pronounced as "see-pedew") or the phrase "Clear, Precise, Direct Writing".
- To remember the difference between active and passive voice, you can use the formula "Subject + Verb + Object" for active voice and "Object + Verb + Subject" for passive voice. For example, "The engineer designed the system" is active voice, while "The system was designed by the engineer" is passive voice.
- To remember the guidelines and best practices for style in technical communication, you can use the acronym PLUTO (pronounced as "plu-toe") or the phrase "Plain Language, Use of visuals, Tone, and Organization".
