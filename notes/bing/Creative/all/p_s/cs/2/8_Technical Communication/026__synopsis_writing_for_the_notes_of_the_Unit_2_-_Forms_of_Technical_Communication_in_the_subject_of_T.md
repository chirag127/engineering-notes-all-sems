### Synopsis Writing

- A synopsis is a brief summary of the main points and purpose of a longer document, such as a report, proposal, article, or book.
- A synopsis is usually written by the author of the document, and is intended to provide an overview of the document for potential readers, editors, or reviewers.
- A synopsis is different from an abstract, which is a more concise and formal summary that is included at the beginning of an academic paper.
- A synopsis is also different from an executive summary, which is a more detailed and persuasive summary that is included at the beginning of a business document, such as a proposal or a report.
- A synopsis should be clear, concise, accurate, and informative. It should highlight the main points, arguments, findings, and recommendations of the document, without going into too much detail or providing unnecessary information.
- A synopsis should also be coherent, logical, and well-organized. It should follow the same structure and order as the document, and use transitions and signposts to guide the reader.
- A synopsis should be written in the same tone and style as the document, and use the same terminology and conventions. It should also be consistent with the document in terms of spelling, grammar, punctuation, and formatting.
- A synopsis should be written in the third person and the present tense, unless otherwise specified by the document or the genre.
- A synopsis should be written after the document is completed, and revised and edited as necessary. It should be checked for accuracy, clarity, completeness, and coherence.
- A synopsis should be tailored to the needs and expectations of the intended audience, and the purpose and context of the document. It should also follow any specific guidelines or requirements provided by the document or the genre.

#### Example of a synopsis

The following is an example of a synopsis for a technical report on the feasibility of a solar-powered car.

> This report evaluates the feasibility of designing and manufacturing a solar-powered car for the Canadian market. It examines the technical, economic, environmental, and social aspects of the project, and compares the solar-powered car with other alternative fuel vehicles. The report concludes that the solar-powered car is technically feasible, but not economically viable, due to the high costs of production and maintenance, and the low demand and availability of solar energy in Canada. The report also identifies some of the environmental and social benefits and challenges of the solar-powered car, such as reducing greenhouse gas emissions, enhancing energy security, and creating public awareness and acceptance. The report recommends that the project should not be pursued at this time, but suggests some possible ways to improve the feasibility and viability of the solar-powered car in the future, such as developing more efficient and affordable solar panels, batteries, and motors, and collaborating with other stakeholders and partners.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of synopsis writing are:

- To remember the difference between a synopsis, an abstract, and an executive summary, you can use the acronym SAE: Synopsis is an Author's summary, Abstract is an Academic summary, and Executive summary is a Business summary.
- To remember the main elements of a synopsis, you can use the acronym CACIC: Clear, Accurate, Concise, Informative, and Coherent.
- To remember the structure and order of a synopsis, you can use the acronym IMRaD: Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion. This is the same structure as most technical and scientific documents.
