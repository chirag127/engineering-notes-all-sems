### The flow of Communication for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication is rhetorical: it is goal-driven, audience-focused, and dynamic. Technical communication responds to a specific rhetorical situation.
- The flow of communication refers to the way information moves from the sender to the receiver in a technical communication context.
- The flow of communication can be classified into four types: linear, interactive, transactional, and networked.

#### Linear communication
- Linear communication is the simplest and most basic form of communication. It involves a one-way transmission of information from the sender to the receiver, without any feedback or response.
- Linear communication is often used for delivering facts, instructions, or commands that do not require any clarification or discussion.
- Linear communication can be effective when the message is clear, concise, and accurate, and when the receiver is attentive and receptive.
- Linear communication can be ineffective when the message is ambiguous, complex, or incomplete, or when the receiver is distracted, confused, or resistant.
- An example of linear communication is a memo, a notice, or a manual.

#### Interactive communication
- Interactive communication is a more complex and dynamic form of communication. It involves a two-way exchange of information between the sender and the receiver, with feedback and response.
- Interactive communication is often used for clarifying, negotiating, or persuading, or for building rapport and trust with the receiver.
- Interactive communication can be effective when the message is relevant, respectful, and persuasive, and when the receiver is engaged and cooperative.
- Interactive communication can be ineffective when the message is irrelevant, disrespectful, or manipulative, or when the receiver is disinterested or hostile.
- An example of interactive communication is a meeting, a presentation, or a conversation.

#### Transactional communication
- Transactional communication is an advanced and holistic form of communication. It involves a continuous and simultaneous exchange of information between the sender and the receiver, with mutual influence and co-creation of meaning.
- Transactional communication is often used for collaborating, problem-solving, or creating, or for developing a shared understanding and vision with the receiver.
- Transactional communication can be effective when the message is authentic, creative, and meaningful, and when the receiver is open and supportive.
- Transactional communication can be ineffective when the message is artificial, repetitive, or superficial, or when the receiver is closed or critical.
- An example of transactional communication is a brainstorming session, a workshop, or a dialogue.

#### Networked communication
- Networked communication is a modern and sophisticated form of communication. It involves a multi-directional and multi-modal transmission of information among multiple senders and receivers, with interactivity and connectivity.
- Networked communication is often used for disseminating, accessing, or integrating information from various sources, or for creating a network of relationships and communities with the receivers.
- Networked communication can be effective when the message is timely, relevant, and diverse, and when the receivers are active and collaborative.
- Networked communication can be ineffective when the message is outdated, irrelevant, or redundant, or when the receivers are passive or competitive.
- An example of networked communication is a website, a blog, or a social media platform.
