### Distinction between General and Technical Communication

General communication and technical communication are two types of communication that differ in their purpose, style, audience, and format. Here are some of the main distinctions between them:

- **Purpose**: General communication is usually aimed at expressing personal opinions, feelings, experiences, or emotions. Technical communication is usually aimed at conveying factual information, instructions, data, or solutions to a specific problem.
- **Style**: General communication is informal, casual, and flexible. Technical communication is formal, precise, and consistent.
- **Audience**: General communication is not always intended for a specific audience, and may vary depending on the context, situation, or relationship of the communicators. Technical communication is intended for a specific audience, and may require adapting the language, tone, and level of detail to suit their needs, expectations, and background knowledge.
- **Format**: General communication has no set pattern or structure, and may use various modes of expression, such as speech, writing, gestures, or images. Technical communication follows a set pattern or structure, and may use various modes of presentation, such as reports, manuals, diagrams, charts, or graphs.
