### Lateral or Horizontal Communication

- Lateral or horizontal communication is the exchange of information, ideas, feedback, or suggestions among people, teams, or departments at the same level or hierarchy in an organization  .
- It can occur within a work unit, involving peers who report to the same supervisor, or across work units, involving individuals who report to different supervisors.
- It can also be diagonal, meaning that it involves people from different functional areas and different levels of authority.
- Lateral or horizontal communication can have various purposes, such as:
  - Coordination of tasks, activities, or projects
  - Sharing of information, knowledge, or expertise
  - Problem-solving or decision-making
  - Conflict resolution or negotiation
  - Innovation or creativity
  - Relationship-building or socialization
- Lateral or horizontal communication can have various advantages, such as  :
  - Improving collaboration and teamwork
  - Enhancing efficiency and productivity
  - Reducing errors and misunderstandings
  - Increasing innovation and creativity
  - Fostering learning and development
  - Boosting morale and motivation
  - Strengthening trust and rapport
- Lateral or horizontal communication can also have some challenges or disadvantages, such as  :
  - Creating information overload or confusion
  - Causing conflicts or disagreements
  - Wasting time or resources
  - Distorting or misinterpreting messages
  - Bypassing or undermining authority
  - Violating confidentiality or security
- Lateral or horizontal communication can be improved by  :
  - Clarifying the goals and expectations of communication
  - Choosing the appropriate channels and tools for communication
  - Providing timely and relevant feedback
  - Listening actively and empathetically
  - Respecting diversity and differences
  - Seeking and offering support
  - Encouraging and rewarding communication
- An example of lateral or horizontal communication is a project manager communicating with other project managers from different departments to coordinate their schedules and resources.
- Another example is a software engineer communicating with a graphic designer to discuss the user interface of a new product.
- A third example is a sales representative communicating with a customer service representative to resolve a customer complaint.
