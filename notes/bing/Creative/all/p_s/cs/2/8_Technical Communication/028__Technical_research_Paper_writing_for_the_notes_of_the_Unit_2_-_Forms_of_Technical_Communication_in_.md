### Technical Research Paper Writing

A technical research paper is a written document that talks about the process and results of conducting technical or scientific research for the purpose of finding solutions to a given problem. It is a form of technical communication that aims to inform, persuade, and instruct the readers about a specific topic. Technical research papers are similar to academic essays, but they are usually longer and more detailed assignments, designed to assess not only your writing skills but also your skills in scholarly research.

Some of the steps involved in writing a technical research paper are:

- Choose and define the topic: You should select a topic that is relevant, interesting, and feasible for your research. You should also narrow down the scope and focus of your topic by defining the research problem, question, or hypothesis that you want to address.
- Know your audience: You should analyze and find out who your potential readers are, what their background knowledge and expectations are, and what they need to know from your paper. You should also consider the purpose and tone of your paper, and tailor your writing accordingly .
- Research the topic: You should conduct a thorough and systematic literature review to find out what has been done and what gaps exist in your field of study. You should also collect and analyze relevant data, using appropriate methods and tools, to support your arguments and findings .
- Write the abstract last: The abstract is a concise summary of your paper that highlights the main points, such as the research problem, methods, results, and conclusions. You should write the abstract after you have completed the rest of the paper, and make sure it is clear, accurate, and informative .
- Make it readable: You should organize your paper into sections, such as introduction, literature review, methods, results, discussion, and conclusion, and use headings and subheadings to guide the readers. You should also use clear and precise language, avoid jargon and technical terms, and use examples, figures, tables, and graphs to illustrate your points  .
- Interpret the results of your project: You should discuss the implications and limitations of your results, and how they relate to the existing literature and the research problem. You should also provide recommendations for future research or practice, and state the main contributions and conclusions of your paper .
- Revise and submit it: You should proofread and edit your paper for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting errors, and check for consistency, clarity, and coherence. You should also follow the guidelines and requirements of the journal or conference where you want to publish or present your paper, and cite and reference your sources correctly  .

Here is an example of a technical research paper outline:

- Abstract
- Introduction
  - Background and context
  - Research problem and question
  - Significance and objectives
  - Overview of the paper
- Literature Review
  - Summary and synthesis of the existing literature
  - Identification of the research gap
  - Theoretical framework and hypotheses
- Methods
  - Description and justification of the research design
  - Data collection and analysis procedures
  - Ethical considerations and limitations
- Results
  - Presentation and description of the data
  - Use of tables, graphs, and figures
  - Statistical tests and results
- Discussion
  - Interpretation and explanation of the results
  - Comparison and contrast with the literature
  - Implications and limitations
  - Recommendations and suggestions
- Conclusion
  - Summary of the main points
  - Restatement of the research problem and question
  - Contributions and conclusions
  - Future directions
- References
- Appendices

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the steps of writing a technical research paper, you can use the acronym CRAWL: Choose and define the topic, Research the topic, Write the abstract last, Make it readable, and Interpret the results of your project.
- To remember the sections of a technical research paper, you can use the acronym ILMDRC: Introduction, Literature Review, Methods, Results, Discussion, and Conclusion.
- To remember the elements of the abstract, you can use the acronym PRIC: Problem, Results, Implications, and Conclusions.
- To remember the elements of the introduction, you can use the acronym BRISCO: Background, Research problem, Significance, Objectives, and Overview.
- To remember the elements of the literature review, you can use the acronym SLIT: Summary, Literature gap, Theoretical framework, and Hypotheses.
- To remember the elements of the methods, you can use the acronym DDE: Design, Data, and Ethics.
- To remember the elements of the results, you can use the acronym PUS: Presentation, Use of visuals, and Statistics.
- To remember the elements of the discussion, you can use the acronym ILCR: Interpretation, Literature comparison, Implications, and Recommendations.
- To remember the elements of the conclusion, you can use the acronym SROC: Summary, Restatement, Contributions, and Future directions.
