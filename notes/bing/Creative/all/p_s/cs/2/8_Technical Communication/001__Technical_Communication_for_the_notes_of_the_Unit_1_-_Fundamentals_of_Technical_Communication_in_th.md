### Technical Communication

Technical communication is a field of communication that involves conveying technical or specialized information to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Technical communication can be defined as  :

- The process of defining, creating and delivering information products for the safe, efficient and effective use of products (technical systems, software, services).
- The communication of expert, technical, or highly complex information to a non-expert or more general audience.
- The communication that works to convince an audience to take some specific action or to achieve some specific goal.

Some examples of technical communication are:

- User manuals, guides, tutorials, and online help for software, hardware, or services.
- Reports, proposals, memos, and presentations for business, academic, or scientific purposes.
- Websites, blogs, podcasts, and social media posts for technical or specialized topics.
- Brochures, flyers, posters, and advertisements for technical or specialized products or services.
- Articles, books, journals, and magazines for technical or specialized fields.

Some characteristics of technical communication are  :

- User-centered: Technical communication focuses on the needs, preferences, and expectations of the intended audience. It considers the context, background, and goals of the users and provides them with the information they need to perform a task, solve a problem, or make a decision.
- Clear and concise: Technical communication uses simple, precise, and unambiguous language to convey the information. It avoids unnecessary details, jargon, and technical terms that may confuse or distract the audience. It uses appropriate visuals, such as tables, graphs, diagrams, and images, to support the text and enhance the understanding of the information.
- Accurate and reliable: Technical communication provides factual, correct, and up-to-date information that is based on credible sources and evidence. It acknowledges the sources of the information and cites them properly. It also checks the information for errors, inconsistencies, and inaccuracies before delivering it to the audience.
- Ethical and respectful: Technical communication follows the ethical principles and standards of the field and the organization. It respects the rights, values, and beliefs of the audience and does not mislead, manipulate, or harm them. It also respects the intellectual property and privacy of the sources and the information.
- Accessible and usable: Technical communication designs and delivers the information in a way that is easy to access, understand, and use by the audience. It considers the format, medium, and channel of the communication and adapts them to the audience's needs and preferences. It also follows the accessibility guidelines and standards to ensure that the information is available and usable by people with different abilities and disabilities.

Some benefits of technical communication are:

- It improves the usability and functionality of the products, systems, or services that the information supports.
- It enhances the efficiency and productivity of the users who use the information to perform their tasks or achieve their goals.
- It reduces the risks and costs of errors, misunderstandings, or miscommunications that may result from the lack of or poor quality of the information.
- It increases the satisfaction and loyalty of the users who appreciate the quality and value of the information.
- It promotes the reputation and credibility of the organization or the individual who provides the information.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the definition of technical communication, you can use the acronym PACE: Process, Audience, Communication, Expertise.
- To remember the characteristics of technical communication, you can use the acronym CLEAR: Clear, Concise, Accurate, Ethical, Respectful.
- To remember the benefits of technical communication, you can use the acronym PURSE: Productivity, Usability, Risk, Satisfaction, Efficiency.
