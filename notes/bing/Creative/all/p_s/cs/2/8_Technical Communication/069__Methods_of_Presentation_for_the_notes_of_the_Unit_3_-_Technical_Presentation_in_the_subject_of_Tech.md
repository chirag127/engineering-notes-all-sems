### Methods of Presentation

Methods of presentation are the ways of delivering information to an audience in a technical communication context. There are different types of presentations, such as oral, written, visual, and multimedia, and each one has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the purpose, audience, and context of the communication.

Some of the common methods of presentation are:

- **Oral presentation**: This is a verbal delivery of information, usually accompanied by visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, or videos. Oral presentations can be formal or informal, depending on the tone, style, and level of interaction with the audience. Oral presentations can be effective for conveying complex or technical information, persuading or motivating the audience, or demonstrating a process or product. However, oral presentations also have some drawbacks, such as the need for preparation, rehearsal, and confidence, the possibility of technical glitches or interruptions, and the difficulty of reaching a large or diverse audience.

- **Written presentation**: This is a written document that provides information, such as a report, a proposal, a manual, or a brochure. Written presentations can be printed or electronic, and they can be linear or nonlinear, depending on the structure and organization of the information. Written presentations can be useful for providing detailed, accurate, and permanent information, allowing the audience to review and refer to the information at their own pace, or complying with legal or ethical requirements. However, written presentations also have some limitations, such as the need for clear and concise writing, the possibility of misinterpretation or misunderstanding, and the difficulty of engaging or retaining the audience's attention.

- **Visual presentation**: This is a presentation that uses images, such as diagrams, graphs, maps, photos, or icons, to convey information. Visual presentations can be standalone or complementary to oral or written presentations, and they can be static or dynamic, depending on the movement and interactivity of the images. Visual presentations can be beneficial for showing relationships, patterns, trends, or comparisons, illustrating concepts or processes, or enhancing the appeal or credibility of the information. However, visual presentations also have some challenges, such as the need for appropriate and accurate design, the possibility of distraction or confusion, and the difficulty of conveying complex or abstract information.

- **Multimedia presentation**: This is a presentation that combines different media, such as text, audio, video, animation, or simulation, to deliver information. Multimedia presentations can be interactive or non-interactive, depending on the level of control and feedback from the audience. Multimedia presentations can be advantageous for providing multiple modes of communication, catering to different learning styles or preferences, or creating an immersive or realistic experience. However, multimedia presentations also have some drawbacks, such as the need for technical skills and equipment, the possibility of overload or inconsistency, and the difficulty of evaluating or revising the information.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the types of presentations, you can use the acronym OWVM, which stands for Oral, Written, Visual, and Multimedia.
- To remember the advantages and disadvantages of each type of presentation, you can use the following table:

| Type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oral | Complex, persuasive, demonstrative | Preparation, rehearsal, confidence, glitches, interruptions, audience size |
| Written | Detailed, accurate, permanent, reviewable, legal, ethical | Writing, interpretation, misunderstanding, attention |
| Visual | Relationships, patterns, trends, comparisons, concepts, processes, appeal, credibility | Design, distraction, confusion, complexity, abstraction |
| Multimedia | Multiple modes, learning styles, preferences, immersive, realistic | Skills, equipment, overload, inconsistency, evaluation, revision |

- To remember the difference between linear and nonlinear written presentations, you can use the analogy of a book and a website. A book is linear, because it has a fixed order and sequence of pages, chapters, and sections. A website is nonlinear, because it has links, menus, and buttons that allow the user to navigate and access the information in different ways.
