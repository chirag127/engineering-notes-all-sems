### Technical Report

A technical report is a written document that conveys information about technical or scientific research in an objective and fact-based manner. It describes the process, progress, and results of the research, and includes in-depth experimental details, data, and analysis. A technical report can serve as a report of accountability to the organization funding the research, a means of communication among researchers, or a source of information for future reference.

Some characteristics of a technical report are:

- It is based on factual and verifiable data, not opinions or assumptions.
- It is clear, concise, and coherent, using appropriate technical terminology and avoiding jargon or slang.
- It is organized and structured according to a standard format, such as introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, and references.
- It follows the conventions of citation and referencing, acknowledging the sources of information and avoiding plagiarism.
- It uses tables, figures, graphs, and charts to present and illustrate data, and explains them in the text.
- It uses headings, subheadings, bullet points, and numbering to divide and highlight the main points and sections.
- It uses appendices to provide supplementary or supporting information that is not essential to the main text.

Some steps to write a technical report are:

- Define the purpose and scope of the report, and identify the target audience and their needs.
- Conduct a literature review to gather relevant and reliable information from previous research and sources.
- Plan and conduct the research, using appropriate methods and tools to collect and analyze data.
- Organize and outline the report, following the standard format and structure.
- Write the draft of the report, using clear and precise language, and following the guidelines of grammar, spelling, and punctuation.
- Revise and edit the report, checking for accuracy, clarity, coherence, and consistency.
- Proofread and format the report, ensuring that it meets the requirements of style, layout, and presentation.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the standard format of a technical report, use the acronym IMRaD: Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion.
- To remember the characteristics of a technical report, use the acronym FACTOR: Factual, Accurate, Clear, Concise, Coherent, and Organized.
- To remember the steps to write a technical report, use the acronym DORWEP: Define, Review, Research, Organize, Write, Edit, and Proofread.
