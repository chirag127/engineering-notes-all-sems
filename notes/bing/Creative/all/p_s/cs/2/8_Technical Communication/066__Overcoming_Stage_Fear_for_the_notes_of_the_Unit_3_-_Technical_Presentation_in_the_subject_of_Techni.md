### Overcoming Stage Fear

Stage fear, also known as performance anxiety or stage fright, is a common phenomenon that affects many people who have to speak, perform, or present in front of an audience. It can cause symptoms such as nervousness, sweating, trembling, dry mouth, rapid heartbeat, and difficulty concentrating. Stage fear can negatively impact one's self-confidence, communication skills, and career prospects.

However, stage fear can be overcome with some strategies and techniques that can help one prepare, relax, and focus on the task at hand. Here are some tips on how to overcome stage fear:

- **Handle your anxiety.** It is natural to feel anxious before a presentation in front of a large crowd, and there is nothing wrong in it. However, one should not let the anxiety overwhelm them or interfere with their performance. One way to handle anxiety is to acknowledge it and accept it as a normal response to a challenging situation. Another way is to reframe it as a positive sign of excitement and enthusiasm. One can also use breathing exercises, positive affirmations, and visualization techniques to calm their nerves and boost their confidence.

- **Take passion.** One of the best ways to overcome stage fear is to be passionate about the topic or the performance. Passion can help one overcome their fear of failure, criticism, or rejection, and motivate them to deliver their best. Passion can also help one connect with the audience and create a rapport. One should choose a topic or a performance that they are genuinely interested in, or find a way to make it interesting for themselves and the audience.

- **Practice more.** Practice makes perfect, and this is especially true for overcoming stage fear. One should practice their speech, performance, or presentation multiple times until they feel confident and comfortable with it. One should also practice in front of a mirror, a recorder, a camera, or a small audience of friends or family, and get feedback and suggestions for improvement. One should also practice in a similar setting and environment as the actual event, and familiarize themselves with the equipment, the stage, and the audience .

- **Relax your body.** Physical relaxation can help reduce the symptoms of stage fear and improve one's performance. One should exercise and move their body before the event, as this can release endorphins and reduce stress. One should also watch a funny video, listen to music, meditate, or do any other activity that can help them relax and loosen up. One should also avoid caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, or any other substance that can increase anxiety or impair their performance.

- **Know when to stop being nervous.** Nervousness is normal and helpful before a presentation or a performance, as it can help one prepare and perform better. However, one should not let the nervousness persist during or after the event, as this can affect their performance and satisfaction. One should know when to stop being nervous and switch to a confident and positive mode. One way to do this is to use a cue or a trigger, such as a word, a gesture, or a smile, that can signal to oneself and the audience that they are ready and confident.

- **Use laughter as a remedy.** Laughter is a powerful tool that can help one overcome stage fear and connect with the audience. Laughter can help one release tension, reduce anxiety, and improve mood. Laughter can also help one create a friendly and relaxed atmosphere, and make the audience more receptive and attentive. One can use humor, jokes, anecdotes, or funny stories to make their presentation or performance more engaging and enjoyable. However, one should be careful not to use offensive, inappropriate, or irrelevant humor, and avoid laughing at their own mistakes or nervousness.

- **Arrive early.** One of the worst things that can increase stage fear is to arrive late or unprepared for the event. This can create a lot of stress, anxiety, and confusion, and affect one's performance and impression. One should arrive early and check the venue, the equipment, the stage, and the audience. One should also use the time to warm up, rehearse, and relax. One should also greet and interact with the audience, the organizers, and the other speakers or performers, and establish a rapport and a connection.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for overcoming stage fear are:

- **LMNOP Cycle.** This is a formula created by Kamal Nanavati, a former Navy SEAL, to help you overcome fear by changing your label, meaning, brain, option, and purpose. The steps are:

  - **L**abel and Language: Identify the emotion you are feeling and name it. For example, "I am feeling nervous."
  - **M**eaning: Understand the meaning that led to the emotion. For example, "I am feeling nervous because I think I will mess up my presentation and embarrass myself."
  - **N**ot you, it's your brain: Realize that the emotion is not your fault, but a result of your brain's response to a perceived threat. For example, "I am feeling nervous because my brain is activating the fight-or-flight response to protect me from danger."
  - **O**pt for a new meaning: Choose a new meaning that is more positive and empowering. For example, "I am feeling nervous because I care about my presentation and I want to do well."
  - **P**urpose and Pre-emptive strikes: Choose an action that aligns with your higher purpose and circle back with pre-emptive strikes. For example, "I will focus on delivering my message and connecting with my audience, and I will practice, breathe, and visualize my success before the presentation."

- **False Evidence Appearing Real.** This is a popular acronym that reminds you that fear is often based on false or exaggerated assumptions that appear real in your mind. For example, "I am afraid of speaking in public because I think everyone will judge me and laugh at me." This acronym can help you challenge and question your fear and replace it with more realistic and positive thoughts. For example, "I am not afraid of speaking in public because I know most people are supportive and interested in what I have to say."

- **Five Minutes Rule.** This is a simple trick that helps you reduce your anxiety by focusing on the first five minutes of your presentation or performance. Instead of worrying about the whole event, just concentrate on getting through the first five minutes, which are usually the most difficult and nerve-wracking. Once you get past the first five minutes, you will likely feel more relaxed and confident, and the rest will be easier.
