### Structure of Technical Communication

Technical communication is the process of conveying expert, technical, or highly complex information to a non-expert or more general audience. Technical communication often works to convince an audience to take some specific action, such as following instructions, making a decision, or adopting a new perspective. Technical communication might define, instruct, inform, or persuade .

The structure of technical communication is based on the following steps:

1. Determine purpose and audience: The technical communicator needs to identify the goal of the communication and the needs, expectations, and background of the intended audience.
2. Collect information: The technical communicator needs to gather relevant and reliable information from various sources, such as research, observation, interviews, surveys, etc. The technical communicator also needs to evaluate the credibility and accuracy of the information.
3. Organize and outline information: The technical communicator needs to structure the information into a logical and coherent framework that can guide detailed development. The technical communicator also needs to choose the appropriate format, style, and tone for the communication, such as a report, a manual, a presentation, etc.
4. Write the first draft: The technical communicator needs to write the content using clear, concise, and precise language that is suitable for the audience and the purpose. The technical communicator also needs to use appropriate visual aids, such as tables, graphs, diagrams, etc., to enhance the communication.
5. Revise and edit: The technical communicator needs to review the draft and make necessary changes to improve the content, organization, style, and accuracy of the communication. The technical communicator also needs to proofread the draft and check for any errors in grammar, spelling, punctuation, etc.
6. Publish output: The technical communicator needs to deliver the final product to the audience using the appropriate medium, such as print, online, or oral. The technical communicator also needs to solicit feedback from the audience and evaluate the effectiveness of the communication.

The structure of technical communication can vary depending on the specific type, context, and scope of the communication, but the general steps outlined above can help the technical communicator to produce high-quality and user-friendly documents.
