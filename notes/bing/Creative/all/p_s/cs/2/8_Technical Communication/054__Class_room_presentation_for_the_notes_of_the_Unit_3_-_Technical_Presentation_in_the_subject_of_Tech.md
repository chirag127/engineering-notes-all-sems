### Classroom presentation for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A classroom presentation is a way of communicating technical information to an audience, usually students or instructors, in an academic setting.
- A classroom presentation can be used to:
  - Condense a longer text, such as a formal report
  - Summarize the most important, useful, or meaningful information from that text
  - Demonstrate learning through testing, replication, or design
  - Translate technical information into lay terms
  - Engage the audience with visual and audio elements
  - Persuade the audience to take action or adopt a viewpoint
- A classroom presentation should follow these steps:
  - Know your audience: consider their background, expectations, needs, and interests
  - Determine your objective: what do you want to accomplish with your presentation?
  - Decide your main message: what is the key point or takeaway for your audience?
  - Build your presentation: organize your content into an introduction, body, and conclusion
  - Plan your opening: grab attention with a hook, such as a question, a story, a statistic, or a quote
  - Plan your closing: move people into action with a call to action, a summary, a recommendation, or a question
  - Design your slides: use clear and consistent fonts, colors, and layouts; avoid clutter and distractions; use images, charts, graphs, or videos to support your message; cite your sources
  - Practice your delivery: rehearse your presentation several times; use notes or cue cards; speak clearly and confidently; use eye contact, gestures, and vocal variety; avoid filler words and jargon; check your timing and equipment
  - Get feedback: ask for constructive criticism from peers, instructors, or mentors; revise your presentation based on the feedback; repeat the process until you are satisfied
- A classroom presentation should be evaluated based on the following criteria:
  - Content: is the presentation relevant, accurate, complete, and well-organized?
  - Structure: does the presentation have a clear introduction, body, and conclusion?
  - Style: does the presentation use appropriate language, tone, and voice for the audience and purpose?
  - Visuals: do the slides enhance the presentation and convey the message effectively?
  - Delivery: does the presenter speak with clarity, confidence, and enthusiasm?
  - Audience: does the presenter engage the audience and respond to their questions or comments?
