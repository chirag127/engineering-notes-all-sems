### Clarity of substance

Clarity of substance is the quality of presenting the content of a technical presentation in a clear, accurate, and well-organized manner. It involves choosing the appropriate level of detail, language, and structure for the audience and the purpose of the presentation. Clarity of substance helps the presenter to convey the main message, avoid confusion, and establish credibility.

Some of the factors that contribute to clarity of substance are:

- **Defining the scope and objective of the presentation.** The presenter should state the topic, the main question or problem, and the goal of the presentation at the beginning. This helps the audience to understand the context and the relevance of the presentation.
- **Organizing the content logically and coherently.** The presenter should use a clear and consistent structure to organize the content of the presentation. The structure should include an introduction, a body, and a conclusion. The introduction should provide an overview of the topic, the objective, and the main points of the presentation. The body should present the main points in a logical order, using transitions and signposts to guide the audience. The conclusion should summarize the main points, restate the objective, and provide a call to action or a recommendation.
- **Using appropriate level of detail and language.** The presenter should tailor the content of the presentation to the audience's level of knowledge, interest, and expectations. The presenter should avoid unnecessary jargon, technical terms, and acronyms, or explain them clearly if they are essential. The presenter should also use clear and precise language, avoiding ambiguity, vagueness, and redundancy.
- **Supporting the content with evidence and examples.** The presenter should provide relevant and reliable evidence and examples to support the claims and arguments of the presentation. The evidence and examples should be cited and referenced properly, following the conventions of the field and the format of the presentation. The presenter should also acknowledge any limitations, assumptions, or alternative perspectives on the topic.
