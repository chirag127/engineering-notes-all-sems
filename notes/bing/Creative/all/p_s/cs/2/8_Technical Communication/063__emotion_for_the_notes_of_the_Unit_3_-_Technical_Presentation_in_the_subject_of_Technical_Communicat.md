### Emotion in Technical Presentation

- Emotion is the feeling or affective state that influences the way we think, act, and communicate.
- Emotion can be a powerful tool in technical presentation, as it can help to capture the audience's attention, interest, and motivation, as well as to persuade them to accept the presenter's message or take action.
- However, emotion can also be a double-edged sword, as it can also distract, confuse, or alienate the audience, or undermine the presenter's credibility or professionalism, if used inappropriately or excessively.
- Therefore, it is important to balance the facts and emotions properly in technical presentation, and to use emotion strategically and ethically, according to the purpose, context, and audience of the presentation.
- Here are some tips on how to use emotion effectively in technical presentation:

  - Develop an emotional message. Identify the main idea or goal of your presentation, and craft a message that appeals to the audience's needs, values, or emotions, as well as to their logic and reason. For example, you can use a story, an analogy, a metaphor, a quote, a statistic, or a question to hook the audience's interest and curiosity, and to show them why your topic matters to them or to others.
  - Use appropriate emotional language. Choose words and phrases that convey the tone, mood, and attitude that you want to express or elicit in your presentation. For example, you can use positive, optimistic, or enthusiastic language to inspire or motivate the audience, or use negative, pessimistic, or critical language to warn or challenge the audience. However, avoid using overly emotional, exaggerated, or biased language that may sound unprofessional, manipulative, or disrespectful.
  - Use nonverbal cues to enhance emotion. Use your voice, facial expressions, gestures, posture, and eye contact to reinforce the emotional message that you want to communicate or evoke in your presentation. For example, you can use a loud, clear, and confident voice to show your passion or authority, or use a soft, slow, and calm voice to show your empathy or sincerity. You can also use smiles, nods, frowns, or eye rolls to express your approval, disapproval, surprise, or sarcasm. However, avoid using nonverbal cues that may contradict, weaken, or distract from your verbal message, or that may offend or annoy the audience.
  - Use visual aids to support emotion. Use images, graphs, charts, diagrams, videos, or animations to illustrate, emphasize, or clarify your emotional message, and to appeal to the audience's senses, imagination, or emotions. For example, you can use a photo, a video, or a cartoon to show a real-life example, a problem, or a solution, or to make a comparison, a contrast, or a joke. However, avoid using visual aids that may be irrelevant, confusing, or misleading, or that may overwhelm or bore the audience.
  - Adapt to the audience and the situation. Consider the characteristics, expectations, and preferences of your audience, and the purpose, context, and constraints of your presentation, and adjust your use of emotion accordingly. For example, you can use more emotion to engage a general or lay audience, or to persuade or inspire them to take action, or use less emotion to inform a technical or expert audience, or to demonstrate your competence or credibility. You can also use different types of emotion depending on the topic, the tone, and the mood of your presentation, such as using humor, curiosity, or excitement to introduce a new or complex topic, or using anger, fear, or sadness to highlight a serious or urgent issue. However, avoid using emotion that may be inappropriate, irrelevant, or insensitive to the audience or the situation, or that may violate the ethical or professional standards of your field or organization.
