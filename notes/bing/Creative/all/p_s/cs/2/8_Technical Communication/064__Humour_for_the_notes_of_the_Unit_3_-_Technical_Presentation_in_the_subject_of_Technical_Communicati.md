### Humour

Humour is the use of language, gestures, images, or sounds to evoke amusement, laughter, or enjoyment in the audience. Humour can be an effective tool to enhance a technical presentation, as it can:

- Capture and maintain the audience's attention
- Create a rapport and connection with the audience
- Make the presentation more memorable and persuasive
- Reduce the tension and boredom of a complex or dry topic
- Show the speaker's confidence and personality

However, humour can also backfire if it is inappropriate, offensive, irrelevant, or overdone. Therefore, it is important to use humour carefully and strategically in a technical presentation. Here are some tips and techniques to use humour effectively:

- Incorporate humour naturally. Use anecdotes and funny stories from things that have happened in real life, preferably related to the topic or the audience. Avoid jokes that are scripted, forced, or clichéd.
- Know your audience. Consider the cultural, professional, and personal backgrounds of your audience, and tailor your humour accordingly. Avoid humour that is insensitive, disrespectful, or controversial to your audience. Also, avoid humour that is too technical, obscure, or self-deprecating for your audience to understand or appreciate.
- Use humour sparingly. Use humour as a spice, not as the main dish. Too much humour can distract from the main message, undermine your credibility, or annoy your audience. Use humour only when it supports or enhances your point, not when it detracts or contradicts it.
- Use humour appropriately. Use humour only when it is relevant, timely, and suitable for the context and tone of your presentation. Avoid humour that is irrelevant, outdated, or inappropriate for the occasion, purpose, or audience of your presentation.
- Use humour creatively. Use different forms and mediums of humour, such as verbal, visual, or audio. For example, you can use wordplay, puns, metaphors, similes, irony, sarcasm, exaggeration, or understatement in your speech. You can also use images, cartoons, memes, videos, or sound effects in your slides or handouts. However, make sure that the humour is clear, simple, and easy to follow for your audience.

Here are some examples of humour techniques that can be used in a technical presentation:

- Verbal humour: Use wordplay, puns, metaphors, similes, irony, sarcasm, exaggeration, or understatement in your speech. For example, "This software is so easy to use, even a monkey can do it. In fact, we tested it on monkeys, and they loved it."
- Visual humour: Use images, cartoons, memes, videos, or sound effects in your slides or handouts. For example, "Here is a graph showing the correlation between coffee consumption and productivity. As you can see, there is a positive relationship, until you reach the point of caffeine overdose."
- Multimedia humour: Use a combination of verbal and visual humour, such as a caption, a quote, or a dialogue that matches or contrasts with an image or a video. For example, "This is a picture of our team working hard on the project. As you can see, we had a lot of fun. And by fun, I mean stress, frustration, and sleep deprivation."
- Anecdotal humour: Use anecdotes and funny stories from things that have happened in real life, preferably related to the topic or the audience. For example, "When I was a student, I had to do a presentation on quantum physics. I was so nervous that I forgot everything I had prepared. So I just started making up stuff, hoping that no one would notice. Luckily, no one did, because no one understood anything I said."
- Self-deprecating humour: Use humour that makes fun of yourself, your mistakes, or your weaknesses, in a modest and humble way. For example, "I'm not an expert on this topic, I'm just a beginner. In fact, I learned everything I know from Wikipedia. Don't worry, I checked the sources."
- Audience-related humour: Use humour that involves or relates to the audience, such as asking a question, making a comment, or giving a compliment. For example, "How many of you have used this software before? Raise your hand if you have. Wow, that's impressive. You must be very smart. Or very bored."

Mnemonics are memory tricks that can help you remember new information by connecting it to something you already know. There are different types of mnemonics, such as rhymes, acronyms, diagrams, or key words. For example, you can use the acronym HOMES to remember the names of the Great Lakes: Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, and Superior. Or you can use the rhyme "Thirty days hath September, April, June, and November" to remember how many days are in each month.

Some mnemonics are easy to remember because they are catchy, funny, or make sense. Others may be harder to remember because they are too long, complicated, or don't relate to the topic. The best mnemonics are the ones that work for you, so you may want to create your own or modify the ones you find online. To use mnemonics effectively, you should:

- Choose the appropriate mnemonic for your situation. For example, if your goal is to learn how to spell a word, you may want to use the spelling mnemonic technique.
- Practice the technique. You may want to practice your mnemonic several times to help you remember it.
- Repeat the mnemonic to others. This can help you reinforce the information and check if you are using it correctly.

Mnemonics can be a useful tool for memory improvement, but they are not a substitute for understanding the material. You should also try to review the information regularly, use different senses to learn it, and apply it to real-life situations. This way, you can make the most of your learning and retention.😊
