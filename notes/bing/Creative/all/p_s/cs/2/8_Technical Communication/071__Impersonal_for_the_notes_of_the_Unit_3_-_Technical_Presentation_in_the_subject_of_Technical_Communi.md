### Impersonal

- Impersonal communication is a style of communication that avoids using personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, we, etc.) and focuses on conveying data and important information.
- Impersonal communication is common in technical communication, where the goal is to inform, instruct, or persuade the audience, rather than to express personal opinions or emotions.
- Impersonal communication can be achieved by using the passive voice, the third person, or the indefinite pronoun one. For example:

  - Passive voice: "The wire was cut" instead of "I cut the wire".
  - Third person: "The engineer calculated the load" instead of "You calculated the load".
  - Indefinite pronoun: "One should always check the voltage" instead of "You should always check the voltage".

- Impersonal communication has some advantages and disadvantages in technical communication. Some of the advantages are:

  - It can create a sense of objectivity and professionalism, as it avoids personal bias and subjectivity.
  - It can avoid ambiguity and confusion, as it clarifies who or what is performing the action or being affected by it.
  - It can avoid offending or alienating the audience, as it does not make assumptions or judgments about them.

- Some of the disadvantages are:

  - It can create a sense of detachment and impersonality, as it does not acknowledge or engage the audience as individuals.
  - It can reduce the impact and persuasiveness of the message, as it does not appeal to the emotions or values of the audience.
  - It can obscure the responsibility and accountability of the writer or the source of the information, as it does not identify who or what is behind the claims or recommendations.

- Impersonal communication should be used with caution and balance in technical communication, as it may not be appropriate or effective for every situation or audience. Some of the factors to consider are:

  - The purpose and context of the communication: Is the communication intended to inform, instruct, persuade, or entertain? Is the communication formal or informal, academic or professional, technical or general?
  - The audience and their expectations: Who is the audience and what are their needs, interests, and preferences? How familiar are they with the topic and the terminology? How do they perceive the writer and the source of the information?
  - The tone and voice of the communication: What is the attitude and personality of the writer and the source of the information? How do they want to relate to the audience and establish their credibility and authority? How do they want to convey their confidence and competence?

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the difference between passive and active voice, use the acronym PAST: Passive voice has the Agent (doer) after the verb, while active voice has the Subject (doer) before the verb.
- To remember the advantages of impersonal communication, use the acronym CAP: Impersonal communication creates a sense of Clarity, Accuracy, and Professionalism.
- To remember the disadvantages of impersonal communication, use the acronym RAP: Impersonal communication reduces the sense of Rapport, Appeal, and Personality.
- To remember the factors to consider when choosing between impersonal and personal communication, use the acronym ACT: Consider the purpose and context (Aim), the audience and their expectations (Characteristics), and the tone and voice (Tone) of the communication.
