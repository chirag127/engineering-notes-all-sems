### C.V./Resume Writing for Technical Communication

A C.V. (curriculum vitae) or a resume is a document that summarizes your education, skills, work experience, and achievements for a potential employer. It is a common form of technical communication that showcases your qualifications and suitability for a job. A well-written C.V. or resume can help you stand out from other candidates and increase your chances of getting an interview.

Here are some tips and examples for writing a technical C.V. or resume:

- Start with the reverse-chronological format. This means listing your most recent or relevant work experience first, followed by your education, skills, and other sections. This format helps the employer see your current level of expertise and achievements.
- Work with CV fonts like Georgia or Lato in 10–12pt. These fonts are easy to read and professional-looking. Use white space as a buffer, and 1-inch margins. Deliver a one-page CV to most technical job openings.
- Include a header that contains your full name and contact information, such as your phone number, email address, and LinkedIn profile. You can also add a professional title or a summary statement that highlights your main skills and goals.
- In the work experience section, list your most impressive accomplishments instead of tasks and responsibilities. Use bullet points and action verbs to describe what you did and how you contributed to the project or organization. Quantify your results with numbers, percentages, or metrics whenever possible. Examples include:

  - Spearheaded the development of a new user interface that increased customer satisfaction by 20%.
  - Mentored a team of 15 web developers and improved their productivity by 25%.
  - Implemented a new communication system that reduced errors and downtime by 40%.

- In the education section, list your degrees, diplomas, certificates, or courses that are relevant to the technical field you are applying for. Include the name of the institution, the location, the dates of attendance, and any honors or awards you received. You can also mention any relevant coursework, projects, or publications that demonstrate your skills and knowledge.
- In the skills section, list the technical skills that are required or preferred for the job. These can include software, hardware, tools, languages, frameworks, methodologies, or standards that you are proficient in. You can also mention any soft skills, such as communication, teamwork, problem-solving, or leadership, that are relevant to the role.
- You can also add other sections, such as certifications, licenses, memberships, hobbies, or interests, that showcase your achievements or personality. However, make sure they are related to the job or industry you are applying for, and do not take up too much space on your C.V. or resume.
- Proofread your C.V. or resume for spelling, grammar, punctuation, and formatting errors. Use consistent fonts, colors, and styles throughout your document. Avoid using jargon, slang, or abbreviations that may confuse the employer or the applicant tracking system (ATS). Use keywords and phrases that match the job description and the employer's expectations.

Here are some mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of C.V./resume writing:

- To remember the reverse-chronological format, think of the acronym RACE: Recent, Achievements, Chronological, Experience.
- To remember the types of skills to include, think of the acronym STAR: Software, Tools, Applications, Relevant.
- To remember the elements of a summary statement, think of the acronym CAP: Career, Achievements, Purpose.
- To remember the action verbs to use, think of the acronym CAR: Created, Achieved, Resolved.
- To remember the types of numbers to use, think of the acronym SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
