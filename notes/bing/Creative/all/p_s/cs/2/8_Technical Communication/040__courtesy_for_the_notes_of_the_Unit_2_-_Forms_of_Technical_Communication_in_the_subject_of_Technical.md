### Courtesy

- Courtesy is one of the key components of effective communication, especially in technical and business contexts.
- Courtesy means showing respect, consideration and goodwill to the receiver of the message, as well as avoiding potentially offensive or biased language, tone and usage.
- Courtesy can enhance the clarity, accuracy and persuasiveness of the message, as well as the credibility and professionalism of the sender.
- Courtesy can also help to prevent or resolve conflicts, misunderstandings and negative emotions that may arise from communication.
- Some of the ways to achieve courtesy in technical communication are:

  - Use positive and polite words that reflect the "you" attitude, such as "please", "thank you", "appreciate", "glad", etc.
  - Avoid negative and harsh words that may imply criticism, blame, threat, sarcasm, etc., such as "must", "fail", "wrong", "never", etc.
  - Address the audience appropriately, using titles, names, pronouns and modes of address that suit the context, relationship and level of formality.
  - Show interest and empathy for the audience's needs, expectations, feelings and viewpoints, and avoid making assumptions or judgments about them.
  - Use inclusive and respectful language that avoids stereotypes, discrimination, bias or prejudice based on gender, race, ethnicity, religion, disability, age, etc.
  - Design a reader-friendly and easy-to-read document, using clear and concise language, proper format, layout, headings, lists, visuals, etc.
  - Proofread and revise the document to eliminate errors, ambiguities, inconsistencies and redundancies that may affect the quality and effectiveness of the message.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of courtesy are:

- To remember the positive and polite words that reflect the "you" attitude, you can use the acronym PTA: Please, Thank you, Appreciate.
- To remember the negative and harsh words that may imply criticism, blame, threat, sarcasm, etc., you can use the acronym FAWN: Fail, Wrong, Never, Must.
- To remember the ways to address the audience appropriately, you can use the acronym TAN: Titles, Names, Pronouns.
- To remember the ways to show interest and empathy for the audience's needs, expectations, feelings and viewpoints, you can use the acronym EASE: Empathy, Attention, Support, Encouragement.
- To remember the ways to use inclusive and respectful language that avoids stereotypes, discrimination, bias or prejudice, you can use the acronym FAIR: Fair, Accurate, Informed, Respectful.
- To remember the ways to design a reader-friendly and easy-to-read document, you can use the acronym CLEAR: Concise, Logical, Effective, Attractive, Relevant.
- To remember the ways to proofread and revise the document to eliminate errors, ambiguities, inconsistencies and redundancies, you can use the acronym CARE: Correct, Accurate, Reliable, Effective.
