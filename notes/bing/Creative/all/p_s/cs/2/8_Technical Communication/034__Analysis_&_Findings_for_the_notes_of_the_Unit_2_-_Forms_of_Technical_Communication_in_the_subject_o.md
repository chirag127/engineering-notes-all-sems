### Analysis & Findings

- Analysis and findings are the two main components of a technical report, which is a form of technical communication that conveys pertinent information to upper management or other stakeholders for decision-making purposes.
- Analysis is the process of examining, interpreting, and evaluating data, evidence, or information collected from various sources, such as experiments, surveys, interviews, observations, etc. Analysis aims to identify patterns, trends, relationships, causes, effects, strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats, etc. that are relevant to the problem or purpose of the report.
- Findings are the results or outcomes of the analysis, which are presented in a clear, concise, and logical manner, using tables, graphs, charts, diagrams, etc. as appropriate. Findings should answer the research questions or objectives, address the problem or purpose of the report, and support the conclusions and recommendations.
- Some examples of technical reports are proposals, regulations, manuals, procedures, requests, progress reports, emails, and memos. Each type of technical report has its own format, structure, and style, depending on the audience, purpose, and context of the communication.
- Some common elements of a technical report are title page, abstract, table of contents, introduction, background, methods, analysis, findings, conclusions, recommendations, references, and appendices. However, these elements may vary depending on the type and scope of the report.
- Some general guidelines for writing a technical report are :
  - Define the problem or purpose of the report clearly and concisely.
  - Conduct a thorough research and analysis of the relevant data, evidence, or information.
  - Organize the report logically and coherently, using headings, subheadings, and transitions.
  - Use clear, simple, and accurate language, avoiding jargon, slang, and ambiguity.
  - Use appropriate visual aids, such as tables, graphs, charts, diagrams, etc. to present the findings effectively and efficiently.
  - Provide sufficient details, explanations, and examples to support the findings, conclusions, and recommendations.
  - Cite the sources of information properly and consistently, using a standard citation style, such as APA, MLA, IEEE, etc.
  - Proofread and edit the report carefully, checking for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting errors.
