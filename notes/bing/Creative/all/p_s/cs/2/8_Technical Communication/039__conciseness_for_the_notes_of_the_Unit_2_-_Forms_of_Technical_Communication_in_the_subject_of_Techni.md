### Conciseness

- Conciseness is one of the six qualities of effective communication, along with clarity, correctness, completeness, coherence, and courtesy.
- Conciseness means saying what needs to be said in as few words as possible, without sacrificing clarity or accuracy .
- Conciseness can make your communication more transparent, engaging, and persuasive to the readers or listeners .
- Conciseness can also save time, space, and resources for both the sender and the receiver of the message.
- Conciseness can be achieved by applying various techniques, such as:

  - Eliminating unnecessary words, phrases, or repetitions .
  - Using specific and concrete words instead of vague and abstract ones .
  - Using active voice instead of passive voice, unless the passive voice is more appropriate for the context or purpose .
  - Using short and simple sentences and paragraphs, but avoiding sentence fragments or run-ons  .
  - Using lists, tables, graphs, or diagrams to present information visually, when possible  .
  - Using transitions, headings, subheadings, and other signposts to guide the reader through the document .

- Conciseness is especially important in technical communication, as technical documents often contain complex and detailed information that needs to be conveyed clearly and precisely .
- Conciseness can help technical communicators to:

  - Avoid confusion, misunderstanding, or misinterpretation of the information .
  - Enhance the credibility, authority, and professionalism of the document and the writer .
  - Meet the expectations and needs of the audience, who may have limited time, attention, or background knowledge  .
  - Achieve the purpose and goals of the document, such as informing, instructing, persuading, or recommending  .

- Conciseness is not the same as brevity, which means being brief or concise without regard to clarity or accuracy. Conciseness does not mean omitting important or relevant information, or using jargon, acronyms, or abbreviations that may confuse the reader  .
- Conciseness is a skill that can be improved with practice and feedback. It is advisable to revise and edit the document several times, and to seek feedback from peers, instructors, or experts, to ensure that the document is concise, clear, and accurate  .

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of conciseness are:

- To remember the six qualities of effective communication, use the acronym C6: Clarity, Correctness, Completeness, Coherence, Courtesy, and Conciseness.
- To remember the techniques for achieving conciseness, use the acronym EUSALT: Eliminate, Use specific words, Use active voice, Use short sentences, Use lists or visuals, Use transitions .
- To remember the benefits of conciseness in technical communication, use the acronym ACE: Avoid confusion, Enhance credibility, Meet expectations  .
- To remember the difference between conciseness and brevity, use the phrase "Be concise, not brief" or "Concise is nice, brief is grief" .
