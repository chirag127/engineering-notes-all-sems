### Completeness

Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective communication. It means that the communication should provide all the information that the audience needs to understand the message and take appropriate action. Completeness also prevents the need for further communication, clarification, or correction, which can save time and resources.

Some of the benefits of completeness in technical communication are:

- It enhances the credibility and trustworthiness of the sender.
- It reduces the chances of misunderstanding, confusion, or misinterpretation.
- It increases the satisfaction and loyalty of the receiver.
- It improves the efficiency and effectiveness of the communication process.

Some of the strategies for achieving completeness in technical communication are:

- Identify the purpose and scope of the communication and tailor it to the specific needs and expectations of the audience.
- Provide all the relevant facts, figures, data, evidence, and examples that support the main message and answer the possible questions of the audience.
- Use clear and precise language and avoid ambiguity, vagueness, or jargon.
- Organize the information in a logical and coherent manner and use headings, subheadings, lists, tables, graphs, and other visual aids to enhance the readability and comprehension of the document.
- Include a summary, conclusion, or recommendation that summarizes the main points and provides a clear call to action for the audience.
- Review and revise the document for accuracy, completeness, and consistency and check for any errors, gaps, or redundancies in the information.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of completeness are:

- To remember the seven C's of effective communication, you can use the acronym CCCCCCC, which stands for Clear, Concise, Correct, Complete, Courteous, Concrete, and Coherent.
- To remember the benefits of completeness, you can use the acronym CREST, which stands for Credibility, Reduced misunderstanding, Efficiency, and Satisfaction.
- To remember the strategies for achieving completeness, you can use the acronym POUR, which stands for Purpose, Organization, Use of language, and Review.
