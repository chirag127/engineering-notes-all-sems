### Definition of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of **communicating expert, technical, or highly complex information** to a **non-expert or more general audience** .
- Technical communication has various **purposes**, such as to **define, instruct, inform, or persuade** .
- Technical communication is a **user-centered approach** that aims to provide the **right information, in the right way, at the right time** so that the user's life is more productive.
- Technical communication is a **task performed by specialized employees or consultants**, such as technical writers, programmers, engineers, or scientists.
- Technical communication involves the **delivery of clear, consistent, and factual information** that stems from complex concepts and is intended for **safe and efficient use and effective comprehension by users**.
- Technical communication can take various **forms**, such as manuals, reports, proposals, websites, blogs, podcasts, videos, etc.
- Technical communication can use various **tools**, such as word processors, graphics software, presentation software, web design software, etc.
- Technical communication can have various **audiences**, such as customers, clients, colleagues, managers, regulators, etc.
- Technical communication can have various **contexts**, such as academic, professional, industrial, governmental, etc.
- Technical communication can have various **challenges**, such as ethical, legal, cultural, linguistic, technical, etc.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the purposes of technical communication, you can use the acronym **DIP**: Define, Instruct, Persuade.
- To remember the user-centered approach of technical communication, you can use the phrase **Right information, Right way, Right time**.
- To remember the forms of technical communication, you can use the word **MR. P. W. B. P. V.**: Manuals, Reports, Proposals, Websites, Blogs, Podcasts, Videos.
- To remember the tools of technical communication, you can use the word **W. G. P. W.**: Word processors, Graphics software, Presentation software, Web design software.
- To remember the audiences of technical communication, you can use the word **C. C. C. M. R.**: Customers, Clients, Colleagues, Managers, Regulators.
- To remember the contexts of technical communication, you can use the word **A. P. I. G.**: Academic, Professional, Industrial, Governmental.
- To remember the challenges of technical communication, you can use the word **E. L. C. L. T.**: Ethical, Legal, Cultural, Linguistic, Technical.
