### Methods of Technical Communication

Technical communication is a form of communication that deals with technical or specialized topics, uses technology to create or distribute information, and provides instructions or explanations about how to do something. Technical communication can have various purposes, such as defining, instructing, informing, or persuading an audience.

Some of the methods of technical communication are:

- **Written communication**: This method involves using written words to convey technical information, such as reports, manuals, proposals, memos, emails, etc. Written communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and the audience. Written communication can also use visual elements, such as graphs, charts, tables, diagrams, etc., to enhance the clarity and effectiveness of the message.
- **Oral communication**: This method involves using spoken words to communicate technical information, such as presentations, lectures, meetings, interviews, podcasts, etc. Oral communication can be synchronous or asynchronous, depending on the time and location of the communication. Oral communication can also use visual aids, such as slides, videos, images, etc., to support the verbal message.
- **Video communication**: This method involves using moving images and sounds to communicate technical information, such as tutorials, demonstrations, documentaries, webinars, etc. Video communication can be live or recorded, depending on the availability and preference of the audience. Video communication can also use text, graphics, animations, etc., to supplement the audiovisual message.
- **Infographic communication**: This method involves using graphical representations of data or information to communicate technical information, such as charts, maps, icons, timelines, etc. Infographic communication can be static or interactive, depending on the level of engagement and feedback required from the audience. Infographic communication can also use colors, shapes, symbols, etc., to highlight the key points and patterns of the message.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the four methods of technical communication, you can use the acronym **W-O-V-I**, which stands for **W**ritten, **O**ral, **V**ideo, and **I**nfographic communication.
- To remember the difference between formal and informal written communication, you can use the phrase **F-I-R-E**, which stands for **F**ormal, **I**mpersonal, **R**ules, and **E**vidence. Formal written communication is impersonal, follows rules of grammar and style, and provides evidence to support the claims. Informal written communication is personal, follows conversational norms, and provides opinions or feelings.
- To remember the difference between synchronous and asynchronous oral communication, you can use the phrase **S-A-M-E**, which stands for **S**ame, **A**part, **M**oment, and **E**vent. Synchronous oral communication happens at the same moment and event, while asynchronous oral communication happens apart from the moment and event.
- To remember the difference between live and recorded video communication, you can use the phrase **L-R-A-D**, which stands for **L**ive, **R**eal-time, **A**djust, and **D**elay. Live video communication happens in real-time and allows the speaker and the audience to adjust the communication based on feedback. Recorded video communication has a delay between the recording and the viewing and does not allow the speaker and the audience to adjust the communication based on feedback.
- To remember the difference between static and interactive infographic communication, you can use the phrase **S-I-F-E**, which stands for **S**tatic, **I**nteractive, **F**ixed, and **E**xplorable. Static infographic communication has a fixed layout and content, while interactive infographic communication allows the user to explore the data or information by changing the layout or content.
