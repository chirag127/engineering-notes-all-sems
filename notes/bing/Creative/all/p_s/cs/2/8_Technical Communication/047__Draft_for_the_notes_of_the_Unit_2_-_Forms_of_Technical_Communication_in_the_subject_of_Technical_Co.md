### Draft for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

Technical communication is a form of communication based on the writing and sharing of technical information with a specialized target audience. Technical communicators create, design, and deliver various forms of technical information, such as user manuals, websites, product specifications, tutorials, eBooks, webinars, and so on.

Technical communication is a broad field and includes any form of communication that exhibits one or more of the following characteristics:

- Communicating about technical or specialized topics, such as computer applications, medical procedures, or environmental regulations.
- Communicating by using technology, such as web pages, social media, or software.
- Providing instructions about how to do something, regardless of how technical the task is or even if technology is used to create or distribute that communication.

Some of the common forms of technical communication are :

- Proposals and requests for proposals (RFPs): These are documents that outline a project plan, its objectives, methods, budget, and timeline, and solicit bids from potential contractors or partners.
- Technical or research reports: These are documents that present the results of a study, experiment, or analysis, and provide recommendations or conclusions based on the data.
- Documentation records and product specifications: These are documents that describe the features, functions, and requirements of a product, system, or process, and provide instructions for its installation, operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting.
- User guides (step-by-step instructions, procedures, manuals): These are documents that explain how to use a product, system, or process, and provide tips, warnings, and troubleshooting information.
- Online help, technical support: These are documents that provide assistance to users who encounter problems or difficulties with a product, system, or process, and offer solutions or guidance.
- Reference information (encyclopedia-style information): These are documents that provide factual information about a topic, such as definitions, terms, concepts, principles, or examples.
- Consumer literature (information for the public about regulations, safety issues, etc.): These are documents that inform the general public about technical or specialized topics that affect their lives, such as health, environment, or consumer rights.
- Presentations and webinars: These are oral or visual forms of communication that deliver technical information to an audience, using slides, videos, audio, or interactive features.
- Websites and blogs: These are online forms of communication that provide technical information to a specific or general audience, using text, images, links, or multimedia.
- eBooks and tutorials: These are digital forms of communication that provide technical information to a specific or general audience, using text, images, links, or multimedia, and often allow the user to interact with the content or follow a learning path.
- Social media and podcasts: These are online forms of communication that provide technical information to a specific or general audience, using text, images, links, or multimedia, and often allow the user to comment, share, or subscribe to the content.

Each form of technical communication has its own advantages, disadvantages, examples, and applications, depending on the purpose, audience, context, and medium of the communication. Technical communicators need to be aware of the characteristics, strengths, and limitations of each form, and choose the most appropriate one for their communication goals.
