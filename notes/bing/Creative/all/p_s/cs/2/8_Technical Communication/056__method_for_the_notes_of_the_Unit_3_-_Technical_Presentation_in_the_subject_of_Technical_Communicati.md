### Method for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that aims to inform, persuade, motivate or entertain a specific audience on a technical topic.
- A technical presentation requires careful planning, preparation, practice and delivery to achieve its purpose and objectives.
- Some of the methods for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation are:

  - **Identify the purpose and audience of the presentation.** This will help to determine the scope, content, tone, style and format of the presentation. For example, a presentation to inform or educate will focus on facts and explanations, while a presentation to persuade will use arguments and evidence.
  - **Conduct research and analysis on the topic.** This will help to gather relevant and reliable information, data, examples, illustrations and references to support the main points and sub-points of the presentation. For example, a presentation on a new technology will require technical details, specifications, benefits and applications.
  - **Organize the information and data into a logical structure.** This will help to create a clear and coherent flow of ideas, arguments and evidence throughout the presentation. For example, a presentation can follow the introduction-body-conclusion structure, or the problem-solution-benefits structure, or the chronological, spatial, topical or comparative structure.
  - **Prepare the presentation materials and aids.** This will help to enhance the visual, auditory and interactive aspects of the presentation. For example, a presentation can use slides, charts, graphs, images, videos, audio clips, animations, handouts, demonstrations, quizzes, polls or questions to engage the audience and convey the message effectively.
  - **Practice the presentation skills and techniques.** This will help to improve the confidence, clarity, fluency, accuracy, voice, body language, eye contact, gestures, facial expressions and timing of the presentation. For example, a presentation can use rehearsing, recording, feedback, peer review, self-evaluation or coaching to refine the delivery and performance of the presentation.

Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of technical presentation are:

- **Use the acronym PEP to remember the three main parts of a presentation: Purpose, Evidence and Preview.** For example, in the introduction, you can state the purpose of your presentation, the evidence you will use to support it, and the preview of the main points you will cover.
- **Use the rhyme "Tell them what you're going to tell them, tell them, then tell them what you told them" to remember the structure of a presentation: Introduction, Body and Conclusion.** For example, in the introduction, you can tell the audience what your presentation is about and what you will cover, in the body, you can tell them the main points and sub-points with evidence and examples, and in the conclusion, you can tell them the summary and implications of your presentation.
- **Use the song "Do-Re-Mi" from The Sound of Music to remember the seven elements of a good presentation: Delivery, Relevance, Message, Interest, Structure, Tone and Audience.** For example, you can sing "Do, a delivery that's clear and confident, Re, a relevance that makes them care, Mi, a message that's concise and coherent, Fa, an interest that keeps them engaged, Sol, a structure that's logical and organized, La, a tone that's appropriate and respectful, Ti, an audience that's understood and satisfied, and that brings us back to Do, Do, Do, Do."
- **Use the acrostic SMART to remember the criteria for effective presentation aids: Simple, Meaningful, Attractive, Relevant and Timely.** For example, you can make sure that your presentation aids are simple and not cluttered, meaningful and not confusing, attractive and not distracting, relevant and not irrelevant, and timely and not outdated.
