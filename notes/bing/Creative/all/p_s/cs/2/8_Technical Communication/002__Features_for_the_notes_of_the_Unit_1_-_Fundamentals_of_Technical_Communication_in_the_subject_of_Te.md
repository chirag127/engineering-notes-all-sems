### Features of Technical Communication

Technical communication is a broad field that involves communicating about technical or specialized topics, such as computer applications, medical procedures, or environmental regulations, in the workplace or for specific audiences. Technical communication can take various forms, such as letters, emails, instructions, reports, proposals, websites, and blogs. Some of the features of technical communication are:

- **Clarity**: It is important to use language and terms that are appropriate for the intended audience and purpose of the communication. Clarity means avoiding ambiguity, jargon, and unnecessary complexity, and using simple and direct sentences, active voice, and concrete examples.
- **Conciseness**: Information or details should not be sacrificed for the sake of brevity, but unnecessary words, repetitions, and redundancies should be avoided. Conciseness means expressing the main idea and supporting points in the fewest possible words, without compromising the meaning or tone of the message.
- **Objectivity**: Objectivity, unbiased opinions, and factual information are essential for technical communication. Objectivity means avoiding personal feelings, preferences, assumptions, and judgments, and presenting the information in a neutral and balanced way, supported by evidence and logic.
- **Detail-oriented**: Often, technical communication contains specific and precise information that requires attention to detail and accuracy. Detail-oriented means checking and verifying the facts, figures, data, and sources, and ensuring that the information is consistent, complete, and correct.
- **Use of visuals**: Visuals, such as graphs, charts, tables, diagrams, illustrations, and photographs, can enhance the effectiveness and appeal of technical communication. Visuals can help to summarize, clarify, explain, or emphasize the information, and to attract and maintain the attention of the audience. However, visuals should be relevant, clear, labeled, and integrated with the text.
- **Audience-specific**: Technical communication should be tailored to the needs, expectations, and background of the specific audience. Audience-specific means analyzing the audience's characteristics, such as their level of knowledge, interest, attitude, and motivation, and adapting the content, style, tone, and format of the communication accordingly.
- **Compliance with standards and regulations**: Technical communication should follow the standards and regulations that apply to the field, industry, or organization. Compliance means adhering to the ethical, legal, and professional norms and guidelines, such as citation styles, document formats, accessibility requirements, and confidentiality policies.
