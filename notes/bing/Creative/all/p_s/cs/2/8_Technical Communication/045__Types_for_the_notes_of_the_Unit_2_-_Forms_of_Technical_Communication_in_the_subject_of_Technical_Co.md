### Types of Technical Communication

Technical communication is a form of communication based on the writing and sharing of technical information with a specialized target audience. Technical communicators create, design, and deliver various forms of technical information, such as user manuals, websites, product specifications, video tutorials, e-books, webinars, and so on .

There are many types of technical communication, depending on the purpose, audience, and context of the communication. Some of the common types are:

- **Instructional documents**: These are documents that provide step-by-step guidance on how to perform a task, such as operating a device, installing a software, or following a procedure. Examples of instructional documents are user guides, manuals, tutorials, and training materials.
- **Informative documents**: These are documents that provide factual and objective information on a topic, such as a concept, a process, a system, or a phenomenon. Examples of informative documents are reports, articles, white papers, and specifications.
- **Persuasive documents**: These are documents that aim to convince or influence the audience to take a certain action, such as buying a product, adopting a policy, or supporting a cause. Examples of persuasive documents are proposals, brochures, flyers, and advertisements.
- **Descriptive documents**: These are documents that describe the features, characteristics, or appearance of something, such as a product, a place, or a person. Examples of descriptive documents are product reviews, catalogs, profiles, and resumes.
- **Analytical documents**: These are documents that analyze, evaluate, or interpret data, information, or evidence, such as statistics, trends, or results. Examples of analytical documents are case studies, feasibility studies, surveys, and critiques.

Technical communication can also be classified based on the mode or medium of delivery, such as:

- **Written communication**: This is the most common form of technical communication, which involves creating and delivering text-based documents, such as books, articles, manuals, and web pages.
- **Oral communication**: This is the form of technical communication that involves speaking and listening, such as giving presentations, lectures, podcasts, or interviews.
- **Visual communication**: This is the form of technical communication that involves creating and delivering images, graphics, diagrams, charts, or videos, such as illustrations, animations, infographics, or screencasts.
- **Multimodal communication**: This is the form of technical communication that combines two or more modes or media of delivery, such as a web page that contains text, images, and video, or a presentation that uses slides, audio, and gestures.

Technical communication can also be categorized based on the field or industry that requires it, such as:

- **Engineering communication**: This is the form of technical communication that deals with the design, development, testing, and maintenance of engineering systems, products, or processes, such as mechanical, electrical, civil, or software engineering.
- **Scientific communication**: This is the form of technical communication that deals with the discovery, exploration, and dissemination of scientific knowledge, methods, or findings, such as biology, chemistry, physics, or astronomy.
- **Medical communication**: This is the form of technical communication that deals with the diagnosis, treatment, prevention, and education of health and wellness issues, such as medicine, nursing, dentistry, or pharmacy.
- **Business communication**: This is the form of technical communication that deals with the management, operation, and communication of business activities, such as accounting, marketing, finance, or human resources.
- **Legal communication**: This is the form of technical communication that deals with the creation, interpretation, and application of laws, regulations, and policies, such as contracts, patents, court cases, or legislation.

Technical communication is a broad and diverse field that requires various skills, knowledge, and tools to create and deliver effective and appropriate technical information for different purposes, audiences, and contexts. Technical communicators must be able to:

- **Research**: This is the skill of finding, evaluating, and using relevant and reliable sources of information, such as books, journals, websites, or databases.
- **Write**: This is the skill of producing clear, concise, and coherent text that follows the conventions of grammar, spelling, punctuation, and style.
- **Design**: This is the skill of organizing, formatting, and enhancing the appearance and usability of technical documents, such as using headings, lists, tables, fonts, colors, or images.
- **Edit**: This is the skill of reviewing, revising, and proofreading technical documents to improve their quality, accuracy, and consistency.
