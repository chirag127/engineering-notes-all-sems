### Interpersonal Communication

Interpersonal communication is the process by which people exchange information, feelings, and meaning through verbal and non-verbal messages. It usually involves verbal, face-to-face communication with two or more people, and relies on tone, gestures, facial expressions, and body language to convey the intended message. Interpersonal communication is often defined as communication that takes place between people who are interdependent and have some knowledge of each other. For example, communication between a son and his father, an employer and an employee, two sisters, a teacher and a student, two lovers, two friends, and so on.

Some of the characteristics of interpersonal communication are:

- It is dynamic, meaning it changes over time and according to the context and the relationship between the communicators.
- It is transactional, meaning it involves mutual influence and exchange of feedback between the communicators.
- It is irreversible, meaning it cannot be undone or taken back once it has occurred.
- It is complex, meaning it involves multiple factors and dimensions, such as verbal and non-verbal codes, emotions, perceptions, expectations, and cultural differences.

Some of the types of interpersonal communication are:

- Intrapersonal communication, which is communication with oneself, such as self-talk, inner dialogue, or reflection.
- Dyadic communication, which is communication between two people, such as a conversation, an interview, or a counseling session.
- Small group communication, which is communication among three or more people who share a common goal, such as a team, a committee, or a family.
- Public communication, which is communication from one person to a large audience, such as a speech, a lecture, or a presentation.

Interpersonal communication is important for various reasons, such as:

- It helps to establish and maintain relationships, such as friendship, romance, family, or work.
- It helps to express and satisfy needs, such as physical, social, emotional, or intellectual needs.
- It helps to share and gain information, such as facts, opinions, beliefs, or values.
- It helps to influence and persuade others, such as to change their attitudes, behaviors, or decisions.
- It helps to manage and resolve conflicts, such as to negotiate, compromise, or cooperate.

Some of the skills and strategies that can improve interpersonal communication are:

- Active listening, which is paying attention, showing interest, and providing feedback to the speaker.
- Empathy, which is understanding and sharing the feelings and perspectives of the other person.
- Self-disclosure, which is revealing personal information and feelings to the other person.
- Assertiveness, which is expressing one's needs and rights in a respectful and confident manner.
- Non-verbal communication, which is using gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and body language to support and enhance the verbal message.
- Feedback, which is providing positive and constructive responses to the other person's message.
- Adaptability, which is adjusting one's communication style and behavior according to the situation and the audience.
