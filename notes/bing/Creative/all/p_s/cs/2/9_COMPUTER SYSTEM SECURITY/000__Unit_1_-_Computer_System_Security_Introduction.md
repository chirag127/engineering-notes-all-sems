## Unit 1 - Computer System Security Introduction

- Computer system security is the protection of computer systems and information from harm, theft, and unauthorized use  .
- Computer system security involves the following aspects  :
  - Hardware security: the physical protection of computer hardware from damage, theft, or tampering.
  - Firmware security: the protection of permanent software that is embedded in hardware devices from unauthorized modification or exploitation.
  - Software security: the protection of software applications and operating systems from malicious attacks, such as viruses, worms, ransomware, spyware, etc.
  - Data security: the protection of data stored or transmitted by computer systems from unauthorized access, modification, or deletion.
  - Network security: the protection of computer networks and communication channels from external or internal threats, such as hackers, denial-of-service attacks, eavesdropping, etc.
  - User security: the protection of users' identities, credentials, and privileges from misuse or compromise.
- Computer system security is important for the following reasons  :
  - To ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information and resources.
  - To prevent financial losses, reputational damage, legal liabilities, and regulatory penalties.
  - To protect the privacy and safety of users, customers, and stakeholders.
  - To comply with ethical and legal obligations and standards.
  - To support the mission and goals of an organization or entity.
- Computer system security faces various challenges and threats, such as  :
  - The complexity and diversity of computer systems and networks, which create multiple vulnerabilities and attack vectors.
  - The rapid evolution and innovation of technology, which outpace the development and implementation of security measures and solutions.
  - The human factor, which introduces errors, negligence, or malicious intent in the design, operation, or use of computer systems and networks.
  - The global and interconnected nature of cyberspace, which enables attackers to operate across borders and jurisdictions, and to exploit the weaknesses of different legal and regulatory frameworks.
  - The asymmetry and unpredictability of cyberattacks, which allow attackers to launch low-cost, high-impact, and stealthy attacks against high-value targets, and to adapt and change their tactics and techniques.

Mnemonics and learning tricks are techniques that can help you remember new information by connecting it to something you already know. They can use words, sounds, images, feelings, or actions to create associations that make the information easier to recall. Some examples of mnemonic techniques are:

- Spelling mnemonics: These are phrases or sentences that help you spell a word correctly by using the first letter of each word. For example, to spell "arithmetic", you can use the phrase "A Rat In The House May Eat The Ice Cream".
- Rhyming mnemonics: These are words that rhyme with the information you want to remember. For example, to remember the number of days in each month, you can use the rhyme "Thirty days hath September, April, June, and November".
- Acronym mnemonics: These are words that are formed by using the first letter of each word in a list or a name. For example, to remember the names of the Great Lakes, you can use the word "HOMES", which stands for Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, and Superior.
- Keyword mnemonics: These are words that sound like or remind you of the information you want to remember. For example, to remember the capital of Brazil, you can use the word "brass", which sounds like "Brasilia".
- Pegword mnemonics: These are words that rhyme with numbers and are used to remember a list of items in order. For example, to remember the first 10 presidents of the United States, you can use the words "one is a bun, two is a shoe, three is a tree, four is a door, five is a hive, six is a stick, seven is heaven, eight is a gate, nine is a line, and ten is a hen". Then, you can associate each president with the corresponding word. For example, "one is a bun" can remind you of George Washington, who had a bun-like hairstyle.

To use mnemonic techniques effectively, you should follow these guidelines:

- Choose the appropriate mnemonic for your situation. For example, if your goal is to learn how to spell a word, you may want to use the spelling mnemonic technique.
- Practice the technique. You may want to practice your mnemonic several times to help you remember it.
- Repeat the mnemonic to others. This can help you reinforce the information and check your accuracy.
- Review the mnemonic periodically. This can help you prevent forgetting the information over time.

Mnemonic techniques can be very helpful for learning, but they are not foolproof. You should also try to understand the meaning and context of the information you are memorizing, and use other strategies such as repetition, elaboration, and testing to enhance your memory.
