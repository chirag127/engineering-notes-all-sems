### What is computer security and what to learn for the notes of the Unit 1 - Computer System Security Introduction in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Computer security, also called cybersecurity or information technology security, is the protection of computer systems and networks from attack by malicious actors that may result in unauthorized information disclosure, theft of, or damage to hardware, software, or data, as well as from the disruption or misdirection of the services they provide.
- Computer security can be implemented using various methods, such as passwords, encryption, and firewalls, and denying physical access to a computer's location.
- Computer security is a broad and multidisciplinary field that covers topics such as cryptography, network security, operating system security, web security, malware analysis, digital forensics, and ethical hacking.
- Some of the objectives of computer security are to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources, to prevent unauthorized access and use, to detect and respond to attacks, and to recover from incidents.
- Some of the challenges of computer security are the complexity and diversity of systems and networks, the evolving and sophisticated nature of threats, the human factor and social engineering, the legal and ethical issues, and the trade-offs between security and usability.
- Some of the benefits of computer security are to protect the privacy and identity of users, to safeguard the intellectual property and assets of organizations, to ensure the reliability and functionality of systems and services, and to support the economic and social development of society.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the three main objectives of computer security, use the acronym CIA: Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
- To remember the five main components of a computer system, use the acronym SHARP: Software, Hardware, Application, Resource, and Protocol.
- To remember the four main types of malware, use the acronym VRTW: Virus, Rootkit, Trojan, and Worm.
- To remember the six main types of network attacks, use the acronym DDoS-SPAM: Denial-of-Service, Distributed Denial-of-Service, Spoofing, Phishing, Authentication, and Man-in-the-Middle.
