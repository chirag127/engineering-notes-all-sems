### Bus Architecture

- A bus is a communication system that transfers data between components inside a computer, or between computers.
- A bus consists of a set of wires or lines, each of which carries a single bit of information.
- A bus can be classified into three functional groups: data lines, address lines, and control lines .
- Data lines are used to transfer data between the components of a computer system.
- Address lines are used to specify the source or destination of the data on the data lines.
- Control lines are used to coordinate the activities of the components and to signal the type and direction of the data transfer.
- A system bus is a bus that connects the major components of a computer system, such as CPU, memory, and I/O devices.
- A system bus can be further divided into internal bus and external bus.
- The internal bus, also known as internal data bus, memory bus, system bus or front-side bus, connects all the internal components of a computer, such as CPU and memory, to the motherboard.
- The external bus, also known as expansion bus, peripheral bus or back-side bus, connects the external devices, such as disk drives, printers, scanners, etc., to the motherboard.
- The design of a system bus depends on various factors, such as the number of lines, the width of the lines, the speed of the lines, the multiplexing of the lines, the arbitration of the bus, etc.
- A common bus system is a type of system bus design that uses a single set of lines for data, address, and control, and multiplexes them to reduce the number of wires.
- A common bus system requires additional hardware, such as multiplexers and demultiplexers, to select the appropriate signals and to separate them at the destination.
- A common bus system has the advantage of simplicity and low cost, but the disadvantage of low performance and limited scalability.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the three functional groups of a bus, you can use the acronym DAC: Data, Address, Control.
- To remember the difference between internal and external bus, you can use the phrases "inside the box" and "outside the box", or "front-side bus" and "back-side bus".
- To remember the advantages and disadvantages of a common bus system, you can use the words "simple" and "slow", or "cheap" and "cheap".
