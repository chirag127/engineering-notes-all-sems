### Multiprocess Systems

- A multiprocess system is a computer system that has two or more central processing units (CPUs) that work in parallel to perform multiple tasks. 
- A multiprocess system can be classified into two types: symmetric multiprocessing (SMP) and asymmetric multiprocessing (AMP). 
- In SMP, all the processors are identical and have equal access to the shared resources, such as memory, I/O devices, and interconnection network. The operating system can assign any task to any processor, and the processors can communicate and synchronize with each other. SMP systems are scalable, reliable, and easy to program.  
- In AMP, the processors are different and have different roles and access privileges to the shared resources. The operating system can assign specific tasks to specific processors, and the processors can communicate and synchronize with each other through a master processor. AMP systems are less expensive, less complex, and more efficient for specialized applications.  
- The main advantages of multiprocess systems are:  
  - They can increase the performance and speed of the system by dividing the workload among multiple processors.
  - They can improve the reliability and fault tolerance of the system by providing redundancy and backup for the processors.
  - They can enhance the functionality and flexibility of the system by supporting parallel and distributed processing and multitasking.
- The main challenges of multiprocess systems are:  
  - They require a complex and sophisticated operating system that can manage the coordination, communication, and synchronization of the processors and the shared resources.
  - They incur a high cost and overhead for the hardware and software components, such as the interconnection network, the memory hierarchy, and the synchronization mechanisms.
  - They face scalability and compatibility issues when adding or removing processors or changing the system architecture.
