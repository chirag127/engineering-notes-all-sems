### Precedence Constraints and Data Dependency

- Precedence constraints and data dependency are two types of constraints that may affect the scheduling of jobs in real-time systems.
- Precedence constraints specify the order in which jobs must execute, based on their control or data flow dependencies. For example, a job that processes the input data must execute before a job that displays the output data.
- Data dependency occurs when jobs communicate via shared data, and the data of one job is dependent on the data of another job. For example, a job that updates a shared variable must execute before a job that reads the same variable.
- Precedence constraints and data dependency can be represented by a directed acyclic graph (DAG), where the nodes are the jobs and the edges are the dependencies. A DAG is said to be consistent if there is no cycle in the graph, meaning that there is no circular dependency among the jobs.
- Precedence constraints and data dependency can affect the feasibility and optimality of the scheduling algorithms for real-time systems. Some scheduling algorithms, such as rate-monotonic scheduling (RMS) and earliest deadline first (EDF) scheduling, assume that the jobs are independent and do not have any precedence constraints or data dependency. Other scheduling algorithms, such as deadline monotonic scheduling (DMS) and least laxity first (LLF) scheduling, can handle precedence constraints and data dependency by using techniques such as task splitting, task merging, and task duplication.
- Precedence constraints and data dependency can also introduce challenges such as jitter, blocking, and priority inversion in real-time systems. Jitter is the variation in the arrival time of the data for a job, which can affect the timing behavior of the job. Blocking is the delay caused by a lower priority job that holds a shared resource needed by a higher priority job. Priority inversion is the situation where a higher priority job is preempted by a lower priority job that depends on a shared resource held by a medium priority job. These challenges can be addressed by using mechanisms such as timed release counters, priority inheritance, and priority ceiling protocols.
