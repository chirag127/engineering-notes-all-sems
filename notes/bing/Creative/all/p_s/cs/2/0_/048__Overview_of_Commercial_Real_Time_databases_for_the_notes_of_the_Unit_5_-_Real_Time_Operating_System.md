### Overview of Commercial Real Time Databases

- A commercial real time database is a database system that uses real time processing to handle workloads whose state is constantly changing.
- Real time processing means that the database can respond to queries and transactions within a specified deadline, usually in milliseconds or seconds.
- Commercial real time databases are useful for applications that require timely and accurate data, such as accounting, banking, law, medical records, multi-media, process control, reservation systems, and scientific data analysis.
- Commercial real time databases differ from traditional databases in several aspects, such as:
  - Data model: Commercial real time databases may use relational, object-oriented, or NoSQL data models, depending on the application requirements and the nature of the data.
  - Concurrency control: Commercial real time databases need to ensure that concurrent transactions do not violate the data consistency and the temporal constraints of the application. They may use optimistic or pessimistic concurrency control techniques, or a combination of both.
  - Scheduling: Commercial real time databases need to schedule the transactions according to their priorities and deadlines, and may use static or dynamic scheduling algorithms. They may also use admission control to reject transactions that cannot meet their deadlines.
  - Recovery: Commercial real time databases need to recover from failures and maintain the data availability and integrity. They may use logging, checkpointing, replication, or caching techniques, or a combination of them.
- Some examples of commercial real time databases are:
  - Google Cloud Firestore: A highly scalable, fully managed NoSQL database service for large analytical and operational workloads. It offers up to 99.999% availability, processes more than 5 billion requests per second at peak, and supports real time data synchronization and offline access.
  - Raima Database Manager: A high-performance, embedded, in-memory database system for mission-critical applications. It supports SQL and NoSQL data models, ACID transactions, optimistic and pessimistic concurrency control, static and dynamic scheduling, and various recovery techniques.
  - Hazelcast Jet: A distributed, in-memory, stream and batch processing engine that can be used as a real time database. It supports SQL and NoSQL queries, event-driven architecture, fault tolerance, and high availability.
