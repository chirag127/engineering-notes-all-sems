### Offline Versus Online Scheduling

- Offline scheduling is a scheduling approach that makes use of pre-computed schedule of all hard real time jobs before the system begins to execute. The schedule is based on the knowledge of release time, execution time, resource requirement, and deadline of all jobs for all time. Offline scheduling can guarantee deadlines as well as achieve optimal ordering of jobs.
- Online scheduling is a scheduling approach that makes scheduling decisions during the run-time of the system. The scheduler does not have prior information about the jobs that will be released in the future and the parameters of each job are known to the scheduler only after the release of the job. Online scheduling has to accept heuristics as being good enough and adapt to the dynamic changes in the system.
- The advantages of offline scheduling are:
  - It can achieve optimal performance and resource utilization.
  - It can avoid overheads of run-time scheduling decisions and context switches.
  - It can handle complex constraints and dependencies among jobs.
- The disadvantages of offline scheduling are:
  - It requires complete and accurate information about all jobs in advance.
  - It cannot handle unpredictable events and uncertainties in the system.
  - It is not suitable for dynamic and interactive systems that need to respond to user inputs and external stimuli.
- The advantages of online scheduling are:
  - It can handle dynamic and unpredictable situations in the system.
  - It can respond to user inputs and external stimuli in a timely manner.
  - It can cope with uncertainties and variations in the job parameters.
- The disadvantages of online scheduling are:
  - It may not achieve optimal performance and resource utilization.
  - It may incur overheads of run-time scheduling decisions and context switches.
  - It may not guarantee deadlines and quality of service for all jobs.
- An example of offline scheduling is a table-driven scheduler that uses a pre-computed table of scheduling decisions for all hard real time jobs. An example of online scheduling is a priority-driven scheduler that assigns priorities to jobs based on their deadlines and executes the highest priority job at each instant.
- The applications of offline scheduling are mainly in static and deterministic systems that have fixed and known job parameters and do not need to interact with the environment. The applications of online scheduling are mainly in dynamic and stochastic systems that have variable and unknown job parameters and need to interact with the environment.
