### Basic Concepts in Real time Communication

Real-time communication (RTC) is a category of software protocols and communication hardware media that gives real-time guarantees. It is a means of sharing information and interacting with people through network connections just as if they were face-to-face through real-time computing.

Some of the basic concepts in real-time communication are:

- **Real-time guarantees**: RTC requires that the data is delivered within a specified time bound, which is often determined by the application requirements. For example, a video call may require a latency of less than 100 milliseconds, while a sensor network may tolerate a delay of a few seconds. Real-time guarantees are achieved by using various techniques, such as priority scheduling, resource reservation, admission control, and quality of service (QoS) mechanisms.
- **Half duplex and full duplex**: RTC can take place in half duplex or full duplex modes. Half duplex means that the communication in one channel is in one direction at a time. Sender or receiver can send but not receive at the same time (think of an old-style walkie-talkie where the user has to say “over” to tell the person on the other end that she can speak). Full duplex means that sender and receiver can send and receive messages simultaneously in two parallel communication pathways (think of a telephone conversation where both parties can talk and listen at the same time).
- **Synchronous and asynchronous**: RTC can also be classified as synchronous or asynchronous. Synchronous means that the communication is coordinated by a common clock or a shared event, and the sender and receiver are expected to be available at the same time. Asynchronous means that the communication is independent of any clock or event, and the sender and receiver can communicate at their own convenience. Synchronous communication is more suitable for interactive and collaborative applications, such as video conferencing and online gaming, while asynchronous communication is more suitable for data transfer and storage applications, such as email and file sharing.
- **Peer-to-peer and client-server**: RTC can also be based on different network architectures, such as peer-to-peer (P2P) or client-server. P2P means that the communication is directly between the end devices, without any intermediary server or broker. P2P communication can reduce the network load and latency, but also introduce challenges such as security, scalability, and reliability. Client-server means that the communication is mediated by a central server or a cloud service, which can provide additional features such as authentication, encryption, routing, and caching. Client-server communication can improve the security, scalability, and reliability of the communication, but also increase the network load and latency.
