### Effect of Resource Contention and Resource Access Control (RAC)

- Resource contention occurs when two or more jobs or processes need to access a shared resource at the same time, such as a hardware device, a memory buffer, a lock, or a queue.
- Resource contention often leads to performance degradation in the applications contending for the shared resource, as they have to wait for the resource to become available or compete for the resource access.
- Resource contention also affects the execution behavior and schedulability of jobs, as it may introduce blocking time, priority inversion, deadlock, or livelock.
- A resource access control protocol, or simply an access control protocol, is a set of rules that govern (1) when and under what conditions each request for resource is granted and (2) how jobs requiring resources are scheduled.
- One of the major objectives of resource access control is to minimize the undesirable effects of resource contention, such as reducing the blocking time, preventing or resolving priority inversion, avoiding deadlock and livelock, and preserving the temporal correctness of the system.
- Resource access control protocols can be classified into two categories: non-preemptive and preemptive.
  - Non-preemptive protocols do not allow a job to be preempted while holding a resource. Examples of non-preemptive protocols are Priority Inheritance Protocol (PIP), Priority Ceiling Protocol (PCP), and Stack Resource Policy (SRP).
  - Preemptive protocols allow a job to be preempted while holding a resource, but require the job to release the resource before being preempted. Examples of preemptive protocols are Immediate Priority Ceiling Protocol (IPCP), Preemptive Priority Ceiling Protocol (PPCP), and Preemptive Stack Resource Policy (PSRP).
- The choice of resource access control protocol depends on the characteristics of the system, such as the number and type of resources, the criticality and priority of jobs, the degree of concurrency and contention, and the timing constraints and requirements.
