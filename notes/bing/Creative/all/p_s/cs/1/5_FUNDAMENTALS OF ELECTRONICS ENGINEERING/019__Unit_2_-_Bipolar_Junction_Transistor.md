## Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

- A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a type of transistor that uses both electrons and holes as charge carriers.
- A transistor is a device that can amplify or switch electrical signals by controlling the flow of current or voltage in a circuit.
- A BJT has three terminals: the emitter (E), the base (B), and the collector (C).
- The emitter is the terminal that supplies the majority charge carriers (electrons or holes) to the base region.
- The base is the terminal that controls the amount of current flowing from the emitter to the collector.
- The collector is the terminal that collects the majority charge carriers from the emitter through the base region.
- A BJT can be either npn or pnp, depending on the arrangement of the p-type and n-type semiconductor layers.
- An npn transistor has a thin p-type layer sandwiched between two n-type layers, while a pnp transistor has a thin n-type layer sandwiched between two p-type layers.
- The operation of a BJT depends on the biasing of the two pn junctions: the emitter-base junction and the collector-base junction.
- Biasing means applying a voltage across a pn junction to make it either forward biased or reverse biased.
- A forward biased junction allows current to flow easily, while a reverse biased junction blocks current flow.
- The emitter-base junction is always forward biased, while the collector-base junction is usually reverse biased.
- The forward bias of the emitter-base junction causes a large current to flow from the emitter to the base, called the emitter current (I_E).
- The reverse bias of the collector-base junction causes a small current to flow from the base to the collector, called the base current (I_B).
- The difference between the emitter current and the base current is the collector current (I_C), which flows from the emitter to the collector through the base region.
- The ratio of the collector current to the base current is called the current gain (β) of the BJT, which is a measure of its amplification ability.
- The current gain is usually much larger than 1, meaning that a small change in the base current can cause a large change in the collector current.
- The voltage between the base and the emitter is called the base-emitter voltage (V_BE), which is usually around 0.7 V for silicon BJTs.
- The voltage between the collector and the emitter is called the collector-emitter voltage (V_CE), which is the output voltage of the BJT.
- The relationship between the collector current and the collector-emitter voltage is called the output characteristic of the BJT, which shows how the BJT behaves as a switch or an amplifier.
- A BJT can be used in three different modes of operation: cut-off, active, and saturation.
- In the cut-off mode, the base current is zero, and the collector current is negligible. The BJT acts as an open switch, and the collector-emitter voltage is high.
- In the active mode, the base current is positive, and the collector current is proportional to it. The BJT acts as a current amplifier, and the collector-emitter voltage is variable.
- In the saturation mode, the base current is high, and the collector current is maximum. The BJT acts as a closed switch, and the collector-emitter voltage is low.
- A BJT can be used in various circuits and applications, such as amplifiers, oscillators, logic gates, switches, and sensors.
