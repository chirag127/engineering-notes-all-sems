### Zener Diodes breakdown mechanism (Zener and avalanche)

- A Zener diode is a heavily doped semiconductor device that is designed to operate in the reverse direction.
- When the voltage across the terminals of a Zener diode is reversed, and the potential reaches the Zener Voltage (knee voltage), the junction breaks down, and the current flows in the reverse direction.
- This breakdown phenomenon is called Zener breakdown and it is used to stabilize the voltage of low-current applications.
- The Zener breakdown voltage decreases as the temperature increases, creating a negative temperature coefficient (TC).
- Zener breakdown occurs in heavily doped junctions (p-type semiconductor moderately doped and n-type heavily doped), which produces a narrow depletion region.
- The valence electrons are pulled into conduction band by the strong electric field at the junction, creating a large reverse current.
- Zener breakdown is independent of the applied reverse voltage and depends only on the doping concentration.

- Another breakdown phenomenon that can occur in a Zener diode is avalanche breakdown.
- Avalanche breakdown occurs in lightly doped junctions, which produce a wider depletion region.
- The valence electrons are pushed into conduction band by the collisions with the lattice atoms, creating a large reverse current.
- Avalanche breakdown is dependent on the applied reverse voltage and increases with the temperature, creating a positive TC.
- Avalanche breakdown occurs at higher reverse voltages than Zener breakdown.

- A Zener diode can have two distinctly different breakdown mechanisms: Zener breakdown and avalanche breakdown.
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode show a sharp knee at the breakdown voltage, where the reverse current increases rapidly.
- The breakdown voltage can be controlled by the doping process and can range from a few volts to hundreds of volts.
- A Zener diode can be used as a voltage reference or a shunt regulator to regulate the voltage across small circuits.
- When connected in parallel with a variable voltage source so that it is reverse biased, a Zener diode conducts when the voltage reaches the diode's reverse breakdown voltage.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the difference between Zener and avalanche breakdown, you can use the word ZAP: Zener is At low Potential, avalanche is At high Potential.
- To remember the difference between the doping levels of Zener and avalanche diodes, you can use the word ZAD: Zener is heavily doped, Avalanche is lightly Doped.
- To remember the difference between the temperature coefficients of Zener and avalanche breakdown, you can use the word ZAT: Zener has a negative TC, Avalanche has a positive TC.
