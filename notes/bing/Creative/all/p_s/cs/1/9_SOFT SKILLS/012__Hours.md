## Hours

- Hours are units of time that measure the duration of events or activities. 
- One hour is equal to 60 minutes or 3600 seconds. 
- There are 24 hours in a day, which can be divided into two 12-hour periods (AM and PM) or one 24-hour period (military time).
- Hours can also refer to the time of day, which is indicated by a clock or a watch. The time of day can be expressed in different formats, such as 12-hour clock (e.g., 4:30 PM), 24-hour clock (e.g., 16:30), or decimal time (e.g., 4.5).
- Hours can also refer to the amount of time that a person works or performs a certain task. For example, an employee may have regular hours, overtime hours, vacation hours, sick hours, etc. 
- Different types of hours may have different rates of pay or benefits, depending on the employer's policies and the employee's contract. 
- Some examples of hours types are:

  - Regular hours: The normal hours that an employee works in a week, usually 40 hours for full-time employees and less for part-time employees.
  - Overtime hours: The hours that an employee works beyond their regular hours, usually at a higher rate of pay. Overtime hours may be required or voluntary, depending on the employer's needs and the employee's availability.
  - Vacation hours: The hours that an employee can take off from work for personal reasons, such as travel, leisure, or family. Vacation hours are usually paid and accrued based on the employee's length of service and performance.
  - Sick hours: The hours that an employee can take off from work due to illness or injury. Sick hours are usually paid and may require a doctor's note or other proof of condition.
  - Doubletime hours: The hours that an employee works at twice their regular rate of pay, usually on holidays or special occasions. Doubletime hours are rare and may be subject to certain limitations or conditions.
  - Jury duty hours: The hours that an employee spends serving as a juror in a court of law. Jury duty hours are usually paid by the employer or the government and may require a summons or a certificate of attendance.
  - Bereavement hours: The hours that an employee can take off from work to mourn the death of a family member or a close friend. Bereavement hours are usually paid and may vary depending on the relationship and the circumstances of the loss.
