### 3. Students will be able to create substantial base by the formation of strong professional

- A substantial base is a solid foundation of knowledge, skills, and values that can support students in their academic and career pursuits.
- A strong professional is someone who has the competence, confidence, and credibility to perform well in their chosen field and contribute to the society.
- To create a substantial base by the formation of strong professional, students need to:
  - Identify their interests, strengths, and goals, and align them with the requirements and opportunities of their desired profession.
  - Seek relevant and quality education and training that can equip them with the necessary knowledge and skills for their profession.
  - Engage in continuous learning and improvement, and seek feedback and guidance from mentors, peers, and experts.
  - Develop a professional network and a personal brand that can help them establish their reputation and credibility, and access resources and opportunities.
  - Demonstrate ethical, responsible, and respectful behavior in their professional interactions and activities, and uphold the standards and values of their profession.
  - Apply their knowledge and skills to solve real-world problems and create value for their clients, employers, and society.
- Some of the benefits of creating a substantial base by the formation of strong professional are:
  - It can enhance students' employability and career prospects, and help them achieve their personal and professional goals.
  - It can increase students' confidence and satisfaction in their work, and help them cope with challenges and changes in their profession.
  - It can foster students' creativity and innovation, and help them discover new possibilities and opportunities in their profession.
  - It can inspire students' passion and commitment to their profession, and help them make a positive difference in the world.

Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic of professional development are:

- Using acronyms to remember the key elements of a professional network, such as **R.E.A.C.H.** (Relationships, Expertise, Attitude, Communication, Helpfulness).
- Using rhymes to remember the benefits of continuous learning, such as **"The more you learn, the more you earn"** or **"Learning is earning"**.
- Using songs or poems to remember the steps of problem-solving, such as **"Define, Analyze, Generate, Evaluate, Implement"** or **"D.A.G.E.I."**.
- Using images or visual cues to remember the ethical principles of a profession, such as **"The Golden Rule"** (treat others as you want to be treated) or **"The Four-Way Test"** (is it the truth, is it fair, will it build goodwill, will it benefit all).
- Using key words or phrases to remember the components of a personal brand, such as **"Who, What, Why, How"** or **"Your Story, Your Value, Your Purpose, Your Strategy"**.

These mnemonics and learning tricks are easy to remember because they use auditory, verbal, or acoustic cues to connect new information to prior knowledge. They also enhance memory of complex words or ideas and promote better retention of material to be learned . They are especially beneficial to students who may have difficulty with information recall.
