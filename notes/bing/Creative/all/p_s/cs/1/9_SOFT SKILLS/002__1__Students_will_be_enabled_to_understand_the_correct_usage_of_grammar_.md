### 1. Students will be enabled to understand the correct usage of grammar.

Grammar is the set of rules and conventions that govern how words and sentences are structured and used in a language. Grammar helps to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency in communication. 

Some of the benefits of learning and using correct grammar are:

- It improves the quality and effectiveness of your writing and speaking.
- It enhances your credibility and professionalism in academic, professional, and social contexts.
- It helps you avoid misunderstandings and misinterpretations that can arise from ambiguous or incorrect language.
- It shows respect and consideration for your audience and their expectations.

Some of the main aspects of grammar that students need to understand and master are:

- Parts of speech: These are the categories of words that have different functions and meanings in a sentence, such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections.
- Sentence structure: This is the way that words and phrases are arranged and connected to form complete and meaningful sentences, such as simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences.
- Subject-verb agreement: This is the rule that the verb in a sentence must match the number and person of the subject, such as singular or plural, first, second, or third person.
- Verb tenses: These are the forms of verbs that indicate the time and aspect of an action or state, such as present, past, future, perfect, progressive, and modal verbs.
- Pronoun-antecedent agreement: This is the rule that the pronoun in a sentence must match the number, person, and gender of the noun or noun phrase that it refers to, also known as the antecedent.
- Modifiers: These are words or phrases that add detail or description to another word or phrase, such as adjectives, adverbs, phrases, and clauses. Modifiers must be placed correctly and clearly to avoid confusion or ambiguity.
- Punctuation: These are the symbols and marks that separate and organize words and sentences, such as periods, commas, semicolons, colons, apostrophes, quotation marks, parentheses, dashes, and hyphens. Punctuation helps to convey the tone, rhythm, and meaning of the language.
- Capitalization: This is the rule that certain letters must be written in uppercase or lowercase depending on their position and function in a sentence, such as the first letter of a sentence, proper nouns, titles, acronyms, and abbreviations.
- Spelling: This is the correct way of writing words according to the standard or accepted conventions of a language, such as the use of vowels, consonants, syllables, prefixes, suffixes, and root words.

To understand and apply the correct usage of grammar, students need to:

- Learn and memorize the definitions, rules, and examples of each aspect of grammar.
- Practice and review the grammar exercises and activities in their textbooks, workbooks, and online resources.
- Check and edit their own and others' writing and speaking for grammar errors and mistakes.
- Seek feedback and guidance from their teachers, peers, and tutors on their grammar performance and improvement.
- Read and listen to a variety of texts and genres that demonstrate correct and effective grammar usage.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for grammar are:

- To remember the difference between "since" and "for" when expressing duration, use the phrase "point in time since, period of time for". For example, "I have been studying English since 2010" (point in time) and "I have been studying English for 10 years" (period of time).
- To remember the simple past tense of regular verbs, use the rhyme "Did in the simple past, add -ed to the verb". For example, "I walked to school" (walk + ed).
- To remember the comma rules, use the acronym FANBOYS, which stands for the coordinating conjunctions For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, and So. Use a comma before these words when they connect two independent clauses. For example, "She likes apples, but he prefers oranges" (comma before but).
- To remember that the letter Q is always followed by the letter U in English words, use the phrase "Wherever there is a Q, there is a U too". For example, "queen", "question", "quiet", etc.
- To remember the difference between similar words that can be nouns or verbs, use the stress pattern. Nouns are usually stressed on the first syllable, while verbs are usually stressed on the second syllable. For example, "record" (noun) and "record" (verb).
- To remember the pronunciation of vowel pairs, use the phrase "When two vowels go walking, the first does the talking". This means that the first vowel is usually pronounced with its long sound, while the second vowel is silent. For example, "boat", "rain", "team", etc.
- To remember the meaning and spelling of new vocabulary words, use images, sounds, rhymes, or acronyms that relate to the word. For example, to remember the word "cemetery", use the mnemonic "ends in ery. There is nary an ary in it". To remember the word "definite", use the mnemonic "ends with it followed by a silent e because it is finite".
- To remember the idioms and phrases, use stories, examples, or associations that explain their meaning and usage. For example, to remember the idiom "a piece of cake", use the story "Imagine you have to do a very easy task, like eating a piece of cake. That's how you can say something is a piece of cake, meaning very easy".
