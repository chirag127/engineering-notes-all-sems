### D’ Alembert’s test for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sequence and Series in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-II

- D’ Alembert’s test is a criterion for the convergence of a series of real or complex numbers, where each term is nonzero when n is large .
- The test is based on the ratio of consecutive terms of the series and the limit of that ratio as n tends to infinity  .
- The test can be stated as follows  :

  - Let $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ be a series of real or complex numbers, and let the sequence $a_n$ satisfy: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = L$$
  - If $L > 1$, then the series diverges.
  - If $L < 1$, then the series converges absolutely.
  - If $L = 1$, then the test is inconclusive and the series may converge or diverge.

- The test is also known as the ratio test of convergence of a series  .
- The test is useful for series whose terms involve factorials, exponentials, or powers.
- The test can be derived from the comparison test by comparing the series with a geometric series.
- The test can be generalized to series whose terms are functions of a real or complex variable.

- An example of applying the test is as follows:

  - Consider the series $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{n^n}$$
  - The ratio of consecutive terms is $$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(n+1)!}{(n+1)^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{n^n}{n!} = \frac{(n+1)}{(n+1)^{n+1}} \cdot n^n = \frac{n^n}{(n+1)^n}$$
  - The limit of the ratio as n tends to infinity is $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^n}{(n+1)^n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 - \frac{1}{n+1}\right)^n = e^{-1} < 1$$
  - Therefore, by D’ Alembert’s test, the series converges absolutely.
