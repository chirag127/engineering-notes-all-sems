- A function f(t) has a Laplace transform F(s) if and only if:
  - f(t) is piecewise continuous on every finite interval in [0, ∞), meaning that f(t) has only a finite number of discontinuities and jumps in any bounded interval.

- The Laplace transform F(s) is defined as:

  F(s) = L[f(t)] = ∫[0, ∞) f(t)e^(-st) dt

- where s is a complex variable and the integral converges for s > c.
- The existence theorem guarantees that the Laplace transform is well-defined and unique for a wide class of functions, such as polynomials, exponentials, trigonometric functions, etc.
- The existence theorem also implies that the Laplace transform is a linear operator, meaning that:

  L[af(t) + bg(t)] = aL[f(t)] + bL[g(t)]

- where a and b are constants and f(t) and g(t) are functions with Laplace transforms.
