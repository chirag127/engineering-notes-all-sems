### Definition of Sequence and Series with Examples

- A **sequence** is an ordered list of numbers or objects that follow a certain rule or pattern. For example, the sequence 2, 4, 6, 8, ... is an arithmetic sequence with a common difference of 2. The sequence 1, 2, 4, 8, ... is a geometric sequence with a common ratio of 2. The sequence 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ... is a Fibonacci sequence with a rule that each term is the sum of the previous two terms.
- A **series** is the sum of the terms of a sequence. For example, the series 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + ... is the sum of the terms of the arithmetic sequence 2, 4, 6, 8, ... . The series 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + ... is the sum of the terms of the geometric sequence 1, 2, 4, 8, ... . The series 1 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 5 + 8 + ... is the sum of the terms of the Fibonacci sequence 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ... .
- A series can be finite or infinite, depending on whether the sequence has a finite or infinite number of terms. For example, the series 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 is a finite series with 4 terms, while the series 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + ... is an infinite series with infinitely many terms.
- A series can be convergent or divergent, depending on whether the sum of the terms approaches a finite or infinite value as the number of terms increases. For example, the series 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... is a convergent series with a sum of 2, while the series 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + ... is a divergent series with an infinite sum.

Mnemonics are techniques that can help you remember new information by associating it with something you already know. They can be words, phrases, images, sounds, or actions that trigger your memory. For example, you can use the acronym **ROYGBIV** to remember the colors of the rainbow: **R**ed, **O**range, **Y**ellow, **G**reen, **B**lue, **I**ndigo, and **V**iolet.

Some tips for using mnemonics are:

- Choose the appropriate mnemonic for your situation. For example, if you want to learn how to spell a word, you can use a spelling mnemonic like **i** before **e** except after **c**.
- Practice the technique until you can recall it easily. You can repeat it to yourself, write it down, or say it out loud to someone else.
- Use mnemonics that are meaningful, memorable, and fun. You can create your own mnemonics based on your interests, hobbies, or experiences. For example, if you like music, you can use a song lyric to remember a fact.

Some examples of different types of mnemonics are  :

- Keyword mnemonics: Use a word that sounds like or reminds you of the new information, and create a mental image that links the two words. For example, to remember that the capital of Norway is Oslo, you can imagine a man saying "Oh, slow" as he sees a snowman in Norway.
- Rhyming mnemonics: Use a rhyme or a poem to remember a list of items or facts. For example, to remember the number of days in each month, you can use the rhyme: "Thirty days hath September, April, June, and November. All the rest have thirty-one, except February alone."
- Acronym mnemonics: Use the first letter of each word in a list or a phrase to form a new word. For example, to remember the order of operations in math, you can use the acronym **PEMDAS**: **P**arentheses, **E**xponents, **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision, **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction.
- Acrostic mnemonics: Use the first letter of each word in a list or a phrase to form a new sentence. For example, to remember the names of the Great Lakes, you can use the acrostic: **H**omes: **H**uron, **O**ntario, **M**ichigan, **E**rie, and **S**uperior.
- Chunking mnemonics: Break down a long piece of information into smaller groups or chunks that are easier to remember. For example, to remember a phone number, you can chunk it into three parts: area code, prefix, and suffix.
