## Unit 4 - Complex Variable–Differentiation

- Complex differentiation is the study of how complex functions change with respect to complex variables.
- A complex function is a function that maps complex numbers to complex numbers, such as f(z) = z^2 + i, where z and i are complex numbers.
- A complex variable is a variable that can take complex values, such as z = x + iy, where x and y are real numbers and i is the imaginary unit.
- A complex function can be written in terms of its real and imaginary parts, such as f(z) = u(x,y) + iv(x,y), where u and v are real functions of two real variables.
- The derivative of a complex function is defined as the limit of the ratio of the change in the function value to the change in the variable, as the change in the variable approaches zero, such as f'(z) = lim_(Δz->0) (f(z+Δz) - f(z))/Δz, where Δz is a complex number.
- The derivative of a complex function measures the rate of change of the function value with respect to the variable, and also gives the slope and direction of the tangent line to the function graph at a given point.
- A complex function is said to be complex differentiable or holomorphic at a point z if the derivative f'(z) exists and is finite at that point. A complex function is said to be analytic or regular in a domain D if it is complex differentiable at every point in D.
- A key result that we will establish is that a complex function is complex differentiable at a point z if and only if (a) it is differentiable when considered as two real functions of two real variables and also (b) the Cauchy–Riemann equations (partial differential equations) apply. These equations link the real and imaginary parts of the function, such as ∂u/∂x = ∂v/∂y and ∂u/∂y = -∂v/∂x.
- The Cauchy–Riemann equations are necessary and sufficient conditions for complex differentiability, and they also imply that complex differentiable functions are infinitely differentiable and satisfy the Laplace equation, which is a second-order partial differential equation that describes the behavior of harmonic functions.
- Complex differentiation has many applications in mathematics, physics, engineering, and other fields, such as complex analysis, conformal mapping, potential theory, fluid dynamics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, and signal processing.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the topic are:

- To remember the Cauchy–Riemann equations, you can use the acronym CURE: Complex, U, Real, Equal. This means that for a complex function f(z) = u(x,y) + iv(x,y), the partial derivatives of u and v with respect to x and y must be real and equal, such as ∂u/∂x = ∂v/∂y and ∂u/∂y = -∂v/∂x.
- To remember the Laplace equation, you can use the phrase LAPLACE: LAPlacian is zero for Complex Equations. This means that for a complex function f(z) = u(x,y) + iv(x,y), the Laplacian operator applied to u and v must be zero, such as ∂^2u/∂x^2 + ∂^2u/∂y^2 = 0 and ∂^2v/∂x^2 + ∂^2v/∂y^2 = 0.
- To remember the definition of the derivative of a complex function, you can use the formula f'(z) = lim_(Δz->0) (f(z+Δz) - f(z))/Δz and think of it as a fraction: f over z. This means that the derivative is the ratio of the change in the function value to the change in the variable, as the change in the variable approaches zero.
