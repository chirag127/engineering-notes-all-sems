Metallurgy is the science and art of extracting and processing metals from their ores and alloys. Metallurgy in India and Ancient India has a long and rich history, dating back to the Chalcolithic period (around 3000 BCE) and continuing until the medieval times (around 1200 CE). India was one of the earliest civilizations to develop advanced metallurgical skills and produce various metals such as copper, bronze, iron, zinc, gold, silver, lead, tin and mercury. India also exported its metallurgical products to other regions such as China, West Asia, Europe and Africa.

- Mining: This is the process of extracting ore samples from the earth's crust. Ore samples are rocks that contain metal or metal compounds. Mining in India and Ancient India was done by using tools such as picks, axes, chisels, hammers and fire-setting. Some of the ancient mine pits in India are found at Khetri in Rajasthan (copper), Mitathal in Haryana (copper), Zawar in Rajasthan (zinc), Laurion in Bihar (silver) and Kolar in Karnataka (gold).

- Smelting: This is the process of separating the metal from the ore by heating it in a furnace or a kiln. Smelting in India and Ancient India was done by using various types of furnaces such as pit furnaces, shaft furnaces, crucible furnaces and reverberatory furnaces. The fuel used for smelting was mainly charcoal, but sometimes also coal, peat, dung and wood. The flux used for smelting was mainly lime, but sometimes also borax, soda, ash and clay. The slag produced from smelting was either discarded or reused for other purposes.

- Refining: This is the process of purifying the metal from the impurities and alloying it with other metals to improve its properties. Refining in India and Ancient India was done by using various methods such as distillation, liquation, cupellation, cementation, amalgamation and electrolysis. The refined metal was then cast into ingots or bars for further processing or trade.

- Metal objects: These are the final products of metallurgy, which include various tools, weapons, ornaments, coins, statues, vessels and structures made of metal or metal alloys. Metal objects in India and Ancient India show a high degree of craftsmanship and artistic expression. Some of the famous metal objects in India and Ancient India are the Delhi Iron Pillar (iron), the Ashoka Pillar (iron), the Dhar iron pillar (iron), the Mehrauli iron pillar (iron), the Wootz steel (steel), the Nataraja statue (bronze), the Dancing Girl of Mohenjo-daro (bronze), the Kushan coins (gold and silver), the Gupta coins (gold and silver), the Chola bronzes (bronze), the Bidriware (zinc and copper) and the Taj Mahal (marble and gold).
