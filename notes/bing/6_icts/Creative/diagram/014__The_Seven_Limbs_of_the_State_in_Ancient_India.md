The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India is a political theory formulated by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra. It describes the seven essential elements of a state that are necessary for its stability and prosperity. The seven limbs are:

- King: The ruler of the state who is responsible for peace, justice and welfare of the people. He is the supreme authority and the source of law.
- Amatya: The ministers or bureaucrats who assist the king in administration and policy making. They are selected based on merit and loyalty.
- Janapada: The territory or population of the state. It is the basis of the state's resources and power. It should be well protected and cultivated.
- Durga: The fort or capital of the state. It is the center of the state's defense and economy. It should be strategically located and well fortified.
- Kosa: The treasury or wealth of the state. It is the source of the state's expenditure and revenue. It should be well managed and increased.
- Danda: The coercive authority or military of the state. It is the instrument of the state's security and expansion. It should be well trained and disciplined.
- Mitra: The ally or friend of the state. It is the partner of the state's diplomacy and trade. It should be well chosen and maintained.
