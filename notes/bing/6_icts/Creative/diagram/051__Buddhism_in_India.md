Buddhism is an ancient Indian religion that is based on the teachings of the Buddha, who was born in the 6th century BCE in the kingdom of Magadha (now in Bihar, India). Buddhism spread throughout India and beyond in the following centuries, and developed into various schools and traditions. Buddhism declined in India from the 8th century CE onwards, due to various factors such as invasions, persecution, and assimilation. However, Buddhism still survives in some regions of India, especially in the Himalayan areas and the south.

Early Buddhism is the oldest form of Buddhism, which is based on the original teachings of the Buddha and the Pali Canon. Mahayana is a later development of Buddhism, which emphasizes the bodhisattva ideal and the use of skillful means to help all sentient beings attain enlightenment. Vajrayana is a form of tantric Buddhism, which uses esoteric practices and rituals to achieve Buddhahood in this very life.

Theravada is the dominant form of Early Buddhism in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, which follows the Pali Canon and the commentaries of the Theravada elders. Sarvastivada is another school of Early Buddhism, which was influential in northern India and Central Asia, and which follows the Abhidharma literature. Yogacara is based on the teachings of Asanga and Vasubandhu, who emphasized the mind-only nature of reality and the cultivation of the eight consciousnesses. Pure Land and Zen are two of the main devotional and meditative schools of Mahayana Buddhism, which originated in China and Japan. Other schools of Mahayana Buddhism include Tiantai, Huayan, and Nichiren.
