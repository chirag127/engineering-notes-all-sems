The Dancing Girl is a bronze figurine of a young woman from the Harappan civilization. It is one of the earliest examples of realistic human representation in Indian art. It shows the skill and sophistication of the ancient metalworkers.

The Ashoka Pillar is a stone column with a carved capital of four lions facing the four directions. It was erected by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka to mark the site where Buddha gave his first sermon. It is a symbol of the spread of Buddhism and the imperial power of Ashoka.

The Buddha Statue is a sandstone sculpture of the seated Buddha in the meditation pose. It is located in the Mahabodhi Temple complex in Bodh Gaya, where Buddha attained enlightenment. It is a masterpiece of the Gupta period, which is considered the golden age of Indian art.

The Nataraja Idol is a bronze statue of the Hindu god Shiva as the lord of dance. It depicts him performing the cosmic dance of creation and destruction. It is a symbol of the dynamic and rhythmic aspects of the universe. It is a masterpiece of the Chola period, which is known for its excellence in bronze casting.
