Chemistry in ancient India was a branch of science that dealt with the extraction, purification, and transformation of various substances, such as metals, minerals, plants, and animal products. It was also closely related to medicine, alchemy, metallurgy, and art. Chemistry in ancient India was called "Rasayana" in Sanskrit, which means "the way of essence" or "the science of mercury". Rasayana was one of the eight branches of Ayurveda, the traditional system of medicine in India.

Some of the notable achievements of chemistry in ancient India are:

- The discovery of zinc and its extraction by distillation.
- The development of steel and iron technology, such as the wootz steel and the iron pillar of Delhi.
- The invention of various types of dyes, paints, cosmetics, and perfumes.
- The formulation of various herbal and mineral medicines, such as the soma, the rasasindura, and the bhasmas.
- The practice of alchemy and the search for the philosopher's stone and the elixir of life.
