The Force Theory of origin of State is a theory that proposes that the state emerged from the use of force by one person or a small group of people over a larger population in a certain territory. The theory suggests that the state is a result of war, conquest, or domination by the strong over the weak. The theory implies that the state is based on coercion, violence, and power, rather than consent, cooperation, or legitimacy.

The diagram shows that the state is formed by applying force on a population living in a territory. The force can be physical, psychological, or ideological, and it can come from a single person, a small group, or a foreign power. The force is used to establish control, authority, and order over the population and the territory, and to extract resources, taxes, or obedience from them. The force can also be used to defend the state from external or internal threats, or to expand the state by conquering other territories and populations. The state, in turn, can provide some benefits to the population, such as security, stability, or infrastructure, or it can oppress, exploit, or neglect them. The state can also change over time, as the force can be challenged, resisted, or replaced by other forces, or as the population or the territory can change due to migration, revolt, or natural disasters. The state can also interact with other states, either peacefully or violently, depending on the balance of power and the interests of the actors involved. The state can also evolve into different forms of government, such as monarchy, dictatorship, democracy, or federation, depending on the distribution of power and the participation of the population in the decision-making process. The state can also dissolve, collapse, or merge with other states, depending on the internal or external factors that affect its stability and viability. The state, therefore, is a dynamic and complex entity that is shaped by the use of force and its consequences.
