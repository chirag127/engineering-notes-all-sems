Northern Indian languages are mainly Indo-Aryan languages that are spoken by millions of people in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal and other countries. They are derived from the ancient language of Sanskrit and have various dialects and scripts. Some of the major Northern Indian languages are:

- Hindi: The most widely spoken language in India and the official language of the central government. It is written in Devanagari script and has many regional varieties such as Braj, Awadhi, Bhojpuri, Rajasthani and Haryanvi.
- Urdu: The national language of Pakistan and one of the official languages of India. It is written in Perso-Arabic script and has a rich literary tradition. It is mutually intelligible with Hindi, but has more Persian and Arabic influences.
- Punjabi: The language of the Punjab region, which spans parts of India and Pakistan. It is written in Gurmukhi script in India and Shahmukhi script in Pakistan. It is known for its musical and poetic expressions, such as bhangra and gurbani.
- Kashmiri: The language of the Kashmir valley, which is disputed between India and Pakistan. It is written in Perso-Arabic script, but also has its own native script called Sharda. It has many loanwords from Persian, Arabic, Sanskrit and Tibetan.
- Dogri: The language of the Jammu region, which is part of the Indian state of Jammu and Kashmir. It is written in Devanagari script and has a rich folk literature. It is closely related to Punjabi and Pahari languages.
- Pahari: A group of languages spoken in the Himalayan foothills of India, Pakistan and Nepal. They are written in Devanagari, Perso-Arabic or Takri scripts and have many regional variations. Some of the prominent Pahari languages are Garhwali, Kumaoni, Nepali and Himachali.
