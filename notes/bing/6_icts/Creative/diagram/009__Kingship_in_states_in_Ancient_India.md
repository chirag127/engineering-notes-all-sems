Kingship in states in Ancient India was a complex and diverse phenomenon that evolved over time and across regions. There were different theories of origin of kingship, such as divine, social contract, and patriarchal. There were also different types of kingship, such as hereditary, elective, and nominal. The role and functions of the king varied according to the political, social, religious, and cultural context of each state. The king was expected to protect the territory, maintain justice, uphold dharma, perform sacrifices, and patronize arts and sciences. The king was also subject to the law and the advice of the council of ministers, elders, and priests. The king was often associated with various symbols of authority, such as the throne, the crown, the scepter, the umbrella, the chariot, and the lion.
