The four-class classification of society in ancient India, also known as the varna system, was a hierarchical division of people based on their occupation and duty. The four classes were:

- Brahmins: the priests, scholars, and teachers who performed rituals and studied the sacred texts.
- Kshatriyas: the rulers, warriors, and administrators who protected the society and maintained order.
- Vaishyas: the farmers, traders, and artisans who produced and exchanged goods and services.
- Shudras: the laborers and service providers who performed menial and low-caste tasks.
