### Water Management in India and Ancient India

Water management is the process of planning, developing, distributing and managing the optimum use of water resources. It is essential for the survival and prosperity of human societies, especially in a country like India, which has a diverse and complex hydrological system.

Water management in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient civilizations of the Indus Valley, such as Mohenjodaro and Harappa, which were well-organized and built of brick and stone. They had elaborate drainage systems, wells, reservoirs, canals and public baths. They also had a sophisticated knowledge of hydrological processes, such as the hydrologic cycle, rainfall patterns, groundwater recharge and river flow regimes.

In ancient India, water was considered as a sacred and vital element of life, and was revered and worshipped by various religions and cultures. Water was also associated with various deities, such as Varuna, the god of water and oceans, Indra, the god of rain and thunder, and Ganga, the goddess of the holy river Ganges. Water was also used for various rituals, such as bathing, cleansing, purification, offering and sacrifice.

Water management in ancient India was also influenced by the principles of Vastu Shastra, the traditional science of architecture and design, which aimed to harmonize the natural and human-made environments. Vastu Shastra prescribed various rules and guidelines for the construction and maintenance of water structures, such as wells, tanks, ponds, lakes, dams, canals and stepwells. These structures were designed to capture, store, distribute and utilize water efficiently and sustainably, while also enhancing the aesthetic and spiritual value of the surroundings.

One of the most remarkable and unique examples of water management in ancient India is the stepwell, also known as baori, bawdi, vav or kund. A stepwell is a deep, multi-storied well with a series of steps leading down to the water level. Stepwells were mainly used for drinking, washing, irrigation and religious purposes, but they also served as social and cultural hubs, where people gathered, exchanged, learned and celebrated. Stepwells were built in various shapes, sizes and styles, reflecting the regional and historical diversity of India. Some of the most famous and magnificent stepwells are Rani ki Vav in Gujarat, Chand Baori in Rajasthan, Agrasen ki Baoli in Delhi and Adalaj Vav in Gujarat.
