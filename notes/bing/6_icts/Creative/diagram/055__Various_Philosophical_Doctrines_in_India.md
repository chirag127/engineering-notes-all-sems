Various Philosophical Doctrines in India

According to Britannica, Indian philosophy consists of both orthodox (astika) and unorthodox (nastika) systems of thought. The orthodox systems accept the authority of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism, while the unorthodox systems reject or question it. The six orthodox schools of philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva-Mimamsa, and Vedanta. The unorthodox schools include Buddhism, Jainism, and other movements such as Ajivika, Charvaka, and Lokayata.

Each school of philosophy has its own distinctive features, methods, and doctrines, but they also share some common themes, such as the concepts of dharma, karma, samsara, reincarnation, dukkha, renunciation, meditation, and liberation (moksha, nirvana). Some schools also interacted and influenced each other, such as Vedanta and Buddhism, or Yoga and Samkhya. Indian philosophy has a rich and diverse history, spanning from the ancient times to the present day, and covering various aspects of human life and experience.
