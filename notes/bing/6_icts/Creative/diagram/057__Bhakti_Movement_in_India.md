The Bhakti movement was a religious and social reform movement that emerged in medieval India and spread across the subcontinent. It emphasized the love and devotion of a devotee towards a personal god and the god's grace and compassion for the devotee. The Bhakti movement challenged the caste system, the ritualism and the authority of the Brahmins, and promoted the use of vernacular languages for religious expression. The Bhakti movement also influenced the development of various literary, musical and artistic traditions in India.
