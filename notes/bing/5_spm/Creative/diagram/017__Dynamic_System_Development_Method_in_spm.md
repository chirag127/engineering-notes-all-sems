Dynamic System Development Method (DSDM) is an agile project delivery framework, initially used as a software development method. It is based on the principles of rapid application development (RAD), user involvement, empowerment of teams, frequent delivery, integrated testing, and stakeholder collaboration.

The diagram shows the eight phases of DSDM, which are:

- Pre-project: This phase ensures that only the right projects are started and that they are set up correctly.
- Feasibility: This phase quickly investigates the problem domain and the potential solutions, and determines whether the project is feasible and viable.
- Foundations: This phase establishes the foundations for the project, such as the business case, the requirements, the architecture, the development approach, and the management and delivery plans.
- Exploration: This phase iteratively develops the potential solutions and tests them against the requirements and the business objectives.
- Engineering: This phase iteratively develops and tests the solution in more detail and ensures that it meets the quality standards and the acceptance criteria.
- Deployment: This phase delivers the solution to the business and ensures that it is ready for use and meets the expectations of the stakeholders.
- Post-project: This phase reviews the project outcomes and the lessons learned, and ensures that the benefits are realized and sustained.
- Benefits Realisation: This phase monitors and evaluates the benefits of the solution over time and identifies any opportunities for improvement or further development.
- Maintenance: This phase provides ongoing support and maintenance for the solution and ensures that it remains fit for purpose and aligned with the business needs.
