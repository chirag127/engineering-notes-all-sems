Software estimation is the process of predicting the effort, cost, and duration of a software project based on various factors, such as project scope, complexity, work environment, and human resources. Software estimation is an important part of software project management (SPM), as it helps in planning, budgeting, and controlling the project.

There are different approaches and techniques for software estimation, such as expert judgment, analogy, parametric models, algorithmic models, and machine learning. Each approach has its own advantages and limitations, and the choice of the best method depends on the context and characteristics of the project.

The diagram shows that the software estimation process starts with defining the project scope, which includes the requirements, objectives, deliverables, and constraints of the project. The project scope is then used as the input for applying one or more estimation techniques, which produce estimates of the effort, cost, and duration of the project. These estimates are then used to create a project plan and schedule, which specify the tasks, resources, milestones, and deadlines of the project.

The project plan and schedule are then monitored and controlled throughout the project execution, and any deviations or changes are reported and analyzed. The project monitoring may also provide feedback to the estimation and scope definition steps, as the actual data and experience may reveal errors or uncertainties in the previous estimates or scope. The estimation and scope validation steps are then performed to check the accuracy and completeness of the estimates and scope, and to make any necessary adjustments or revisions. The software estimation process is thus an iterative and dynamic process that aims to improve the quality and reliability of the project outcomes.
