The diagram shows the main components of the framework for management and control, which are:

- Data collection: The process of gathering relevant information about the project's progress, costs, quality, risks, issues, and changes.
- Data visualization: The process of presenting the collected data in a clear and concise way, using charts, graphs, tables, dashboards, or reports.
- Cost monitoring: The process of tracking and comparing the actual and planned costs of the project, using techniques such as budget variance analysis, earned value analysis, or cost performance index.

- Prioritizing monitoring: The process of identifying and focusing on the most critical aspects of the project, such as the key deliverables, milestones, risks, issues, or changes, using techniques such as risk matrix, issue log, or change log.
- Project tracking: The process of updating and adjusting the project plan and schedule based on the actual performance and progress of the project, using techniques such as critical path analysis, network diagram, or Gantt chart.
- Change control: The process of managing and controlling the changes that occur during the project, such as scope changes, requirement changes, or design changes, using techniques such as change request, change impact analysis, or change approval.
- Software configuration management: The process of identifying, organizing, and controlling the software products and artifacts that are produced and modified during the project, such as code, documents, or test cases, using techniques such as version control, baseline, or audit.
- Contract management: The process of managing and controlling the contracts that are involved in the project, such as contracts with customers, suppliers, or subcontractors, using techniques such as contract negotiation, contract administration, or contract closure.
