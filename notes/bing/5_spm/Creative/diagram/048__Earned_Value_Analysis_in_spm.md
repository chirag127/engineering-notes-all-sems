Earned Value Analysis (EVA) is a project management technique that allows to measure the performance and progress of a project by comparing the planned effort (costs), the actual effort (costs), and the earned value (work completed) in terms of monetary values  . EVA helps to identify deviations from the project baseline and to take corrective actions if needed  .

Legend:
- The horizontal axis represents the time in units (e.g. weeks, months, etc.)
- The vertical axis represents the monetary value in units (e.g. dollars, euros, etc.)
- The blue line represents the planned value (PV), which is the budgeted cost of work scheduled (BCWS) at a given point in time   . It is also called the performance measurement baseline (PMB).
- The red line represents the actual cost (AC), which is the actual cost of work performed (ACWP) at a given point in time   .
- The green line represents the earned value (EV), which is the budgeted cost of work performed (BCWP) at a given point in time   . It is also called the budget at completion (BAC).
- The yellow area represents the cost variance (CV), which is the difference between EV and AC at a given point in time   . It indicates whether the project is over budget or under budget. CV = EV - AC   .
- The purple area represents the schedule variance (SV), which is the difference between EV and PV at a given point in time   . It indicates whether the project is ahead of schedule or behind schedule. SV = EV - PV   .
- The orange area represents the variance at completion (VAC), which is the difference between BAC and the estimated cost at completion (EAC) at the end of the project   . It indicates whether the project will finish over budget or under budget. VAC = BAC - EAC   .
