Organizational structures in SPM (Supplier Relationship Management) are the ways of dividing, coordinating, and directing the groups involved in the management of suppliers. There are different types of organizational structures that can be used in SPM, depending on the context, goals, and strategies of the organization. Some of the common types are:

- Functional/Hierarchical: This is a traditional structure where the organization is divided into functional units, such as finance, marketing, production, etc. Each unit has its own manager and staff, and they are responsible for their own tasks and decisions. The advantage of this structure is that it allows for specialization and efficiency within each function. The disadvantage is that it can create silos and communication barriers between different functions, and it can limit the flexibility and innovation of the organization.

- Matricial: This is a hybrid structure where the organization is divided into two dimensions: functional and project. Each employee belongs to a functional unit, but they can also be assigned to a project team that crosses the functional boundaries. The advantage of this structure is that it allows for better coordination and collaboration across functions, and it can leverage the skills and expertise of different employees. The disadvantage is that it can create role ambiguity and conflict, as each employee has to report to two managers: the functional manager and the project manager.

- RACI: This is a tool that can be used to define the roles and responsibilities of different stakeholders in a project or a process. RACI stands for Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed. Responsible means the person who does the work or performs the task. Accountable means the person who has the authority and accountability for the outcome of the work or task. Consulted means the person who provides input or advice to the work or task. Informed means the person who needs to be notified or updated about the work or task. The advantage of this tool is that it can clarify the expectations and communication channels of different stakeholders, and it can avoid duplication or omission of work or tasks. The disadvantage is that it can be time-consuming and complex to create and maintain, and it can change frequently depending on the situation.

- Agile: This is a flexible and adaptive structure where the organization is composed of self-organizing and cross-functional teams that deliver value to the customer in short iterations. Each team has a product owner, a scrum master, and a development team. The product owner is the person who represents the customer and defines the product vision and priorities. The scrum master is the person who facilitates the team's work and removes any impediments. The development team is the group of people who design, build, test, and deliver the product. The advantage of this structure is that it can respond quickly and effectively to changing customer needs and market conditions, and it can foster innovation and creativity. The disadvantage is that it can require a high level of trust and collaboration among the team members, and it can challenge the traditional management and control systems of the organization.
