The Framework for Management and Control in SPM is a set of processes and tools that help software project managers monitor and control the progress, quality, cost, and scope of their projects. It consists of four main components:

- Creating Framework: This involves defining the project objectives, scope, deliverables, milestones, tasks, resources, risks, and quality standards, and establishing a baseline plan and budget for the project. It also involves setting up a communication and reporting system, and assigning roles and responsibilities to the project team and stakeholders.
- Collecting Data: This involves gathering and recording information about the project activities, such as the actual time, cost, and quality of the work done, the status of the tasks and deliverables, the issues and changes encountered, and the feedback and satisfaction of the customers and users.
- Visualizing Progress: This involves analyzing and presenting the collected data in a clear and meaningful way, such as using charts, graphs, dashboards, and reports, to show the project performance, variance, trends, and risks. It also involves comparing the actual data with the planned data, and identifying any deviations, gaps, or problems that need to be addressed.
- Cost Monitoring: This involves tracking and controlling the project expenses, such as the labor, material, equipment, and overhead costs, and ensuring that they are within the allocated budget and the expected return on investment. It also involves using techniques such as earned value management, which measures the value of the work completed against the planned value and the actual cost, to assess the project efficiency and profitability.
- Prioritizing Monitoring: This involves focusing on the most critical and important aspects of the project, such as the key deliverables, milestones, risks, and quality standards, and ensuring that they are given the highest priority and attention. It also involves using techniques such as the Pareto principle, which states that 80% of the effects come from 20% of the causes, to identify and eliminate the major sources of problems and waste.
- Getting Project Back to Target: This involves taking corrective and preventive actions to resolve the issues and problems that affect the project performance, quality, cost, and scope, and to bring the project back on track with the planned objectives and expectations. It also involves managing the changes and requests that arise during the project, and ensuring that they are evaluated, approved, and implemented in a controlled and systematic way.
- Change Control: This involves establishing and following a formal process for managing the changes and requests that occur during the project, such as the changes in the requirements, scope, schedule, budget, or quality standards. It also involves documenting and communicating the changes and their impacts, and updating the project plan and baseline accordingly.
- Managing Contracts: This involves negotiating and administering the contracts and agreements that are involved in the project, such as the contracts with the customers, suppliers, vendors, and subcontractors. It also involves ensuring that the contractual terms and conditions are met, and that the deliverables and payments are exchanged as agreed.
