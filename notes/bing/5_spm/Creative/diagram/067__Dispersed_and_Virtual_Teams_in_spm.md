A dispersed team is a group of people who work together on a common project, but are located in different places, countries, cultures, or time zones. A virtual team is a type of dispersed team that relies on information and communication technologies to collaborate and coordinate their work.

Each team member may have different roles, skills, and responsibilities in the project, depending on the project scope, objectives, and requirements. The project manager is responsible for leading, planning, monitoring, and controlling the project activities and deliverables. The project sponsor is the person or entity that provides the resources, support, and authorization for the project. The project client is the person or entity that receives the benefits and outcomes of the project.

The team members communicate and collaborate with each other and with the project manager, sponsor, and client through various channels, such as email, phone, video conferencing, chat, online platforms, etc. The team members may face challenges such as cultural differences, time zone differences, communication barriers, lack of trust, coordination issues, etc. The team members need to overcome these challenges by establishing clear goals, roles, and expectations, building trust and rapport, using appropriate communication tools and methods, managing conflicts and feedback, and celebrating successes and achievements.
