### Agile Methods in Software Project Management

- Agile methods are a set of project management frameworks that break projects down into several dynamic phases, commonly known as sprints.
- Agile methods are based on delivering requirements iteratively and incrementally throughout the life cycle.
- Agile methods are an iterative methodology, meaning that after every sprint, teams reflect and look back to see if there was anything that could be improved so they can adjust their strategy for the next sprint.
- Agile methods are suitable for projects that have changing or uncertain requirements, need frequent feedback from users and stakeholders, and value working software over comprehensive documentation .
- Agile methods can improve customer satisfaction, team collaboration, quality, productivity, and adaptability .
- There are many different agile methods, each with its own principles, practices, roles, and artifacts. Some of the popular agile methods include:
  - Scrum: A common agile method for small teams that involves sprints of fixed length (usually two to four weeks), daily stand-up meetings, a product backlog, a sprint backlog, a sprint goal, a sprint review, and a sprint retrospective .
  - Kanban: A visual approach to agile that uses a board with columns to represent where certain tasks are in the workflow, such as to-do, doing, and done. Kanban limits the number of tasks that can be in each column to avoid bottlenecks and improve flow .
  - Lean: A philosophy of agile that focuses on eliminating waste, maximizing value, and delivering fast. Lean principles include defining value from the customer's perspective, mapping the value stream, implementing pull systems, seeking perfection, and empowering the team .
  - Extreme Programming (XP): A method of agile that emphasizes technical excellence, frequent releases, customer involvement, and team collaboration. XP practices include pair programming, test-driven development, continuous integration, collective ownership, and simple design .
  - Other agile methods include Crystal, Feature-Driven Development (FDD), Domain-Driven Design (DDD), Dynamic Systems Development Method (DSDM), ScrumBan, Agile-Waterfall/Hybrid, and Agile Scrum XP Hybrid.
