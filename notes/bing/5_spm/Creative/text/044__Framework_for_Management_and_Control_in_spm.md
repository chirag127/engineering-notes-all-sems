### Framework for Management and Control in SPM

- Framework for management and control in software project management (SPM) is a set of processes, tools, and techniques that help to monitor and control the project activities and ensure the project meets its goals and stays on budget.
- The framework consists of the following elements :
  - **Creating framework**: This involves defining the project scope, objectives, deliverables, milestones, tasks, resources, risks, and quality standards, and establishing a baseline plan and schedule for the project execution.
  - **Collecting data**: This involves gathering and recording relevant information about the project progress, performance, issues, changes, and risks, using various methods such as reports, meetings, surveys, audits, and reviews.
  - **Visualizing progress**: This involves presenting and analyzing the collected data using various tools and techniques such as charts, graphs, dashboards, indicators, and metrics, to compare the actual output with the expected one and identify any deviations, trends, or problems.
  - **Cost monitoring**: This involves tracking and controlling the project costs, using methods such as budgeting, estimating, forecasting, and earned value analysis, to ensure the project does not exceed the allocated funds and delivers the expected value.
  - **Prioritizing monitoring**: This involves focusing on the most critical and important aspects of the project, such as scope, quality, schedule, and risks, and allocating the appropriate resources, time, and attention to them, to ensure the project meets the stakeholder expectations and requirements.
  - **Getting project back to target**: This involves taking corrective and preventive actions to address any issues, problems, or deviations that affect the project performance, quality, or outcome, using methods such as change management, risk management, quality management, and issue management, to bring the project back on track and avoid further delays or failures.
  - **Change control**: This involves managing and controlling any changes that occur during the project execution, such as changes in scope, requirements, specifications, design, or resources, using methods such as change request, change approval, change implementation, and change evaluation, to ensure the changes are justified, authorized, documented, and communicated, and do not adversely affect the project goals, schedule, or budget.
  - **Managing contracts**: This involves managing and controlling the contractual relationships and obligations between the project and the external parties, such as vendors, suppliers, contractors, or customers, using methods such as contract negotiation, contract placement, contract administration, and contract closure, to ensure the contracts are clear, fair, legal, and beneficial, and the parties fulfill their responsibilities and expectations.
