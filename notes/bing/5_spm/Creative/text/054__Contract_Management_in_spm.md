### Contract Management in SPM

- SPM stands for Supplier Performance Management, which is a process of evaluating, monitoring and improving the performance of suppliers in a project or a business.
- Contract management is a subset of SPM, which focuses on managing the contractual aspects of the relationship between the buyer and the supplier, such as terms and conditions, deliverables, payments, incentives, penalties, disputes and changes.
- Contract management in SPM involves the following stages:
  - Contract planning: This is the stage where the buyer identifies the needs and requirements for the project, and decides on the type of contract, the scope of work, the selection criteria and the evaluation method for the suppliers.
  - Contract formation: This is the stage where the buyer invites bids or proposals from potential suppliers, evaluates them, negotiates with them and awards the contract to the best supplier.
  - Contract execution: This is the stage where the buyer and the supplier perform their obligations under the contract, such as delivering the goods or services, making the payments, reporting the progress and resolving any issues or conflicts.
  - Contract closure: This is the stage where the buyer and the supplier complete the contract, such as verifying the quality and quantity of the deliverables, settling the final accounts, releasing the warranties and bonds, and conducting a lessons learned review.
- Contract management in SPM requires the following skills and tools:
  - Communication skills: The buyer and the supplier need to communicate effectively and frequently throughout the contract lifecycle, using various modes and channels, such as emails, phone calls, meetings, reports and feedback forms.
  - Negotiation skills: The buyer and the supplier need to negotiate fairly and constructively on various aspects of the contract, such as price, schedule, quality, scope and risks, and reach a mutually beneficial agreement.
  - Risk management skills: The buyer and the supplier need to identify, analyze, prioritize and mitigate the potential risks that may affect the contract performance, such as technical, financial, legal, operational and environmental risks.
  - Change management skills: The buyer and the supplier need to manage the changes that may occur during the contract execution, such as changes in requirements, specifications, regulations, market conditions or customer expectations, and follow a formal change control process.
  - Performance measurement tools: The buyer and the supplier need to use appropriate tools and metrics to measure and monitor the performance of the contract, such as key performance indicators (KPIs), service level agreements (SLAs), scorecards, dashboards and benchmarks.
  - Contract management software: The buyer and the supplier need to use a software application that can help them manage the contract documents, data, activities, tasks, milestones, issues, payments, reports and audits in a centralized and secure manner.
