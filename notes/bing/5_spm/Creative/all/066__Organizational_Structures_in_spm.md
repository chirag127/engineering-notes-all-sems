### Organizational Structures in spm

Organizational structures in software project management (spm) are the ways of organizing the roles, responsibilities, and relationships of the project team members. Different organizational structures have different advantages and disadvantages for the project, depending on the project size, complexity, scope, and objectives. Some of the common organizational structures in spm are:

- **Functional structure**: In this structure, the project team members are grouped by their functional areas, such as engineering, marketing, finance, etc. Each functional group reports to a functional manager, who is responsible for the coordination and supervision of the group. The functional manager also acts as the project manager for the projects within his or her domain. The advantages of this structure are that it allows for specialization, efficiency, and clear lines of authority. The disadvantages are that it may cause communication problems, conflicts, and delays across different functional groups, and that it may not be suitable for complex or multidisciplinary projects.

- **Projectized structure**: In this structure, the project team members are grouped by their projects, rather than their functional areas. Each project has a dedicated project manager, who has full authority and responsibility over the project and the project team. The project manager reports to a senior manager or a project management office (PMO), who oversees multiple projects. The advantages of this structure are that it allows for flexibility, responsiveness, and customer focus. The disadvantages are that it may cause duplication of resources, lack of continuity, and low morale among the project team members, and that it may not be suitable for stable or routine projects.

- **Matrix structure**: In this structure, the project team members are grouped by both their functional areas and their projects. Each project team member reports to both a functional manager and a project manager, who share the authority and responsibility over the project and the project team. The functional manager is responsible for the allocation and evaluation of the project team members, while the project manager is responsible for the planning and execution of the project. The advantages of this structure are that it allows for balance, integration, and innovation. The disadvantages are that it may cause role ambiguity, power struggles, and stress among the project team members, and that it may not be suitable for simple or independent projects.

- **Hybrid structure**: In this structure, the project team members are grouped by a combination of different structures, depending on the needs and characteristics of the project. For example, a project may have a functional structure for some phases, a projectized structure for some phases, and a matrix structure for some phases. The advantages of this structure are that it allows for customization, adaptation, and optimization. The disadvantages are that it may cause complexity, inconsistency, and confusion among the project team members, and that it may not be suitable for standard or predictable projects.

A mnemonic to remember the four types of organizational structures in spm is:

- **F**unctional structure: **F**unctional groups
- **P**rojectized structure: **P**roject groups
- **M**atrix structure: **M**ixed groups
- **H**ybrid structure: **H**eterogeneous groups
