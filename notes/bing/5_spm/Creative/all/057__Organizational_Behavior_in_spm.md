### Organizational Behavior in spm

- Organizational behavior (OB) is the study of how people interact and behave within organizations, such as businesses, teams, groups, or communities.
- OB is an interdisciplinary field that draws from psychology, sociology, anthropology, economics, and management.
- OB can help managers and leaders understand, predict, and influence the behavior of individuals and groups in organizations, as well as the performance and outcomes of organizational processes and systems.
- OB covers various topics, such as:

  - Motivation: the factors that drive and sustain human behavior, such as needs, goals, rewards, and incentives.
  - Attitude: the evaluations and beliefs that people hold about themselves, others, situations, or objects, and how they affect behavior and emotions.
  - Perception: the process of selecting, organizing, and interpreting sensory information, and how it influences behavior and judgment.
  - Personality: the stable and unique patterns of traits, characteristics, and preferences that shape an individual's behavior and identity.
  - Emotion: the complex and subjective feelings that arise from internal or external stimuli, and how they affect behavior, cognition, and well-being.
  - Learning: the process of acquiring, modifying, or reinforcing knowledge, skills, abilities, or attitudes through experience, observation, or feedback.
  - Decision making: the process of choosing among alternatives based on preferences, values, criteria, or rules, and how it affects behavior and outcomes.
  - Communication: the process of exchanging information, ideas, or emotions through verbal or nonverbal means, and how it influences understanding, relationships, and coordination.
  - Leadership: the process of influencing, inspiring, or directing others to achieve a common goal or vision, and the traits, styles, and behaviors that characterize effective leaders.
  - Power and politics: the sources, distribution, and use of influence, authority, or control within organizations, and how they affect behavior, conflict, and cooperation.
  - Conflict and negotiation: the process of managing or resolving disagreements, disputes, or differences among individuals or groups, and the strategies, tactics, and outcomes of bargaining or compromising.
  - Culture and diversity: the shared values, norms, beliefs, and practices that shape the identity and behavior of an organization or a group, and the differences and similarities among individuals or groups based on various dimensions, such as gender, race, ethnicity, age, or religion.
  - Ethics and social responsibility: the principles, standards, or codes of conduct that guide the behavior of individuals or organizations, and the obligations or expectations to act in the best interest of society, stakeholders, or the environment.
  - Change and innovation: the process of introducing, adopting, or adapting to new or different ideas, products, processes, or practices within organizations, and the factors that facilitate or hinder change and innovation.
  - Stress and well-being: the physical, psychological, or emotional reactions to demands, challenges, or threats that exceed one's resources or coping abilities, and the factors that promote or impair health, happiness, or satisfaction.

- OB can be applied at different levels of analysis, such as:

  - Individual level: the behavior and outcomes of a single person within an organization, such as motivation, performance, or satisfaction.
  - Group level: the behavior and outcomes of two or more people who interact and share a common identity, goal, or task within an organization, such as teamwork, collaboration, or conflict.
  - Organizational level: the behavior and outcomes of the whole organization or its subunits, such as culture, structure, strategy, or effectiveness.

- OB can be studied using various methods, such as:

  - Surveys: the collection of data from a sample of individuals or groups using standardized questionnaires or interviews, and the analysis of the data using statistical techniques.
  - Experiments: the manipulation of one or more variables and the measurement of their effects on another variable, while controlling for other factors, and the comparison of the results between different groups or conditions.
  - Case studies: the in-depth examination of a single or multiple instances of a phenomenon, using multiple sources of evidence, such as documents, observations, interviews, or artifacts, and the analysis of the data using qualitative or quantitative techniques.
  - Meta-analysis: the systematic review and synthesis of the results of multiple studies on a topic, using statistical techniques to estimate the overall effect size and the sources of variation among the studies.
