### Stress in spm

- SPM stands for **Single Particle Model**, a simplified model of a lithium-ion battery that captures the main dynamics of the electrochemical reactions, the diffusion of lithium ions, and the mechanical stress in the active material.
- Stress in spm refers to the **mechanical stress** generated in the active material particles due to the **volume change** caused by the **intercalation and deintercalation** of lithium ions.
- Stress in spm is an important factor that affects the **performance, safety, and lifetime** of lithium-ion batteries, as it can lead to **particle fracture, electrode cracking, and capacity fade**.
- Stress in spm can be modeled by **coupling** the spm with a **stress sub-model** that accounts for the **elastic and plastic deformation** of the particles, the **interaction between particles**, and the **boundary conditions** of the electrode.
- Stress in spm can be **measured** by using **sensors** such as **strain gauges** or **acoustic emission** devices, or by using **non-invasive techniques** such as **X-ray diffraction** or **Raman spectroscopy**.
- Stress in spm can be **reduced** by using **materials** with **low volume change**, **high fracture toughness**, and **good conductivity**, or by using **design strategies** such as **coating**, **porosity control**, and **particle size distribution**.
