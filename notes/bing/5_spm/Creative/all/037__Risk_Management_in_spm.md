### Risk Management in spm

- Risk management is a sequence of steps that help a software team to understand, analyze and manage uncertainty.
- Risk management consists of four main phases: risk identification, risk analysis, risk planning and risk monitoring.
- Risk identification is the process of finding out the potential sources of risk that may affect the software project, such as requirements, technology, resources, stakeholders, etc.
- Risk analysis is the process of estimating the probability and impact of each identified risk, and prioritizing them according to their severity.
- Risk planning is the process of developing strategies to avoid, reduce, transfer or accept the risks, and allocating resources and responsibilities for implementing them.
- Risk monitoring is the process of tracking the status of the risks and the effectiveness of the risk plans, and updating them as needed.
- Risk management is one of the core tents of the philosophy of Software Project Management (SPM), as it helps to ensure the quality, cost and schedule of the software project.
- Risk management is also a system-wide managerial tool to analyze and manage safety and security risks to personnel, assets and operations, as in the case of the United Nations Security Management System (UNSMS).

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for risk management in spm are:

- To remember the four phases of risk management, use the acronym **IAPM** (Identification, Analysis, Planning, Monitoring).
- To remember the four types of risk strategies, use the acronym **ARTA** (Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, Accept).
- To remember the factors that affect the probability and impact of risks, use the acronym **TRUST** (Time, Resources, Users, Scope, Technology).
- To remember the components of a risk register, use the acronym **PIMP** (Probability, Impact, Mitigation, Plan).
