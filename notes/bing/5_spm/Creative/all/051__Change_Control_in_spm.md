### Change Control in spm

- Change control is the process of managing and documenting any changes that occur during the life cycle of a project or a system.
- Change control aims to ensure that the changes are beneficial, feasible, consistent, and aligned with the project or system objectives.
- Change control in spm (strategic portfolio management) involves the following steps:
  - Propose change: A change request (CR) is submitted by anyone who identifies a need for change, such as a stakeholder, a customer, or a team member.
  - Impact summary: The CR is evaluated to assess its technical merit, potential side effects, overall impact on other configuration objects and system functions, and the projected cost of the change.
  - Making a decision: The CR is approved, rejected, or deferred by the change control board (CCB), which is a group of authorized decision-makers.
  - Make the change: If the CR is approved, the change is implemented according to the agreed plan and schedule.
  - Closure: The change is verified, tested, and documented, and the CR is closed.
- Change control in spm helps to visualize strategies and targets, know whether the objectives are met, and make decisions with confidence.
- Change control in spm also helps to respond quickly when changes are needed, prioritize and schedule the right work, align teams, drive efficiency, and stay on strategy.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the steps of change control in spm is **PIMMC** (Propose, Impact, Make, Make, Closure).
- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of change control in spm is to compare it to a recipe. A recipe is a plan for making a dish, but sometimes you may need to change it based on the availability of ingredients, the preferences of the customers, or the feedback of the testers. Change control is the process of managing and documenting those changes, and making sure they are beneficial, feasible, consistent, and aligned with the original recipe.
