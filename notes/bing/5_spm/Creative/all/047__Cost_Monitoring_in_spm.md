### Cost Monitoring in spm

- Cost monitoring in Software Performance Management (SPM) is a process or function that monitors the expenses related to a software project, including hardware, development, maintenance and operations.
- Cost monitoring helps project managers to track progress and make sure they stay within budget. It also helps to identify and control any deviations or risks that may affect the project's cost performance.
- Cost monitoring involves collecting and analyzing cost data, such as planned costs, actual costs, and earned value, to measure the project's cost performance and forecast the project's cost at completion .
- Cost monitoring can be done using various tools and techniques, such as cost reports, cost variance analysis, cost performance index, cost baseline, and earned value analysis .
- Cost monitoring can provide several benefits for software projects, such as:
  - Improving the accuracy and reliability of cost estimates and budgets.
  - Enhancing the visibility and accountability of project costs and resources.
  - Supporting the decision-making and problem-solving processes of project managers and stakeholders.
  - Increasing the efficiency and effectiveness of project execution and delivery.
  - Reducing the risks of cost overruns, delays, and quality issues.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the benefits of cost monitoring is **IERSR** (Improve, Enhance, Support, Increase, Reduce).
- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of earned value analysis is to use the analogy of a pizza delivery. Suppose you order a pizza that costs $10 and is expected to be delivered in 30 minutes. The planned value (PV) of the pizza is $10, the actual cost (AC) of the pizza is the amount you pay for it, and the earned value (EV) of the pizza is the value of the pizza that has been delivered to you. For example, if the pizza arrives in 20 minutes and you pay $12 for it, then the AC is $12 and the EV is $10. If the pizza arrives in 40 minutes and you pay $8 for it, then the AC is $8 and the EV is $10. If the pizza arrives in 30 minutes and you pay $10 for it, then the AC is $10 and the EV is $10. The cost variance (CV) is the difference between the EV and the AC, and the cost performance index (CPI) is the ratio of the EV to the AC. For example, if the pizza arrives in 20 minutes and you pay $12 for it, then the CV is -$2 and the CPI is 0.83. If the pizza arrives in 40 minutes and you pay $8 for it, then the CV is $2 and the CPI is 1.25. If the pizza arrives in 30 minutes and you pay $10 for it, then the CV is $0 and the CPI is 1. The CV and the CPI indicate whether the project is under budget or over budget. A negative CV or a CPI less than 1 means the project is over budget, while a positive CV or a CPI greater than 1 means the project is under budget. A zero CV or a CPI equal to 1 means the project is on budget.
