### Critical Path (CPM) Method in spm

- The critical path method (CPM) is a technique where you identify tasks that are necessary for project completion and determine scheduling flexibilities  .
- A critical path in project management is the longest sequence of activities that must be finished on time in order for the entire project to be complete  .
- The critical path method helps you to plan, schedule, monitor, and control your project effectively  .
- The critical path method can help you to:
  - Identify the shortest possible duration of the project
  - Identify the activities that are critical and non-critical for the project
  - Identify the slack or float time for each activity
  - Identify the risks and uncertainties associated with the project
  - Optimize the use of resources and avoid bottlenecks
  - Manage changes and updates to the project schedule
- The critical path method involves six steps:
  1. Specify each activity using the work breakdown structure (WBS)
  2. Establish the sequence of activities and their dependencies
  3. Estimate the duration of each activity
  4. Draw the network diagram of the project
  5. Calculate the earliest and latest start and finish times for each activity
  6. Identify the critical path and the critical activities
- The critical path is the longest path in the network diagram, and it determines the minimum time required to complete the project   .
- The critical activities are the activities that lie on the critical path, and they have zero slack or float time   .
- The slack or float time is the amount of time that an activity can be delayed without affecting the project duration   .
- The network diagram is a graphical representation of the project activities and their dependencies, using nodes and arrows   .
- The nodes represent the activities, and the arrows represent the dependencies   .
- The dependencies can be of four types   :
  - Finish-to-start (FS): The successor activity cannot start until the predecessor activity is finished
  - Start-to-start (SS): The successor activity cannot start until the predecessor activity has started
  - Finish-to-finish (FF): The successor activity cannot finish until the predecessor activity is finished
  - Start-to-finish (SF): The successor activity cannot finish until the predecessor activity has started
- The network diagram can be drawn using different methods, such as the activity-on-node (AON) or the activity-on-arrow (AOA) method   .
- The activity-on-node (AON) method uses nodes to represent activities and arrows to represent dependencies   .
- The activity-on-arrow (AOA) method uses arrows to represent activities and nodes to represent dependencies   .
- The network diagram can be used to calculate the earliest and latest start and finish times for each activity, using the forward and backward pass methods   .
- The forward pass method calculates the earliest start and finish times for each activity, starting from the first activity and moving forward   .
- The backward pass method calculates the latest start and finish times for each activity, starting from the last activity and moving backward   .
