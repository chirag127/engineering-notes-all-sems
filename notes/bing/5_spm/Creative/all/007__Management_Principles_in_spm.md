### Management Principles in SPM

SPM stands for Software Project Management or Strategic Portfolio Management, depending on the context. Both are related to managing the resources, processes, and outcomes of projects or portfolios that involve software development or innovation. Here are some of the common management principles in SPM:

- **Define and document requirements**: A clear and detailed specification of the project or portfolio scope, objectives, deliverables, constraints, assumptions, and risks is essential for effective planning, execution, and evaluation of SPM.
- **Manage changes effectively**: Changes are inevitable in any project or portfolio, especially in dynamic and uncertain environments. SPM requires a systematic and flexible approach to identify, assess, approve, communicate, and implement changes, while minimizing the negative impacts and maximizing the positive opportunities.
- **Prioritize projects**: SPM involves managing multiple projects or portfolios that may have different goals, stakeholders, resources, and risks. SPM requires a rational and transparent method to rank and select the most valuable and feasible projects or portfolios, based on their alignment with the strategic vision, mission, and objectives of the organization .
- **Maintain quality**: Quality is the degree to which the project or portfolio meets or exceeds the expectations of the customers, users, and stakeholders. SPM requires a continuous and proactive process to define, measure, monitor, and improve the quality of the project or portfolio outputs and outcomes, as well as the quality of the processes and practices involved in SPM.
- **Utilize automation**: Automation is the use of technology to perform tasks or processes that would otherwise require human intervention or effort. SPM can benefit from automation in various aspects, such as data collection, analysis, reporting, communication, testing, deployment, and maintenance. Automation can enhance the efficiency, accuracy, consistency, and scalability of SPM.
- **Establish communication protocols**: Communication is the exchange of information, ideas, feedback, and decisions among the project or portfolio team members, stakeholders, and other parties involved in SPM. SPM requires a clear and consistent communication plan that specifies the purpose, frequency, mode, format, and content of communication, as well as the roles and responsibilities of the communicators and the recipients.
- **Manage risk**: Risk is the uncertainty or variability of the project or portfolio outcomes, which can have positive or negative effects on the project or portfolio objectives. SPM requires a systematic and proactive process to identify, analyze, evaluate, mitigate, monitor, and control the risks that may affect the project or portfolio performance, quality, scope, schedule, and budget.
- **Utilize a systematic approach**: A systematic approach is a structured and logical method to plan, execute, and evaluate the project or portfolio activities and results. SPM requires a systematic approach that follows a standard or customized framework, model, or methodology that guides the project or portfolio lifecycle, from initiation to closure, and ensures the alignment, integration, and coherence of the project or portfolio components .
