### Communications Genres in spm

- Communications genres are the types of documents or artifacts that are used to communicate information in software project management (spm).
- Communications genres can be classified into four categories: **planning**, **reporting**, **controlling**, and **supporting**.
- Planning genres are those that help define the scope, objectives, schedule, budget, risks, and quality of the project. Examples of planning genres are project charter, project plan, work breakdown structure, risk register, quality plan, etc.
- Reporting genres are those that provide information on the status, progress, performance, and outcomes of the project. Examples of reporting genres are status report, progress report, performance report, deliverable, etc.
- Controlling genres are those that help monitor and control the project activities, resources, and changes. Examples of controlling genres are issue log, change request, change log, variance analysis, corrective action plan, etc.
- Supporting genres are those that facilitate the communication and collaboration among the project stakeholders, such as team members, customers, sponsors, and suppliers. Examples of supporting genres are meeting agenda, meeting minutes, email, memo, presentation, etc.

- A mnemonic to remember the four categories of communications genres is **PRCS** (Planning, Reporting, Controlling, Supporting).
- A learning trick to understand the purpose and audience of each communications genre is to ask the following questions:

  - **What** is the information that needs to be communicated?
  - **Why** is the information important or relevant for the project?
  - **Who** are the intended recipients or users of the information?
  - **How** will the information be delivered or accessed?
  - **When** will the information be available or updated?
