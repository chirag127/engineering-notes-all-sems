### Extreme Programming in spm

- Extreme Programming (XP) is an agile software development framework that aims to produce higher quality software, and higher quality of life for the development team.
- XP is based on five values: communication, simplicity, feedback, courage, and respect .
- XP follows five rules: planning, managing, designing, coding, and testing.
- XP implements 12 practices: the planning game, small releases, metaphor, simple design, testing, refactoring, pair programming, collective ownership, continuous integration, 40-hour week, on-site customer, and coding standards  .
- XP is suitable for projects that have changing or unclear requirements, small and medium-sized teams, and customers who are willing to be involved in the development process  .
- XP has some advantages, such as: faster delivery, higher customer satisfaction, lower defect rates, improved teamwork, and increased flexibility  .
- XP also has some disadvantages, such as: higher risk of scope creep, lower documentation, higher dependency on individual skills, and potential resistance to change  .
- A possible mnemonic to remember the 12 XP practices is: **P**lan **S**mall **M**etaphors **S**imply **T**est **R**efactor **P**air **C**ollect **C**ontinuously **W**ork **C**ustomer **C**ode. The first letters of each word form the acronym PSMSTRPCCWCC, which can be pronounced as "Piss Mister Pickwick".
- An example of an application of XP is the development of the Chrysler Comprehensive Compensation System (C3), which was a payroll system for 10,000 employees. The project used XP practices such as on-site customer, pair programming, testing, and refactoring to deliver a working system in four months.
