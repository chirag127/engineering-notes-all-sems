### Management Control in spm

Management control in software project management (spm) is the process of monitoring and controlling project activities to ensure the project meets its goals and stays on budget. It involves setting performance targets, measuring progress towards those targets, and taking corrective action to ensure the project is on track.

Some of the key aspects of management control in spm are:

- **Scope control**: This is the process of defining and managing the scope of the project, which includes the features and functions of the software product, the deliverables, the assumptions, and the constraints. Scope control helps to avoid scope creep, which is the tendency of the project to expand beyond its original objectives.
- **Schedule control**: This is the process of developing and maintaining a realistic and feasible project schedule, which defines the sequence and duration of project tasks and milestones. Schedule control helps to avoid schedule slippage, which is the delay in completing the project on time.
- **Cost control**: This is the process of estimating and managing the budget of the project, which includes the resources, materials, and labor required to complete the project. Cost control helps to avoid cost overrun, which is the excess of actual project cost over the estimated project cost.
- **Quality control**: This is the process of ensuring that the software product meets the quality standards and expectations of the stakeholders, which includes the functional and non-functional requirements, the design specifications, and the testing criteria. Quality control helps to avoid quality defects, which are the errors or faults in the software product that affect its performance or functionality.
- **Risk control**: This is the process of identifying and managing the potential threats and opportunities that may affect the project, which includes the technical, organizational, environmental, and external factors. Risk control helps to avoid or mitigate the negative impacts of risks and to exploit or enhance the positive impacts of opportunities.

Some of the tools and techniques that can be used for management control in spm are:

- **Project plan**: This is the document that describes the scope, schedule, cost, quality, and risk of the project, as well as the roles and responsibilities of the project team, the communication and reporting mechanisms, and the change and issue management procedures. The project plan serves as the baseline for measuring and controlling the project performance.
- **Work breakdown structure (WBS)**: This is the hierarchical decomposition of the project scope into smaller and manageable units, which are called work packages. Each work package has a unique identifier, a description, a duration, a cost, and a set of deliverables. The WBS helps to define and organize the project work and to allocate the resources and responsibilities.
- **Gantt chart**: This is a graphical representation of the project schedule, which shows the start and end dates of the project tasks and milestones, as well as the dependencies and constraints among them. The Gantt chart helps to visualize and track the project progress and to identify and resolve any schedule conflicts or delays.
- **Earned value management (EVM)**: This is a method of measuring and reporting the project performance, which integrates the scope, schedule, and cost aspects of the project. EVM uses three key metrics: the planned value (PV), which is the budgeted cost of the work scheduled; the earned value (EV), which is the budgeted cost of the work completed; and the actual cost (AC), which is the actual cost of the work completed. EVM also calculates three key indicators: the schedule variance (SV), which is the difference between EV and PV; the cost variance (CV), which is the difference between EV and AC; and the schedule performance index (SPI) and the cost performance index (CPI), which are the ratios of EV to PV and EV to AC, respectively. EVM helps to evaluate the project status and to forecast the project outcome.
- **Quality assurance (QA) and quality control (QC)**: These are the processes of ensuring and verifying the quality of the software product, respectively. QA involves the planning and implementation of the quality standards and procedures, such as the quality management plan, the quality audit, and the quality review. QC involves the testing and inspection of the software product, such as the unit testing, the integration testing, the system testing, and the user acceptance testing. QA and QC help to identify and correct any quality defects and to ensure the customer satisfaction.
- **Risk register**: This is the document that records the identified risks and their characteristics, such as the probability, the impact, the priority, the trigger, the owner, and the response strategy. The risk register helps to monitor and control the risks and to update the risk status and information.
