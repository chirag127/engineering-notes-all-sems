### Dynamic System Development Method in spm

- Dynamic System Development Method (DSDM) is an agile project delivery framework, initially used as a software development method.
- It provides a structure for building and maintaining systems that meet the needs of stakeholders in a timely and cost-effective manner .
- It is based on the following principles :
  - Focus on the business need: The project should deliver what the business requires, not what the technical team prefers.
  - Deliver on time: The project should adhere to the agreed schedule and budget, using timeboxing and prioritization techniques.
  - Collaborate: The project should involve active participation and feedback from all stakeholders, including users, developers, managers, and testers.
  - Never compromise quality: The project should ensure that the delivered products meet the predefined quality standards and acceptance criteria.
  - Build incrementally from firm foundations: The project should start with a clear and stable vision and scope, and then develop the system in small and frequent increments.
  - Develop iteratively: The project should use feedback loops and testing to refine and improve the system throughout the development process.
  - Communicate continuously and clearly: The project should use various communication channels and methods to share information and knowledge among the project team and stakeholders.
  - Demonstrate control: The project should use effective planning, monitoring, and reporting mechanisms to ensure that the project is under control and aligned with the business objectives.
- It consists of the following phases :
  - Pre-project: This phase involves identifying the project sponsor, the project vision, and the feasibility of the project.
  - Project life cycle: This phase involves the following stages:
    - Feasibility study: This stage involves analyzing the business problem, the potential solutions, and the viability of the project.
    - Business study: This stage involves defining the business requirements, the system architecture, and the project plan.
    - Functional model iteration: This stage involves creating and testing a functional prototype of the system, based on the prioritized requirements.
    - Design and build iteration: This stage involves designing and building a complete and integrated system, based on the functional prototype and the quality standards.
    - Implementation: This stage involves deploying the system to the live environment, training the users, and providing support.
  - Post-project: This phase involves reviewing the project outcomes, identifying the lessons learned, and ensuring the benefits realization.
- It uses the following roles :
  - Executive sponsor: This role is responsible for providing the strategic direction, the funding, and the high-level approval for the project.
  - Visionary: This role is responsible for defining the vision and the scope of the project, and ensuring that the system meets the business needs.
  - Ambassador user: This role is responsible for representing the interests and expectations of the end users, and providing feedback and validation for the system.
  - Advisor user: This role is responsible for providing domain knowledge and advice to the project team, and supporting the ambassador user.
  - Project manager: This role is responsible for planning, organizing, and controlling the project activities, and ensuring that the project delivers on time, on budget, and on quality.
  - Technical coordinator: This role is responsible for coordinating the technical aspects of the project, such as the system architecture, the design, and the integration.
  - Solution developer: This role is responsible for developing and testing the system components, and ensuring that they meet the functional and non-functional requirements.
  - Solution tester: This role is responsible for testing the system components and the integrated system, and ensuring that they meet the quality standards and the acceptance criteria.
  - Business analyst: This role is responsible for eliciting, analyzing, and documenting the business requirements, and ensuring that they are aligned with the business objectives and the system vision.
  - Workshop facilitator: This role is responsible for facilitating the workshops and meetings among the project team and stakeholders, and ensuring that they are productive and collaborative.
  - DSDM coach: This role is responsible for providing guidance and support to the project team and stakeholders on the application of the DSDM framework and principles.
- It uses the following techniques :
  - MoSCoW prioritization: This technique involves categorizing the requirements into four levels of importance: Must have, Should have, Could have, and Won't have.
  - Timeboxing: This technique involves dividing the project into fixed-length periods of time, each with a specific goal and deliverable.
