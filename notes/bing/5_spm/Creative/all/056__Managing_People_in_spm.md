### Managing People in spm

- spm stands for software project management, which is the discipline of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling software projects.
- Managing people is one of the key skills of a software project manager, as the human factor is the main cause of project success or failure.
- Managing people involves selecting the best people for the project, motivating them, organizing them into effective teams, and resolving any conflicts or issues that may arise.
- Some of the best practices for managing people in spm are:

  - Manage your own workload first, before delegating tasks to others. This will help you set realistic expectations, prioritize your activities, and avoid micromanaging your team members.
  - Communicate clearly and frequently with your team, stakeholders, and customers. Use appropriate channels and tools, such as email, chat, video conferencing, collaboration platforms, etc. to share information, feedback, and updates. Establish regular meetings and reports to monitor progress and address any problems or risks.
  - Recognize and reward the achievements and contributions of your team members. Provide positive and constructive feedback, praise, recognition, incentives, and opportunities for growth and development. This will help you build trust, loyalty, and motivation among your team.
  - Understand the different models of motivation and apply them to your team members. Some of the common models are:

    - Maslow's hierarchy of needs, which suggests that people have five levels of needs: physiological, safety, social, esteem, and self-actualization. A higher level of need becomes a motivator only when the lower level is satisfied.
    - Herzberg's two-factor theory, which distinguishes between hygiene factors and motivators. Hygiene factors are the basic conditions that prevent dissatisfaction, such as salary, working environment, policies, etc. Motivators are the factors that increase satisfaction, such as achievement, recognition, responsibility, etc.
    - McClelland's theory of needs, which identifies three types of needs: achievement, affiliation, and power. Achievement-oriented people seek challenging goals, feedback, and recognition. Affiliation-oriented people seek social interaction, cooperation, and harmony. Power-oriented people seek influence, authority, and status.
    - Expectancy theory, which suggests that people are motivated by the expected outcomes of their actions. The outcomes depend on three factors: expectancy, instrumentality, and valence. Expectancy is the belief that one's effort will lead to performance. Instrumentality is the belief that one's performance will lead to rewards. Valence is the value that one assigns to the rewards.

  - Organize your team into a suitable structure and size, depending on the nature and complexity of the project. Some of the common types of team structures are:

    - Functional teams, which are composed of members from the same functional area, such as development, testing, design, etc. They have a clear hierarchy, authority, and communication. They are suitable for stable and predictable projects, but may lack cross-functional collaboration and innovation.
    - Project teams, which are composed of members from different functional areas, who are dedicated to a specific project. They have a high degree of autonomy, flexibility, and creativity. They are suitable for complex and dynamic projects, but may face conflicts, ambiguity, and resource constraints.
    - Matrix teams, which are composed of members from different functional areas, who are assigned to multiple projects. They have a dual reporting relationship, to both the functional manager and the project manager. They are suitable for balancing the needs of the organization and the projects, but may experience role confusion, power struggles, and communication issues.

  - Foster a positive and productive team culture, based on shared values, norms, and goals. Some of the ways to do this are:

    - Define and communicate the vision, mission, and objectives of the project and the team. Align them with the expectations and interests of the stakeholders and the customers.
    - Establish and enforce the roles and responsibilities of each team member. Clarify the scope, deliverables, deadlines, and quality standards of the project. Provide the necessary resources, tools, and support for the team to perform their tasks.
    - Promote collaboration, cooperation, and coordination among the team members. Encourage them to share their ideas, opinions, and feedback. Use brainstorming, problem-solving, and decision-making techniques to generate and evaluate solutions. Delegate tasks and authority appropriately, and hold the team accountable for the results.
    - Manage and resolve any conflicts or issues that may arise within or outside the team. Use constructive and respectful communication, negotiation, and mediation skills to address the sources and consequences of the conflicts.
