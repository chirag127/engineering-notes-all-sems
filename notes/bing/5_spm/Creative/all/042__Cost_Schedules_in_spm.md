### Cost Schedules in spm

- Cost schedules are tools that help project managers to estimate, monitor, and control the costs of a project.
- Cost schedules are usually based on the work breakdown structure (WBS) of the project, which divides the project into manageable tasks and assigns them cost estimates and durations.
- Cost schedules can be presented in different formats, such as tables, charts, graphs, or histograms, depending on the level of detail and the purpose of the communication.
- Cost schedules can help project managers to:
  - Plan and allocate the project budget and resources.
  - Track and measure the actual costs and variances against the planned costs and baselines.
  - Forecast the future costs and cash flows of the project.
  - Identify and mitigate the risks and issues related to the project costs.
  - Report and communicate the project cost performance and status to the stakeholders.
- Cost schedules can be created and updated using various methods and techniques, such as:
  - Analogous estimating: using the historical data and experience from similar projects to estimate the costs of the current project.
  - Parametric estimating: using statistical models and formulas to calculate the costs based on the project parameters and variables.
  - Bottom-up estimating: aggregating the costs of the lower-level tasks and components to estimate the costs of the higher-level deliverables and the whole project.
  - Three-point estimating: using the optimistic, pessimistic, and most likely estimates to calculate the expected costs and the range of uncertainty.
  - Reserve analysis: adding contingency reserves and management reserves to the cost estimates to account for the known and unknown risks and changes.
- Cost schedules can be influenced by various factors and constraints, such as:
  - Scope: the size, complexity, and quality of the project deliverables and requirements.
  - Time: the duration, sequence, and dependencies of the project activities and milestones.
  - Resources: the availability, quantity, and quality of the human, material, and equipment resources needed for the project.
  - Quality: the standards, criteria, and specifications that the project deliverables and processes must meet or exceed.
  - Risks: the uncertainties, threats, and opportunities that may affect the project costs positively or negatively.
  - Stakeholders: the expectations, interests, and influence of the people and organizations involved in or affected by the project.
  - Changes: the modifications, additions, or deletions of the project scope, time, resources, quality, risks, or stakeholders that may impact the project costs.
- Cost schedules can be evaluated and controlled using various tools and techniques, such as:
  - Earned value management (EVM): a method that compares the planned value, earned value, and actual cost of the project to measure the cost variance and the cost performance index.
  - Trend analysis: a technique that examines the patterns and changes of the project costs over time to identify the trends and forecast the future costs.
  - Variance analysis: a technique that analyzes the differences and deviations between the planned and actual costs to identify the causes and effects of the variances.
  - Performance reviews: a process that assesses and reports the project cost performance and status to the stakeholders using various metrics and indicators.
  - Change control: a process that manages and approves the changes to the project cost baselines and budgets using a change request system.
