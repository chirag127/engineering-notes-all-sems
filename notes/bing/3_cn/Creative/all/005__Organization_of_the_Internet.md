#### Organization of the Internet

- The Internet is a global network of interconnected computers and devices that communicate using standardized protocols and procedures .
- The Internet is composed of many smaller networks, called internets, that are not connected to the global Internet but use the same standards.
- The Internet is organized at four levels:
  - Computer and network hardware: This level consists of the physical devices and components that enable data transmission, such as routers, switches, cables, modems, etc.
  - Internet access and Internet service providers (ISPs): This level consists of the organizations and entities that provide access to the Internet, such as telecom companies, cable operators, wireless providers, etc.
  - Navigation within the Internet: This level consists of the tools and services that help users find and access information on the Internet, such as domain names, IP addresses, search engines, web browsers, etc.
  - Individual sites and communities: This level consists of the websites, platforms, applications, and online groups that create and share content and services on the Internet, such as social media, e-commerce, e-learning, etc.
- The Internet is governed by a variety of stakeholders, such as governments, intergovernmental organizations, technical bodies, civil society, academia, and users, who collaborate and coordinate through various forums and mechanisms.
- The Internet is constantly evolving and facing new challenges and opportunities, such as security, privacy, digital inclusion, innovation, and human rights.
