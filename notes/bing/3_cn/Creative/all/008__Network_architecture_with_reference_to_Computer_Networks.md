#### Network architecture with reference to Computer Networks

- Network architecture is the design of a computer network    .
- It is a framework for the specification of a network's physical components and their functional organization and configuration, its operational principles and procedures, as well as communication protocols used   .
- Network architecture components include hardware, software, transmission media (wired or wireless), network topology, and communications protocols  .
- Based on the network's size and purpose, numerous ways to design network architecture exist .
- Here are the most common and widely used computer network types  :
  - LAN (local area network): A LAN connects computers over a relatively short distance, allowing them to share data, files, printers, and other resources. A LAN typically covers a single building or a group of buildings, such as a home, office, or campus. A LAN can use Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or other technologies to connect devices.
  - WLAN (wireless local area network): A WLAN is just like a LAN but connections between devices on the network are wireless, using radio waves or infrared signals. A WLAN can provide mobility, flexibility, and scalability to a LAN. A WLAN typically uses Wi-Fi or Bluetooth to connect devices.
  - WAN (wide area network): A WAN connects computers over a large geographical area, such as a city, country, or the world. A WAN can span multiple LANs and use different technologies to connect them, such as leased lines, satellite links, or the Internet. A WAN can provide access to remote resources, such as web servers, email servers, or cloud services.
  - MAN (metropolitan area network): A MAN connects computers over a metropolitan area, such as a city or a town. A MAN can cover a larger area than a LAN but a smaller area than a WAN. A MAN can use fiber-optic cables, microwave links, or other technologies to connect devices. A MAN can provide high-speed data transfer and support multimedia applications, such as video conferencing or streaming.
  - PAN (personal area network): A PAN connects devices around a single person, such as a laptop, smartphone, smartwatch, or headphones. A PAN can enable data exchange, synchronization, and communication between personal devices. A PAN typically uses Bluetooth, NFC, or USB to connect devices.
  - SAN (storage area network): A SAN connects storage devices, such as hard disks, tape drives, or optical disks, to servers or other computers. A SAN can provide high-performance, reliable, and secure data storage and backup. A SAN can use SCSI, Fibre Channel, or iSCSI to connect devices.
  - CAN (campus area network): A CAN connects computers over a campus area, such as a university, school, or research center. A CAN can consist of multiple LANs and use switches, routers, or firewalls to connect them. A CAN can provide access to academic resources, such as libraries, laboratories, or classrooms.
  - VPN (virtual private network): A VPN connects computers over a public network, such as the Internet, but creates a secure and private tunnel for data transmission. A VPN can encrypt, authenticate, and compress data to ensure its confidentiality, integrity, and efficiency. A VPN can use IPsec, SSL, or PPTP to connect devices.
  - BAN (body area network): A BAN connects devices on or inside a human body, such as sensors, implants, or wearable devices. A BAN can monitor, diagnose, or treat various health conditions, such as heart rate, blood pressure, or glucose level. A BAN can use ZigBee, Bluetooth, or RFID to connect devices.

- A mnemonic to remember the network types is **LWMPCSCVB** (pronounced as "lum-pix-cub"), where each letter stands for the first letter of each network type. For example, L stands for LAN, W stands for WAN, and so on.
- A learning trick to understand the network types is to compare them with different modes of transportation, such as cars, buses, trains, planes, bikes, etc. For example, a LAN is like a car that can transport a few people within a short distance, a WAN is like a plane that can transport many people across a long distance, a PAN is like a bike
