### TCP Congestion control in transport layer

- TCP stands for Transmission Control Protocol. It is a connection-oriented and reliable transport layer protocol that facilitates the transmission of packets from source to destination.
- TCP congestion control is the main technique that prevents the Internet from slowing down due to over-utilization of network resources.
- TCP congestion control aims to achieve two goals: high throughput and fairness. High throughput means that the sender can utilize the available bandwidth efficiently and avoid wasting network resources. Fairness means that the sender can share the network resources fairly with other competing flows and avoid starving them.
- TCP congestion control is based on the concept of congestion window (cwnd), which is the amount of data that the sender can send without receiving an acknowledgment from the receiver. The sender adjusts the cwnd based on the feedback from the network, such as acknowledgments, timeouts, and congestion signals.
- There are three phases that TCP uses for congestion control: slow start, congestion avoidance, and congestion detection:
  - Slow start: In this phase, the sender starts with a small cwnd and doubles it every round trip time (RTT) until it reaches a threshold (ssthresh) or a loss occurs. This phase allows the sender to probe the network capacity and find the optimal cwnd.
  - Congestion avoidance: In this phase, the sender increases the cwnd by one segment every RTT, which results in a linear growth of cwnd. This phase allows the sender to maintain a high throughput and avoid causing congestion.
  - Congestion detection: In this phase, the sender detects a loss event, which indicates that the network is congested. The sender reduces the cwnd and the ssthresh by half and enters either slow start or congestion avoidance phase depending on the new cwnd value. This phase allows the sender to react to congestion and reduce the network load.
- TCP uses different mechanisms to detect congestion, such as timeouts, duplicate acknowledgments, and explicit congestion notification (ECN):
  - Timeouts: A timeout occurs when the sender does not receive an acknowledgment for a segment within a certain time interval. This indicates that the segment was lost or delayed in the network. The sender retransmits the segment and enters the slow start phase.
  - Duplicate acknowledgments: A duplicate acknowledgment occurs when the receiver receives an out-of-order segment and sends an acknowledgment for the last in-order segment. This indicates that a segment was lost or reordered in the network. If the sender receives three duplicate acknowledgments for the same segment, it performs a fast retransmit and enters the congestion avoidance phase. This is also known as fast recovery.
  - Explicit congestion notification (ECN): ECN is a mechanism that allows the network devices, such as routers and switches, to mark the packets with a congestion signal when they experience congestion. The receiver echoes the congestion signal back to the sender in the acknowledgment. The sender reduces the cwnd and enters the congestion avoidance phase. ECN is not widely used and most operating systems have it disabled by default.
- TCP congestion control is a dynamic and adaptive process that adjusts the cwnd according to the network conditions. It is a key factor that affects the performance and efficiency of TCP and the Internet.
