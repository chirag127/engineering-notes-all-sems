#### Network performance and transmission impairments in Computer Networks

- Network performance is a measure of how well a network can deliver data and services to its users. It can be evaluated by various metrics, such as throughput, delay, jitter, loss, and error rate.
- Throughput is the amount of data successfully transferred over the network in a given time. It depends on the bandwidth, congestion, and protocol efficiency of the network. Higher throughput means better network performance.
- Delay is the time it takes for a packet to travel from the source to the destination. It consists of four components: propagation delay, transmission delay, processing delay, and queuing delay. Lower delay means better network performance.
- Jitter is the variation in delay of packets in a network. It is caused by factors such as network congestion, routing changes, and buffer overflows. High jitter can affect the quality of real-time applications such as voice and video. Lower jitter means better network performance.
- Loss is the percentage of packets that are dropped or lost in the network due to errors, congestion, or timeouts. It can reduce the throughput and reliability of the network. Lower loss means better network performance.
- Error rate is the percentage of packets that are corrupted or altered in the network due to noise, interference, or distortion. It can affect the accuracy and integrity of the data. Lower error rate means better network performance.

- Transmission impairments are any factors that cause the received signal to be different from the transmitted signal. They can degrade the quality and performance of the network. There are three main types of transmission impairments: attenuation, distortion, and noise.
- Attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it travels through the transmission medium. It is caused by factors such as distance, resistance, and interference. Higher attenuation means lower signal quality and higher error rate.
- Distortion is the change in shape or form of the signal as it travels through the transmission medium. It is caused by factors such as frequency-dependent attenuation, phase shift, and nonlinearity. Higher distortion means lower signal quality and higher error rate.
- Noise is the unwanted or random signal that is added to the original signal as it travels through the transmission medium. It is caused by factors such as thermal noise, induced noise, crosstalk noise, and impulse noise. Higher noise means lower signal quality and higher error rate.

- A mnemonic to remember the types of transmission impairments is **A**ttenuation, **D**istortion, and **N**oise, or **ADN**.
- A mnemonic to remember the components of delay is **P**ropagation, **T**ransmission, **P**rocessing, and **Q**ueuing, or **PTPQ**.
- A mnemonic to remember the metrics of network performance is **T**hroughput, **D**elay, **J**itter, **L**oss, and **E**rror rate, or **TDJLE**.
