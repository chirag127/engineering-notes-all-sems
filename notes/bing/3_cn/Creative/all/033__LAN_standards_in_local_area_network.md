#### LAN standards in local area network

- LAN standards are the specifications and protocols that define how devices communicate and share data over a local area network (LAN).
- LAN standards are developed and maintained by various organizations, such as the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), and the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF).
- LAN standards cover different aspects of LAN communication, such as physical layer, data link layer, network layer, and application layer.
- Some of the most common LAN standards are:

  - Ethernet: The most widely used LAN standard, which defines how devices transmit and receive data over a shared medium, such as twisted pair, coaxial cable, or optical fiber. Ethernet uses the Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD) protocol to avoid data collisions and ensure reliable data transfer. Ethernet supports various data rates, such as 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, and 10 Gbps. Ethernet is standardized by the IEEE 802.3 series of standards.
  - Wi-Fi: The most popular wireless LAN standard, which defines how devices communicate over radio waves using the IEEE 802.11 series of standards. Wi-Fi supports various data rates, such as 11 Mbps, 54 Mbps, 600 Mbps, and 1.3 Gbps. Wi-Fi uses the Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA) protocol to avoid data collisions and ensure reliable data transfer. Wi-Fi also provides security features, such as encryption, authentication, and access control, using the IEEE 802.11i standard.
  - Token Ring: A LAN standard that uses a token-passing protocol to regulate data transmission over a ring topology. Token Ring supports data rates of 4 Mbps and 16 Mbps. Token Ring is standardized by the IEEE 802.5 standard.
  - FDDI: A LAN standard that uses a token-passing protocol to regulate data transmission over a dual ring topology. FDDI supports data rates of 100 Mbps and uses optical fiber as the transmission medium. FDDI is standardized by the ANSI X3T9.5 standard.
  - ATM: A LAN standard that uses fixed-length cells to transmit data over various types of media, such as twisted pair, coaxial cable, optical fiber, or wireless. ATM supports data rates of up to 622 Mbps and provides quality of service (QoS) features, such as guaranteed bandwidth, delay, and jitter. ATM is standardized by the ITU-T I.150 series of standards.

- A mnemonic to remember some of the LAN standards is: **E**very **W**izard **T**akes **F**ancy **A**pples. (Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Token Ring, FDDI, ATM)
