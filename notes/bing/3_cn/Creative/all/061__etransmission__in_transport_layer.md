### etransmission in transport layer

- The transport layer is the fourth layer in the OSI model and the third layer in the TCP/IP model. It provides end-to-end communication services for applications, such as reliable data transfer, flow control, congestion control, error control, and multiplexing.
- etransmission is the process of sending and receiving data segments between the transport layer entities at the source and destination hosts. It involves the following steps:
  - The transport layer at the source host receives data from the application layer and divides it into segments. Each segment has a header that contains information such as source and destination port numbers, sequence numbers, acknowledgment numbers, checksum, and flags.
  - The transport layer at the source host sends the segments to the network layer, which encapsulates them into packets and forwards them to the destination host through the underlying network.
  - The transport layer at the destination host receives the packets from the network layer and extracts the segments from them. It checks the checksum and the flags to verify the integrity and validity of the segments. It also checks the sequence numbers and acknowledgment numbers to detect any lost, duplicated, or out-of-order segments.
  - The transport layer at the destination host sends acknowledgments (ACKs) to the source host for the segments that it has received correctly. It may also send negative acknowledgments (NACKs) or duplicate ACKs for the segments that it has not received or received incorrectly.
  - The transport layer at the source host receives the ACKs, NACKs, or duplicate ACKs from the destination host and updates its window size, retransmission timer, and congestion window accordingly. It retransmits the segments that have not been acknowledged or have been negatively acknowledged. It also adjusts its sending rate to avoid or alleviate congestion in the network.
  - The transport layer at the destination host reassembles the segments into the original data and delivers it to the application layer.
- The transport layer can use different protocols to perform etransmission, such as TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) or UDP (User Datagram Protocol). TCP is a connection-oriented, reliable, and full-duplex protocol that provides error control, flow control, and congestion control. UDP is a connectionless, unreliable, and simplex protocol that does not provide any of these services. TCP is suitable for applications that require high reliability and low latency, such as web browsing, email, and file transfer. UDP is suitable for applications that can tolerate some loss and delay, such as video streaming, voice over IP, and online gaming.
