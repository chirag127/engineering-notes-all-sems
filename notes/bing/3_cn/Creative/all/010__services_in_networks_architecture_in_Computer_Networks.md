#### Services in networks architecture in Computer Networks

- Services are the **capabilities** that each layer in a network architecture provides to the layer above it.
- Services are **implemented** by using protocols, which are sets of rules and formats that define the communication between entities in a network.
- Services can be **classified** into two types: connection-oriented and connectionless.
- Connection-oriented services **require** the establishment of a logical connection between the sender and the receiver before any data can be transferred. They provide **reliable**, **ordered**, and **error-free** delivery of data. An example of a connection-oriented service is TCP.
- Connectionless services **do not require** any connection setup or teardown. They provide **unreliable**, **unordered**, and **error-prone** delivery of data. An example of a connectionless service is UDP.
- Services can also be **categorized** into three levels: application, network, and data link.
- Application services are the **highest-level** services that provide functionality to end-user applications, such as web browsing, email, file transfer, etc. They are often implemented using a **client-server** or **peer-to-peer** architecture based on application layer network protocols. Examples of application services are HTTP, SMTP, FTP, etc.
- Network services are the **intermediate-level** services that provide connectivity and routing between different networks or subnetworks. They are responsible for **addressing**, **forwarding**, and **delivering** packets across the network. They are often implemented using **routers** and **switches**. Examples of network services are IP, ICMP, ARP, etc.
- Data link services are the **lowest-level** services that provide physical transmission of bits over a single link or medium. They are responsible for **framing**, **error detection**, and **flow control** of data. They are often implemented using **network interface cards** and **cables**. Examples of data link services are Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, etc.
