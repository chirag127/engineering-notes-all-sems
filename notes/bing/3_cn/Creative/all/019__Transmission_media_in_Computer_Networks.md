#### Transmission media in Computer Networks

Transmission media in computer networks are the physical channels that carry data from one device to another. They can be classified into two major categories: guided and unguided media.

- Guided media are those that provide a physical path for the data to travel, such as wires, cables, and optical fibers. They can be further divided into three types: twisted pair, coaxial, and fiber optic cables.
  - Twisted pair cables consist of two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce electromagnetic interference. They are commonly used for telephone and Ethernet connections. They have low cost, easy installation, and moderate bandwidth, but they are also susceptible to noise, attenuation, and eavesdropping. A mnemonic to remember the advantages and disadvantages of twisted pair cables is **TAME**: Twisted, Affordable, Moderate, Eavesdroppable.
  - Coaxial cables consist of a central copper core surrounded by an insulating layer, a braided metal shield, and an outer cover. They are used for cable TV, broadband internet, and long-distance telephone networks. They have high bandwidth, low noise, and high security, but they are also expensive, bulky, and difficult to install. A mnemonic to remember the advantages and disadvantages of coaxial cables is **CHUBS**: Coaxial, High, Unwieldy, Bulky, Secure.
  - Fiber optic cables consist of thin glass or plastic fibers that transmit data using light pulses. They are used for high-speed internet, long-distance communication, and medical imaging. They have very high bandwidth, low attenuation, low interference, and high security, but they are also very expensive, fragile, and require special devices to connect. A mnemonic to remember the advantages and disadvantages of fiber optic cables is **FLASH**: Fiber, Light, Attenuation, Speed, High-cost.

- Unguided media are those that do not provide a physical path for the data to travel, but rather use electromagnetic waves or signals to transmit data through air, space, or water. They can be further divided into three types: radio waves, microwaves, and infrared waves.
  - Radio waves are electromagnetic waves that can travel long distances and penetrate through walls and buildings. They are used for radio, TV, cellular phones, and wireless LANs. They have low cost, easy installation, and wide coverage, but they are also prone to interference, noise, and security issues. A mnemonic to remember the advantages and disadvantages of radio waves is **RICE**: Radio, Inexpensive, Coverage, EMI.
  - Microwaves are electromagnetic waves that have higher frequencies and shorter wavelengths than radio waves. They are used for satellite communication, radar, and wireless WANs. They have high bandwidth, low attenuation, and high security, but they are also expensive, require line-of-sight, and suffer from rain fade. A mnemonic to remember the advantages and disadvantages of microwaves is **MARS**: Microwave, Attenuation, Rain, Sight.
  - Infrared waves are electromagnetic waves that have higher frequencies and shorter wavelengths than microwaves. They are used for remote controls, wireless keyboards and mice, and short-range communication. They have low cost, low interference, and high security, but they are also limited by distance, line-of-sight, and ambient light. A mnemonic to remember the advantages and disadvantages of infrared waves is **ILLS**: Infrared, Light, Line, Short.
