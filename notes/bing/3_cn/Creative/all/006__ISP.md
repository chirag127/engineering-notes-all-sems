#### ISP

- ISP stands for Internet Service Provider. It is a company or organization that provides access to the internet and other related services such as web hosting, email, and domain name registration.
- ISPs can be classified into different types based on the level of service they offer, the technology they use, and the customers they serve. Some common types of ISPs are:
  - Access ISPs: They provide internet access to end users or customers, usually through a modem, cable, DSL, fiber, wireless, or satellite connection. Examples of access ISPs are Comcast, AT&T, Verizon, and Spectrum.
  - Hosting ISPs: They provide web hosting services, such as storing and serving web pages, email, databases, and applications. Examples of hosting ISPs are GoDaddy, Bluehost, HostGator, and Amazon Web Services.
  - Transit ISPs: They provide network access to other ISPs, usually by charging a fee based on the amount of traffic they carry. Examples of transit ISPs are Level 3, Cogent, GTT, and NTT.
  - Virtual ISPs: They provide internet services by reselling or leasing the network infrastructure and bandwidth of another ISP. Examples of virtual ISPs are NetZero, EarthLink, and AOL.
  - Wireless ISPs: They provide internet access using wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, WiMAX, LTE, or 5G. Examples of wireless ISPs are T-Mobile, Sprint, and MetroPCS.
- ISPs are connected to each other through network access points (NAPs), internet exchange points (IXPs), or peering agreements. These are locations where ISPs exchange traffic and data with each other, either for free or for a fee.
- ISPs are regulated by different laws and policies in different countries and regions. Some of the issues that affect ISPs are net neutrality, privacy, censorship, security, and taxation.
