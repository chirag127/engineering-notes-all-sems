#### Goals and applications of networks and protocols

A network is a system of two or more devices that are connected to each other and can communicate and share resources. A protocol is a set of rules and standards that define how devices on a network exchange information and cooperate.

Some of the main goals and applications of networks and protocols are:

- **Resource sharing**: Networks allow devices to share hardware and software resources, such as printers, scanners, storage, applications, etc. This can reduce the cost and increase the efficiency of using these resources. For example, a group of office workers can share a common printer instead of having individual printers for each computer  .
- **High reliability**: Networks provide multiple sources of supply and backup for data and services. This can improve the availability and fault-tolerance of the network. For example, if one server fails, another server can take over its tasks and prevent data loss or service interruption  .
- **Greater flexibility**: Networks enable devices to connect and communicate with each other regardless of their physical location and type. This can increase the mobility and scalability of the network. For example, a laptop can connect to the internet via a wireless network and access data and services from anywhere  .
- **Increased productivity**: Networks facilitate data access and collaboration among users and devices. This can enhance the speed and quality of work and decision making. For example, a team of workers can share and edit documents online and communicate via email or video conferencing  .
- **Connection**: Protocols establish and maintain stable connections between different devices on the same network. This can ensure the proper delivery and reception of data and services. For example, the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) ensures that data packets are sent and received reliably and in order.
- **Link aggregation**: Protocols allow devices to combine multiple network connections into one link between two devices. This can improve the bandwidth and redundancy of the network. For example, the Link Aggregation Control Protocol (LACP) enables devices to bundle several physical links into a single logical link.
- **Security**: Protocols provide mechanisms for protecting data and devices from unauthorized access and attacks. This can ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the network. For example, the Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) protocol encrypts and authenticates data exchanged over the internet.
- **Routing**: Protocols determine the best path for data to travel from one device to another on a network. This can optimize the performance and efficiency of the network. For example, the Open Shortest Path First (OSPF) protocol calculates the shortest and least congested route for data packets.
- **Synchronization**: Protocols coordinate the timing and order of data transmission and reception among devices on a network. This can prevent data loss and corruption due to delays or collisions. For example, the Network Time Protocol (NTP) synchronizes the clocks of devices on a network to a common reference time.
