## Unit 2 - Real Time Scheduling
Real-time scheduling is a method of scheduling processes in computer systems that are event-driven and time-sensitive. It is used to guarantee that a process will be completed within a specified amount of time. The goal of real-time scheduling is to ensure that deadlines are met, and that the system remains responsive and predictable. Real-time scheduling algorithms are designed to prioritize processes based on their time constraints, and to allocate resources such as CPU time, memory, and I/O bandwidth to ensure that deadlines are met. Some common real-time scheduling algorithms include Earliest Deadline First (EDF), Rate Monotonic (RM), and Deadline Monotonic (DM).
