### Risk Management in spm

1. Identify Risks: The first step is to identify the potential risks in the project. Some common risks include delays in schedules, cost overruns, lack of resources, scope creep, quality issues, etc. Brainstorm with the project team and stakeholders to list down all possible risks.

2. Assess Risks: Next, assess the likelihood and impact of the identified risks. Assign a risk rating to each risk based on these two factors. Higher rating implies higher priority risks that need attention.

3. Mitigate Risks: Now, determine risk response plans to mitigate the high priority risks. The options include avoiding the risk, reducing the risk impact, sharing the risk, or accepting the risk. Choose appropriate mitigation strategies based on the nature of the risk and project objectives.

4. Monitor & Control: Closely monitor the key risks and trigger the mitigation plans if required. Also, keep monitoring for new risks and update risk profiles periodically. Risk management is an ongoing process throughout the project lifecycle.

The above points cover the key steps in risk management for a project. A proactive approach to anticipate and address risks can help minimize project failures and achieve objectives successfully. Regular risk review meetings and reports help keep the project team and stakeholders informed of any risk changes.
