### Setting Objectives in SPM

1. Clearly define the objectives: The objectives should be clearly defined in specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound terms. Vague objectives like 'increase sales' or 'improve processes' are not useful. Instead, define objectives such as 'increase sales of product X by 25% in 6 months' or 'reduce production defects by 50% in 3 months'.

2. Align objectives with organizational goals: The objectives must align with the broader goals and strategies of the organization. There should be a clear linkage between team or individual objectives and organizational goals. Objectives that are misaligned lead to the effort being spent in ineffective directions.

3. Focus on outcomes, not activities: The objectives must focus on outcomes and results, not activities or tasks. The outcomes are the end results that the activities and tasks are expected to achieve. For example, the objective should be 'launch new product' and not 'conduct market research for new product'. The activities and tasks can be planned once the outcomes are clear.

4. Keep the number of objectives limited: It is better to have a limited set of clear and focused objectives rather than a long list of vague and disparate objectives. As a general rule, a team or individual should not have more than 3-5 objectives at a time. This makes it easier to focus efforts and track progress.

5. Set deadlines and metrics: Each objective must have clearly defined deadlines and metrics to track progress. The deadlines specify when the objective needs to be achieved. The metrics are parameters that can be measured to monitor how close the objective is to being achieved. Measuring metrics periodically helps to take any corrective actions required and meet the objectives within deadlines.
