Ethical and professional concerns in software project management (SPM) are related to the moral principles and values that guide the behavior and decisions of software project managers and their teams. Ethics are important for SPM because they affect the quality, reliability, and trustworthiness of software products and services, as well as the relationships and reputation of software organizations and professionals. Some of the main ethical and professional concerns in SPM are:

- Honesty: Software project managers should be truthful and accurate in their communication, reporting, and documentation of software projects. They should not misrepresent, exaggerate, or falsify information or data. They should also acknowledge and correct any errors or mistakes promptly and transparently.
- Transparency: Software project managers should disclose and share relevant information and data with their stakeholders, such as clients, users, sponsors, team members, and senior management. They should not conceal, withhold, or manipulate information or data for personal or organizational gain. They should also respect the confidentiality and privacy of sensitive information and data, and protect them from unauthorized access or use.
- Accountability: Software project managers should take responsibility and ownership for their actions and decisions, and their consequences and impacts on software projects and stakeholders. They should also hold their team members and other parties accountable for their actions and decisions, and ensure that they comply with the agreed standards, policies, and regulations. They should also report and address any ethical violations or misconduct promptly and appropriately.
- Obedience to the law: Software project managers should abide by the applicable laws and regulations that govern software projects and their domains, such as intellectual property rights, data protection, security, privacy, and quality assurance. They should also respect the legal and contractual obligations and commitments that they have with their stakeholders, and avoid any conflicts of interest or unethical practices that may compromise their integrity or professionalism.
- Objectivity: Software project managers should make decisions and judgments based on facts, evidence, and logic, rather than on personal opinions, emotions, biases, or prejudices. They should also avoid any undue influence or pressure from internal or external sources that may affect their objectivity or impartiality. They should also seek feedback and input from diverse and qualified sources, and consider multiple perspectives and alternatives before making decisions or judgments.
- Respect: Software project managers should treat their stakeholders, such as clients, users, sponsors, team members, and senior management, with dignity, courtesy, and fairness. They should also respect the diversity, culture, values, and opinions of their stakeholders, and avoid any discrimination, harassment, or abuse. They should also respect the professional competence and autonomy of their team members, and support their development and growth.
- Integrity: Software project managers should adhere to the highest standards of ethical and professional conduct, and act in a manner that is consistent with their values and principles. They should also demonstrate honesty, transparency, accountability, obedience to the law, objectivity, and respect in their actions and decisions, and encourage and promote the same in their team members and other parties. They should also avoid any actions or decisions that may compromise their integrity or professionalism, or harm the reputation or interests of their stakeholders or the software profession.
