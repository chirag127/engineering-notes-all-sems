Contract management in software project management (SPM) is the process of managing the contracts between a software development organization and its clients or suppliers. Contract management involves the following stages:

- Requirements analysis: This stage involves defining the needs and expectations of the client or supplier, and the scope and specifications of the software project. The requirements document should state the mandatory and desirable features, the deadlines, the standards, and the compatibility issues of the software project. The requirements document is sometimes called an operational requirement or OR.
- Contract negotiation: This stage involves selecting the potential suppliers or clients, inviting them to submit proposals, evaluating the proposals, and negotiating the terms and conditions of the contract. The contract should specify the type of contract, the price, the payment schedule, the deliverables, the quality criteria, the change management process, the risk management plan, the dispute resolution mechanism, and the termination clauses of the software project. The type of contract can vary depending on the nature and complexity of the software project, and can include fixed price, cost reimbursable, time and materials, or unit price contracts.
- Contract execution: This stage involves monitoring and controlling the performance of the contract, ensuring that the deliverables are delivered on time and meet the quality standards, managing the changes and risks, resolving the issues and disputes, and reporting the progress and status of the contract. The contract execution stage requires effective communication and coordination between the parties, and the use of tools and techniques such as supplier performance management (SPM), earned value management (EVM), and key performance indicators (KPIs).
- Contract closure: This stage involves completing and accepting the deliverables, verifying and settling the payments, releasing the resources, evaluating the lessons learned, and terminating the contract. The contract closure stage requires formal documentation and sign-off from the parties, and the archiving of the contract records and documents.
