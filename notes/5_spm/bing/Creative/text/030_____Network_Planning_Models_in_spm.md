### Network Planning Models in SPM

- Network planning models are used to plan and manage software projects by using graphical representations of activities and events.
- They help to visualize the sequence, duration, order, and dependencies of tasks necessary to complete the project.
- They also help to estimate the project cost, time, and resources, and to monitor and control the project progress and performance.
- There are two main types of network planning models: activity-on-node (AON) and activity-on-arrow (AOA).
- In AON, activities are represented as nodes (boxes) and the lines between nodes represent the dependencies. The nodes can also show the activity name, duration, start and finish times, and other information.
- In AOA, activities are represented as links (arrowed lines) and the nodes (circles) represent the events of activities starting and finishing. The links can also show the activity name, duration, and other information.
- AON is more commonly used than AOA, as it is easier to draw and modify, and can handle complex dependencies and constraints better.
- Some examples of network planning models are critical path method (CPM), program evaluation and review technique (PERT), and precedence diagramming method (PDM).
- CPM is a deterministic model that assumes that the activity durations are fixed and known. It identifies the critical path, which is the longest path of activities in the network, and determines the minimum project duration and the slack time of each activity.
- PERT is a probabilistic model that accounts for the uncertainty and variability of activity durations. It uses three estimates of activity durations: optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic. It calculates the expected duration and variance of each activity and the project, and determines the probability of completing the project within a given time.
- PDM is a generalization of CPM and PERT that allows different types of dependencies and constraints among activities, such as finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish, and start-to-finish. It also allows the use of lags and leads to specify the time gaps or overlaps between activities.
