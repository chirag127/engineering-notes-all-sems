### Leadership in SPM

- SPM stands for Strategic Portfolio Management, which describes the processes and tools that businesses may use to align available resources to meet strategic goals.
- Leadership in SPM refers to the art and science of guiding a team towards successful project completion, while ensuring that the projects, programs, products or initiatives support the desired business outcomes and drive the most value.
- Some of the benefits of leadership in SPM are:
  - Improved efficiency: Leaders can identify and prioritize profitable investments, while avoiding wasting resources on those that are less likely to succeed or align with the organization's goals.
  - Cohesion of multiple disciplines: Leaders can integrate different methodologies and structures, such as program portfolio management, agile financial management, strategy management, and integrated IT portfolio analysis, to achieve strategic results.
  - Enhanced communication: Leaders can establish and maintain an effective channel of communication with stakeholders, ensuring that they understand what needs to be done and how it contributes to the business goals.
- Some of the roles and responsibilities of a leader in SPM are:
  - Planning, scheduling, monitoring, and coordination of project activities
  - Building, maintaining, and managing projects, from initiation to closure
  - Hiring, training, motivating, and evaluating project team members
  - Managing the project budget, scope, quality, and risks
  - Detecting and resolving issues and problems that arise during the project
  - Releasing the finished product and evaluating its performance
- Some of the skills that a leader in SPM needs are:
  - Technical skills: Leaders need to have a solid understanding of the tools, techniques, and processes involved in project management, as well as the specific domain knowledge of the project.
  - Interpersonal skills: Leaders need to have the ability to communicate effectively, build trust, influence others, resolve conflicts, and collaborate with diverse stakeholders.
  - Strategic skills: Leaders need to have the ability to align project objectives with business goals, analyze the external and internal environment, identify opportunities and threats, and make informed decisions.
  - Ethical skills: Leaders need to have the ability to act with fairness, respect, honesty, and responsibility, and uphold the values and standards of the project management profession.
