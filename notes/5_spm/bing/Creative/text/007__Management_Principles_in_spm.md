### Management Principles in SPM

SPM stands for either Strategic Portfolio Management or Software Project Management, depending on the context. Both are related to managing projects and resources effectively and efficiently. Here are some of the common principles of SPM in both domains:

- Define and document requirements: This principle involves identifying and specifying the scope, objectives, deliverables, and constraints of a project or a portfolio of projects. Requirements should be clear, measurable, and agreed upon by all stakeholders. 
- Manage changes effectively: This principle involves anticipating, assessing, and controlling the impact of changes on a project or a portfolio of projects. Changes can be caused by internal or external factors, such as customer feedback, market conditions, technical issues, or resource availability. Changes should be communicated, evaluated, and approved in a timely and transparent manner. 
- Prioritize projects: This principle involves ranking and selecting projects or initiatives based on their strategic alignment, value, risk, and resource requirements. Prioritization helps to allocate resources optimally and to focus on the most important and feasible opportunities.  
- Maintain quality: This principle involves ensuring that the project or portfolio outcomes meet or exceed the expectations of the customers and stakeholders. Quality can be measured by various criteria, such as functionality, reliability, usability, performance, security, and compliance. Quality should be planned, monitored, and controlled throughout the project or portfolio lifecycle. 
- Utilize automation: This principle involves using technology and tools to automate and streamline the project or portfolio management processes, such as data collection, analysis, reporting, communication, and decision-making. Automation can help to reduce errors, save time, increase efficiency, and improve consistency. 
- Establish communication protocols: This principle involves defining and following the methods, channels, frequency, and content of communication among the project or portfolio team members and stakeholders. Communication should be clear, concise, and relevant, and should facilitate collaboration, feedback, and learning. 
- Manage risk: This principle involves identifying, analyzing, and mitigating the potential threats and uncertainties that may affect the project or portfolio objectives, scope, schedule, cost, or quality. Risk management should be proactive, systematic, and continuous, and should involve contingency planning and risk response strategies.  
- Utilize a systematic approach: This principle involves applying a structured and disciplined methodology to the project or portfolio management process, such as the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK), the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe), or the Scientific Police Management (SPM). A systematic approach can help to ensure alignment, consistency, transparency, and accountability among the project or portfolio stakeholders.
