## Unit - 3 - Activity Planning and Risk Management

### Activity Planning

- Activity planning is the process of identifying and organizing the tasks and resources needed to complete a software project within the given constraints of time, budget and quality.
- Activity planning involves the following steps:
  - Define the project scope and objectives
  - Break down the project into manageable work packages or activities
  - Estimate the effort, duration and cost of each activity
  - Identify the dependencies and constraints among the activities
  - Create a project schedule or timeline that shows the start and end dates of each activity and the milestones or deliverables
  - Allocate and assign the resources (human, material, equipment, etc.) to each activity
  - Monitor and control the progress and performance of the activities and make adjustments as needed
- Activity planning can be done using various tools and techniques, such as:
  - Work breakdown structure (WBS): a hierarchical decomposition of the project scope into smaller and more manageable work packages or activities
  - Gantt chart: a graphical representation of the project schedule that shows the start and end dates of each activity and the dependencies among them
  - Network diagram: a graphical representation of the project schedule that shows the logical sequence and interrelationships of the activities and the critical path
  - Resource histogram: a graphical representation of the resource allocation and utilization over time
  - Earned value management (EVM): a technique that measures the project progress and performance by comparing the actual work done and the actual cost incurred with the planned work and the planned cost

### Risk Management

- Risk management is the process of identifying, analyzing, prioritizing, mitigating and monitoring the uncertainties and threats that may affect the project objectives, scope, schedule, cost, quality and stakeholders.
- Risk management involves the following steps:
  - Identify the potential risks that may affect the project positively or negatively
  - Analyze the probability and impact of each risk and assign a risk score or rating
  - Prioritize the risks based on their risk score or rating and classify them into high, medium and low categories
  - Mitigate the risks by developing strategies and actions to reduce or eliminate their probability and impact or to exploit or enhance their opportunities
  - Monitor the risks by tracking their status and occurrence and evaluating the effectiveness of the mitigation strategies and actions
  - Communicate the risks and their status to the relevant stakeholders and update the risk register or log
- Risk management can be done using various tools and techniques, such as:
  - Risk register or log: a document that records the identified risks, their analysis, prioritization, mitigation and monitoring
  - Risk matrix: a graphical representation of the risks that shows their probability and impact on a two-dimensional grid
  - Risk response plan: a document that describes the strategies and actions to deal with each risk and the roles and responsibilities of the risk owners and managers
  - Risk breakdown structure (RBS): a hierarchical decomposition of the project risks into categories and subcategories
  - SWOT analysis: a technique that identifies the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats of the project or the organization
