## Unit - 4 - Project Management and Control

- Project management and control is the process of planning, organizing, directing, monitoring and controlling the activities and resources of a software project to achieve its objectives within the constraints of scope, time, cost and quality.
- Project management and control involves the following activities:
  - Defining the project scope, objectives, deliverables, stakeholders and success criteria.
  - Developing a project plan that describes the tasks, dependencies, milestones, schedule, budget, risk management, quality assurance, communication and change management strategies.
  - Executing the project plan by assigning and coordinating the work of the project team and other stakeholders, managing the project resources, resolving issues and conflicts, and ensuring the quality of the project deliverables.
  - Monitoring and controlling the project progress by measuring the performance indicators, comparing them with the baseline plan, identifying and analyzing the variances, and taking corrective and preventive actions to keep the project on track.
  - Closing the project by delivering the final product or service to the customer, obtaining the customer acceptance and feedback, conducting a project review and evaluation, documenting the lessons learned and best practices, and releasing the project resources.
- Project management and control requires the use of various tools and techniques, such as:
  - Work breakdown structure (WBS): a hierarchical decomposition of the project scope into manageable and measurable work packages and tasks.
  - Gantt chart: a graphical representation of the project schedule that shows the start and finish dates, durations, dependencies and status of the project tasks and milestones.
  - Network diagram: a graphical representation of the project logic that shows the sequence, interrelationships and dependencies of the project tasks and milestones.
  - Critical path method (CPM): a technique for determining the shortest possible duration of the project by identifying the longest sequence of dependent tasks, called the critical path, and calculating the earliest and latest start and finish dates for each task.
  - Program evaluation and review technique (PERT): a technique for estimating the duration of the project tasks by using optimistic, pessimistic and most likely estimates, and calculating the expected value and variance for each task.
  - Earned value management (EVM): a technique for measuring the project performance by comparing the actual work completed, the planned work and the budgeted cost at any point in time, and calculating the schedule variance, cost variance, schedule performance index, cost performance index and estimate at completion.
  - Risk register: a document that records the identified risks, their probability and impact, their priority and status, and their response strategies and actions.
  - Quality plan: a document that defines the quality standards, criteria, methods, tools and activities for ensuring the quality of the project deliverables and processes.
  - Communication plan: a document that defines the communication objectives, stakeholders, messages, channels, frequency, methods, tools and responsibilities for effective and efficient communication within and outside the project.
  - Change request: a formal document that describes a proposed change to the project scope, schedule, cost or quality, and its justification, impact, alternatives and approval status.
  - Change log: a document that records the change requests, their sources, dates, reasons, impacts, approvals and actions.
