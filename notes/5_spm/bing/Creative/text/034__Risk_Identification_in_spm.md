### Risk Identification in SPM

Risk identification is the process of finding and documenting the potential threats and opportunities that may affect a project's objectives, feasibility, or management plan. Risk identification is an essential and iterative step in the risk management process, as new risks may emerge or old risks may become obsolete as the project progresses. The output of risk identification is a risk register, which contains the list and characteristics of all the identified risks.

Some of the techniques that can be used for risk identification are:

- Talking about risks: This is not a formal technique, but rather a good practice that involves communicating and collaborating with all project stakeholders to identify risks in every activity and document of the project. For example, when collecting requirements, writing specifications, creating work breakdown structures, estimating resources, scheduling tasks, or budgeting costs, the project team should always look for assumptions, ambiguities, uncertainties, and dependencies that may pose a risk to the project.
- Checklists: This technique involves using a predefined list of risk categories or sources that may apply to the project, such as technical, external, organizational, or environmental factors. The project team can use the checklists to review the project scope, objectives, deliverables, assumptions, constraints, and plans, and identify any potential risks that fall under the categories. The checklists can be based on historical data, industry standards, or expert judgment.
- Document analysis: This technique involves reviewing and analyzing the project documents, such as contracts, agreements, plans, reports, specifications, or diagrams, to identify any inconsistencies, gaps, errors, or omissions that may lead to risks. The document analysis can also reveal the assumptions, constraints, dependencies, and requirements that may affect the project's performance, quality, scope, time, or cost.
- Root cause analysis: This technique involves identifying the underlying causes or factors that may contribute to the occurrence or impact of a risk. The root cause analysis can help to understand the nature and origin of a risk, and to design more effective responses or mitigation strategies. Some of the tools that can be used for root cause analysis are fishbone diagrams, 5 whys, or fault tree analysis.
- Assumptions analysis: This technique involves examining and validating the assumptions that are made during the project planning and execution, and identifying any risks that may arise if the assumptions are incorrect, invalid, or change over time. The assumptions analysis can help to reduce the uncertainty and increase the accuracy of the project estimates, plans, and decisions.
- Brainstorming: This technique involves generating and collecting ideas from a group of people, such as project team members, stakeholders, experts, or customers, to identify potential risks that may affect the project. Brainstorming can be done in a structured or unstructured way, using tools such as sticky notes, whiteboards, or online platforms. The brainstorming session should be facilitated by a moderator who can encourage participation, creativity, and diversity of opinions, and avoid criticism, judgment, or domination by some individuals.
- Interviews: This technique involves asking questions to individuals or groups of people who have relevant knowledge, experience, or insight about the project or its context, and identifying any risks that they may mention or imply. The interviews can be done in person, by phone, or online, using structured or semi-structured questionnaires, surveys, or scripts. The interviewees can be project team members, stakeholders, experts, customers, or end-users.
