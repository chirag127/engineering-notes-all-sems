### Organizational Structures in SPM

Organizational structures in SPM (Strategy Performance Management) are the ways in which an organization arranges its processes, governance, information technology, structure, people and culture to execute its strategy and achieve its objectives. Different types of organizational structures can have different impacts on the effectiveness and efficiency of strategy execution. Some of the most common organizational structures in SPM are:

- **Functional/Hierarchical**: This structure organizes the organization according to functions or divisions, such as marketing, finance, production, etc. Each function or division has a clear superior and a chain of command. This structure can facilitate specialization and coordination within functions or divisions, but it can also create silos and barriers to cross-functional communication and collaboration. Projects in this structure can either live in a subtree of the organization, temporarily borrow resources from different functions or divisions, or negotiate project activities with the responsible of the organizational functions. 

- **Matricial**: This structure combines the functional and project dimensions in a matrix, where each team member belongs to a function or division and is assigned to a project. This structure can accommodate multiple projects and leverage the expertise and resources of different functions or divisions, but it can also create conflicts and confusion due to the dual reporting lines and authority. The balance of power between the functional and project managers can vary from weak to strong, depending on the importance and priority of the projects. This structure may or may not contain a PMO (Project Management Office) for sharing resources, monitoring and control. 

- **Dedicated Team**: This structure creates a temporary structure in the organization and allocates a dedicated team for a large or significant project. This structure can enhance the focus and commitment of the team members, but it can also create groupthink and isolation from the rest of the organization. This structure also poses challenges for reallocating personnel when the project ends. 

- **RACI**: This structure is a tool for clarifying roles and responsibilities in a project or process. RACI stands for Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed. Responsible refers to the person who performs the task or activity. Accountable refers to the person who has the authority and accountability for the task or activity. Consulted refers to the person who provides input or advice for the task or activity. Informed refers to the person who needs to be notified of the outcome or result of the task or activity. This structure can improve communication and coordination among the stakeholders, but it can also create complexity and bureaucracy if not used properly. 

- **Agile**: This structure is a flexible and adaptive approach to project management that emphasizes customer value, collaboration, feedback and continuous improvement. Agile structures can vary depending on the methodology or framework used, such as Scrum, Kanban, XP, etc. Some common elements of agile structures are self-organizing and cross-functional teams, iterative and incremental delivery, frequent and transparent communication, and empirical and adaptive planning. This structure can increase customer satisfaction, quality and innovation, but it can also require a high level of trust, commitment and empowerment from the organization and the stakeholders.
