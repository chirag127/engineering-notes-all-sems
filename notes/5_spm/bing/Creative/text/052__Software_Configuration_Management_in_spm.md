### Software Configuration Management in SPM

Software configuration management (SCM) is the process of controlling and tracking changes in the software, part of the larger cross-disciplinary field of configuration management. SCM is a part of software project management (SPM) in which software projects are planned, implemented, monitored, and controlled. SCM is necessary to ensure the quality, consistency, and integrity of the software products and to accommodate changes in user requirements, policies, budgets, schedules, etc.

The main tasks in SCM process are:

- **Configuration identification**: This is the process of determining the scope and structure of the software system and identifying the configuration items (CIs) that make up the system. CIs are the software entities that are subject to change control, such as source code modules, test cases, requirements specifications, etc. Each CI should have a unique name, version number, and description that distinguish it from other CIs. Configuration identification also involves defining the relationships and dependencies among the CIs and establishing baselines that represent the approved versions of the CIs at different stages of the software development life cycle.
- **Configuration control**: This is the process of managing and documenting the changes to the CIs and ensuring that only authorized changes are made. Configuration control involves evaluating and approving change requests, implementing and testing the changes, updating the configuration records, and communicating the changes to the relevant stakeholders. Configuration control also involves resolving conflicts and merging changes from different sources.
- **Configuration status accounting**: This is the process of recording and reporting the status and history of the CIs and the changes made to them. Configuration status accounting involves maintaining configuration records that track the identification, location, ownership, and status of each CI and the baselines that contain them. Configuration status accounting also involves generating configuration reports that provide information on the current and historical state of the CIs and the changes made to them.
- **Configuration audits and reviews**: This is the process of verifying and validating that the CIs and the baselines conform to the specified requirements and standards. Configuration audits and reviews involve checking the completeness, correctness, consistency, and quality of the CIs and the baselines and identifying and resolving any discrepancies or defects. Configuration audits and reviews also involve evaluating the effectiveness and efficiency of the SCM process and identifying and implementing any improvements.

The main participants of the SCM process are:

- **SCM manager**: This is the person who is responsible for planning, coordinating, and overseeing the SCM activities and ensuring that the SCM objectives and policies are met. The SCM manager also acts as the liaison between the SCM team and the other project stakeholders and resolves any issues or conflicts related to SCM.
- **SCM team**: This is the group of people who perform the SCM tasks and maintain the SCM tools and repositories. The SCM team consists of configuration analysts, configuration controllers, configuration librarians, and configuration auditors. The SCM team works closely with the software developers, testers, and users to ensure the proper identification, control, status accounting, and auditing of the CIs and the baselines.
- **Software developers**: These are the people who create, modify, and test the software CIs and deliver them to the SCM team for configuration control. The software developers also provide feedback and suggestions on the SCM process and tools and cooperate with the SCM team and the other project stakeholders to ensure the quality and consistency of the software products.
- **Software testers**: These are the people who verify and validate the functionality, performance, and reliability of the software CIs and the baselines and report any defects or issues to the SCM team and the software developers. The software testers also provide feedback and suggestions on the SCM process and tools and cooperate with the SCM team and the other project stakeholders to ensure the quality and consistency of the software products.
- **Software users**: These are the people who use the software products and provide their requirements, expectations, and feedback to the SCM team and the software developers. The software users also participate in the configuration audits and reviews and approve the changes and baselines of the software products.

The main output of the SCM process is the software configuration management plan (SCMP) which describes the scope, objectives, policies, procedures, roles, responsibilities, tools, and standards for the SCM activities and deliverables. The SCMP is developed at the early phases of the project and may be updated or revised during the project as needed. The SCMP can follow a public standard like the IEEE 828 or an organization-specific standard.
