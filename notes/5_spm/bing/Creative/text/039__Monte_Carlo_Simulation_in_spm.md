### Monte Carlo Simulation in SPM

- Monte Carlo Simulation is a probabilistic numerical technique used to estimate the possible outcomes of an uncertain event or process that involves random variables .
- It is based on repeated random sampling and statistics to approximate the likelihood of a range of results occurring.
- It was invented by John von Neumann and Stanislaw Ulam during World War II to improve decision making under uncertain conditions .
- It is named after a well-known casino town, Monaco, since the element of chance is core to the modeling approach, similar to a game of roulette.
- Monte Carlo Simulation can be used to assess the impact of risk in many real-life scenarios, such as in artificial intelligence, stock prices, sales forecasting, project management, pricing, health, and service supply chain  .
- Monte Carlo Simulation can also provide a number of advantages over predictive models with fixed inputs, such as the ability to conduct sensitivity analysis or calculate the correlation of inputs.
- Monte Carlo Simulation works by building a model of possible results by leveraging a probability distribution, such as a uniform or normal distribution, for any variable that has inherent uncertainty.
- It then recalculates the results over and over, each time using a different set of random numbers between the minimum and maximum values.
- In a typical Monte Carlo experiment, this exercise can be repeated thousands of times to produce a large number of likely outcomes .
- Monte Carlo Simulation can also be used for long-term predictions due to their accuracy. As the number of inputs increase, the number of forecasts also grows, allowing to project outcomes farther out in time with more accuracy.
- When a Monte Carlo Simulation is complete, it yields a range of possible outcomes with the probability of each result occurring.
- Monte Carlo Simulation involves three basic steps: setting up the predictive model, identifying the dependent and independent variables, assigning probability distributions to the independent variables, and generating random numbers for each variable .
- Monte Carlo Simulation can be implemented using various tools, such as Excel, Python, R, MATLAB, or specialized software .
- Monte Carlo Simulation can be applied to different types of problems, such as estimating the value of pi, calculating the probability of rolling dice, forecasting sales, evaluating project risks, optimizing portfolios, and more  .
- Monte Carlo Simulation is a versatile and powerful method for parameters estimation and uncertainty analysis that can help in making better decisions under complex and uncertain situations .
