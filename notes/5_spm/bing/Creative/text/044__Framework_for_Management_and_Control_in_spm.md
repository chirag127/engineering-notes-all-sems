### Framework for Management and Control in SPM

- Framework for management and control in SPM refers to the set of processes, tools, and techniques that enable effective monitoring and management of software projects.
- The framework aims to ensure that the project objectives are met within the constraints of scope, time, cost, quality, and risk.
- The framework consists of four key elements: project control cycle, data collection, data visualization, and cost monitoring.

#### Project Control Cycle

- The project control cycle provides functions for monitoring and management of the project progress, performance, and issues.
- The project control cycle involves the following steps :
  - Setting checkpoints: defining the milestones, deliverables, and review points that mark the completion of project phases or activities.
  - Reporting: communicating the project status, achievements, problems, and risks to the stakeholders, such as the project steering committee or the project board.
  - Assessing progress: measuring and evaluating the project performance against the baseline plan, using metrics and indicators such as earned value, schedule variance, and budget variance.
  - Taking corrective actions: identifying and implementing the changes or adjustments needed to keep the project on track, such as revising the scope, schedule, or resources.

#### Data Collection

- Data collection refers to the process of gathering and recording the information and data related to the project activities, outputs, and outcomes.
- Data collection involves the following aspects:
  - Contents: defining the types and sources of data that are relevant and useful for the project, such as partial completion reports, risk reports, time sheets, and activity assessment sheets.
  - Methods: choosing the appropriate methods and tools for collecting and storing the data, such as surveys, interviews, observations, databases, and spreadsheets.
  - Frequency: determining the optimal frequency and timing of data collection, depending on the project needs and availability of data.

#### Data Visualization

- Data visualization refers to the process of presenting and displaying the data in a graphical or visual form, such as charts, graphs, tables, or diagrams.
- Data visualization involves the following aspects:
  - Purpose: defining the objectives and audience of the data visualization, such as informing, persuading, or educating the stakeholders, or supporting the decision making or problem solving processes.
  - Techniques: choosing the appropriate techniques and tools for creating and formatting the data visualization, such as Gantt charts, slip charts, ball charts, time lining, or dashboards.
  - Interpretation: explaining and analyzing the data visualization, highlighting the key findings, trends, patterns, or anomalies, and drawing the conclusions or recommendations.

#### Cost Monitoring

- Cost monitoring refers to the process of tracking and controlling the project costs, comparing the actual expenditures with the planned budget, and forecasting the future costs.
- Cost monitoring involves the following aspects:
  - Expenditure monitoring: framing and updating the cumulative expenditure chart, showing the actual and planned costs over time, and identifying the deviations or variances.
  - Projected future costs: estimating the remaining costs to complete the project, based on the current performance and assumptions, and adjusting the budget accordingly.
  - Earned value analysis: assigning a value to each project task, based on the planned and actual costs and the percentage of completion, and calculating the performance indicators, such as budget variance, schedule variance, cost performance index, and schedule performance index.
