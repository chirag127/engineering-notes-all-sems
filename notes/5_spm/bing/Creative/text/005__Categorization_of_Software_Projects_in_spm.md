### Categorization of Software Projects in spm

Software projects can be categorized based on different criteria, such as:

- Development model: This refers to the methodology or framework used to plan, design, develop, test, and deliver software products. Some common development models are agile, waterfall, iterative, and incremental.
- Platform: This refers to the type of device or system that the software product runs on or interacts with. Some common platforms are desktop, mobile, web-based, and embedded.
- Scope: This refers to the extent and complexity of the software product's functionality, features, and requirements. Some common categories of scope are small, medium, and large.
- User interaction: This refers to the mode of communication between the software product and its users. Some common types of user interaction are batch and interactive.
- Maturity level: This refers to the stage of development or evolution of the software product. Some common categories of maturity level are new, legacy, and mature.
- Open-source software participation: This refers to the degree of involvement or contribution of the software product to the open-source software community. Some common categories of open-source software participation are none, partial, or full.
- Delivery method: This refers to the way the software product is deployed or distributed to its users or customers. Some common delivery methods are internal, outsource, hosted, or cloud-based.
- Type: This refers to the nature or purpose of the software product. Some common types of software products are industrial and non-industrial.
- Level of technology: This refers to the level of innovation or sophistication of the technology used in the software product. Some common categories of level of technology are conventional, non-conventional, high-tech, and low investment.
- Size and scale of operations: This refers to the amount of resources, such as time, money, and personnel, involved in the software product. Some common categories of size and scale of operations are large scale, medium scale, and small scale.

These categories are not mutually exclusive or exhaustive, and they may overlap or vary depending on the context and perspective of the software project. Categorizing software projects can help in planning, managing, and evaluating them effectively.
