### Communication Plans in SPM

- SPM stands for Strategic Performance Management, which is a multistep process that guides an organization's leadership in designing and revising a system of strategic performance management.
- Communication is a critical component of the development and implementation of a strategic performance system, as it helps to engage and collaborate with internal and external stakeholders.
- A communication plan is an outline of how to communicate important, ongoing project information to key stakeholders, such as project team members, project manager, project sponsors, customers, contractors, etc.
- A communication plan should include the following elements:
  - The communication channels that will be used, such as email, phone, chat, video conference, etc.
  - The purpose and frequency of each communication channel, such as daily updates, weekly reports, monthly reviews, etc.
  - The roles and responsibilities of each communication channel, such as who will send, receive, and respond to each message.
  - The communication guidelines and expectations, such as tone, style, format, and etiquette of each communication channel.
- A communication plan should not include the following elements:
  - The specific content or details of each communication message, such as what to say, how to say it, etc. This should be determined by the sender and receiver of each message, based on the context and purpose of the communication.
  - The communication tools or platforms that will be used, such as Asana, Slack, Zoom, etc. This should be decided by the project team and stakeholders, based on their preferences and availability.
  - The communication feedback or evaluation, such as how to measure the effectiveness, quality, and satisfaction of each communication channel. This should be done periodically by the project team and stakeholders, based on the project goals and outcomes.
