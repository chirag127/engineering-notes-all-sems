### Network Planning Models in spm

- Network planning models are models that use graphical representations of activities and events to visualize the sequence of tasks necessary to complete the project.
- They are used to identify the duration, order of activities, and dependencies among tasks.
- They help to plan, schedule, monitor, and control the project.
- There are three main types of network planning models in spm: CPM (Critical Path Method), PERT (Program Evaluation Review Technique), and precedence networks.
- CPM and PERT use an activity-on-arrow approach, where activities are drawn as arrows joining circles or nodes, which show the start and completion time of the activities.
- Precedence networks use an activity-on-node approach, where activities are drawn as nodes or boxes, and the lines between nodes represent the dependencies.
- CPM is a deterministic method that assumes fixed activity durations and focuses on finding the critical path, which is the longest sequence of activities that determines the minimum project duration.
- PERT is a probabilistic method that accounts for uncertainty in activity durations and uses three estimates: optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic. It calculates the expected duration and variance of each activity and the project.
- Precedence networks are a variation of CPM that allow more flexibility in defining the dependencies and the logic of the project. They use four types of precedence relationships: finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish, and start-to-finish.
