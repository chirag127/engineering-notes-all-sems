## Unit - 2 - Project Life Cycle and Effort Estimation

- A project life cycle is a series of phases that a project goes through from its initiation to its closure.
- The phases are sequential and usually overlapping, depending on the project management approach used.
- The project life cycle provides a framework for managing the project and its deliverables.
- The project life cycle can be divided into four main phases: initiation, planning, execution, and closure.

### Initiation Phase

- The initiation phase is the first phase of the project life cycle, where the project idea is conceived and evaluated for its feasibility, scope, and objectives.
- The initiation phase involves the following activities:
  - Identifying the project sponsor, who is the person or organization that provides the resources and authority for the project.
  - Defining the project charter, which is a document that formally authorizes the project and outlines its purpose, scope, objectives, constraints, assumptions, risks, and stakeholders.
  - Conducting a feasibility study, which is an analysis of the technical, economic, social, legal, and environmental aspects of the project to determine its viability and benefits.
  - Establishing the project team, which is a group of people with different skills and roles who work together to achieve the project objectives.
  - Developing the project vision and mission, which are statements that describe the desired outcome and purpose of the project.

### Planning Phase

- The planning phase is the second phase of the project life cycle, where the project scope, schedule, budget, quality, resources, risks, and communication are planned and documented.
- The planning phase involves the following activities:
  - Defining the project scope, which is the extent of the work that needs to be done to deliver the project outcomes and benefits.
  - Creating the work breakdown structure (WBS), which is a hierarchical decomposition of the project scope into manageable and measurable work packages and tasks.
  - Developing the project schedule, which is a timeline that shows the start and finish dates, durations, dependencies, and milestones of the project activities.
  - Estimating the project cost, which is the amount of money that will be spent to complete the project.
  - Allocating the project resources, which are the people, equipment, materials, and facilities that are needed to perform the project activities.
  - Planning the project quality, which is the degree to which the project deliverables meet the expectations and requirements of the stakeholders.
  - Identifying and analyzing the project risks, which are uncertain events or conditions that may have a positive or negative impact on the project objectives.
  - Developing the project communication plan, which is a document that describes the information needs, methods, frequency, and responsibilities of the project stakeholders.

### Execution Phase

- The execution phase is the third phase of the project life cycle, where the project deliverables are produced and verified according to the project plan.
- The execution phase involves the following activities:
  - Performing the project activities, which are the actions that are carried out to complete the project tasks and work packages.
  - Monitoring and controlling the project performance, which is the process of measuring, comparing, and adjusting the project progress, quality, cost, schedule, and scope against the project plan.
  - Managing the project changes, which are modifications to the project scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, or risks that may occur during the project execution.
  - Implementing the project quality assurance, which is the application of standards, processes, and procedures to ensure that the project deliverables meet the quality requirements.
  - Communicating the project status, which is the process of reporting and sharing the project information, issues, and achievements with the project stakeholders.

### Closure Phase

- The closure phase is the fourth and final phase of the project life cycle, where the project is formally completed and handed over to the customer or sponsor.
- The closure phase involves the following activities:
  - Delivering the project deliverables, which are the products, services, or results that are produced by the project and accepted by the customer or sponsor.
  - Closing the project contracts, which are the agreements that are made between the project and the external parties, such as suppliers, vendors, or contractors.
  - Evaluating the project outcomes and benefits, which are the effects and impacts that the project has on the organization, the customer, and the society.
  - Conducting the project lessons learned, which are the reviews and reflections that are done to identify the project successes, failures, best practices, and improvement opportunities.
  - Celebrating the project achievements, which are the recognition and appreciation that are given to the project team and stakeholders for their contributions and efforts.
