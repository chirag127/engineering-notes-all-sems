### Network Planning Models in spm

Network planning models in software project management are models that use graphical representations of activities and events to visualize the sequence of tasks necessary to complete the project. They are used to identify the duration, order of activities, and dependencies among tasks. They also help to monitor the progress and performance of the project.

There are different types of network planning models, such as:

- **Critical Path Method (CPM)**: This method uses an "activity-on-arrow" approach, where activities are drawn as arrows joining circles or nodes, which show the start and completion time of the activities. The critical path is the longest path of activities in the network, which determines the minimum time required to finish the project. Any delay in the critical path activities will delay the project completion. CPM was developed by the Du Pont Chemical Company in 1958.
- **Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT)**: This method is similar to CPM, but it also considers the uncertainty and variability of activity durations. PERT uses three estimates for each activity: optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic. The expected duration of each activity is calculated using a weighted average of these estimates. PERT also calculates the probability of completing the project within a given time frame. PERT was developed by the US Navy in 1958 for the Polaris missile project.
- **Precedence Networks**: This method uses an "activity-on-node" approach, where activities are represented as nodes or boxes, and the lines between nodes represent the dependencies. The nodes can also show the duration and resources of each activity. Precedence networks allow more flexibility and complexity in defining the relationships among activities, such as finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish, and start-to-finish. Precedence networks are more popular and widely used in computer applications than CPM and PERT.

Some advantages of using network planning models are:

- They provide a clear and logical representation of the project scope, structure, and sequence.
- They help to identify the critical activities and the slack or float of non-critical activities, which can be used for resource allocation and risk management.
- They facilitate the estimation of project duration, cost, and quality.
- They enable the tracking and control of project progress and performance, by comparing the actual and planned values of activities and milestones.
- They support the communication and coordination among project stakeholders, by providing a common and visual reference for the project plan.

Some disadvantages of using network planning models are:

- They can be time-consuming and complex to construct and update, especially for large and dynamic projects.
- They may not capture all the details and uncertainties of the project, such as external factors, human factors, and interdependencies among activities.
- They may not reflect the actual behavior and variability of the project, such as changes in scope, resources, and priorities.
- They may not account for the multiple objectives and constraints of the project, such as quality, cost, and customer satisfaction.

Some applications of network planning models are:

- Software development projects, such as designing, coding, testing, and integrating software modules.
- Construction projects, such as building, renovating, or demolishing structures.
- Manufacturing projects, such as producing, assembling, or delivering products.
- Research and development projects, such as conducting experiments, analyzing data, or publishing results.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for network planning models are:

- CPM: Critical Path Method, Critical activities have zero slack, Critical path is the longest path.
- PERT: Program Evaluation and Review Technique, PERT uses three estimates, PERT calculates the expected duration and variance.
