### Risk Planning in spm

Risk planning is the process of identifying, prioritizing, and managing risk in software project management (spm). Risk is defined as an uncertain event or condition that if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on a project's objectives. Risk planning helps to control possible future events that may harm the overall project.

Some of the steps involved in risk planning are:

- **Risk identification**: This is the process of finding out the potential sources of risk that may affect the project. Risk identification can be done using various techniques, such as brainstorming, checklists, risk breakdown structure, assumptions analysis, expert judgment, etc. 
- **Risk analysis**: This is the process of assessing the probability and impact of each identified risk. Risk analysis can be done using qualitative or quantitative methods, such as risk matrix, probability and impact assessment, expected monetary value, decision tree analysis, etc. 
- **Risk management and control**: This is the process of developing and implementing strategies to deal with the risks. Risk management and control can be done using various techniques, such as risk avoidance, risk mitigation, risk transfer, risk acceptance, risk monitoring and review, etc. 

Risk planning is important for software project management because it helps to:

- Align the project with the business strategy and objectives
- Optimize the use of resources and budget 
- Reduce the uncertainty and variability of the project outcomes 
- Enhance the quality and performance of the project deliverables 
- Increase the stakeholder satisfaction and trust 

Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for risk planning in spm are:

- **RISK**: Remember the four steps of risk planning: **R**isk identification, **I**mpact and probability analysis, **S**trategy development, and **K**eep track of risks
- **PMBOK**: Remember the six processes of risk management according to the Project Management Body of Knowledge: **P**lan risk management, **I**dentify risks, **P**erform qualitative risk analysis, **P**erform quantitative risk analysis, **P**lan risk responses, and **M**onitor and control risks
- **SMART**: Remember the criteria for effective risk responses: **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**chievable, **R**elevant, and **T**ime-bound
