### Organizational Behavior in spm

- Organizational behavior (OB) is the systematic study and application of knowledge about how individuals and groups act within the organizations where they work.
- OB matters for your career, and successful companies tend to employ effective OB practices.
- OB is an applied behavioral science built on contributions from a number of behavioral disciplines, mainly psychology and social psychology, sociology, and anthropology.
- OB covers a broad range of topics, such as motivation, communication, managing groups and teams, conflict resolution, power and politics, decision making, leadership, organizational culture, and organizational change .
- OB can help you understand, predict, and influence the behavior of people in organizations, as well as improve your own performance and satisfaction.
- OB can also help you select the right person for the right job, which is an important aspect of software project management (spm).
- Some of the OB concepts and theories that are relevant for spm are:

  - The Big Five personality traits, which are openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism. These traits can affect how people work in teams, cope with stress, and perform tasks.
  - Maslow's hierarchy of needs, which is a theory of human motivation that proposes that people have five levels of needs: physiological, safety, social, esteem, and self-actualization. These needs can influence how people prioritize their goals, seek feedback, and pursue growth.
  - Herzberg's two-factor theory, which is a theory of job satisfaction that suggests that there are two types of factors that affect people's attitudes toward work: hygiene factors and motivators. Hygiene factors are the basic conditions that must be met to avoid dissatisfaction, such as salary, working conditions, and supervision. Motivators are the factors that can enhance satisfaction, such as recognition, achievement, and responsibility.
  - Expectancy theory, which is a theory of motivation that states that people's behavior is determined by their expectations of the outcomes and the value they place on those outcomes. According to this theory, people will be motivated to work hard if they believe that their effort will lead to good performance, that their performance will be rewarded, and that the rewards are desirable.
  - Equity theory, which is a theory of motivation that focuses on how people perceive the fairness of their treatment in relation to others. According to this theory, people will compare their inputs (such as effort, skills, and experience) and outputs (such as pay, recognition, and benefits) with those of others, and will feel either underrewarded, overrewarded, or equitably rewarded. People who feel underrewarded or overrewarded will experience inequity and will try to restore balance by changing their inputs, outputs, or referents.
  - Goal-setting theory, which is a theory of motivation that emphasizes the importance of setting specific, challenging, and attainable goals for oneself and others. According to this theory, goals can direct attention, mobilize effort, enhance persistence, and foster the development of strategies and action plans.
  - Group development stages, which are the phases that groups go through as they form, perform, and disband. The most common model of group development is Tuckman's five-stage model, which consists of forming, storming, norming, performing, and adjourning. Forming is the stage where group members get to know each other and establish roles and expectations. Storming is the stage where group members experience conflicts and disagreements over goals, tasks, and processes. Norming is the stage where group members resolve their differences and develop norms and cohesion. Performing is the stage where group members work effectively and efficiently toward their objectives. Adjourning is the stage where group members complete their tasks and terminate their relationships.
  - Group decision making techniques, which are the methods that groups can use to improve the quality and efficiency of their decisions. Some of the common techniques are brainstorming, nominal group technique, Delphi technique, and stepladder technique. Nominal group technique is a technique where group members write down their ideas individually, then share and rank them in order of preference.
