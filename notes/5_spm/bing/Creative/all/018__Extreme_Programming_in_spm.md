### Extreme Programming in spm

- Extreme Programming (XP) is an agile software development framework that aims to produce higher quality software, and higher quality of life for the development team. 
- XP is the most specific of the agile frameworks regarding appropriate engineering practices for software development. 
- XP advocates frequent releases in short development cycles, intended to improve productivity and introduce checkpoints at which new customer requirements can be adopted. 
- XP takes the beneficial elements of traditional software engineering practices to "extreme" levels. For example, code reviews are considered a beneficial practice; taken to the extreme, code can be reviewed continuously (i.e. the practice of pair programming). 
- XP is based on five values: communication, simplicity, feedback, courage, and respect. 
- XP follows five rules: planning, managing, designing, coding, and testing. 
- XP implements 12 practices: the planning game, small releases, metaphor, simple design, testing, refactoring, pair programming, collective ownership, continuous integration, 40-hour week, on-site customer, and coding standards.  

#### Advantages of XP

- XP improves customer satisfaction by delivering software that meets their needs and expectations. 
- XP reduces development costs and risks by eliminating waste and rework. 
- XP enhances team collaboration and morale by fostering a culture of trust and respect. 
- XP increases software quality and reliability by applying rigorous engineering practices and testing. 
- XP adapts to changing requirements and feedback by embracing change and learning. 

#### Disadvantages of XP

- XP may not be suitable for large, complex, or distributed projects that require more documentation and coordination.  
- XP may not be compatible with some organizational cultures or customers that prefer more formal or upfront planning and design.  
- XP may not be feasible for some technologies or domains that do not support automated testing or refactoring.  
- XP may not be easy to adopt or sustain for some teams or individuals that lack the skills, discipline, or motivation to follow the XP practices.  

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks for XP

- To remember the five values of XP, think of the acronym CSF CR: Communication, Simplicity, Feedback, Courage, Respect. 
- To remember the five rules of XP, think of the acronym PMDCT: Planning, Managing, Designing, Coding, Testing. 
- To remember the 12 practices of XP, think of the acronym SMT TRP CCC OT: Small releases, Metaphor, Testing, Refactoring, Pair programming, Collective ownership, Continuous integration, Coding standards, On-site customer, The planning game, 40-hour week, Simple design.
