### Software Configuration Management in spm

Software Configuration Management (SCM) is a process of controlling and tracking changes in the software, part of the larger cross-disciplinary field of configuration management. SCM practices include revision control and the establishment of baselines.

SCM is an essential part of software project management (SPM), which is a proper way of planning and leading software projects. SPM involves planning, implementing, monitoring, and controlling software projects, as well as managing risks, requirements, changes, and releases.

SCM consists of the following activities:

- Identification and Establishment: Identifying the configuration items (CIs) from products that compose baselines at given points in time. A baseline is a set of mutually consistent CIs, which has been formally reviewed and agreed upon, and serves as the basis of further development. Establishing relationships among CIs, creating a mechanism to manage multiple levels of control, and defining procedures for change management system.
- Version Control: Creating versions or specifications of the existing product to build new products with the help of SCM system. A version is a named state of a CI that reflects a set of changes made to it. Versions can be numbered or labeled to indicate the degree of change and the evolutionary path of the CI.
- Change Control: Controlling changes to CIs. The change control process involves submitting and evaluating a change request (CR), which is a proposal to modify or update a CI. The CR is assessed for its technical merit, potential side effects, overall impact on other CIs and system functions, and the projected cost of the change. The results of the evaluation are presented as a change report, which is used by a change control board (CCB), a person or group who makes a final decision on the status and priority of the change. The CCB can approve, reject, defer, or modify the CR. If the CR is approved, the change is implemented and verified, and the affected CIs are updated and baselined.
- Configuration Audit: Checking that the CIs and their versions are consistent and complete, and that they comply with the requirements and standards. Configuration audit can be performed at various stages of the software development life cycle, such as design, coding, testing, and deployment. Configuration audit can be divided into two types: functional configuration audit (FCA), which verifies that the functional and performance attributes of the software are achieved; and physical configuration audit (PCA), which verifies that the software conforms to the specified design and interface documentation.
- Status Accounting: Recording and reporting the status and history of the CIs and their versions, as well as the changes made to them. Status accounting provides information about the current state of the software, such as what CIs are included, what versions are used, what changes are pending, and what problems are encountered. Status accounting also provides traceability of the CIs and their versions, as well as the changes and their rationale.

Some of the benefits of SCM are:

- It improves the quality and reliability of the software by ensuring that only authorized and tested changes are incorporated into the product.
- It enhances the productivity and efficiency of the software development team by facilitating communication, coordination, and collaboration among the stakeholders.
- It reduces the cost and risk of the software project by minimizing errors, conflicts, and rework, and by enabling early detection and resolution of problems.
- It supports the maintenance and evolution of the software by providing a consistent and accurate view of the software and its changes over time.

Some of the challenges of SCM are:

- It requires a significant amount of planning, coordination, and documentation, which can be time-consuming and complex.
- It involves a trade-off between flexibility and stability, as too much control can stifle creativity and innovation, while too little control can lead to chaos and inconsistency.
- It depends on the availability and quality of the tools and techniques that support the SCM activities, such as version control systems, change management systems, configuration audit tools, and status accounting tools.

Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for SCM are:

- To remember the five activities of SCM, use the acronym VICCS: Version control, Identification and establishment, Change control, Configuration audit, and Status accounting.
- To remember the four steps of the change control process, use the acronym EIVU: Evaluate, Implement, Verify, and Update.
- To remember the two types of configuration audit, use the acronym FCP: Functional configuration audit and Physical configuration audit.
