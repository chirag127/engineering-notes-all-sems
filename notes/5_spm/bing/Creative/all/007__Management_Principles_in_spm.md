### Management Principles in SPM

SPM stands for Software Project Management, which is the discipline of planning, organizing, monitoring, and controlling software projects. SPM can also refer to Strategic Portfolio Management, which is the process of aligning investments and resources with business goals and strategies. Both SPMs share some common principles that can help managers achieve better outcomes and deliver value to stakeholders. Here are some of the management principles in SPM:

- Define and document requirements: A clear and comprehensive set of requirements is essential for any project or portfolio. Requirements should specify the scope, quality, schedule, budget, and risks of the project or portfolio, as well as the expectations and needs of the customers and users. Requirements should be validated, verified, and updated throughout the project or portfolio lifecycle. 
- Manage changes effectively: Changes are inevitable in any project or portfolio, due to changing customer needs, market conditions, technical challenges, or other factors. Changes should be managed through a formal and transparent process that involves identifying, assessing, approving, implementing, and communicating the changes. Changes should be aligned with the project or portfolio objectives and should not compromise the quality or value of the deliverables. 
- Prioritize projects: In a portfolio, not all projects are equally important or feasible. Projects should be prioritized based on their strategic alignment, expected value, risk, and resource availability. Prioritization should be done using objective and consistent criteria and should be reviewed and revised periodically. Prioritization should help managers allocate resources optimally and focus on the most valuable and viable projects. 
- Maintain quality: Quality is the degree to which a product or service meets or exceeds the customer's expectations and requirements. Quality should be planned, assured, controlled, and improved throughout the project or portfolio lifecycle. Quality should be measured using appropriate metrics and standards and should be monitored and reported regularly. Quality should be ensured by applying best practices, methodologies, tools, and techniques, as well as by conducting reviews, tests, audits, and inspections. 
- Utilize automation: Automation is the use of technology to perform tasks or processes that would otherwise require human intervention or effort. Automation can help improve the efficiency, accuracy, speed, and consistency of project or portfolio management activities, such as data collection, analysis, reporting, communication, scheduling, tracking, and delivery. Automation can also help reduce errors, costs, and risks, as well as enhance quality and value. Automation should be used where appropriate and feasible, and should not compromise the human aspects of project or portfolio management, such as creativity, collaboration, and decision-making. 
- Establish communication protocols: Communication is the exchange of information, ideas, feedback, and opinions among the project or portfolio stakeholders, such as managers, team members, customers, users, sponsors, and suppliers. Communication should be clear, timely, relevant, and consistent, and should use the appropriate channels, formats, and media. Communication should be planned, executed, and evaluated, and should follow the agreed-upon protocols, such as frequency, mode, content, and tone. Communication should facilitate the coordination, collaboration, and alignment of the project or portfolio stakeholders, as well as the management of expectations, issues, and risks. 
- Manage risk: Risk is the uncertainty or possibility of a negative or undesirable outcome or event that can affect the project or portfolio objectives, deliverables, or stakeholders. Risk should be identified, analyzed, evaluated, and prioritized, and should be addressed by implementing appropriate strategies, such as avoidance, mitigation, transfer, or acceptance. Risk should be monitored and controlled, and should be reported and communicated to the relevant stakeholders. Risk should be managed proactively and systematically, and should not be ignored or underestimated. 
- Utilize a systematic approach: A systematic approach is a structured and logical way of performing project or portfolio management activities, such as planning, executing, monitoring, controlling, and closing. A systematic approach should follow a defined and documented process, methodology, framework, or model, such as the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK), the Agile Manifesto, or the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe). A systematic approach should be customized and adapted to the specific context, characteristics, and needs of the project or portfolio, and should be flexible and responsive to changes. A systematic approach should help managers achieve the project or portfolio objectives and deliver value to the stakeholders.
