### Risk Evaluation in spm

Risk evaluation is an important part of the Software Project Management (SPM) process. It is used to identify potential risks and to assess their impact on the project. The risk evaluation process consists of four steps:

- Analyzing the project environment: This step involves understanding the context and scope of the project, the stakeholders and their expectations, the project objectives and constraints, and the external factors that may affect the project.
- Identifying risks: This step involves brainstorming, researching, and listing all the possible risks that may affect the project. Risks can be categorized into different types, such as technical, operational, organizational, financial, legal, or environmental. Some common sources of risks are:

  - Requirements: Changes, ambiguities, or conflicts in the project requirements may lead to rework, delays, or errors.
  - Technology: The use of new or unfamiliar technologies may cause technical difficulties, compatibility issues, or performance problems.
  - Resources: The availability, quality, or reliability of the project resources, such as staff, equipment, or materials, may affect the project schedule, budget, or quality.
  - Stakeholders: The expectations, communication, or involvement of the project stakeholders, such as customers, users, sponsors, or suppliers, may influence the project scope, quality, or satisfaction.
  - Environment: The external factors, such as market conditions, competitors, regulations, or natural disasters, may pose threats or opportunities for the project.

- Assigning risk ratings: This step involves estimating the likelihood and the impact of each risk on the project. Likelihood is the probability of the risk occurring, and impact is the severity of the risk consequences. Risk ratings can be expressed qualitatively (such as high, medium, or low) or quantitatively (such as percentages or numbers). A common tool for assigning risk ratings is the risk matrix, which plots the likelihood and impact of each risk on a grid. For example:

| Impact | High | Medium | Low |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | Critical | Major | Moderate |
| Medium | Major | Moderate | Minor |
| Low | Moderate | Minor | Negligible |

- Creating a risk management plan: This step involves developing strategies and actions to prevent, reduce, transfer, or accept the risks. The risk management plan should include the following elements:

  - Risk owner: The person or group who is responsible for monitoring and managing the risk.
  - Risk response: The action or decision that is taken to address the risk, such as avoiding, mitigating, transferring, or accepting the risk.
  - Risk trigger: The event or indicator that signals the occurrence or the likelihood of the risk.
  - Risk contingency: The backup plan or alternative solution that is prepared in case the risk response fails or the risk impact is higher than expected.
  - Risk reserve: The amount of time, money, or resources that is allocated to deal with the risk.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the risk evaluation in spm are:

- Remember the four steps of risk evaluation as ARIA: Analyze, Identify, Rate, and Act.
- Use the acronym RATER to remember the elements of the risk management plan: Response, Owner, Trigger, Contingency, and Reserve.
- Use the phrase "High and dry, low and wet" to remember the risk matrix: High likelihood and high impact risks are critical and dry (need immediate attention), low likelihood and low impact risks are negligible and wet (can be ignored).
