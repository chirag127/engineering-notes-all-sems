### Communication Plans in SPM

- SPM stands for Strategic Performance Management, which is a multistep process that guides an organization's leadership in designing and revising a system of strategic performance management.
- Communication is key to the success of any change initiative, such as SPM. A communication plan is an outline of how important information will be communicated to stakeholders throughout the project .
- A communication plan should include the following elements :
  - The communication channels that will be used, such as email, chat, phone, video conference, etc.
  - The purpose of each channel, such as for updates, feedback, questions, etc.
  - The frequency and timing of communication, such as daily, weekly, monthly, etc.
  - The roles and responsibilities of the project team and the project stakeholders, such as who will send, receive, and respond to communication.
  - The communication expectations and norms, such as how to address issues, escalate concerns, acknowledge receipt, etc.
- A communication plan should not include irrelevant or confidential information, such as personal details, sensitive data, or PR strategies.
- A communication plan should be aligned with the project goals, scope, and timeline, and should be updated as the project progresses.
- A communication plan should be shared and agreed upon by all the project team members and stakeholders, and should be accessible and transparent .

Here is an example of a communication plan template:

| Communication channel | Purpose | Frequency | Roles and responsibilities | Expectations and norms |
| --------------------- | ------- | --------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Email | To share formal and official project information, such as status reports, meeting agendas, action items, etc. | Weekly or as needed | Project manager: send email to project team and stakeholders Project team: read and reply to email as needed Stakeholders: read and provide feedback as needed | Use clear and concise subject lines Include relevant attachments and links Use professional and respectful tone Acknowledge receipt and follow up on action items |
| Chat | To communicate informal and quick project information, such as questions, clarifications, reminders, etc. | Daily or as needed | Project team: use chat to communicate with each other and the project manager Project manager: use chat to communicate with the project team and stakeholders Stakeholders: use chat to communicate with the project manager and the project team as needed | Use appropriate chat tool, such as Slack, Teams, etc. Use relevant chat channels and threads Use emojis and reactions to express emotions and reactions Respond in a timely manner |
| Phone | To communicate urgent and complex project information, such as issues, risks, changes, etc. | As needed | Project manager: initiate and lead phone calls with project team and stakeholders Project team: participate and contribute to phone calls as needed Stakeholders: participate and provide input to phone calls as needed | Use phone numbers or online tools, such as Zoom, Skype, etc. Schedule phone calls in advance if possible Notify participants of the agenda and the duration of the phone call Use mute and unmute functions appropriately Record and summarize phone calls if needed |
| Video conference | To communicate interactive and collaborative project information, such as brainstorming, planning, reviewing, etc. | Monthly or as needed | Project manager: initiate and facilitate video conferences with project team and stakeholders Project team: participate and collaborate in video conferences as needed Stakeholders: participate and provide feedback in video conferences as needed | Use online tools, such as Zoom, Skype, etc. Schedule video conferences in advance and send invitations and reminders Use video and audio functions appropriately Share screen and use whiteboard or other tools as needed Record and summarize video conferences if needed |
