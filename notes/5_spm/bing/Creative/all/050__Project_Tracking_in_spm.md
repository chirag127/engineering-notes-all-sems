### Project Tracking in spm

Project tracking is the process of monitoring and controlling the progress, quality, and performance of a software project. It involves collecting, analyzing, and reporting data on the project's status, issues, risks, and changes. Project tracking helps to ensure that the project meets its objectives, delivers value to the stakeholders, and stays within the budget and schedule constraints.

Some of the benefits of project tracking are:

- It provides visibility and transparency into the project's activities, deliverables, and outcomes.
- It enables timely and effective communication and collaboration among the project team, sponsors, and customers.
- It facilitates decision making and problem solving by identifying and resolving issues and risks before they escalate.
- It supports continuous improvement and learning by capturing and sharing lessons learned and best practices.
- It increases customer satisfaction and trust by demonstrating the project's value and quality.

Some of the challenges of project tracking are:

- It requires a clear and consistent definition of the project's scope, objectives, and success criteria.
- It demands a reliable and accurate data collection and reporting system that can handle the complexity and dynamics of the project.
- It involves a balance between the level of detail and the frequency of reporting, depending on the needs and preferences of the stakeholders.
- It depends on the commitment and participation of the project team and the stakeholders to provide timely and honest feedback and information.

Some of the tools and techniques for project tracking are:

- Project management software: This is a software application that helps to plan, execute, monitor, and control software projects. It can provide features such as task management, resource management, schedule management, cost management, risk management, change management, quality management, communication management, and reporting. Some examples of project management software are ServiceNow Strategic Portfolio Management (SPM) , Microsoft Project, Jira, and Trello.
- Dashboards and reports: These are graphical and textual representations of the project's data and information that provide an overview and insight into the project's status, performance, and trends. They can include metrics, indicators, charts, tables, and narratives that highlight the project's progress, issues, risks, and changes. Some examples of dashboards and reports are performance analytics , agile dashboards , and release management .
- Meetings and reviews: These are formal and informal interactions among the project team and the stakeholders that facilitate communication and collaboration, provide feedback and guidance, and enable decision making and problem solving. They can include regular meetings, status updates, stand-up meetings, retrospectives, and lessons learned sessions. Some examples of meetings and reviews are scrum program boards , scenario planning , and innovation management .
