### Communications Genres in spm

- Communications genres are the types of documents or artifacts that are used to communicate information in software project management (spm).
- Communications genres can be classified into four categories: formal, informal, internal, and external.
- Formal communications genres are those that follow a predefined structure, format, and style, and are usually intended for official or legal purposes. Examples of formal communications genres are project charter, project plan, requirements specification, design document, test plan, user manual, etc.
- Informal communications genres are those that do not follow a predefined structure, format, or style, and are usually intended for casual or personal purposes. Examples of informal communications genres are email, chat, blog, wiki, memo, note, etc.
- Internal communications genres are those that are used within the project team or the organization, and are usually intended for coordination, collaboration, or information sharing. Examples of internal communications genres are status report, meeting agenda, meeting minutes, risk register, issue log, change request, etc.
- External communications genres are those that are used outside the project team or the organization, and are usually intended for marketing, promotion, or feedback. Examples of external communications genres are press release, newsletter, brochure, flyer, survey, questionnaire, etc.
- A mnemonic to remember the four categories of communications genres is **FIRE**: Formal, Informal, Internal, External.
- A learning trick to understand the difference between formal and informal communications genres is to think of them as **suit** and **jeans**: formal communications genres are like wearing a suit, they are more professional, formal, and structured; informal communications genres are like wearing jeans, they are more casual, personal, and flexible.
- A learning trick to understand the difference between internal and external communications genres is to think of them as **home** and **away**: internal communications genres are like communicating at home, they are more familiar, comfortable, and confidential; external communications genres are like communicating away, they are more unfamiliar, challenging, and public.
