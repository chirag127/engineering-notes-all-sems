### A Parametric Productivity Model in spm

- A parametric productivity model in spm is a method of estimating the productivity of software development projects based on some parameters that affect the effort, cost, and duration of the project.
- The parameters can be derived from historical data, expert judgment, or other sources, and can be used to predict the productivity of future projects or to compare the productivity of different projects or teams.
- One example of a parametric productivity model in spm is the Constructive Cost Model (COCOMO), which was developed by Barry Boehm in 1981 and later revised in 2000.
- COCOMO uses three basic parameters to estimate the productivity of a software project: the size of the software (measured in lines of code or function points), the complexity of the software (measured by a set of cost drivers that reflect the technical and environmental factors of the project), and the capability of the software team (measured by a set of scale factors that reflect the experience, skills, and processes of the team).
- COCOMO has three levels of detail: basic, intermediate, and detailed, which differ in the number and accuracy of the parameters used. The basic level uses only the size parameter, the intermediate level uses the size and complexity parameters, and the detailed level uses the size, complexity, and capability parameters.
- COCOMO can be used to estimate the effort, cost, and duration of a software project, as well as the optimal staffing level and the reliability of the software. It can also be used to perform trade-off analysis, risk analysis, and sensitivity analysis, to evaluate the impact of different choices and scenarios on the productivity of the project.
- COCOMO is based on empirical data and statistical analysis, and it can be calibrated and validated using historical data from similar projects. It can also be adapted and customized to fit different domains, technologies, and organizations, by adjusting the parameters and coefficients of the model.
- COCOMO is one of the most widely used and accepted parametric productivity models in spm, and it has been applied to various types of software projects, such as embedded systems, web applications, and open source software. It has also been extended and modified by various researchers and practitioners, to incorporate new factors and features, such as agile methods, reuse, quality, and security.
