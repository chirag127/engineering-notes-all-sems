## Unit - 4 - Project Management and Control for the notes of the course Software Project Management in spm

- Project management and control is the process of planning, organizing, monitoring and controlling the activities and resources of a software project to achieve its objectives within the constraints of time, cost and quality.
- Project management and control involves the following steps:
  - Creating a framework for management and control: This involves defining the project scope, objectives, deliverables, milestones, tasks, roles and responsibilities, reporting structures, communication channels, risk management plan, quality assurance plan and project control cycle.
  - Collecting the data: This involves gathering information from various sources, such as team members, stakeholders, customers, documents, tools and metrics, to measure the progress and performance of the project.
  - Visualizing the progress: This involves presenting the data in a clear and concise way, such as using charts, graphs, tables, dashboards and reports, to show the status and trends of the project.
  - Cost monitoring: This involves tracking and analyzing the actual and planned costs of the project, such as using budget, variance, earned value and cash flow analysis, to identify and control the cost overruns and under-runs.
  - Earned value analysis: This involves comparing the actual work completed and the actual cost incurred with the planned work and the planned cost, such as using schedule variance, cost variance, schedule performance index and cost performance index, to evaluate the project performance and forecast the project outcome.
  - Prioritizing monitoring: This involves focusing on the most critical and important aspects of the project, such as using risk matrix, issue log, quality metrics and stakeholder analysis, to prioritize the monitoring activities and allocate the resources accordingly.
  - Getting the project back to target: This involves taking corrective and preventive actions to address the deviations and problems of the project, such as using change requests, contingency plans, re-baselining and escalation, to bring the project back on track and ensure its success.
  - Change control: This involves managing and controlling the changes that occur during the project, such as using change management plan, change control board, change log and impact analysis, to ensure that the changes are approved, documented, communicated and implemented in a systematic and consistent way.
  - Software configuration management: This involves managing and controlling the versions and variants of the software products and artifacts, such as using configuration management plan, configuration items, configuration identification, configuration status accounting and configuration audit, to ensure the integrity, traceability and quality of the software.
  - Managing contracts: This involves managing and controlling the contracts that are established between the project and the external parties, such as using contract management plan, contract types, contract terms and conditions, contract administration and contract closure, to ensure the fulfillment of the contractual obligations and the satisfaction of the contractual expectations.
- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the unit are:
  - To remember the steps of project management and control, use the acronym CVCPEPGCCM, which stands for Creating, Visualizing, Cost, Prioritizing, Earned value, Getting back, Change, Configuration and Managing contracts.
  - To remember the types of reports in formal and informal reporting, use the acronym ROAR, which stands for Regular Oral, Ad hoc and Regular written.
  - To remember the types of contract, use the acronym FCR, which stands for Fixed price, Cost reimbursable and Time and material.
  - To remember the components of earned value analysis, use the acronym PVAC, which stands for Planned value, Actual cost, Variance and Performance index.
