### Project Schedules in spm

- Project schedules are the core of the project plan. They show how the project activities will be performed, monitored and controlled in terms of time, resources and costs. 
- Project schedules are created and tracked with project scheduling software, which has key features that allow project managers to monitor the progress of tasks, resources and costs in real time. They can also assign work, link dependent tasks, view dashboards, allocate resources and more. 
- Project schedules are based on the work breakdown structure (WBS), which is a hierarchical decomposition of the project scope into manageable deliverables and activities. 
- Project schedules are guided by the schedule management plan (SMP), which is a document that details how the project schedule will be created, managed and monitored. The SMP typically includes information about the scheduling methodology, tools, processes, roles and responsibilities, reporting, change control and risk management. 
- Project schedules are developed by identifying the dependencies, durations, resources and constraints of the project activities. Dependencies are the logical relationships among activities that determine the sequence of execution. Durations are the estimates of how long each activity will take to complete. Resources are the people, equipment, materials and facilities needed to perform the activities. Constraints are the factors that limit the options for scheduling the activities, such as deadlines, availability, budget, quality and scope.  
- Project schedules are analyzed by finding the critical path, which is the longest sequence of dependent activities that determines the minimum possible duration of the project. The critical path method (CPM) is a technique that calculates the earliest and latest start and finish dates for each activity, and identifies the activities that have no slack or float, meaning they cannot be delayed without affecting the project completion date. The critical path can be used to optimize the project schedule by compressing or crashing the activities that have the least impact on cost and risk.  
- Project schedules are communicated and updated throughout the project life cycle, using various tools and techniques, such as Gantt charts, network diagrams, milestone charts, histograms, calendars, reports and dashboards. Gantt charts are graphical representations of the project schedule that show the start and finish dates, durations, dependencies and progress of the project activities. Network diagrams are graphical representations of the project schedule that show the logical relationships and dependencies among the project activities. Milestone charts are graphical representations of the project schedule that show the key deliverables and events that mark the completion of major phases or goals. Histograms are graphical representations of the project schedule that show the distribution of resources over time. Calendars are graphical representations of the project schedule that show the working days and hours, holidays and other exceptions that affect the availability of resources. Reports and dashboards are graphical or textual representations of the project schedule that show the status, performance, issues and risks of the project activities.   

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for project schedules in spm are:

- To remember the four main components of the project schedule, use the acronym DDRR: Dependencies, Durations, Resources and Constraints.
- To remember the six steps of creating a schedule management plan, use the acronym UDCRAC: Understand the project objectives, Define the scheduling methodology, Choose the scheduling tools and processes, Assign roles and responsibilities, Create the communication and reporting plan, and Apply change and risk management. 
- To remember the formula for calculating the float or slack of an activity, use the acronym LS-ES or LF-EF: Latest Start minus Earliest Start, or Latest Finish minus Earliest Finish.
