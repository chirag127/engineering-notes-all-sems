## Unit - 3 - Activity Planning and Risk Management

- Activity planning is the process of defining, sequencing, and estimating the duration and resources for the tasks or activities required to complete a project.
- Risk management is the process of identifying, analyzing, evaluating, and treating the potential threats or uncertainties that may affect the project objectives, schedule, budget, or quality.
- Both activity planning and risk management are essential for successful software project management, as they help to ensure that the project is delivered on time, within budget, and meets the customer's expectations and requirements.

### Activity Planning

- Activity planning involves the following steps:
  - Define the project scope and objectives, and decompose them into manageable work packages or deliverables.
  - Identify the tasks or activities needed to produce each deliverable, and their dependencies and constraints.
  - Estimate the effort, duration, and resources required for each activity, using various techniques such as expert judgment, analogy, parametric, or bottom-up methods.
  - Sequence the activities according to their logical order and dependencies, and create a network diagram or a Gantt chart to visualize the project schedule.
  - Identify the critical path, which is the longest sequence of activities that determines the minimum project duration, and the slack or float, which is the amount of time that an activity can be delayed without affecting the project completion date.
  - Review and refine the activity plan, and obtain approval from the project stakeholders.

### Risk Management

- Risk management involves the following steps:
  - Plan risk management, which is the procedure of defining how to perform risk management activities for a project.
  - Identify the risks, which are the potential events or conditions that may have a positive or negative impact on the project objectives, scope, schedule, budget, or quality.
  - Analyze the risks, which is the process of assessing the probability and impact of each risk, and prioritizing them according to their severity and urgency.
  - Evaluate the risks, which is the process of determining the best response strategy for each risk, based on the cost-benefit analysis and the risk appetite of the project stakeholders.
  - Treat the risks, which is the process of implementing the risk response strategy, which can be one of the following:
    - Avoid: eliminate the risk or change the project plan to avoid the risk exposure.
    - Accept: acknowledge the risk and be prepared to deal with its consequences if it occurs.
    - Reduce: take actions to reduce the probability or impact of the risk, such as testing, training, or quality control.
    - Transfer: shift the responsibility or impact of the risk to a third party, such as insurance, contract, or warranty.
  - Monitor the risks, which is the process of tracking and reviewing the risk status, triggers, and response effectiveness, and updating the risk register and plan accordingly.
