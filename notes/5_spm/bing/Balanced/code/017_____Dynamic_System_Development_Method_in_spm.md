### Dynamic System Development Method in spm

- Dynamic System Development Method (DSDM) is an agile project delivery framework, initially used as a software development method.
- It is based on the principles of rapid application development (RAD), which aims to deliver software in short iterations and with frequent user feedback .
- DSDM follows a predefined and structured lifecycle, which consists of the following phases :
  - Feasibility: This phase evaluates the viability and suitability of the project, and identifies the main requirements and risks.
  - Business Study: This phase analyzes the business needs and priorities, and defines the system architecture and prototyping plan.
  - Functional Model Iteration: This phase develops and tests the functional prototypes of the system, and incorporates user feedback.
  - Design and Build Iteration: This phase refines and enhances the functional prototypes, and ensures that they meet the quality standards and non-functional requirements.
  - Implementation: This phase deploys the system to the live environment, and provides user training and support.
  - Post-Project: This phase reviews the project outcomes and benefits, and identifies the lessons learned and best practices.
- DSDM is based on the following principles :
  - Focus on the business need: The project should deliver what the business values most, and align with the strategic goals and objectives.
  - Deliver on time: The project should adhere to the agreed time and budget constraints, and avoid scope creep and gold plating.
  - Collaborate: The project should involve active and continuous communication and cooperation among all the stakeholders, including the users, developers, managers, and sponsors.
  - Never compromise quality: The project should ensure that the system meets the agreed quality criteria and standards, and that the testing and quality assurance activities are integrated throughout the lifecycle.
  - Build incrementally from firm foundations: The project should start with a clear and stable vision and scope, and then deliver the system in small and frequent increments, with regular user feedback and validation.
  - Develop iteratively: The project should use an iterative and adaptive approach, which allows for learning and improvement, and accommodates changing requirements and expectations.
  - Communicate continuously and clearly: The project should use various methods and channels of communication, such as workshops, meetings, documents, and models, and ensure that the information is consistent, accurate, and timely.
  - Demonstrate control: The project should use effective and appropriate management and governance processes, such as planning, monitoring, reporting, and risk management, and ensure that the project is aligned with the business case and the stakeholder expectations.
