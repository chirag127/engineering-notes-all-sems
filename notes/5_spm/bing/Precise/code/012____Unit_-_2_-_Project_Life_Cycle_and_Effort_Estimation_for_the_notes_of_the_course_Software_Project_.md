## Unit - 2 - Project Life Cycle and Effort Estimation

The project life cycle is the series of phases that a project passes through from its initiation to its closure. The phases are generally sequential and are usually defined by the completion of one or more deliverables. The project life cycle provides a framework for managing any type of project within an organization.

Effort estimation is the process of predicting the amount of effort required to complete a project. This is an important aspect of project management, as it helps to determine the resources required for the project, the schedule, and the budget.

The project life cycle typically includes the following phases:

1. **Initiation:** This phase involves defining the project and determining its feasibility. This includes identifying the project sponsor, defining the project objectives, and developing a project charter.

2. **Planning:** During this phase, the project team develops a detailed project plan, including the scope, schedule, budget, and resources required for the project. This phase also involves identifying and assessing risks, and developing a risk management plan.

3. **Execution:** This phase involves implementing the project plan and carrying out the project activities. This includes managing the project team, monitoring progress, and ensuring that the project stays on track.

4. **Monitoring and Control:** During this phase, the project team monitors the project progress and makes any necessary adjustments to the project plan. This includes tracking project performance, identifying and addressing issues, and implementing corrective actions.

5. **Closure:** This phase involves completing the project and delivering the final product or service to the customer. This includes conducting a project review, documenting lessons learned, and releasing project resources.

Effort estimation can be done using a variety of techniques, including expert judgment, analogy, parametric estimation, and bottom-up estimation. The choice of technique will depend on the nature of the project and the availability of data.

In conclusion, understanding the project life cycle and effort estimation is essential for effective project management. These concepts provide a framework for planning, executing, and controlling projects, and help to ensure that projects are completed on time, within budget, and to the satisfaction of the customer.
