### Management Control in SPM

Management control refers to the process of ensuring that an organization's resources are being used effectively and efficiently in the pursuit of organizational goals. In the context of Strategic Performance Management (SPM), management control involves the use of performance measures and targets to monitor and evaluate the progress of the organization towards its strategic objectives.

Some key points to consider when implementing management control in SPM include:

1. **Alignment with strategy:** Management control systems should be designed to support the organization's overall strategy and help ensure that resources are being allocated and used in a manner that is consistent with the organization's strategic objectives.

2. **Use of performance measures:** Performance measures are a critical component of management control in SPM. These measures should be carefully selected to provide meaningful information about the organization's progress towards its strategic objectives.

3. **Setting targets:** In addition to selecting appropriate performance measures, it is important to set targets for these measures. These targets should be challenging but achievable and should be aligned with the organization's overall strategy.

4. **Monitoring and evaluation:** Regular monitoring and evaluation of performance against targets is essential for effective management control in SPM. This process should involve the collection and analysis of performance data, as well as the identification of areas where performance is falling short of expectations.

5. **Taking corrective action:** When performance falls short of expectations, it is important to take corrective action to address the underlying issues. This may involve making changes to the organization's strategy, reallocating resources, or implementing other measures to improve performance.

In summary, management control in SPM involves the use of performance measures and targets to monitor and evaluate the organization's progress towards its strategic objectives, and the taking of corrective action when necessary to ensure that the organization remains on track. This process is critical for ensuring that the organization's resources are being used effectively and efficiently in the pursuit of its goals.
