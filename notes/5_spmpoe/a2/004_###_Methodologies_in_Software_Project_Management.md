### Methodologies in Software Project Management

Some of the popular methodologies used in Software Project Management are:

1. Waterfall Model:
- Sequential design process. Progress flows steadily downwards like a waterfall.
- rigid, sequential steps - Requirements, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment.
- Next phase starts only after previous phase is complete.
- Not suitable for projects with changing requirements.
- Mnemonic: Roy Mustang Creates Ice To Defeat Enemies (Requirements, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment).

2. Agile Methodology:
- Incremental, iterative approach.
- Requirements and solutions evolve through collaboration.
- Focuses on adaptability to changing requirements.
- Uses Scrum and XP (Extreme Programming) frameworks.
- Deliver working software frequently in short time periods (2 weeks).
- Advantages: Adaptability, fast delivery, team engagement.
- Disadvantages: Difficult to estimate timelines, lack of documentation.

3. Spiral Model:
- Combines elements of prototyping and waterfall model.
- Software is developed in iterative loops (spirals). Each loop involves planning, risk analysis, engineering and evaluation.
- Start with a small set of features and additional features added in subsequent spirals.
- Appropriate for large, expensive and complicated software projects, especially with unclear requirements.
