# Software Project Management

Software Project Management involves managing the process of developing software to ensure that it is completed on time and within budget. It includes:

- Planning the project: Identifying the scope, tasks, schedule, and budget. Some key terms:

- Work breakdown structure (WBS): Breaking down the project into smaller tasks.
- Gantt chart: A bar chart showing schedule of tasks.
- Critical path: The sequence of tasks that determines the minimum duration of the project.

- Estimating costs and timeframes: This is difficult and involves considering resources, risks, and complexity. Some techniques include:

- Expert judgment: Estimates from experienced personnel.
- Analogous estimation: Comparing to similar previous projects.
- Bottom-up estimation: Summing estimates of lower-level tasks.
- Three-point estimation: Best case, most likely case, and worst case estimates.

- Scheduling and resource allocation: Assigning tasks and resources to meet deadline. Need to manage resource conflicts and track progress.

- Monitoring and control: Track progress, resolve issues, manage risks and quality, and handle changes.

- Software configuration management: Keeping track of changes to software and documentation.

Mnemonics:

- Words to remember phases: Plan, Do, Check, Act (PDCA cycle)
- Acronyms: WBS, Gantt, CPM (Critical Path Method)

Advantages: Increased likelihood of success and customer satisfaction.
Disadvantages: Time-consuming and difficult to plan accurately.
