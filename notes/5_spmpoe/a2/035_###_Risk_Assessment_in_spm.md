### Risk Assessment in spm

* Risk assessment is the process of identifying potential hazards and analyzing what could happen if something goes wrong. It allows you to determine the likelihood and consequences of adverse events.
* In spm, risk assessment is done to identify, analyze and evaluate risks that could affect the project and document findings and recommendations to improve project outcomes.
* Steps involved in risk assessment in spm:

1. Identify risks: Brainstorm and document all possible risks that could affect the project. This can be done through interviews, workshops, checklists, etc.
2. Analyze risks: Assess the likelihood and impact of each risk on project objectives. Rate the risks on a risk matrix with impact and likelihood.
3. Evaluate risks: Review and prioritize the risks. Focus on high impact and high likelihood risks.
4. Develop risk responses: Come up with actions to reduce likelihood or impact of risks. Select appropriate responses for high priority risks.
5. Monitor risks: Continuously monitor identified risks and trigger risk responses when required. Review risks and responses periodically and update risk register.

* Advantages: Helps identify and prepare for undesirable events, enables effective risk management, increases chances of project success.
* Disadvantages: Time consuming, requires expertise, assumptions and judgments involved can be incorrect.
* Examples: Natural disasters, loss of key resources, delays from suppliers, change in requirements, etc.
* Applications: All projects to ensure objectives are met within constraints.

* Mnemonics: RAID - Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies; CRAM - Cost, Revenue, Access, Market
* Learning tricks: Continue practicing by assessing risks of personal projects or case studies. Apply risk management techniques and track effectiveness.
