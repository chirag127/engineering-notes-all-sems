## Unit - 3 - Activity Planning and Risk Management

### Activity Planning
- Work Breakdown Structure (WBS): Break down the project into smaller manageable components called activities/tasks. Arrange them in a hierarchy. Can use Mnemonics like "obstacle faced plans development" where each word indicates a level in WBS.
- Activity List: List of activities with descriptions.
- Activity Attributes: Duration, start date, end date, resources, precedence relationships, etc.
- Scheduling: Arrange activities in a sequence based on precedence relationships. Calculate start and end dates. Can use diagrams like Gantt Chart, Network Diagram, etc.
- Estimation: Estimate duration and costs of activities. Can use Analogous Estimation, Parametric Estimation, Bottom-Up Estimation, etc.

### Risk Management
- Risk: Potential problem that can affect the project objectives.
- Risk Management: Process of identifying, analyzing and responding to risks.
- Risk Identification: Identify risks, their causes and impacts. Can use Brainstorming, Checklists, etc.
- Risk Analysis: Assess likelihood and impact of risks. Can use Probability Impact Matrix, Sensitivity Analysis, etc.
- Risk Response: Decide how to deal with risks. Options: Avoid, Transfer, Mitigate, Accept.
- Monitoring and Control: Track identified risks and execution of risk responses.

Advantages: Activity planning provides a systematic approach. Risk management reduces threats to project objectives.
Disadvantages: Activity planning can be time-consuming. Risk management does not eliminate all risks.
Examples: WBS for building a house. Risk of technical issues in software project.
Applications: Used in all projects to complete activities on time and achieve objectives despite risks.
