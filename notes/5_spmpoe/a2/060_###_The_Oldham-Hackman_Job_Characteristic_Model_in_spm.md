### The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model in spm

The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model proposes that employees will be more motivated if their jobs have the following core characteristics:

1. Skill Variety: The degree to which a job requires a variety of different activities and skills to complete the work. Jobs high in skill variety are more motivating as they are more interesting and challenging.
2. Task Identity: The degree to which a job involves completion of a whole, identifiable piece of work. Jobs high in task identity are more motivating as employees can see the bigger picture and feel a sense of accomplishment.
3. Task Significance: The degree to which a job has a substantial impact on the lives or work of other people. Jobs high in task significance are more motivating as employees feel their work is important and meaningful.
4. Autonomy: The degree to which a job provides freedom, independence, and discretion to the individual in scheduling work and in determining the procedures to be used in carrying it out. Jobs high in autonomy are more motivating as employees have flexibility and control over their work.
5. Feedback: The degree to which carrying out the work activities required by a job results in the individual obtaining direct and clear information about the effectiveness of his or her performance. Jobs high in feedback are more motivating as employees know how they are performing and can make improvements.

According to the model, the more these characteristics are present in a job, the more motivating and satisfying the job will be for employees. Managers should aim to design jobs with high skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, and feedback to maximize employee motivation, performance, and satisfaction.

The model provides a useful framework for examining how job characteristics impact motivation. However, it assumes employees react homogeneously to job characteristics, when in reality individual differences may moderate the relationship. The model also does not consider other factors such as pay, relationships, and working conditions that can also impact motivation.
