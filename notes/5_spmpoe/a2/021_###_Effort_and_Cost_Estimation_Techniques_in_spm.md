### Effort and Cost Estimation Techniques in spm

The following are the commonly used effort and cost estimation techniques in software project management (spm):

1. Expert Judgment: Experts who have experience in similar projects are consulted and their estimates are taken. The experts base their estimates on their intuition and experience.
Pros: Quick and inexpensive. Cons: Estimates can be biased or inaccurate.

2. Analogous Estimation: The cost/effort of a project is estimated based on actual costs/efforts of similar previous projects.
Pros: Easy and quick. Cons: Similar projects may not be available, challenging to quantify similarities and differences.

3. Bottom-up Estimation: The effort/cost is estimated by estimating individual tasks/work packages and aggregating them.
Pros: More accurate if estimates are done properly. Cons: Time-consuming, challenging to account for dependencies and interactions.

4. Three-point Estimation: Three estimates are given - optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic. The final estimate is calculated using a weighted average.
Pros: Incorporates inherent uncertainty. Cons: Biases can still affect the estimates.

5. Use Case Points: Estimates are based on counts of user inputs, outputs, inquiries, and files. Conversion factors are applied to get effort/cost estimates.
Pros: Simple and intuitive. Cons: Not very accurate, does not consider project complexity.
