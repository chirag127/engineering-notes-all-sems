### Agile Methods in Software Project Management

Agile methods in software project management focuses on iterative and incremental delivery of software. Some of the popular agile methods are:

- Scrum: Scrum is one of the most popular agile frameworks. It divides the project into short sprints of 2-4 weeks. Each sprint starts with a planning meeting where user stories are chosen to be completed in the sprint. Daily stand-up meetings are held to track progress. At the end of the sprint, a final demo is presented and feedback is gathered. Key artifacts include product backlog, sprint backlog and burn down charts.
- Kanban: Kanban focuses on visualizing work and limiting work in progress to reduce bottlenecks. Work items are represented on a Kanban board with columns for different stages. Work is pulled as capacity frees up. Key metrics tracked are throughput and work-in-progress.
- XP (Extreme Programming): XP follows practices like frequent releases, tester involvement, pair programming, continuous integration, refactoring, simplicity, feedback, courage, and communication. The goal is to produce higher quality software quickly and continuously.
- Lean: Lean focuses on delivering value to the customer by eliminating waste. Key principles are amplifying learning, deciding as late as possible, delivering as fast as possible, empowering the team, building integrity in, seeing the whole, and optimizing the whole.

Advantages of agile methods include faster delivery, better quality, and higher customer satisfaction. Disadvantages include lack of upfront planning leading to unpredictability and technical debt.

Agile methods are best suited for projects with dynamic requirements or technologies. They work best with co-located teams and require cultural changes in organizations.
