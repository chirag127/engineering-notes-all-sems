### Risk Evaluation in spm

Risk evaluation is an important step in software project management (spm). Some key points to cover under risk evaluation in spm are:

1. Identify potential risks: Some potential risks to identify are - delays in project completion, going over budget, lack of skilled resources, requirement changes, technical challenges, etc. Brainstorming and risk assessment techniques can be used to identify potential risks.

2. Assess the impact of risks: Assess the impact of each risk on project constraints like schedule, cost and scope. The risks with high impact need to be focused on. Some factors to consider for impact assessment are - likelihood of risk occurring, severity of impact if risk occurs, etc.

3. Prioritize the risks: Prioritize the risks based on their impact. The risks with highest impact should be prioritized first followed by risks with medium and low impact. This helps to focus on significant risks first. Some prioritization techniques are risk score, risk exposure, etc.

4. Prepare risk response plans: Prepare response plans to address the prioritized risks. The response can be to avoid, mitigate, transfer or accept the risk. Appropriate responses are selected based on the type and impact of risk. Response plans detail the actions to take to respond to the risk.

5. Monitor and control risks: Once response plans are implemented, constantly monitor the risks and control them. Track if risk responses are effective or require modifications. Unforeseen risks may also occur and need to be evaluated and responded to. Regular risk reviews help to have risk handling under control leading to successful project completion.

**Mnemonics:**

- RISK stands for - Recognize, Identify, Score, Know response
- Impact, Probability, Risk score (I, P, R)

**Advantages:** Helps to be prepared for challenges and handle them effectively. Increases chances of achieving project goals.
**Disadvantages:** Can be time consuming and tedious to evaluate risks in detail. Does not guarantee that all risks would be accurately identified and responded to.
