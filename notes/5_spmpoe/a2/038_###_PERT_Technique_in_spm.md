### PERT Technique in spm

The PERT (Program Evaluation and Review Technique) technique is used to estimate project completion times and evaluate risks. It incorporates uncertainty by using three time estimates for each activity:

- Optimistic time (O) - shortest possible time required to complete an activity.
- Most likely time (M) - realistic time required to complete an activity.
- Pessimistic time (P) - longest possible time required to complete an activity.

From these three estimates, the expected time (TE) and variance (VAR) for each activity can be calculated as:

TE = (O + 4M + P) / 6
VAR = (P - O) / 6

The total project duration and variance can then be estimated by summing the expected times and variances of the individual activities on the critical path.

Advantages:

- Accounts for uncertainty in activity duration estimates.
- Provides an estimate of variability in project completion time.

Disadvantages:

- Estimating three time durations for each activity increases effort.
- Optimistic and pessimistic estimates may be unrealistic.
- Only provides an approximation of the probability distribution for project completion time.

 Examples and applications:

- PERT is commonly used in project management to analyze and plan projects with a significant level of uncertainty.
- It provides a useful way to incorporate a range of possible activity durations into project schedules and analyze the resulting ranges of possible completion times and costs.
- PERT charts are a common way to visually display project schedules using the PERT technique.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- Remember OMP for Optimistic, Most Likely and Pessimistic time estimates.
- Think of PERT as providing a "range" of possible project outcomes due to uncertainty.
