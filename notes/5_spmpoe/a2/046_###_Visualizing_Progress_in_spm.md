### Visualizing Progress in spm

The following are some ways to visualize progress in spm:

1. Progress bar: A progress bar is a visual representation of progress in a process. It is essentially a bar which gets filled as the process progresses. This gives the user a quick idea of how much of the process is complete and how much is left. Progress bars can be text based using symbols or can be graphical using widths of bars. They are commonly used in installations, file transfers, downloads, etc. to show progress.

2. Percentage completion: Showing percentage completion is another simple way to represent progress. Just display the percentage of steps completed out of total steps. For example, if 30 out of 100 steps are complete, show 30%. This is easy to understand but does not give a sense of how much is left to complete. It can be used in combination with progress bars for a better representation.

3. Time elapsed and time remaining: Displaying time elapsed and estimated time remaining is also helpful in understanding progress. For lengthy processes, this helps set expectations on how much longer the process might take to complete. The estimates can get more accurate as the process progresses and more data is available. However, for unpredictable processes, time estimates may not be very accurate.

4. Status messages: Simple status messages can also help indicate progress, for e.g. "Step 1 of 10 complete", "Halfway through", "Almost done", etc. These are easy to implement but may not be as visually compelling as progress bars or percentages. They can be used in combination with other progress indicators.

Mnemonics/Tricks:
- Think of a progress bar filling up as tasks getting ticked off a checklist.
- Imagine percentage as parts of a whole and how much is complete/remaining.
- Think of time elapsed/remaining as a countdown timer going down/up.

Advantages:
- Provides feedback and visibility into progress.
- Sets expectations and reduces anxiety/uncertainty.
- Can increase patience and persistence.

Disadvantages:
- Progress can be difficult to estimate accurately, leading to unrealistic timelines or percentages.
- Progress indicators themselves can be distracting or anxiety inducing if a process is taking longer than shown.
