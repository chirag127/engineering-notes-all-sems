### Earned Value Analysis in SPM

Earned Value Analysis (EVA) is a project management technique used to measure the progress of a project by comparing the planned work with the actual completed work. It is an effective tool for monitoring project performance and predicting the final outcome of a project. EVA is widely used in the field of project management, especially in the context of project cost and schedule control.

#### Key Concepts in EVA

1. Planned Value (PV): This is the budgeted cost of the work scheduled to be done on a project. It is also referred to as the budgeted cost of work scheduled (BCWS).

2. Actual Cost (AC): This is the actual cost incurred to complete the work on a project. It is also referred to as the actual cost of work performed (ACWP).

3. Earned Value (EV): This is the value of the work completed on a project. It is also referred to as the budgeted cost of work performed (BCWP).

4. Schedule Variance (SV): This is the difference between the planned value and the earned value. A positive SV indicates that the project is ahead of schedule, while a negative SV indicates that the project is behind schedule.

5. Cost Variance (CV): This is the difference between the earned value and the actual cost. A positive CV indicates that the project is under budget, while a negative CV indicates that the project is over budget.

6. Estimate at Completion (EAC): This is the estimated cost of completing the project based on the current performance. It is calculated using the formula: EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)/(CPI x SPI), where BAC is the budget at completion, CPI is the cost performance index, and SPI is the schedule performance index.

7. Variance at Completion (VAC): This is the difference between the budget at completion and the estimate at completion. A positive VAC indicates that the project is likely to come in under budget, while a negative VAC indicates that the project is likely to go over budget.

#### Advantages of EVA

1. Provides a comprehensive picture of project performance in terms of cost and schedule.

2. Enables early identification of potential problems and allows for timely corrective action.

3. Provides a basis for forecasting the final outcome of the project.

4. Facilitates effective communication and reporting to stakeholders.

#### Disadvantages of EVA

1. Requires a significant amount of data and analysis, which can be time-consuming and expensive.

2. Assumes that the project plan is accurate and that the estimates are reliable.

3. Can be difficult to interpret and may require specialized knowledge and training.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

1. PV: Think of it as the "Planned Value" or "Planned Victory" that you hoped to achieve in the project.

2. AC: Think of it as the "Actual Cost" or "Actual Cash" that you spent to complete the project.

3. EV: Think of it as the "Earned Value" or "Earned Victory" that you achieved in the project.

4. SV: Think of it as the "Schedule Variance" or "Schedule Victory" that you achieved in the project.

5. CV: Think of it as the "Cost Variance" or "Cost Victory" that you achieved in the project.

Remembering these key concepts can help you understand and apply EVA in your project management activities.
