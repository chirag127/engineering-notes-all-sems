### Organizational Structures in SPM

Organizational structure refers to the way in which an organization is arranged to carry out its operations and achieve its goals. In project management, the organizational structure is critical in determining the success of the project. The SPM (Systems Project Management) framework proposes four organizational structures for managing projects:

1. Functional Organizational Structure
- This structure is based on the functional areas of an organization such as finance, marketing, engineering, and so on.
- In this structure, the project manager has limited authority, and the project team members report to their respective functional managers.
- This structure is suitable for projects that require specialized skills and expertise from various functional areas.

2. Projectized Organizational Structure
- This structure is designed around the project and is temporary in nature.
- The project manager has complete authority over the project team, and the team members report directly to the project manager.
- This structure is suitable for projects that require a high degree of coordination and integration among team members.

3. Matrix Organizational Structure
- This structure is a combination of the functional and projectized structures.
- In this structure, the project team members report to both the project manager and their respective functional managers.
- This structure is suitable for projects that require a balance between specialized skills and a high degree of coordination and integration among team members.

4. Composite Organizational Structure
- This structure is a combination of the projectized and matrix structures.
- In this structure, the project team members report to both the project manager and their respective functional managers, but the project manager has more authority than the functional managers.
- This structure is suitable for projects that require a balance between specialized skills and a high degree of coordination and integration among team members, with a greater emphasis on project management.

Mnemonic: FPMC (Functional, Projectized, Matrix, Composite) - to remember the four types of organizational structures in SPM.

Advantages of Organizational Structures in SPM:
- Clear lines of authority and communication
- Efficient use of resources and specialized skills
- Better coordination and integration among team members
- Improved decision-making and problem-solving
- Enhanced accountability and responsibility

Disadvantages of Organizational Structures in SPM:
- Limited flexibility and adaptability to changing project requirements
- Potential for conflicts and power struggles among team members
- Overemphasis on functional goals at the expense of project goals
- Complexity in managing multiple reporting relationships

Examples of Organizational Structures in SPM:
- Functional Organizational Structure: A construction company that has separate departments for engineering, procurement, and construction.
- Projectized Organizational Structure: A software development company that creates a project team for each software product it develops.
- Matrix Organizational Structure: A pharmaceutical company that creates a project team for each new drug it develops while still maintaining separate departments for research, manufacturing, and marketing.
- Composite Organizational Structure: A consulting company that creates a project team for each client project it undertakes while still maintaining separate departments for marketing, finance, and operations.

Applications of Organizational Structures in SPM:
- Construction projects
- Product development projects
- Research and development projects
- Consulting projects
- Marketing campaigns

Remembering the different types of organizational structures and their characteristics can be essential for project managers to choose the right structure for their project. Understanding the advantages and disadvantages of each structure can help in making informed decisions while managing a project.
