### Cost Schedules in SPM

Cost schedules in SPM refer to the relationship between the total cost of production and the level of output produced. In other words, it is a tabular representation of the cost of producing different levels of output. Cost schedules are important in the field of strategic performance management (SPM) as they help in analyzing the cost behavior of a company and making informed decisions regarding production.

There are three types of cost schedules in SPM:

1. Total Cost Schedule: The total cost schedule shows the relationship between the total cost of production and the level of output produced. It includes all costs associated with production, such as fixed costs and variable costs.

2. Average Cost Schedule: The average cost schedule shows the relationship between the average cost of production and the level of output produced. It is obtained by dividing the total cost of production by the level of output.

3. Marginal Cost Schedule: The marginal cost schedule shows the relationship between the marginal cost of production and the level of output produced. It is obtained by calculating the change in total cost due to a unit change in output.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- To remember the different types of cost schedules, you can use the acronym TAM, which stands for Total, Average, and Marginal.
- To remember the difference between average and marginal cost, you can use the phrase "average is what we've done, while marginal is what we'll do."

Advantages of cost schedules:

- Cost schedules help in analyzing the cost behavior of a company and making informed decisions regarding production.
- They help in determining the optimal level of output that will maximize profits.
- They assist in identifying cost drivers and cost-saving opportunities.

Disadvantages of cost schedules:

- Cost schedules assume that all inputs are variable or fixed, which may not necessarily be the case in reality.
- They do not account for external factors such as changes in the market or the economy.

Examples of cost schedules:

Suppose a company produces widgets and has the following cost schedules:

| Level of Output | Total Cost | Average Cost | Marginal Cost |
|----------------|------------|--------------|---------------|
| 0              | $100       | -            | -             |
| 10             | $300       | $30          | $20           |
| 20             | $500       | $25          | $10           |
| 30             | $700       | $23.33       | $8.33         |
| 40             | $900       | $22.50       | $6.67         |

From the above schedule, we can see that the total cost increases with an increase in output, while the average and marginal costs decrease initially and then increase.

Applications of cost schedules:

- Cost schedules are used in budgeting and forecasting to estimate the cost of production and plan for future investments.
- They are used in determining the price of goods and services in the market.
- They are used in making decisions regarding capacity planning and resource allocation.
