### Activities in SPM

Scanning Probe Microscopy (SPM) is a powerful tool for studying the properties of surfaces and materials on a nanoscale level. The technique involves scanning a sharp probe over a sample surface to measure various properties, such as topography, conductivity, and magnetic fields. The following are some of the common activities in SPM:

1. Contact mode: In contact mode, the probe is scanned over the sample surface while maintaining a constant force between the probe and the sample. The probe is deflected as it encounters the sample's surface, and the deflection is used to create a map of the surface topography.

2. Non-contact mode: In non-contact mode, the probe is oscillated near the sample surface, and the van der Waals forces between the probe and the sample cause the probe to vibrate. The amplitude and frequency of the vibration are used to create a map of the surface topography.

3. Tapping mode: Tapping mode is a variation of non-contact mode where the probe is oscillated at a frequency close to its resonance frequency, causing it to tap the surface. The tapping force is much smaller than in contact mode, making it suitable for imaging delicate samples.

4. Scanning tunneling microscopy (STM): STM is a type of SPM that measures the electrical conductivity of a sample surface by passing a small current through the probe and measuring the resulting voltage. STM is capable of imaging individual atoms and has been used to study electronic properties of materials.

5. Atomic force microscopy (AFM): AFM is a type of SPM that measures the forces between the probe and the sample surface. The probe is scanned over the sample surface while maintaining a constant force, and the deflection of the probe is used to create a map of the surface topography. AFM is capable of imaging individual molecules and has been used to study mechanical properties of materials.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

- To remember the different modes of SPM, you can use the acronym "CAN'T ASK FOR MORE." The first letter of each mode corresponds to a letter in the acronym: Contact, Non-contact, Tapping, STM, AFM, Kelvin probe, Force modulation, and Magnetic force microscopy.

- Another trick to remember the different modes is to associate each mode with a characteristic sound. For example, contact mode can be associated with the sound of a ball bouncing on a hard surface, non-contact mode with the sound of a tuning fork, and tapping mode with the sound of a drumstick tapping on a cymbal.

Overall, understanding the different activities in SPM is important for researchers and engineers working in fields such as materials science, nanotechnology, and surface physics. By using SPM, they can gain insights into the properties of materials at the nanoscale, leading to new discoveries and technological advancements.
