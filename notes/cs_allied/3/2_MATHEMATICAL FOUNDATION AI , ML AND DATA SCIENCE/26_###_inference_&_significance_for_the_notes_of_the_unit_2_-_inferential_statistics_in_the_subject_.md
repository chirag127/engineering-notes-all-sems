Inference and significance are important concepts in inferential statistics.

1. Definition: Inference refers to the process of drawing conclusions about a population based on sample data. Significance refers to the likelihood that the results of a statistical test are not due to chance.

2. Inference: In inference, sample data is used to make inferences about a population. This involves estimating population parameters, such as the mean or variance, based on sample statistics.

3. Significance: Significance is a measure of the likelihood that the results of a statistical test are not due to chance. Significance is typically measured using a p-value, which represents the probability of observing a test statistic as extreme or more extreme than the observed value if the null hypothesis is true.

4. Null Hypothesis: The null hypothesis is the hypothesis that the results of a statistical test are due to chance. The null hypothesis is typically tested against an alternative hypothesis, which represents the opposite of the null hypothesis.

5. p-value: The p-value is a measure of significance that represents the probability of observing a test statistic as extreme or more extreme than the observed value if the null hypothesis is true. A p-value less than a predetermined significance level, such as 0.05, indicates that the results of the test are statistically significant and the null hypothesis can be rejected.

6. Confidence Intervals: Confidence intervals are a type of inference that provide a range of values that are likely to contain the population parameter with a certain degree of confidence. Confidence intervals are calculated using sample statistics and are used to make inferences about population parameters.
