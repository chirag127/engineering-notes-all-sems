Importance Sampling

Importance sampling is a statistical technique used to estimate the expected value of a random variable. The idea behind importance sampling is to sample from a distribution that is more likely to produce values that are important for the estimate of the expected value.

In importance sampling, the target distribution is the distribution of the random variable of interest, and the proposal distribution is a distribution that is easier to sample from and that is more likely to produce values that are important for the estimate of the expected value. The idea is to use the proposal distribution to generate a sample of values, and then to weight each value by the ratio of the target distribution to the proposal distribution.

Importance sampling is used in a variety of applications, including Monte Carlo simulation, Bayesian inference, and machine learning. In Monte Carlo simulation, importance sampling is used to estimate the expected value of a random variable by generating a sample of values from a proposal distribution that is more likely to produce values that are important for the estimate of the expected value. In Bayesian inference, importance sampling is used to estimate the posterior distribution of a parameter by generating a sample of values from a proposal distribution that is more likely to produce values that are important for the estimate of the posterior distribution.
