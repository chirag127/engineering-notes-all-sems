Triangle Inequality is a mathematical theorem that states that the sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than or equal to the length of the third side. In other words, the length of one side of a triangle cannot be greater than the sum of the lengths of the other two sides. This theorem applies to both real and complex numbers and is a fundamental concept in geometry and linear algebra.

Triangle Inequality has several important implications in mathematics, including the fact that it provides a way to determine whether a set of points in a space can form a triangle. It is also used to define the concept of a metric space, which is a space in which distances between points can be measured. A metric space is defined as a space in which the triangle inequality holds for any three points in the space.

In addition to its applications in geometry and linear algebra, the triangle inequality is also used in various areas of mathematics, including optimization, topology, and differential geometry.
