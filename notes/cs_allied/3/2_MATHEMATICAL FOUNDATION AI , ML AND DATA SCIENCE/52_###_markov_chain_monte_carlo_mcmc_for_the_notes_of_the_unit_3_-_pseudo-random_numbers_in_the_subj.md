Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is a simulation-based method for estimating probability distributions.

1. Definition: Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is a simulation-based method for estimating probability distributions. MCMC generates a sequence of samples from a target distribution by using a Markov chain to explore the state space.

2. Markov Chain: A Markov chain is a sequence of random variables where the value of each variable depends only on the value of the previous variable. In MCMC, the Markov chain is used to explore the state space and generate samples from the target distribution.

3. Monte Carlo: Monte Carlo is a simulation-based method that uses random numbers to solve problems. In MCMC, Monte Carlo is used to generate a sequence of samples from the target distribution.

4. Advantages: MCMC has several advantages, including the ability to estimate probability distributions for complex models, the ability to handle high-dimensional state spaces, and the ability to handle models with intractable likelihood functions.

5. Limitations: Despite its advantages, MCMC also has several limitations, including the difficulty of choosing the appropriate Markov chain, the difficulty of ensuring convergence to the target distribution, and the difficulty of estimating the accuracy of the results.

6. Applications: MCMC is used in a wide range of applications, including Bayesian inference, machine learning, and data science. MCMC is also used in fields such as statistics, mathematics, and engineering.
