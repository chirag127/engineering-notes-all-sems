Chi-square is a statistical test used to determine if there is a significant association between two categorical variables.

1. Definition: The chi-square test is a statistical test used to determine if there is a significant association between two categorical variables. The chi-square test is based on the difference between the observed frequencies and the expected frequencies of the variables.

2. Hypothesis Testing: The chi-square test is used for hypothesis testing, where the null hypothesis is that there is no association between the two variables and the alternative hypothesis is that there is a significant association between the two variables.

3. Goodness of Fit: The chi-square test can also be used for goodness of fit, where the null hypothesis is that the observed frequencies match the expected frequencies and the alternative hypothesis is that the observed frequencies do not match the expected frequencies.

4. Degrees of Freedom: The degrees of freedom for the chi-square test are calculated as the number of categories minus 1. The degrees of freedom determine the critical value of the chi-square distribution and are used to determine the significance of the test.

5. Advantages: The chi-square test has several advantages, including the ability to determine if there is a significant association between two categorical variables, the ability to perform hypothesis testing and goodness of fit, and the ability to handle large sample sizes.

Limitations: Despite its advantages, the chi-square test also has several limitations, including the difficulty of accurately modeling complex relationships between variables, the difficulty of accurately estimating expected frequencies, and the difficulty of accurately interpreting the results of the test.
