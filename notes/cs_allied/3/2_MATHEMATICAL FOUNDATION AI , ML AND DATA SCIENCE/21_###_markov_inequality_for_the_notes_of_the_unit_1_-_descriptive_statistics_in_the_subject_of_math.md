Markov Inequality

The Markov inequality is a mathematical theorem that provides a bound on the tail of a non-negative random variable. The theorem states that for any non-negative random variable X and any positive number a, the probability that X is greater than or equal to a is less than or equal to the expected value of X divided by a.

The Markov inequality is used in a variety of applications, including probability theory, statistics, and machine learning. In machine learning, the Markov inequality is used to bound the generalization error of a model, which is the difference between the expected error of the model on unseen data and the error of the model on the training data.

The Markov inequality is also used in the analysis of algorithms, where it is used to bound the running time of an algorithm in terms of its expected input size.
