Orthogonal Vectors

Orthogonal vectors are two vectors that are perpendicular to each other. In mathematics, orthogonal vectors have a dot product of zero, meaning that the angle between the vectors is 90 degrees.

Orthogonal vectors are a fundamental concept in linear algebra, and they are used in a variety of applications, including geometry, physics, and computer graphics. In geometry, orthogonal vectors are used to describe the orientation of lines and planes. In physics, orthogonal vectors are used to describe the direction of forces and the orientation of objects. In computer graphics, orthogonal vectors are used to describe the orientation of objects in a virtual space.

Orthogonal vectors are also used in the study of eigenvectors and eigenvalues, which are important concepts in linear algebra and machine learning. Eigenvectors are vectors that are orthogonal to the eigenvalues, and they are used to describe the direction of the eigenvalues.

In conclusion, orthogonal vectors are two vectors that are perpendicular to each other and have a dot product of zero.
