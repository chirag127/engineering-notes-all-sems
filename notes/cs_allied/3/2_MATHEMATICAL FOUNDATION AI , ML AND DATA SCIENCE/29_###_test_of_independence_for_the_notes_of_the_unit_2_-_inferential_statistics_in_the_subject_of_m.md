Test of Independence

A test of independence is a statistical test used to determine if there is a relationship between two categorical variables. The goal of a test of independence is to determine if the values of one variable are independent of the values of another variable.

There are several tests of independence that can be used, including the chi-square test of independence and Fisher's exact test. The chi-square test of independence is a non-parametric test that is used to determine if there is a relationship between two categorical variables, and it is based on the chi-square distribution. Fisher's exact test is a more powerful test of independence that is used when the sample size is small, and it is based on the hypergeometric distribution.

In conclusion, a test of independence is a statistical test used to determine if there is a relationship between two categorical variables. There are several tests of independence that can be used, including the chi-square test of independence and Fisher's exact test.
