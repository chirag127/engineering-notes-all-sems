The Central Limit Theorem is a fundamental concept in probability and statistics.

1. Definition: The Central Limit Theorem states that the distribution of the sum or average of a large number of independent and identically distributed random variables approaches a normal distribution, regardless of the shape of the original distribution.

2. Key Characteristics: The key characteristics of the Central Limit Theorem include the convergence of the distribution to a normal distribution, the independence of the random variables, and the identically distributed property of the random variables.

3. Importance: The Central Limit Theorem is important because it provides a mathematical explanation for why many real-world phenomena, such as height, weight, and IQ, follow a normal distribution.

4. Applications: The Central Limit Theorem has several applications in probability and statistics, including hypothesis testing, estimation, and prediction. The Central Limit Theorem is also used in fields such as finance, engineering, and data science.

5. Limitations: Despite its importance and widespread use, the Central Limit Theorem has several limitations, including the requirement of a large sample size, the requirement of independent and identically distributed random variables, and the limited applicability to non-normal distributions.

6. Proof: The Central Limit Theorem can be proven mathematically using the law of large numbers and the characteristic function of a normal distribution.
