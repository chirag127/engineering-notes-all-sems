Unit 4: Vector Spaces

Dimension

The dimension of a vector space is a measure of the number of basis vectors required to span the space. A basis vector is a non-zero vector that can be used to generate any other vector in the space by linear combination.

In a finite-dimensional vector space, the dimension is equal to the number of basis vectors in the space. In an infinite-dimensional vector space, the dimension is not necessarily equal to the number of basis vectors, but it is still a well-defined concept.

The dimension of a vector space is a fundamental concept in linear algebra, and it is used to characterize the structure of the space. For example, the dimension of a vector space can be used to determine the rank of a matrix, which is the number of linearly independent rows or columns in the matrix.

In conclusion, the dimension of a vector space is a measure of the number of basis vectors required to span the space. In a finite-dimensional vector space, the dimension is equal to the number of basis vectors, while in an infinite-dimensional vector space, the dimension is a well-defined concept that characterizes the structure of the space.
