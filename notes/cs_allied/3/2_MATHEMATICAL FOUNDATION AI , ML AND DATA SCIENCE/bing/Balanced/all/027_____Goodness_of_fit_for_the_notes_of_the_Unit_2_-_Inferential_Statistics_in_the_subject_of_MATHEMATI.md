# Goodness of fit

- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a statistical model fits the observed data.
- It can be used to compare different models or to test hypotheses about the underlying distribution of the data.
- There are different methods to assess the goodness of fit, such as graphical methods, numerical methods, and hypothesis tests.
- Graphical methods include plotting the observed data against the expected values from the model, or using a probability plot, a Q-Q plot, or a P-P plot to compare the empirical and theoretical quantiles or probabilities.
- Numerical methods include calculating summary statistics, such as the mean, the variance, the standard deviation, the skewness, and the kurtosis, and comparing them with the values expected from the model.
- Hypothesis tests include the chi-square test, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, the Anderson-Darling test, and the Shapiro-Wilk test, which compare the observed and expected frequencies or distances and compute a test statistic and a p-value to determine the significance of the difference.
- The choice of the method depends on the type and size of the data, the assumptions and properties of the model, and the purpose and level of the analysis.
- The goodness of fit can be affected by various factors, such as outliers, measurement errors, sampling bias, model misspecification, and overfitting or underfitting.
- A good fit indicates that the model is consistent with the data and can be used for inference or prediction, while a poor fit suggests that the model is inadequate or inappropriate and should be modified or rejected.
