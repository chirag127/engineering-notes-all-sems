# Measures of Probability

- Probability is a measure of how likely an event is to occur in a random experiment.
- An event is a subset of the sample space, which is the set of all possible outcomes of the experiment.
- A probability function assigns a number between 0 and 1 to each event, such that the sum of the probabilities of all events is 1.
- There are different ways to define and calculate probability, depending on the nature of the experiment and the available information.
- Some of the common measures of probability are:

## Classical Probability

- Classical probability is based on the assumption that all outcomes are equally likely, and the probability of an event is the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes.
- For example, if a fair coin is tossed, the sample space is {H, T}, and the probability of getting heads is 1/2.
- Classical probability can be used when the experiment is simple and symmetric, and the outcomes can be counted easily.

## Relative Frequency

- Relative frequency is based on the observation of the actual outcomes of the experiment, and the probability of an event is the ratio of the number of times the event occurred to the total number of trials.
- For example, if a coin is tossed 100 times and heads comes up 60 times, the relative frequency of getting heads is 60/100 = 0.6.
- Relative frequency can be used when the experiment is complex or asymmetric, and the outcomes cannot be counted easily.

## Subjective Probability

- Subjective probability is based on the personal judgment or belief of the person who assigns the probability, and the probability of an event reflects the degree of confidence or uncertainty of the person.
- For example, if a person thinks that it will rain tomorrow with a 70% chance, the subjective probability of raining is 0.7.
- Subjective probability can be used when the experiment is uncertain or incomplete, and the outcomes cannot be observed or predicted easily.
