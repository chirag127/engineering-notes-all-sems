# Sampling & Confidence Interval

- Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of individuals or units from a population of interest for the purpose of statistical analysis.
- Sampling allows us to make inferences about the population based on the sample data, without having to measure or observe every individual or unit in the population.
- Sampling can be done using different methods, such as random sampling, stratified sampling, cluster sampling, systematic sampling, convenience sampling, etc.
- Sampling methods can be classified into two types: probability sampling and non-probability sampling.
- Probability sampling is when every individual or unit in the population has a known and non-zero chance of being selected in the sample. This ensures that the sample is representative of the population and reduces the sampling bias.
- Non-probability sampling is when the selection of individuals or units in the sample is based on some subjective criteria, such as convenience, availability, or judgment. This may introduce sampling bias and affect the validity and generalizability of the results.
- Sampling error is the difference between the sample statistic and the population parameter due to random variation in the sampling process. Sampling error can be reduced by increasing the sample size or using a more efficient sampling method.
- Confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the true value of an unknown population parameter with a certain level of confidence.
- Confidence interval is calculated from the sample data using a point estimate (such as the sample mean or proportion) and a margin of error (which depends on the sampling distribution, the sample size, and the confidence level).
- Confidence level is the probability that the confidence interval will capture the population parameter in repeated sampling. Commonly used confidence levels are 95% and 99%.
- Confidence interval can be interpreted as follows: If we repeat the sampling process many times and construct a confidence interval for each sample, then a certain percentage (equal to the confidence level) of those intervals will contain the true population parameter.
- Confidence interval can be used to assess the precision and uncertainty of the point estimate, to test hypotheses about the population parameter, and to compare the effects of different treatments or groups.
