# Transformations for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that mimics the properties of random numbers, such as uniformity and independence.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that produce pseudo-random numbers from a seed or initial value, using arithmetic operations such as modular arithmetic, linear congruence, or bitwise operations.
- PRNGs are useful for applications that require randomness, such as simulations, cryptography, games, or statistical analysis, but they are not truly random and may exhibit patterns or correlations if not designed carefully.
- There are different methods for generating pseudo-random numbers from other probability distributions, such as the inverse transform method, the rejection method, or the convolution method.
- The inverse transform method is based on the fact that if U is a uniform random variable on [0,1], then F^-1(U) is a random variable with cumulative distribution function F, where F^-1 is the inverse function of F. This method can be used to generate random variables from any distribution that has an invertible and computable CDF.
- The rejection method is based on the idea of sampling from a proposal distribution that is easy to generate from and has a similar shape to the target distribution, and then accepting or rejecting the samples based on a criterion that ensures the correct probability. This method can be used to generate random variables from any distribution that has a bounded and computable probability density function.
- The convolution method is based on the fact that the sum of independent random variables from different distributions has a distribution that is the convolution of their individual distributions. This method can be used to generate random variables from any distribution that can be expressed as a sum of simpler distributions.
