- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that simulates randomness, but are not truly random.
- Pseudo-random numbers are useful for applications that require randomness, such as cryptography, simulation, sampling, etc.
- Pseudo-random numbers should have certain properties, such as uniformity, independence, unpredictability, and reproducibility.
- One of the most common methods for generating pseudo-random numbers is the linear congruential method, which uses the following equation:

  X_n+1 = (aX_n + c) mod m

  where X_n is the n-th pseudo-random number, a is the multiplier, c is the increment, m is the modulus, and X_0 is the seed.

- The linear congruential method has a period of at most m, which means that the sequence of pseudo-random numbers will repeat after m numbers. To achieve a long period, the parameters a, c, and m should be chosen carefully.
- The quality of the pseudo-random numbers generated by the linear congruential method depends on the statistical tests that are applied to them. Some of the common tests are frequency test, runs test, autocorrelation test, etc.
- Convergence is a property that measures how close the pseudo-random numbers are to the true random numbers. Convergence can be defined in different ways, such as absolute convergence, relative convergence, or probabilistic convergence.
- Convergence can be affected by the choice of the pseudo-random number generator, the size of the sample, the distribution of the random variable, and the accuracy of the estimation method.
- One way to measure convergence is to use the mean squared error (MSE), which is the average of the squared differences between the pseudo-random numbers and the true random numbers. A smaller MSE indicates a better convergence.
- Another way to measure convergence is to use the confidence interval, which is a range of values that contains the true random number with a certain probability. A narrower confidence interval indicates a better convergence.
