# Measures of Dispersion

- Measures of dispersion are statistical tools that describe how spread out or varied a set of data is.
- They help to understand the variability or diversity of the data and to compare different data sets.
- They are also useful for identifying outliers or extreme values in the data.

## Types of Measures of Dispersion

- There are two main types of measures of dispersion: absolute and relative.
- Absolute measures of dispersion have the same units as the data, while relative measures of dispersion are dimensionless ratios or percentages.
- Some common absolute measures of dispersion are:

  - **Range**: the difference between the maximum and minimum values in the data set. It is the simplest measure of dispersion, but it is sensitive to outliers and does not reflect the distribution of the data.
  - **Variance**: the average of the squared deviations of the data values from the mean. It measures how far the data values are from the center of the data set, but it is not easy to interpret because it is in squared units.
  - **Standard deviation**: the square root of the variance. It measures how much the data values vary around the mean, and it has the same units as the data. It is the most widely used measure of dispersion, but it is also affected by outliers and skewed data.
  - **Interquartile range (IQR)**: the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1) of the data set. It measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data, and it is not influenced by outliers or skewed data.

- Some common relative measures of dispersion are:

  - **Coefficient of variation (CV)**: the ratio of the standard deviation to the mean, expressed as a percentage. It measures the variability of the data relative to the mean, and it is useful for comparing data sets with different units or scales.
  - **Coefficient of dispersion (CD)**: the ratio of the range to the mean, expressed as a percentage. It measures the spread of the data relative to the mean, and it is mostly used for count data.
  - **Index of dispersion (ID)**: the ratio of the variance to the mean. It measures the degree of clustering or dispersion of the data, and it is mostly used for nominal data.
