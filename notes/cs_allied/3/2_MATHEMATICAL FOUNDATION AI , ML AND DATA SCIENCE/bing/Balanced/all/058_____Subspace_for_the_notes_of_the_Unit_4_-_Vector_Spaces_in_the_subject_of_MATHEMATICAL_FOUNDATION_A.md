# Subspace

- A subspace is a subset of a vector space that is also a vector space itself.
- A subspace must satisfy three conditions:
  - It must contain the zero vector.
  - It must be closed under scalar multiplication, meaning that if a vector is in the subspace, then any scalar multiple of that vector is also in the subspace.
  - It must be closed under vector addition, meaning that if two vectors are in the subspace, then their sum is also in the subspace.
- Examples of subspaces are:
  - The zero subspace, which contains only the zero vector.
  - The entire vector space, which is a subspace of itself.
  - Any line or plane that passes through the origin in R^3, which can be defined by one or two linearly independent vectors.
  - The span of a set of vectors, which is the set of all linear combinations of those vectors.
  - The null space of a matrix, which is the set of all vectors that satisfy the equation Ax = 0, where A is the matrix and x is the vector.
  - The column space of a matrix, which is the span of the columns of the matrix.
  - The row space of a matrix, which is the span of the rows of the matrix.
