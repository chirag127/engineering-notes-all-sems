### Sampling and Confidence Interval

- Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of the population for analysis and inference.
- Sampling can be done in different ways, such as random sampling, stratified sampling, cluster sampling, etc.
- Sampling can reduce the cost and time of data collection and analysis, but it can also introduce sampling error and bias.
- Sampling error is the difference between the sample statistic and the population parameter due to random variation.
- Sampling bias is the systematic deviation of the sample from the population due to non-random selection or non-response.
- To measure the uncertainty of the sample statistic, we can use the concept of confidence interval.
- A confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the population parameter with a certain level of confidence.
- A confidence interval can be calculated using the formula:

```
CI = sample statistic ± margin of error
```

- The margin of error depends on the sampling distribution of the sample statistic, the sample size, and the confidence level.
- The sampling distribution of the sample statistic is the distribution of all possible values of the sample statistic from repeated sampling of the same size from the population.
- The sampling distribution of the sample statistic can be approximated by the central limit theorem, which states that if the sample size is large enough, the sampling distribution of the sample mean (or proportion) is approximately normal, regardless of the shape of the population distribution.
- The sample size determines the precision of the sample statistic. The larger the sample size, the smaller the margin of error and the narrower the confidence interval.
- The confidence level determines the probability of the confidence interval containing the population parameter. The higher the confidence level, the larger the margin of error and the wider the confidence interval.
- A common confidence level is 95%, which means that 95% of the confidence intervals constructed from repeated sampling of the same size from the population will contain the population parameter.
- A confidence interval can be interpreted as follows: We are 95% confident that the population parameter is between the lower and upper bounds of the confidence interval.
