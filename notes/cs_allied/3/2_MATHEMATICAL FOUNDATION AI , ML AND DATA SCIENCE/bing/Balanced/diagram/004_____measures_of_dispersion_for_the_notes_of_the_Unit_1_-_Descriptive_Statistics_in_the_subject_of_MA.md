### Measures of Dispersion

- Measures of dispersion are numerical values that indicate the variability or spread of a data set.
- They complement the measures of central tendency, such as mean, median and mode, which describe the average or typical value of a data set.
- Measures of dispersion help to understand how much the data values deviate from the central value, and how consistent or inconsistent the data values are.
- Some common measures of dispersion are:

  - Range: The difference between the maximum and minimum values of a data set. It is the simplest measure of dispersion, but it is sensitive to outliers and does not consider the distribution of the data.
  - Variance: The average of the squared deviations of the data values from the mean. It measures how far the data values are spread around the mean, but it is not in the same unit as the data values and it is also affected by outliers.
  - Standard deviation: The positive square root of the variance. It measures how much the data values deviate from the mean, and it is in the same unit as the data values. It is the most widely used measure of dispersion, but it is also influenced by outliers.
  - Coefficient of variation: The ratio of the standard deviation to the mean, expressed as a percentage. It measures the relative variability of the data values, and it is useful for comparing the dispersion of data sets with different units or means.
  - Interquartile range: The difference between the third quartile and the first quartile of a data set. It measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data values, and it is not affected by outliers or the shape of the distribution.
  - Mean absolute deviation: The average of the absolute deviations of the data values from the mean. It measures how much the data values deviate from the mean, and it is in the same unit as the data values. It is less sensitive to outliers than the variance or standard deviation, but it is not as widely used.
