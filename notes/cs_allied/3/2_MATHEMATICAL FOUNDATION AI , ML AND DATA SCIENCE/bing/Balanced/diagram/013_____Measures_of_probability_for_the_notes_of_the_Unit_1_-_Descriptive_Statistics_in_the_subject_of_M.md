### Measures of probability

- Probability is a way of quantifying the uncertainty of an event or outcome.
- Probability can be expressed as a number between 0 and 1, where 0 means impossible and 1 means certain.
- Probability can be interpreted as the relative frequency of an event in the long run, or as the degree of belief in the occurrence of an event.
- A probability measure is a mathematical function that assigns probabilities to events in a sample space, which is the set of all possible outcomes of an experiment or observation.
- A probability measure must satisfy the following axioms  :
  - The probability of the entire sample space is 1, i.e., P(S) = 1.
  - The probability of any event is non-negative, i.e., P(A) ≥ 0 for any event A.
  - The probability of the union of countably many disjoint events is equal to the sum of their probabilities, i.e., P(A1 ∪ A2 ∪ ...) = P(A1) + P(A2) + ... for any countable collection of disjoint events A1, A2, ...
- A probability measure can be specified by giving a probability mass function (PMF) for discrete sample spaces, or a probability density function (PDF) for continuous sample spaces.
- A PMF assigns a probability to each possible outcome in a discrete sample space, such that the sum of all probabilities is 1.
- A PDF assigns a probability to each infinitesimal interval in a continuous sample space, such that the integral of the PDF over the entire sample space is 1.
- The probability of an event in a continuous sample space is equal to the integral of the PDF over the region corresponding to the event.
- Some examples of probability measures are:
  - The uniform distribution, which assigns equal probability to all outcomes in a finite or infinite sample space.
  - The binomial distribution, which models the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials with the same probability of success.
  - The normal distribution, which models the distribution of many natural phenomena, such as heights, weights, errors, etc.
  - The Poisson distribution, which models the number of rare events that occur in a fixed interval of time or space.
