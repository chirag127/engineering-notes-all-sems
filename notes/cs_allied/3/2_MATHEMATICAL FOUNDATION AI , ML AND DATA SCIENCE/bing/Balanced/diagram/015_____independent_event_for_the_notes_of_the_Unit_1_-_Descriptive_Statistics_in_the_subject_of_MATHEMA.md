### Independent Event

- An independent event is an event whose occurrence is not affected by any other event.  
- For example, if we flip a coin and get heads, then flip it again and get tails, the two events are independent of each other. The outcome of the first flip does not influence the outcome of the second flip.   
- The probability of an independent event is calculated by multiplying the probabilities of each individual event.  
- For example, if we flip a coin twice, the probability of getting heads both times is 1/2 x 1/2 = 1/4.  
- A Venn diagram can be used to illustrate independent events. Two circles that do not overlap represent two independent events. The area of each circle represents the probability of each event, and the area of the rectangle represents the sample space.
