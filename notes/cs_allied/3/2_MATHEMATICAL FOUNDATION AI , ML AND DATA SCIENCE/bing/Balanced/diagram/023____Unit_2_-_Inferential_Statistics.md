## Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics

Inferential statistics is the branch of statistics that uses sample data to draw conclusions about a population or a process. Inferential statistics can help us to:

- Test hypotheses and make decisions based on data
- Estimate unknown parameters or quantities of interest
- Compare different groups or treatments
- Assess the relationship between variables
- Predict future outcomes or behaviors

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- Sampling methods and sampling distributions
- Confidence intervals and margin of error
- Hypothesis testing and p-values
- Type I and Type II errors and power
- One-sample and two-sample tests for means and proportions
- ANOVA and F-test
- Chi-square test and contingency tables
- Correlation and regression
- Multiple regression and model selection
- Residual analysis and diagnostics

To learn inferential statistics, you will need to understand some basic concepts and terminology, such as:

- Population and sample
- Parameter and statistic
- Random variable and distribution
- Standard error and standard deviation
- Central limit theorem and normal approximation
- Null and alternative hypotheses
- Significance level and confidence level
- Test statistic and critical value
- Effect size and practical significance
- Assumptions and conditions

Inferential statistics can help us to make sense of data and draw valid conclusions from it. However, inferential statistics also has some limitations and challenges, such as:

- Sampling bias and variability
- Measurement error and validity
- Confounding and causality
- Generalization and external validity
- Multiple testing and false discovery
- Ethical and social implications

In this unit, you will learn how to apply inferential statistics to different scenarios and problems, and how to interpret and communicate the results. You will also learn how to evaluate the quality and validity of statistical inference, and how to avoid common pitfalls and errors.
