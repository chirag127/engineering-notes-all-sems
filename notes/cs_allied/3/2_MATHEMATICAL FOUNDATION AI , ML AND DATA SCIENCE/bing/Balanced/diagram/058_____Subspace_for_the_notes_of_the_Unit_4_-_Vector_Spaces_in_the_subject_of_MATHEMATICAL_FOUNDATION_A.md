### Subspace

A subspace is a subset of a vector space that is also a vector space itself. That means, a subspace has the following properties:

- It contains the zero vector.
- It is closed under vector addition, meaning if u and v are in the subspace, then u + v is also in the subspace.
- It is closed under scalar multiplication, meaning if u is in the subspace and c is any scalar, then c * u is also in the subspace.

Some examples of subspaces are:

- The set of all vectors of the form (x, 0, 0) is a subspace of R^3, since it satisfies the three properties above. This subspace is a line passing through the origin along the x-axis.
- The set of all vectors of the form (x, y, 0) is a subspace of R^3, since it satisfies the three properties above. This subspace is a plane passing through the origin parallel to the xy-plane.
- The set of all vectors of the form (x, y, z) such that x + y + z = 0 is a subspace of R^3, since it satisfies the three properties above. This subspace is a plane passing through the origin that is not parallel to any of the coordinate planes.
- The set of all polynomials of degree at most n is a subspace of the vector space of all polynomials, since it satisfies the three properties above. This subspace has dimension n + 1, since it has n + 1 basis elements, such as 1, x, x^2, ..., x^n.
- The set of all solutions to a homogeneous linear system of equations is a subspace of R^n, where n is the number of variables, since it satisfies the three properties above. This subspace is called the null space of the coefficient matrix of the system.
