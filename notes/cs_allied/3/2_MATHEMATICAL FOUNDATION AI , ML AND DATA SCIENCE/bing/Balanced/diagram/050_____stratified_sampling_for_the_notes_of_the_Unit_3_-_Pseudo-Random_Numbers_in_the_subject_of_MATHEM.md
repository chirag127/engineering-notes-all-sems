### Stratified Sampling

Stratified sampling is a method of sampling from a population that can be divided into subgroups or strata that share some common characteristics. The purpose of stratified sampling is to ensure that each subgroup is adequately represented in the sample, and to reduce the sampling error and increase the precision of the estimates.

Some examples of stratified sampling are:

- A survey of students' academic performance that divides the population into strata by grade level, and then randomly selects a proportionate number of students from each grade.
- A study of customer satisfaction that splits the population into strata by product category, and then randomly chooses a fixed number of customers from each category.
- A simulation of a physical system that partitions the input space into strata by some criteria, and then randomly generates a set of inputs from each stratum.

The steps to perform stratified sampling are:

1. Define the population and the subgroups of interest.
2. Separate the population into non-overlapping strata based on the chosen criteria.
3. Determine the sample size and the allocation method for each stratum.
4. Randomly select the sample units from each stratum using simple random sampling or systematic sampling.
5. Analyze the data using appropriate statistical techniques that account for the stratification.

Some advantages of stratified sampling are:

- It can improve the accuracy and representativeness of the sample by reducing the sampling variability within each stratum.
- It can ensure that all subgroups of interest are included in the sample and have sufficient sample size for analysis.
- It can allow for different sampling methods or different levels of precision for different strata, depending on the research objectives and resources.

Some disadvantages of stratified sampling are:

- It requires prior knowledge of the population and the relevant criteria for stratification, which may not be available or easy to obtain.
- It can be more complex and costly to implement than simple random sampling, especially if the number of strata is large or the strata are not easily identifiable.
- It can introduce bias or inefficiency if the strata are not homogeneous or the allocation method is not optimal.
