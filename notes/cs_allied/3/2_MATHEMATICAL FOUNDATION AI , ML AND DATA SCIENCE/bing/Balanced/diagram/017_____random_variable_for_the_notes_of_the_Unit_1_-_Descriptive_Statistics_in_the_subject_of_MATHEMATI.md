### Random Variable

- A random variable is a variable that is used to denote the numerical outcome of a random experiment.
- A random experiment is an event or process that has a random outcome, such as rolling a die, choosing a card, or playing a slot machine .
- A random variable can be either discrete or continuous, depending on the type of values it can take.
- A discrete random variable can take only a countable number of distinct values, such as 0, 1, 2, 3, etc. Examples of discrete random variables are the number of heads in a coin toss, the number of students in a class, or the number of defects in a product .
- A continuous random variable can take any value within a range of values, such as 0.03, 1.2374553, etc. Examples of continuous random variables are the height of a person, the weight of a fruit, or the time of arrival of a bus .
- A random variable is usually denoted by a capital letter, such as X, Y, or Z.
- A probability distribution is a function that assigns probabilities to the possible values of a random variable .
- The probability distribution of a discrete random variable can be represented by a table, a graph, or a formula that gives the probability of each value.
- The probability distribution of a continuous random variable can be represented by a curve, called a probability density function, that gives the probability of any interval of values.
- The mean (or expected value) of a random variable is the average value that the random variable takes in the long run. It is calculated by multiplying each possible value by its probability and adding them up .
- The variance of a random variable is a measure of how much the values of the random variable vary from the mean. It is calculated by squaring the difference between each possible value and the mean, multiplying by the probability, and adding them up .
- The standard deviation of a random variable is the square root of the variance. It is a measure of how much the values of the random variable deviate from the mean .
