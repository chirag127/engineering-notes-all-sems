## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that appear to be random but are actually generated by a deterministic algorithm.
- Pseudo-random numbers are useful for applications such as cryptography, simulation, gaming, and statistical analysis.
- Pseudo-random numbers are not truly random because they can be predicted or reproduced given the same algorithm and initial state (seed).
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that produce pseudo-random numbers from a seed.
- PRNGs can be classified into two types: linear and nonlinear.
- Linear PRNGs use arithmetic operations such as addition, multiplication, and modulo to generate pseudo-random numbers. Examples of linear PRNGs are linear congruential generators (LCGs) and linear feedback shift registers (LFSRs).
- Nonlinear PRNGs use more complex operations such as bitwise operations, hashing, and encryption to generate pseudo-random numbers. Examples of nonlinear PRNGs are Blum-Blum-Shub (BBS), Mersenne Twister (MT), and secure hash algorithm (SHA).
- PRNGs can be evaluated based on their statistical properties, such as uniformity, independence, and period.
- Uniformity means that the pseudo-random numbers are evenly distributed over a given range.
- Independence means that the pseudo-random numbers are not correlated with each other or with the seed.
- Period means that the pseudo-random numbers eventually repeat after a certain number of iterations.
- PRNGs can also be evaluated based on their security, such as unpredictability, irreversibility, and randomness.
- Unpredictability means that the pseudo-random numbers cannot be guessed or inferred from previous or future outputs.
- Irreversibility means that the seed cannot be derived from the pseudo-random numbers.
- Randomness means that the pseudo-random numbers pass various statistical tests that check for patterns or biases.
