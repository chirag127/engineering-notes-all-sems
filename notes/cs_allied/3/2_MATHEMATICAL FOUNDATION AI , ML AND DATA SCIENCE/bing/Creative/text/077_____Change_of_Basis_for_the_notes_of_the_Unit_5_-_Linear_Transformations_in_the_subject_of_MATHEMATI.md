### Change of Basis

- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the whole space.
- A vector can be represented as a linear combination of the basis vectors using some coefficients called coordinates or components.
- The same vector can have different coordinates depending on the choice of basis.
- Changing the basis means finding the coordinates of the same vector in a different basis.
- To change the basis, we need to find a matrix that transforms the coordinates from one basis to another. This matrix is called the change of basis matrix.
- The change of basis matrix can be found by multiplying the inverse of the matrix formed by the old basis vectors with the matrix formed by the new basis vectors.
- The change of basis matrix can be used to convert any vector from the old basis to the new basis by multiplying the matrix with the vector.
- The change of basis matrix can also be used to convert a linear transformation from the old basis to the new basis by multiplying the matrix with the transformation matrix.
- The change of basis matrix preserves the properties of the vector space and the linear transformation, such as dimension, rank, nullity, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors.
