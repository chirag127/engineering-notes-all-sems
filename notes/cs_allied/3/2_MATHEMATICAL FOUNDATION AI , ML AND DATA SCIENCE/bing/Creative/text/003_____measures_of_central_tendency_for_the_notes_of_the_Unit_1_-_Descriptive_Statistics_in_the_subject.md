### Measures of Central Tendency

- Measures of central tendency are numerical values that summarize the distribution of a variable by indicating its center or typical value.
- There are three common measures of central tendency: mean, median, and mode.
- Mean: The mean is the arithmetic average of a set of values. It is calculated by adding up all the values and dividing by the number of values. The mean is sensitive to outliers, which are extreme values that deviate from the rest of the data.
- Median: The median is the middle value of a set of values when they are arranged in ascending or descending order. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the middle two values. The median is not affected by outliers, and it divides the data into two equal halves.
- Mode: The mode is the most frequently occurring value in a set of values. There can be more than one mode if there are multiple values with the same frequency. The mode is not affected by outliers, and it reflects the most typical value in the data.
- The choice of the measure of central tendency depends on the type and shape of the data. For example, the mean is appropriate for quantitative data that are symmetrically distributed, while the median is appropriate for quantitative data that are skewed or have outliers. The mode is appropriate for qualitative or categorical data, or for quantitative data that have distinct values.
