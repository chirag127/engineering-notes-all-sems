### Conditional Probability

- Conditional probability is the probability of an event occurring given that another event has already occurred.
- Conditional probability can be calculated using the formula: P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), where P(A|B) is the probability of A given B, P(A and B) is the probability of both A and B happening, and P(B) is the probability of B happening.
- Conditional probability can also be calculated using the formula: P(A|B) = P(A) * P(B|A), where P(B|A) is the probability of B given A, and P(A) is the probability of A happening.
- Conditional probability can be used to update our beliefs about an event based on new information or evidence.
- Conditional probability can be represented using a Venn diagram or a tree diagram. A Venn diagram shows the intersection of two events as a shaded region, and a tree diagram shows the branches of possible outcomes and their probabilities.
- Conditional probability can be applied to various scenarios, such as coin tossing, card drawing, medical testing, and Bayesian inference.
