### Sampling & Confidence Interval

- Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of the population for analysis and inference.
- Sampling can be done in different ways, such as random sampling, stratified sampling, cluster sampling, etc.
- Sampling can reduce the cost and time of data collection and analysis, but it can also introduce sampling error and bias.
- Sampling error is the difference between the sample statistic and the population parameter due to random variation.
- Sampling bias is the systematic deviation of the sample from the population due to non-random selection or non-response.
- To measure the uncertainty of the sample statistic, we can use the concept of confidence interval.
- A confidence interval is an interval estimate that contains the population parameter with a certain probability, called the confidence level.
- The confidence level is usually expressed as a percentage, such as 95% or 99%.
- The confidence interval depends on the sample size, the sample standard deviation, and the sampling distribution of the statistic.
- The sampling distribution of the statistic is the probability distribution of the possible values of the statistic based on repeated sampling from the population.
- The most common sampling distribution is the normal distribution, which is symmetric and bell-shaped, and has two parameters: the mean and the standard deviation.
- The mean of the sampling distribution is equal to the population parameter, and the standard deviation of the sampling distribution is called the standard error of the statistic.
- The standard error measures the variability of the statistic due to sampling error, and it decreases as the sample size increases.
- The confidence interval for the population mean can be calculated as:

$$\bar{x} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$$

where $\bar{x}$ is the sample mean, $z_{\alpha/2}$ is the critical value of the standard normal distribution for the given confidence level, $s$ is the sample standard deviation, and $n$ is the sample size.
- The confidence interval for the population proportion can be calculated as:

$$\hat{p} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}$$

where $\hat{p}$ is the sample proportion, and the other terms are the same as above.
- The confidence interval can be interpreted as follows: if we repeat the sampling process many times and calculate the confidence interval for each sample, then the proportion of the intervals that contain the population parameter will be equal to the confidence level.
