### Definition of Eigenvalue and Eigenvector

- An eigenvalue is a scalar that describes how much a linear transformation changes the length or direction of a vector.
- An eigenvector is a nonzero vector that does not change its direction under a linear transformation, but only its length by a factor of the eigenvalue.
- Formally, if **A** is a square matrix of order n and **x** is a nonzero vector in R^n, then **x** is an eigenvector of **A** and λ is an eigenvalue of **A** if and only if **Ax** = λ**x**.
- The set of all eigenvalues of a matrix is called the spectrum of the matrix, and the set of all eigenvectors corresponding to a given eigenvalue is called the eigenspace of that eigenvalue.
- Eigenvectors and eigenvalues are useful for analyzing the properties and behavior of linear transformations, such as rotations, scaling, shearing, and projections. They can also be used to decompose a matrix into simpler components, such as diagonal or orthogonal matrices.
