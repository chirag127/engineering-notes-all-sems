### Estimation and Hypothesis Testing

- Estimation is the process of using sample data to estimate the value of an unknown population parameter, such as the mean, proportion, variance, etc.
- Hypothesis testing is the process of using sample data to test a claim or a statement about a population parameter, such as the mean, proportion, variance, etc.
- Both estimation and hypothesis testing are based on the concept of sampling distribution, which is the distribution of a sample statistic obtained from repeated random samples of the same size from a population.
- There are two types of estimation: point estimation and interval estimation.
  - Point estimation is the process of using a single value, called a point estimate, to estimate the population parameter. For example, the sample mean is a point estimate of the population mean.
  - Interval estimation is the process of using an interval, called a confidence interval, to estimate the population parameter. A confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the population parameter with a certain level of confidence. For example, a 95% confidence interval for the population mean is a range of values that has a 95% chance of containing the true population mean.
- There are two types of hypothesis testing: parametric and nonparametric.
  - Parametric hypothesis testing is the process of using sample data to test a claim or a statement about a population parameter, assuming that the population follows a certain distribution, such as the normal distribution. For example, a t-test is a parametric hypothesis test that can be used to compare the means of two populations or groups, assuming that they are normally distributed.
  - Nonparametric hypothesis testing is the process of using sample data to test a claim or a statement about a population parameter, without making any assumptions about the population distribution. For example, a chi-square test is a nonparametric hypothesis test that can be used to test the independence or association between two categorical variables.
