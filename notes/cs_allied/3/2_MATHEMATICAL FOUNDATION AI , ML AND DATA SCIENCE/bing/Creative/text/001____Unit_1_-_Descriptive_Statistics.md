## Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics are methods of summarizing and displaying data in a meaningful way. They help us to understand the main features and patterns of a data set, such as its center, spread, shape, and outliers. Descriptive statistics do not involve generalizing beyond the data or making any inferences about a population.

Some of the common descriptive statistics are:

- **Measures of central tendency**: These are values that indicate the center or typical value of a data set. The most common measures of central tendency are the mean, median, and mode.
- **Measures of variability**: These are values that indicate how much the data vary or deviate from the center. The most common measures of variability are the range, interquartile range, standard deviation, and variance.
- **Measures of relative standing**: These are values that indicate how a data value compares to the rest of the data set or to a reference value. The most common measures of relative standing are percentiles, z-scores, and quartiles.
- **Graphical displays**: These are visual representations of the data that help us to see the overall shape and distribution of the data, as well as identify any outliers or unusual values. Some of the common graphical displays are histograms, boxplots, stem-and-leaf plots, dot plots, and scatter plots.
