### Central Limit Theorem

- The central limit theorem is a fundamental concept in statistics that describes how the sampling distribution of a sample mean approaches a normal distribution as the sample size increases, regardless of the shape of the population distribution    .
- The central limit theorem also implies that the sampling distribution of the sample mean will have the following properties:
  - The mean of the sampling distribution will be equal to the mean of the population.
  - The standard deviation of the sampling distribution will be equal to the standard deviation of the population divided by the square root of the sample size.
  - The shape of the sampling distribution will become more symmetric and bell-shaped as the sample size increases.
- The central limit theorem is useful for making inferences about the population mean based on the sample mean, such as constructing confidence intervals or performing hypothesis tests    .
- The central limit theorem can be illustrated by an example :
  - Suppose a population follows a Poisson distribution with a mean of 10 (left image below).
  - If we take a random sample of size 5 from this population and calculate the sample mean, we might get a value that is not very close to the population mean, such as 6.8 (right image below).
  - However, if we repeat this process many times and plot the distribution of the sample means, we will see that it becomes more normal and centered around the population mean as the sample size increases (middle image below).

- The central limit theorem can be applied to any population distribution, as long as the sample size is large enough (usually at least 30) and the samples are independent and identically distributed    .
