### Independent Event

- An independent event is an event that is not affected by the occurrence of another event.
- For example, tossing a coin and rolling a die are independent events, because the outcome of one does not influence the outcome of the other.
- The probability of an independent event is the same regardless of whether another event has occurred or not.
- For example, the probability of getting heads when tossing a coin is 1/2, no matter how many times you toss the coin or what the previous outcomes were.
- The probability of two independent events occurring together is the product of their individual probabilities.
- For example, the probability of getting heads when tossing a coin and getting a 6 when rolling a die is 1/2 * 1/6 = 1/12, because the events are independent and do not affect each other.
- To check if two events are independent, you can use the following formula:

P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B)

- If the formula holds true, then the events are independent. If the formula does not hold true, then the events are dependent and influence each other.
