### Length

- The length of a vector is also called its norm or magnitude. It is a measure of how far the vector extends from the origin.
- The length of a vector can be calculated using the Pythagorean theorem, which states that the square of the length of the hypotenuse of a right triangle is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides.
- For a vector $\vec{v} = (v_1, v_2, ..., v_n)$ in an n-dimensional vector space, the length of $\vec{v}$ is given by:

$$\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + ... + v_n^2}$$

- The length of a vector is always a non-negative real number. It is zero if and only if the vector is the zero vector, which has all its components equal to zero.
- The length of a vector can be used to compare the sizes of different vectors, or to measure the distance between two points in a vector space. For example, if $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are two vectors, then the distance between them is given by:

$$d(\vec{u}, \vec{v}) = \|\vec{u} - \vec{v}\|$$

- The length of a vector can also be used to normalize a vector, which means to make it have a length of one. A normalized vector is also called a unit vector. To normalize a vector $\vec{v}$, we divide it by its length:

$$\hat{v} = \frac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|}$$

- A unit vector has the same direction as the original vector, but a different magnitude. Normalizing a vector can be useful for simplifying calculations or for representing directions without considering the magnitude. For example, a unit vector can be used to specify the direction of a force or a velocity.
