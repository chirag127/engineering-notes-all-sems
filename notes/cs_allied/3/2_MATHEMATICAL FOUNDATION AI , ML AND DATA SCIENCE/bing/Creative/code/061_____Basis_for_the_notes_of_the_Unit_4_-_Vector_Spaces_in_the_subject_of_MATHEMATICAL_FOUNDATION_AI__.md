- A vector space is a set of objects called vectors that can be added and multiplied by scalars (usually real or complex numbers) in a way that satisfies certain axioms .
- A vector space is also called a linear space because the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication are linear, meaning they satisfy the distributive, associative, and commutative properties .
- The basic example of a vector space is the Euclidean space mathbb {R}^n Rn, where every element is represented by a list of n real numbers, and the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication are performed componentwise .
- Other examples of vector spaces include the space of polynomials of a given degree, the space of matrices of a given size, the space of functions of a given domain, and the space of solutions of a homogeneous linear system .
- A vector space is characterized by its dimension, which is the number of linearly independent vectors that span the space. For example, the dimension of mathbb {R}^n Rn is n, and the dimension of the space of polynomials of degree at most n is n+1 .
- A vector space can have subspaces, which are subsets of the vector space that are also vector spaces under the same operations. For example, the x-axis and the y-axis are subspaces of mathbb {R}^2 R2, and the space of constant functions is a subspace of the space of all functions .
- A vector space can also have bases, which are sets of linearly independent vectors that span the space. For example, the standard basis of mathbb {R}^n Rn is the set of n unit vectors along each coordinate axis, and the monomial basis of the space of polynomials of degree at most n is the set of n+1 polynomials x^k for k=0,1,...,n .
- A vector space can be represented by different bases, and the coordinates of a vector in a vector space depend on the choice of basis. To change the coordinates of a vector from one basis to another, a linear transformation called a change of basis matrix can be used .
- A vector space can have various properties and structures, such as inner products, norms, metrics, angles, orthogonality, distances, lengths, and projections. These properties and structures can be used to measure and compare vectors in a vector space, and to perform operations such as projections, rotations, reflections, and decompositions .
- A vector space can also have linear maps, which are functions from one vector space to another that preserve the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication. Linear maps can be represented by matrices, and their properties and effects can be studied using concepts such as kernels, images, ranks, nullities, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and determinants .
