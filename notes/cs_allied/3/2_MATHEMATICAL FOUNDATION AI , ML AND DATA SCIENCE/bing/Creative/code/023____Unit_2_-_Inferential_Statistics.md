## Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics

Inferential statistics is the branch of statistics that uses sample data to draw conclusions about a population or a process. Inferential statistics can help us to:

- Estimate unknown parameters, such as the mean, proportion, or correlation coefficient of a population, based on sample data.
- Test hypotheses or claims about a population or a process, such as whether the mean of a population is equal to a certain value, or whether two variables are independent, based on sample data.
- Compare two or more populations or processes, such as whether the mean of one population is higher than the mean of another population, or whether the variance of one process is lower than the variance of another process, based on sample data.
- Predict future outcomes or behaviors, such as the probability of an event occurring, or the expected value of a variable, based on sample data.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- Sampling methods and sampling distributions, such as how to select a representative sample from a population, and how to describe the distribution of a sample statistic, such as the sample mean or the sample proportion.
- Confidence intervals, such as how to construct and interpret an interval estimate for a population parameter, such as the population mean or the population proportion, based on sample data and a level of confidence.
- Hypothesis testing, such as how to formulate and test a null and alternative hypothesis about a population parameter, such as the population mean or the population proportion, based on sample data and a significance level.
- Analysis of variance (ANOVA), such as how to compare the means of two or more populations or groups, based on sample data and a significance level, using the F-test and the ANOVA table.
- Chi-square tests, such as how to test the independence or the homogeneity of two categorical variables, based on sample data and a significance level, using the chi-square statistic and the contingency table.
- Correlation and regression, such as how to measure and test the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two quantitative variables, based on sample data and a significance level, using the correlation coefficient and the regression equation.
