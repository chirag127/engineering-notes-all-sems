### Sampling & Confidence Interval

- Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of observations from a population to estimate some characteristics of the population.
- Sampling is useful because it can reduce the cost and time of collecting data, and it can also improve the accuracy and quality of the data analysis.
- There are two main types of sampling methods: probability sampling and non-probability sampling.
- Probability sampling is a sampling method that ensures that every element in the population has a known and non-zero chance of being selected in the sample.
- Probability sampling methods include simple random sampling, stratified sampling, cluster sampling, and systematic sampling.
- Non-probability sampling is a sampling method that does not guarantee that every element in the population has a known and non-zero chance of being selected in the sample.
- Non-probability sampling methods include convenience sampling, quota sampling, judgment sampling, and snowball sampling.
- The advantages of probability sampling are that it can produce representative and unbiased samples, and it can allow for the estimation of sampling errors and confidence intervals.
- The disadvantages of probability sampling are that it can be complex and expensive to implement, and it may not be feasible or practical in some situations.
- The advantages of non-probability sampling are that it can be simple and cheap to implement, and it can be useful for exploratory or qualitative research.
- The disadvantages of non-probability sampling are that it can produce unrepresentative and biased samples, and it cannot allow for the estimation of sampling errors and confidence intervals.

- A confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the true value of a population parameter with a certain level of confidence.
- A confidence interval is calculated from a sample statistic and a margin of error, which depends on the sample size, the sampling distribution, and the confidence level.
- A confidence level is the probability that the confidence interval contains the true population parameter. It is usually expressed as a percentage, such as 95% or 99%.
- A confidence interval can be interpreted as follows: if we repeat the sampling process many times and calculate the confidence interval for each sample, then the proportion of confidence intervals that contain the true population parameter will be equal to the confidence level.
- A confidence interval can be used to assess the precision and reliability of a sample estimate, and to compare the estimates from different samples or populations.
- A confidence interval can be constructed for different types of population parameters, such as the mean, the proportion, the difference between means, the difference between proportions, the correlation coefficient, the regression coefficient, etc.
- A confidence interval can be affected by the sample size, the sampling method, the sampling distribution, the confidence level, and the population variability.
- A larger sample size, a higher confidence level, and a higher population variability will result in a wider confidence interval, while a smaller sample size, a lower confidence level, and a lower population variability will result in a narrower confidence interval.
- A confidence interval can be calculated using different methods, such as the z-score method, the t-score method, the bootstrap method, etc., depending on the type of population parameter, the shape of the sampling distribution, and the availability of the population standard deviation.
