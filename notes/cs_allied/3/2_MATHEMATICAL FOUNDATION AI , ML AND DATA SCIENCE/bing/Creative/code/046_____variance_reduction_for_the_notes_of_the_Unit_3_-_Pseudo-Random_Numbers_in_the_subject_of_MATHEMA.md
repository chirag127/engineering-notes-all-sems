### Variance Reduction for Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that mimics the properties of true random numbers.
- Pseudo-random numbers are often used in simulation studies to model stochastic phenomena, such as physical systems, biological processes, or financial markets.
- However, pseudo-random numbers may introduce errors or biases in the simulation results, due to their finite length, periodicity, or correlation.
- Variance reduction techniques are methods for improving the accuracy and efficiency of simulation estimates by reducing the variance of the sample mean or other statistics computed from pseudo-random numbers.
- Variance reduction techniques can be classified into two categories: (1) methods that modify the generation of pseudo-random numbers, and (2) methods that modify the simulation model or the estimator.
- Some examples of variance reduction techniques are:

  - Antithetic variates: This method uses pairs of pseudo-random numbers that are negatively correlated, such as x and 1-x, to cancel out some of the variance in the simulation output. For example, if x is a pseudo-random number from a uniform distribution on [0,1], then 1-x is also a pseudo-random number from the same distribution, but with a negative correlation of -1 with x. This method can reduce the variance of the sample mean by a factor of 2, if the simulation output is a monotonic function of the pseudo-random numbers .
  - Stratified sampling: This method divides the range of the pseudo-random numbers into subintervals or strata, and generates a fixed number of pseudo-random numbers in each stratum. This ensures that the pseudo-random numbers are more evenly distributed and representative of the population, and reduces the variance of the sample mean by a factor of the number of strata. For example, if x is a pseudo-random number from a uniform distribution on [0,1], then stratified sampling can divide the interval into k equal subintervals, and generate n/k pseudo-random numbers in each subinterval. This method can reduce the variance of the sample mean by a factor of k, if the simulation output is a linear function of the pseudo-random numbers .
  - Control variates: This method uses a known function of the pseudo-random numbers that is correlated with the simulation output, and subtracts a weighted version of it from the simulation output. This reduces the variance of the simulation output by exploiting the covariance between the function and the output. For example, if x is a pseudo-random number from a uniform distribution on [0,1], and y is the simulation output, then a possible control variate is x itself, which has a known mean of 0.5. By subtracting a multiple of x from y, the variance of y can be reduced by a factor of 1 minus the squared correlation coefficient between x and y .
