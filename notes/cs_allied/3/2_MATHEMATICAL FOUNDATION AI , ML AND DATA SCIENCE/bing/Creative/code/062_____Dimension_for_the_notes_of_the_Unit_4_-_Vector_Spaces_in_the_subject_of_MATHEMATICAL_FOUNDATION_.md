### Dimension
- Dimension is a measure of the number of independent directions in a vector space.
- A vector space is a set of vectors that can be added and scaled by scalars, and that satisfies some basic properties such as commutativity, associativity, distributivity, and existence of additive identity and inverse.
- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the whole space, meaning that any vector in the space can be written as a linear combination of the basis vectors.
- The dimension of a vector space is the number of vectors in any basis of the space. It is also the maximum number of linearly independent vectors in the space.
- The dimension of a vector space is unique and does not depend on the choice of basis. It is also invariant under linear transformations, meaning that applying a linear transformation to a vector space does not change its dimension.
- Some examples of vector spaces and their dimensions are:
  - The set of all real numbers R is a vector space of dimension 1, with any nonzero number as a basis.
  - The set of all ordered pairs of real numbers R^2 is a vector space of dimension 2, with (1,0) and (0,1) as a standard basis.
  - The set of all ordered n-tuples of real numbers R^n is a vector space of dimension n, with the standard basis vectors e_1, e_2, ..., e_n, where e_i is the vector with 1 in the i-th position and 0 elsewhere.
  - The set of all polynomials of degree at most n with real coefficients P_n(R) is a vector space of dimension n+1, with 1, x, x^2, ..., x^n as a basis.
  - The set of all continuous functions from a closed interval [a,b] to R, denoted by C[a,b], is a vector space of infinite dimension, with no finite basis.
