### Conditional Probability

- Conditional probability is the probability of an event occurring given that another event has already occurred.
- Conditional probability is denoted by P(A|B), which means the probability of event A happening given that event B has already happened.
- Conditional probability can be calculated using the formula:

```math
P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}
```

- where P(A ∩ B) is the probability of both events A and B occurring, and P(B) is the probability of event B occurring.
- Conditional probability can be used to find the probability of events that are dependent on each other, meaning that the occurrence of one event affects the occurrence of another event.
- For example, if we want to find the probability of drawing a red card from a deck of cards after drawing a king, we can use conditional probability as follows:

```math
P(red|king) = \frac{P(red \cap king)}{P(king)} = \frac{\frac{2}{52}}{\frac{4}{52}} = \frac{1}{2}
```

- where P(red ∩ king) is the probability of drawing a red card and a king, which is 2 out of 52 cards, and P(king) is the probability of drawing a king, which is 4 out of 52 cards.
- Conditional probability can also be used to find the probability of events that are independent of each other, meaning that the occurrence of one event does not affect the occurrence of another event.
- For example, if we want to find the probability of rolling a 6 on a fair die after rolling a 3, we can use conditional probability as follows:

```math
P(6|3) = \frac{P(6 \cap 3)}{P(3)} = \frac{\frac{1}{36}}{\frac{1}{6}} = \frac{1}{6}
```

- where P(6 ∩ 3) is the probability of rolling a 6 and a 3, which is 1 out of 36 possible outcomes, and P(3) is the probability of rolling a 3, which is 1 out of 6 possible outcomes.
- Note that in this case, the conditional probability is equal to the marginal probability, which is the probability of an event occurring without any conditions, such as P(6) = 1/6. This is because the events are independent and do not influence each other.
