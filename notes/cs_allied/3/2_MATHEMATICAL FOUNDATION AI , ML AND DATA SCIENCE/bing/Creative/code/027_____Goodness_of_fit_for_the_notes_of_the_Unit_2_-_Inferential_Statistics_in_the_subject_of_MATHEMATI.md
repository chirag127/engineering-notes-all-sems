### Goodness of fit

- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a statistical model fits the observed data.
- It can be used to compare different models or to test hypotheses about the underlying distribution of the data.
- There are different methods to assess the goodness of fit, such as graphical methods, numerical methods, and hypothesis testing methods.
- Graphical methods involve plotting the observed data and the fitted model on the same graph and visually inspecting the agreement or discrepancy between them. Examples of graphical methods are histograms, scatter plots, Q-Q plots, and residual plots.
- Numerical methods involve calculating summary statistics that quantify the discrepancy between the observed data and the fitted model. Examples of numerical methods are mean squared error (MSE), root mean squared error (RMSE), coefficient of determination (R^2), and Akaike information criterion (AIC).
- Hypothesis testing methods involve setting up a null hypothesis that the observed data follow a certain distribution or model and using a test statistic to measure the evidence against the null hypothesis. Examples of hypothesis testing methods are chi-square test, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, and Anderson-Darling test.
- The choice of the method depends on the type and purpose of the analysis, the availability and quality of the data, and the assumptions and limitations of the model.
