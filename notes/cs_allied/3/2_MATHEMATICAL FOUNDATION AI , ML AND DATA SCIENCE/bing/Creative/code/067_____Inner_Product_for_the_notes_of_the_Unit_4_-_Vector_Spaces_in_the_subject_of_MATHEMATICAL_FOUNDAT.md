### Inner Product

- An inner product is a function that assigns a real number to every pair of vectors in a vector space. It is also called a dot product or a scalar product.
- An inner product satisfies the following properties for any vectors u, v, w and any scalar c in the vector space:

  - Symmetry: <u, v> = <v, u>
  - Linearity: <u + v, w> = <u, w> + <v, w> and <cu, v> = c<u, v>
  - Positivity: <u, u> >= 0 and <u, u> = 0 if and only if u = 0

- An inner product can be used to define the length or norm of a vector as ||u|| = sqrt(<u, u>) and the angle or cosine similarity between two vectors as cos(u, v) = <u, v> / (||u|| ||v||).
- An inner product can also be used to define the orthogonality or perpendicularity of two vectors as u and v are orthogonal if and only if <u, v> = 0.
- An inner product can be generalized to complex vector spaces by using the complex conjugate of the first vector in the product, i.e. <u, v> = <v*, u*> where v* is the complex conjugate of v.
- An example of an inner product is the standard inner product on R^n, which is defined as <u, v> = u1v1 + u2v2 + ... + unvn for any vectors u = (u1, u2, ..., un) and v = (v1, v2, ..., vn) in R^n.
