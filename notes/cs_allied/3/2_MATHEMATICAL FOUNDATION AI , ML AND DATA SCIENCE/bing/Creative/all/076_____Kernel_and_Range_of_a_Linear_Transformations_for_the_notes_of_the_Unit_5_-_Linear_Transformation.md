# Kernel and Range of a Linear Transformation

- A linear transformation is a function that maps vectors from one vector space to another vector space, preserving the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication.
- The kernel (or null space) of a linear transformation is the set of all vectors that are mapped to the zero vector by the transformation.
- The range (or image) of a linear transformation is the set of all vectors that are mapped to by the transformation.
- The kernel and range of a linear transformation are both subspaces of their respective vector spaces.
- The kernel and range of a linear transformation are related by the rank-nullity theorem, which states that the dimension of the domain of the transformation is equal to the sum of the dimension of the kernel and the dimension of the range.
- To find the kernel of a linear transformation, we need to solve the homogeneous system of linear equations corresponding to the transformation, and find the basis of the solution space.
- To find the range of a linear transformation, we need to find the span of the images of the basis vectors of the domain of the transformation, and find the basis of the spanned subspace.
