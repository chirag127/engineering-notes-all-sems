# Bivariate Correlation

- Bivariate correlation is a statistical technique that measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables .
- Bivariate correlation can be calculated using different methods, depending on the type and level of measurement of the variables. The most common methods are:
  - Pearson's correlation coefficient (r): This method is used when both variables are continuous and have a linear relationship. It ranges from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative linear relationship, 0 indicates no linear relationship, and 1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship  .
  - Spearman's rank correlation coefficient (rho): This method is used when one or both variables are ordinal (ranked) or when the relationship is nonlinear. It also ranges from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative monotonic relationship, 0 indicates no monotonic relationship, and 1 indicates a perfect positive monotonic relationship. A monotonic relationship is one that either increases or decreases, but not both .
  - Kendall's rank correlation coefficient (tau): This method is similar to Spearman's, but it is based on the number of concordant and discordant pairs of observations rather than the ranks. A pair is concordant if the two variables change in the same direction, and discordant if they change in opposite directions. Kendall's tau ranges from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative ordinal relationship, 0 indicates no ordinal relationship, and 1 indicates a perfect positive ordinal relationship .
- Bivariate correlation can be used to explore the association between two variables, to test hypotheses about the direction and magnitude of the relationship, and to assess the validity and reliability of measurement instruments  .
- Bivariate correlation does not imply causation, meaning that it does not indicate whether one variable causes or influences the other. To establish causation, other methods such as experiments or regression analysis are needed  .
- Bivariate correlation can be affected by outliers, nonlinearity, heteroscedasticity, multicollinearity, and other factors that may distort the true relationship between the variables. Therefore, it is important to check the assumptions and conditions before applying the appropriate method of correlation  .
