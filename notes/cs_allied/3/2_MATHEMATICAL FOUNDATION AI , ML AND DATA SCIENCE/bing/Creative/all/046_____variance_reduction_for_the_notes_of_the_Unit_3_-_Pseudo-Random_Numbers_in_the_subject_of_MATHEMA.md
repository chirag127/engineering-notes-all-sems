# Variance Reduction for Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that mimics the properties of true random numbers.
- Pseudo-random numbers are often used in Monte Carlo simulations, which are numerical methods that rely on repeated sampling to estimate the expected value of a random variable or a function of random variables.
- Monte Carlo simulations can suffer from high variance, which means that the estimates can be very different from the true value and have a large uncertainty.
- Variance reduction techniques are methods that aim to reduce the variance of the Monte Carlo estimates without increasing the number of samples or the computational cost.
- Variance reduction techniques can improve the accuracy and efficiency of Monte Carlo simulations and make them more reliable for decision making and analysis.
- Some common variance reduction techniques are:

  - Common random numbers: This technique uses the same sequence of pseudo-random numbers for different scenarios or alternatives that are being compared, so that the estimates are more correlated and the difference between them is more precise .
  - Antithetic variables: This technique uses pairs of pseudo-random numbers that are negatively correlated, such as 1-x and x, to cancel out some of the variation in the estimates .
  - Control variates: This technique uses a known function of the pseudo-random numbers that has a similar behavior to the function of interest, and adjusts the estimates by subtracting the difference between the known function and its expected value .
  - Conditioning: This technique uses conditional expectations to reduce the variance of the estimates by conditioning on some information that is known or can be easily obtained .
  - Quasi-Monte Carlo methods: This technique uses low-discrepancy sequences instead of pseudo-random numbers, which are more evenly distributed and cover the sample space more uniformly, resulting in lower variance.
