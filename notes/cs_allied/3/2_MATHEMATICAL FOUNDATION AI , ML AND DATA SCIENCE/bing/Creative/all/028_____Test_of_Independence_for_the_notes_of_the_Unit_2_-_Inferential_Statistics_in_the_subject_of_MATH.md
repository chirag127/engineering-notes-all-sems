# Test of Independence

- A test of independence is a statistical procedure that determines whether two categorical variables are independent or related.
- Independence means that the occurrence of one event does not affect the probability of another event.
- A test of independence uses a contingency table, which is a table that displays the frequencies of two or more variables in different categories.
- A test of independence uses the chi-square statistic, which measures the discrepancy between the observed frequencies and the expected frequencies under the assumption of independence.
- The null hypothesis of a test of independence is that the two variables are independent, and the alternative hypothesis is that they are dependent or related.
- The steps of a test of independence are:

  1. Construct a contingency table with the observed frequencies of the two variables in different categories.
  2. Calculate the expected frequencies for each cell of the table, using the formula: E = (row total * column total) / grand total
  3. Calculate the chi-square statistic, using the formula: X^2 = sum of [(O - E)^2 / E], where O is the observed frequency and E is the expected frequency for each cell.
  4. Determine the degrees of freedom, using the formula: df = (number of rows - 1) * (number of columns - 1)
  5. Find the critical value of chi-square for the given level of significance and degrees of freedom, using a chi-square table or a calculator.
  6. Compare the chi-square statistic with the critical value. If the chi-square statistic is greater than or equal to the critical value, reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the two variables are dependent. If the chi-square statistic is less than the critical value, fail to reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the two variables are independent.
  7. Report the results, including the chi-square statistic, the degrees of freedom, the p-value, and the conclusion.
