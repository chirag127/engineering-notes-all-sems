# t-test/z-test (one sample, independent, paired)

- A t-test is a statistical test that is used to compare the means of two groups or the mean of one group against a known value.
- A z-test is a statistical test that is used to compare the proportions of two groups or the proportion of one group against a known value .
- Both tests are based on the assumption that the samples are normally distributed and have equal variances.
- The main difference between the two tests is that a t-test uses the sample standard deviation to estimate the population standard deviation, while a z-test uses the known population standard deviation .
- A t-test is more appropriate when the sample size is small (less than 30) or the population standard deviation is unknown, while a z-test is more appropriate when the sample size is large (more than 30) or the population standard deviation is known .
- There are three types of t-tests and z-tests, depending on the nature of the samples and the research question   :
  - One sample t-test/z-test: A statistical test used to test the hypothesis that the mean/proportion of a single group is equal to a specified value.
  - Independent sample t-test/z-test: A statistical test used to test the hypothesis that the means/proportions of two independent groups are equal.
  - Paired sample t-test/z-test: A statistical test used to test the hypothesis that the mean/proportion of the same group at two different times or under two different conditions are equal.
