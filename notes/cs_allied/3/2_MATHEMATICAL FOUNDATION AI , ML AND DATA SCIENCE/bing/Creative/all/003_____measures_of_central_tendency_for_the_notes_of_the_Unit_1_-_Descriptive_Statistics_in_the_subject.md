# Measures of Central Tendency

- Measures of central tendency are numerical values that summarize the distribution of a variable by indicating its center or typical value.
- There are three common measures of central tendency: mean, median and mode.
- Mean: The mean of a variable is the sum of all the values divided by the number of values. It is also called the average or the arithmetic mean. The mean is sensitive to outliers, which are extreme values that deviate from the rest of the data.
- Median: The median of a variable is the middle value when the values are arranged in ascending or descending order. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the middle two values. The median is not affected by outliers, and it divides the data into two equal halves.
- Mode: The mode of a variable is the most frequently occurring value. There can be more than one mode if there are multiple values with the same frequency. The mode is not affected by outliers, and it reflects the most typical value of the data.
- Example: Consider the following data on the heights (in cm) of 10 students: 160, 162, 164, 165, 166, 167, 168, 170, 172, 180.
  - The mean height is (160 + 162 + ... + 180) / 10 = 166.4 cm.
  - The median height is the average of the middle two values when the data is arranged in ascending order: (166 + 167) / 2 = 166.5 cm.
  - The mode height is the most frequent value, which is 166 cm.
