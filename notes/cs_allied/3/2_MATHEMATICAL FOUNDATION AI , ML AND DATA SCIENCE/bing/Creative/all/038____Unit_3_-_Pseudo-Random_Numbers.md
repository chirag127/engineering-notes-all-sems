## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that appear to be random but are actually generated by a deterministic algorithm.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that produce pseudo-random numbers using a seed value and a mathematical formula.
- PRNGs are useful for applications that require randomness, such as cryptography, simulations, games, and statistical analysis.
- PRNGs have different properties and characteristics, such as period, uniformity, independence, and unpredictability, that affect their quality and suitability for different purposes.
- PRNGs can be classified into two main types: linear and nonlinear. Linear PRNGs use linear operations, such as addition and multiplication, to generate pseudo-random numbers. Nonlinear PRNGs use nonlinear operations, such as bitwise operations, modular arithmetic, and hashing, to generate pseudo-random numbers.
- Examples of linear PRNGs are linear congruential generators (LCGs), linear feedback shift registers (LFSRs), and Blum-Blum-Shub (BBS). Examples of nonlinear PRNGs are Mersenne Twister (MT), Xorshift, and Secure Hash Algorithm (SHA).
- PRNGs can be evaluated and tested using various statistical tests, such as frequency test, runs test, autocorrelation test, and chi-square test, to measure their randomness and detect any patterns or biases.
- PRNGs can also be compared and ranked using various criteria, such as speed, memory, portability, security, and quality, to determine their advantages and disadvantages.
