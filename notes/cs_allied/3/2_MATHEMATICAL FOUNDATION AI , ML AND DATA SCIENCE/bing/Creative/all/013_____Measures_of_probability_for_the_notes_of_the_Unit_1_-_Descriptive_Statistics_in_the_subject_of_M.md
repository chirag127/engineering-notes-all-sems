# Measures of Probability

- Probability is a numerical measure of the likelihood of an event occurring.
- Probability can range from 0 to 1, where 0 means the event is impossible and 1 means the event is certain.
- Probability can be calculated using different approaches, such as classical, relative frequency, or subjective methods.
- Classical probability is based on the assumption that all possible outcomes of an experiment are equally likely, and it is calculated by dividing the number of favorable outcomes by the total number of possible outcomes.
- Relative frequency probability is based on the observation of the frequency of an event occurring in a large number of trials, and it is calculated by dividing the number of times the event occurred by the total number of trials.
- Subjective probability is based on the personal judgment or belief of an individual or a group, and it is not based on any formal calculation or data.
- Probability can be used to describe the behavior of random variables, which are variables that take on different values depending on the outcome of an experiment.
- Probability can also be used to describe the relationship between two or more events or random variables, such as independence, dependence, conditional probability, and joint probability.
- Independence means that the occurrence of one event does not affect the probability of another event occurring.
- Dependence means that the occurrence of one event affects the probability of another event occurring.
- Conditional probability is the probability of an event occurring given that another event has occurred, and it is calculated by dividing the joint probability of the two events by the probability of the given event.
- Joint probability is the probability of two or more events occurring together, and it is calculated by multiplying the probabilities of the individual events if they are independent, or by using the formula of conditional probability if they are dependent.
