- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that simulates randomness, but are not truly random.
- Pseudo-random numbers are useful for applications that require randomness, such as cryptography, simulation, sampling, and testing.
- Pseudo-random numbers should have certain statistical properties, such as uniformity, independence, and unpredictability, to be considered as good approximations of random numbers.
- One of the most common methods for generating pseudo-random numbers is the linear congruential method, which uses the following recurrence relation:

  X<sub>n+1</sub> = (aX<sub>n</sub> + c) mod m

  where X<sub>n</sub> is the n-th pseudo-random number, a, c, and m are constants, and mod is the modulo operation.

- The linear congruential method has a period of at most m, which means that the sequence of pseudo-random numbers will repeat after m iterations. To achieve a long period, the constants a, c, and m should be chosen carefully, following some criteria.
- The quality of the pseudo-random numbers generated by the linear congruential method depends on the statistical tests that are applied to them. Some of the common tests are the frequency test, the runs test, the autocorrelation test, and the chi-square test.
- A pseudo-random number generator is said to converge if the distribution of the pseudo-random numbers approaches the desired distribution as the number of iterations increases. For example, if the pseudo-random numbers are supposed to be uniformly distributed in [0, 1], then the pseudo-random number generator converges if the empirical cumulative distribution function of the pseudo-random numbers converges to the theoretical cumulative distribution function of the uniform distribution.
- Convergence is an important property for pseudo-random number generators, as it ensures that the pseudo-random numbers are close to the true random numbers in terms of their statistical characteristics. Convergence can be measured by various metrics, such as the mean square error, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance, or the discrepancy .
- One way to improve the convergence of pseudo-random number generators is to use quasi-random numbers, which are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that minimizes the discrepancy. Quasi-random numbers are more evenly distributed than pseudo-random numbers, and can reduce the variance of Monte Carlo methods.
- Quasi-random numbers are also known as low-discrepancy numbers, and some of the common methods for generating them are the Halton sequence, the Sobol sequence, and the Niederreiter sequence. Quasi-random numbers have some advantages over pseudo-random numbers, such as faster convergence, better accuracy, and easier parallelization. However, quasi-random numbers are not suitable for applications that require unpredictability, such as cryptography.
