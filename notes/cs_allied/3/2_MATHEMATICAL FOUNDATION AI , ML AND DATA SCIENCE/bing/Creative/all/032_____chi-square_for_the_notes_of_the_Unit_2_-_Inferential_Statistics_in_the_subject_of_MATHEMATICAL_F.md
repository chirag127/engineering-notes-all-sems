# Chi-square test

- A chi-square test is a statistical hypothesis test used to compare observed frequencies of categorical variables with expected frequencies based on some null hypothesis .
- A chi-square test can be used to test whether two categorical variables are independent, whether a single categorical variable follows a specified distribution, or whether a set of observed counts fits a theoretical model  .
- A chi-square test uses the chi-square statistic, which measures the discrepancy between the observed and expected frequencies. The chi-square statistic follows a chi-square distribution with a certain number of degrees of freedom, which depends on the number of categories and the type of test  .
- A chi-square test can be performed by calculating the chi-square statistic, comparing it with a critical value from the chi-square distribution table, and drawing a conclusion based on the level of significance and the p-value .
- A chi-square test can be one-tailed or two-tailed, depending on whether the alternative hypothesis is directional or non-directional .
- A chi-square test has some assumptions, such as the independence of observations, the randomness of sampling, and the adequacy of sample size. Violating these assumptions may affect the validity and reliability of the test  .
- A chi-square test can be applied to various fields of study, such as biology, psychology, sociology, medicine, and education, where categorical data are often encountered  .
