### Convergence for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI, ML AND DATA SCIENCE

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that simulates randomness, but are not truly random.
- A pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) is a function that takes a short random seed and outputs a longer bit sequence that “appears random.”
- A common PRNG technique is called the linear congruential method. The pseudo-random numbers are generated using following equation:

```
Xn+1 = (aXn + c) mod m
```

where,

- Xn is the previous pseudo number
- a is a constant multiplier
- c is a constant increment
- m is a constant modulus
- X0 is the initial value of the sequence known as the seed

- The choice of a, c, m, and X0 affects the quality and period of the pseudo-random sequence. For a good PRNG, the period should be almost equal to m, and the sequence should pass various randomness tests.
- Convergence is a property of a sequence of numbers that indicates whether it approaches a limit or not. A sequence converges if the distance between successive terms becomes arbitrarily small as the index increases.
- Convergence is important for PRNGs because it affects the accuracy and reliability of the applications that use them, such as Monte Carlo methods, cryptography, simulation, etc.
- A PRNG should have a high convergence rate, meaning that it should produce a sequence that is close to the true random distribution in a short time. A low convergence rate means that the PRNG takes a long time to produce a good approximation of randomness, or may never achieve it at all.
- One way to measure the convergence rate of a PRNG is to use the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, which compares the empirical cumulative distribution function (ECDF) of the pseudo-random sequence with the theoretical cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the true random distribution. The test computes the maximum difference between the two functions, and rejects the null hypothesis that they are the same if the difference is larger than a critical value.
- Another way to measure the convergence rate of a PRNG is to use the chi-squared test, which compares the observed frequencies of the pseudo-random numbers in different bins with the expected frequencies under the true random distribution. The test computes the sum of the squared differences between the observed and expected frequencies, and rejects the null hypothesis that they are the same if the sum is larger than a critical value.
- Quasi-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that produces a low-discrepancy sequence, meaning that the numbers are evenly distributed over a given range. Quasi-random numbers are not random, but they can avoid clustering and can speed up convergence in some applications.
- Quasi-random numbers are more uniform than pseudo-random numbers and may be more appropriate for applications that require greater regularity, such as numerical integration, optimization, etc. However, quasi-random numbers are generally too uniform to pass randomness tests and may not be suitable for applications that require greater randomness, such as cryptography, simulation, etc.
