### Change of Basis

- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the whole space.
- A vector can be represented as a linear combination of the basis vectors using some coefficients called coordinates or components.
- The same vector can have different coordinates depending on the choice of basis.
- Changing the basis means finding the coordinates of the same vector in a different basis.
- To change the basis, we need to find a matrix that relates the coordinates of a vector in the old basis to the coordinates of the same vector in the new basis. This matrix is called the change of basis matrix.
- The change of basis matrix can be found by writing the new basis vectors as linear combinations of the old basis vectors, and then arranging the coefficients in a matrix.
- Alternatively, the change of basis matrix can be found by writing the old basis vectors as linear combinations of the new basis vectors, and then taking the inverse of the matrix of coefficients.
- The change of basis matrix can be used to transform any vector from the old basis to the new basis by multiplying the vector by the matrix.
- The change of basis matrix can also be used to transform a linear transformation from the old basis to the new basis by multiplying the matrix of the linear transformation by the change of basis matrix on both sides.
- Changing the basis can simplify the representation of a vector or a linear transformation, or make it more suitable for a certain application.
