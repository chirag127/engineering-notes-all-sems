### Orthonormal Basis

- A set of vectors is **orthonormal** if each vector is a **unit vector** (length or norm is equal to 1) and all vectors in the set are **orthogonal** to each other.
- That is, the vectors satisfy the following properties:
  - For any vector **v** in the set, ||**v**|| = 1
  - For any two distinct vectors **u** and **v** in the set, <**u**, **v**> = 0
- A **basis** is a set of linearly independent vectors that span a vector space.
- An **orthonormal basis** is a basis that is also orthonormal.
- The simplest example of an orthonormal basis is the **standard basis** for Euclidean space, which consists of the unit vectors along each coordinate axis.
- Every finite-dimensional inner product space has an orthonormal basis, which may be obtained from an arbitrary basis using the **Gram–Schmidt process**.
- An orthonormal basis has some advantages over a general basis, such as:
  - It is easier to compute the coordinates of a vector with respect to an orthonormal basis, since they are simply the inner products of the vector with the basis vectors.
  - It preserves the inner product and the norm of vectors, since <**u**, **v**> = <**u**', **v**'> and ||**u**|| = ||**u**'||, where **u**' and **v**' are the coordinates of **u** and **v** with respect to the orthonormal basis.
  - It simplifies the expression of the **orthogonal projection** of a vector onto a subspace, since it is just the sum of the coordinates of the vector times the basis vectors.
