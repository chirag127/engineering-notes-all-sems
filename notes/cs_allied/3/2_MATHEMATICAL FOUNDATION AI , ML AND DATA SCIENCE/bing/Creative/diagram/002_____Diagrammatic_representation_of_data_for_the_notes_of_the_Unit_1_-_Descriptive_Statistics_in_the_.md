### Diagrammatic representation of data

- Diagrammatic representation of data is a method of presenting numerical data in a visual form, such as graphs, charts, maps, etc.
- Diagrammatic representation of data helps to summarize, compare, and analyze data in an effective and efficient way.
- Diagrammatic representation of data can also help to reveal patterns, trends, outliers, and relationships among data that may not be obvious from numerical tables or calculations.
- Diagrammatic representation of data can be classified into two types: univariate and multivariate.
  - Univariate diagrams are used to display the distribution of a single variable, such as frequency distribution, histogram, pie chart, etc.
  - Multivariate diagrams are used to display the relationship between two or more variables, such as scatter plot, line graph, bar chart, etc.
- Diagrammatic representation of data should follow some general principles, such as:
  - Choosing an appropriate type of diagram that suits the purpose and nature of the data.
  - Labeling the axes, titles, legends, and units clearly and accurately.
  - Scaling the axes and the data points proportionally and consistently.
  - Avoiding unnecessary clutter, distortion, or misleading effects that may confuse or misinterpret the data.
  - Using colors, shapes, symbols, and patterns to enhance the visual appeal and readability of the diagram.
