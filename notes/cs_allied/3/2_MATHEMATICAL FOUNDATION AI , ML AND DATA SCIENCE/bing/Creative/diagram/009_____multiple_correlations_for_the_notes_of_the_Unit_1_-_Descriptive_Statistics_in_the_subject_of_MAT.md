### Multiple Correlations

- Multiple correlation is a statistical technique that predicts values of one variable on the basis of two or more other variables .
- Multiple correlation coefficient (R) is a measure of the strength and direction of the linear relationship between one dependent variable (Y) and a set of independent variables (X1, X2, ..., Xk).
- R can range from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative linear relationship, 0 indicates no linear relationship, and 1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship.
- R can be calculated using the formula:

![R = \sqrt{\frac{SSR}{SST}}](https://latex.codecogs.com/png.latex?R%20%3D%20%5Csqrt%7B%5Cfrac%7BSSR%7D%7BSST%7D%7D)

where SSR is the sum of squares due to regression and SST is the total sum of squares.

- R can also be interpreted as the square root of the coefficient of determination (R^2^), which is the proportion of the variance in Y that is explained by the regression model.

![R^2 = \frac{SSR}{SST}](https://latex.codecogs.com/png.latex?R%5E2%20%3D%20%5Cfrac%7BSSR%7D%7BSST%7D)

- R^2^ can range from 0 to 1, where 0 indicates that the regression model does not explain any variation in Y, and 1 indicates that the regression model explains all the variation in Y.
- Multiple correlation can be used to test the significance of the overall regression model, as well as the significance of each individual independent variable.
- Multiple correlation can also be used to assess the assumptions of the regression model, such as linearity, normality, homoscedasticity, and independence of errors.
- Multiple correlation can be extended to more than one dependent variable, resulting in canonical correlation analysis, which measures the maximum correlation between linear combinations of two sets of variables.
