### Symmetric Matrices and Orthogonal Diagonalization

- A symmetric matrix is a square matrix that is equal to its transpose, i.e., A = A^T^.
- A symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors, i.e., the eigenvectors are perpendicular to each other and have unit length.
- An orthogonal matrix is a square matrix whose columns (or rows) are orthogonal eigenvectors, i.e., Q^T^ = Q^-1^.
- An orthogonal diagonalization of a symmetric matrix is a diagonalization by means of an orthogonal change of coordinates, i.e., A = QDQ^T^, where Q is an orthogonal matrix and D is a diagonal matrix.
- The diagonal entries of D are the eigenvalues of A, and the columns of Q are the corresponding eigenvectors of A.
- The orthogonal diagonalization of a symmetric matrix is unique up to the order and sign of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
- The orthogonal diagonalization of a symmetric matrix can be used to simplify quadratic forms, i.e., expressions of the form x^T^Ax, where x is a vector and A is a symmetric matrix.
- The orthogonal diagonalization of a symmetric matrix can also be used to find the principal axes and the principal values of a quadratic form, i.e., the directions and the values of the maximum and minimum values of the quadratic form.
