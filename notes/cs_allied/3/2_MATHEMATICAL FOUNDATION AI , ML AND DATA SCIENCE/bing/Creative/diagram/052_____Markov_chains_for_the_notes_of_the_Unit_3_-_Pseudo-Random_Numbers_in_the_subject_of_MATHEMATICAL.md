### Markov chains

- A Markov chain is a type of Markov process that has either a discrete state space or a discrete index set (often representing time) .
- A Markov process is a stochastic process that satisfies the Markov property, which means that the future state of the process depends only on the present state, and not on the past states .
- A Markov chain can be represented by a directed graph, where the nodes are the states and the edges are labeled with the probabilities of transitioning from one state to another .
- A Markov chain can also be described by a transition matrix, which is a square matrix that contains the probabilities of moving from one state to another in each row .
- A Markov chain is said to be irreducible if it is possible to reach any state from any other state in a finite number of steps .
- A Markov chain is said to be recurrent if every state has a positive probability of being visited infinitely often .
- A Markov chain is said to be ergodic if it is irreducible and recurrent, and has a unique stationary distribution, which is a probability vector that does not change under the action of the transition matrix .
- A Markov chain is said to be periodic if there exists a positive integer k such that the probability of returning to a state is zero unless the number of steps is a multiple of k .
- A Markov chain is said to be aperiodic if it is not periodic, or equivalently, if the greatest common divisor of the lengths of the cycles in the graph is one .

- One example of a Markov chain is the weather model, where the states are sunny, cloudy, and rainy, and the probabilities of changing from one state to another are given by a transition matrix .
- Another example of a Markov chain is the board game "Chutes and Ladders", where the states are the positions on the board, and the probabilities of moving from one state to another are determined by the dice and the presence of chutes and ladders .
