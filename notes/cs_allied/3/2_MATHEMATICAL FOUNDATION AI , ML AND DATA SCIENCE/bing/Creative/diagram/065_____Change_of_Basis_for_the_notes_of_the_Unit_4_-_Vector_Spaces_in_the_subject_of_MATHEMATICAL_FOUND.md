### Change of Basis

- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the space.
- Any vector in the space can be written as a unique linear combination of the basis vectors.
- The coefficients of the linear combination are called the coordinates or components of the vector with respect to the basis.
- Different bases can be used to represent the same vector space, as long as they satisfy the conditions of linear independence and spanning.
- Changing the basis of a vector space means finding a new set of basis vectors that are related to the original ones by a linear transformation.
- The linear transformation that maps one basis to another is called a change of basis matrix.
- The change of basis matrix is invertible and its inverse is the matrix that maps the new basis to the old one.
- To find the change of basis matrix from basis B to basis C, we can write each vector in B as a linear combination of the vectors in C, and then form a matrix with the coefficients as columns.
- To find the coordinates of a vector v with respect to a new basis C, we can multiply the change of basis matrix from the old basis B to C by the coordinates of v with respect to B.
- To find the vector v from its coordinates with respect to a new basis C, we can multiply the inverse of the change of basis matrix from B to C by the coordinates of v with respect to C.
