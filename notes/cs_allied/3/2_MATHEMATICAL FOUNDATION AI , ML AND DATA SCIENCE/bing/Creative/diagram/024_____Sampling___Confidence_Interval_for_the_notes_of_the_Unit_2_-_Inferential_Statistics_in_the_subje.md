### Sampling & Confidence Interval

- Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of the population for analysis and inference.
- Sampling can be done in different ways, such as random sampling, stratified sampling, cluster sampling, systematic sampling, etc.
- Sampling can reduce the cost and time of data collection and analysis, and can also increase the accuracy and precision of the results.
- Sampling can also introduce some errors and biases, such as sampling error, non-sampling error, undercoverage, nonresponse, etc.
- Sampling error is the difference between the sample statistic and the population parameter due to random variation.
- Non-sampling error is the error that arises from other sources, such as measurement error, data processing error, response error, etc.
- Undercoverage is the bias that occurs when some groups of the population are not adequately represented in the sample.
- Nonresponse is the bias that occurs when some units of the sample do not respond or provide incomplete or inaccurate information.
- To measure the reliability and variability of the sample statistic, we can use the concept of confidence interval.
- Confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the population parameter with a certain level of confidence.
- Confidence interval can be calculated using the formula:

`CI = sample statistic ± margin of error`

- Margin of error is the maximum possible difference between the sample statistic and the population parameter for a given confidence level.
- Margin of error can be calculated using the formula:

`ME = critical value × standard error`

- Critical value is the value that corresponds to the confidence level in the sampling distribution of the sample statistic.
- Standard error is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample statistic.
- Standard error can be estimated using the formula:

`SE = sample standard deviation / square root of sample size`

- The confidence level is the probability that the confidence interval contains the population parameter.
- The confidence level is usually expressed as a percentage, such as 90%, 95%, 99%, etc.
- The confidence level is related to the significance level, which is the probability that the confidence interval does not contain the population parameter.
- The significance level is usually denoted by alpha (α), and is equal to 1 minus the confidence level.
- The confidence interval can be interpreted as follows:

`We are (confidence level)% confident that the (population parameter) is between (lower limit) and (upper limit).`

- The confidence interval can be used to test hypotheses, estimate parameters, and compare groups.
