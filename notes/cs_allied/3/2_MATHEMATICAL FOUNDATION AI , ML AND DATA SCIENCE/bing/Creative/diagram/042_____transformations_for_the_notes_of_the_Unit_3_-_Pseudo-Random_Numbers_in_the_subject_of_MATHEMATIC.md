### Transformations for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm that mimics the properties of random numbers.
- Pseudo-random numbers are useful for applications such as simulation, cryptography, gaming, and statistical analysis.
- There are different methods for generating pseudo-random numbers from various probability distributions, such as uniform, normal, exponential, etc.
- Some of the common methods are:

  - **Linear congruential generator (LCG)**: This is the oldest and simplest method, which uses the recurrence relation Xn+1 = (aXn + c) mod m, where Xn is the nth pseudo-random number, a, c, and m are constants, and mod is the modulo operation. The choice of the constants affects the quality and period of the generated sequence.
  - **Inverse transform sampling**: This is a method that uses the inverse of the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the desired distribution to transform a uniform pseudo-random number into a pseudo-random number from that distribution. The basic idea is that if U is a uniform random variable on [0, 1], then F^-1(U) is a random variable with CDF F, where F^-1 is the inverse function of F.
  - **Summation and transformation**: This is a method that uses the properties of some distributions to generate pseudo-random numbers from them by summing or transforming other pseudo-random numbers. For example, if X1, X2, ..., Xn are independent and identically distributed (iid) exponential random variables with mean 1, then their sum Y = X1 + X2 + ... + Xn is a gamma random variable with shape parameter n and scale parameter 1. Similarly, if Z is a standard normal random variable, then e^Z is a log-normal random variable.
