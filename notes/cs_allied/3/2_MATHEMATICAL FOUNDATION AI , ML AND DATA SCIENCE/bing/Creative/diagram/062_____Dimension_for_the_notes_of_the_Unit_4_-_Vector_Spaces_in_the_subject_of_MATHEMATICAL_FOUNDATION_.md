### Dimension

- The dimension of a vector space is the number of linearly independent vectors that form a basis for the space.
- A basis for a vector space is a set of vectors that is linearly independent and spans the space, meaning that every vector in the space can be written as a linear combination of the basis vectors.
- The dimension of a vector space is unique and does not depend on the choice of basis.
- The dimension of a vector space can be finite or infinite, depending on the size of the space and the field of scalars.
- Some examples of vector spaces and their dimensions are:

  - The zero vector space, which contains only the zero vector, has dimension zero.
  - The real line R, which is a vector space over itself, has dimension one. A basis for R is {1}.
  - The complex plane C, which is a vector space over R, has dimension two. A basis for C is {1, i}, where i is the imaginary unit.
  - The space of polynomials of degree at most n over a field F, denoted by P_n(F), has dimension n + 1. A basis for P_n(F) is {1, x, x^2, ..., x^n}.
  - The space of m x n matrices over a field F, denoted by M_mn(F), has dimension mn. A basis for M_mn(F) is {E_ij}, where E_ij is the matrix with 1 in the (i, j) entry and 0 elsewhere.
  - The space of continuous functions from R to R, denoted by C(R), has infinite dimension. A basis for C(R) does not exist, but a spanning set is {e^x, sin x, cos x, ...}.
