### Change of Basis

- A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the space.
- Given a basis for a vector space, any vector in the space can be written uniquely as a linear combination of the basis vectors.
- The coefficients of this linear combination are called the coordinates of the vector with respect to the basis.
- If we have two different bases for a vector space, we can express the same vector in terms of each basis, resulting in two different coordinate representations of the same vector.
- The change of basis matrix is a matrix that allows us to convert the coordinates of a vector from one basis to another.
- To find the change of basis matrix from basis B to basis C, we express each vector in basis B as a linear combination of the vectors in basis C, and the coefficients of these linear combinations form the columns of the change of basis matrix.
- The change of basis matrix is invertible, and its inverse is the change of basis matrix from basis C to basis B.
- If we have a linear transformation between two vector spaces, and we know the matrix of the transformation with respect to some bases of the two spaces, we can use the change of basis matrices to find the matrix of the transformation with respect to different bases of the two spaces.
- The process of changing the basis of a vector space is called a change of coordinates.
- A change of coordinates can be useful for simplifying calculations or for gaining a better understanding of the geometric properties of a vector space or linear transformation.
