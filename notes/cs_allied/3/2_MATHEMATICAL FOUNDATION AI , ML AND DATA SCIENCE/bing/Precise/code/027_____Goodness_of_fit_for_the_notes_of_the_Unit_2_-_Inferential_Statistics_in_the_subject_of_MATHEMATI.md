### Goodness of fit

Goodness of fit is a statistical measure that is used to determine how well a model fits a set of observed data. It is commonly used in inferential statistics, which is a branch of statistics that deals with making conclusions about a population based on a sample of data.

Here are some key points to remember about goodness of fit:

1. Goodness of fit tests are used to compare observed data with expected data based on a particular model or hypothesis.
2. The chi-squared test is a commonly used goodness of fit test. It compares the observed frequencies of different categories with the expected frequencies based on the model or hypothesis.
3. The p-value is used to determine the significance of the test. A low p-value indicates that the observed data is unlikely to have occurred by chance, given the model or hypothesis.
4. The degrees of freedom for a chi-squared test is calculated as the number of categories minus one.
5. Goodness of fit tests can be used for both discrete and continuous data.
6. It is important to ensure that the assumptions of the test are met before conducting a goodness of fit test.

These are some of the key points to remember about goodness of fit in the context of inferential statistics. It is an important concept to understand when studying mathematical foundations of AI, ML, and data science.
