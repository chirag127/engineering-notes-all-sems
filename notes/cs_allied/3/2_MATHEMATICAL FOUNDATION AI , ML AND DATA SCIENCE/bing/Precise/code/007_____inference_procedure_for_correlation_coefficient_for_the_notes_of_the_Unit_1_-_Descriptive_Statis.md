### Inference Procedure for Correlation Coefficient

1. The correlation coefficient is a measure of the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two variables.
2. The correlation coefficient is denoted by the letter 'r' and can take on values between -1 and 1.
3. A correlation coefficient of -1 indicates a perfect negative linear relationship, while a correlation coefficient of 1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship.
4. A correlation coefficient of 0 indicates no linear relationship between the two variables.
5. To test the significance of the correlation coefficient, a t-test can be used.
6. The null hypothesis for the t-test is that the population correlation coefficient is equal to 0, indicating no linear relationship between the two variables.
7. The alternative hypothesis is that the population correlation coefficient is not equal to 0, indicating a linear relationship between the two variables.
8. The test statistic for the t-test is calculated as t = r * sqrt(n-2) / sqrt(1-r^2), where n is the sample size.
9. The degrees of freedom for the t-test is n-2.
10. The p-value for the t-test can be calculated using a t-distribution table or a statistical software package.
11. If the p-value is less than the chosen significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected and it can be concluded that there is a significant linear relationship between the two variables.
12. If the p-value is greater than the chosen significance level, the null hypothesis is not rejected and it cannot be concluded that there is a significant linear relationship between the two variables.

This is a brief overview of the inference procedure for the correlation coefficient. It is important to note that this procedure is only applicable for testing the significance of the linear relationship between two variables. Other statistical tests may be necessary for testing the significance of non-linear relationships or relationships between more than two variables.
