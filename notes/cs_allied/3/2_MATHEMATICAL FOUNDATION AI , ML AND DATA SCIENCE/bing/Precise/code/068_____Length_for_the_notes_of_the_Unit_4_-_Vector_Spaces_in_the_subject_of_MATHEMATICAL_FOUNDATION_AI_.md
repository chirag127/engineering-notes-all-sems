1. Vector spaces are a fundamental concept in linear algebra.
2. A vector space is a set of vectors that can be added together and multiplied by scalars to produce new vectors.
3. The set of all linear combinations of a set of vectors is called the span of the set.
4. A basis for a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that spans the space.
5. The dimension of a vector space is the number of vectors in a basis for the space.
6. Subspaces are subsets of a vector space that are themselves vector spaces.
7. The intersection of two subspaces is also a subspace.
8. The sum of two subspaces is the smallest subspace containing both subspaces.
9. Linear transformations are functions that preserve the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication.
10. The kernel and range of a linear transformation are subspaces of the domain and codomain, respectively.
11. The rank and nullity of a linear transformation are the dimensions of its range and kernel, respectively.
12. The rank-nullity theorem states that the sum of the rank and nullity of a linear transformation is equal to the dimension of its domain.
13. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors are important concepts in the study of linear transformations.
14. An eigenvector of a linear transformation is a nonzero vector that, when acted on by the transformation, is scaled by a scalar factor called an eigenvalue.
15. The characteristic polynomial of a linear transformation is a polynomial whose roots are the eigenvalues of the transformation.
16. The determinant of a square matrix is a scalar value that encodes certain properties of the associated linear transformation.
17. The trace of a square matrix is the sum of its diagonal entries and is equal to the sum of its eigenvalues.
18. The Cayley-Hamilton theorem states that a square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation.
19. The Jordan canonical form is a matrix representation of a linear transformation that is particularly useful for studying the transformation's properties.
20. Inner products are functions that assign a scalar value to a pair of vectors in a vector space.
21. An inner product space is a vector space equipped with an inner product.
22. The norm of a vector in an inner product space is the square root of the inner product of the vector with itself.
23. Orthogonal vectors are vectors whose inner product is zero.
24. An orthonormal basis for an inner product space is a basis consisting of orthogonal unit vectors.
25. The Gram-Schmidt process is an algorithm for constructing an orthonormal basis for a subspace of an inner product space.
26. Orthogonal projections are linear transformations that project vectors onto a subspace along directions orthogonal to the subspace.
27. The least squares method is a technique for finding the best-fit line or plane to a set of data points by minimizing the sum of the squared residuals.
28. Singular value decomposition is a factorization of a matrix into the product of three matrices, one of which is diagonal.
29. The singular values of a matrix are the square roots of the eigenvalues of the matrix's transpose times the matrix.
30. The pseudoinverse of a matrix is a matrix that generalizes the notion of an inverse to non-square matrices.
31. The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse is a particular type of pseudoinverse that can be computed using singular value decomposition.
32. Principal component analysis is a technique for reducing the dimensionality of a data set by projecting it onto a lower-dimensional subspace.
33. The principal components of a data set are the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix of the data, ordered by the magnitude of their corresponding eigenvalues.
34. The k-means clustering algorithm is a method for partitioning a data set into k clusters by minimizing the sum of the squared distances between the data points and the centroids of their respective clusters.
35. The k-means algorithm is an iterative process that alternates between assigning data points to the nearest cluster and updating the cluster centroids to be the mean of the data points assigned to the cluster.
36. The k-means algorithm is not guaranteed to find the global optimum and is sensitive to the initial placement of the cluster centroids.
37. The k-means++ algorithm is a modification of the k-means algorithm that uses a more sophisticated method for initializing the cluster centroids.
38. The expectation-maximization algorithm is a generalization of the k-means algorithm that can be used to fit mixture models to data.
