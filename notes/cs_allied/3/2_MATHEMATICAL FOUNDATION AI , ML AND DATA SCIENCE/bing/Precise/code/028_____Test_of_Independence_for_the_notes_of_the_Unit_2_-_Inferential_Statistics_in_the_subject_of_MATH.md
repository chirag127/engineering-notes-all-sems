### Test of Independence

The test of independence is a statistical test used to determine whether two categorical variables are independent of each other or not. It is commonly used in inferential statistics as a part of the Chi-Square test.

Here are the key points to remember about the test of independence:

1. The test of independence is used to determine if there is a relationship between two categorical variables.
2. The null hypothesis for the test of independence is that the two variables are independent, meaning there is no relationship between them.
3. The alternative hypothesis is that the two variables are not independent, meaning there is a relationship between them.
4. The test statistic for the test of independence is calculated using the Chi-Square formula.
5. The degrees of freedom for the test of independence is calculated as (number of rows - 1) * (number of columns - 1).
6. The p-value for the test of independence is calculated using the Chi-Square distribution.
7. If the p-value is less than the chosen significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected, and it is concluded that there is a relationship between the two variables.
8. If the p-value is greater than the chosen significance level, the null hypothesis is not rejected, and it is concluded that there is no evidence of a relationship between the two variables.
