### Measures of Skewness and Kurtosis

Skewness and kurtosis are two commonly used measures in descriptive statistics that help describe the shape of a data distribution.

#### Skewness
Skewness measures the degree of asymmetry of a distribution. A distribution is said to be skewed if it is not symmetric, i.e., if it is "lopsided". There are two types of skewness: positive skewness and negative skewness.

- **Positive skewness:** If the distribution has a longer tail to the right, it is said to be positively skewed. In a positively skewed distribution, the mean is typically greater than the median.

- **Negative skewness:** If the distribution has a longer tail to the left, it is said to be negatively skewed. In a negatively skewed distribution, the mean is typically less than the median.

There are several methods to measure skewness, including the Pearson's first and second coefficients of skewness, and the moment coefficient of skewness.

#### Kurtosis
Kurtosis measures the "tailedness" of a distribution, i.e., the relative concentration of scores in the center, the upper and lower ends (tails), and the shoulders (between the center and the tails) of the distribution.

- **Mesokurtic:** A distribution is said to be mesokurtic if it has a kurtosis value similar to that of a normal distribution. A normal distribution has a kurtosis value of 3.

- **Leptokurtic:** A distribution is said to be leptokurtic if it has a kurtosis value greater than 3. Leptokurtic distributions have more scores in the tails and the center, and fewer scores in the shoulders, than a normal distribution.

- **Platykurtic:** A distribution is said to be platykurtic if it has a kurtosis value less than 3. Platykurtic distributions have fewer scores in the tails and the center, and more scores in the shoulders, than a normal distribution.

There are several methods to measure kurtosis, including the Pearson's first and second coefficients of kurtosis, and the moment coefficient of kurtosis.

These measures of skewness and kurtosis can be useful in understanding the distribution of data in the context of descriptive statistics, particularly in the subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML, and Data Science. They can provide valuable insights into the shape of the data distribution and can help inform further analysis and modeling.
