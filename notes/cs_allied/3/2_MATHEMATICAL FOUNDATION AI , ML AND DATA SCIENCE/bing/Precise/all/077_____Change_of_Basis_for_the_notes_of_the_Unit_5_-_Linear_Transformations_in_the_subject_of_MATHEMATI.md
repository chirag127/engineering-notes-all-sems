### Change of Basis

A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that spans the space. Given a basis for a vector space, any vector in the space can be expressed uniquely as a linear combination of the basis vectors. The coefficients of this linear combination are called the coordinates of the vector with respect to the basis.

Sometimes it is useful to change the basis of a vector space. This can be done by expressing the vectors of the new basis in terms of the old basis, and then finding the matrix that transforms the coordinates of a vector with respect to the old basis into the coordinates of the same vector with respect to the new basis. This matrix is called the change of basis matrix.

The change of basis matrix is useful for many applications, including diagonalizing a matrix, finding the eigenvectors of a matrix, and changing the coordinate system in which a linear transformation is represented.

Here are the steps to find the change of basis matrix:
1. Express the vectors of the new basis in terms of the old basis.
2. Write the coordinates of the new basis vectors as columns of a matrix.
3. The resulting matrix is the change of basis matrix.

It is important to note that the change of basis matrix is invertible, and its inverse is the matrix that changes the coordinates of a vector with respect to the new basis back into the coordinates of the same vector with respect to the old basis. This means that the change of basis matrix and its inverse can be used to switch back and forth between the two bases.

In summary, the change of basis matrix is a powerful tool for working with vector spaces and linear transformations. It allows us to change the coordinate system in which we represent vectors and transformations, which can simplify calculations and provide new insights into the structure of the space.
