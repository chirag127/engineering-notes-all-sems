# Discrete and Continuous Probability Distributions

Probability distributions are used to describe the likelihood of different outcomes in an experiment. There are two types of probability distributions: discrete and continuous.

## Discrete Probability Distributions

A discrete probability distribution is used to describe the probability of outcomes in an experiment where the outcomes are discrete, meaning they can be counted. For example, the number of heads that come up when flipping a coin three times is a discrete outcome, because it can only be 0, 1, 2, or 3.

The most common discrete probability distribution is the binomial distribution. This distribution is used to model the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, where the probability of success is the same for each trial.

## Continuous Probability Distributions

A continuous probability distribution is used to describe the probability of outcomes in an experiment where the outcomes are continuous, meaning they can take on any value within a certain range. For example, the time it takes for a light bulb to burn out is a continuous outcome, because it can take on any value within a certain range.

The most common continuous probability distribution is the normal distribution, also known as the Gaussian distribution. This distribution is used to model many natural phenomena, such as the heights of people or the scores on a test.
