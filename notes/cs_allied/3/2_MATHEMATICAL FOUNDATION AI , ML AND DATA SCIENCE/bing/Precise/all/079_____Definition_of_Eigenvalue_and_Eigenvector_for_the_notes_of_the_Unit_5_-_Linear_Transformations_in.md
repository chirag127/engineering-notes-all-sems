### Definition of Eigenvalue and Eigenvector

In the context of Unit 5 - Linear Transformations in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI, ML AND DATA SCIENCE, an eigenvalue is a scalar value that, when multiplied by a vector, results in a linear transformation of that vector. The vector that is transformed is called an eigenvector.

More formally, let `A` be an `n x n` matrix and `x` be a non-zero vector in `R^n`. If there exists a scalar `λ` such that `Ax = λx`, then `λ` is called an eigenvalue of `A` and `x` is called an eigenvector of `A` corresponding to `λ`.

Some key points to remember about eigenvalues and eigenvectors are:

1. An `n x n` matrix has `n` eigenvalues, some of which may be repeated.
2. The sum of the eigenvalues of a matrix is equal to its trace (the sum of the diagonal elements).
3. The product of the eigenvalues of a matrix is equal to its determinant.
4. Eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent.
5. If a matrix is diagonalizable, then its eigenvectors form a basis for `R^n`.
