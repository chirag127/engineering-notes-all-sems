# Measures of Probability

Probability is a branch of mathematics that deals with the likelihood of events occurring. It is used to quantify the uncertainty associated with an event. In the context of Descriptive Statistics, probability is used to make predictions about the likelihood of events based on data.

Some common measures of probability include:

1. **Classical Probability**: This measure of probability is based on the assumption that all outcomes of an experiment are equally likely. The probability of an event is calculated by dividing the number of favorable outcomes by the total number of possible outcomes.

2. **Empirical Probability**: This measure of probability is based on the relative frequency of an event occurring in a large number of trials. The probability of an event is calculated by dividing the number of times the event occurred by the total number of trials.

3. **Subjective Probability**: This measure of probability is based on personal judgment or belief. It is not based on any mathematical calculation, but rather on an individual's assessment of the likelihood of an event occurring.

These are some of the common measures of probability used in Descriptive Statistics. Understanding these measures can help in making predictions and decisions based on data.
