## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

- Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that are generated by a deterministic algorithm, but appear to be random.
- These numbers are not truly random, but they can be used in many applications where true randomness is not required.
- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that generate a sequence of numbers that are statistically random.
- PRNGs are initialized with a seed value, which determines the sequence of numbers that will be generated.
- The same seed value will always produce the same sequence of numbers.
- PRNGs are commonly used in computer simulations, cryptography, and other applications where randomness is required.
- There are many different algorithms for generating pseudo-random numbers, including linear congruential generators, lagged Fibonacci generators, and Mersenne Twister.
- The quality of a PRNG is determined by its statistical properties, such as its period, distribution, and independence.
- It is important to use a high-quality PRNG in applications where randomness is critical, such as in cryptography.
