# Triangle Inequality

The triangle inequality is a fundamental concept in the study of vector spaces, and is an important topic in Unit 4 - Vector Spaces of the subject Mathematical Foundation AI, ML and Data Science.

The triangle inequality states that for any vectors **u** and **v** in a vector space, the following inequality holds:

`||u + v|| ≤ ||u|| + ||v||`

where `||u||` and `||v||` denote the norms of the vectors **u** and **v**, respectively.

This inequality can be visualized geometrically by considering the vectors **u** and **v** as sides of a triangle, with the vector **u + v** representing the third side. The triangle inequality states that the length of any side of a triangle must be less than or equal to the sum of the lengths of the other two sides.

The triangle inequality has several important consequences in the study of vector spaces. For example, it can be used to prove the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, which is a fundamental result in the study of inner product spaces.

In summary, the triangle inequality is an important concept in the study of vector spaces, with far-reaching consequences in the field of mathematical foundations of AI, ML, and data science. It is a key topic to understand and master for students studying this subject.
