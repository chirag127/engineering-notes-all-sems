# Change of Basis

In the context of vector spaces, a basis is a set of linearly independent vectors that spans the entire space. Given a basis, any vector in the space can be expressed as a unique linear combination of the basis vectors.

When working with vector spaces, it is often useful to change the basis. This can be done by expressing the vectors of the new basis in terms of the old basis, and then using this information to transform the coordinates of any vector in the space.

The process of changing the basis involves the use of a transformation matrix. This matrix is constructed by taking the vectors of the new basis, expressing them in terms of the old basis, and then arranging the coefficients of these expressions as columns in the matrix.

Once the transformation matrix has been constructed, it can be used to transform the coordinates of any vector in the space. This is done by multiplying the vector by the transformation matrix.

In summary, the process of changing the basis of a vector space involves the following steps:

1. Express the vectors of the new basis in terms of the old basis.
2. Construct the transformation matrix using the coefficients of these expressions.
3. Use the transformation matrix to transform the coordinates of any vector in the space.

This process can be useful in a variety of contexts, including the simplification of calculations and the analysis of data in different coordinate systems. It is an important concept in the study of vector spaces and has applications in many areas of mathematics, including AI, ML, and data science.
