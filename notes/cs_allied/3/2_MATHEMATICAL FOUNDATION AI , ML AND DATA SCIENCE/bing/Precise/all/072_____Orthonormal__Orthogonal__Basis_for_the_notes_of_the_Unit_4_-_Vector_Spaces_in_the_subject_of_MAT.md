# Orthonormal (Orthogonal) Basis

An orthonormal basis for a vector space is a set of vectors that are both orthogonal and of unit length. This means that any two vectors in the set are perpendicular to each other, and each vector has a length of one.

An orthogonal basis is a set of vectors that are orthogonal, but not necessarily of unit length. To convert an orthogonal basis to an orthonormal basis, each vector in the set can be normalized by dividing it by its length.

An orthonormal basis has several useful properties. For example, it is easy to compute the coordinates of a vector with respect to an orthonormal basis. Given a vector v and an orthonormal basis {e1, e2, ..., en}, the coordinates of v with respect to the basis are given by the dot products of v with each basis vector: (v•e1, v•e2, ..., v•en).

Additionally, the matrix whose columns are the vectors of an orthonormal basis is an orthogonal matrix. This means that its transpose is equal to its inverse. Orthogonal matrices have several useful properties, such as preserving the length of vectors and the angle between vectors when they are multiplied by the matrix.

In summary, an orthonormal basis is a set of vectors that are orthogonal and of unit length. It has several useful properties, such as making it easy to compute the coordinates of a vector with respect to the basis and resulting in an orthogonal matrix when the basis vectors are used as columns of a matrix. An orthogonal basis can be converted to an orthonormal basis by normalizing each vector in the set.
