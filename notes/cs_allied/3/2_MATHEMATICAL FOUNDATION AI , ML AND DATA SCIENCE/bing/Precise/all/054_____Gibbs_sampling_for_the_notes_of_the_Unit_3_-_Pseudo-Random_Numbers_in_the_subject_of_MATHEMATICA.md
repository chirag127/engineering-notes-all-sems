### Gibbs Sampling

Gibbs sampling is a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) technique for obtaining a sequence of observations which are approximated from a specified multivariate probability distribution, when direct sampling is difficult. This method is commonly used in Bayesian statistics, specifically in the estimation of posterior distributions.

Here are some key points to remember about Gibbs sampling:

1. Gibbs sampling is used to generate a sequence of samples from the joint probability distribution of two or more random variables.
2. The idea behind Gibbs sampling is to generate each sample by updating the value of one variable at a time, conditional on the current values of the other variables.
3. Gibbs sampling is particularly useful when the conditional distribution of each variable is known and easy to sample from.
4. The sequence of samples generated by Gibbs sampling can be used to estimate the joint distribution of the variables, as well as marginal distributions and other quantities of interest.
5. Gibbs sampling is a special case of the more general Metropolis-Hastings algorithm.
