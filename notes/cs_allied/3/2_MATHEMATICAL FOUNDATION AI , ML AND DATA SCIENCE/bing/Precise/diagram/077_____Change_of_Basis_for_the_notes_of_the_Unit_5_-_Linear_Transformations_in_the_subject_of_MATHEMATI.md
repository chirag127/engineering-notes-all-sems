### Change of Basis

A basis of a vector space is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the vector space. Given a basis, every vector in the vector space can be expressed uniquely as a linear combination of the basis vectors. The coefficients of this linear combination are called the coordinates of the vector with respect to the basis.

Sometimes, it is useful to change the basis of a vector space. This can be done by expressing the coordinates of each vector with respect to the new basis. The matrix that performs this transformation is called the change of basis matrix.

To find the change of basis matrix from one basis to another, we need to express each vector of the new basis as a linear combination of the vectors of the old basis. The coefficients of these linear combinations form the columns of the change of basis matrix.

Once we have the change of basis matrix, we can use it to transform the coordinates of any vector from the old basis to the new basis. This is done by multiplying the vector of coordinates with respect to the old basis by the change of basis matrix.

In summary, the change of basis is a useful tool for expressing vectors in different bases. It allows us to transform the coordinates of vectors from one basis to another using the change of basis matrix. This can be useful in many applications, including the study of linear transformations.
