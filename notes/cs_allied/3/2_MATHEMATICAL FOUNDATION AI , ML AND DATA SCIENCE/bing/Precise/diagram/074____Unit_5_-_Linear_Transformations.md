## Unit 5 - Linear Transformations

1. **Definition:** A linear transformation is a function between two vector spaces that preserves the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication.
2. **Properties:** A linear transformation has the following properties:
    - For any vectors u and v in the domain, T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v)
    - For any scalar c and vector u in the domain, T(cu) = cT(u)
3. **Matrix representation:** A linear transformation can be represented by a matrix. The columns of the matrix represent the images of the basis vectors under the transformation.
4. **Kernel and range:** The kernel of a linear transformation is the set of all vectors in the domain that are mapped to the zero vector in the codomain. The range is the set of all vectors in the codomain that can be obtained by applying the transformation to a vector in the domain.
5. **One-to-one and onto:** A linear transformation is one-to-one if its kernel is the zero vector. It is onto if its range is the entire codomain.
6. **Inverse:** If a linear transformation is one-to-one and onto, it has an inverse transformation that undoes the effect of the original transformation.
7. **Composition:** The composition of two linear transformations is a linear transformation. The matrix representing the composition is the product of the matrices representing the individual transformations.
8. **Change of basis:** A change of basis is a linear transformation that expresses vectors in one basis in terms of another basis. The matrix representing the change of basis is the transition matrix between the two bases.
