### Chebyshev’s Inequality

Chebyshev’s Inequality is a theorem in probability theory that states that for any given probability distribution, the proportion of observations that fall within k standard deviations of the mean is at least 1 - 1/k^2, where k is any positive real number greater than 1.

In other words, Chebyshev’s Inequality provides a lower bound on the probability that a random variable will fall within a certain distance of its mean.

Some key points to remember about Chebyshev’s Inequality are:

1. It applies to any probability distribution, regardless of its shape.
2. It provides a lower bound, meaning that the actual probability of an observation falling within k standard deviations of the mean may be higher than the value given by the inequality.
3. The value of k must be greater than 1 for the inequality to hold.

Chebyshev’s Inequality is often used in the field of statistics to make inferences about a population based on a sample. It can also be used to derive other statistical results, such as the Weak Law of Large Numbers.
