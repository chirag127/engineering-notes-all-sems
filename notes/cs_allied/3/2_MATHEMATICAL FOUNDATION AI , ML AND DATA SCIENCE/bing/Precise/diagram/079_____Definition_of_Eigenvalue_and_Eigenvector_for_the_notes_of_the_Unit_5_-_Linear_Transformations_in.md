### Definition of Eigenvalue and Eigenvector

In the context of linear transformations, an **eigenvalue** is a scalar value that, when multiplied by a non-zero vector, results in a linear transformation of that vector that is equivalent to scaling the vector by the same scalar value. Mathematically, this can be expressed as:

`Av = λv`

where `A` is a square matrix representing the linear transformation, `v` is a non-zero vector, and `λ` is the eigenvalue.

An **eigenvector** is the non-zero vector `v` in the above equation. It is a vector that, when acted upon by the linear transformation `A`, results in a scaled version of itself, with the scaling factor being the eigenvalue `λ`.

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors have important applications in many areas of mathematics, including matrix diagonalization, differential equations, and principal component analysis in data science. They are also used in machine learning algorithms such as PCA and LDA.

- An eigenvector is the non-zero vector in the above equation, which when acted upon by the linear transformation results in a scaled version of itself, with the scaling factor being the eigenvalue.
