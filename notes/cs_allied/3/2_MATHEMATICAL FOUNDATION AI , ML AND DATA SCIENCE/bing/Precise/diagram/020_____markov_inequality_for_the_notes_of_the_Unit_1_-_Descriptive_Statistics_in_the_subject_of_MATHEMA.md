### Markov Inequality

Markov's inequality is a statement about non-negative random variables. It provides an upper bound on the probability that a non-negative random variable is greater than or equal to a certain value.

Let X be a non-negative random variable and let a > 0 be a positive constant. Then, Markov's inequality states that:

P(X >= a) <= E(X) / a

where E(X) is the expected value of X.

This inequality can be useful in providing bounds on tail probabilities of non-negative random variables. It is often used as a stepping stone to prove other inequalities, such as Chebyshev's inequality.

Some key points to remember about Markov's inequality are:
- It applies only to non-negative random variables.
- It provides an upper bound on the probability that a non-negative random variable is greater than or equal to a certain value.
- The bound is given in terms of the expected value of the random variable and the value of interest.
- It is often used as a stepping stone to prove other inequalities.
