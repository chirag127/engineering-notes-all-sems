### Multivariate Probability Calculations

Multivariate probability calculations are used to determine the likelihood of multiple events occurring simultaneously. These calculations are commonly used in the field of statistics and are an important concept in the study of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data science.

1. **Joint Probability:** Joint probability is the probability of two or more events occurring simultaneously. It is calculated by multiplying the probabilities of each individual event. For example, if the probability of event A occurring is 0.5 and the probability of event B occurring is 0.4, the joint probability of both events occurring is 0.5 * 0.4 = 0.2.

2. **Conditional Probability:** Conditional probability is the probability of an event occurring given that another event has already occurred. It is calculated by dividing the joint probability of the two events by the probability of the event that has already occurred. For example, if the probability of event A occurring is 0.5 and the joint probability of events A and B occurring is 0.2, the conditional probability of event B occurring given that event A has occurred is 0.2 / 0.5 = 0.4.

3. **Bayes' Theorem:** Bayes' theorem is a mathematical formula used to calculate conditional probabilities. It states that the probability of an event occurring given that another event has occurred is equal to the probability of the second event occurring given that the first event has occurred, multiplied by the probability of the first event occurring, divided by the probability of the second event occurring.

4. **Marginal Probability:** Marginal probability is the probability of a single event occurring, regardless of the occurrence of other events. It is calculated by summing the joint probabilities of the event occurring with all possible outcomes of the other events. For example, if the joint probability of events A and B occurring is 0.2 and the joint probability of events A and C occurring is 0.3, the marginal probability of event A occurring is 0.2 + 0.3 = 0.5.

These concepts are important for understanding and calculating probabilities in multivariate systems, and are commonly used in the generation of pseudo-random numbers in the field of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data science.
