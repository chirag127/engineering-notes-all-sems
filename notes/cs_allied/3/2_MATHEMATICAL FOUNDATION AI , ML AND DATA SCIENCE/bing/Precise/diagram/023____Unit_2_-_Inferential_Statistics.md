## Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics

Inferential statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with making conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. It involves using probability theory to estimate population parameters and make decisions based on data.

Some key concepts in inferential statistics include:

1. **Sampling**: The process of selecting a representative sample from a population to make inferences about the population as a whole.
2. **Estimation**: The process of using sample data to estimate population parameters, such as the mean or proportion.
3. **Hypothesis Testing**: A statistical method used to test a claim or hypothesis about a population parameter.
4. **Confidence Intervals**: A range of values that is likely to contain the true population parameter with a certain level of confidence.
5. **Significance Level**: The probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true, also known as the Type I error rate.
6. **Power**: The probability of correctly rejecting the null hypothesis when it is false, also known as the Type II error rate.

Inferential statistics is a powerful tool for making decisions and drawing conclusions from data. It is widely used in fields such as medicine, psychology, and economics to make evidence-based decisions.
