Inner Product

An inner product is a binary operation that takes two vectors and returns a scalar. The inner product is used to define the length and angle between vectors in a vector space.

The most common inner product is the dot product, which is defined as the sum of the product of the corresponding components of the two vectors. The dot product is used to define the length of a vector, as well as the angle between two vectors.

In addition to the dot product, there are several other types of inner products, including the cross product, which is used to define the orientation of vectors in three-dimensional space, and the outer product, which is used to define the product of two vectors as a matrix.

Inner products are used in a variety of applications, including linear algebra, geometry, and machine learning. In linear algebra, inner products are used to define the length and angle between vectors, as well as to define orthogonal and orthonormal bases. In geometry, inner products are used to define the distance between points and the angle between lines. In machine learning, inner products are used to define the similarity between vectors, and they are used in algorithms such as principal component analysis and singular value decomposition.
