## Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers

Pseudo-random numbers are numbers generated by a deterministic algorithm that mimic the properties of random numbers.

1. Definition: Pseudo-random numbers are numbers generated by a deterministic algorithm that mimic the properties of random numbers. The algorithm uses an initial seed value to generate a sequence of numbers that appear to be random but are actually determined by the algorithm.

2. Properties: Pseudo-random numbers have several properties that mimic the properties of random numbers, including uniform distribution, independence, and unpredictability.

3. Generation: Pseudo-random numbers are generated using algorithms such as linear congruential generators, Mersenne Twister, and cryptographic random number generators. The choice of algorithm depends on the desired properties of the pseudo-random numbers, such as speed, quality, and security.

4. Applications: Pseudo-random numbers are used in a wide range of applications, including simulation, cryptography, and machine learning. Pseudo-random numbers are also used in fields such as computer science, engineering, and data science.

5. Limitations: Despite their widespread use, pseudo-random numbers have several limitations, including the determinism of the algorithm, the potential for predictability and bias, and the difficulty of generating truly random numbers.

6. Testing: Pseudo-random numbers are tested using statistical tests, such as the chi-squared test and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, to ensure that they have the desired properties of random numbers.

In conclusion, pseudo-random numbers are numbers generated by a deterministic algorithm that mimic the properties of random numbers. Pseudo-random numbers are used in a wide range of applications and are tested using statistical tests to ensure that they have the desired properties of random numbers. Despite their widespread use, pseudo-random numbers have several limitations, including the determinism of the algorithm and the potential for predictability and bias, but are still an important tool in fields such as simulation, cryptography, and machine learning.
