### Permutations and Randomization Test

- Permutation test: A permutation test is a type of statistical significance test in which the distribution of the test statistic under the null hypothesis is obtained by permuting the labels on the observed data points. It is a non-parametric test that makes minimal assumptions about the underlying distribution.
- Steps:
    1. Calculate the test statistic for the original observed data.
    2. Randomly permute the labels on the data points many times and calculate the test statistic for each permutation.
    3. Count how many times the permuted test statistic is as extreme or more extreme than the original test statistic.
    4. Divide this count by the total number of permutations to get the p-value.
    5. If the p-value is low, the null hypothesis is rejected.
- Advantage: They are exact tests that make minimal assumptions about the distributions of the data.
- Disadvantage: They tend to have low power compared to parametric tests when the parametric assumptions are met.
- Randomization Test: A randomization test is similar to a permutation test, but instead of permuting the labels on the observed data points, the treatment conditions are randomly assigned many times. The distribution of the test statistic under random assignment is used to calculate the p-value. This test is useful when the treatment conditions cannot be naturally ordered/permuted. The steps and advantages/disadvantages are similar to the permutation test.
