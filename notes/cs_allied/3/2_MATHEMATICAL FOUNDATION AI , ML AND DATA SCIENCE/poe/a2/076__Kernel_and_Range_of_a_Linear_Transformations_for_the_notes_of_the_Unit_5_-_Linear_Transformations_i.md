### Kernel and Range of a Linear Transformations

- The kernel of a linear transformation T: V->W is the set of all vectors v in V such that T(v) = 0. It is denoted by ker(T).
- The range of a linear transformation T: V->W is the set of all vectors w in W such that w = T(v) for some v in V. It is denoted by range(T).
- A linear transformation is injective if its kernel is {0}. It is surjective if its range is W. It is bijective if it is both injective and surjective.
- The dimension of the kernel plus the dimension of the range equals the dimension of the domain. That is, dim(ker(T)) + dim(range(T)) = dim(V).
- The rank-nullity theorem: For any linear transformation T: V->W, dim(range(T)) + dim(ker(T)) = dim(V). The rank of T is the dimension of its range.
