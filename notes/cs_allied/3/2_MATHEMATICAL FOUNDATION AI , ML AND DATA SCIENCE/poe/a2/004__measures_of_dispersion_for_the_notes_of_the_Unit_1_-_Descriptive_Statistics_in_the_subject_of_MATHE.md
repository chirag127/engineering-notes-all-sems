### Measures of Dispersion

- Range: Difference between the maximum and minimum values. It only uses the extreme values and ignores other data points.
- Interquartile Range (IQR): Difference between the first and third quartiles (Q3-Q1). It is a more robust measure than range as it uses the middle 50% of the data and ignores outliers.
- Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD): Average of the absolute deviations from the mean. It measures the average spread of the data from the mean.
- Standard Deviation (SD): Square root of the variance. It measures the average squared deviation from the mean. It is the most popular measure of dispersion as it considers all data points and uses squaring to give more weight to larger differences.

The measures of dispersion summarize the spread or variability in a data set. They give us an idea about the clustering of the data around the central tendency. A lower dispersion indicates that the data points tend to be close to the central value while a higher dispersion indicates that the data are spread out over a wider range of values.
