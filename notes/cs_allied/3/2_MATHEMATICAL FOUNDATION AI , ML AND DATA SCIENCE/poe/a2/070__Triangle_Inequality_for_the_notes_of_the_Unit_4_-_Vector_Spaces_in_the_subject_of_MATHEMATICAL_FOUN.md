### Triangle Inequality

- For any vectors **u**, **v** and **w** in a vector space, the following inequality holds:
|u + v| <= |u| + |v|

- This is known as the triangle inequality and it must hold for any norm defined on the vector space.
- It essentially means that the length of a sum of two vectors cannot be greater than the sum of their individual lengths.
- The triangle inequality is a fundamental property that is used to prove various other results and inequalities related to vectors and norms.
- It comes from the fact that the length of a line segment cannot be greater than the sum of lengths of its constituent line segments. The same logic applies to vectors as well.
- The triangle inequality leads to many important extensions and generalizations in functional analysis and other related fields. It is a key result with numerous applications in mathematics and science.
