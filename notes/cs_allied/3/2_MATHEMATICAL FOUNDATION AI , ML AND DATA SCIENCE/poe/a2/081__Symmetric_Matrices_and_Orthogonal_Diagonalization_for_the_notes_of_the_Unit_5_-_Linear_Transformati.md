### Symmetric Matrices and Orthogonal Diagonalization

- A square matrix A is symmetric if A = A^T. The elements on the main diagonal of a symmetric matrix are real.
- A symmetric matrix can be diagonalized by an orthogonal transformation. That is, there exists an orthogonal matrix Q and a diagonal matrix D such that A = QDQ^T.
- The diagonal entries of D are the eigenvalues of A. The columns of Q are the corresponding eigenvectors.
- The eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix are orthogonal. The eigenvectors form an orthogonal basis for the vector space.
- The eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix are real. The spectral theorem for symmetric matrices states that a symmetric matrix can be decomposed into a basis of eigenvectors with real eigenvalues on the diagonal.
- The decomposition A = QDQ^T is unique up to the ordering of the diagonal entries of D and the corresponding columns of Q. The eigenvalues are unique but the eigenvectors have arbitrary signs.
- The trace of a symmetric matrix is the sum of its eigenvalues. The determinant of a symmetric matrix is the product of its eigenvalues.
