### Random Variable

- A random variable is a variable whose value is a numerical outcome of a random phenomenon.
- It is a function that maps outcomes of a random experiment to real numbers.
- A random variable can take on different values depending on the outcome of the random experiment.
- The possible values that a random variable can assume are called its sample space.
- Two types of random variables:
-- Discrete random variable: Can only take on a countable number of isolated values (e.g. number of heads in 3 coin flips).
-- Continuous random variable: Can take on any value in an interval (e.g. height of adults in a country).
- Probability distribution describes the possible values a random variable can take and their probabilities of occurrence.
- Probability mass function is used for discrete random variables. Probability density function is used for continuous random variables.
- Expectation or mean of a random variable is the weighted average of its possible values based on their probabilities. Variance measures the spread of a random variable.
- Transforming and combining random variables lead to new random variables, allowing us to analyze complex problems by breaking them into simpler components.
