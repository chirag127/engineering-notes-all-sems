### Convergence

- Pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) are algorithms that generate numbers that mimic properties of truly random numbers.
- PRNGs are not truly random, but generate sequences of numbers that appear random.
- The randomness comes from an initial seed value. Different seeds produce different random number sequences.
- It is important that the numbers generated by a PRNG converge to a uniform distribution. That is, each possible outcome should be equally likely over a large number of trials.
- Tests such as chi-square, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, and autocorrelation tests can check if a PRNG is converging to a uniform distribution.
- Failure of convergence can lead to biases in estimates and results. For example, Monte Carlo simulations and bootstrapping rely on random numbers, and non-convergence can affect accuracy.
- Some common PRNGs are the linear congruential generator and Mersenne Twister. Cryptographic PRNGs are designed to withstand attempts to determine the seed and predict outputs.
