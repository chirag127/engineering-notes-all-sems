### Vector Space

- A vector space is a mathematical structure that consists of a set of vectors along with operations for combining vectors such as addition, scalar multiplication, and dot product.
- Vectors are quantities that have magnitude and direction such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, etc.
- The set of vectors forms a vector space if the following axioms are satisfied:

1. The set is closed under vector addition. That is, if v and w are vectors, the vector v + w is also a vector in the set.
2. Vector addition is associative. That is, for vectors v, w, and x, (v + w) + x = v + (w + x).
3. There exists an additive identity vector 0 such that for any vector v, 0 + v = v + 0 = v.
4. For every vector v, there exists an additive inverse vector -v such that v + (-v) = -v + v = 0.
5. Scalar multiplication is defined and compatible with vector addition. That is, for any scalar k and vectors v and w, k(v + w) = kv + kw.

- Examples of vector spaces include Euclidean space Rn, functions spaces, and sequence spaces. Vector spaces are fundamental to machine learning and data science where vectors and linear transformations are widely used.
- The dot product and cross product are ways to combine two vectors to get a scalar and vector quantity respectively. They are useful in applications like computing distances, angles, and other geometric properties.
