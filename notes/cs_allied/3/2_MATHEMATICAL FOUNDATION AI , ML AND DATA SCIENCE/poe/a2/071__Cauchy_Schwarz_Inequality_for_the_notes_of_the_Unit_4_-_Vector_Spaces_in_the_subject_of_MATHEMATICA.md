### Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality

- For any vectors $\\mathbf{u}, \\mathbf{v} \\in \\mathbb{R}^n$, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality states that:
$|\\langle \\mathbf{u}, \\mathbf{v} \\rangle| \\leq \\|\\mathbf{u}\\|\\|\\mathbf{v}\\|$.
- In other words, the magnitude of the inner product is bounded above by the product of the Euclidean norms of the vectors.
- The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is a fundamental result in linear algebra with many applications in probability and statistics, including bounding the variance of random variables and providing convergence guarantees for sums of random variables.
- The name comes from the mathematicians Augustin Louis Cauchy and Hermann Schwarz who discovered this inequality independently in the 1800s.
- *Proof:* Let $\\mathbf{u} = (u_1, u_2, ..., u_n)$ and $\\mathbf{v} = (v_1, v_2, ..., v_n)$. Then,
$|\\langle \\mathbf{u}, \\mathbf{v} \\rangle| = |u_1v_1 + u_2v_2 + ... + u_nv_n| \\leq \\sqrt{u_1^2 + u_2^2 + ... + u_n^2}\\sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + ... + v_n^2} = \\|\\mathbf{u}\\|\\|\\mathbf{v}\\|$
