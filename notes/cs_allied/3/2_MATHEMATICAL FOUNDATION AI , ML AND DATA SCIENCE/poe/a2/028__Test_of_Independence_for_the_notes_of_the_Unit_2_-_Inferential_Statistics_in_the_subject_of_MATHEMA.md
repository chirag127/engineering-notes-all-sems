### Test of Independence for the notes of the Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI , ML AND DATA SCIENCE

1. Introduction
- A test of independence is used to check whether two variables are independent or not.
- It checks whether the probability of one event is affected by the occurrence of another event or not.

2. Chi-Square Test
- It is the most commonly used test to check independence between two categorical variables.
- It calculates the chi-square statistic and compares it with the chi-square distribution to get the p-value.
- If the p-value is less than the significance level, we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the variables are dependent. Else, we accept the null hypothesis and say that the variables are independent.

3. Conditions for Chi-Square Test
- The observations must be independent.
- The variables must be categorical.
- The expected frequency for each cell must be at least 5.
- The categories must be mutually exclusive.

4. steps to perform chi-square test
- State the null and alternative hypothesis. Null hypothesis is that the variables are independent.
- Create the contingency table by cross tabulating the variables.
- Calculate the expected frequency for each cell using row and column totals.
- Calculate the chi-square statistic using the formula: chi-square = sum((observed - expected)^2 / expected)
- Calculate the p-value using chi-square distribution and degree of freedom (df = (r-1) x (c-1) where r is number of rows and c is number of columns in the contingency table)
- Draw inference based on p-value and significance level.
