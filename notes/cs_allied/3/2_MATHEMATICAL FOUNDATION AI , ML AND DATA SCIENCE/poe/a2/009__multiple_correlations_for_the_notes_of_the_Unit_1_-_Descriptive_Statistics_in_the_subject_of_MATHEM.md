### Multiple Correlations

- Multiple correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two or more independent variables and a single dependent variable.
- It is denoted by R or r. R square or r square known as coefficient of determination indicates the proportion of variance in the dependent variable that is predictable from the independent variable(s).
- The multiple correlation coefficient (R) always lies between -1 and +1. The closer R is to +1 or -1, the stronger is the linear relationship.
- A positive value of R indicates a positive relationship while a negative value indicates a negative relationship.
- The multiple correlation does not indicate anything about causality. It only measures the strength of a linear association.
- Assumptions:
-- Linear relationship between dependent and independent variables.
-- Independent variables are not perfectly correlated.
-- Error terms are normally distributed with mean 0.
- Uses:
-- To predict the value of a dependent variable from a set of independent variables.
-- To determine the degree of relationship between a dependent variable and a set of independent variables.
-- To select a subset of independent variables that best predict the variation in the dependent variable.
