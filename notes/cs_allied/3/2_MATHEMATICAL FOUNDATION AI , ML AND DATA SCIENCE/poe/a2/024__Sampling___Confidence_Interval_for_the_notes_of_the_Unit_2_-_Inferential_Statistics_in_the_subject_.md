### Sampling & Confidence Interval

- Sampling: Selecting a subset of individuals from a population to estimate characteristics of the whole population. A sample should be representative of the population.
- Sample size: The number of individuals selected from the population. A larger sample size leads to a more accurate estimate of the population.
- Confidence interval: A range of values that is likely to contain the true population parameter. Calculated from a given sample. Provides a measure of uncertainty.
- Confidence level: The probability that the confidence interval contains the true population parameter. A 95% confidence level means there is a 95% chance that the confidence interval contains the population parameter.
- Margin of error: The amount of error we are willing to accept. A smaller margin of error leads to a narrower confidence interval and more precise estimate. Depends on sample size and confidence level.

The key concepts to understand are:
1. We cannot measure an entire population, so we select a representative sample.
2. A larger sample size and higher confidence level lead to a more precise estimate.
3. A confidence interval gives us a range of likely values for the population parameter.
