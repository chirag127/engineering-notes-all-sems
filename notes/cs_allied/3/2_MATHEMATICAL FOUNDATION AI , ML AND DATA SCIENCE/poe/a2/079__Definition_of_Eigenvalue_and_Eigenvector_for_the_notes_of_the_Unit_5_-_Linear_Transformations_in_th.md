### Definition of Eigenvalue and Eigenvector

- An Eigenvalue is a scalar quantity that, when multiplied by a linear transformation, does not change the direction of the vector. In other words, the transformed vector is a scalar multiple of the original vector.
- An Eigenvector is a vector that remains in the same direction after applying a linear transformation. The Eigenvalue is the scalar by which the Eigenvector is scaled.
- If [;T\\mathbf{v} = \\lambda \\mathbf{v};], then [;\\lambda;] is an Eigenvalue of the linear transformation [;T;] and [;\\mathbf{v};] is the corresponding Eigenvector. Here, [;\\lambda;] is a scalar and [;\\mathbf{v};] is a vector.
- A linear transformation can have multiple Eigenvalues and corresponding Eigenvectors. The Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of a linear transformation characterize its properties.
- Knowing the Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors is useful in various applications like data analysis, machine learning, quantum mechanics, etc. They provide key insights into the behavior of the system or data.
