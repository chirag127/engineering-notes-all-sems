### Central Limit Theorem

- The Central Limit Theorem states that the distribution of sample means approaches a normal distribution as the sample size increases, regardless of the shape of the underlying distribution.
- As the sample size (n) increases, the sample means distribution becomes more concentrated around the true mean (μ) of the population.
- The central limit theorem holds for any distribution of the population, not just for symmetric distributions. It applies to any distribution that has a finite mean and variance.
- The central limit theorem is the foundation for many statistical techniques, including confidence intervals and hypothesis testing. It allows us to assume that many distributions are approximately normal, which makes calculations and inferences much easier.
- The central limit theorem requires a sufficiently large sample size (n) for the approximation to be accurate. A rule of thumb is that n > 30 is sufficient, but for distributions that are very non-normal a larger sample size may be needed.
