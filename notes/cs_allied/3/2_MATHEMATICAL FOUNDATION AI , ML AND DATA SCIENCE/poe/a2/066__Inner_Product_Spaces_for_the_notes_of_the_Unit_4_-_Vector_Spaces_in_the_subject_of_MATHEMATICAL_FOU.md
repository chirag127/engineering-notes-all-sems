### Inner Product Spaces

- An inner product on a vector space V is a function that associates a scalar quantity to each pair of vectors in V.
- It must satisfy the following properties:
1. Conjugate symmetry: <u,v> = <v,u>*
2. Bilinearity: <u+v,w> = <u,w> + <v,w> and <u,av> = a<u,v>
3. Positive definiteness: <u,u> >= 0 with equality only when u = 0
- The pair (V,<.,.>) is called an inner product space.
- Every inner product space is a metric space with distance defined by d(u,v) = square root of (<u-v,u-v>). This metric induces a topology on the space.
- Examples:
1. R^n with usual dot product: <(x1,x2,..xn),(y1,y2,..yn)> = x1y1 + x2y2 + ... + xnyn
2. L^2([a,b]) with usual inner product: <f,g> = integral(f(t)g(t)dt)
3. C^n with <(z1,z2..zn),(w1,w2..wn)> = z1w1+z2w2+...+znw*n (complex conjugate of wn)
- Every inner product space is a normed vector space with ||u|| = square root of <u,u>.
- Important types of inner product spaces:
1. Hilbert spaces: Complete inner product spaces. All L^p spaces for 1 <= p < infinity are Hilbert spaces.
2. Pre-Hilbert spaces: Inner product spaces with the induced norm.
