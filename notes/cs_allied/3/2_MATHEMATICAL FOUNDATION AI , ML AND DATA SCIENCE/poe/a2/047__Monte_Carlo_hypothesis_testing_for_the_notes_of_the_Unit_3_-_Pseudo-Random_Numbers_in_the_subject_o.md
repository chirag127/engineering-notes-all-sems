### Monte Carlo hypothesis testing

- Monte Carlo methods are a broad class of computational algorithms that rely on repeated random sampling to obtain numerical results.
- They are often used in physical and mathematical problems and are most useful when it is difficult or impossible to compute an exact result with a deterministic algorithm.
- Monte Carlo hypothesis testing is a statistical technique where random sampling is used to determine whether a hypothesis is true or false. It involves the following steps:

1. State the null hypothesis (H0) and alternative hypothesis (H1)
2. Generate simulated data from the null hypothesis
3. Compute the test statistic for the simulated data
4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 many times to build up the distribution of the test statistic under H0
5. Compare the observed value of the test statistic (from real data) to the distribution from step 4. If the observed value is very unlikely under H0, reject H0 in favor of H1.

- The key advantage of Monte Carlo hypothesis testing is that it can be applied even in complex problems where analytical solutions do not exist. The validity of the method depends on generating simulated data that truly reflects the null hypothesis.
- Monte Carlo hypothesis testing is a powerful technique but can be computationally intensive due to the repeated random sampling and test statistic calculations required. However, modern computing power makes this feasible for many problems.
