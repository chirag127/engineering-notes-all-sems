
### Markov Inequality

Markov Inequality is a fundamental result in probability theory. It states that for any non-negative random variable X, the following inequality holds: 

$$P(X \geq a) \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{a}$$

where $\mathbb{E}[X]$ is the expected value of X. This inequality is useful for bounding the probability of a random variable being greater than some value, given its expected value.

Markov Inequality is particularly useful in the context of Descriptive Statistics. It can be used to bound the probability of a sample mean being greater than some value, given its expected value. This can be used to obtain a confidence interval around the mean, or to determine the sample size required to obtain a certain level of confidence.
