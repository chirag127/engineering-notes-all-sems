
### Chebyshev's Inequality for the Notes of Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics in the Subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML, and Data Science

1. Chebyshev's inequality is a theorem in probability theory that states that for all distributions, the probability that a random variable is at least a certain number of standard deviations away from the mean is always less than or equal to a certain number.

2. The theorem is named after Russian mathematician Pafnuty Chebyshev.

3. Chebyshev's inequality states that for any random variable X with mean μ and standard deviation σ, the following inequality holds:

$$P(|X - \mu| \geq k\sigma) \leq \frac{1}{k^2}$$

where k is any positive real number.

4. This inequality can be used to bound the probability of any given random variable being more than k standard deviations away from the mean.

5. Chebyshev's inequality is useful for finding bounds on the probability of rare events, such as extreme values of a random variable.

6. The inequality is also useful for finding the probability of events that occur frequently, such as values of a random variable that are within a certain range.
