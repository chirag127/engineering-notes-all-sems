
### Test of Independence 

1. Test of independence is a statistical test that is used to determine if two variables are independent of each other. 
2. It is used to determine if the occurrence of one variable is associated with the occurrence of another variable. 
3. It is a type of inferential statistic that is used to make decisions about a population based on a sample. 
4. The test of independence requires the use of a contingency table, which is a table that shows the observed frequencies of two variables in a cross-tabulated form. 
5. The test is based on the chi-square statistic, which is calculated by subtracting the expected frequencies from the observed frequencies and then squaring and summing the differences. 
6. The p-value is then used to determine the significance of the test. A p-value of less than 0.05 indicates that the two variables are independent. 
7. The test of independence can be used to test hypotheses about relationships between variables and to determine if the relationship between two variables is statistically significant.
