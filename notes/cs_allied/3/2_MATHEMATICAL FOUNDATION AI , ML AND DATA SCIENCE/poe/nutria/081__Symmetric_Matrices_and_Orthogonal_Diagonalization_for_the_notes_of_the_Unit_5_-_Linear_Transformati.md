
### Symmetric Matrices and Orthogonal Diagonalization

- A **symmetric matrix** is a matrix that is equal to its transpose; that is, it is a matrix whose elements are symmetric with respect to the main diagonal.
- An **orthogonal matrix** is a matrix whose rows and columns are orthonormal vectors. This means that the dot product of any two distinct rows or columns is zero.
- **Orthogonal diagonalization** is a process in which a symmetric matrix is transformed into a diagonal matrix with the help of an orthogonal matrix.
- The **eigenvalues** of a symmetric matrix are the roots of its characteristic polynomial and are real numbers.
- The **eigenvectors** of a symmetric matrix are orthogonal to each other, and the corresponding eigenvalues are the diagonal entries of the diagonal matrix.
- The **diagonalization** of a symmetric matrix is a process in which the matrix is transformed into a diagonal matrix by multiplying it with an orthogonal matrix.
- The **Spectral Theorem** states that any real symmetric matrix can be diagonalized by an orthogonal matrix. This means that the eigenvalues of the matrix are the diagonal entries of the diagonal matrix.
