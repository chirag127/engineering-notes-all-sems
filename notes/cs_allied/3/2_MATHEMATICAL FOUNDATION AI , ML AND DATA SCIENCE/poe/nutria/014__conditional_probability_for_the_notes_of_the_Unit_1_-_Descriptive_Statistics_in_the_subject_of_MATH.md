
### Conditional Probability for Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics

1. Conditional probability is the probability of an event occurring, given that another event has already occurred. 
2. In order to calculate conditional probability, the event of interest must be related to another event. 
3. For example, the probability that it will rain today, given that it rained yesterday, is a conditional probability. 
4. Conditional probability is often expressed as a ratio of two probabilities, such as P(A|B) / P(B), where P(A|B) is the probability of A given B, and P(B) is the probability of B. 
5. Conditional probability can also be expressed as a fraction or percentage. 
6. Conditional probability can be used to calculate the probability of a certain event occurring, given the knowledge of other related events. 
7. For example, the probability of a student getting an A in a course, given that they have passed all the tests in the course, is a conditional probability. 
8. Conditional probability can also be used to calculate the probability of an event occurring, given the knowledge of other unrelated events. 
9. For example, the probability of a student getting an A in a course, given that they have received an A in all their other courses, is a conditional probability. 
10. Conditional probability can be used to make predictions about future events, based on the knowledge of past events. 
11. For example, the probability of a student getting an A in a course, given that they have received an A in all their other courses, is a conditional probability that can be used to predict the student's performance in the course.
