
### Triangle Inequality

The triangle inequality states that the sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the length of the third side. This can be expressed mathematically as: 

$$a + b \geq c$$

where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are the lengths of the sides of the triangle.

The triangle inequality is an important concept in mathematics, particularly in vector spaces. In a vector space, the triangle inequality states that the magnitude of the sum of two vectors must be less than or equal to the sum of the magnitudes of the two vectors. This can be expressed mathematically as: 

$$||\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}|| \leq ||\mathbf{a}|| + ||\mathbf{b}||$$

where $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are two vectors in the vector space.

The triangle inequality is also used in the definition of a metric space. A metric space is a set of elements with a distance function that satisfies the triangle inequality. This means that the distance between two points must be less than or equal to the sum of the distances from each point to a third point. This can be expressed mathematically as:

$$d(x,z) \leq d(x,y) + d(y,z)$$

where $x$, $y$ and $z$ are three points in the metric space and $d$ is the distance function.
