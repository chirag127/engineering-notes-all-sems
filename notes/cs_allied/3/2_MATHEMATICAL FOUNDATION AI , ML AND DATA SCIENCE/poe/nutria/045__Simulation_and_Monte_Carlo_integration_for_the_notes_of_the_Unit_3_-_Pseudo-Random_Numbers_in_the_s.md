
### Simulation and Monte Carlo Integration 

Simulation and Monte Carlo integration are methods of approximating a certain quantity using random numbers. The goal is to produce a result that is close to the exact answer, but without the need for an exact calculation. 

**Simulation** 

Simulation is a method of approximating a certain quantity by randomly sampling from a distribution. This method is useful when an exact answer is difficult or impossible to calculate. The idea is to use a large number of samples and then use the average of those samples as an estimate of the true value. 

**Monte Carlo Integration** 

Monte Carlo integration is a method of approximating a certain quantity by randomly sampling from a distribution. This method is useful when an exact answer is difficult or impossible to calculate. The idea is to use a large number of samples and then use the average of those samples as an estimate of the true value. The main difference between Monte Carlo integration and simulation is that Monte Carlo integration requires a probability distribution, while simulation does not. 

**Pseudo-Random Numbers** 

Pseudo-random numbers are numbers that appear to be random, but are actually generated using an algorithm. These numbers are used in Monte Carlo integration and simulation to generate the samples needed for the approximation. Pseudo-random numbers are important for Monte Carlo integration, as they allow for the generation of large numbers of samples quickly and efficiently.
