### Measures of Probability for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics in the Subject of Mathematical Foundation AI, ML and Data Science

Probability theory is the foundation of many fields, including statistics, data science, and artificial intelligence. It is essential to understand the different measures of probability to analyze data and make informed decisions. Here are some measures of probability that you should know:

- **Sample Space:** The sample space is the set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment. For example, if you toss a coin, the sample space would be {Heads, Tails}.

- **Event:** An event is a subset of the sample space. It represents the possible outcomes of interest. For example, if you are interested in getting heads when you toss a coin, the event would be {Heads}.

- **Probability of an Event:** The probability of an event is the proportion of times the event occurs in the long run. It is denoted by P(E). The probability of an event lies between 0 and 1, where 0 means the event is impossible, and 1 means the event is certain. For example, the probability of getting heads when you toss a fair coin is 0.5.

- **Complement of an Event:** The complement of an event is the set of all outcomes that are not in the event. It is denoted by E'. For example, if E is the event of getting heads when you toss a coin, then E' is the event of getting tails.

- **Union of Events:** The union of two events is the set of all outcomes that belong to either event. It is denoted by E ∪ F. For example, if E is the event of getting heads when you toss a coin, and F is the event of getting tails, then E ∪ F is the event of getting either heads or tails.

- **Intersection of Events:** The intersection of two events is the set of all outcomes that belong to both events. It is denoted by E ∩ F. For example, if E is the event of getting heads when you toss a coin, and F is the event of getting heads on the second toss, then E ∩ F is the event of getting heads on both tosses.

- **Conditional Probability:** Conditional probability is the probability of an event given that another event has occurred. It is denoted by P(E|F), which is read as the probability of E given F. For example, the probability of getting heads on the second toss given that you have already got heads on the first toss is 0.5.

- **Independent Events:** Two events are said to be independent if the occurrence of one event does not affect the probability of the other event. For example, if you toss a coin twice, the outcome of the first toss does not affect the outcome of the second toss.

These measures of probability are crucial in understanding the basics of statistics, data science, and artificial intelligence. You should practice solving problems related to probability to master these concepts.
