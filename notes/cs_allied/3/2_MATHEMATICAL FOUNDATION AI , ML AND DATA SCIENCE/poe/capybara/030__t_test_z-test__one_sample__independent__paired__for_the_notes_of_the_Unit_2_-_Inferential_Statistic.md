### T-Test/Z-Test (One Sample, Independent, Paired)

Inferential statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with making conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. One of the most commonly used methods in inferential statistics is hypothesis testing. Hypothesis testing involves comparing the results of a sample to what we would expect if a particular hypothesis were true. 

Two of the most commonly used tests in hypothesis testing are the t-test and z-test. These tests help us to determine whether a difference between groups is statistically significant, or whether it could have occurred by chance. 

#### One Sample T-Test

A one sample t-test is used to compare the mean of a sample to a known or hypothesized value. The test determines whether the difference between the mean of the sample and the hypothesized value is statistically significant. 

The steps for conducting a one sample t-test are as follows:

1. State the null and alternative hypotheses.
2. Determine the level of significance (alpha) and the degrees of freedom (df).
3. Calculate the t-statistic using the formula: t = (sample mean - hypothesized value) / (standard error of the mean).
4. Determine the critical value of t using a t-table or calculator.
5. Compare the calculated t-value with the critical value of t.
6. If the calculated t-value is greater than the critical value of t, reject the null hypothesis. If the calculated t-value is less than the critical value of t, fail to reject the null hypothesis.

#### Independent Samples T-Test

An independent samples t-test is used to compare the means of two independent samples. The test determines whether the difference between the means of the two samples is statistically significant. 

The steps for conducting an independent samples t-test are as follows:

1. State the null and alternative hypotheses.
2. Determine the level of significance (alpha) and the degrees of freedom (df).
3. Calculate the t-statistic using the formula: t = (mean difference - hypothesized value) / (standard error of the mean difference).
4. Determine the critical value of t using a t-table or calculator.
5. Compare the calculated t-value with the critical value of t.
6. If the calculated t-value is greater than the critical value of t, reject the null hypothesis. If the calculated t-value is less than the critical value of t, fail to reject the null hypothesis.

#### Paired Samples T-Test

A paired samples t-test is used to compare the means of two related samples. The test determines whether the difference between the means of the two samples is statistically significant. 

The steps for conducting a paired samples t-test are as follows:

1. State the null and alternative hypotheses.
2. Determine the level of significance (alpha) and the degrees of freedom (df).
3. Calculate the t-statistic using the formula: t = (mean difference - hypothesized value) / (standard error of the mean difference).
4. Determine the critical value of t using a t-table or calculator.
5. Compare the calculated t-value with the critical value of t.
6. If the calculated t-value is greater than the critical value of t, reject the null hypothesis. If the calculated t-value is less than the critical value of t, fail to reject the null hypothesis.

#### Z-Test

A z-test is used to compare the mean of a sample to a known or hypothesized value when the population standard deviation is known. The test determines whether the difference between the mean of the sample and the hypothesized value is statistically significant.

The steps for conducting a z-test are as follows:

1. State the null and alternative hypotheses.
2. Determine the level of significance (alpha).
3. Calculate the z-statistic using the formula: z = (sample mean - hypothesized value) / (population standard deviation / square root of sample size).
4. Determine the critical value of z using a z-table or calculator.
5. Compare the calculated z-value with the critical value of z.
6. If the calculated z-value is greater than the critical value of z, reject the null hypothesis. If the calculated z-value is less than the critical value of z, fail to reject the null hypothesis.

In conclusion, t-tests and z-tests are commonly used in hypothesis testing in inferential statistics. One sample t-tests are used to compare the mean of a sample to a known or hypothesized value, independent samples t-tests are used to compare the means of two independent samples, paired samples t-tests are used to compare the means of two related samples, and z-tests are used to compare the mean of a sample to a known or hypothesized value when the population standard deviation is known.
