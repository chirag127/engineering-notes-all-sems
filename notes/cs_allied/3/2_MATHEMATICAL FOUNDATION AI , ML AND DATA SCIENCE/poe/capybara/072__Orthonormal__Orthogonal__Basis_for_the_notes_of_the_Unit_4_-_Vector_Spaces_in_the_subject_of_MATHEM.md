### Orthonormal (Orthogonal) Basis for the notes of the Unit 4 - Vector Spaces in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI , ML AND DATA SCIENCE

- An orthonormal (orthogonal) basis is a set of vectors in a vector space that are both orthogonal and normalized.

- Orthogonal vectors are those that are perpendicular to each other, meaning the dot product of any two vectors in an orthogonal basis is zero.

- Normalized vectors are those that have a magnitude of one, meaning the length of each vector in an orthonormal basis is equal to one.

- Orthonormal bases are particularly useful in linear algebra, as they simplify many calculations involving vectors and matrices.

- Any vector in a vector space can be represented as a linear combination of the vectors in an orthonormal basis.

- The process of finding an orthonormal basis for a vector space involves a technique called Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization.

- Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization involves taking a set of linearly independent vectors and constructing an orthogonal set of vectors from them.

- Once an orthogonal basis has been constructed, each vector in the basis is then normalized to obtain an orthonormal basis.

- Orthonormal bases have many applications in machine learning and data science, particularly in the fields of feature extraction and dimensionality reduction.

- Principal component analysis (PCA) is a common technique in machine learning that involves finding the orthonormal basis that captures the most variance in a dataset.
