### Stratified Sampling

Stratified sampling is a technique used in statistics to divide a population into smaller, more manageable groups known as strata. This method is commonly used in research studies, as it ensures that each stratum is represented proportionally in the sample.

Following are some important points related to stratified sampling:

- Stratified sampling is used when the population is too large to be sampled in its entirety, and when there are differences within the population that are important to the study.
- In stratified sampling, the population is divided into strata based on similar characteristics or attributes, such as age, gender, or income.
- A sample is then taken from each stratum, with the sample size proportional to the size of the stratum within the population.
- Stratified sampling is considered more efficient than simple random sampling, as it reduces the sampling error and increases the accuracy of the results.
- Stratified sampling can also be more cost-effective than other sampling methods, as it enables researchers to target specific groups within the population.
- Stratified sampling is commonly used in various fields such as social sciences, marketing, and medical research.

In conclusion, stratified sampling is an important technique used in statistics to ensure representative and accurate results in research studies. It is a useful tool for researchers who wish to target specific groups within a population and is considered more efficient and cost-effective than other sampling methods.
