### Goodness of Fit

Goodness of fit is a statistical concept that is used to determine how well an observed set of data fits a particular hypothesis. It is a measure of the discrepancy between the observed data and the expected data under the hypothesis being tested.

Here are some key points to remember about goodness of fit:

- Goodness of fit is used to test whether an observed set of data fits a particular theoretical distribution or model.
- The goodness of fit test is based on the comparison of the observed data with the expected data under the null hypothesis.
- The null hypothesis assumes that the observed data follows a particular distribution or model.
- The goodness of fit test measures the discrepancy between the observed data and the expected data under the null hypothesis.
- The goodness of fit test is based on the chi-square statistic, which measures the difference between the observed frequencies and the expected frequencies.
- The chi-square statistic follows a chi-square distribution with k-1 degrees of freedom, where k is the number of categories or classes in the data.
- The p-value associated with the chi-square statistic is used to determine the level of significance of the test.
- If the p-value is less than the chosen level of significance, then the null hypothesis is rejected and it is concluded that the observed data does not fit the theoretical distribution or model.
- If the p-value is greater than the chosen level of significance, then the null hypothesis is not rejected and it is concluded that the observed data fits the theoretical distribution or model.

In summary, goodness of fit is an important concept in inferential statistics that is used to test the fit of observed data to a particular theoretical distribution or model. The chi-square goodness of fit test is a commonly used method for testing goodness of fit, and it is based on the comparison of observed and expected frequencies. Understanding the concept of goodness of fit is important for data analysts, machine learning engineers, and data scientists who need to make statistical inferences from data.
