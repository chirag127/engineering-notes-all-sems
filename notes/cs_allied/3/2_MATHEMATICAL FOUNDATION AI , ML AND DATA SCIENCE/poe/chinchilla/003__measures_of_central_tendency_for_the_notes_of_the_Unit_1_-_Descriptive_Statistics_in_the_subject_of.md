### Measures of Central Tendency for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Descriptive Statistics in the Subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI, ML, and Data Science

Central tendency is a statistical term that refers to the middle value or a typical value of a dataset. It is used to describe the center of a distribution of data. The three main measures of central tendency are the mean, median, and mode. Let's discuss each of them in detail.

1. Mean:
   - The mean is also known as the arithmetic mean or average.
   - It is calculated by adding all the values in the dataset and dividing by the number of values.
   - Mean = (Sum of all values in the dataset) / (Number of values in the dataset)
   - The mean is sensitive to extreme values, also known as outliers, which can skew the result.

2. Median:
   - The median is the middle value of a dataset when arranged in ascending or descending order.
   - If the dataset has an odd number of values, the median is the middle value.
   - If the dataset has an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values.
   - The median is not affected by extreme values or outliers.

3. Mode:
   - The mode is the value that appears most frequently in a dataset.
   - A dataset can have one mode, more than one mode, or no mode.
   - The mode is useful for finding the most common value in a dataset.

All three measures of central tendency have their uses in data analysis. The mean is useful when the dataset is normally distributed, and there are no extreme values. The median is useful when there are outliers in the dataset. The mode is useful when finding the most common value in a dataset.

In conclusion, understanding measures of central tendency is an essential part of data analysis. They provide information about the center of a distribution of data and help to summarize the dataset. Knowing which measure to use in different situations is important for accurate data analysis.
