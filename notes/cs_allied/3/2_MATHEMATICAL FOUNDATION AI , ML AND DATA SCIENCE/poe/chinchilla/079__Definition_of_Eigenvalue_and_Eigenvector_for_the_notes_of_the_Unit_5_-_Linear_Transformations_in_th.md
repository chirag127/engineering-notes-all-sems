### Definition of Eigenvalue and Eigenvector

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors are essential concepts in linear algebra that play a crucial role in various fields of science and engineering. In this section, we will define eigenvalues and eigenvectors and discuss their properties and applications.

1. Eigenvalue:
An eigenvalue of a square matrix A is a scalar λ that satisfies the equation:
        
        Ax = λx
        
where x is a non-zero vector, which is called an eigenvector of A corresponding to the eigenvalue λ. In other words, when we multiply a matrix A by its eigenvector x, the result is a scalar multiple of x.

2. Eigenvector:
An eigenvector of a square matrix A is a non-zero vector x that satisfies the equation:
        
        Ax = λx
        
where λ is a scalar, which is called an eigenvalue of A corresponding to the eigenvector x. In other words, the vector x remains in the same direction after multiplication by the matrix A, but its magnitude changes by a factor of λ.

3. Properties of eigenvalues and eigenvectors:
    - The set of all eigenvectors corresponding to a particular eigenvalue λ of a matrix A forms a subspace of the vector space.
    - If A is an n x n matrix, then it has at most n distinct eigenvalues.
    - The determinant of a matrix A is equal to the product of its eigenvalues.
    - The trace of a matrix A is equal to the sum of its eigenvalues.
    - If a matrix A is symmetric, then its eigenvectors are orthogonal.
    - If a matrix A is invertible, then its eigenvectors form a basis for the vector space.

4. Applications of eigenvalues and eigenvectors:
    - In physics, eigenvalues and eigenvectors are used to solve problems related to quantum mechanics, such as determining the energy levels of a particle in a potential well.
    - In computer science, eigenvalues and eigenvectors are used in data analysis and machine learning, such as in principal component analysis (PCA) and linear discriminant analysis (LDA).
    - In engineering, eigenvalues and eigenvectors are used to study the stability and oscillatory behavior of systems, such as in control theory and signal processing.

In conclusion, eigenvalues and eigenvectors are powerful concepts in linear algebra that have various applications in science and engineering. Understanding these concepts is essential for any student or researcher in the field of mathematical foundations for AI, ML, and data science.
