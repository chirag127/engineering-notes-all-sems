### Markov chains for the notes of the Unit 3 - Pseudo-Random Numbers in the subject of MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AI , ML AND DATA SCIENCE

Markov chains are a mathematical model used to describe a system in which the probability of a future event depends only on the present state of the system, and not on any past events. They are widely used in many fields, including physics, chemistry, biology, economics, and computer science.

In this unit, we will focus on the application of Markov chains to the generation of pseudo-random numbers. Pseudo-random numbers are a sequence of numbers that appear to be random, but are actually generated by a deterministic algorithm.

Here are some key concepts related to Markov chains for generating pseudo-random numbers:

1. State space: The state space of a Markov chain is the set of all possible states that the system can be in. In the context of generating pseudo-random numbers, the state space is typically a finite set of integers.

2. Transition matrix: The transition matrix of a Markov chain describes the probabilities of transitioning from one state to another. In the context of generating pseudo-random numbers, the transition matrix is typically a square matrix in which each entry represents the probability of transitioning from one state to another.

3. Stationary distribution: The stationary distribution of a Markov chain is the probability distribution that the system approaches as the number of transitions approaches infinity. In the context of generating pseudo-random numbers, the stationary distribution is the distribution of the generated numbers.

4. Ergodicity: A Markov chain is said to be ergodic if it has a unique stationary distribution and if all starting states eventually converge to that distribution. In the context of generating pseudo-random numbers, ergodicity ensures that the generated numbers are uniformly distributed.

5. Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods: MCMC methods are a family of algorithms that use Markov chains to generate samples from complex probability distributions. In the context of generating pseudo-random numbers, MCMC methods can be used to generate samples from non-uniform distributions.

Overall, Markov chains provide a powerful tool for generating pseudo-random numbers and for simulating complex systems. By understanding the key concepts and properties of Markov chains, we can develop more effective algorithms for generating pseudo-random numbers and for solving a wide range of problems in science, engineering, and mathematics.
