### Sampling & Confidence Interval

Inferential Statistics involves making inferences and conclusions about a population based on data collected from a sample. Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of individuals from a population to represent the population. The goal is to obtain a sample that is representative of the population and that can be used to make accurate inferences about the population.

In this unit, we will study Sampling and Confidence Intervals, which are important concepts in Inferential Statistics. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

#### Sampling

- A sample is a subset of individuals from a population.
- Random sampling is the process of selecting individuals from a population at random, so that every individual has an equal chance of being selected. This helps to ensure that the sample is representative of the population.
- Sampling bias occurs when certain individuals are more likely to be selected than others, which can lead to a sample that is not representative of the population.
- The sample size is the number of individuals in the sample. A larger sample size generally leads to more accurate inferences about the population.

#### Confidence Interval

- A confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the true value of a population parameter with a certain level of confidence.
- The confidence level is the probability that the true value of the population parameter falls within the confidence interval. Common confidence levels are 90%, 95%, and 99%.
- The width of the confidence interval depends on the sample size and the level of confidence. A larger sample size or a higher level of confidence will result in a wider confidence interval.
- Confidence intervals are used to estimate population parameters such as the mean, standard deviation, and proportion.

In conclusion, Sampling and Confidence Intervals are important concepts in Inferential Statistics that help us make accurate inferences about a population based on data collected from a sample. Understanding these concepts is crucial for anyone working in the field of AI, ML, and Data Science.
