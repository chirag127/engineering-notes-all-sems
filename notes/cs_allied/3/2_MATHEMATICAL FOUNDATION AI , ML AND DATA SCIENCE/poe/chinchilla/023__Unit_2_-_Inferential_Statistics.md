## Unit 2 - Inferential Statistics

Inferential statistics is a branch of statistics that deals with making inferences or conclusions about a population based on a sample of data. In this unit, we will cover the following topics:

1. Sampling Methods:
   - Simple Random Sampling
   - Stratified Sampling
   - Cluster Sampling
   - Systematic Sampling

2. Central Limit Theorem:
   - Definition and Explanation
   - Application in Practice

3. Confidence Intervals:
   - Definition and Explanation
   - Calculation of Confidence Intervals
   - Interpretation of Confidence Intervals

4. Hypothesis Testing:
   - Definition and Explanation
   - Steps in Hypothesis Testing
   - Types of Errors in Hypothesis Testing
   - P-Value Interpretation

5. Chi-Squared Test:
   - Definition and Explanation
   - Calculation of Chi-Squared Test
   - Interpretation of Chi-Squared Test

6. T-Test:
   - Definition and Explanation
   - Types of T-Tests
   - Calculation of T-Test
   - Interpretation of T-Test

It is important to note that inferential statistics is a powerful tool for making conclusions about populations, but it is also subject to certain limitations and assumptions. In order to use inferential statistics effectively, it is important to have a solid understanding of the topics listed above and to apply them correctly in practice.

As you study this material, remember to focus on the concepts and how they relate to real-world scenarios. Practice using statistical software and tools to apply these concepts in practice. By doing so, you will develop a deeper understanding of inferential statistics and be better equipped to use it in your own research and analysis.
