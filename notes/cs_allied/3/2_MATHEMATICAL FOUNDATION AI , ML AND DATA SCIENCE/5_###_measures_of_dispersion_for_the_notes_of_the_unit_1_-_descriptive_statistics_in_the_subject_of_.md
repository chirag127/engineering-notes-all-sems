Measures of Dispersion

Measures of dispersion are statistical measures that describe the spread of a set of data. They provide information about how much the data deviates from the average, and they help to identify outliers and skewness in the data.

There are several measures of dispersion, including range, variance, standard deviation, and interquartile range. The range is the difference between the largest and smallest value in a set of data. The variance is a measure of the spread of the data around the mean, and the standard deviation is the square root of the variance. The interquartile range is the range of the middle 50% of the data, and it is a robust measure of dispersion that is not affected by outliers.

In addition to these measures, there are also other measures of dispersion, including mean absolute deviation, median absolute deviation, and coefficient of variation. Mean absolute deviation measures the average distance between each data point and the mean, and median absolute deviation measures the average distance between each data point and the median. The coefficient of variation is a normalized measure of dispersion that is used to compare the spread of data between different datasets.

In conclusion, measures of dispersion are statistical measures that describe the spread of a set of data.
