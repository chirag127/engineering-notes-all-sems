### Laptop for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the field of technology that involves devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, smart glasses, fitness trackers, etc.
- Wearable devices usually have microprocessors, batteries, sensors, and wireless connectivity to collect data and communicate with other electronics, such as mobile devices or laptops.
- A laptop for wearable computing should have the following features:
  - A rugged design that can withstand harsh environments, such as dust, water, shock, and vibration.
  - A bright and high-resolution display that can be easily viewed indoors and outdoors.
  - A long battery life that can support continuous use and hot-swappable batteries that can be replaced without shutting down the device.
  - A fast and reliable wireless connection that can sync data with wearable devices and the internet.
  - A powerful processor and sufficient memory that can run multiple applications and handle large amounts of data.
  - A compatible operating system that can support various wearable platforms, such as Android, FreeRTOS, etc.
  - A user-friendly interface that can be controlled by touch, voice, or gestures.
  - A lightweight and portable design that can be easily carried and stored.
- Some examples of laptops that meet these criteria are:
  - The Getac B360, which is a fully rugged laptop with a 13.3-inch full HD touch display, dual hot-swappable batteries, and a best-in-class warranty.
  - The Zebra WS50, which is a wearable mobile computer that can be attached to a wrist or a belt, and has a 3.2-inch touch display, a barcode scanner, and a modular design.
  - The Dell Latitude 7424 Rugged Extreme, which is a durable laptop with a 14-inch full HD touch display, a backlit keyboard, and a dual-battery system.
