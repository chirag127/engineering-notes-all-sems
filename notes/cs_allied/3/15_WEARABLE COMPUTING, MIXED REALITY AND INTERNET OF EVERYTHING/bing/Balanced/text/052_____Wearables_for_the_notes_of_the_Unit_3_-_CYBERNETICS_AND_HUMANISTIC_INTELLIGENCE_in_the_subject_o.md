### Wearables for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearables are devices that are worn on the body or integrated into clothing, accessories, or implants, that can sense, compute, communicate, or actuate.
- Wearables can be classified into three categories: passive, active, and interactive. Passive wearables only sense or display information, such as watches, glasses, or heart rate monitors. Active wearables can perform some computation or communication, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, or earphones. Interactive wearables can also actuate or control other devices or the environment, such as smart gloves, exoskeletons, or brain-computer interfaces.
- Cybernetics is the science of communication and control in living organisms and machines, that studies the feedback loops and self-regulation mechanisms that enable adaptation, learning, and goal-oriented behavior.
- Humanistic intelligence (HI) is a concept that defines intelligence as the result of a human being in the feedback loop of a computational process, where the human and computer are inextricably intertwined. HI is different from artificial intelligence (AI), which aims to create machines that can perform tasks that require human intelligence, and from natural intelligence (NI), which is the innate intelligence of living beings.
- Wearable computing is a form of HI, where the wearable device becomes an extension of the human body and mind, augmenting the human capabilities and experiences. Wearable computing can enable new forms of human-computer interaction, such as gesture, voice, eye, or brain control, as well as new forms of human-human and human-environment interaction, such as social networking, augmented reality, or ubiquitous computing.
- Some examples of wearable computing applications are:
  - Health and wellness: wearables can monitor vital signs, track physical activity, provide feedback, or deliver therapy, such as smartwatches, fitness bands, or glucose sensors.
  - Entertainment and education: wearables can provide immersive and engaging experiences, such as games, music, or learning, such as virtual reality headsets, smart glasses, or earbuds.
  - Productivity and security: wearables can enhance work efficiency, safety, or convenience, such as navigation, communication, or authentication, such as smart badges, head-up displays, or smart rings.
  - Art and expression: wearables can enable new forms of creativity, communication, or identity, such as fashion, performance, or social media, such as smart clothing, LED accessories, or wearable cameras.
