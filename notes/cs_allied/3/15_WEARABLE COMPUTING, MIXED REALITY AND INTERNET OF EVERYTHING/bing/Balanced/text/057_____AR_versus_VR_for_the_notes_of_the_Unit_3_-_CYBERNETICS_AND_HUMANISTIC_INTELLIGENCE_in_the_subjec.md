### AR versus VR

- AR stands for **augmented reality**, which is a technology that **adds** digital elements to the real world, such as images, sounds, or text, through a device like a smartphone or a computer .
- VR stands for **virtual reality**, which is a technology that **replaces** the real world with a simulated one, such as a game or a movie, through a device like a headset or a controller .
- The main differences between AR and VR are  :
  - VR is **75 percent** virtual, while AR is only **25 percent** virtual.
  - VR requires a **headset** device, while AR does not.
  - VR users move in a **completely fictional** world, while AR users are in contact with the **real world**.
  - AR requires **higher bandwidth** than VR.
  - AR is intended to **enhance** the virtual world and the real world, while VR is intended to **replace** the real world with a fictional reality.
- Some examples of AR applications are :
  - Pokemon Go, a game that lets users catch virtual creatures in real locations.
  - Snapchat, a social media app that lets users apply filters and stickers to their photos and videos.
  - Google Translate, a tool that lets users translate text and speech in real time.
- Some examples of VR applications are :
  - Oculus Rift, a headset that lets users immerse themselves in various games and experiences.
  - Google Cardboard, a low-cost device that lets users turn their smartphones into VR viewers.
  - The Void, a location-based entertainment that lets users interact with physical and virtual environments.
