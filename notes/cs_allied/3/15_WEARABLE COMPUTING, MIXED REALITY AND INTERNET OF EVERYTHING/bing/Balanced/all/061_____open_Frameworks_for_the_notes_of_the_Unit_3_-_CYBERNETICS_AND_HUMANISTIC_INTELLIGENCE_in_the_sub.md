# Open Frameworks for Wearable Computing

- Wearable computing is the integration of computing devices and sensors into clothing and accessories that can be worn on the body, such as smart glasses, smart watches, smart jewelry, etc.
- Wearable computing aims to provide ubiquitous access to information and communication technologies, as well as to augment human perception, cognition, and interaction with the environment.
- Open frameworks for wearable computing are software platforms that enable the development and deployment of wearable applications across different hardware devices and operating systems.
- Open frameworks for wearable computing can offer various benefits, such as:
  - Hardware independence: the same application can run on different wearable devices without requiring specific adaptations or modifications.
  - Software reuse: existing libraries and modules can be leveraged to create new applications or extend existing ones.
  - Interoperability: different wearable devices can communicate and exchange data with each other and with other systems, such as smartphones, cloud services, or internet of things devices.
  - Customization: users can tailor the applications to their preferences and needs, such as choosing the input and output modalities, the level of feedback, the type of information, etc.
  - Innovation: developers can experiment with new ideas and features, and users can provide feedback and suggestions, fostering a collaborative and creative process.
- Some examples of open frameworks for wearable computing are:
  - The Open Wearable Computing Framework (OWCF): a central, easily wearable and hardware-independent computing unit that gives access to an ICT environment, with components such as wireless communication, positioning systems, speech recognition, interface devices, and low-level software platforms.
  - Wearable and Autonomous Computing for Future Smart Cities (WACFSC): a framework that addresses the challenges of wearable and autonomous computing systems in the context of smart cities, such as energy efficiency, security, privacy, reliability, and scalability.
  - Wearable Intelligence System (WIS): a wearable computing software framework for intelligence augmentation research and applications, with features such as voice command, speech recognition, computer vision, UI, sensors, smartphone connection, NLP, facial recognition, database, cloud connection, and more.
  - MIT Wearable Computing Project: a research project that explores the design and implementation of wearable computers as intelligent assistants, with applications such as remembrance agents, augmented reality, and intellectual collectives.
