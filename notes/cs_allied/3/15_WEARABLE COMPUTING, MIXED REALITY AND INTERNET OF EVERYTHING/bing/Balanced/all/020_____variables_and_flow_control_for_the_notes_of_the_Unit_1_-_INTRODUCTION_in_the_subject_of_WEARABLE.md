# Variables and Flow Control

## Variables
- Variables are named containers that store data or values in a program.
- Variables can have different types, such as integers, floats, strings, booleans, etc.
- Variables can be declared, assigned, and used in expressions or statements.
- Variables can be modified or updated during the execution of a program.
- Variables can have different scopes, such as global, local, or static, depending on where and how they are defined.
- Variables can be passed as arguments to functions or returned as results from functions.

## Flow Control
- Flow control is the process of determining the order or sequence of execution of statements or instructions in a program.
- Flow control can be influenced by variables, constants, operators, expressions, and functions.
- Flow control can be divided into three basic types: sequence, selection, and iteration.
- Sequence is the default flow of control, where statements are executed one after another in the order they appear in the program.
- Selection is the conditional flow of control, where statements are executed based on the evaluation of a condition or a logical expression. Examples of selection structures are if, if-else, switch, etc.
- Iteration is the repetitive flow of control, where statements are executed multiple times until a condition or a logical expression is satisfied. Examples of iteration structures are for, while, do-while, etc.
