## Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS

### Wearable Computing

- Wearable computing is the integration of computing devices and sensors into clothing and accessories that can be worn on the body.
- Wearable computing enables continuous and context-aware interaction between the user and the environment, as well as between users.
- Wearable computing can be classified into three categories: passive, active, and interactive.
  - Passive wearable computing: devices that only sense and record data, such as fitness trackers, smart watches, and health monitors.
  - Active wearable computing: devices that can process and display data, such as smart glasses, head-mounted displays, and smart clothing.
  - Interactive wearable computing: devices that can communicate and control other devices, such as gesture recognition, voice control, and brain-computer interfaces.

### Mixed Reality

- Mixed reality is the combination of real and virtual worlds, where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time.
- Mixed reality can be seen as a spectrum that ranges from augmented reality (AR) to virtual reality (VR).
  - Augmented reality: the overlay of digital information on the real world, such as navigation, annotations, and games.
  - Virtual reality: the immersion of the user in a fully synthetic environment, such as simulations, training, and entertainment.
- Mixed reality can be achieved by various technologies, such as optical see-through, video see-through, projection mapping, and spatial sound.

### Internet of Everything

- Internet of Everything (IoE) is the network of people, processes, data, and things that are connected and communicate through the internet.
- Internet of Everything can enable new applications and services that can improve efficiency, productivity, safety, and quality of life.
- Internet of Everything can be divided into four domains: people, things, data, and processes.
  - People: the connection of people to each other, to things, and to data, such as social media, telepresence, and wearable devices.
  - Things: the connection of physical objects to the internet, such as sensors, actuators, and smart devices.
  - Data: the collection, analysis, and distribution of data from various sources, such as cloud computing, big data, and artificial intelligence.
  - Processes: the integration and optimization of business and operational processes, such as automation, decision making, and feedback loops.
