# Sensors and Software for Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything

- Wearable computing is the use of electronic devices that are worn on the body or embedded in clothing to perform various functions, such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, or interacting with the environment.
- Mixed reality is the combination of real and virtual worlds, where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time.
- Internet of Everything (IoE) is the network of physical objects, people, processes, and data that are interconnected and communicate with each other through the internet.
- Sensors are devices that detect and measure physical phenomena, such as temperature, pressure, motion, sound, light, or chemical composition, and convert them into electrical signals.
- Software is the set of instructions and data that control the operation and behavior of a computer system or a device.
- Sensors and software are essential components of wearable computing, mixed reality, and IoE, as they enable the collection, processing, and transmission of data from the physical and digital worlds, and provide the functionality and user interface for the applications and services.
- Some examples of sensors and software for wearable computing, mixed reality, and IoE are:

  - Artificial intelligence (AI) hearing aids, which use sensors to capture sound and software to analyze and enhance it, and communicate with other devices through Bluetooth.
  - Google Glass and Microsoft HoloLens, which are head-mounted displays that use sensors to track the user's head movement and eye gaze, and software to project holographic images and information on the user's field of view.
  - Smart rings, wristbands, watches, and pins, which use sensors to monitor the user's biometric data, such as heart rate, blood pressure, and activity level, and software to display and interact with the data on a smartphone app.
  - Body-mounted sensors, which use sensors to measure and transmit the user's biological data, such as blood glucose, oxygen saturation, and electrocardiogram, for healthcare purposes, and software to analyze and alert the user, caregivers, and clinicians of any abnormality or emergency .
  - Smart sensors, which use sensors to detect and measure environmental phenomena, such as temperature, humidity, gas, fire, or intruders, and software to compute and communicate the data to other devices or systems, and perform self-diagnostics and repair.
