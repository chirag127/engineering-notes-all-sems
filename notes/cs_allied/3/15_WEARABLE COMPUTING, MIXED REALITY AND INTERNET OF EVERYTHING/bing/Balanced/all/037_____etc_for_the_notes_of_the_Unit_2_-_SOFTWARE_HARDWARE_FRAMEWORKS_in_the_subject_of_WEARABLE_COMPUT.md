## Wearable Computing

- Wearable computing is the use of devices that can be worn on the body to perform various functions, such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, or interacting with the environment.
- Wearable devices can be classified into three categories: passive, active, and interactive. Passive devices only collect data, such as fitness trackers or smartwatches. Active devices can also process or transmit data, such as smart glasses or earbuds. Interactive devices can also receive input from the user or the environment, such as virtual reality (VR) or augmented reality (AR) headsets.
- Wearable computing can provide various benefits, such as convenience, personalization, health monitoring, entertainment, education, and social interaction. However, wearable computing also poses some challenges, such as privacy, security, usability, compatibility, and battery life.

## Mixed Reality

- Mixed reality (MR) is a type of immersive technology that combines elements of the real and virtual worlds, creating a hybrid environment where physical and digital objects can coexist and interact.
- MR can be achieved by using devices that overlay digital content onto the real world, such as smart glasses or holographic displays, or by using devices that project the real world onto the virtual world, such as VR headsets with cameras or sensors.
- MR can enable various applications, such as gaming, education, training, entertainment, tourism, art, and collaboration. MR can also enhance the user's perception, cognition, and interaction with the environment, creating a more engaging and realistic experience .

## Internet of Everything

- Internet of Everything (IoE) is a concept that extends the Internet of Things (IoT) by incorporating not only devices, but also people, data, and processes into a network of connections that provide general intelligence and improved cognition across the networked environment.
- IoE can be seen as a combination of four dimensions: things, data, people, and processes. Things refer to the devices and sensors that collect and transmit data. Data refer to the information and insights that are generated and analyzed by the network. People refer to the users and stakeholders that interact with the network and each other. Processes refer to the actions and decisions that are enabled and optimized by the network.
- IoE can enable various opportunities, such as smart cities, smart homes, smart health, smart education, smart agriculture, and smart industry. IoE can also create various challenges, such as security, privacy, scalability, interoperability, and governance.
