# Wearables for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearables are devices that can be worn on the body or integrated into clothing or accessories, and that can sense, process, and communicate information.
- Wearables can be classified into three categories: passive, interactive, and proactive. Passive wearables only collect data and display it to the user or a remote system. Interactive wearables allow the user to control or manipulate the data or the device. Proactive wearables act autonomously or semi-autonomously based on the data or the user's context.
- Cybernetics is the science of communication and control in living organisms and machines. It studies the principles of feedback, adaptation, and self-organization in complex systems.
- Humanistic intelligence (HI) is a concept that defines intelligence as the result of a human being in the feedback loop of a computational process, where the human and the computer are inextricably intertwined. HI is different from artificial intelligence (AI) and natural intelligence (NI) in that it does not aim to replicate or replace human intelligence, but to augment and enhance it.
- Wearable computing is a perfect tool for embodying HI, as it allows the user to integrate the computer into their everyday life and activities, and to interact with it in a natural and intuitive way. Wearable computing can also create new forms of expression, communication, and social interaction, as well as new modes of perception and cognition.
- Some examples of wearable computing devices that embody HI are:
  - Smart glasses, such as Google Glass, that can provide augmented reality (AR) information and services to the user, such as navigation, translation, or face recognition.
  - Smart watches, such as Apple Watch, that can monitor the user's health and fitness, and provide notifications, reminders, and access to apps.
  - Smart clothing, such as Levi's Commuter Jacket, that can sense the user's gestures and control their smartphone or other devices.
  - Smart jewelry, such as Ringly, that can vibrate or light up to alert the user of incoming calls, messages, or notifications.
  - Smart tattoos, such as DuoSkin, that can change color, display information, or act as touch-sensitive interfaces.
  - Smart implants, such as Neuralink, that can interface directly with the user's brain and enable new forms of communication, learning, and enhancement.
