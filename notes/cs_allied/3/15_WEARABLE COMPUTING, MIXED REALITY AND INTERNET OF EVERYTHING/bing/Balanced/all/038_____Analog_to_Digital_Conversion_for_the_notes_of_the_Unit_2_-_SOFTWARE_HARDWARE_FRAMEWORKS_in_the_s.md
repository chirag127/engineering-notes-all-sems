# Analog to Digital Conversion

- Analog to digital conversion (ADC) is an electronic process that converts a continuous analog signal, such as a sound wave or a light intensity, into a discrete digital signal, such as a binary code, without altering its essential content .
- ADC is useful for interfacing digital circuits, such as microcontrollers, with the analog world, such as sensors, actuators, or communication channels .
- ADC involves sampling the analog signal at regular intervals, quantizing the sampled values into discrete levels, and encoding the quantized levels into binary digits .
- The performance of an ADC depends on several factors, such as the resolution, the sampling rate, the accuracy, the linearity, the noise, and the power consumption .
- There are different types of ADCs, such as successive approximation, flash, sigma-delta, dual-slope, and pipeline, each with its own advantages and disadvantages  .
- ADCs are widely used in various applications, such as digital imaging, radar, audio, video, data acquisition, and signal processing  .
