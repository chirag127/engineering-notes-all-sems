# IoT and Wearables

- IoT (Internet of Things) is a network of interconnected devices that can collect, transmit and process data without human intervention.
- Wearables are devices that can be worn on the body or embedded in clothing or accessories, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, smart jewelry, etc.
- Wearables are a subset of IoT devices that have sensors, chips, wireless communication protocols and applications that enable various functions and features for the users.
- Wearables and IoT have applications and risks in various domains, such as health, fitness, entertainment, education, security, finance, etc.

## Applications of Wearables and IoT

- Health and fitness: Wearables can monitor biometric measurements, such as heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen levels, calories burned, sleep quality, etc. and provide feedback and recommendations to the users. Wearables can also alert users or medical professionals in case of emergencies, such as falls, seizures, strokes, etc.
- Entertainment and education: Wearables can provide immersive and interactive experiences, such as virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed reality, etc. Wearables can also enhance learning and creativity, such as smart glasses that can display information, smart gloves that can translate sign language, smart rings that can control devices, etc.
- Security and finance: Wearables can enable easy and secure authentication, identification and payment, such as smart cards, wristbands, clothes and jewelry that can store personal and financial information and use biometrics, NFC, RFID, etc. Wearables can also protect users from physical and cyber threats, such as smart helmets that can detect collisions, smart vests that can detect gunshots, smart bracelets that can emit alarms, etc.

## Risks of Wearables and IoT

- Privacy and security: Wearables and IoT devices collect and transmit sensitive and personal data, such as location, health, behavior, preferences, etc. that can be accessed, hacked, stolen or misused by unauthorized parties, such as hackers, advertisers, governments, etc. Wearables and IoT devices can also be vulnerable to malware, viruses, denial-of-service attacks, etc. that can compromise their functionality and safety.
- Ethical and social: Wearables and IoT devices can raise ethical and social issues, such as consent, ownership, accountability, responsibility, etc. Wearables and IoT devices can also affect human relationships, interactions, emotions, values, etc. such as creating dependency, addiction, isolation, discrimination, etc. Wearables and IoT devices can also pose ethical dilemmas, such as who has the right to access, control, modify or delete the data collected by the devices, who is liable for the consequences of the devices' actions or inactions, etc.
