# Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE

- Cybernetics is the science of human-machine interaction that employs the principles of feedback, control and communication .
- Humanistic intelligence is the intelligence that arises because of a human being in the feedback loop of a computational process, where the human and computer are inextricably intertwined.
- Cybernetics and humanistic intelligence are related fields that explore the relationship between humans and machines, and the implications of this relationship for society, culture, ethics and identity.
- Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

  - The history and development of cybernetics, from Norbert Wiener's seminal work on control and communication in the animal and the machine to the contemporary applications of cybernetics in robotics, artificial intelligence, biotechnology and cyberculture.
  - The concept and practice of humanistic intelligence, as proposed by Steve Mann and others, which involves the use of wearable computing, mediated reality, and human-computer symbiosis to augment human perception, cognition and expression.
  - The philosophical and ethical issues raised by cybernetics and humanistic intelligence, such as the nature and limits of human intelligence, the possibility and desirability of downloading human consciousness into a machine, the role and responsibility of humans in a cybernetic world, and the impact of human-machine interaction on human identity, agency and autonomy .
  - The social and cultural implications of cybernetics and humanistic intelligence, such as the emergence of new forms of communication, collaboration, creativity, education, entertainment, and activism, as well as the challenges and opportunities of living in a networked, digital and globalized society.
