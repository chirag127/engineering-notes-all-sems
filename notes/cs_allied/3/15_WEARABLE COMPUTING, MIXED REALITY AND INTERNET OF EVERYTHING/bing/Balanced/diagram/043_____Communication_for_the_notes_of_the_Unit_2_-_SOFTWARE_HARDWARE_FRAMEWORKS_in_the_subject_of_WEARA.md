### Communication

Communication is the process of exchanging information and data between different entities or devices. In the context of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything, communication can be classified into three types:

- **Human-to-human communication**: This is the communication between two or more humans, using wearable devices or mixed reality interfaces as mediators. For example, a wearable user can communicate with a remote expert using a head-mounted display and a microphone, or a group of people can share a virtual space using wearable sensors and projectors.
- **Human-to-machine communication**: This is the communication between a human and a machine, such as a wearable device, a smart object or a virtual agent. For example, a human can control a wearable device using voice commands, gestures or eye movements, or a human can interact with a virtual character using a mixed reality interface.
- **Machine-to-machine communication**: This is the communication between two or more machines, such as wearable devices, smart objects or virtual agents, without human intervention. For example, a wearable device can communicate with other wearable devices or sensors to collect and process data, or a virtual agent can communicate with other virtual agents or smart objects to coordinate actions.

Some of the characteristics and challenges of communication in wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything are:

- **Ubiquity**: Communication can occur anytime, anywhere and with any device, using different modalities and formats. This requires the communication systems to be adaptable, scalable and interoperable.
- **Immersiveness**: Communication can create immersive and realistic experiences, where the boundaries between the real and the virtual world are blurred. This requires the communication systems to be accurate, responsive and engaging.
- **Security**: Communication can involve sensitive and personal information and data, such as biometric, location or health data. This requires the communication systems to be secure, reliable and trustworthy.
