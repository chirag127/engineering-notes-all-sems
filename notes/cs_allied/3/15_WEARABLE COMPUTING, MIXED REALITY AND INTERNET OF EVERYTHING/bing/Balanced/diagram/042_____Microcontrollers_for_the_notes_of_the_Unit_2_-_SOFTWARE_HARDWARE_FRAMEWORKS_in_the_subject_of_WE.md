### Microcontrollers

- A microcontroller is a small computer on a single VLSI integrated circuit (IC) chip that can govern a specific operation in an embedded system.
- A microcontroller contains one or more CPUs (processor cores) along with memory and programmable input/output peripherals.
- Microcontrollers are used in automatically controlled products and devices, such as automobile engine control systems, implantable medical devices, remote controls, office machines, appliances, power tools, toys and other embedded systems.
- Microcontrollers can be classified by their architecture, memory size, instruction set, peripheral features, power consumption, speed, cost, etc.
- Some examples of microcontroller families are PIC, AVR, MSP430, 8051, ARM, etc.
- Microcontrollers can be programmed using various languages, such as assembly, C, C++, Python, etc.
- Microcontrollers can communicate with other devices or sensors using various protocols, such as UART, SPI, I2C, CAN, USB, etc.
- Microcontrollers can perform various tasks, such as analog-to-digital conversion, pulse-width modulation, timers, interrupts, etc.
- Microcontrollers can be interfaced with various external components, such as LEDs, LCDs, buttons, switches, motors, speakers, etc.
- Microcontrollers can be powered by various sources, such as batteries, solar cells, USB, etc.
