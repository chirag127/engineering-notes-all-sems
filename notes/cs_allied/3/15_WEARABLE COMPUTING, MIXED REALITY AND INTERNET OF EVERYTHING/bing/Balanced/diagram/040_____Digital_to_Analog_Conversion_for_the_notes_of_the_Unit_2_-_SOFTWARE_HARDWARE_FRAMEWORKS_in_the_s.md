### Digital to Analog Conversion

- Digital to analog conversion (DAC) is the process by which digital signals (which have a binary state) are converted to analog signals (which theoretically have an infinite number of states).
- DAC is necessary when we communicate information from one system to another across a public access phone line, or when we want to reproduce high-quality sound or video from digital data .
- There are several DAC architectures, such as resistor ladder, sigma-delta, and pulse-width modulation, each with different advantages and disadvantages.
- The suitability of a DAC for a particular application is determined by figures of merit, such as resolution, accuracy, speed, power consumption, and cost.
- One of the common techniques for DAC is amplitude shift keying (ASK), which is a form of modulation where the amplitude of the analog carrier signal is varied according to the binary data.
- Another technique is frequency shift keying (FSK), which is a form of modulation where the frequency of the analog carrier signal is varied according to the binary data.
- A third technique is phase shift keying (PSK), which is a form of modulation where the phase of the analog carrier signal is varied according to the binary data.
- A fourth technique is quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM), which is a form of modulation where both the amplitude and the phase of the analog carrier signal are varied according to the binary data.
- A DAC can be used for various applications, such as audio, video, telecommunications, instrumentation, and control systems .
- A DAC can improve the quality and performance of the analog output, depending on the specifications and design of the device.
