### Parts identification

- Wearable computing is the field of computer science that deals with the design, development and application of wearable devices that can be worn on the body or embedded in clothing or accessories.
- Wearable devices can perform various functions, such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, interacting, augmenting or enhancing human capabilities.
- Wearable devices can be classified into different categories, such as:
  - Input devices: These are devices that allow the user to provide input to the system, such as voice, gesture, touch, eye movement, etc. Examples are microphones, cameras, touchscreens, keyboards, etc.
  - Output devices: These are devices that provide output to the user, such as sound, image, video, haptic, etc. Examples are speakers, headphones, displays, projectors, etc.
  - Processing devices: These are devices that perform computation, storage, or networking functions, such as processors, memory, sensors, etc. Examples are smartphones, smartwatches, smart glasses, etc.
  - Power devices: These are devices that provide or manage power supply for the system, such as batteries, chargers, solar cells, etc.
  - Communication devices: These are devices that enable data transmission or reception between the system and other devices or networks, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular, etc.
  - Sensing devices: These are devices that measure or detect physical or environmental parameters, such as temperature, humidity, heart rate, motion, etc. Examples are thermometers, accelerometers, gyroscopes, etc.
  - Actuating devices: These are devices that perform physical actions or movements, such as motors, servos, etc.
- Mixed reality is the field of computer science that deals with the creation and manipulation of virtual environments that blend with the real world, such as augmented reality, virtual reality, or mixed reality.
- Mixed reality devices can be classified into different categories, such as:
  - Head-mounted displays (HMDs): These are devices that are worn on the head and provide immersive visual and auditory experiences to the user, such as virtual reality headsets, augmented reality glasses, etc.
  - Hand-held displays: These are devices that are held in the hand and provide interactive visual and auditory experiences to the user, such as smartphones, tablets, etc.
  - Spatial displays: These are devices that project virtual images or sounds onto physical surfaces or objects, such as walls, tables, etc. Examples are projectors, speakers, etc.
  - Haptic devices: These are devices that provide tactile or force feedback to the user, such as gloves, vests, etc.
- Internet of Everything (IoE) is the field of computer science that deals with the interconnection and integration of people, processes, data, and things through the internet, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, etc.
- Internet of Everything devices can be classified into different categories, such as:
  - People: These are devices that are worn or carried by people, such as wearable devices, smartphones, etc.
  - Processes: These are devices that perform or support business or social processes, such as cloud computing, artificial intelligence, etc.
  - Data: These are devices that collect, store, or analyze data, such as sensors, databases, etc.
  - Things: These are devices that are embedded or attached to physical objects, such as smart appliances, smart vehicles, etc.
