### Case studies

- **Digital Eye Glass**: This is a wearable computing device that augments the user's vision with digital information, such as text, images, video, or audio. The device can also capture the user's perspective and share it with others. The device can be used for various applications, such as education, entertainment, navigation, communication, and personal cybernetics. One example of a digital eye glass is the EyeTap, developed by Steve Mann  . The EyeTap uses a beam splitter to combine the real and virtual images, and a camera to record the user's view. The device can also modify the user's perception of reality, such as by dimming or brightening the scene, or by adding or removing objects. This is called Augmediated Reality, a form of Mixed Reality that enhances or diminishes the user's sensory experience .
- **Smart Clothing**: This is a wearable computing device that integrates sensors, actuators, and computing elements into the fabric or accessories of the clothing. The device can monitor the user's physiological or environmental data, such as heart rate, temperature, or location, and provide feedback or assistance accordingly. The device can also communicate with other devices or networks, such as smartphones, computers, or the Internet of Things. The device can be used for various applications, such as health care, fitness, fashion, entertainment, or security. One example of smart clothing is the BioShirt, developed by Sensatex. The BioShirt is a shirt that measures the user's electrocardiogram, respiration, and temperature, and transmits the data wirelessly to a smartphone or a computer. The device can be used for monitoring the user's health or performance, or for alerting emergency services in case of a cardiac event.
- **Brain-Computer Interface**: This is a wearable computing device that connects the user's brain activity to a computer or a machine, and allows the user to control or communicate with the device using their thoughts. The device can use invasive or non-invasive methods to measure the user's brain signals, such as electroencephalography, magnetoencephalography, or functional magnetic resonance imaging. The device can also provide feedback or stimulation to the user's brain, such as auditory, visual, or electrical signals. The device can be used for various applications, such as medical, entertainment, education, or military. One example of a brain-computer interface is the Emotiv EPOC, developed by Emotiv Systems. The Emotiv EPOC is a headset that measures the user's brain waves using 14 electrodes, and translates them into commands or expressions that can be used to control a computer or a game. The device can also detect the user's emotional state, such as excitement, frustration, or boredom, and adjust the game accordingly.
