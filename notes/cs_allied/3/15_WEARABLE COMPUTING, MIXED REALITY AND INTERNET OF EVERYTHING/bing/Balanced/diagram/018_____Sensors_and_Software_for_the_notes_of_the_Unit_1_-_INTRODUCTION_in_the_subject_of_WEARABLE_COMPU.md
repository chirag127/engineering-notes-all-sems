### Sensors and Software for Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything

- Wearable computing is the use of electronic devices that are worn on the body or embedded in clothing to perform various functions, such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, or interacting with the environment.
- Mixed reality is the combination of real and virtual worlds, where physical and digital objects coexist and interact in real time.
- Internet of Everything is the concept of connecting people, processes, data, and things through the internet, enabling new levels of intelligence, efficiency, and innovation.
- Sensors are devices that detect and measure physical phenomena, such as temperature, pressure, motion, sound, light, or chemical composition, and convert them into electrical signals.
- Software is the set of instructions and data that control the operation of a computer system or a specific application.
- Sensors and software are essential components of wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything, as they enable the collection, processing, and transmission of data from the physical and digital worlds, and the creation of immersive and interactive experiences for the users.
- Some examples of sensors and software for wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything are:

  - Artificial intelligence (AI) hearing aids, which use sensors to detect the sound environment and adjust the amplification and noise reduction accordingly, and software to learn the user's preferences and optimize the hearing performance.
  - Google Glass and Microsoft's HoloLens, which are head-mounted displays that use sensors to track the user's head movements and gestures, and software to project holographic images and information onto the user's field of view.
  - Smart rings, wristbands, watches, and pins, which use sensors to monitor the user's biometric data, such as heart rate, blood pressure, sleep quality, and activity level, and software to sync with a smartphone app for display and interaction.
  - Body-mounted sensors, which use sensors to monitor and transmit biological data, such as blood glucose, oxygen saturation, electrocardiogram, and body temperature, for healthcare purposes, and software to analyze the data and alert the user or the caregiver in case of anomalies .
  - Smart Sensoria® socks, which use sensors to measure the pressure and force applied by the foot during walking or running, and software to provide real-time feedback and coaching to the user via an app and an alert system.
  - Motus Smart, which is a smart wearable device that uses sensors to measure the motion and orientation of the injured limb, and software to provide personalized rehabilitation exercises and progress tracking to the user via an app and a dashboard.
  - Smart sensors with self-diagnostics and repair capabilities, which use sensors to detect and measure hazardous gas, fire, or intruders, and software to perform self-checks, calibrations, and repairs, and to communicate the sensor status and the detected events to the user or the system.
