### Case Study 1: Digital Eye Glass

- A wearable computing device that augments the user's vision with digital information, such as text, images, video, etc.
- Developed by Steve Mann, a pioneer of wearable computing and humanistic intelligence, who coined the term "augmediated reality" to describe the mediated perception of reality through the eye glass.
- The device consists of a head-mounted display, a camera, a computer, and a wireless connection to the internet or other devices.
- The device can perform various functions, such as:
  - Enhancing the user's vision by adjusting the contrast, brightness, zoom, etc.
  - Providing contextual information about the user's surroundings, such as names, faces, directions, etc.
  - Recording and streaming the user's visual experience to others or to a personal archive.
  - Modifying the user's appearance by overlaying digital masks, filters, or effects.
  - Creating interactive and immersive environments by blending virtual and physical elements.
- The device challenges the conventional notions of privacy, security, and social interaction, as it allows the user to access, manipulate, and share visual information in real time.
- The device also exemplifies the concept of humanistic intelligence, as it creates a feedback loop between the human and the computer, and integrates the processing apparatus with the natural capabilities of the human mind and body.

### Case Study 2: Norbert Wiener and Cybernetics

- Norbert Wiener was a mathematician and philosopher who is widely regarded as the father of cybernetics, the science of communication and control in the animal and the machine.
- Wiener defined cybernetics as "the entire field of control and communication theory, whether in the machine or in the animal".
- Wiener was interested in the similarities and differences between biological and artificial systems, and how they can interact and cooperate with each other.
- Wiener developed the concept of feedback, which is the process of adjusting the output of a system based on the input from the environment or from another system.
- Wiener also explored the ethical and social implications of cybernetics, and warned about the potential dangers of automation, artificial intelligence, and information overload.
- Wiener's book, The Human Use of Human Beings, published in 1950, is a popularization of his earlier work, Cybernetics: or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine, published in 1948.
- The book investigates the impact of cybernetics on human society, culture, and values, and advocates for a humanistic approach to technology that respects human dignity, freedom, and creativity.
