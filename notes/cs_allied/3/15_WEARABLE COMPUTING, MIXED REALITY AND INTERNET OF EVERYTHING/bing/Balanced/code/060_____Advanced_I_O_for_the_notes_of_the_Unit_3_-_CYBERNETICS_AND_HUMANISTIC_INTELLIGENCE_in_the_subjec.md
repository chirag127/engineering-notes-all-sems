### Advanced I/O for Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything

- Advanced I/O refers to the input/output methods and devices that enable seamless interaction between humans and computers in wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything (IoE) scenarios.
- Wearable computing is the integration of computing devices into clothing or accessories that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, glasses, earbuds, etc.
- Mixed reality is the blending of physical and digital worlds, where real and virtual objects coexist and interact in real time, such as augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR).
- Internet of everything (IoE) is the network of people, processes, data and things that are connected and communicate with each other, creating value and opportunities for various domains and applications, such as smart cities, health care, education, etc.
- Some of the examples of advanced I/O methods and devices are:
  - Voice and speech recognition: the ability of a computer to understand and respond to human speech, such as Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant, etc.
  - Gesture and motion recognition: the ability of a computer to detect and interpret human body movements, such as Kinect, Leap Motion, Wii, etc.
  - Eye tracking and gaze estimation: the ability of a computer to measure and analyze the direction and focus of human eyes, such as Tobii, Eye Tribe, Eye Control, etc.
  - Brain-computer interface (BCI): the direct communication between a computer and the human brain, using electrodes, sensors, or implants, such as NeuroSky, Emotiv, Neuralink, etc.
  - Haptic feedback: the use of tactile sensations to convey information or create realistic experiences, such as vibration, force, pressure, temperature, etc.
  - Biometric sensors: the use of physiological or behavioral characteristics to identify or monitor humans, such as fingerprint, face, iris, heart rate, etc.
  - Wearable displays: the use of screens or projectors that can be worn on the body or embedded in clothing or accessories, such as smart glasses, smart watches, smart rings, etc.
  - Wearable cameras: the use of cameras that can be worn on the body or embedded in clothing or accessories, such as GoPro, Snapchat Spectacles, Google Clips, etc.
  - Wearable audio: the use of speakers or headphones that can be worn on the body or embedded in clothing or accessories, such as AirPods, Bose Frames, Bone conduction headphones, etc.
  - Wearable sensors: the use of devices that can measure and transmit various physical or environmental parameters, such as temperature, humidity, light, sound, etc.
