# Interfacing to the Real World

- Interfacing is the method of connecting or linking together one device, especially a computer or micro-controller, with another device or the physical world.
- Interfacing allows us to design or adapt the output and input configurations of the two electronic devices so that they can work together.
- Interfacing can be done using various methods, such as mechanical switches, LEDs, sensors, actuators, communication protocols, etc. 
- Interfacing can be classified into two types: input interfacing and output interfacing.
- Input interfacing is the process of receiving data or signals from the external world and converting them into a form that can be processed by the device. 
- Output interfacing is the process of sending data or signals from the device to the external world and converting them into a form that can be perceived by the user or another device. 
- Interfacing can also be classified into two modes: polling and interrupt.
- Polling is the mode where the device continuously checks the status of the external device or signal and responds accordingly.
- Interrupt is the mode where the device is notified by the external device or signal when it needs its service and suspends its current task to serve it.
- Interfacing can enable various applications, such as video, audio, real-world interactions, and more, by using different components and platforms, such as Raspberry Pi, Arduino, etc. 
- Interfacing can also facilitate semantic interoperability, which is the ability of different systems or devices to exchange and use information in a meaningful way.
