# Hardware for Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything

- Wearable computing is the use of small, portable, and often wireless devices that are worn on the body or embedded in clothing or accessories. These devices can perform various functions, such as sensing, computing, communicating, displaying, or interacting with the user or the environment.
- Mixed reality is the blending of the physical and the digital worlds, where virtual objects can coexist and interact with real ones. Mixed reality can be achieved by using devices such as head-mounted displays, smart glasses, projectors, or cameras that can overlay digital information onto the physical space.
- Internet of Everything is the concept of connecting any device, object, or entity to the internet and to each other, enabling data exchange, communication, automation, and intelligence. Internet of Everything can be applied to various domains, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, smart agriculture, and smart industry.
- Hardware for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything can be classified into four categories: sensors, processors, displays, and communication modules.
  - Sensors are devices that can measure physical or environmental parameters, such as temperature, pressure, motion, light, sound, or biometrics. Sensors can be used to monitor the user's health, activity, location, or context, or to capture the user's input or feedback. Sensors can be embedded in wearable devices, such as smart watches, smart bands, smart rings, smart shoes, or smart clothing, or in the environment, such as smart objects, smart furniture, or smart buildings.
  - Processors are devices that can perform computations, data processing, or logic operations, such as microcontrollers, microprocessors, or application-specific integrated circuits. Processors can be used to run the software, algorithms, or applications that enable the functionality of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything. Processors can be integrated in wearable devices, such as smart glasses, smart helmets, or smart earbuds, or in external devices, such as smartphones, tablets, or computers, that can communicate with the wearable devices via wireless connections.
  - Displays are devices that can present visual information, such as images, text, graphics, or videos, to the user or the environment. Displays can be used to show the user the output of the wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, or to provide the user with feedback, guidance, or instructions. Displays can be implemented in wearable devices, such as head-mounted displays, smart glasses, or smart lenses, that can project digital content onto the user's field of view, or in the environment, such as smart walls, smart mirrors, or smart windows, that can display digital content on the physical surfaces.
  - Communication modules are devices that can enable data transmission, reception, or exchange, such as wireless transceivers, antennas, or modems. Communication modules can be used to connect the wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything devices to the internet, to each other, or to other devices, such as smartphones, tablets, or computers, using various wireless protocols, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, or 5G. Communication modules can facilitate the data transfer, synchronization, or sharing among the devices, or enable the access to cloud services, databases, or online platforms.
