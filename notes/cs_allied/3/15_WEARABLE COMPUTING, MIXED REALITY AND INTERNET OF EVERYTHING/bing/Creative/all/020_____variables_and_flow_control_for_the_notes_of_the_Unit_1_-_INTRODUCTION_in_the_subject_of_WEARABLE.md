# Variables and Flow Control

## Variables
- Variables are named containers that store data or values in a program.
- Variables can have different data types, such as integers, floats, strings, booleans, etc.
- Variables can be declared, assigned, and used in a program to perform operations, store results, and pass arguments.
- Variables can have different scopes, such as global, local, or static, depending on where they are defined and accessed.
- Variables can be constant or mutable, depending on whether their values can be changed or not.

## Flow Control
- Flow control is the process of determining the order of execution of statements or instructions in a program.
- Flow control can be influenced by variables, expressions, conditions, and loops.
- Flow control can be divided into three basic types: sequence, selection, and iteration.
- Sequence flow is the default flow of control, where statements are executed in the order they appear in the program.
- Selection flow is the conditional flow of control, where statements are executed based on the evaluation of a condition or a choice. Examples of selection flow are if-else, switch-case, and ternary operators.
- Iteration flow is the repetitive flow of control, where statements are executed multiple times until a condition is met or a break is encountered. Examples of iteration flow are for, while, and do-while loops.
