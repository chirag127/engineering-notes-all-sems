## Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE

- Cybernetics is the interdisciplinary study of the structure and function of systems that can adapt, learn, and self-organize, such as biological, social, and artificial systems.
- Humanistic intelligence is the ability to use technology as an extension of human cognition, creativity, and communication, rather than as a tool or a substitute.
- The main objectives of this unit are to:
  - Understand the basic concepts and principles of cybernetics and its applications in various domains.
  - Explore the historical and philosophical foundations of humanistic intelligence and its implications for human-computer interaction.
  - Analyze the ethical, social, and cultural issues related to cybernetics and humanistic intelligence.
- The main topics covered in this unit are:
  - Introduction to cybernetics: definition, history, scope, and examples of cybernetic systems.
  - Feedback and control: the concepts of feedback, control, homeostasis, and adaptation in cybernetic systems.
  - Information and communication: the concepts of information, entropy, noise, redundancy, and coding in cybernetic systems.
  - Learning and self-organization: the concepts of learning, memory, intelligence, and self-organization in cybernetic systems.
  - Cybernetics and society: the applications and impacts of cybernetics in fields such as biology, psychology, sociology, economics, and politics.
  - Introduction to humanistic intelligence: definition, history, motivation, and examples of humanistic intelligence systems.
  - Human-computer symbiosis: the concept of human-computer symbiosis and its challenges and opportunities for human-computer interaction.
  - Wearable computing: the concept of wearable computing and its characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages.
  - Augmented reality: the concept of augmented reality and its types, technologies, and applications.
  - Ambient intelligence: the concept of ambient intelligence and its features, components, and scenarios.
  - Ethical, social, and cultural issues: the ethical, social, and cultural issues related to cybernetics and humanistic intelligence, such as privacy, security, identity, responsibility, and human dignity.
