# History

- Wearable computing is the field of technology that involves devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, glasses, headsets, rings, etc.
- Wearable computing has a long and diverse history, dating back to ancient times when people used beads, rings, and abacuses to perform calculations and keep track of information.
- One of the earliest examples of a wearable computer was a device hidden in a shoe that could predict the outcome of roulette by measuring the speed of the ball and the wheel. This device was invented by Edward Thorp and Claude Shannon in the 1960s and was used to cheat at casinos .
- Another pioneer of wearable computing was Steve Mann, who started experimenting with wearable devices in the late 1970s. He developed various systems that integrated cameras, displays, keyboards, and wireless communication, and coined the term "wearable computing" in 1981 .
- In the 1990s, wearable computing became more popular and accessible, thanks to the development of smaller and cheaper components, such as microprocessors, batteries, sensors, and displays. Some of the notable wearable devices from this era include the Private Eye head-mounted display, the Twiddler chording keyboard, the MIT Tin Lizzy wearable computer, and the IBM Linux Watch .
- In the 2000s, wearable computing expanded to include more applications and domains, such as fitness, health, entertainment, gaming, education, and social networking. Some of the influential wearable devices from this era include the Nike+ running system, the Fitbit activity tracker, the Looxie camera, the Google Glass, and the Oculus Rift  .
- In the 2010s, wearable computing became more mainstream and ubiquitous, thanks to the emergence of smartphones, smartwatches, and wearable platforms, such as Android Wear, Apple Watch, and Samsung Gear. Some of the current trends and challenges in wearable computing include improving battery life, enhancing user interface, ensuring privacy and security, and integrating with the Internet of Things .
