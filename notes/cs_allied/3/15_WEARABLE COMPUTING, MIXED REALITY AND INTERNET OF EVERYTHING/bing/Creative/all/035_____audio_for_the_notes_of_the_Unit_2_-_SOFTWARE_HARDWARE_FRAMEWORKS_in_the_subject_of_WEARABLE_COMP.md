## Introduction

- We will also examine some examples of existing frameworks and platforms that support the development and deployment of these applications.

## Cloud and Edge Computing

- Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence over the internet.
- Edge computing is the processing of data closer to the source, such as sensors, devices, or users, to reduce latency, bandwidth, and cost.
- Cloud and edge computing are complementary technologies that can work together to provide scalable, reliable, and efficient solutions for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications.
- For example, Azure IoT is a cloud platform that offers various services and tools for connecting, managing, and analyzing data from IoT devices and applications.
- Multi-access Edge Computing (MEC) is an edge computing paradigm that provides computing and storage resources next to low-power, low-resource mobile devices, such as wearables and smartphones. It ensures connectivity and computing opportunities through wireless networks.

## Digital Twins

- A digital twin is a virtual representation of a physical entity or system that can be used for simulation, analysis, and optimization.
- Digital twins can be integrated with wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications to create immersive and interactive experiences that bridge the physical and digital worlds.
- For example, Microsoft HoloLens is a mixed reality device that can project holograms of digital twins onto the real world, allowing users to interact with them using gestures, voice, and gaze.

## Component-Based Software Architecture

- A component-based software architecture is a design approach that divides a complex system into reusable and independent modules or components that communicate with each other through well-defined interfaces.
- A component-based software architecture can facilitate the development and maintenance of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications by enabling modularity, reusability, extensibility, and interoperability.
- For example, a component-based software framework for augmented reality (AR) systems can consist of reusable distributed services for key subproblems of AR, such as tracking, rendering, and registration, and a middleware to combine them.

## Cooperative Networking

- Cooperative networking is a communication technique that leverages the cooperation of multiple nodes or devices to improve the performance and reliability of wireless networks.
- Cooperative networking can enhance the connectivity and quality of service of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications by exploiting the diversity, redundancy, and spatial multiplexing of cooperative nodes or devices.
- For example, a cooperative networking scheme for mobile augmented reality (MAR) applications can use the cooperation of nearby smartphones, tablets, and wearables to offload computation, share data, and provide feedback.
