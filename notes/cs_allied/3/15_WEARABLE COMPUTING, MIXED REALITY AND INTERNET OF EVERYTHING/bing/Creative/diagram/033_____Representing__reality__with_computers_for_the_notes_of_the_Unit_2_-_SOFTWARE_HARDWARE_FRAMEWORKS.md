### Representing “reality” with computers

- Computers can model some aspects of reality by using data, algorithms and simulations.
- Data is a collection of facts or measurements that can be processed by a computer. Data can be represented in binary, which is a system of two symbols (0 and 1) that can encode any information.
- Algorithms are a set of rules or instructions that tell a computer how to perform a specific task or solve a problem. Algorithms can be expressed in different programming languages or pseudocode.
- Simulations are computer programs that mimic the behavior of a real system or phenomenon by using data and algorithms. Simulations can be used to study, test, predict or visualize complex scenarios that are difficult or impossible to observe or manipulate in reality.
- Some examples of computer simulations are:
  - Weather forecasting: using data from satellites, radars, sensors and historical records, and algorithms based on mathematical and physical models, to predict the atmospheric conditions and climate changes.
  - Nuclear physics: using data from experiments and observations, and algorithms based on quantum mechanics and relativity, to study the structure and interactions of atomic nuclei and subatomic particles.
  - Virtual reality: using data from sensors, cameras and user inputs, and algorithms based on computer graphics and artificial intelligence, to create immersive and interactive environments that simulate the real or imagined world.
