# WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the use of devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, or virtual reality headsets, to collect and analyse data, provide feedback, or enhance user experience.
- Mixed reality is a type of immersive technology that combines the physical and digital worlds, allowing users to interact with virtual objects and environments in real time, using devices such as head-mounted displays, spatial audio, or gesture recognition.
- Internet of Everything (IoE) is a network of connections between people, things, data, and processes that provide general intelligence and improved cognition across the networked environment. It is an extension of the Internet of Things (IoT), which focuses on the connectivity of devices and sensors.

Some of the applications and benefits of these technologies are:

- Wearable computing can enable health and fitness monitoring, personal assistance, entertainment, education, social interaction, and accessibility .
- Mixed reality can enable immersive learning, gaming, entertainment, collaboration, communication, and creativity.
- Internet of Everything can enable smart cities, smart homes, smart industries, smart agriculture, smart healthcare, and smart transportation .

Some of the challenges and limitations of these technologies are:

- Wearable computing can pose privacy, security, ethical, and social issues, as well as technical challenges such as battery life, compatibility, and usability .
- Mixed reality can pose human factors, ethical, and social issues, as well as technical challenges such as latency, resolution, and cost.
- Internet of Everything can pose scalability, interoperability, security, and governance issues, as well as technical challenges such as data management, analytics, and integration.
