### AR versus VR

- AR stands for **augmented reality**, which is a technology that overlays digital information or objects onto the real world, enhancing the user's perception and interaction with the environment.
- VR stands for **virtual reality**, which is a technology that creates a fully immersive and simulated environment, replacing the user's sensory input and isolating them from the real world.
- Both AR and VR are examples of **mixed reality**, which is a spectrum of technologies that combine the real and virtual worlds in different ways and degrees.
- Some of the differences between AR and VR are:

  - AR **adds** to the real world, while VR **replaces** it.
  - AR **enhances** the user's senses, while VR **substitutes** them.
  - AR **connects** the user with the environment, while VR **isolates** them from it.
  - AR **requires** a transparent display or a camera, while VR **requires** a closed display or a headset.
  - AR **can be used** in any setting or context, while VR **requires** a dedicated space or equipment.
  - AR **has more** applications and use cases, while VR **has more** challenges and limitations.

- Some of the similarities between AR and VR are:

  - Both AR and VR are **based** on computer-generated graphics and sounds.
  - Both AR and VR are **designed** to create immersive and interactive experiences for the user.
  - Both AR and VR are **influenced** by the principles of cybernetics and humanistic intelligence, which are the fields of study that explore the relationship between humans and machines, and the potential of enhancing human capabilities and creativity through technology.
  - Both AR and VR are **part of** the emerging domain of wearable computing, which is the integration of computing devices into the user's clothing or body, enabling constant and seamless access to information and communication.
  - Both AR and VR are **connected** to the internet of everything, which is the network of physical objects, people, and processes that are embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity, enabling data exchange and interaction across multiple domains and platforms.
