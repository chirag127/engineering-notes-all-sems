### Interfacing to the Real World

Interfacing is the method of connecting or linking together one device, especially a computer or micro-controller, with another device or the real world. Interfacing allows us to design or adapt the output and input configurations of the two electronic devices so that they can work together.

Some of the topics covered in this section are:

- Input interfacing circuits: These are circuits that allow us to use mechanical switches, sensors, or other devices as inputs to the computer or micro-controller. Input interfacing circuits can use different techniques such as polling or interrupts to communicate with the computer or micro-controller .
- Output interfacing circuits: These are circuits that allow us to use LEDs, displays, motors, or other devices as outputs from the computer or micro-controller. Output interfacing circuits can use different techniques such as parallel or serial communication, pulse width modulation, or digital to analog conversion to control the output devices.
- Communication interfacing circuits: These are circuits that allow us to use various protocols and standards to communicate with other devices or networks. Communication interfacing circuits can use different techniques such as UART, SPI, I2C, Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or USB to transfer data between devices  .
- Real-world applications: These are examples of how interfacing can be used to create various projects that involve video, audio, sensors, actuators, or other real-world interactions. Real-world applications can use different platforms such as Raspberry Pi, Arduino, or BeagleBone to implement interfacing with embedded Linux or other operating systems .
