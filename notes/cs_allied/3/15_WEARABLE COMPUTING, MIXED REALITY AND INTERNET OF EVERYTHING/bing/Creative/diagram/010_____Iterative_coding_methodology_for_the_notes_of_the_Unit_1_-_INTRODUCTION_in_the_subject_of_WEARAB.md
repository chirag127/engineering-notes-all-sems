### Iterative coding methodology

- Iterative coding methodology is a way of developing a product or service in phases, with each phase building on the previous one.
- The basic idea behind it is that development takes place through repeated cycles (iterations) and in small parts at a time.
- The iterative methodology is commonly used in software development but is often applied in other fields.
- The key advantage of this method is that you can test your ideas early and often. This means less wasted time, money and resources later on when it comes to implementing your product or service.
- The iterative methodology also allows you to adapt to changing requirements and feedback from users or stakeholders.
- The iterative methodology consists of four main phases: planning, design, implementation and evaluation.
- Planning: This phase involves defining the scope, objectives, deliverables and schedule of the project.
- Design: This phase involves creating a prototype or a mock-up of the product or service, based on the requirements and specifications.
- Implementation: This phase involves coding, testing and debugging the product or service, based on the design.
- Evaluation: This phase involves reviewing, assessing and validating the product or service, based on the criteria and feedback.
- The iterative methodology can be applied to different types of projects, such as waterfall, agile, spiral or incremental.
- The iterative methodology can also be combined with other methods, such as user-centered design, rapid prototyping or test-driven development.
- Iterative coding is a process where you code a small piece of your project and then test it, then code the next piece and test it, so on and so forth.
- Iterative coding helps you to avoid errors, bugs and complexity in your code, by breaking it down into manageable chunks.
- Iterative coding also helps you to improve your code quality, readability and maintainability, by following the principles of refactoring, abstraction and modularity.
- Iterative coding requires you to have a clear understanding of the problem, the solution and the tools you are using.
- Iterative coding also requires you to have a good testing strategy, such as unit testing, integration testing or end-to-end testing.
