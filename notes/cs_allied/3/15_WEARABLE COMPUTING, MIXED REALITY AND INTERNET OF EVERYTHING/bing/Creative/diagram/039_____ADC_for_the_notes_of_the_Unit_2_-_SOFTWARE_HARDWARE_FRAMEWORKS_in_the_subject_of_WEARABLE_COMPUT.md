### ADC

Analog-to-digital converter (ADC) is a circuit that converts a continuous voltage value (analog) to a binary value (digital) that can be understood by a digital device which could then be used for digital computation. ADCs are essential for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, as they enable the processing and communication of analog signals such as sound, light, temperature, pressure, etc.

Some of the topics related to ADCs in the context of wearable computing are:

- ADC drivers: These are specialty amplifiers that are designed specifically to work alongside ADCs, including successive approximation, pipelined and delta-sigma based architectures. They provide an instantaneous charge to ensure that the sampling node is settled within the track time, thus minimizing any distortion related to settling. They also provide a hardware pin that enables the user to level shift the common mode voltage.
- ADC architectures: There are different types of ADCs based on their architecture, such as flash, successive approximation, pipelined, delta-sigma, etc. Each type has its own advantages and disadvantages in terms of speed, resolution, power consumption, complexity, etc. For example, flash ADCs are the fastest but consume the most power and require the most components, while delta-sigma ADCs are the slowest but have the highest resolution and the lowest power consumption.
- ADC design for wearable sensors: Wearable sensors require low-power, low-noise, and high-resolution ADCs to capture and transmit the biometric signals such as electrocardiogram (ECG), electroencephalogram (EEG), etc. One of the approaches to design such ADCs is to use delta-modulation, which is a technique that encodes the signal by the slope of the analog input rather than the amplitude. This reduces the number of bits required to represent the signal and saves power through non-uniform sampling. Another approach is to use asynchronous level crossing ADCs, which are based on the principle of detecting the level crossings of the analog input and generating a digital output accordingly. This eliminates the need for a clock and reduces the power consumption and the quantization noise.
