### Hardware for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Hardware refers to the physical components and devices that enable the functionality and interaction of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications.
- Hardware can be classified into different categories based on the type, purpose, form factor, and connectivity of the devices.
- Some of the common hardware categories are:

  - **Sensors**: These are devices that measure or detect physical phenomena, such as motion, temperature, light, sound, etc. Sensors can provide input data for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, such as activity tracking, gesture recognition, environmental monitoring, etc. Sensors can be embedded in wearable devices, such as smartwatches, glasses, helmets, etc., or attached to external objects, such as furniture, appliances, vehicles, etc. Sensors can communicate with other devices or systems using wired or wireless connections, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, etc.  
  - **Actuators**: These are devices that produce physical effects, such as movement, vibration, sound, light, etc. Actuators can provide output or feedback for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, such as haptic feedback, audio feedback, visual feedback, etc. Actuators can be embedded in wearable devices, such as smartwatches, glasses, helmets, etc., or attached to external objects, such as furniture, appliances, vehicles, etc. Actuators can communicate with other devices or systems using wired or wireless connections, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, etc.  
  - **Displays**: These are devices that present visual information, such as text, images, videos, graphics, etc. Displays can provide output or feedback for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, such as augmented reality, virtual reality, information visualization, etc. Displays can be embedded in wearable devices, such as smartwatches, glasses, helmets, etc., or attached to external objects, such as furniture, appliances, vehicles, etc. Displays can communicate with other devices or systems using wired or wireless connections, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, etc.  
  - **Processors**: These are devices that perform computations, such as arithmetic, logic, control, etc. Processors can provide the functionality and intelligence for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, such as data processing, data analysis, data storage, data transmission, etc. Processors can be embedded in wearable devices, such as smartwatches, glasses, helmets, etc., or attached to external objects, such as furniture, appliances, vehicles, etc. Processors can communicate with other devices or systems using wired or wireless connections, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, etc.  
  - **Batteries**: These are devices that store and supply electrical energy, such as voltage, current, power, etc. Batteries can provide the power source for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, such as powering the sensors, actuators, displays, processors, etc. Batteries can be embedded in wearable devices, such as smartwatches, glasses, helmets, etc., or attached to external objects, such as furniture, appliances, vehicles, etc. Batteries can communicate with other devices or systems using wired or wireless connections, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, NFC, etc.  

- Hardware can also be classified into different categories based on the level of immersion and reality of the devices.
