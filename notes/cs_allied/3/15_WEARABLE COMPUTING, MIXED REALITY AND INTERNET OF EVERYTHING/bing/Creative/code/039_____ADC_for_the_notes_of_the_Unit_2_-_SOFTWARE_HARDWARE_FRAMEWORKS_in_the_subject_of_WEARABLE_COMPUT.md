### ADC for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- ADC stands for Analog to Digital Converter, a device that converts analog signals into digital signals for processing and communication.
- ADC is an essential component for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, as they enable the interaction between the physical world and the digital world.
- ADC can be classified into different types based on the conversion method, resolution, sampling rate, power consumption, accuracy, etc.
- Some of the common types of ADC are:
  - Flash ADC: Uses a parallel array of comparators to compare the input voltage with a set of reference voltages and generate a binary code. It is the fastest type of ADC, but also the most expensive and power-hungry.
  - Successive Approximation Register (SAR) ADC: Uses a binary search algorithm to find the closest match to the input voltage. It is slower than flash ADC, but more efficient and accurate.
  - Sigma-Delta (ΣΔ) ADC: Uses an oversampling technique to filter out the noise and quantization errors from the input signal. It is the slowest type of ADC, but also the most precise and low-power.
  - Pipeline ADC: Uses a series of sub-ADCs to divide the input voltage range into smaller segments and convert them sequentially. It is a compromise between speed and accuracy, and suitable for high-resolution applications.
- ADC can be integrated with different software and hardware frameworks to enable various applications of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything, such as:
  - Sensor networks: ADC can be used to convert the analog signals from various sensors, such as temperature, pressure, humidity, light, etc., into digital signals that can be transmitted and processed by a central node or a cloud server.
  - Biomedical devices: ADC can be used to convert the analog signals from various biosensors, such as electrocardiogram (ECG), electroencephalogram (EEG), electromyogram (EMG), etc., into digital signals that can be analyzed and displayed by a wearable device or a smartphone.
  - Augmented reality: ADC can be used to convert the analog signals from various cameras, microphones, speakers, etc., into digital signals that can be processed and mixed with virtual content by a head-mounted display or a smart glass.
  - Internet of things: ADC can be used to convert the analog signals from various actuators, such as motors, valves, switches, etc., into digital signals that can be controlled and monitored by a smart device or a web application.
