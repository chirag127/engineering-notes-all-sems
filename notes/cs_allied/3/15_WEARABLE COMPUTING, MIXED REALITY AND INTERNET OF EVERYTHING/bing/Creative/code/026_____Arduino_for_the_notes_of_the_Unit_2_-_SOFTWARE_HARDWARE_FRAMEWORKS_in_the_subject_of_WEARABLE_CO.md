### Arduino

Arduino is an open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software. Arduino boards are able to read inputs - light on a sensor, a finger on a button, or a Twitter message - and turn it into an output - activating a motor, turning on an LED, publishing something online.

Some key features of Arduino are:

- Arduino boards are based on single-board microcontrollers and microcontroller kits that can be programmed using the Arduino IDE (Integrated Development Environment) or other compatible software.
- Arduino boards can communicate with other devices using standard protocols such as serial, I2C, SPI, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Ethernet, etc.
- Arduino boards can be powered by USB, batteries, solar panels, or external power sources.
- Arduino boards can be extended with shields, modules, sensors, actuators, and other components that can be plugged into the board or connected with wires.
- Arduino boards can be used for various applications such as robotics, IoT (Internet of Things), wearable computing, mixed reality, art, education, etc.

Some examples of Arduino boards are:

- Arduino Uno: The most popular and widely used Arduino board. It has 14 digital input/output pins, 6 analog inputs, a 16 MHz quartz crystal, a USB connection, a power jack, an ICSP header, and a reset button.
- Arduino Nano: A small and breadboard-friendly Arduino board. It has 14 digital input/output pins, 8 analog inputs, a 16 MHz quartz crystal, a mini USB connection, an ICSP header, and a reset button.
- Arduino Mega: A large and powerful Arduino board. It has 54 digital input/output pins, 16 analog inputs, a 16 MHz quartz crystal, a USB connection, a power jack, an ICSP header, and a reset button.
- Arduino Due: A 32-bit ARM-based Arduino board. It has 54 digital input/output pins, 12 analog inputs, a 84 MHz clock, a USB OTG capable connection, 2 DAC (digital to analog), 2 TWI, a power jack, an SPI header, a JTAG header, a reset button and an erase button.
- Arduino MKR: A family of Arduino boards designed for IoT applications. They have 22 digital input/output pins, 7 analog inputs, a 32-bit SAMD21 microcontroller, a micro USB connection, a Li-Po battery connector, and an integrated Wi-Fi or LoRa module.
