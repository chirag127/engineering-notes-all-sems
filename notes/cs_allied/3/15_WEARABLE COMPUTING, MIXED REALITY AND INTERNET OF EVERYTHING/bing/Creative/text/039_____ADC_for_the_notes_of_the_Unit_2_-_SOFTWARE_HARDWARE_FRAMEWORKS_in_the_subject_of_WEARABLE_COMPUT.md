### ADC for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- ADC stands for **analog-to-digital converter**, a circuit that converts a continuous voltage value (analog) to a binary value (digital) that can be understood by a digital device.
- ADCs are essential for wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything applications, as they enable the processing and communication of analog signals such as sound, light, temperature, pressure, etc. from sensors and actuators.
- ADCs can be classified into different types based on their architecture, resolution, sampling rate, power consumption, accuracy, etc. Some common types are:
  - **Successive approximation ADC**: This type of ADC uses a comparator and a digital-to-analog converter (DAC) to compare the input analog signal with a series of binary values and find the closest match. It is fast, accurate and widely used in microcontrollers and data acquisition systems.
  - **Pipelined ADC**: This type of ADC divides the conversion process into several stages, each consisting of a sample-and-hold circuit, a sub-ADC and a sub-DAC. The output of each stage is passed to the next stage with a delay, forming a pipeline. It is suitable for high-speed and high-resolution applications such as video and imaging.
  - **Delta-sigma ADC**: This type of ADC uses a feedback loop to oversample the input analog signal and produce a stream of single-bit values that represent the difference (delta) between the input and the output of a DAC. The stream is then filtered and decimated to obtain the final digital output. It is ideal for low-speed and high-accuracy applications such as audio and biomedical signals .
  - **Level crossing ADC**: This type of ADC uses a comparator and a timer to sample the input analog signal only when it crosses a certain threshold level. It is based on the principle of non-uniform sampling, which saves power and bandwidth by avoiding unnecessary samples. It is suitable for wearable devices that require low-power and low-data-rate ADCs.
- ADCs can be designed and implemented using various hardware and software frameworks, such as integrated circuits, microcontrollers, field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs), digital signal processors (DSPs), etc. The choice of the framework depends on the application requirements, such as performance, cost, size, flexibility, etc.
