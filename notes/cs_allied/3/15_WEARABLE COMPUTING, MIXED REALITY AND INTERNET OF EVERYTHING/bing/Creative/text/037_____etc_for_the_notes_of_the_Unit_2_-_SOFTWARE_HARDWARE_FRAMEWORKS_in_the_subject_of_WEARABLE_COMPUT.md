### Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS

- Wearable computing is the integration of computing devices into clothing or accessories that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, or virtual reality headsets.
- Mixed reality is a type of immersive technology that combines the physical and digital worlds, allowing users to interact with virtual objects and environments in real time, using wearable devices such as head-mounted displays or smart glasses.
- Internet of Everything (IoE) is a concept that extends the Internet of Things (IoT) by including not only devices and sensors, but also people, data, and processes, creating a network of intelligence and value across the connected environment.
- The software and hardware frameworks for wearable computing, mixed reality, and IoE are the components and systems that enable the development, deployment, and operation of these technologies, such as operating systems, platforms, applications, protocols, standards, interfaces, and architectures.
- Some examples of software and hardware frameworks for wearable computing are:
  - Android Wear: an operating system based on Android that runs on smartwatches and other wearables, providing access to Google services, notifications, voice control, and fitness tracking.
  - Apple WatchOS: an operating system based on iOS that runs on Apple Watch, offering features such as Siri, Apple Pay, health and fitness monitoring, and integration with iPhone and other Apple devices.
  - Fitbit OS: an operating system that runs on Fitbit devices, supporting apps, watch faces, notifications, music, and health and fitness tracking.
  - Arduino: an open-source platform that consists of hardware and software for creating interactive electronic projects, such as wearable devices, sensors, and IoT applications.
  - Raspberry Pi: a low-cost, credit-card-sized computer that can run various operating systems and be used for various projects, such as wearable computing, IoT, and mixed reality.
