### Inter-Integrated Circuit

- Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) is a serial communication bus that connects low-speed peripherals to a motherboard, mobile phone, embedded system or other electronic devices   .
- I2C was invented in 1982 by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP Semiconductors) and is also known as a two-wire interface .
- I2C requires only two wires: a serial data line (SDA) and a serial clock line (SCL) that carry information between the devices connected to the bus  .
- I2C can support up to 1008 slave devices and multiple masters, where each device is recognized by a unique address  .
- I2C is a packet-switched protocol, where each packet consists of a start bit, an address byte, a data byte, an acknowledge bit, and a stop bit .
- I2C is widely used in applications where low costs and ease-of-implementation take priority over high speed, such as sensors, EEPROMs, LCDs, and touchscreens  .
- I2C is also compatible with other protocols, such as SMBus and PMBus, that are derived from it .
