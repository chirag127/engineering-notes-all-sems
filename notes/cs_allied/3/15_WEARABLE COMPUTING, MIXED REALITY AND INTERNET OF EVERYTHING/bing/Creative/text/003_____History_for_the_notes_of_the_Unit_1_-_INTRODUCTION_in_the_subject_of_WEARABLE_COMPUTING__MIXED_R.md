### History

- Wearable computing is the field of technology that involves devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, glasses, headsets, rings, etc.
- The history of wearable computing can be traced back to different devices and inventions that were used for various purposes, such as calculation, communication, navigation, entertainment, health, etc.
- Some examples of early wearable devices are:

  - The abacus ring, a 16th-century device that could perform simple arithmetic operations by sliding beads on a metal ring.
  - The covert timing devices hidden in shoes by Edward Thorp and Claude Shannon in the 1960s and 1970s, which were used to cheat at roulette by predicting the outcome of the wheel.
  - The wearable computer rig by Steve Mann, a professor at the University of Toronto, who is considered the father of wearable computing. He started wearing a backpack-sized computer with a head-mounted display and a camera in the late 1970s, and later developed smaller and more integrated systems .
  - The Trekker, a wearable computer system developed by Thad Starner and others at MIT in 1993, which used a kit made by Park Enterprises, a Private Eye display, and the Twiddler chording keyboard. The system was used for augmented reality applications, such as navigation, translation, and note-taking .
  - The AiRScouter, a head-mounted display developed by Brother Industries in 2010, which projected images onto the retina of the user. The device was designed for industrial and military use cases, such as inspection, maintenance, and training.
  - The Zypad, a wrist-mounted computer developed by Eurotech in 2010, which featured a touchscreen, a keyboard, a camera, a GPS, and wireless connectivity. The device was aimed at workers in harsh environments, such as firefighters, soldiers, and medical personnel.
  - The Fitbit, a wearable fitness tracker launched in 2009, which measured steps, distance, calories, and sleep quality. The device was one of the first consumer-oriented wearables that popularized the concept of quantified self and personal health monitoring.
  - The Nike+, a wearable system that consisted of a sensor embedded in a shoe and a receiver attached to an iPod or iPhone. The system tracked the user's running performance and synced the data with an online platform. The system was launched in 2006 and was one of the first examples of wearable tech integrated with social media and gamification.
  - The Looxie, a wearable camera that could be clipped to the ear and streamed live video to a smartphone. The device was launched in 2008 and was one of the first wearable cameras that enabled lifelogging and social sharing.

- The history of wearable computing shows the evolution of the technology, design, and applications of wearable devices over time. The current trends and challenges in wearable computing include:

  - Improving the battery life, performance, and usability of wearable devices.
  - Developing new sensors, displays, and interfaces that can enhance the user experience and interaction.
  - Addressing the privacy, security, and ethical issues that arise from the collection and use of personal data by wearable devices.
  - Exploring the social and cultural implications and impacts of wearable devices on human behavior and society.
  - Creating new applications and services that can leverage the potential of wearable devices for various domains, such as education, entertainment, health, etc.
