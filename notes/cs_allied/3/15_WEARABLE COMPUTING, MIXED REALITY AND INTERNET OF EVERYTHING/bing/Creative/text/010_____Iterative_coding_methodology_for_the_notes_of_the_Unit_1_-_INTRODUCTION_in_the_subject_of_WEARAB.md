### Iterative coding methodology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Iterative coding methodology is a way of developing a product or service in phases, with each phase building on the previous one.
- The basic idea behind it is that development takes place through repeated cycles (iterations) and in small parts at a time.
- The iterative methodology is commonly used in software development but is often applied in other fields.
- The key advantage of this method is that you can test your ideas early and often. This means less wasted time, money and resources later on when it comes to implementing your product or service.
- The iterative process can help you reduce risk, manage efficiency, and approach problems in a more flexible and dynamic way.
- Iterative coding is a process where you code a small piece of your project and then test it, then code the next piece and test it, so on and so forth.
- Iterative coding can help you improve your code quality, debug errors, and refine your design as you go along.
- Iterative coding can also help you learn from your mistakes and feedback, and adapt to changing requirements and user needs.
- Iterative coding can be applied to various domains, such as wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything, where innovation and user experience are crucial.
- Some examples of iterative coding in these domains are:

  - Developing a smartwatch app that tracks your health and fitness data, and iteratively adding new features and functionalities based on user feedback and testing.
  - Creating a virtual reality game that immerses you in a realistic environment, and iteratively enhancing the graphics, sound, and interaction based on user preferences and performance.
  - Building a smart home system that connects your devices and appliances, and iteratively optimizing the security, efficiency, and convenience based on user behavior and feedback.
