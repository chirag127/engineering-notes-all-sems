### Laptop for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing is the field of designing and developing devices that can be worn on the body, such as smartwatches, smart glasses, fitness trackers, etc. 
- Wearable devices usually have microprocessors, batteries, sensors, and wireless connectivity to collect and transmit data to other electronics, such as mobile devices or laptops. 
- A laptop for wearable computing should have the following features:
  - A rugged design that can withstand harsh environments, such as dust, water, shock, and vibration. 
  - A bright and high-resolution display that can be easily viewed indoors and outdoors. 
  - A long battery life that can support continuous use and hot-swappable batteries for extended operation. 
  - A fast and reliable wireless connection that can sync with wearable devices and the internet. 
  - A powerful processor and sufficient memory that can run multiple applications and handle large amounts of data. 
  - A compatible operating system that can support various wearable platforms, such as Android, FreeRTOS, or Windows.   
  - A user-friendly interface that can interact with wearable devices and provide feedback and guidance.  
- Some examples of laptops that meet these criteria are:
  - Getac B360: A fully rugged laptop with a 13.3-inch full HD touch display, dual hot-swappable batteries, and a best-in-class warranty. 
  - Dell Latitude 7424 Rugged Extreme: A durable laptop with a 14-inch full HD touch display, a long-lasting battery, and a modular design. 
  - Panasonic Toughbook 55: A versatile laptop with a 14-inch full HD touch display, a modular bay, and a glove-enabled touchpad.
