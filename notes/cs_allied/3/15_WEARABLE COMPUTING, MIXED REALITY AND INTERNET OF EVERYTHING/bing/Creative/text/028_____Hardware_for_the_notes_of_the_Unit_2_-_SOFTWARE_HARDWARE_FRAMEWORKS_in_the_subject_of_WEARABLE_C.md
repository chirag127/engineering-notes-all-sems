### Hardware for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Hardware refers to the physical components and devices that enable the functionality and interaction of wearable computing, mixed reality and internet of everything systems.
- Hardware can be classified into different categories based on the type, purpose, function, and form factor of the devices.
- Some of the common hardware categories are:
  - Sensors: devices that measure or detect physical phenomena, such as motion, temperature, light, sound, etc. Sensors provide input data to the system and enable context-awareness and interactivity. Examples of sensors are accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers, cameras, microphones, etc.
  - Actuators: devices that produce physical effects or actions, such as movement, vibration, sound, light, etc. Actuators provide output feedback to the user and enable haptic and multimodal interaction. Examples of actuators are motors, speakers, LEDs, etc.
  - Processors: devices that perform computation and data processing, such as arithmetic, logic, control, etc. Processors provide the intelligence and functionality of the system and enable data analysis and decision making. Examples of processors are microcontrollers, microprocessors, GPUs, etc.
  - Memory: devices that store data and information, such as instructions, programs, variables, etc. Memory provides the storage and retrieval of data and information for the system and enables data persistence and access. Examples of memory are RAM, ROM, flash, etc.
  - Communication: devices that enable data transmission and reception, such as signals, messages, packets, etc. Communication provides the connectivity and networking of the system and enables data exchange and synchronization. Examples of communication are Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, etc.
  - Power: devices that provide or manage the energy supply for the system, such as voltage, current, etc. Power provides the operation and maintenance of the system and enables energy efficiency and management. Examples of power are batteries, chargers, converters, etc.
  - Display: devices that present visual information to the user, such as images, text, graphics, etc. Display provides the user interface and visualization of the system and enables visual feedback and interaction. Examples of display are LCD, OLED, LED, etc.
- Hardware can also be classified into different categories based on the level of immersion and reality of the system, such as:
  - Wearable computing: hardware that is worn or attached to the body, such as clothing, accessories, implants, etc. Wearable computing provides personal and ubiquitous computing and enables seamless and natural interaction. Examples of wearable computing are smartwatches, smart glasses, fitness trackers, etc.
  - Mixed reality: hardware that blends the physical and virtual worlds, such as augmenting, enhancing, or replacing the real environment with digital content. Mixed reality provides immersive and realistic computing and enables spatial and contextual interaction. Examples of mixed reality are augmented reality, virtual reality, holograms, etc.
  - Internet of everything: hardware that connects everything to the internet, such as objects, people, animals, etc. Internet of everything provides distributed and collaborative computing and enables data sharing and communication. Examples of internet of everything are smart home, smart city, smart grid, etc.
