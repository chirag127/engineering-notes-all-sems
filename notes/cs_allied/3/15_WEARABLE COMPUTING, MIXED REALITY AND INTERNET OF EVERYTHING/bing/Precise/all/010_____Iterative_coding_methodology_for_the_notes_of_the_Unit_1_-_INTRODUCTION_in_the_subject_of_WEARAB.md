# Iterative Coding Methodology

Iterative coding methodology is a software development process that involves the repeated refinement of code through multiple iterations. This approach is commonly used in the development of software applications, particularly in the field of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything.

The key features of iterative coding methodology include:

1. **Incremental development**: The software is developed in small increments, with each increment building upon the previous one. This allows for the early detection and resolution of issues, and enables the development team to respond quickly to changing requirements.

2. **Continuous feedback**: Feedback from users and stakeholders is continuously sought and incorporated into the development process. This helps to ensure that the final product meets the needs and expectations of its intended audience.

3. **Frequent testing**: Testing is performed frequently throughout the development process to identify and address issues as early as possible. This helps to improve the quality of the final product and reduce the risk of defects.

4. **Adaptive planning**: The development plan is regularly reviewed and updated to reflect changes in requirements, technology, and other factors. This helps to ensure that the development process remains on track and that the final product is delivered on time and within budget.

Overall, the iterative coding methodology is a flexible and responsive approach to software development that is well-suited to the rapidly-evolving field of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything. By incorporating feedback, testing, and adaptive planning into the development process, this methodology helps to ensure that the final product is of high quality and meets the needs of its intended audience.
