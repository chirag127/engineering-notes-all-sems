# Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION: Circuit Theory

Circuit theory is a branch of electrical engineering that deals with the analysis and design of electric circuits. It is used to understand the behavior of electric circuits and to predict their performance. Some key concepts in circuit theory include:

1. **Circuit elements**: These are the basic building blocks of electric circuits, such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors. Each element has its own characteristic behavior and is used to achieve a specific function in the circuit.

2. **Ohm's law**: This is a fundamental law of circuit theory that relates the voltage across a resistor to the current flowing through it. It states that the voltage across a resistor is equal to the product of the current flowing through it and its resistance.

3. **Kirchhoff's laws**: These are two laws that are used to analyze electric circuits. Kirchhoff's current law states that the sum of the currents entering a node in a circuit must equal the sum of the currents leaving that node. Kirchhoff's voltage law states that the sum of the voltage drops around any closed loop in a circuit must equal zero.

4. **Network analysis**: This is the process of analyzing a circuit to determine its behavior. It involves using techniques such as nodal analysis and mesh analysis to solve for the voltages and currents in the circuit.

5. **Thevenin's and Norton's theorems**: These are two theorems that are used to simplify the analysis of complex circuits. They allow a circuit to be replaced by an equivalent circuit that is simpler to analyze.

Circuit theory is an important foundation for the study of wearable computing, mixed reality, and the Internet of Everything, as these technologies rely on the use of electric circuits to function. Understanding circuit theory can help in the design and analysis of these systems.
