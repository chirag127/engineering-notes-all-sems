### Analog to Digital Conversion

Analog to Digital Conversion (ADC) is the process of converting an analog signal into a digital signal. This is an important process in the field of Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything, as it allows for the processing and manipulation of real-world signals by digital systems.

1. **Analog Signals**: Analog signals are continuous signals that can take on any value within a given range. These signals are commonly found in the real world, such as in sound waves, temperature readings, and light intensity.

2. **Digital Signals**: Digital signals, on the other hand, are discrete signals that can only take on a finite number of values. These signals are represented using binary code, with each value being represented by a series of 0s and 1s.

3. **Sampling**: The first step in the ADC process is sampling. This involves taking periodic measurements of the analog signal at regular intervals. The rate at which these measurements are taken is known as the sampling rate.

4. **Quantization**: The next step in the ADC process is quantization. This involves assigning a discrete value to each of the sampled measurements. This is done by dividing the range of possible values into a finite number of levels, and assigning each measurement to the nearest level.

5. **Encoding**: The final step in the ADC process is encoding. This involves representing each of the quantized values using binary code. This allows for the digital representation of the analog signal, which can then be processed and manipulated by digital systems.

In summary, Analog to Digital Conversion is a crucial process in the field of Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality and Internet of Everything, as it allows for the processing and manipulation of real-world signals by digital systems. The process involves sampling the analog signal, quantizing the sampled measurements, and encoding the quantized values using binary code.
