# WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

- Wearable computing refers to the use of small, portable electronic devices that are worn on the body or attached to clothing. These devices are often equipped with sensors and can connect to the internet or other devices to exchange data.

- Mixed reality is a combination of virtual reality and augmented reality, where digital and physical objects coexist and interact in real-time.

- The Internet of Everything (IoE) is a concept that extends the Internet of Things (IoT) to include people, processes, and data, in addition to connected devices.

- Wearable devices connect people to the IoE through direct contact with the body, such as on the wrist with smartwatches or fitness trackers, on the face with smart glasses or necklaces, and virtual reality (VR) headsets.

- Wearable mobile computers like Magic Leap One or HoloLens 2 are capable of augmented reality (AR), mixed reality (MR), and spatial computing. They offer a new pattern of computing, with displays that feel vast when compared to screens because of their ability to project the digital into the physical space all around you.

- In the future, wearable technology could support the creation of an entire user-generated, non-verbal language. With a single swipe, consumers could send a loved one a message they can feel through unique light, sound, and vibration patterns on their wearable device.

- The integration of mixed reality and the Internet of Things can also serve as an assistive technology for elderly people, aiding them in daily activities such as analyzing the environment, recognizing and searching for objects, wayfinding, and navigation.

- The Internet of Things (IoT) describes the growing network of internet-enabled devices, including wearable technology. Along with other emerging technologies such as AI, the IoT is part of the Fourth Industrial Revolution.
