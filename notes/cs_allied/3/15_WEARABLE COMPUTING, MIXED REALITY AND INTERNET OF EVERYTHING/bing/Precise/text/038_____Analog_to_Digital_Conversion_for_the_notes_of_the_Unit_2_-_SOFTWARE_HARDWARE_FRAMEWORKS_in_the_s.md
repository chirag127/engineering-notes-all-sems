### Analog to Digital Conversion

Analog to Digital Conversion (ADC) is the process of converting an analog signal into a digital signal. This is an important process in the field of Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality, and the Internet of Everything, as it allows for the processing and transmission of data from analog sensors and devices.

1. **Sampling**: The first step in ADC is sampling, where the analog signal is measured at regular intervals. The rate at which the signal is sampled is known as the sampling rate and is measured in Hertz (Hz). The sampling rate must be at least twice the highest frequency component of the analog signal, according to the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem, to avoid aliasing.

2. **Quantization**: The next step is quantization, where the sampled signal is rounded to the nearest value in a finite set of discrete levels. The number of levels is determined by the number of bits used to represent the digital signal, with more bits resulting in a higher resolution and more accurate representation of the analog signal.

3. **Encoding**: The final step is encoding, where the quantized signal is converted into a digital code, typically in binary form. This code can then be transmitted, stored, or processed by digital systems.

ADC is a crucial component in many applications, including audio and video recording, data acquisition, and sensor networks. It is important to carefully select the appropriate ADC parameters, such as the sampling rate and resolution, to ensure accurate and reliable conversion of the analog signal.
