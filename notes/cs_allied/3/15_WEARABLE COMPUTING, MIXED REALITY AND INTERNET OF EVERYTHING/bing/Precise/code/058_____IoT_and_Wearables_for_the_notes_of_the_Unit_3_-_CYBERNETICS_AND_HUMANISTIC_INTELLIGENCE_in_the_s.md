### IoT and Wearables

IoT, or the Internet of Things, refers to the interconnection of everyday devices and objects, allowing them to collect and exchange data. Wearables are a subset of IoT devices that are worn on the body, such as smartwatches and fitness trackers.

Here are some key points to consider when studying IoT and Wearables for Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING:

1. IoT devices, including wearables, use sensors and software to collect and transmit data.
2. This data can be used for a variety of purposes, including monitoring health, tracking fitness, and controlling home automation systems.
3. Wearables are becoming increasingly popular, with a growing number of devices available on the market.
4. The use of IoT and wearables raises concerns about privacy and security, as the data collected by these devices can be sensitive.
5. There are also concerns about the potential for these devices to be hacked, with attackers gaining access to personal data or control over the device.
6. Despite these concerns, the use of IoT and wearables is expected to continue to grow, with the technology offering many potential benefits.
