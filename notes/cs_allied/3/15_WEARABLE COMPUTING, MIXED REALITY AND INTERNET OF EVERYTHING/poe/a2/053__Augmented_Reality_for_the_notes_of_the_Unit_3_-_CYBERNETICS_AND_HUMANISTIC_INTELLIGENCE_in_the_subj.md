### Augmented Reality for the notes of the Unit 3 - CYBERNETICS AND HUMANISTIC INTELLIGENCE in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

1. Augmented Reality (AR) is an interactive experience of a real-world environment where the objects that reside in the real-world are enhanced by computer-generated perceptual information, sometimes across multiple sensory modalities including visual, auditory, haptic, somatosensory, and olfactory.

2. The augmentation is conventionally in real-time and in semantic context with environmental elements, such as sports scores on TV during a match, or statistics about nearby buildings via an app.

3. AR is related to a more general concept called mediated reality, in which a view of reality is modified (possibly even diminished rather than augmented) by a computer. Augmented reality enhances one's current perception of reality, whereas virtual reality replaces the real world with a simulated one.

4. Augmentation techniques are typically performed in real-time and in semantic context with environmental elements. AR interfaces supplement the real world with digital or computer-generated information through the use of mobile apps, smart glasses or head-up displays (HUD).

5. The real-world environment can be supplemented with virtual objects like text messages, navigation arrows, and more using see-through displays. The real and the virtual dimensions are unified into a mixed reality that enables seamless interaction using gesture, gaze and voice technologies.

6. The key capabilities of augmented reality are:

- Combination of real and virtual worlds
- Interaction in real-time
- Alignment of virtual objects with the real world
- Anchoring of virtual objects to a point in real world space
- Environmental and contextual awareness
