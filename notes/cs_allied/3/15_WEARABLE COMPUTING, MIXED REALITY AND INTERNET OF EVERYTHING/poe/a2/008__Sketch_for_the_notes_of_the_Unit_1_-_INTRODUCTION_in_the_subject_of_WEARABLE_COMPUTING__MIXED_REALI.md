### Sketch for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

1. Introduction to Wearable Computing
- Definition: Wearable computers are small computing devices that are worn on the body, either as accessories or as part of the material in clothing. They are equipped with sensors, interfaces and networking capabilities.
- Types of wearable computers: smart watches, smart glasses, virtual reality headsets, smart clothes, fitness trackers, health monitoring devices etc.
- Applications: health monitoring, fitness tracking, communication, augmented reality, entertainment etc.
- Challenges: limited computing power and battery life, user acceptance, data security and privacy etc.

2. Introduction to Mixed Reality
- Definition: Mixed reality (MR) is the merging of real and virtual worlds to produce new environments and visualizations where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time.
- Types: augmented reality (AR), augmented virtuality (AV) and mixed reality continuum.
- Differences from virtual reality: MR incorporates and combines aspects of both physical and virtual reality. Real world objects are not completely blocked out like in VR.
- Applications: retail, engineering design, healthcare, education, entertainment, navigation etc.
- Challenges: limited field of view, latency, form factor, user experience design etc.

3. Introduction to Internet of Everything
- Definition: The Internet of Everything (IoE) refers to the interconnection of everyday physical objects and their virtual representations via the internet, enabling them to send and receive data.
- How it works: Devices are equipped with sensors and networking capabilities to connect to the internet and exchange data. This allows the devices and their data to be monitored, integrated and controlled from anywhere.
- Applications: smart homes, smart cities, industrial automation, transportation, healthcare, environmental monitoring etc.
- Challenges: interoperability, security, privacy, bandwidth, cost, complexity etc.
