### IoT and Wearables

- IoT or Internet of Things refers to the interconnection of everyday physical objects and appliances via the internet. These devices are equipped with sensors, processors, and networking hardware that allows them to send and receive data.
- Wearable devices are mini computers that can be worn on the body as accessories or implants. They incorporate sensors and networking capabilities to track and monitor various metrics related to health, fitness, environment, etc. and provide the data to the user or other connected devices.
- The convergence of IoT and wearables is enabling advanced applications in areas such as:
-- Health monitoring: Wearable sensors can track various health parameters and anomalies which can be monitored by doctors and medical systems in real-time.
-- Fitness tracking: Wearable fitness trackers can monitor physical activity, exercise, and other metrics to provide users with data to improve their fitness.
-- Smart home automation: IoT devices can automate various home functions like lighting, temperature, security, appliances, etc. based on presence and personal preferences. Wearables can be used to control these devices.
-- Industrial/enterprise applications: Wearables and IoT can be used to monitor processes, workers, and environments in industries and enterprises to enable optimizations and safety.
- While there are many promising applications, IoT and wearables also introduce risks and challenges including data security and privacy, device security, and interoperability. As these technologies become increasingly integrated into critical systems, these challenges must be addressed to enable widespread and secure use.
