### Hardware for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE HARDWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of WEARABLE COMPUTING, MIXED REALITY AND INTERNET OF EVERYTHING

Here are some key points on the hardware used in wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything:

- **Sensors**: Wearable devices are equipped with various sensors, such as accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers, heart rate monitors, and GPS. These sensors collect data about the user's surroundings and physical activity, which can then be used to provide personalized feedback or trigger actions in the device.
- **Processors**: With the increasing complexity of wearable devices, powerful processors are required to handle the data processing and decision making. Some popular processors used in wearable devices include ARM Cortex-M series, Intel Curie, and Qualcomm Snapdragon Wear.
- **Battery**: Wearable devices need to be lightweight and compact, which limits the size of the battery. To address this challenge, manufacturers use various techniques to optimize battery life, such as low-power modes, efficient power management, and use of energy harvesting technologies.
- **Display**: Wearable devices may have various types of displays, such as OLED, LCD, or e-ink. The display should be small and low-power, while providing clear and readable information to the user.
- **Communication**: Wearable devices need to communicate with other devices, such as smartphones or cloud services. Common communication protocols used in wearable devices include Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and cellular networks.
- **Haptic feedback**: Haptic feedback provides touch-based feedback to the user, such as vibrations or pressure. This can be used to provide notifications or alerts without the need for visual or auditory cues.
- **Cameras**: Some wearable devices, such as smart glasses, may have cameras that capture images or videos of the user's surroundings. These cameras can be used for various applications, such as augmented reality or remote assistance.
- **Microphones and speakers**: Wearable devices may have built-in microphones and speakers, which can be used for voice recognition, phone calls, or audio notifications.

Overall, the hardware used in wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything must be designed to be lightweight, compact, and power efficient, while providing accurate and reliable data collection and communication capabilities.
