### Audio for the Notes of Unit 2 - Software Hardware Frameworks in the Subject of Wearable Computing, Mixed Reality, and Internet of Everything

- Audio is an essential component of wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything systems.
- Audio input is used to record speech, ambient sound, and other audio signals.
- Audio output is used to play back audio signals, including speech, sound effects, and music.
- Audio processing is used to filter, analyze, and enhance audio signals.
- Audio sensors, such as microphones, are used to capture audio input.
- Audio actuators, such as speakers, are used to produce audio output.
- Audio processing algorithms, such as noise cancellation and speech recognition, are used to improve the quality of audio input and output.
- Audio codecs, such as AAC and MP3, are used to compress and decompress audio signals for efficient storage and transmission.
- Audio interfaces, such as Bluetooth audio and USB audio, are used to connect audio devices to other devices.
- Audio drivers, such as audio device drivers and audio processing drivers, are used to control audio hardware and software.
- Audio programming interfaces, such as the Web Audio API and the Android Audio SDK, are used to develop audio applications and services.
- Audio standards, such as the Audio Engineering Society (AES) standards and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standards, are used to ensure interoperability and compatibility between audio devices and systems.

In conclusion, audio is a crucial component of wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything systems, and understanding its role and capabilities is essential for developing effective and efficient audio applications and services.
