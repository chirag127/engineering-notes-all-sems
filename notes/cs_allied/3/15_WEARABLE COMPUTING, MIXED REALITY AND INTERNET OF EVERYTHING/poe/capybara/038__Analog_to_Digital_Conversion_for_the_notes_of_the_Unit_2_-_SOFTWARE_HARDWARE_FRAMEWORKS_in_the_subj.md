### Analog to Digital Conversion

Analog to Digital Conversion (ADC) is the process of converting analog signals into digital signals. It is an essential component of many electronic systems, including wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything devices.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying ADC:

- An analog signal is a continuous signal that varies in amplitude over time.
- A digital signal is a discrete signal that only takes on a finite number of values.
- ADC involves taking an analog input signal and converting it into a digital output signal.
- The process of ADC involves three main stages: sampling, quantization, and encoding.
- Sampling involves taking regular measurements of the analog signal at specific intervals of time.
- Quantization involves dividing the range of the analog signal into a finite number of levels, or bits.
- Encoding involves assigning a digital code to each quantization level.
- The resolution of an ADC is determined by the number of bits used for quantization.
- The accuracy of an ADC is determined by its signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and its total harmonic distortion (THD).
- Different types of ADCs include flash, successive approximation, delta-sigma, and pipeline ADCs.
- The choice of ADC type depends on factors such as speed, accuracy, and power consumption.

Overall, ADC is a crucial process for converting analog signals into digital data that can be processed by electronic systems. Understanding the basics of ADC is essential for designing and implementing effective wearable computing, mixed reality, and internet of everything devices.
