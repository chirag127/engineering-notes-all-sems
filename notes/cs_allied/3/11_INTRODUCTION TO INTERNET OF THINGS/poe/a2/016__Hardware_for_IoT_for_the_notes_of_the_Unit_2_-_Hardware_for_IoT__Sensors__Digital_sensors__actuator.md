### Hardware for IoT

- Sensors: Detect environmental conditions such as temperature, humidity, light, motion etc. and convert the physical quantity into electrical signals. Ex: Temperature sensor, Motion sensor etc.
- Digital sensors: Sensors which provide digital output (ON/OFF) indicating a threshold condition. Ex: Pushbutton switch.
- Actuators: Convert electrical signals into mechanical movements. Ex: Motor, Solenoid etc.
- Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology: Uses electromagnetic fields to automatically identify and track tags attached to objects. RFID tags contain electronically stored information which can be read from a distance without physical contact.
- Wireless Sensor Networks: A network of sensors which communicate wirelessly to accomplish a common task like environment monitoring.
- Participatory Sensing Technology: Collection of sensor data through mobile phones carried by human participants.

Embedded Platforms for IoT:
- Embedded computing basics: constrained computing devices integrated into larger systems to perform dedicated functions. Ex: Arduino, Raspberry Pi etc.
- Overview of IOT supported Hardware platforms:
-- Arduino: Open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software. Used for building digital devices and interactive objects.
-- Raspberry Pi: Credit-card sized single-board computer capable of running Linux and programming languages like Python and Scratch.
-- Beagle Bone: Open source hardware platform with lots of GPIO pins and capable of running Linux.
-- Intel Galileo boards: Development boards designed for Intel processors to support various shields and operating systems.
-- ARM cortex: Family of 32-bit RISC ARM processor cores designed for low power consumption. Widely used in mobile devices and IoT.
