### Sensors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT:

1. Sensors: Sensors are devices that detect and respond to some type of input from the physical environment. They convert the physical input into an electrical signal which can be measured and recorded. Examples include temperature sensors, humidity sensors, motion sensors, pressure sensors, etc.
2. Digital sensors: Digital sensors produce a digital output in the form of 0s and 1s which can be easily interpreted by microcontrollers. They are more accurate and less noisy than analog sensors. Example: Ultrasonic distance sensor.
3. Actuators: Actuators are devices that convert electrical signals into physical movement. They are used to perform physical actions based on the data from sensors. Examples include motors, servos, relays, LEDs, etc.
4. Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology: RFID uses electromagnetic fields to automatically identify and track tags attached to objects. RFID tags contain electronically stored information which can be read from a distance without any physical contact.
5. Wireless Sensor Networks: A wireless sensor network consists of spatially distributed autonomous sensors to monitor physical or environmental conditions, such as temperature, sound, vibration, pressure, motion or pollutants and to cooperatively pass their data through the network to a main location.
6. Participatory Sensing Technology: Participatory Sensing utilizes sensors and mobile devices with sensing capabilities carried by humans to capture data reflecting human exposure to and interactions with environmental phenomena. The data can provide valuable contextual information relevant to the applications.

Embedded Platforms for IoT:

1. Embedded computing basics: Embedded systems are dedicated computer systems designed to perform a specific task. They have software embedded into hardware. They are constrained in terms of resources but are efficient and Real-time.
2. Overview of IOT supported Hardware platforms:
   - Arduino: Open-source hardware platforms designed for prototyping projects. Supports add-on hardware modules called "shields".
   - Raspberry Pi: Credit card-sized single board computers capable of running Linux. Inexpensive and flexible platform for rapid prototyping.
   - Beagle Bone: Community-supported open hardware platform designed for developers and hobbyists.
   - Intel Galileo boards: Development boards designed for Intel processors to support IoT and wearable devices.
   - ARM Cortex: Family of 32-bit RISC processor cores designed for low power consumption. Supports a wide range of devices including smartphones and IoT devices.
