### Sources of the IoT

The key sources that contribute to the Internet of Things are:

1.  Sensors: Sensors are devices that detect and respond to physical inputs from the real-world environment. They convert the physical parameters into electrical signals which are sent to computing systems for processing. Examples include temperature sensors, motion sensors, pressure sensors, etc.
2.  RFID Tags: Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID) tags are low-cost microchips that contain small amounts of data and can be attached to physical objects. They use radio-frequency electromagnetic fields to automatically identify and track tags attached to objects.
3.  Smart Devices: Smart devices are internet-enabled physical devices that have sensors, computing capabilities and network connectivity. Examples include smartphones, smart speakers, smart appliances, smart meters, etc. They can collect data, run applications and interact with users.
4.  Networks: The networks provide the communication infrastructure to connect the various IoT components. This includes wireless networks such as Wi-Fi, cellular networks, low-power networks such as LoRaWAN and Sigfox, etc. The networks transport the data from the sensors and devices to the cloud for processing and storage.

The key sources work together to gather data from the physical world, transfer the data over the networks and deliver the data to computing systems in the cloud where it can be processed and analyzed to derive insights and enable automation. The insights can then be sent back over the networks to the smart devices to enable actions in the physical world.
