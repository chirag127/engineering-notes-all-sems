### Wireless Medium access issues

- Wireless medium is shared among multiple devices. This can lead to collisions and interference which degrades performance.
- Hidden terminal problem: When two nodes are out of range of each other but can affect a third node in between them. The third node may not be able to resolve the collision.
- Exposed terminal problem: When a node avoids transmission due to an ongoing transmission on the channel but the transmission was not actually going to affect it. This leads to underutilization of the channel.
- Near-far effect: When a strong signal from a nearby transmitter overpowers a weaker signal from a farther node. The farther node gets masked leading to unfairness.
- Frequency interference from other systems or devices can corrupt the communication.

### Mitigation techniques

- Contention-based MAC protocols like CSMA/CA and its variants help resolve medium access issues.
- Using directional antennas and limiting the transmission power can reduce the near-far effect.
- Using frequency hopping and spread spectrum can avoid interference.
- Reserving time slots for critical transmissions can provide guaranteed delivery.
- Coordinating transmissions spatially or temporally can avoid hidden and exposed terminal problems.
