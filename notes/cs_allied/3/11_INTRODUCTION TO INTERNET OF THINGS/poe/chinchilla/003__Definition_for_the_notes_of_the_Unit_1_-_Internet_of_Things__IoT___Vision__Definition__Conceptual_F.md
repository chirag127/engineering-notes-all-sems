### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT):

Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical devices, vehicles, home appliances, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and connectivity which enables these objects to connect and exchange data. IoT allows these objects to be controlled and monitored remotely, creating a smarter and more efficient world.

### Vision:

The vision of IoT is to create a world where everyday objects can communicate with each other and with humans seamlessly, allowing for greater efficiency, productivity, and convenience.

### Conceptual Framework:

The conceptual framework for IoT includes three main components: the physical devices or things, the connectivity and communication infrastructure, and the data analytics and processing capabilities. These components work together to enable the functionality of IoT.

### Architectural View:

The architecture of IoT includes three layers: the perception layer, the network layer, and the application layer. The perception layer includes sensors and devices that collect data, the network layer includes the communication infrastructure that connects the devices, and the application layer includes the software and analytics that process and interpret the data.

### Technology behind IoT:

The technology behind IoT includes a variety of hardware and software components, including sensors, actuators, microcontrollers, wireless communication technologies, cloud computing, and data analytics.

### Sources of the IoT:

The sources of IoT include a range of industries, including manufacturing, healthcare, transportation, and smart homes. These industries are leveraging IoT technology to create more efficient and effective systems.

### M2M Communication:

Machine-to-machine (M2M) communication is a key aspect of IoT. M2M communication allows devices to communicate with each other without human intervention, enabling automation and greater efficiency.

### IoT Examples:

Examples of IoT include smart homes, wearable technology, smart cities, and industrial automation. These examples demonstrate the potential for IoT to transform various industries and create a more connected world.

### Design Principles for Connected Devices:

Design principles for connected devices include considerations such as IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing, and affordability. These principles help ensure that IoT devices are efficient, effective, and user-friendly.
