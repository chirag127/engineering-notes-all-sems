IoT Examples:
1. Smart homes: Automated lighting, temperature control, security systems, and other connected devices that can be monitored and controlled remotely
2. Wearable technology: Fitness trackers, smartwatches, and other wearable devices that collect and transmit data about their wearer's health and activity
3. Industrial IoT: Connected machines and equipment in factories, warehouses, and other industrial settings that can be monitored and controlled for increased efficiency and safety
4. Connected cars: Automobiles equipped with sensors, cameras, and other technologies that collect and transmit data about the vehicle's performance, location, and environment
5. Healthcare IoT: Medical devices, such as wearable monitors and telemedicine tools, that collect and transmit data about a patient's health and well-being

Design Principles for Connected Devices:
1. Interoperability: Devices should be able to communicate and exchange data with one another, regardless of the manufacturer or technology used
2. Security: Devices should be protected against unauthorized access and data breaches to ensure the privacy and safety of users
3. Scalability: The system should be able to accommodate a large number of connected devices, and be able to easily add or remove devices as needed
4. Reliability: Devices should be able to operate reliably and consistently, even in challenging environments
5. Affordability: Devices should be affordable and accessible to a wide range of users, and not limited to only those with the financial means to purchase and maintain them.
