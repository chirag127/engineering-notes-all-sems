# Unit 3 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT

- Network and communication aspects in IoT refer to the methods and protocols that enable IoT devices to exchange data and interact with other devices, applications, and services over the internet or local networks.
- IoT devices can communicate using different types of communication, such as device-to-device, device-to-cloud, device-to-gateway, and back-end data-sharing.
- IoT devices can use various wireless technologies and standards for local communication, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, Z-Wave, NFC, RFID, LoRaWAN, etc.
- IoT devices can also use different application protocols for data transport and messaging, such as MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, AMQP, XMPP, etc.
- IoT devices need to follow certain network protocols to ensure secure and reliable communication over the internet, such as IPv6, TCP/IP, UDP, TLS, DTLS, etc.
- IoT devices may require gateways to translate and re-transmit information between different networks and protocols, such as from a local device network to the internet or cloud.
- IoT devices may also need to use various routing protocols to find the optimal path for data transmission in a network, such as AODV, DSR, OLSR, RPL, etc.
- IoT devices may face some wireless medium access issues, such as interference, congestion, collision, hidden terminal, exposed terminal, etc. These issues can affect the performance and quality of service of IoT communication.
- IoT devices may use different MAC protocols to coordinate and manage the access to the wireless medium, such as CSMA/CA, TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, etc. These protocols can help to reduce the medium access issues and improve the efficiency and fairness of IoT communication.
- IoT devices may need to perform some network functions, such as sensor deployment, node discovery, data aggregation, and data dissemination. These functions can help to optimize the network topology, reduce the energy consumption, and enhance the data quality and security of IoT communication.
