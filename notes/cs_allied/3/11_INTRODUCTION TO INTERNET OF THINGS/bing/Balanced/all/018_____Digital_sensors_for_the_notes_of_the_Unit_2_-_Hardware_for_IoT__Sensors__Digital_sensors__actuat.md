# Digital sensors for IoT

- Digital sensors are pieces of hardware that detect changes in an environment and collect data in a digital format .
- Digital sensors can interact directly with an IoT microcontroller, which is a small computer that controls the functions of an IoT device.
- Digital sensors can measure various physical phenomena, such as temperature, pressure, humidity, light, sound, motion, proximity, gas, etc .
- Digital sensors can be used in various IoT applications, such as consumer devices, industry 4.0, smart cities, smart homes, healthcare, agriculture, etc  .
- Digital sensors can be connected to a network, such as the internet, a wireless sensor network, or a participatory sensing network, to share data with other devices, servers, or users  .
- Digital sensors can be integrated with other IoT components, such as actuators, radio frequency identification (RFID) tags, embedded platforms, cloud services, etc, to create a complete IoT system  .
- Digital sensors can be classified into different types based on their function, output, interface, communication, power, etc . Some examples of digital sensor types are:

  - Binary sensors: These sensors have only two possible output values, such as on/off, open/closed, high/low, etc. For example, a door sensor, a motion sensor, a tilt sensor, etc.
  - Discrete sensors: These sensors have a finite number of output values, such as 0, 1, 2, ..., n. For example, a switch, a button, a keypad, etc.
  - Continuous sensors: These sensors have an infinite number of output values within a certain range, such as 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, ..., 1.0. For example, a temperature sensor, a pressure sensor, a light sensor, etc.
  - Serial sensors: These sensors use a serial communication protocol, such as I2C, SPI, UART, etc, to send data to the microcontroller. For example, a gyroscope, an accelerometer, a magnetometer, etc.
  - Parallel sensors: These sensors use a parallel communication protocol, such as GPIO, to send data to the microcontroller. For example, a LED, a LCD, a buzzer, etc.
  - Wireless sensors: These sensors use a wireless communication protocol, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Zigbee, LoRa, etc, to send data to the network. For example, a GPS, a RFID tag, a wireless camera, etc.
  - Wired sensors: These sensors use a wired communication protocol, such as Ethernet, USB, CAN, etc, to send data to the network. For example, a webcam, a microphone, a keyboard, etc.
  - Passive sensors: These sensors do not require any external power source to operate. They generate their own voltage or current based on the physical phenomenon they measure. For example, a thermocouple, a piezoelectric sensor, a photovoltaic cell, etc.
  - Active sensors: These sensors require an external power source to operate. They use a transducer to convert the physical phenomenon they measure into an electrical signal. For example, a thermistor, a potentiometer, a strain gauge, etc.
