# Design Principles for Connected Devices

Connected devices are physical objects that can communicate with other devices or systems over the internet or other networks. They are also known as Internet of Things (IoT) or Machine-to-Machine (M2M) devices. Connected devices can collect, process, and share data, as well as perform actions based on commands or rules. Examples of connected devices include smart home appliances, wearable devices, industrial sensors, and autonomous vehicles.

Designing connected devices requires considering various aspects such as functionality, usability, security, scalability, interoperability, and serviceability. The following are some of the design principles that can help create successful connected devices:

- **Do your research.** Before designing a connected device, it is important to understand the needs and expectations of the target users, as well as the context and environment in which the device will operate. Research can help identify the problems that the device can solve, the value that it can provide, and the features that it should have.
- **Align features with user value.** Connected devices should offer features that are relevant, useful, and meaningful to the users. Features that are not aligned with user value can create confusion, frustration, or dissatisfaction. Designers should prioritize the features that deliver the most value and avoid adding unnecessary or redundant features .
- **Look at the whole picture.** Connected devices are part of a larger system that includes other devices, networks, platforms, and services. Designers should consider how the device will interact with other elements in the system, and how it will fit into the overall user experience. Designers should also ensure that the device is interoperable and can integrate with different standards, protocols, and technologies  .
- **Consider the operating settings.** Connected devices can operate in various settings, such as indoors, outdoors, urban, rural, or remote areas. Designers should take into account the physical, environmental, and social factors that can affect the device's performance, reliability, and usability. For example, designers should consider the power supply, network connectivity, weather conditions, user behavior, and cultural norms that can influence the device's functionality and user acceptance.
- **Remember about the security.** Connected devices can pose significant security risks, as they can expose sensitive data, enable unauthorized access, or cause physical harm. Designers should adopt a security-by-design approach, which means incorporating security measures throughout the design process, rather than adding them as an afterthought. Security measures can include encryption, authentication, authorization, data protection, and device management .
- **Build with the context in mind.** Connected devices should be designed to adapt to the context of use, which can vary depending on the user, the device, the location, the time, and the situation. Designers should enable the device to sense and respond to the context, and provide appropriate feedback and guidance to the user. Designers should also avoid creating unnecessary or intrusive interactions, and respect the user's privacy and preferences .
- **Make good use of prototypes.** Prototyping is a valuable tool for testing and validating the design of connected devices. Prototypes can help designers evaluate the feasibility, usability, and desirability of the device, as well as identify and resolve any issues or challenges. Prototypes can also help designers communicate and collaborate with other stakeholders, such as developers, manufacturers, and users.
- **Design for serviceability.** Connected devices should be designed to be easy to maintain, update, and repair. Designers should consider the device's lifecycle, and anticipate the potential changes, failures, or obsolescence that can occur over time. Designers should also provide clear and accessible documentation, instructions, and support for the device's users and service providers .
