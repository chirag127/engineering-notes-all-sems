## Definition

- The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet .
- The IoT can also be defined as the technology that allows us to add a device to an inert object that can measure environmental parameters, generate associated data and transmit them through a communications network.
- The IoT is a giant network of connected things and people that collect and share data about the way they are used and about the environment around them.

## Vision

- The vision of the IoT is to create a world where everything is connected, intelligent, and responsive, enabling new levels of efficiency, productivity, and innovation.
- The IoT vision also aims to improve the quality of life, health, and safety of people and the environment, by providing access to information, services, and opportunities.
- The IoT vision is driven by the convergence of various technologies, such as cloud computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, machine learning, and 5G .

## Conceptual Framework

- A conceptual framework for the IoT can be described as a layered model that consists of four main components: devices, networks, platforms, and applications .
- Devices are the physical objects that are equipped with sensors, actuators, and communication modules that enable them to interact with the environment and other devices .
- Networks are the communication infrastructures that provide connectivity and data transmission between devices and platforms .
- Platforms are the software systems that provide data storage, processing, analysis, and management services for the IoT devices and applications .
- Applications are the software solutions that provide specific functionalities and value-added services for the end-users of the IoT .

## Architectural View

- An architectural view of the IoT can be described as a three-tier model that consists of three main layers: perception, network, and application.
- Perception layer is the lowest layer that consists of the IoT devices and sensors that collect and process data from the physical world.
- Network layer is the middle layer that consists of the communication networks and protocols that enable data transmission and exchange between the perception and application layers.
- Application layer is the highest layer that consists of the IoT applications and services that provide specific functionalities and value-added services for the end-users of the IoT.

## Technology behind IoT

- The technology behind the IoT can be categorized into three main domains: hardware, software, and communication.
- Hardware domain includes the devices, sensors, actuators, and communication modules that enable the physical interaction and data collection of the IoT.
- Software domain includes the platforms, applications, and algorithms that enable the data storage, processing, analysis, and management of the IoT.
- Communication domain includes the networks, protocols, and standards that enable the data transmission and exchange of the IoT.

## Sources of the IoT

- The sources of the IoT can be classified into three main types: human-generated, machine-generated, and environment-generated.
- Human-generated sources are the data that are produced by human activities, such as social media, online transactions, and mobile applications.
- Machine-generated sources are the data that are produced by machines, such as vehicles, industrial systems, and smart appliances.
- Environment-generated sources are the data that are produced by natural phenomena, such as weather, climate, and geolocation.

## M2M Communication

- M2M communication is the term used to describe the direct communication and data exchange between machines or devices without human intervention.
- M2M communication is enabled by various technologies, such as wireless, cellular, satellite, and radio frequency identification (RFID).
- M2M communication is a key enabler of the IoT, as it allows the devices to autonomously monitor, control, and optimize their operations and performance.
