### Sensors for IoT

- Sensors are devices that detect and measure physical phenomena, such as temperature, pressure, motion, light, sound, etc.
- Sensors are essential components of IoT systems, as they collect data from the physical world and send it to the cloud or other devices for processing, analysis, and action.
- Sensors can be classified into two types: digital and analog. Digital sensors produce discrete values, such as on/off, high/low, etc. Analog sensors produce continuous values, such as voltage, current, resistance, etc.
- Sensors can also be categorized based on their function, such as temperature sensors, proximity sensors, pressure sensors, water quality sensors, chemical and gas sensors, infrared sensors, smoke sensors, motion sensors, etc.   
- Some examples of sensors for IoT are:
  - Temperature sensors: measure the amount of heat generated from an area or an object. They can be used for monitoring environmental conditions, food safety, health care, etc.
  - Proximity sensors: detect the presence or absence of objects near the sensor without physical contact. They can be used for security, automation, robotics, etc.
  - Pressure sensors: detect changes in a gas or liquid. They can be used for measuring altitude, depth, flow, force, etc.
  - Water quality sensors: measure the levels of pollutants, pH, dissolved oxygen, etc. in water. They can be used for environmental monitoring, agriculture, aquaculture, etc.
  - Chemical and gas sensors: measure the concentration of specific chemicals or gases in the air. They can be used for detecting leaks, fires, pollution, etc.
  - Infrared sensors: detect infrared radiation emitted by objects or sources. They can be used for night vision, thermal imaging, remote sensing, etc.
  - Smoke sensors: detect the presence of smoke particles in the air. They can be used for fire detection, air quality monitoring, etc.
  - Motion sensors: detect the movement of objects or people. They can be used for security, automation, gaming, etc.
