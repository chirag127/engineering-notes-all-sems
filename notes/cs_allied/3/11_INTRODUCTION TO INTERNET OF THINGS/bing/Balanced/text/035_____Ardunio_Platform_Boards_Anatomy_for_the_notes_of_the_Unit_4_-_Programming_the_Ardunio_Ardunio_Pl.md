### Arduino Platform Boards Anatomy

Arduino boards are the microcontroller development platform that will be at the heart of your projects. They sense the environment by receiving inputs from many sensors, and affect their surroundings by controlling lights, motors, and other actuators. When making something you will be building the circuits and interfaces for interaction, and telling the microcontroller how to interface with other components. Here are some of the main features of Arduino Uno board, which is one of the most popular Arduino boards  :

- **Power jack**: This is where you can plug a 7-12V external power supply to power the board and the connected components.
- **USB connector**: This is where you can connect the board to your computer via a USB cable. You can use this connection to upload sketches (programs) to the board, and also to power the board and communicate with it.
- **Reset button**: This is a small button that you can press to reset the board. This will restart the sketch that is running on the board, and also reset the communication with the computer.
- **Power LED**: This is a small LED that lights up when the board is powered on. It indicates that the board is receiving power from either the power jack or the USB connector.
- **TX and RX LEDs**: These are two small LEDs that blink when the board is transmitting or receiving data via the USB connection or the serial pins (0 and 1).
- **Digital pins**: These are 14 pins that can be used as digital input or output. They can read or write values of either HIGH (5V) or LOW (0V). Some of these pins have special functions, such as PWM (pulse-width modulation), interrupt, or serial communication.
- **Analog pins**: These are 6 pins that can be used as analog input. They can read values from 0 to 1023, corresponding to a voltage range of 0 to 5V. They can also be used as digital pins, but they have no special functions.
- **AREF pin**: This is a pin that can be used to set the reference voltage for the analog inputs. By default, the reference voltage is 5V, but you can change it by connecting a different voltage source to this pin, or by using the analogReference() function in your sketch.
- **GND pins**: These are pins that provide a common ground for the board and the connected components. You need to connect one of these pins to the negative terminal of your power supply or battery, and also to the ground of any component that you want to use with the board.
- **5V and 3.3V pins**: These are pins that provide a regulated 5V or 3.3V output that you can use to power other components. They are connected to the internal voltage regulator of the board, which can supply up to 50 mA of current. Do not connect more than 5V or 3.3V to these pins, or you may damage the board.
- **Vin pin**: This is a pin that can be used to access the unregulated input voltage that is supplied to the board via the power jack or the USB connector. You can use this pin to power components that require a higher voltage than 5V or 3.3V, but be careful not to exceed the maximum input voltage of the board, which is 20V.
- **ICSP header**: This is a 6-pin header that can be used to program the board using an external programmer, such as another Arduino board or an AVR ISP. You can also use this header to access the SPI (serial peripheral interface) pins of the board, which are MISO, MOSI, SCK, and RESET.
- **Microcontroller**: This is the brain of the board, which executes the sketch that you upload to it. It is an ATmega328P chip, which has 32 KB of flash memory, 2 KB of SRAM, and 1 KB of EEPROM. It runs at 16 MHz, and has a built-in bootloader that allows you to program it via the USB connection.
