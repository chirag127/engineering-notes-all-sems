# INTRODUCTION TO INTERNET OF THINGS

- The Internet of Things (IoT) describes the network of physical objects that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet.
- The term IoT was coined by Kevin Ashton in 1999, who envisioned a system where the physical world could be connected to the digital world through sensors and RFID tags.
- IoT devices can range from ordinary household objects to sophisticated industrial tools, and can include anything with a sensor assigned a unique identifier (UID).
- IoT applications can span across various domains, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, smart agriculture, smart manufacturing, smart energy, smart transportation, and more .
- IoT systems consist of four main functional blocks: devices, communications, services, and applications.
  - Devices: These are the physical objects that have sensors, actuators, and embedded systems that enable them to collect, process, and act on data.
  - Communications: These are the protocols and technologies that enable the devices to communicate with each other and with the cloud or edge servers.
  - Services: These are the software platforms and tools that provide data storage, processing, analytics, visualization, and management for the IoT devices and applications.
  - Applications: These are the end-user solutions that leverage the data and services from the IoT devices to provide value and benefits to the users.
- IoT systems also require security, privacy, and trust mechanisms to ensure the protection and integrity of the data and devices, as well as the user's rights and preferences.
- IoT systems follow a design methodology that involves identifying the problem, defining the requirements, selecting the devices, choosing the communication protocols, developing the services, and implementing the applications.
- IoT systems can also be classified into different architectures, such as centralized, decentralized, distributed, or hybrid, depending on the level of data processing and decision making that occurs at the device, edge, or cloud level.
- IoT systems can provide various benefits, such as increased efficiency, productivity, quality, safety, convenience, and sustainability, as well as new opportunities for innovation, business, and social impact .
