## Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT): Vision, Definition, Conceptual Framework, Architectural view, technology behind IoT, Sources of the IoT, M2M Communication, IoT Examples. Design Principles for Connected Devices: IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing and affordability

- **IoT Vision**: The vision behind IoT is to have plug-n-play smart objects that can be deployed in any environment with an interoperable interconnection backbone that allows them to blend with other smart objects around them. Standardization of frequency bands and protocols plays a pivotal role in accomplishing this goal .
- **IoT Definition**: The Internet of Things, also called The Internet of Objects, refers to a wireless network between objects. By embedding short-range mobile transceivers into a wide array of additional gadgets and everyday items, enabling new forms of communication between people and things, and between things themselves.
- **IoT Conceptual Framework**: The IoT conceptual framework consists of four main layers: the device layer, the network layer, the service layer, and the application layer. The device layer includes the physical objects that are equipped with sensors, actuators, and communication capabilities. The network layer provides the connectivity and routing of data among the devices and the service layer. The service layer offers the functionalities and interfaces for data processing, storage, and management. The application layer delivers the end-user services and applications that utilize the data and resources from the lower layers .
- **IoT Architectural view**: The IoT architectural view can be divided into three domains: the perception domain, the network domain, and the application domain. The perception domain is responsible for collecting data from the physical world and converting it into digital signals. The network domain is responsible for transmitting, processing, and storing the data from the perception domain and providing it to the application domain. The application domain is responsible for providing the user interface and the business logic for the IoT applications .
- **Technology behind IoT**: The technology behind IoT includes various hardware and software components that enable the communication, computation, and coordination of the IoT devices and applications. Some of the key technologies are: RFID, wireless sensor networks, cloud computing, big data analytics, artificial intelligence, machine learning, and blockchain .
- **Sources of the IoT**: The sources of the IoT are the devices and objects that generate data and interact with the IoT network. They can be classified into three categories: passive devices, active devices, and hybrid devices. Passive devices are those that do not have any processing or communication capabilities, such as RFID tags. Active devices are those that have both processing and communication capabilities, such as smartphones, smart watches, and smart meters. Hybrid devices are those that have processing capabilities but rely on external communication devices, such as sensors connected to gateways .
- **M2M Communication**: M2M communication refers to the direct exchange of data between machines or devices without human intervention. M2M communication can enable various applications such as remote monitoring, control, automation, and optimization. M2M communication can use different technologies such as cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, and LoRaWAN .
- **IoT Examples**: Some of the examples of IoT applications are: smart home, smart city, smart grid, smart health, smart agriculture, smart transportation, smart industry, and smart environment .
- **Design Principles for Connected Devices**: Some of the design principles for connected devices are: IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing and affordability. IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization refer to the use of common and interoperable protocols and architectures for the IoT devices and systems. Communication technologies refer to the selection of appropriate and efficient wireless technologies for the IoT devices and systems. Data enrichment and consolidation refer to the use of data processing and analysis techniques to extract meaningful information from the IoT data. Ease of designing and affordability refer to the use of low-cost and user-friendly tools and platforms for the IoT device and system development .
