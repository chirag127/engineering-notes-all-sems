- Internet of Things (IoT) is a technology that allows us to add a device to an inert object (for example: vehicles, plant electronic systems, roofs, lighting, etc.) that can measure environmental parameters, generate associated data and transmit them through a communications network.
- IoT is also the network of physical objects—“things”—that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet.
- IoT is the concept of connecting any device (so long as it has an on/off switch) to the Internet and to other connected devices. The IoT is a giant network of connected things and people – all of which collect and share data about the way they are used and about the environment around them.
- The vision of IoT is to enable a seamless integration of the physical and digital worlds, where objects can communicate and cooperate with each other to achieve various goals, such as improving efficiency, safety, convenience, and quality of life.
- The conceptual framework of IoT consists of four main layers: the device layer, the network layer, the service layer, and the application layer. The device layer includes the physical objects that are equipped with sensors, actuators, and identifiers. The network layer provides the connectivity and communication protocols for data transmission and management. The service layer offers the functionalities and capabilities for data processing, storage, and analysis. The application layer delivers the end-user services and solutions based on the data and information from the service layer.
- The architectural view of IoT can vary depending on the specific context and requirements, but a common approach is to use a three-tier architecture: the edge tier, the platform tier, and the enterprise tier. The edge tier consists of the devices and gateways that collect and preprocess the data from the physical world. The platform tier provides the cloud-based infrastructure and services for data aggregation, integration, and analytics. The enterprise tier enables the business logic and applications that utilize the data and insights from the platform tier.
- The technology behind IoT involves a combination of hardware, software, and communication technologies that enable the sensing, identification, communication, computation, and control of the IoT devices and systems. Some of the key technologies include: wireless sensor networks, radio-frequency identification (RFID), near-field communication (NFC), Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular networks, low-power wide-area networks (LPWAN), cloud computing, edge computing, artificial intelligence, machine learning, big data, blockchain, and cybersecurity.
- The sources of the IoT can be classified into three categories: human-generated, machine-generated, and environment-generated. Human-generated sources refer to the data and information that are created by human activities, such as social media, online transactions, and wearable devices. Machine-generated sources refer to the data and information that are produced by machines, such as industrial equipment, vehicles, and smart appliances. Environment-generated sources refer to the data and information that are derived from the natural or physical environment, such as weather, air quality, and soil moisture.
- M2M communication is a term that describes the exchange of data and information between machines without human intervention. M2M communication is an essential component of IoT, as it enables the automation and coordination of the IoT devices and systems. M2M communication can be achieved through various technologies, such as wired or wireless networks, cellular networks, satellite networks, and optical networks.
- IoT examples are numerous and diverse, covering various domains and applications, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart agriculture, smart healthcare, smart manufacturing, smart transportation, smart energy, smart retail, and smart education.
