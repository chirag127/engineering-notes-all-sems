### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- Connected devices are products that use the Internet of Things (IoT) or machine-to-machine (M2M) communication to interact with other devices, systems, or users.
- Designing connected devices requires considering various aspects such as functionality, usability, security, scalability, interoperability, and serviceability.
- Some of the design principles for connected devices are:

  - Do your research: Understand the purpose, value, and needs of the users and the context of the device. 
  - Align features with user value: Focus on the benefits and outcomes that the device can provide to the users, rather than the technical specifications or capabilities. 
  - Look at the whole picture: Consider how the device fits into the larger ecosystem of IoT or M2M systems, and how it can communicate and integrate with other devices or services.  
  - Consider the operating settings: Design the device according to the environmental, physical, and social conditions where it will be used, and how it will affect or be affected by them. 
  - Remember about the security: Protect the device, the data, and the users from unauthorized access, tampering, or malicious attacks, by applying appropriate security measures and best practices.  
  - Build with the context in mind: Design the device to be adaptable and responsive to the changing needs and preferences of the users and the environment, and to provide relevant and timely feedback.  
  - Make good use of prototypes: Test and validate the device design with real users and scenarios, and iterate based on the feedback and insights. 
  - Ensure serviceability: Design the device to be easy to maintain, update, repair, or replace, and to provide clear and accessible support and documentation to the users.
