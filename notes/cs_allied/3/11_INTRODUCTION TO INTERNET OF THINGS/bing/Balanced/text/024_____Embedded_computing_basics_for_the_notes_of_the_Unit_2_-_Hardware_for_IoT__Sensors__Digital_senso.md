### Embedded computing basics

- Embedded computing is the use of special-purpose computer systems that are designed to perform one or a few dedicated functions, usually with very specific requirements and constraints  .
- Embedded systems are often embedded in larger systems, such as cars, medical devices, robots, appliances, etc., and interact with the physical world through sensors and actuators.
- Embedded systems have some common characteristics, such as:
  - They perform specific tasks, often in real-time or with strict deadlines .
  - They have low cost, low power consumption, and small size .
  - They have limited resources, such as memory, processing power, and communication bandwidth .
  - They have high reliability, safety, and security requirements .
- Embedded systems can be classified into four types, based on their connectivity and mobility:
  - Standalone embedded systems: They operate independently and do not rely on any external network or system.
  - Network embedded systems: They communicate with other systems or devices through a wired or wireless network, such as the Internet, LAN, WAN, etc.
  - Mobile embedded systems: They are portable and can move from one location to another, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, etc.
  - Real-time embedded systems: They have to respond to events or inputs within a specified time frame, such as control systems, signal processing, etc.
