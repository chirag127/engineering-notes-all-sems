## Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

### Vision
The vision of the Internet of Things (IoT) is to create a world where physical objects are seamlessly integrated into the information network, and where they can become active participants in various processes and services.

### Definition
The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of uniquely identifiable embedded computing devices within the existing Internet infrastructure.

### Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components:
- **Sensors/Actuators**: These are the devices that collect data from the environment or perform actions based on the data received.
- **Connectivity**: This refers to the communication technologies used to transmit data between the sensors/actuators and the rest of the system.
- **Data Processing**: This involves the processing and analysis of the data collected by the sensors/actuators.
- **User Interface**: This is the interface through which users can interact with the IoT system.

### Architectural View
The architectural view of IoT can be divided into three layers:
- **Perception Layer**: This layer is responsible for collecting data from the environment using sensors and actuators.
- **Network Layer**: This layer is responsible for transmitting the data collected by the perception layer to the application layer.
- **Application Layer**: This layer is responsible for processing the data received from the network layer and providing services to the users.

### Technology behind IoT
The technology behind IoT includes various communication technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, and cellular networks, as well as data processing technologies such as cloud computing and big data analytics.

### Sources of the IoT
The sources of the IoT include various sensors and actuators that collect data from the environment, as well as devices such as smartphones and wearables that can act as both sensors and actuators.

### M2M Communication
Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication refers to the communication between two or more machines without the need for human intervention. M2M communication is an essential component of the IoT, as it allows devices to exchange data and perform actions autonomously.

### IoT Examples
Some examples of IoT applications include smart homes, where various devices such as thermostats, lighting, and security systems are connected and can be controlled remotely; and smart cities, where various systems such as traffic management, waste management, and energy management are interconnected and can be monitored and controlled in real-time.

### Design Principles for Connected Devices
The design principles for connected devices in the context of IoT/M2M systems include the following:
- **Layers and Design Standardization**: The design of IoT/M2M systems should follow a layered approach, with each layer responsible for a specific function. Additionally, the design should follow standardization to ensure interoperability between different devices and systems.
- **Communication Technologies**: The choice of communication technology should be based on factors such as range, power consumption, and data rate requirements.
- **Data Enrichment and Consolidation**: The data collected by the sensors/actuators should be enriched and consolidated to provide more meaningful information to the users.
- **Ease of Designing and Affordability**: The design of IoT/M2M systems should be easy and affordable to ensure widespread adoption.
