# Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

### Definition

- The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of physical devices, vehicles, buildings, and other objects embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and network connectivity, which enables these objects to collect and exchange data.

### Vision

- The vision of IoT is to create a world where physical objects are seamlessly integrated into the information network, and where the physical objects can become active participants in business processes.

### Conceptual Framework

- The conceptual framework of IoT includes the following components:
  - Devices: Physical objects that are connected to the internet and can collect and exchange data.
  - Connectivity: The means by which devices communicate with each other and with the cloud.
  - Data: The information collected by devices and transmitted over the internet.
  - Analytics: The process of analyzing data to extract insights and make decisions.

### Architectural View

- The architectural view of IoT includes the following layers:
  - Perception Layer: This layer is responsible for collecting data from the physical world using sensors and other devices.
  - Network Layer: This layer is responsible for transmitting data from the perception layer to the processing layer.
  - Processing Layer: This layer is responsible for processing and analyzing data to extract insights and make decisions.
  - Application Layer: This layer is responsible for delivering services to users based on the insights and decisions made by the processing layer.

### Technology behind IoT

- The technology behind IoT includes sensors, actuators, communication protocols, cloud computing, and data analytics.

### Sources of the IoT

- The sources of the IoT include sensors, RFID tags, and other devices that can collect and transmit data.

### M2M Communication

- Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication refers to the direct communication between devices without the need for human intervention.

### IoT Examples

- Some examples of IoT applications include smart homes, smart cities, connected cars, and wearable devices.

### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- The design principles for connected devices include the following:
  - IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization: The design of IoT systems should follow a standardized architecture with well-defined layers.
  - Communication technologies: IoT devices should use appropriate communication technologies to ensure reliable and efficient data transmission.
  - Data enrichment and consolidation: IoT data should be enriched and consolidated to extract meaningful insights.
  - Ease of designing and affordability: The design of IoT devices should be easy and affordable to encourage widespread adoption.
