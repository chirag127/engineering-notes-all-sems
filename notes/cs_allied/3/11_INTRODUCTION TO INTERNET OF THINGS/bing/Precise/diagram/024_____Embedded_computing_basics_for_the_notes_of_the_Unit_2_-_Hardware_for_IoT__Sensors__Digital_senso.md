### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing refers to the use of computer systems and software to perform dedicated functions within larger systems. These systems are often designed to perform a specific task or set of tasks, and are typically integrated into other devices or systems.

Some key characteristics of embedded computing systems include:
- They are designed to perform a specific function or set of functions.
- They are often integrated into other devices or systems.
- They typically have limited resources, such as processing power, memory, and power consumption.
- They often operate in real-time, with strict timing constraints.

In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), embedded computing plays a crucial role in enabling the connectivity and functionality of IoT devices. Many IoT devices, such as sensors, actuators, and wireless sensor networks, rely on embedded computing systems to process and transmit data.

There are several popular hardware platforms that support the development of IoT devices, including Arduino, Netduino, Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, Intel Galileo, and ARM Cortex. These platforms provide a range of capabilities and resources, allowing developers to choose the platform that best fits their needs.

In summary, embedded computing is a key component of IoT devices, providing the processing power and functionality needed to enable connectivity and data transmission. There are a variety of hardware platforms available to support the development of IoT devices, each with its own strengths and capabilities.
