### Hardware for IoT

Unit 2 of the subject "Introduction to Internet of Things" covers the hardware components used in IoT systems. These include:

1. **Sensors**: Sensors are devices that detect and respond to changes in the environment. They can measure physical quantities such as temperature, humidity, light, and motion, and convert them into digital signals that can be processed by a computer.

2. **Digital sensors**: Digital sensors are sensors that produce a digital output signal. They are commonly used in IoT systems because they can be easily interfaced with microcontrollers and other digital devices.

3. **Actuators**: Actuators are devices that convert electrical signals into physical actions. They are used in IoT systems to control physical processes, such as turning on a light or opening a valve.

4. **Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology**: RFID technology uses radio waves to identify and track objects. It is commonly used in IoT systems for inventory management and asset tracking.

5. **Wireless sensor networks**: Wireless sensor networks are networks of sensors that communicate wirelessly. They are used in IoT systems to collect data from multiple sensors and transmit it to a central location for processing.

6. **Participatory sensing technology**: Participatory sensing technology involves using mobile devices and other personal sensors to collect data from individuals. It is used in IoT systems to gather data from large groups of people.

7. **Embedded Platforms for IoT**: Embedded platforms are small, low-power computers that are designed for use in IoT systems. Some popular embedded platforms for IoT include Arduino, Netduino, Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, Intel Galileo, and ARM Cortex.

These hardware components are essential for building IoT systems that can sense, process, and act on data from the physical world. By understanding how these components work and how they can be used together, you can design and build effective IoT systems.
