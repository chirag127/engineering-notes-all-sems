### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing refers to the use of computer systems in devices and products that are not traditionally considered computers. These systems are typically designed to perform specific tasks and are optimized for size, power consumption, and cost.

Some key characteristics of embedded computing systems include:

1. They are designed for a specific purpose and are optimized for that purpose.
2. They are often small in size and have low power consumption.
3. They are typically less expensive than general-purpose computers.
4. They often have real-time constraints, meaning they must respond to events within a certain time frame.

Embedded computing systems are used in a wide range of applications, including:

- Sensors: Embedded systems are used to collect data from the environment using sensors. This data can be used for a variety of purposes, such as monitoring temperature, humidity, or air quality.
- Digital sensors: Digital sensors are sensors that produce a digital output, rather than an analog output. This makes it easier to interface with other digital systems, such as microcontrollers or computers.
- Actuators: Actuators are devices that convert electrical signals into physical movement. Embedded systems are often used to control actuators, such as motors or solenoids, to perform specific tasks.
- Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology: RFID technology uses radio waves to communicate with RFID tags, which are small, low-power devices that can be attached to objects. Embedded systems are often used to read and write data to RFID tags.
- Wireless sensor networks: Wireless sensor networks are networks of sensors that communicate wirelessly. Embedded systems are often used to collect data from these sensors and transmit it to a central location for analysis.
- Participatory sensing technology: Participatory sensing technology refers to the use of mobile devices, such as smartphones, to collect data from the environment. Embedded systems are often used to process this data and transmit it to a central location for analysis.

There are several popular embedded platforms that are commonly used for IoT development, including:

- Arduino: Arduino is an open-source platform that is based on easy-to-use hardware and software. It is designed to make it easy for anyone to build interactive projects using sensors, actuators, and other components.
- Netduino: Netduino is a platform that is similar to Arduino, but it is based on the .NET Micro Framework. This makes it easy to develop applications using C# and other .NET languages.
- Raspberry Pi: Raspberry Pi is a small, low-cost computer that is designed to be used for education and hobbyist projects. It is capable of running a full Linux operating system and can be used for a wide range of applications.
- BeagleBone: BeagleBone is a platform that is similar to Raspberry Pi, but it is based on the ARM Cortex-A8 processor. It is designed to be used for more advanced projects that require more processing power.
- Intel Galileo: Intel Galileo is a platform that is based on the Intel Quark SoC X1000 processor. It is designed to be used for IoT development and is compatible with the Arduino development environment.
- ARM Cortex: ARM Cortex is a family of processors that are designed for use in embedded systems. They are widely used in a variety of applications, including smartphones, tablets, and other consumer electronics.

These platforms provide a range of options for developing IoT applications, from simple, low-cost systems to more advanced, high-performance systems. They are supported by a large community of developers and are widely used in both hobbyist and professional projects.
