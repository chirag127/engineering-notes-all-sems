### Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing refers to the use of computer systems within other devices or systems to perform specific functions. These computer systems are typically small, low-power, and designed to perform a limited set of tasks. They are often integrated into larger systems, such as appliances, vehicles, and industrial equipment, to provide control, monitoring, and data processing capabilities.

Some key characteristics of embedded computing systems include:

- **Dedicated functionality:** Embedded systems are designed to perform a specific set of tasks, often with real-time constraints.
- **Low power consumption:** Many embedded systems are battery-powered or must operate with limited power resources, so they are designed to be energy-efficient.
- **Small size:** Embedded systems are often integrated into larger systems, so they must be compact and lightweight.
- **Reliability:** Embedded systems must be able to operate reliably in harsh environments and under demanding conditions.

In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), embedded computing plays a crucial role in enabling the development of smart devices and systems. IoT devices often incorporate sensors, actuators, and wireless communication capabilities, and rely on embedded computing systems to process data, make decisions, and interact with the environment.

Some popular embedded platforms for IoT development include Arduino, Netduino, Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, Intel Galileo, and ARM Cortex-based boards. These platforms provide a range of capabilities and are suitable for different types of IoT projects, from simple sensor-based systems to more complex applications involving data processing and communication.

In summary, embedded computing is a key technology for the development of IoT devices and systems. It enables the integration of computing capabilities into a wide range of devices and systems, allowing them to interact with the environment and perform complex tasks. There are many different embedded platforms available for IoT development, each with its own strengths and capabilities.
