# Actuators for IoT

- An actuator is a device that converts energy into motion. It does this by taking an electrical signal and combining it with an energy source. In an IoT system, the actuator can act on data collected by sensors to create an outcome as determined by the chosen settings of the user.
- Actuators are essential for IoT applications that require physical actions, such as controlling valves, switches, motors, lights, etc.
- There are different types of actuators based on the energy source and the movement they produce. Some common types of actuators are :

  - Thermal/Magnetic Actuators: These are actuated by thermal or mechanical energy. Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs) or Magnetic Shape Memory Alloys (MSMAs) are examples of materials that change their shape when heated or magnetized. These actuators can be used for precise positioning, gripping, or bending applications.
  - Mechanical Actuators: A mechanical actuator executes movement by converting rotary motion into linear motion. It uses gears, screws, belts, chains, or other mechanisms to achieve this. A common example of a mechanical actuator is a linear actuator, which can extend or retract a rod or a platform. These actuators can be used for lifting, pushing, or pulling applications.
  - Soft Actuators: These are made of soft materials that can deform or change shape when stimulated by electricity, light, or chemicals. Examples of soft actuators are Shape Memory Polymers (SMPs), Light Activated Polymers (LAPs), or Electroactive Polymers (EAPs). These actuators can be used for flexible, adaptive, or biomimetic applications.
  - Electrical Actuators: These are actuated by electric current or voltage. They use electromagnets, solenoids, piezoelectric materials, or electrostatic forces to produce motion. Examples of electrical actuators are servo motors, stepper motors, DC motors, or relays. These actuators can be used for rotational, linear, or angular applications.

- The selection of an actuator for an IoT system depends on several factors, such as the required motion, speed, force, accuracy, power consumption, size, cost, and compatibility with the IoT platform. Some popular IoT platforms that support various actuators are Arduino, Netduino, Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, Intel Galileo, and ARM Cortex. These platforms provide hardware interfaces, such as GPIO pins, PWM channels, I2C or SPI buses, or USB ports, to connect and control the actuators. They also provide software libraries, frameworks, or tools to program the actuators and integrate them with the IoT network.
