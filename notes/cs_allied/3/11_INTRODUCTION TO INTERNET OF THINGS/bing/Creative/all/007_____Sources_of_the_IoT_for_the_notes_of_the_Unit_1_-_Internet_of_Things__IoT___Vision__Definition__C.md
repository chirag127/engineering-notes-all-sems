# Sources of the IoT

- The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies for the purpose of connecting and exchanging data with other devices and systems over the internet.
- The sources of the IoT are the devices, sensors, and systems that generate, collect, and transmit data over the internet. These sources can be classified into different categories based on their application domains, such as:
  - Industrial IoT (IIoT): The system of interconnected devices in the industrial sector, such as manufacturing machinery, energy management systems, smart grids, etc.
  - Commercial IoT: The tools and systems used outside of the home, such as smart retail, smart buildings, smart transportation, smart healthcare, etc.
  - Consumer IoT: The devices and systems used in the home, such as smart appliances, smart speakers, smart wearables, smart security, etc.
- The sources of the IoT can also be classified based on the types of data they generate, such as:
  - Temperature, flow, pressure, and humidity sensors: These sensors measure the physical properties of the environment or the process and provide data for monitoring, control, and optimization.
  - Video and image sensors: These sensors capture visual data from the surroundings and provide data for surveillance, recognition, analysis, and enhancement.
  - Location and motion sensors: These sensors track the position and movement of the objects or the people and provide data for navigation, tracking, and safety.
  - Sound and vibration sensors: These sensors detect the acoustic signals and vibrations from the environment or the machines and provide data for diagnosis, alert, and quality control.
  - Biometric and health sensors: These sensors measure the physiological and behavioral characteristics of the individuals and provide data for identification, authentication, and wellness.
- The sources of the IoT rely on various technologies to enable the connectivity and data exchange over the internet, such as:
  - Access to low-cost, low-power sensor technology: Affordable and reliable sensors are making IoT technology possible for more manufacturers.
  - Connectivity: A host of network protocols for the internet has made it easy to connect sensors to the cloud and to other “things” for efficient data transfer.
  - Cloud computing platforms: Cloud computing platforms provide the infrastructure and services for storing, processing, and analyzing the data from the IoT sources.
  - Machine learning and analytics: Machine learning and analytics provide the tools and techniques for extracting insights and value from the data generated by the IoT sources.
  - Conversational artificial intelligence (AI): Conversational AI provides the interface and interaction for communicating with the IoT sources using natural language.
