# Digital sensors for IoT

- Digital sensors are pieces of hardware that detect changes in an environment and collect data in a digital format .
- Digital sensors can interact directly with an IoT microcontroller, eliminating the need for an analog-to-digital converter.
- Digital sensors can be classified based on the physical phenomenon they measure, such as temperature, pressure, humidity, light, motion, sound, etc.
- Digital sensors can be used in various IoT applications, such as consumer devices, industry 4.0, medical applications, smart cities, etc .
- Some examples of digital sensors for IoT are:
  - **Thermocouples**: These are devices that measure temperature by generating a voltage difference between two dissimilar metals.
  - **Accelerometers**: These are devices that measure acceleration or vibration by sensing the force exerted on a mass.
  - **Gyroscopes**: These are devices that measure angular velocity or orientation by sensing the Coriolis force on a spinning mass.
  - **Magnetometers**: These are devices that measure magnetic fields by sensing the Lorentz force on a current-carrying wire.
  - **Proximity sensors**: These are devices that measure the presence or distance of an object by emitting and detecting electromagnetic waves, such as infrared, ultrasonic, or radar.
  - **Environmental sensors**: These are devices that measure various environmental parameters, such as air quality, humidity, pressure, etc.
  - **Optical sensors**: These are devices that measure light intensity, color, or wavelength by sensing the interaction of light with a material.
  - **Microphones**: These are devices that measure sound waves by sensing the vibration of a diaphragm.
  - **Cameras**: These are devices that capture images or videos by sensing the light intensity on a pixel array.
