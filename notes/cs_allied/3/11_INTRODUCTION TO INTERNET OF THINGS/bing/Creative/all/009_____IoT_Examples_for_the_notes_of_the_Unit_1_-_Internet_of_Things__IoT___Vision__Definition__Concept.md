# IoT Examples

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that can collect, process, exchange and utilize data interrelatedly, via the internet or other communications networks. IoT has many different applications in various domains, such as transportation, agriculture, retail, healthcare, manufacturing and urban development. Here are some examples of IoT devices and systems that illustrate the potential and benefits of IoT:

- **Connected cars**: IoT enables vehicles to communicate with each other, with the infrastructure, and with the cloud, to provide enhanced safety, efficiency, convenience and entertainment features. For example, Tesla's cars can receive over-the-air (OTA) software updates from Airbiquity, a software and engineering company that provides OTA solutions for the automotive industry. Autonomous vehicles, such as self-driving cars and trucks, use a slew of connected devices, such as artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled cameras, motion sensors and onboard computers, to safely navigate roadways in all sorts of traffic and weather conditions.

- **Smart appliances**: IoT enables household appliances, such as refrigerators, ovens, washers and dryers, to be controlled remotely, to monitor their performance and energy consumption, and to alert users of any issues or maintenance needs. For example, Samsung's Family Hub refrigerator can connect to the internet and other smart devices, such as smartphones and tablets, to display calendars, photos, recipes, shopping lists and more on its touchscreen.

- **Connected security systems**: IoT enables home and business owners to monitor and control their security systems, such as cameras, alarms, locks and sensors, from anywhere, using their smartphones or other devices. For example, Ring's video doorbell can stream live video and audio of visitors, and allow users to speak to them, using the Ring app.

- **Smart agriculture equipment**: IoT enables farmers to optimize their crop production and animal husbandry, by using sensors, drones, GPS, RFID and other devices, to collect and analyze data on soil conditions, weather, plant health, animal behavior, water levels and more. For example, John Deere's Field Connect system can measure soil moisture and temperature, and send alerts and recommendations to farmers, using a web-based interface.

- **Connected retail**: IoT enables retailers to enhance their customer experience, inventory management, supply chain efficiency and marketing strategies, by using sensors, beacons, RFID, digital signage and other devices, to collect and utilize data on customer behavior, preferences, location, product availability and more. For example, Amazon Go is a chain of cashierless convenience stores that use computer vision, deep learning and sensor fusion, to allow customers to shop without scanning or checking out.

- **Connected healthcare monitors**: IoT enables patients and healthcare providers to monitor and manage various health conditions, such as diabetes, hypertension, asthma and heart disease, by using wearable devices, such as fitness trackers, smart watches, blood pressure monitors and glucose meters, that can measure and transmit vital signs, activity levels, medication intake and more. For example, Dexcom's G6 continuous glucose monitoring system can measure glucose levels every five minutes, and send alerts and insights to users and their caregivers, using a smartphone app.

- **Connected manufacturing equipment**: IoT enables manufacturers to improve their productivity, quality, safety and sustainability, by using sensors, actuators, robots, cameras and other devices, to collect and analyze data on machine performance, environmental conditions, product defects, energy consumption and more. For example, Siemens' MindSphere is a cloud-based IoT platform that can connect and integrate various industrial assets, such as machines, plants, systems and fleets, and provide analytics and insights for optimization and innovation.

- **Connected cities**: IoT enables urban planners and managers to enhance their public services, infrastructure, environment and livability, by using sensors, cameras, smart meters, smart lighting and other devices, to collect and utilize data on traffic, pollution, waste, energy, water, safety and more. For example, Barcelona's smart city initiatives include installing smart parking sensors, smart street lights, smart irrigation systems, smart waste bins and smart bus stops, to improve mobility, efficiency, sustainability and citizen engagement.
