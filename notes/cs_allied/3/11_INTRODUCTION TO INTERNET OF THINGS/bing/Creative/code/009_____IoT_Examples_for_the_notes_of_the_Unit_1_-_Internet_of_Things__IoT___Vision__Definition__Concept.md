# IoT Examples

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that can collect, process, exchange and utilize data interrelatedly, via the internet or other communications networks. IoT has many different applications in various domains, such as transportation, agriculture, retail, healthcare, manufacturing and urban development. Here are some examples of IoT devices and systems that illustrate the potential and benefits of IoT:

- **Connected cars**: IoT enables vehicles to communicate with each other, with the infrastructure, and with the cloud, to provide enhanced safety, efficiency, convenience and entertainment features. For example, Tesla's cars can receive over-the-air (OTA) software updates from Airbiquity, a software and engineering company that provides OTA solutions for the automotive industry . Connected cars can also use sensors, cameras and AI to enable autonomous driving, as well as provide real-time traffic and navigation information, remote diagnostics and maintenance, and personalized infotainment services.
- **Smart appliances**: IoT enables household appliances to be controlled and monitored remotely, as well as to optimize their performance and energy consumption. For example, Samsung's Family Hub refrigerator can connect to the internet and other smart devices, such as smartphones, tablets and TVs, to provide features such as food management, family communication, entertainment and smart home control.
- **Connected security systems**: IoT enables home and business owners to protect their properties and assets with smart security devices, such as cameras, locks, alarms and sensors, that can be accessed and controlled remotely, as well as send alerts and notifications in case of any suspicious activity. For example, Ring's video doorbell can stream live video and audio of the front door to the owner's smartphone, tablet or PC, and allow them to see, hear and speak to visitors, as well as trigger a siren or call the police if needed.
- **Smart agriculture equipment**: IoT enables farmers to improve their productivity and sustainability with smart devices and systems that can monitor and control various aspects of the agricultural process, such as soil quality, crop health, irrigation, pest control and livestock management. For example, John Deere's Field Connect system can use sensors to measure soil moisture and temperature, and send the data to the cloud, where it can be accessed and analyzed by the farmer, and used to adjust the irrigation schedule and amount.
- **Connected retail**: IoT enables retailers to enhance their customer experience and operational efficiency with smart devices and systems that can collect and analyze data on customer behavior, preferences, inventory, sales and marketing. For example, Amazon Go is a cashierless store that uses cameras, sensors and AI to track what customers pick up and charge them automatically when they leave the store.
- **Connected healthcare monitors**: IoT enables patients and healthcare providers to monitor and manage various health conditions and wellness goals with smart devices and systems that can measure and transmit vital signs, such as blood pressure, heart rate, glucose level and activity level, and provide feedback and guidance. For example, Fitbit's smartwatches and fitness trackers can track the user's steps, calories, sleep, heart rate and more, and sync with the Fitbit app, where the user can view their progress, set goals and join challenges.
- **Connected manufacturing equipment**: IoT enables manufacturers to improve their quality, efficiency and safety with smart devices and systems that can monitor and control various aspects of the production process, such as machine performance, energy consumption, environmental conditions and product quality. For example, Siemens' MindSphere is a cloud-based IoT platform that can connect and analyze data from machines, plants, systems and products, and provide insights and recommendations for optimization and innovation.
- **Connected cities**: IoT enables urban planners and managers to improve the livability, sustainability and resilience of cities with smart devices and systems that can monitor and control various aspects of the urban environment, such as traffic, parking, lighting, waste management, air quality and public safety. For example, Barcelona's Smart City project uses IoT to provide services such as free Wi-Fi, smart parking, smart lighting, smart waste management, smart water management and smart bus networks.
