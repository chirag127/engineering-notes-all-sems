### Internet of Things (IoT): Vision

- The vision behind IoT is to have **plug-n-play smart objects** that can be deployed in any environment with an **interoperable interconnection backbone** that allows them to blend with other smart objects around them.
- IoT aims to provide the vision of **connected world of Things**. Devices, people and systems are connected with one another in such a way that they can communicate with each other over Internet so gives the illusion of **web of things**.
- IoT is a paradigm based on the Internet that comprises many **interconnected technologies** like RFID (Radio Frequency IDentification) and WSAN (Wireless Sensor and Actor Networks) in order to exchange information.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Definition

- The Internet of Things, also called The Internet of Objects, refers to a **wireless network between objects**. By embedding short-range mobile transceivers into a wide array of additional gadgets and everyday items, enabling new forms of communication between people and things, and between things themselves.
- IoT can be defined as a dynamic global network infrastructure with self-configuring capabilities based on standard and interoperable communication protocols where physical and virtual “things” have identities, physical attributes, and virtual personalities and use intelligent interfaces, and are seamlessly integrated into the information network.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Conceptual Framework

- A conceptual framework for IoT consists of three main layers: **perception layer**, **network layer**, and **application layer**.
- The perception layer is responsible for **collecting data** from the physical world through sensors, RFID tags, cameras, etc. and **identifying objects** based on their unique identifiers.
- The network layer is responsible for **transmitting data** from the perception layer to the application layer through various communication technologies such as wireless, cellular, satellite, etc. and **processing data** using cloud computing, big data analytics, etc.
- The application layer is responsible for **providing services** to the users based on the data from the network layer through various applications such as smart home, smart city, smart health, smart agriculture, etc.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Architectural view

- An architectural view of IoT consists of four main components: **things**, **gateways**, **networks**, and **clouds**.
- Things are the **physical objects** that are connected to the IoT network through sensors, actuators, RFID tags, etc. They have **unique identities**, **physical attributes**, and **virtual personalities**.
- Gateways are the **intermediary devices** that connect things to the networks. They provide **data aggregation**, **protocol translation**, **security**, and **management** functions.
- Networks are the **communication infrastructures** that link things and gateways to the clouds. They use various **communication technologies** and **protocols** such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, 6LoWPAN, CoAP, MQTT, etc.
- Clouds are the **computing platforms** that store, process, and analyze the data from the networks. They provide **scalability**, **reliability**, **availability**, and **serviceability** functions.

### Internet of Things (IoT): Technology behind IoT

- The technology behind IoT can be classified into three categories: **identification technology**, **sensing technology**, and **communication technology**.
- Identification technology is used to **assign unique identifiers** to the objects in the IoT network. It includes technologies such as RFID, NFC, QR code, etc.
- Sensing technology is used to **collect data** from the physical world and **convert it into digital signals**. It includes technologies such as temperature sensors, humidity sensors, pressure sensors, etc.
- Communication technology is used to **transmit data** from the objects to the gateways and from the gateways to the clouds. It includes technologies such as wireless, cellular, satellite, etc.
