### Embedded Platforms for IoT

- Embedded platforms for IoT are hardware and software systems that enable the development and deployment of IoT applications on embedded devices, such as microcontrollers, microprocessors, sensors, and actuators.
- Embedded platforms for IoT provide various features and functionalities, such as connectivity, security, real-time performance, low power consumption, scalability, and interoperability.
- Embedded platforms for IoT can be classified into two categories: general-purpose and specific-purpose platforms.
  - General-purpose platforms are designed to support a wide range of IoT applications and devices, and offer flexibility and customization options. Examples of general-purpose platforms are Mbed OS, Amazon FreeRTOS, Azure RTOS, and Linux-based platforms, such as Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, and Intel Galileo boards  .
  - Specific-purpose platforms are tailored to specific IoT domains or use cases, and offer optimized performance and functionality. Examples of specific-purpose platforms are Cisco Embedded Services, AMD Embedded Solutions, and STMicroelectronics STM32Cube  .
- Embedded platforms for IoT can be compared and evaluated based on various criteria, such as:
  - Hardware specifications: the type, size, speed, memory, and power consumption of the embedded device and its components.
  - Software specifications: the operating system, programming language, libraries, frameworks, and tools that are supported by the platform.
  - Connectivity options: the types and protocols of wireless and wired communication that are available on the platform, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, Ethernet, etc.
  - Security features: the mechanisms and standards that are implemented by the platform to protect the data and devices from cyber threats, such as encryption, authentication, authorization, etc.
  - Cost and availability: the price and accessibility of the platform and its components in the market.
