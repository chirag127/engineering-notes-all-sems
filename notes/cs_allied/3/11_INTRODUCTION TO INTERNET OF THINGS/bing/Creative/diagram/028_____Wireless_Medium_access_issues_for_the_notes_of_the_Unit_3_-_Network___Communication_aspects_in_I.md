### Wireless Medium Access Issues

- Wireless medium access issues refer to the challenges and problems that arise when multiple wireless devices share the same communication channel and compete for the limited bandwidth and resources.
- Some of the common wireless medium access issues are:
  - Interference: Interference occurs when unwanted signals from other sources or devices interfere with the desired signal and degrade its quality or cause errors. Interference can be caused by various factors, such as noise, multipath fading, co-channel interference, adjacent channel interference, or hidden terminal problem  .
  - Collision: Collision occurs when two or more devices transmit data at the same time on the same channel and cause the data to be corrupted or lost. Collision can result in retransmissions, delays, or energy wastage .
  - Congestion: Congestion occurs when the demand for the channel exceeds its capacity and causes the network performance to deteriorate. Congestion can lead to increased packet loss, delay, jitter, or throughput reduction .
  - Energy consumption: Energy consumption is a critical issue for wireless devices, especially for those that are battery-powered or have limited energy sources, such as IoT devices. Energy consumption can be affected by various factors, such as transmission power, reception power, idle listening, overhearing, control overhead, or retransmissions  .

- To address these issues, various medium access control (MAC) protocols have been proposed and designed for wireless networks, especially for IoT networks. MAC protocols are responsible for coordinating and regulating the access of multiple devices to the shared wireless channel and ensuring efficient and fair data transmission  .
- Some of the common MAC protocols for IoT networks are:
  - Carrier sense multiple access (CSMA): CSMA is a contention-based protocol that allows a device to sense the channel before transmitting data and avoid collision with other devices. CSMA can be further classified into CSMA with collision avoidance (CSMA/CA), CSMA with collision detection (CSMA/CD), or CSMA with reservation (CSMA/R) .
  - Time division multiple access (TDMA): TDMA is a reservation-based protocol that divides the channel into fixed time slots and assigns them to different devices in a round-robin or pre-defined manner. TDMA can avoid collision and interference, but it may introduce synchronization and scheduling overhead .
  - Frequency division multiple access (FDMA): FDMA is a reservation-based protocol that divides the channel into fixed frequency bands and assigns them to different devices according to their needs or priorities. FDMA can avoid collision and interference, but it may introduce frequency allocation and coordination overhead .
  - Code division multiple access (CDMA): CDMA is a spread-spectrum protocol that allows multiple devices to transmit data simultaneously on the same channel using different codes or sequences. CDMA can avoid collision and interference, but it may introduce code assignment and correlation overhead .
  - Cognitive radio (CR): CR is a spectrum-aware protocol that allows devices to dynamically access the unused or underutilized spectrum bands of licensed or primary users without causing harmful interference. CR can improve spectrum efficiency and utilization, but it may introduce spectrum sensing and management overhead .
  - Cooperative communication (CC): CC is a relay-based protocol that allows devices to cooperate with each other and forward data for other devices to improve the network performance and reliability. CC can overcome the effects of fading and interference, but it may introduce cooperation and coordination overhead .
