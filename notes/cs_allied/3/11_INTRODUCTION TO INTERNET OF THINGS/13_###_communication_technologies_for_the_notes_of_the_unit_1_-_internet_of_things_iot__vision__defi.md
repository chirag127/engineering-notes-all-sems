The Internet of Things (IoT) is a vision of a future where everyday objects are connected to the internet, allowing them to send and receive data, and interact with other devices and systems. The concept of IoT has been around for several decades, but has only recently become a reality due to advances in technology, particularly in the areas of wireless communication, sensors, and microcontrollers.

The conceptual framework of IoT involves a network of physical devices, vehicles, home appliances, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and connectivity which enables these objects to connect and exchange data. The architectural view of IoT involves the use of a cloud-based infrastructure to manage and store the data generated by these connected devices.

The technology behind IoT includes a variety of communication technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, NFC, and cellular networks. These technologies are used to enable devices to communicate with each other and with the cloud-based infrastructure. In addition, IoT also relies on a range of sensors and microcontrollers to gather data from the physical world, and to control the behavior of connected devices.

The sources of IoT include a wide range of industries, including manufacturing, healthcare, transportation, and consumer electronics. Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication is an important aspect of IoT, as it enables devices to communicate with each other without human intervention.

IoT has a wide range of applications, including smart homes, wearable devices, connected cars, industrial automation, and healthcare systems. Some examples of IoT devices include smart thermostats, connected light bulbs, and wearable fitness trackers.

Design principles for connected devices in IoT/M2M systems include layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing and affordability. The design of IoT systems should take into account the communication technologies used, as well as the data generated by connected devices, and the need for data enrichment and consolidation. In addition, the design should be affordable and easy to implement, to ensure widespread adoption of IoT technology.
