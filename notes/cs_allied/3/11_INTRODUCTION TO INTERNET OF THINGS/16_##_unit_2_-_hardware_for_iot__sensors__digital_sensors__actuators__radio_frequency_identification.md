## Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT: Sensors, Digital sensors, actuators, radio frequency identification (RFID) technology, wireless sensor networks, participatory sensing technology. Embedded Platforms for IoT: Embedded computing basics, Overview of IOT supported Hardware platforms such as Arduino, NetArduino, Raspberry pi, Beagle Bone, Intel Galileo boards and ARM cortex.

Unit 2 of Hardware for IoT covers the various hardware components and platforms used in the Internet of Things (IoT) technology. This unit focuses on the hardware components that are used to sense, process, and communicate data in IoT systems.

The following topics are covered in this unit:

1. Sensors: An overview of different types of sensors used in IoT systems, including digital sensors, analog sensors, and smart sensors.

2. Digital sensors: An introduction to digital sensors, including their working principle, applications, and limitations.

3. Actuators: An overview of different types of actuators used in IoT systems, including motors, solenoids, and relays.

4. Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology: An introduction to RFID technology and its applications in IoT systems.

5. Wireless Sensor Networks: An overview of wireless sensor networks, including their working principle, applications, and limitations.

6. Participatory Sensing Technology: An introduction to participatory sensing technology and its applications in IoT systems.

7. Embedded Computing Basics: An introduction to embedded computing, including its history, architecture, and applications.

8. Overview of IoT Supported Hardware Platforms: An overview of different hardware platforms that support IoT, including Arduino, NetArduino, Raspberry Pi, Beagle Bone, Intel Galileo boards, and ARM cortex.

In conclusion, Unit 2 of Hardware for IoT provides a comprehensive overview of the hardware components and platforms used in IoT systems. This knowledge is essential for designing, implementing, and evaluating IoT systems that are capable of sensing, processing, and communicating data in real-world applications.
