The IoT refers to the network of physical devices, vehicles, home appliances, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and connectivity which enables these objects to connect and exchange data. The concept of IoT has gained significant attention in recent years due to its potential to revolutionize the way we live, work, and interact with the world around us.

The conceptual framework of IoT is based on the idea of connected devices that can communicate with each other and with the internet. The architectural view of IoT involves the use of various technologies and protocols to enable communication between devices and to manage and process the data generated by these devices. The technology behind IoT includes various communication protocols such as MQTT, CoAP, and Zigbee, as well as cloud computing, big data, and artificial intelligence technologies.

The sources of IoT data can come from a wide range of devices, including sensors, cameras, and smart appliances. M2M communication refers to the communication between machines, which is a critical aspect of IoT. IoT examples include smart homes, connected cars, industrial automation, and wearable devices.

Design principles for connected devices in IoT/M2M systems include layers and design standardization, communication technologies, data enrichment and consolidation, ease of designing, and affordability. Designing connected devices for IoT/M2M systems requires a deep understanding of the underlying technologies, protocols, and standards. Standardization is important to ensure interoperability between devices and to facilitate the integration of IoT systems into existing infrastructure.

Ease of designing is a critical aspect of IoT, as it enables developers and manufacturers to quickly and easily create new connected devices. This can be achieved through the use of standardized protocols, APIs, and development platforms, as well as through the availability of low-cost, off-the-shelf components.

Affordability is also an important factor in the design of IoT systems, as the cost of connected devices can have a significant impact on the overall adoption of IoT. To address this issue, manufacturers are increasingly using low-cost components and open-source software to reduce the cost of connected devices. Additionally, the use of cloud computing and big data technologies can help to reduce the cost of data storage and processing, making IoT systems more affordable for businesses and consumers alike.

In conclusion, the ease of designing and affordability are critical factors in the success of IoT systems. By leveraging standardized protocols, low-cost components, and cloud computing technologies, manufacturers and developers can create connected devices that are both easy to design and affordable for businesses and consumers.
