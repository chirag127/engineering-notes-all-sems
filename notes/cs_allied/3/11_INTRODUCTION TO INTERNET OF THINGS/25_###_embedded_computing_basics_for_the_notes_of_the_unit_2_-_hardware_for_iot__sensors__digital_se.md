Embedded computing refers to the use of computer technology that is integrated into other devices, products, or systems. It is the technology that underlies the Internet of Things (IoT) and enables devices to collect, transmit, and process data.

Embedded computing has several key features that make it well-suited for IoT applications. First, embedded systems are designed to be small and low-power, which makes them ideal for use in portable and battery-powered devices. Second, embedded systems are designed to be reliable and robust, which makes them well-suited for use in harsh environments and applications where downtime is not an option. Third, embedded systems are designed to be flexible and customizable, which makes them well-suited for use in a wide range of applications and industries.

Embedded computing platforms for IoT include a variety of hardware platforms such as Arduino, NetArduino, Raspberry Pi, Beagle Bone, Intel Galileo boards, and ARM Cortex. These platforms provide a range of features and capabilities that make them well-suited for use in IoT applications. For example, the Arduino platform is a low-cost and easy-to-use platform that is well-suited for hobbyists and makers, while the Raspberry Pi is a more powerful platform that is well-suited for more complex IoT applications.

In conclusion, embedded computing is a critical component of the Internet of Things (IoT) and enables devices to collect, transmit, and process data. With its ability to provide reliable, low-power, and flexible computing capabilities, embedded computing is well-suited for use in a wide range of IoT applications and industries.
