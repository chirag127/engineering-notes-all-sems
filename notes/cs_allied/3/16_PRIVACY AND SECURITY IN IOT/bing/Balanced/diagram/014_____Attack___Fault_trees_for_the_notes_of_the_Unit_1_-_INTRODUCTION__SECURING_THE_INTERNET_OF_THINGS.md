### Attack & Fault Trees

- Attack and fault trees are graphical methods to model and analyze the reliability and security of systems, especially in the context of IoT.
- A fault tree is a tree structure that shows how different events or failures can lead to a top-level undesired event or fault. The nodes of the tree are connected by logical gates (such as AND, OR, NOT) that indicate how the events combine to cause the fault. The leaves of the tree are basic events or failures that can be assigned probabilities or frequencies. The fault tree can be used to calculate the probability or frequency of the top-level fault, as well as to identify the most critical events or failures that contribute to it.
- An attack tree is a tree structure that shows how different actions or steps can lead to a top-level goal or attack. The nodes of the tree are connected by logical gates (such as AND, OR, NOT) that indicate how the actions combine to achieve the attack. The leaves of the tree are atomic actions or steps that can be assigned costs, resources, or difficulties. The attack tree can be used to evaluate the feasibility or risk of the top-level attack, as well as to identify the most effective or efficient actions or steps to perform it.
- Attack and fault trees can be integrated to model and analyze the security of IoT systems, considering both random failures and malicious attacks. The integration can be done by using attack trees as sub-trees of fault trees, or by using a hybrid approach called attack-fault trees, where the nodes can be either events or actions. The integrated approach can help to assess the impact of cyber attacks on the physical components of IoT systems, as well as to design countermeasures or defenses to mitigate them.
