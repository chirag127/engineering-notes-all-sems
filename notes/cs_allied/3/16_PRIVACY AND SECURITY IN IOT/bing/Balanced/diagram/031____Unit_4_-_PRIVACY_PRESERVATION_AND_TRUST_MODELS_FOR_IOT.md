## Unit 4 - Privacy Preservation and Trust Models for IoT

- Privacy preservation and trust models are important for ensuring the security and reliability of IoT applications and services.
- Privacy preservation refers to the protection of personal and sensitive data from unauthorized access, disclosure, or misuse by IoT devices, networks, or platforms.
- Trust models refer to the mechanisms for evaluating and establishing the trustworthiness of IoT entities, such as devices, users, or service providers, based on their behavior, reputation, or credentials.
- Some of the challenges and issues in privacy preservation and trust models for IoT are:
  - The heterogeneity and diversity of IoT devices, networks, and platforms, which may have different security and privacy capabilities, standards, and policies.
  - The large-scale and dynamic nature of IoT systems, which may involve frequent interactions and data exchanges among numerous and diverse entities, creating complex trust relationships and privacy risks.
  - The resource constraints and limitations of IoT devices, which may affect their ability to perform complex cryptographic operations, store large amounts of data, or communicate securely and efficiently.
  - The trade-off between privacy and utility, which may require balancing the level of data protection and obfuscation with the quality and functionality of IoT services and applications.
- Some of the existing or proposed techniques and solutions for privacy preservation and trust models for IoT are:
  - Encryption and decryption: This technique involves using cryptographic algorithms to transform data into unreadable form, which can only be restored by authorized parties who possess the corresponding keys. Encryption and decryption can be applied to data at rest, in transit, or in use, depending on the security and privacy requirements. For example, the DPP model (Data Privacy Protection) selectively encrypts data based on their sensitivity and the trust level of the receiver.
  - Obfuscation and anonymization: This technique involves modifying or hiding data to reduce their identifiability or sensitivity, while preserving their utility or functionality. Obfuscation and anonymization can be applied to data or metadata, such as location, time, or device ID, to prevent linking or profiling attacks. For example, the EPIC model (Efficient Privacy-preserving IoT Communication) uses a differentially private obfuscation mechanism to add noise to data before sending them to the cloud.
  - Functional encryption and decryption: This technique involves using cryptographic algorithms that allow authorized parties to perform specific functions on encrypted data, without revealing any other information. Functional encryption and decryption can be used to enable fine-grained access control and data sharing among IoT entities, based on their roles, attributes, or contexts. For example, the privacy-preserving trust model proposed by Chen and Tian (2017) uses functional encryption and decryption to evaluate the trustworthiness of IoT devices based on their interactions.
  - Information relevance and contextual privacy perception: This technique involves assessing the relevance and sensitivity of data based on the context and purpose of the IoT application or service, and applying appropriate privacy policies and mechanisms accordingly. Information relevance and contextual privacy perception can help to achieve a balance between privacy and utility, and to enhance the user's awareness and control over their data. For example, the IRM model (Information Relevance Model) proposed by Chen and Tian (2017) uses a fuzzy logic system to determine the relevance of data for different IoT applications.
  - Interaction-based privacy protection and management: This technique involves monitoring and analyzing the interactions and behaviors of IoT entities, and applying privacy protection and management mechanisms based on the results. Interaction-based privacy protection and management can help to detect and prevent privacy breaches, enforce privacy policies, and adjust privacy preferences. For example, the IPPM framework (Interaction-based Privacy Protection Management) proposed by Chen and Tian (2017) uses a combination of techniques, such as restricting and neutralizing non-authorized operations, obfuscating data, and generating informative events and access logs, to protect and manage privacy in IoT interactions.
  - Slicing and mixing: This technique involves dividing data into smaller pieces and mixing them with other data, to reduce their identifiability and sensitivity, while preserving their utility or functionality. Slicing and mixing can be applied to data aggregation or dissemination, to prevent linking or inference attacks. For example, the BPPDA model (Balance Privacy-preserving Data Aggregation) proposed by Li et al. (2017) uses a slicing and mixing technology to aggregate data from multiple IoT devices, while ensuring the privacy of individual data and the accuracy of aggregated data.
