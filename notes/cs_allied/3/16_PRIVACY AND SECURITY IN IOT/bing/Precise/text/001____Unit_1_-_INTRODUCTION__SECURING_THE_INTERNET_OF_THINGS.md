## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION: SECURING THE INTERNET OF THINGS

1. The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of physical devices, vehicles, buildings, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and network connectivity, which enables these objects to collect and exchange data.
2. The IoT has the potential to revolutionize the way we live and work, but it also presents significant security challenges.
3. IoT devices are often vulnerable to hacking and other forms of cyber attack, which can compromise the security of personal and sensitive information.
4. To secure the IoT, it is essential to implement strong security measures at every stage of the device lifecycle, from design and development to deployment and maintenance.
5. This includes using secure communication protocols, encrypting data, and regularly updating software and firmware to address vulnerabilities.
6. It is also important to educate users about the risks associated with IoT devices and to encourage them to take steps to protect their own security, such as changing default passwords and being cautious when connecting to public Wi-Fi networks.
7. By taking a proactive approach to IoT security, we can help to ensure that the benefits of this technology are realized while minimizing the risks.
