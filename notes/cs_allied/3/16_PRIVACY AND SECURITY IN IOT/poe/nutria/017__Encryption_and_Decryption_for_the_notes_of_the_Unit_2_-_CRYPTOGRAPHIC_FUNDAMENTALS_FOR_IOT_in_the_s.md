
### Encryption and Decryption

Encryption and decryption are two fundamental components of cryptographic systems. Encryption is the process of transforming plaintext, or readable data, into ciphertext, or unreadable data. Decryption is the process of transforming ciphertext back into plaintext.

Encryption and decryption are used to protect the confidentiality of data sent over networks, such as the Internet. Without encryption, data sent over a network can be easily intercepted by an attacker.

Encryption algorithms, also known as ciphers, use a key to encrypt and decrypt data. The key is a set of numbers and letters that is used to scramble the data. The encryption algorithm and the key must be known by both the sender and the receiver of the data.

The strength of the encryption is determined by the length and complexity of the key. The longer and more complex the key, the harder it is for an attacker to guess or brute-force the key.

The most common type of encryption is symmetric encryption. In symmetric encryption, the same key is used to encrypt and decrypt data. The sender and receiver must both know the key for the data to be successfully transmitted.

Asymmetric encryption, also known as public-key encryption, uses two different keys to encrypt and decrypt data. The public key is used to encrypt data and the private key is used to decrypt data. The public key is made available to anyone who wants to send data, while the private key is kept secret by the owner.

Encryption and decryption are essential components of cryptographic systems and are used to protect the confidentiality of data sent over networks. Symmetric encryption uses the same key to encrypt and decrypt data, while asymmetric encryption uses two different keys to encrypt and decrypt data.
