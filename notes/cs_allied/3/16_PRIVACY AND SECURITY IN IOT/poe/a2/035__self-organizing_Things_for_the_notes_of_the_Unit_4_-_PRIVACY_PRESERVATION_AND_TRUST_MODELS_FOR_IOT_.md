### Self-Organizing Things

- IoT devices are heterogeneous, autonomous and decentralized. They can automatically discover and organize themselves into dynamic networks.
- Self-organization enables IoT devices to adapt to changes, failures and attacks. It makes IoT systems more robust, scalable and resilient.
- Key characteristics of self-organizing IoT systems:
    - Decentralized control: There is no central controller. Devices collaborate and make decisions locally.
    - Dynamic structure: The network structure evolves over time as devices join, leave or fail. New connections are formed and old ones broken dynamically.
    - Adaptability: The system can adjust itself to changes in the environment, user requirements or device population. It reconfigures itself to optimize performance.
    - Emergence: Complex global behaviors emerge from the interactions between simple local rules and mechanisms. The system as a whole exhibits properties not directly programmed into individual devices.
- Challenges:
    - Complexity: Emergent behaviors can be hard to predict or understand. This makes self-organizing systems difficult to design, verify and debug.
    - Dependence on other devices: Devices have to rely on the proper functioning of other devices they interact with. This can lead to cascading failures or vulnerabilities.
    - Security: Decentralized systems are harder to protect. There is no central authority to enforce security policies or detect attacks. Devices have to coordinate to detect and respond to threats in a distributed manner.
