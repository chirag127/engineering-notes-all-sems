### Security Requirements in IoT

1. Confidentiality - Ensuring that data is only accessible to authorized entities. Data should be encrypted when stored or transmitted.
2. Integrity - Ensuring that data is accurate and complete, and has not been modified by unauthorized entities. Data validation and verification techniques must be used.
3. Availability - Ensuring that IoT systems and data are accessible and usable as required. DoS attacks must be prevented and redundancy/failover mechanisms implemented.
4. Authentication - Ensuring that only legitimate/authorized devices or users can access the IoT system or data. Strong authentication using certificates or cryptographic techniques should be implemented.
5. Authorization - Ensuring that only legitimate/authorized devices or users can perform specific actions or access specific data/resources. Access control mechanisms must be implemented to restrict access to authorized entities only.
6. Non-repudiation - Ensuring that entities cannot deny having performed certain actions or being the source of certain data/communications. This requires recording the identity of the entity that performed an action or sent data.
7. Privacy - Ensuring that personal/private data is only collected and used as permitted by the device owner or user. Data privacy laws must be complied with and data collected/used should be minimized.
8. Resilience - Ensuring that IoT systems can withstand malicious attacks or other disruptions and continue to function. Systems should be designed with security controls and to recover from failures.
9. Safety - Ensuring that IoT systems do not endanger people, property or the environment. Systems should be designed securely and include failsafe mechanisms.
