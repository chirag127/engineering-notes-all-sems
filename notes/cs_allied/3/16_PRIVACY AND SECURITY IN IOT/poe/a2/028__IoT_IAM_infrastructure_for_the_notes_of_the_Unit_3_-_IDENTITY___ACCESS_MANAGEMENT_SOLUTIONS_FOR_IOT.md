### IoT IAM infrastructure

- IoT devices have limited computing power and memory, so lightweight authentication and access control mechanisms are required.
- IoT devices are connected to the internet, so secure communication and data protection are essential.
- IoT ecosystems comprise devices, networks, clouds, and applications from multiple vendors, so interoperability of IAM solutions is necessary.
- The scale of IoT networks requires automated provisioning and management of identities, credentials, and access rights.

The key components of an IoT IAM infrastructure are:

- IoT device identity: Each device is assigned a unique identity to enable authentication and access control. Identities can be credentials, certificates, or biometrics.
- Authentication: IoT devices authenticate to the network and other devices/services. This can use passwords, certificates, or cryptographic proofs. Authentication may be delegated to more powerful edge devices or cloud services.
- Authorization: Access rights are defined based on IoT device identities and attributes. Policies are used to control access to devices, networks, data, and applications.
- Credential/key management: Management of keys, certificates, and other credentials to enable secure authentication and communication. This can be managed locally on devices or delegated to more powerful servers.
- Auditing/logging: Recording of security-relevant events to enable monitoring, analysis, and accountability. Events include authentication attempts, access attempts, and security violations.
