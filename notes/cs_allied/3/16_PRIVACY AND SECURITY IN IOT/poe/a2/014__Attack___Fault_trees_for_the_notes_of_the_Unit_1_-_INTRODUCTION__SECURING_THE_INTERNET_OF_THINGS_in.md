### Attack & Fault trees for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION: SECURING THE INTERNET OF THINGS

1. Attack Trees
- Attack trees are a formal, structured representation of the steps an adversary might take to achieve some goal which threatens the security objectives of a system.
- They show the various ways in which a system can be attacked and help identify vulnerabilities.
- The root node represents the adversary's goal and lower-level nodes represent subgoals or steps required to achieve the parent node.
- They are useful for threat modeling to identify possible threats and vulnerabilities.

2. Fault Trees
- Fault trees are a top-down, deductive analytical technique to identify potential failures in a system.
- The root node represents an undesired state or failure and lower-level nodes represent faults/events that could contribute to the parent node.
- They are useful for risk analysis to determine what could go wrong and implications of potential faults or failures.
- Both attack trees and fault trees use a tree-like structure and logic gates to model the relationship between nodes.
