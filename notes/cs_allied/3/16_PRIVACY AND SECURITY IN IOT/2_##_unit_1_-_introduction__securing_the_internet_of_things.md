## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION: SECURING THE INTERNET OF THINGS

The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnected network of physical devices, vehicles, buildings, and other objects that are embedded with sensors, software, and network connectivity, allowing them to collect and exchange data. However, with the increasing number of connected devices, the security of IoT has become a major concern.

Unit 1 - Introduction: Securing the Internet of Things covers the basics of IoT security, including the challenges and risks associated with securing IoT devices and networks.

1. IoT Security Challenges: The security of IoT devices and networks is challenged by a number of factors, including:
   a. Complexity: IoT devices are often complex and difficult to secure.
   b. Vulnerability: IoT devices are often vulnerable to attacks due to their reliance on software and network connectivity.
   c. Interconnectivity: IoT devices are interconnected with other devices, creating a complex network that can be difficult to secure.
   d. Scalability: The number of IoT devices is growing rapidly, creating a need for scalable security solutions.

2. IoT Security Risks: The security risks associated with IoT devices and networks include:
   a. Privacy: IoT devices collect and transmit sensitive personal and financial information, creating a risk of data breaches and identity theft.
   b. Safety: IoT devices can be used to cause physical harm, such as through the manipulation of industrial control systems.
   c. Reliability: IoT devices can be used to disrupt critical infrastructure, creating a risk to public safety and national security.

In conclusion, Unit 1 - Introduction: Securing the Internet of Things covers the basics of IoT security, including the challenges and risks associated with securing IoT devices and networks. IoT security is challenged by factors such as complexity, vulnerability, interconnectivity, and scalability, and it is crucial to address these challenges in order to secure the growing number of IoT devices and networks.
