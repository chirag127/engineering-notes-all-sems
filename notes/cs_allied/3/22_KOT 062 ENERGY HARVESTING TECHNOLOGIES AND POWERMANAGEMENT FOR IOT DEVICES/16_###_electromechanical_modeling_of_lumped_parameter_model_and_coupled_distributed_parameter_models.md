Electromechanical modeling is the process of modeling the behavior of systems that involve both electrical and mechanical components. In the context of piezoelectric energy harvesting, electromechanical modeling is used to predict the performance of piezoelectric energy harvesters, and to design and optimize these systems for maximum energy conversion efficiency.

There are two main types of electromechanical models used in piezoelectric energy harvesting: lumped parameter models and coupled distributed parameter models.

Lumped parameter models are simple models that represent the behavior of a system using a small number of parameters, such as resistance, inductance, and capacitance. These models are useful for analyzing the behavior of simple systems, but may not accurately represent the behavior of more complex systems.

Coupled distributed parameter models, on the other hand, represent the behavior of a system using a large number of parameters that are distributed throughout the system. These models are more complex than lumped parameter models, but are more accurate and can be used to analyze the behavior of more complex systems.

Closed-form solutions are mathematical solutions that can be used to analyze the behavior of a system without the need for numerical simulations. Closed-form solutions are useful for analyzing the behavior of simple systems, and can provide insights into the behavior of more complex systems.
