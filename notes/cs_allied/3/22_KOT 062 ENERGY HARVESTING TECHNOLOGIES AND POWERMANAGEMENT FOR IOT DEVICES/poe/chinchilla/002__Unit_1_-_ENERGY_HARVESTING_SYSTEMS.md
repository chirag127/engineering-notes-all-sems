## Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

Energy harvesting systems are devices that convert ambient energy from the environment into usable electrical energy. These systems can be used to power various electronic devices without the need for external power sources or batteries. In this unit, we will learn about the different types of energy harvesting systems, their working principles, and their applications.

### Types of Energy Harvesting Systems

1. Solar Energy Harvesting Systems: These systems use solar panels to convert sunlight into electrical energy. They are commonly used in outdoor applications such as street lighting, traffic signals, and remote sensors.

2. Thermoelectric Energy Harvesting Systems: These systems use the temperature difference between two materials to generate electrical energy. They are commonly used in industrial applications to power sensors and other low-power devices.

3. Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting Systems: These systems use the mechanical vibrations of a material to generate electrical energy. They are commonly used in consumer electronics such as watches and calculators.

4. Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting Systems: These systems use the electromagnetic waves present in the environment to generate electrical energy. They are commonly used in wireless sensors and RFID tags.

### Working Principles of Energy Harvesting Systems

1. Conversion of Ambient Energy: Energy harvesting systems convert ambient energy from the environment into electrical energy, which can be stored or used to power electronic devices.

2. Energy Storage: Energy harvesting systems often include a storage device such as a battery or capacitor to store the electrical energy for later use.

3. Power Management: Energy harvesting systems require a power management system to regulate the amount of energy stored and delivered to the electronic device.

### Applications of Energy Harvesting Systems

1. Wireless Sensors: Energy harvesting systems are commonly used to power wireless sensors for environmental monitoring, industrial process control, and home automation.

2. Wearable Electronics: Energy harvesting systems can be used to power wearable electronics such as smart watches and fitness trackers.

3. Internet of Things (IoT): Energy harvesting systems can be used to power IoT devices such as smart homes, smart cities, and industrial automation.

In conclusion, energy harvesting systems are an important technology that allows for the conversion of ambient energy from the environment into usable electrical energy. Understanding the different types of energy harvesting systems, their working principles, and their applications is crucial for the development of sustainable and energy-efficient electronic devices.
