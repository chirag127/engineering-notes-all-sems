### Basic Principles for the Notes of Unit 3 - Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting and Non-Linear Techniques

In this unit, we will be exploring the principles of electromagnetic energy harvesting and non-linear techniques for powering IoT devices. Here are some basic principles to keep in mind while taking notes:

1. Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting:
   - Electromagnetic energy harvesting involves the conversion of ambient electromagnetic radiation into electrical energy.
   - The energy is harvested using an energy harvester, which consists of a transducer that converts the electromagnetic energy into electrical energy, and a rectifier that converts the AC output of the transducer into DC.
   - The harvested energy can be used to power low-power IoT devices.

2. Non-Linear Techniques:
   - Non-linear techniques involve the use of non-linear components such as diodes and capacitors to improve the efficiency of energy harvesting.
   - These techniques can be used to harvest energy from low-frequency and high-frequency sources.
   - Some non-linear techniques include rectenna, voltage multiplier, and charge pump.

3. Design Considerations:
   - When designing an electromagnetic energy harvester, it is important to consider the frequency and power level of the ambient electromagnetic radiation.
   - The choice of transducer and rectifier depends on the frequency and power level of the radiation.
   - Non-linear techniques can be used to improve the efficiency of energy harvesting, but they also introduce complexities in the design.

4. Applications:
   - Electromagnetic energy harvesting is particularly useful for powering low-power IoT devices that operate in environments with ambient electromagnetic radiation.
   - Some applications of electromagnetic energy harvesting include wireless sensor networks, RFID tags, and wearable devices.

By keeping these basic principles in mind, you will be able to take effective notes and understand the principles of electromagnetic energy harvesting and non-linear techniques for powering IoT devices.
