### Harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags

- Radio frequency (RF) sensors and identification (ID) tags have minimal energy requirements and can be powered by energy harvesting from ambient RF signals.
- The RF energy harvesting is done using an antenna and a rectifier circuit to convert the RF energy into DC electricity that can power the sensor or ID tag.
- The rectenna (rectifying antenna) efficiently converts RF energy to DC electricity and can have high conversion efficiency (up to 90%) if designed properly based on the RF frequency.
- RF energy harvesting is suitable for powering sensors and tags in RF-rich environments such as near radio transmitters or Wi-Fi/cellular base stations. The power requirements of the sensors/tags and the available RF energy density determine the feasibility.
- The intermittent nature of RF signals requires energy storage (e.g., capacitors) and power management to provide continuous power to the sensors/tags. Ultra-low power microcontrollers and circuits are needed for RF-powered applications.
- RF energy harvesting does not require any batteries and enables self-sustaining sensor networks and ubiquitous ID tagging. However, the reliance on ambient RF signals can affect performance and robustness. Shielding and antennas can be used to improve RF coupling and harvesting.
