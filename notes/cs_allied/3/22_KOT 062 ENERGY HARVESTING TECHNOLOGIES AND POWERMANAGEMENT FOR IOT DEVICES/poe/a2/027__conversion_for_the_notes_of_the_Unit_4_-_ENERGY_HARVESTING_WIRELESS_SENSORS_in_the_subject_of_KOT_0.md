1. Introduction to Wireless Sensors and Energy Harvesting
- Wireless sensors are small electronic devices that contain sensors, processors, and transmitters.
- They are used to measure physical parameters such as temperature, pressure, motion, etc. and transmit the data wirelessly.
- However, wireless sensors require a power source, usually batteries which have a limited lifetime.
- Energy harvesting is the process of capturing small amounts of energy from the environment and converting it into electric energy to power wireless sensors and eliminate the need for batteries.

2. Types of Energy Sources for Harvesting
- Light energy - using photovoltaic cells to convert sunlight into electricity
- Vibration energy - using piezoelectric transducers or electromagnetic induction to convert mechanical vibrations into electricity
- Thermal energy - using thermoelectric generators to convert heat into electricity
- Radio frequency energy - using rectenna to convert RF signals into DC electricity

3. Comparison of different energy sources
- Light energy - abundant source but dependent on sunlight, low energy density
- Vibration energy - omnipresent but random and intermittent energy source, low energy density
- Thermal energy - omnipresent but energy density dependent on temperature difference
- Radio frequency energy - abundant source but dependent on RF signals in the environment, medium energy density

4. Energy harvesting circuits and systems
- The energy harvesting process requires circuits and systems to convert the input energy into usable electric energy and provide voltage regulation and storage.
- Circuits for maximum power point tracking to get maximum output from the energy source.
- Storage elements like rechargeable batteries or supercapacitors to store the harvested energy.
- Voltage regulators to provide a stable voltage supply to the wireless sensor.

5. Conclusion
- Energy harvesting is a promising solution to power wireless sensors and IoT devices sustainably without the need for batteries.
- Multiple ambient energy sources can be harvested with transducers and converted into electricity using circuits and systems.
- However, the energy densities of natural sources are low and intermittent, so energy harvesting is most suitable for low power sensors and devices.
