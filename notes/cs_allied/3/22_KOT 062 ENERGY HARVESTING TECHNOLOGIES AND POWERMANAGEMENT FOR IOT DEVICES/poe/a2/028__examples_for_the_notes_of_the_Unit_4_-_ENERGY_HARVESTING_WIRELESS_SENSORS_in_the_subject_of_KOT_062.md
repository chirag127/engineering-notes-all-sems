### 1. Introduction
- Wireless sensors are small electronic devices that contain sensors, processors, and transmitters.
- They are used to monitor physical or environmental conditions such as temperature, sound, pressure, etc. and wirelessly transmit the data to a base station.
- However, wireless sensors are battery-powered and have limited lifetimes due to the small size of batteries that can fit into these devices.
- Energy harvesting is a promising solution to provide perpetual power to wireless sensors and eliminate the need for battery replacement.

### 2. Types of Energy Sources
The various ambient energy sources that can be harvested for wireless sensors include:

- Light (solar, infrared)
- Vibration (piezoelectric, electromagnetic)
- Thermal (thermoelectric)
- Radio frequency
- Wind

These energy sources are converted into electrical energy using transduction techniques like photovoltaic, piezoelectric, thermoelectric, etc. The harvested energy is then stored in capacitors or rechargeable batteries and used to power the wireless sensor.

### 3. Energy Harvesting Circuit
The basic energy harvesting circuit consists of:

- Transducer: Converts ambient energy into electrical energy
- Rectifier: Converts AC output of transducer to DC
- Storage: Capacitors or rechargeable batteries to store harvested energy
- Voltage regulator: Provides stable voltage to sensor components
- Sensor electronics: Components powered by harvested energy

The efficiency of the overall system depends on the efficiency of each circuit component and its ability to optimally utilize the harvested energy.
