### Piezoelectric materials

- Piezoelectric materials produce an electric charge in response to applied mechanical stress. They convert mechanical energy into electrical energy and vice-versa.
- Common piezoelectric materials are:
-- Quartz: Naturally occurring crystal used in watches and electronic oscillators.
-- Lead zirconate titanate (PZT): Ceramic material with high piezoelectric effect. Used in sensors and actuators.
-- Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF): Polymer material with relatively high piezoelectric effect. Used in energy harvesting applications.
- The amount of charge generated depends on:
-- Material properties like piezoelectric coefficient. Higher the coefficient, more the charge generated.
-- Applied stress or force. Higher the stress, more the charge generated.
-- Crystal structure and orientation. Proper orientation leads to higher piezoelectric effect.
- Applications:
-- Sensors and actuators as transducers to convert between mechanical and electrical energy.
-- Energy harvesting to power low-power electronic devices using ambient vibrations.
-- Ultrasonic devices like ultrasonic cleaners, sonar, medical imaging, etc.
