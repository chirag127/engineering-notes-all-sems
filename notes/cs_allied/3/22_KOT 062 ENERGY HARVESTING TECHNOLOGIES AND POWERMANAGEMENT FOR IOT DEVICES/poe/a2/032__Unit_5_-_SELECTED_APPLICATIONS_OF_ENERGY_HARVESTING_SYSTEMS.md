## Unit 5 - SELECTED APPLICATIONS OF ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

1. Energy harvesting for wireless sensor networks
- Wireless sensor networks(WSNs) consisting of remotely deployed nodes that operate on limited battery power.
- Energy harvesting can potentially provide a long operational lifetime for such networks by continually replenishing the batteries.
- Harvested energy can come from sources like ambient light, thermal gradients, and vibration/motion.
- Efficient energy harvesting and power management circuits are required to adapt to variable ambient energy levels.

2. Biomedical applications
- Implantable medical devices can use harvested energy from the human body to power sensors, processors, and wireless transmitters.
- Heartbeat, blood flow, muscle motion, etc. can be used as energy sources for biomedical implants using piezoelectric, electromagnetic, or thermoelectric transducers.
- The efficiency of energy conversion and power processing circuits is critical for minimally sized devices with precise functionality.

3. Industrial and commercial systems
- Vibration energy harvesters can use ambient vibration/motion in industrial machines/facilities to power wireless sensors and monitoring devices.
- This eliminates the need for battery maintenance or replacement, enabling long-term continuous monitoring for equipment health/preventive maintenance.
- Photovoltaic panels can be used in outdoor systems where solar energy is harnessed to power units like LED street lights or remote monitoring stations.
