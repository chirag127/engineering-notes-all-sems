### Photovoltaic Cell Technologies

-   **Silicon Wafer Based PV Cells:** Uses high purity silicon wafers. The wafer is exposed to light which excites the electrons causing it to flow through the material to produce electricity. Most common and efficient but more expensive to produce.
-   **Thin Film PV Cells:** Made by depositing one or more thin layers of photovoltaic material onto a substrate. More affordable to produce but less efficient than silicon wafer cells. Made of materials like cadmium telluride, copper indium gallium selenide, etc.
-   **Organic PV Cells:** Made of organic polymers or small organic molecules. Inexpensive to produce but less efficient and durable than inorganic cells. Still in early research stages.
-   **Perovskite PV Cells:** Uses a class of materials called perovskites that have high absorption of light and high charge carrier mobility. Shows high potential for efficiency and low cost but still prone to stability issues. Under active research.
-   **Dye-Sensitized PV Cells:** Uses a photosensitive dye to absorb light and inject electrons into a semiconductor material like titanium dioxide. Inexpensive but suffer from low efficiency and durability.

The key factors in evaluating different PV cell technologies are efficiency, cost of production and durability. More research is focused on improving efficiency and stability of thin film and emerging PV cell technologies to make them commercially viable compared to traditional silicon wafer cells.
