## Unit 3 - ELECTROMAGNETIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND NON-LINEAR TECHNIQUES

Electromagnetic energy harvesting is a technology that enables the conversion of ambient electromagnetic energy into electrical energy. Non-linear techniques, on the other hand, are used to increase the efficiency of energy harvesting systems.

Here are some key points to understand about electromagnetic energy harvesting and non-linear techniques:

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is based on the principles of electromagnetic induction, which involves the generation of an electrical current in a conductor when it is exposed to a changing magnetic field.
- Energy harvesting systems can be designed to operate in various frequency ranges, from low-frequency sources such as mechanical vibrations to high-frequency sources such as radio waves.
- The efficiency of an energy harvesting system can be improved by using non-linear techniques such as impedance matching, rectification, and voltage multiplication.
- Impedance matching involves tuning the electrical impedance of the energy harvesting system to match that of the source, which maximizes the power transfer.
- Rectification is the process of converting AC (alternating current) to DC (direct current) by using diodes, which allows for the efficient storage of electrical energy in a battery or capacitor.
- Voltage multiplication is a technique that involves using capacitors and diodes to increase the voltage output of an energy harvesting system, which can be useful for powering devices that require higher voltages than the source can provide.
- Non-linear techniques can also be used to increase the bandwidth of an energy harvesting system, which allows it to capture energy from a wider range of frequencies.
- Non-linear techniques can be applied to various types of energy harvesting systems, including solar cells, piezoelectric generators, and electromagnetic generators.

In conclusion, electromagnetic energy harvesting and non-linear techniques are important technologies that enable the efficient conversion of ambient energy into electrical energy. Understanding the principles behind these technologies is essential for designing and optimizing energy harvesting systems for various applications.
