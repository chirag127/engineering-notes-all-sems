## Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

Energy harvesting, also known as power harvesting or energy scavenging, is the process of capturing and storing small amounts of energy from external sources such as solar, wind, thermal, and kinetic energy. This energy is then used to power small electronic devices such as sensors, wireless communication devices, and wearable technology.

1. **Solar Energy Harvesting:** Solar energy harvesting involves capturing energy from the sun using photovoltaic cells. These cells convert sunlight into electricity, which can be stored in batteries or used to power electronic devices directly.

2. **Wind Energy Harvesting:** Wind energy harvesting involves capturing energy from the wind using wind turbines. These turbines convert the kinetic energy of the wind into mechanical energy, which is then converted into electricity.

3. **Thermal Energy Harvesting:** Thermal energy harvesting involves capturing energy from temperature differences. This can be done using thermoelectric generators, which convert temperature differences into electricity.

4. **Kinetic Energy Harvesting:** Kinetic energy harvesting involves capturing energy from motion. This can be done using piezoelectric materials, which generate an electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress.

Energy harvesting systems have the potential to provide a sustainable and renewable source of energy for small electronic devices. They can also reduce the need for batteries, which can have a negative impact on the environment. However, the amount of energy that can be harvested is often limited, and the efficiency of energy conversion can vary depending on the specific technology used.
