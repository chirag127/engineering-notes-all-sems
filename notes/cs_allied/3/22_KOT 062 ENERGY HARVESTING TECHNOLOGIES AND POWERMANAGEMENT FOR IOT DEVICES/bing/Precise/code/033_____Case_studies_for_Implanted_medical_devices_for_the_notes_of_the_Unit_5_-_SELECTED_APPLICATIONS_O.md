### Case studies for Implanted medical devices

- Several case-control studies have observed associations of implanted medical devices and certain connective-tissue and neurologic diseases. These associations were reexamined using cohort comparisons.
- Six cohorts were defined by implantation of medical devices (silicone, metal bone or joint implants, breast implants, penile implants, pacemakers, artificial heart valves), and four comparison cohorts were defined by surgeries not involving implants.
- Wireless implanted medical devices (WIMD) help millions of device users experience a better quality of life. However, there are concerns about the cybersecurity of these devices.
- The new culture of networked systems that offer everywhere accessible services has given rise to various types of security tradeoffs in cyber physical systems, including implantable medical devices.
- The number of implanted medical devices has increased dramatically in recent years because of an increase in minimally invasive procedures, development of new devices, and the spectrum of clinical indications for which these devices are used.
- Medical implants are devices or tissues that are placed inside or on the surface of the body. Many implants are prosthetics, intended to replace missing body parts.
