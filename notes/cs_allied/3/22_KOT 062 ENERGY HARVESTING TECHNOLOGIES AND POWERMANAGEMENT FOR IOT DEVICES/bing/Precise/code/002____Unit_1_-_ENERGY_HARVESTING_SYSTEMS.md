## Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

Energy harvesting, also known as power harvesting or energy scavenging, is the process of capturing and storing small amounts of energy from external sources such as solar, wind, thermal, and kinetic energy. This energy is then used to power small electronic devices such as sensors, wireless communication devices, and wearable technology.

1. **Types of Energy Harvesting Systems**
    - Solar energy harvesting: This involves capturing energy from sunlight using photovoltaic cells.
    - Wind energy harvesting: This involves capturing energy from wind using wind turbines.
    - Thermal energy harvesting: This involves capturing energy from temperature differences using thermoelectric generators.
    - Kinetic energy harvesting: This involves capturing energy from motion using piezoelectric materials or electromagnetic generators.

2. **Applications of Energy Harvesting Systems**
    - Wireless sensor networks: Energy harvesting systems can be used to power wireless sensors in remote or hard-to-reach locations.
    - Wearable technology: Energy harvesting systems can be used to power wearable devices such as fitness trackers and smartwatches.
    - Internet of Things (IoT) devices: Energy harvesting systems can be used to power IoT devices such as smart home appliances and environmental sensors.

3. **Advantages of Energy Harvesting Systems**
    - Reduced dependence on batteries: Energy harvesting systems can reduce or eliminate the need for batteries, which can be expensive and harmful to the environment.
    - Increased device lifespan: Energy harvesting systems can extend the lifespan of devices by providing a continuous source of power.
    - Reduced maintenance: Energy harvesting systems can reduce the need for maintenance by eliminating the need to replace batteries.

4. **Challenges of Energy Harvesting Systems**
    - Limited energy availability: The amount of energy that can be harvested from external sources is often limited and can vary depending on environmental conditions.
    - Energy storage: Energy harvesting systems often require energy storage devices such as capacitors or batteries to store the harvested energy for later use.
    - System design: Designing energy harvesting systems can be challenging due to the need to balance energy capture, storage, and usage.
