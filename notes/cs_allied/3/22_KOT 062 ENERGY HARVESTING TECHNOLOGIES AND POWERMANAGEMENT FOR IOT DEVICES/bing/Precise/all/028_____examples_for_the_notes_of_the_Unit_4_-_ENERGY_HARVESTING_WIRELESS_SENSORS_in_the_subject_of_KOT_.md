# Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS

Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can convert ambient energy sources into electrical energy to power themselves. These sensors are used in a variety of applications, including environmental monitoring, structural health monitoring, and industrial process control.

Some examples of energy harvesting wireless sensors include:

1. **Solar-powered sensors:** These sensors use photovoltaic cells to convert sunlight into electrical energy. They are commonly used in outdoor applications where sunlight is readily available.

2. **Thermoelectric sensors:** These sensors use the Seebeck effect to generate electricity from temperature differences. They are commonly used in applications where there is a temperature gradient, such as in industrial processes or in the human body.

3. **Piezoelectric sensors:** These sensors use the piezoelectric effect to generate electricity from mechanical stress or strain. They are commonly used in applications where there is vibration or movement, such as in machinery or in wearable devices.

4. **Electromagnetic sensors:** These sensors use electromagnetic induction to generate electricity from changes in magnetic fields. They are commonly used in applications where there is rotation or movement, such as in wind turbines or in vehicles.

These are just a few examples of energy harvesting wireless sensors. There are many other types of sensors that can harvest energy from ambient sources, including radio frequency, acoustic, and kinetic energy. These sensors are an important part of the Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem, as they allow for the deployment of self-powered, low-maintenance sensor networks.
