# Power sources for WSN

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are composed of small, low-power devices that are used to monitor and collect data from the environment. These devices, known as nodes, require a power source to operate. There are several options for powering WSNs, including:

1. **Batteries:** One of the most common power sources for WSNs is batteries. These can be either primary (non-rechargeable) or secondary (rechargeable) batteries. The choice of battery type depends on the application and the expected lifetime of the node.

2. **Energy harvesting:** Another option for powering WSNs is through energy harvesting. This involves capturing energy from the environment, such as solar, wind, or thermal energy, and converting it into electrical energy to power the nodes.

3. **Power over Ethernet (PoE):** In some cases, it may be possible to power WSNs using Power over Ethernet (PoE). This involves transmitting electrical power along with data over Ethernet cables.

4. **Wireless power transfer:** Wireless power transfer is another option for powering WSNs. This involves transmitting power wirelessly from a power source to the nodes.

Each of these power sources has its advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of power source will depend on the specific requirements of the WSN application. It is important to carefully consider the power requirements of the nodes and the availability of power sources when designing a WSN.
