# Case Studies for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Energy Harvesting Wireless Sensors

Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can collect ambient energy from various sources, such as light, heat, vibration, or radio frequency, and use it to power themselves and transmit data wirelessly. Energy harvesting wireless sensors have many advantages, such as:

- They do not need batteries or wires, which reduces maintenance costs and environmental impact.
- They can be deployed in remote, inaccessible, or hazardous locations, where replacing batteries or wires is impractical or impossible.
- They can extend the lifetime and functionality of wireless sensor networks, which are widely used for various applications, such as environmental monitoring, industrial automation, smart buildings, healthcare, and security.

Here are some examples of case studies that demonstrate the use and benefits of energy harvesting wireless sensors:

- **EnOcean** is a company that specializes in energy harvesting wireless sensors and switches for building automation and smart home applications. EnOcean's products use solar, thermal, or mechanical energy to power themselves and communicate via radio. EnOcean's sensors and switches can measure and control parameters such as temperature, humidity, light, motion, occupancy, and air quality, and can be integrated with various systems and platforms, such as KNX, ZigBee, Bluetooth, or IoT cloud services. EnOcean's products can help reduce energy consumption, improve comfort, and enhance security in buildings.
- **Everactive** is a company that develops energy harvesting wireless sensors for industrial and commercial applications. Everactive's products use thermoelectric, photovoltaic, or electromagnetic energy to power themselves and communicate via low-power wide-area networks (LPWANs), such as LoRaWAN or NB-IoT. Everactive's sensors can monitor parameters such as vibration, temperature, pressure, flow, and corrosion, and can be used for predictive maintenance, asset tracking, and process optimization. Everactive's products can help increase operational efficiency, reduce downtime, and prevent failures in industrial and commercial facilities.
- **Wireless Sensor Networks for Environmental Monitoring** is a research project that aims to develop energy harvesting wireless sensors for measuring various environmental parameters, such as temperature, humidity, soil moisture, air quality, and radiation. The project uses different types of energy harvesters, such as solar cells, piezoelectric generators, thermoelectric generators, and electromagnetic generators, to power the sensors and transmit data via ZigBee or Wi-Fi. The project also uses energy management techniques, such as duty cycling, adaptive sampling, and data compression, to optimize the performance and lifetime of the sensors. The project can help improve the accuracy and reliability of environmental monitoring and provide valuable data for scientific research and policy making.
- **RF Energy Harvesting for Wireless Sensors** is a research project that aims to develop wireless sensors that can harvest energy from radio frequency (RF) signals, such as TV, cellular, or Wi-Fi signals, and use it to power themselves and transmit data wirelessly. The project uses rectennas, which are devices that can convert RF signals into direct current (DC) voltage, to power the sensors and communicate via Bluetooth or ZigBee. The project also uses power management techniques, such as maximum power point tracking (MPPT), voltage regulation, and energy storage, to optimize the efficiency and stability of the sensors. The project can help enable wireless sensors to operate in locations where other energy sources are scarce or unavailable, and to reduce the dependence on batteries or wires.
- **Solar Energy Harvesting System for Wireless Sensors** is a research project that aims to develop wireless sensors that can harvest energy from solar radiation and use it to power themselves and transmit data wirelessly. The project uses photovoltaic (PV) cells, which are devices that can convert solar radiation into electric current, to power the sensors and communicate via Wi-Fi or LoRa. The project also uses energy management techniques, such as maximum power point tracking (MPPT), voltage regulation, and energy storage, to optimize the performance and lifetime of the sensors. The project can help enable wireless sensors to operate in outdoor environments, where solar radiation is abundant and reliable, and to reduce the environmental impact of batteries or wires.
