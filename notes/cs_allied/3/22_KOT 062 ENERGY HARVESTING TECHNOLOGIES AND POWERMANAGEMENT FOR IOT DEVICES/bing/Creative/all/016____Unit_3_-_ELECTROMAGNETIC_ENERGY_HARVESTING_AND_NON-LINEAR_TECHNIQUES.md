# Unit 3 - ELECTROMAGNETIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND NON-LINEAR TECHNIQUES

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is the process of converting ambient electromagnetic waves into electrical energy for powering wireless devices or sensors .
- Electromagnetic energy harvesting can be classified into two types: far-field and near-field.
  - Far-field harvesting captures the electromagnetic radiation from sources such as radio, TV, cellular or satellite signals, which have low power density and require large antennas.
  - Near-field harvesting exploits the magnetic or electric fields from sources such as power lines, transformers, motors or RFID readers, which have higher power density and allow smaller coils or capacitors.
- Non-linear techniques are methods that enhance the performance of electromagnetic energy harvesting systems by exploiting the non-linear behavior of some components or structures  .
  - Non-linear techniques can increase the bandwidth, output voltage, power density or efficiency of the harvesting systems, depending on the design and application  .
  - Non-linear techniques can be implemented using various mechanisms, such as magnetic levitation, bistability, snap-through, frequency up-conversion, synchronization or chaos  .
- Some examples of non-linear electromagnetic energy harvesting systems are:
  - A harvester that consists of a small magnet moving in a tube surrounded by a coil and two fixed magnets at the ends, which creates a non-linear magnetic force and a high output voltage .
  - A harvester that uses a magnetic levitation system with a permanent magnet and a coil, which induces a non-linear damping force and a wide bandwidth.
  - A harvester that employs a bistable cantilever beam with a magnet and a coil, which exhibits a snap-through behavior and a frequency up-conversion effect.
