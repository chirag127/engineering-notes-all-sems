# Generation of Electric Power in Semiconductor PV Cells

- PV cells, or photovoltaic cells, are devices that convert light energy into electrical energy using the photovoltaic effect .
- The photovoltaic effect is the phenomenon of generating electric voltage or current in a material when it is exposed to light .
- The material used in PV cells is usually a semiconductor, such as silicon, which has the property of changing its electrical conductivity when light is absorbed .
- A semiconductor is a material that can conduct electricity better than an insulator but not as well as a metal .
- A PV cell consists of two layers of semiconductor with different types of impurities, called dopants, that create a positive (p-type) and a negative (n-type) region .
- The junction between the p-type and n-type regions is called the p-n junction, which forms an electric field that prevents the free movement of electrons .
- When light photons with enough energy strike the p-n junction, they can excite electrons from the valence band to the conduction band, creating electron-hole pairs .
- An electron-hole pair is a pair of free electrons and positively charged holes that can move within the semiconductor .
- The electric field at the p-n junction separates the electron-hole pairs, pushing the electrons to the n-type region and the holes to the p-type region .
- This creates a potential difference, or voltage, between the two regions, which can be used to power an external circuit .
- The power generated by a single PV cell is typically only about two watts, so multiple cells are connected in series or parallel to form a PV module or panel .
- The PV modules or panels can be further connected to form a PV array or system, which can provide electricity for various applications, such as homes, buildings, or grids .
- Some of the factors that affect the performance and efficiency of PV cells are the type and quality of the semiconductor material, the intensity and spectrum of the light source, the temperature and orientation of the cell, and the presence of dust or shading  .
