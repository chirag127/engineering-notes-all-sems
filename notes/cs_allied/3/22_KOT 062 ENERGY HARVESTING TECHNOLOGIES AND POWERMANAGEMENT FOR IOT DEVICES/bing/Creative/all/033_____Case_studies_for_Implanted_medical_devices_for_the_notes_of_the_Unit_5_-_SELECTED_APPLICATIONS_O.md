# Case studies for Implanted medical devices

- Implantable medical devices (IMDs) are electronic devices that are inserted into the human body to monitor, diagnose, or treat various medical conditions. Examples of IMDs include pacemakers, cochlear implants, neurostimulators, drug delivery systems, and biosensors.
- IMDs require a reliable and long-lasting power source to function properly. However, conventional batteries have several limitations, such as limited lifespan, bulky size, toxic materials, and risk of infection or leakage. Therefore, alternative power sources are needed for IMDs.
- Energy harvesting is a promising technique to provide power for IMDs by converting ambient energy sources, such as mechanical, thermal, or chemical energy, into electrical energy. Energy harvesting can reduce or eliminate the need for batteries, prolong the device lifetime, and improve the patient comfort and safety.
- In this section, we will review some case studies of energy harvesting for IMDs, focusing on the energy source, the harvesting mechanism, the power output, and the application.

## Case study 1: Piezoelectric energy harvesting from heartbeat

- Piezoelectric materials can generate electric voltage when subjected to mechanical stress or strain. This property can be exploited to harvest energy from the heartbeat, which is a periodic and rhythmic motion of the heart muscle.
- A piezoelectric energy harvester (PEH) can be attached to the surface of the heart or implanted in the pericardial sac, which is a thin membrane that surrounds the heart. The PEH can convert the mechanical deformation of the heart into electrical energy, which can be used to power cardiac devices, such as pacemakers or defibrillators.
- A typical PEH consists of a piezoelectric layer sandwiched between two electrodes and a flexible substrate. The piezoelectric layer can be made of ceramic, polymer, or composite materials, such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT), polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), or zinc oxide (ZnO) nanowires. The electrodes can be made of metal, such as gold, silver, or platinum. The substrate can be made of polymer, such as polyimide, polyethylene terephthalate (PET), or silicone.
- The power output of a PEH depends on several factors, such as the piezoelectric material, the device geometry, the load resistance, and the frequency and amplitude of the heartbeat. The power output can range from nanowatts to microwatts, depending on the design and optimization of the PEH. For example, a PEH made of PZT and implanted in the pericardial sac of a sheep can generate an average power of 0.18 μW at a load resistance of 100 kΩ and a heart rate of 80 beats per minute. A PEH made of PVDF and attached to the surface of a pig heart can generate an average power of 0.32 μW at a load resistance of 10 MΩ and a heart rate of 120 beats per minute.

## Case study 2: Thermoelectric energy harvesting from body heat

- Thermoelectric materials can generate electric voltage when subjected to a temperature gradient. This property can be exploited to harvest energy from the body heat, which is a constant and abundant source of thermal energy.
- A thermoelectric energy harvester (TEH) can be implanted in the subcutaneous tissue, which is the layer of fat and connective tissue under the skin. The TEH can convert the temperature difference between the body and the environment into electrical energy, which can be used to power implantable devices, such as biosensors, drug delivery systems, or neurostimulators.
- A typical TEH consists of a thermoelectric module (TEM) and a heat sink. The TEM is composed of an array of thermoelectric couples, which are pairs of p-type and n-type semiconductors connected in series by metal electrodes. The TEM can be made of various materials, such as bismuth telluride (Bi2Te3), lead telluride (PbTe), or silicon germanium (SiGe). The heat sink is a metal or polymer structure that dissipates the heat from the hot side of the TEM to the environment. The heat sink can be designed to have a high thermal conductivity and a large surface area to enhance the heat transfer.
