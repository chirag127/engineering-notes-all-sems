# Strategies for enhancing the performance of energy harvesters

Energy harvesters are devices that can convert ambient energy sources, such as vibration, wind, light, heat, etc., into electrical energy. Energy harvesting is a promising technology for powering low-power consumption devices, such as sensors, wireless networks, wearable electronics, etc., without the need for batteries or external power supply. However, the performance of energy harvesters is often limited by the low and variable energy density of the ambient sources, the mismatch between the frequency and amplitude of the input and the output, the nonlinear and complex dynamics of the harvesting systems, and the losses in the conversion process. Therefore, various strategies have been proposed to enhance the performance of energy harvesters, such as increasing the power output, widening the frequency bandwidth, improving the efficiency, and optimizing the design and materials. Some of the common strategies are:

- **Adopting new materials**: The choice of materials for energy harvesters affects their output voltage, current, power, and durability. For example, piezoelectric materials, which can generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress, have different properties depending on their composition, crystal structure, polarization, and fabrication methods. Some of the new materials that have been explored for piezoelectric energy harvesting include lead-free ceramics, polymers, composites, nanomaterials, etc. These materials can offer advantages such as higher piezoelectric coefficients, lower density, higher flexibility, and environmental friendliness .

- **Optimizing the harvesting devices**: The design and configuration of the harvesting devices can also affect their performance. For example, the shape, size, mass, and stiffness of the cantilever beam, which is a common structure for piezoelectric energy harvesting, can influence its natural frequency, mode shape, and deflection. Some of the optimization methods that have been used for piezoelectric energy harvesting include geometric optimization, topology optimization, multi-objective optimization, etc. These methods can help to maximize the power output, match the resonance frequency to the ambient vibration frequency, and reduce the stress and strain in the beam.

- **Introducing nonlinearities**: Nonlinearities are phenomena that cause the response of a system to deviate from the linear relationship between the input and the output. Nonlinearities can be introduced in energy harvesters by using nonlinear elements, such as magnets, springs, bistable structures, etc. Nonlinearities can enhance the performance of energy harvesters by increasing the power output, widening the frequency bandwidth, inducing chaotic behavior, and enabling frequency tuning. For example, bistable energy harvesters, which have two stable equilibrium positions, can exhibit large-amplitude oscillations and snap-through transitions when subjected to broadband vibrations, resulting in higher power output and wider frequency response .

- **Applying power-amplifying strategies**: Power-amplifying strategies are methods that can increase the power output of energy harvesters by modifying the input or the output of the system. For example, one power-amplifying strategy for piezoelectric energy harvesting is to connect the additional lumped mass to a harmonically oscillating base through an elastic foundation. This can create a power-amplifying effect by increasing the relative displacement between the mass and the base, and by tuning the resonance frequency of the system. Another power-amplifying strategy is to use a synchronized switch harvesting on inductor (SSHI) circuit, which can increase the output voltage and power by switching the piezoelectric element to an inductor at optimal instants.

- **Hybridizing with other technologies**: Hybridization is the combination of two or more energy harvesting technologies or mechanisms to achieve a synergistic effect. Hybridization can enhance the performance of energy harvesters by increasing the power output, widening the frequency bandwidth, improving the reliability, and reducing the dependence on a single energy source. For example, triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs), which can generate electric charge by the contact and separation of two materials, can be hybridized with piezoelectric, electromagnetic, or electrostatic energy harvesters to harvest wind energy from different directions, speeds, and frequencies.
