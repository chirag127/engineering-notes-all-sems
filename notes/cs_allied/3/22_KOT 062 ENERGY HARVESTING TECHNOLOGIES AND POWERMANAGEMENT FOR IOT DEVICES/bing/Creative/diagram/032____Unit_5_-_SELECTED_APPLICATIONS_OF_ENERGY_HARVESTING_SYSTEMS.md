## Unit 5 - SELECTED APPLICATIONS OF ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

Energy harvesting systems are devices that collect ambient energy from the environment and convert it into electrical energy for powering wireless devices. Energy harvesting systems can extend the battery life of remote sensors, reduce the maintenance cost of replacing batteries, and enable autonomous operation of devices in inaccessible locations. Some of the common sources of ambient energy are solar, thermal, kinetic, and electromagnetic.

Some of the selected applications of energy harvesting systems are:

- **Industrial applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for remote monitoring, predictive maintenance, and energy-efficient solutions in various industrial sectors, such as manufacturing, oil and gas, mining, and agriculture. For example, energy harvesting systems can power wireless sensors that measure temperature, pressure, vibration, humidity, and gas levels in industrial equipment and pipelines. These sensors can transmit the data to a central server for analysis and alert the operators of any potential faults or failures. Energy harvesting systems can also power actuators that control valves, switches, and motors in industrial processes.  

- **Consumer electronics applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for powering wearable devices, smart watches, fitness trackers, and other low-power electronic gadgets. For example, energy harvesting systems can harvest kinetic energy from the user's body movements, thermal energy from the body heat, or solar energy from the ambient light and use it to charge the battery of the device. Energy harvesting systems can also power wireless keyboards, mice, and remote controls that use radio frequency or infrared signals.  

- **Building and home automation applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for powering smart lighting, heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC), security, and fire alarm systems in buildings and homes. For example, energy harvesting systems can power wireless switches, sensors, and actuators that control the lighting, temperature, humidity, and air quality in different rooms. Energy harvesting systems can also power wireless cameras, smoke detectors, and door locks that enhance the security and safety of the building or home. Energy harvesting systems can reduce the energy consumption and carbon footprint of the building or home by optimizing the use of natural resources and minimizing the use of grid power.  

- **Wireless sensor network (WSN) applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for powering wireless sensor nodes that collect and transmit data in various domains, such as environmental monitoring, health care, smart cities, and military. For example, energy harvesting systems can power wireless sensors that measure temperature, humidity, rainfall, soil moisture, air pollution, and wildlife activity in remote areas. Energy harvesting systems can also power wireless sensors that monitor the vital signs, activity, and location of patients, elderly, and soldiers. Energy harvesting systems can enable long-term and reliable operation of wireless sensor networks without the need for frequent battery replacement or recharging.  

- **Oceanographic monitoring applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for powering oceanographic sensors and devices that measure and transmit data about the ocean conditions, such as salinity, pH, dissolved oxygen, currents, waves, and marine life. For example, energy harvesting systems can power wave energy converters that harvest the kinetic energy of ocean waves and use it to generate electricity for oceanographic sensors and devices. Energy harvesting systems can also power underwater acoustic modems that use sound waves to communicate with other devices or with a surface station. Energy harvesting systems can enable autonomous and continuous operation of oceanographic monitoring systems without the need for external power sources or cables.
