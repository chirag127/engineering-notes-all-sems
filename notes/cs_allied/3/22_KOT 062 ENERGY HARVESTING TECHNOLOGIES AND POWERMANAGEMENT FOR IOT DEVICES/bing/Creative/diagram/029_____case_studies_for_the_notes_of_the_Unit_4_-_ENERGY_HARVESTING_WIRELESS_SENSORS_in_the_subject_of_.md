### Case Studies for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Energy Harvesting Wireless Sensors in the Subject of KOT 062 Energy Harvesting Technologies and Power Management for IoT Devices KCS

Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can collect ambient energy from various sources such as light, heat, vibration, radio frequency, etc. and convert it into electrical energy to power wireless sensor networks (WSNs). WSNs are networks of sensor nodes that can communicate wirelessly and perform sensing, processing, and transmitting functions. WSNs have many applications in intelligent environment-monitoring, industrial automation, smart cities, health care, etc. However, WSNs face the challenge of limited battery life and frequent maintenance. Energy harvesting can provide a sustainable and maintenance-free solution for WSNs by enabling self-powered sensor nodes.

Some of the case studies of energy harvesting wireless sensors are:

- **Using energy harvesting, radio technologies to power wireless sensors for industrial applications**: This case study describes how energy harvesting and radio technologies can be used to power wireless sensors for industrial internet of things (IIoT) applications. The case study explains the benefits of energy harvesting, such as reducing wiring costs, increasing flexibility, and enhancing reliability. It also discusses the challenges of energy harvesting, such as low power output, variable energy availability, and energy management. The case study presents some examples of energy harvesting sources, such as vibration, RF, and light energy, and how they can be used to power different types of sensors, such as pressure, temperature, ultrasound, or video. The case study also introduces some radio technologies, such as Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), ZigBee, and LoRa, and how they can be integrated with energy harvesting to enable low-power and long-range wireless communication.

- **A Study on Piezoelectric Energy-Harvesting Wireless Sensor Networks Deployed in a Weak Vibration Environment**: This case study presents a piezoelectric energy-harvesting wireless sensor network (PEH-WSN) that can be deployed in a weak vibration environment, such as a bridge or a building. The case study explains the working principle of piezoelectric energy harvesting, which is based on the conversion of mechanical strain into electrical voltage. The case study also describes the design and implementation of the PEH-WSN, which consists of piezoelectric energy harvesters, energy storage units, sensor nodes, and a base station. The case study evaluates the performance of the PEH-WSN in terms of energy harvesting efficiency, power consumption, data transmission, and network lifetime. The case study shows that the PEH-WSN can achieve a stable and continuous operation in a weak vibration environment.

- **Energy Prediction and Energy Management in Kinetic Energy-Harvesting Wireless Sensor Networks for Industry 4.0**: This case study focuses on the use of kinetic energy harvesting using piezoelectric technologies to power wireless sensor networks for Industry 4.0 applications. The case study proposes a novel energy prediction and energy management scheme that can optimize the energy consumption and data transmission of the sensor nodes. The case study also presents a prototype of a kinetic energy-harvesting wireless sensor (KEH-WS) that can be attached to a rotating machine to harvest its vibration energy. The case study evaluates the performance of the KEH-WS and the proposed scheme in terms of energy harvesting, energy prediction, energy management, and data transmission. The case study demonstrates that the KEH-WS and the proposed scheme can improve the energy autonomy and the reliability of the wireless sensor network.

- **Powering nodes of wireless sensor networks with energy harvesters for smart agriculture applications**: This case study explores the potential of energy harvesting to power wireless sensor networks for smart agriculture applications. The case study reviews the existing energy harvesting technologies, such as solar, wind, thermal, and piezoelectric, and their suitability for different agricultural scenarios, such as greenhouses, orchards, vineyards, and fields. The case study also presents some examples of energy harvesting wireless sensor networks that have been deployed for smart agriculture applications, such as soil moisture monitoring, irrigation control, pest detection, and crop yield estimation. The case study highlights the benefits of energy harvesting, such as reducing the environmental impact, increasing the scalability, and enhancing the resilience of the wireless sensor networks.
