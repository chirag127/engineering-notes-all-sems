### Energy Harvesting Systems

- Energy harvesting systems are devices or systems that convert ambient energy, such as light, heat, vibration, or radio waves, into electrical energy that can be used to power wireless devices or sensors  .
- Energy harvesting systems are attractive alternatives to battery-operated systems, especially for long-term, low-power, and self-sustaining applications, such as body sensor networks, remote monitoring, and internet of things (IoT) devices .
- Energy harvesting systems typically consist of four main components: an energy transducer, an energy storage element, a power management circuit, and a load .
- The energy transducer is the component that converts the ambient energy into electrical energy. The type of energy transducer depends on the type of ambient energy source. For example, solar cells, thermoelectric generators, piezoelectric devices, and electromagnetic generators are some common types of energy transducers .
- The energy storage element is the component that stores the electrical energy generated by the energy transducer. The type of energy storage element depends on the characteristics of the energy source and the load. For example, capacitors, batteries, and supercapacitors are some common types of energy storage elements .
- The power management circuit is the component that regulates and controls the flow of energy between the energy transducer, the energy storage element, and the load. The power management circuit usually includes functions such as voltage conversion, maximum power point tracking, power conditioning, and power switching .
- The load is the component that consumes the electrical energy provided by the energy harvesting system. The load can be any wireless device or sensor that requires low power and intermittent operation. For example, microcontrollers, wireless transceivers, and actuators are some common types of loads .
