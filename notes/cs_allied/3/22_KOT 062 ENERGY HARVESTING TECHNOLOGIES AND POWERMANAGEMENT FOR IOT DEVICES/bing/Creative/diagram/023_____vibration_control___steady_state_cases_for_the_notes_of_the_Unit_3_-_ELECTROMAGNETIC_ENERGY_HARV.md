### Vibration Control and Steady State Cases for Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting

- Vibration control is the process of reducing or eliminating unwanted vibrations in a system or structure, which can cause noise, damage, fatigue, or instability.
- Vibration energy harvesting is the concept of converting vibration energy into electrical energy, which can be used to power wireless sensors, devices, or systems, or to produce electricity.
- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is one of the main transduction principles for vibration energy harvesting, which uses electromagnetic induction to generate electric current from relative motion between a magnet and a coil.
- Steady state cases are the situations where the vibration energy harvester reaches a constant amplitude and frequency of oscillation, which depends on the input vibration parameters, the harvester properties, and the load resistance.
- Some of the factors that affect the steady state performance of electromagnetic energy harvesters are:

  - The natural frequency of the harvester, which should match the frequency of the input vibration for maximum power output.
  - The damping ratio of the harvester, which determines the bandwidth and the quality factor of the harvester. A low damping ratio means a narrow bandwidth and a high quality factor, which implies a high sensitivity to frequency mismatch, but a high power output at resonance. A high damping ratio means a wide bandwidth and a low quality factor, which implies a low sensitivity to frequency mismatch, but a low power output at resonance.
  - The magnetic flux density of the magnet, which affects the induced voltage and the power output of the harvester. A higher magnetic flux density means a higher induced voltage and a higher power output, but also a higher magnetic attraction force, which can reduce the displacement of the harvester.
  - The number of turns and the cross-sectional area of the coil, which affect the induced voltage and the power output of the harvester. A higher number of turns and a larger cross-sectional area mean a higher induced voltage and a higher power output, but also a higher coil resistance, which can reduce the current and the power output of the harvester.
  - The load resistance of the harvester, which affects the power output and the efficiency of the harvester. The optimal load resistance is the one that matches the internal resistance of the harvester, which maximizes the power output. The efficiency of the harvester is the ratio of the power output to the input vibration power, which can be improved by reducing the losses in the harvester, such as friction, hysteresis, and eddy currents.

- Some of the methods that can be used to improve the vibration control and the steady state performance of electromagnetic energy harvesters are:

  - Using nonlinear techniques, such as bistable or tristable mechanisms, to increase the bandwidth and the power output of the harvester, and to enable multiple resonant modes and frequency up-conversion.
  - Using feedback control, such as active or semi-active damping, to adjust the damping ratio and the natural frequency of the harvester, and to enhance the stability and the robustness of the harvester.
  - Using hybrid transduction, such as combining electromagnetic and piezoelectric principles, to increase the power output and the efficiency of the harvester, and to exploit different vibration modes and directions.
  - Using array or network configurations, such as connecting multiple harvesters in series or parallel, to increase the power output and the reliability of the harvester, and to adapt to different vibration sources and environments.
