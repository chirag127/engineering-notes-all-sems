### Piezoelectric materials

- Piezoelectric materials are materials that can convert mechanical stress into electrical charge, or vice versa. This phenomenon is called the piezoelectric effect .
- Piezoelectric materials can be classified into the following categories:
  - Single crystalline materials, such as quartz (SiO2), which is the best known natural piezoelectric material.
  - Piezoceramics, such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT), barium titanate, and lead titanate, which are the most commonly produced and widely used synthetic piezoelectric materials  .
  - Piezoelectric semiconductors, such as zinc oxide (ZnO2), which have a relatively wide band gap and can be used as piezoelectric sensors and actuators.
  - Piezopolymers, such as polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), which are flexible and can be fabricated into thin films and fibers.
  - Piezoelectric composites, which are combinations of piezoelectric materials and other materials, such as polymers, metals, or ceramics, to enhance the performance and functionality of the piezoelectric components.
  - Glass ceramics, such as lithium disilicate (Li2Si2O5) and barium titanate silicate (Ba2TiSiO6), which are transparent and have high piezoelectric coefficients.
- Piezoelectric materials have useful applications in many industries, such as  :
  - Energy harvesting, where piezoelectric materials can convert ambient vibrations, such as from human motion, wind, or traffic, into electrical energy for powering low-power devices, such as wireless sensors, microelectromechanical systems (MEMS), or wearable electronics.
  - Sensing and measurement, where piezoelectric materials can detect and measure various physical quantities, such as force, pressure, acceleration, strain, temperature, or sound, by generating electrical signals proportional to the applied mechanical stimuli.
  - Actuation and control, where piezoelectric materials can produce precise and rapid mechanical movements, such as displacement, vibration, or rotation, by applying electrical voltages across the piezoelectric elements. This can be used for applications such as microfluidics, optical devices, ultrasonic motors, or smart structures.
  - Medical imaging and therapy, where piezoelectric materials can generate and receive high-frequency ultrasound waves for imaging internal organs, tissues, or blood vessels, or for delivering therapeutic effects, such as tissue ablation, drug delivery, or gene transfection.
  - Acoustics and sound, where piezoelectric materials can act as speakers, microphones, or headphones, by converting electrical signals into sound waves, or vice versa. They can also be used for noise cancellation, sonar, or musical instruments.
