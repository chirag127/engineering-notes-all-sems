### Case studies for Implanted medical devices

- Implantable medical devices (IMDs) are electronic devices that are inserted into the human body for various purposes, such as monitoring, diagnosis, therapy, or enhancement.
- IMDs require a reliable and long-lasting power source to function properly. However, conventional batteries have limitations such as limited lifespan, toxicity, infection risk, and need for replacement surgery.
- Energy harvesting is an alternative approach that can provide continuous and sustainable power for IMDs by converting ambient energy sources into electrical energy. Some examples of ambient energy sources are mechanical, thermal, and chemical energy from the human body.
- Some case studies of energy harvesting for IMDs are:

  - **Piezoelectric energy harvesting from heartbeat**: Piezoelectric materials can generate electric voltage when subjected to mechanical stress or strain. A piezoelectric energy harvester can be attached to the heart or the aorta to harvest energy from the heartbeat motion. This energy can be used to power cardiac pacemakers, defibrillators, or other cardiac devices .
  - **Thermoelectric energy harvesting from body heat**: Thermoelectric materials can generate electric voltage when exposed to a temperature gradient. A thermoelectric energy harvester can be implanted under the skin to harvest energy from the body heat. This energy can be used to power sensors, drug delivery systems, or other medical devices .
  - **Biofuel cell energy harvesting from glucose**: Biofuel cells can generate electric current from the oxidation of glucose and the reduction of oxygen in the biological fluids. A biofuel cell energy harvester can be implanted in the blood vessels or the interstitial fluid to harvest energy from the glucose metabolism. This energy can be used to power biosensors, insulin pumps, or other medical devices .

- Energy harvesting for IMDs has several advantages, such as:

  - Eliminating or reducing the need for battery replacement and recharging, which can improve the safety, convenience, and cost-effectiveness of IMDs.
  - Enabling the development of smaller, lighter, and more functional IMDs, which can improve the comfort, performance, and quality of life of the patients.
  - Expanding the range of applications and functionalities of IMDs, such as wireless communication, data transmission, and remote control.

- Energy harvesting for IMDs also faces some challenges, such as:

  - Achieving sufficient and stable power output to meet the demand of IMDs, which can vary depending on the type, location, and function of the device.
  - Ensuring the biocompatibility, durability, and reliability of the energy harvesters and the IMDs, which can be affected by the harsh and dynamic environment of the human body.
  - Addressing the ethical, social, and regulatory issues related to the implantation, operation, and removal of the energy harvesters and the IMDs, which can involve the consent, privacy, and security of the patients.
