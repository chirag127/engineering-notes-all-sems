# Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS

## Introduction

- Energy harvesting wireless sensors (EHWS) are devices that can collect and convert ambient energy sources into electrical energy to power themselves and communicate with other nodes.
- EHWS can eliminate the need for batteries and wires, which can reduce maintenance costs, environmental impact and installation complexity .
- EHWS can enable new applications and scenarios for the Internet of Things (IoT), such as smart buildings, industrial automation, environmental monitoring and healthcare  .

## Energy Sources and Harvesting Techniques

- The main ambient energy sources that can be harvested by EHWS are solar, thermal, kinetic, radio frequency and vibration .
- Solar energy harvesting uses photovoltaic cells to convert light into electricity. It can provide high power density, but it depends on the availability and intensity of light .
- Thermal energy harvesting uses thermoelectric generators to convert heat gradients into electricity. It can exploit waste heat from industrial processes, human body or natural sources .
- Kinetic energy harvesting uses piezoelectric, electromagnetic or electrostatic transducers to convert mechanical motion into electricity. It can capture energy from human activities, wind, water or machines .
- Radio frequency energy harvesting uses antennas to capture electromagnetic waves from wireless transmissions or ambient sources. It can provide continuous and ubiquitous power, but it has low power density and efficiency .
- Vibration energy harvesting uses piezoelectric, electromagnetic or electrostatic transducers to convert mechanical vibrations into electricity. It can exploit vibrations from structures, machines or vehicles .

## Energy Storage and Management

- EHWS need to store the harvested energy in order to use it when needed and to smooth out the fluctuations and intermittency of the energy sources .
- The main energy storage devices for EHWS are capacitors and batteries. Capacitors can provide high power density, fast charging and discharging, and long lifetime, but they have low energy density and high leakage. Batteries can provide high energy density, low leakage and stable voltage, but they have low power density, slow charging and discharging, and limited lifetime .
- EHWS need to manage the energy flow between the harvesting, storage and consumption components in order to optimize the performance and lifetime of the system .
- The main energy management techniques for EHWS are maximum power point tracking (MPPT), power conditioning, voltage regulation and power scheduling .
- MPPT is a technique that adjusts the input impedance of the harvesting circuit to match the output impedance of the energy source, in order to maximize the power transfer .
- Power conditioning is a technique that converts the harvested energy into a suitable form for the storage and consumption components, such as AC to DC, DC to DC or DC to AC .
- Voltage regulation is a technique that maintains a constant and stable voltage level for the consumption component, regardless of the variations and fluctuations of the energy source and storage .
- Power scheduling is a technique that adapts the power consumption of the sensor node to the available energy, by adjusting the duty cycle, sampling rate, transmission power or data rate .

## Wireless Communication and Networking

- EHWS need to communicate with other nodes or a base station in order to transmit the sensed data or receive commands or queries .
- The main wireless communication technologies for EHWS are radio frequency, infrared, optical and acoustic .
- Radio frequency communication uses electromagnetic waves to transmit data wirelessly. It can provide long range, high data rate and low power consumption, but it is susceptible to interference, attenuation and multipath fading .
- Infrared communication uses infrared light to transmit data wirelessly. It can provide high data rate, low power consumption and high security, but it requires line-of-sight, has short range and is affected by ambient light .
