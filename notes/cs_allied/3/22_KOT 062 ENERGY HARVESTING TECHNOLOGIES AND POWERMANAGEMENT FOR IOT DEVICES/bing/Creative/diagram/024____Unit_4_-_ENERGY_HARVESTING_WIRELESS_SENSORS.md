## Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS

- Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can collect and convert ambient energy from their environment into electrical energy for powering themselves and transmitting data wirelessly  .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors have several advantages over battery-powered sensors, such as:
  - They are maintenance-free, flexible and inexpensive to install  .
  - They can be placed practically anywhere, even in hard-to-reach or hazardous locations .
  - They can extend the lifetime and reliability of wireless sensor networks (WSNs) by avoiding battery depletion and replacement .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can use different types of ambient energy sources, such as:
  - Solar energy: the most abundant and widely available source of energy, which can be harvested by photovoltaic cells .
  - Thermal energy: the difference in temperature between two objects or environments, which can be harvested by thermoelectric generators .
  - Kinetic energy: the movement or vibration of objects or fluids, which can be harvested by piezoelectric, electromagnetic or electrostatic generators .
  - Radio frequency (RF) energy: the electromagnetic waves emitted by wireless devices or transmitters, which can be harvested by RF antennas or rectennas .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can be classified into two categories based on their energy storage unit:
  - Rechargeable sensors: sensors that have a rechargeable battery or capacitor that can store the harvested energy and provide a stable power supply to the sensor and the transmitter.
  - Transient sensors: sensors that have no energy storage unit and operate only when the harvested energy is sufficient to power the sensor and the transmitter.
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can be used for various applications in different domains, such as:
  - Smart buildings: sensors that can monitor and control temperature, humidity, lighting, occupancy, air quality, fire, security, etc. in buildings  .
  - Industrial IoT: sensors that can monitor and optimize the performance, efficiency, safety and quality of industrial processes, machines, equipment, etc. in factories, warehouses, mines, etc.  .
  - Environmental monitoring: sensors that can measure and report the parameters, conditions and events of natural phenomena, such as weather, climate, soil, water, air, wildlife, etc.  .
  - Healthcare: sensors that can monitor and track the vital signs, activities, behaviors and locations of patients, elderly, disabled, etc.  .
  - Smart cities: sensors that can improve the management, planning and operation of urban services, such as transportation, energy, water, waste, etc.  .
