### Case Studies for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Energy Harvesting Wireless Sensors in the Subject of KOT 062 Energy Harvesting Technologies and Power Management for IoT Devices KCS

- Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can collect ambient energy from sources such as vibration, light, radio frequency, and temperature gradients, and use it to power wireless communication and sensing functions.
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors have many advantages over battery-powered sensors, such as longer lifetime, lower maintenance cost, higher reliability, and environmental friendliness.
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors have many applications in various domains, such as industrial, environmental, medical, and smart city.
- Here are some case studies of energy harvesting wireless sensors in different scenarios:

  - **Using energy harvesting, radio technologies to power wireless sensors in industrial environments**
    - In this case study, a company called Everactive used energy harvesting and ultra-low-power radio technologies to power wireless sensors for industrial internet of things (IIoT) applications, such as steam trap monitoring, machine health monitoring, and flare stack monitoring.
    - The wireless sensors can harvest energy from sources such as vibration, thermal gradients, and indoor light, and use it to transmit data to a cloud-based platform for analysis and visualization.
    - The wireless sensors can operate continuously without batteries, reducing the need for frequent maintenance and replacement, and improving the safety and efficiency of industrial operations.
  - **A study on piezoelectric energy-harvesting wireless sensor networks deployed in a weak vibration environment**
    - In this case study, a research team from Korea developed a piezoelectric energy-harvesting wireless sensor network (PEH-WSN) for monitoring the structural health of a bridge.
    - The PEH-WSN consisted of sensor nodes that can harvest energy from the weak vibration of the bridge, and a base station that can collect and process the data from the sensor nodes.
    - The PEH-WSN was able to detect the damage and deterioration of the bridge by analyzing the changes in the vibration signals, and provide real-time information for maintenance and repair.
  - **Energy harvesting in wireless sensor network for smart agriculture**
    - In this case study, a company called Utthunga Technologies used energy harvesting wireless sensors to implement a smart agriculture system for monitoring and controlling the irrigation and fertigation of crops.
    - The wireless sensors can harvest energy from solar panels, and use it to measure parameters such as soil moisture, temperature, humidity, and pH, and send the data to a gateway device.
    - The gateway device can then control the irrigation and fertigation valves based on the data and the predefined rules, and also send the data to a cloud server for further analysis and visualization.
    - The wireless sensors can operate autonomously without batteries, reducing the operational cost and environmental impact of the system.
