### Harvesting for RF Sensors and ID Tags

- RF sensors and ID tags are devices that use radio frequency (RF) signals to communicate data, such as identification, location, temperature, humidity, etc.
- RF sensors and ID tags can be classified into passive, semi-passive, and active, depending on their power source and transmission mode.
- Passive RF sensors and ID tags do not have a battery and rely on the RF reader signal to deliver energy and power up their circuitry . They can only transmit data when they are in the reader's field.
- Semi-passive RF sensors and ID tags have a battery that powers the sensor and the logic circuit, but not the RF transmission. They use the RF reader signal to modulate and reflect back their data . They can transmit data even when they are not in the reader's field, but they have a limited battery life.
- Active RF sensors and ID tags have a battery that powers the sensor, the logic circuit, and the RF transmission. They can transmit data continuously and over longer distances, but they have a higher power consumption and a shorter battery life .
- Energy harvesting is a technique that enables RF sensors and ID tags to collect energy from the environment, such as RF, motion, heat, light, etc., and store it in a capacitor or a battery for later use .
- Energy harvesting can extend the battery life of semi-passive and active RF sensors and ID tags, or even eliminate the need for a battery in passive RF sensors and ID tags .
- Energy harvesting can also enable new applications and functionalities for RF sensors and ID tags, such as sensing, computation, encryption, authentication, etc .
- Energy harvesting can be performed using different methods and devices, such as antennas, rectifiers, piezoelectric materials, thermoelectric materials, solar cells, etc .
- Energy harvesting for RF sensors and ID tags faces several challenges, such as low power density, low conversion efficiency, mismatched impedance, variable load, etc .
