### Piezoelectric materials

- Piezoelectric materials are materials that can convert mechanical stress or strain into electrical charge or voltage, and vice versa. This phenomenon is called the piezoelectric effect.
- Piezoelectric materials can be broadly classified into the following categories :
  - Crystalline materials, such as quartz (SiO2), which are natural and have a fixed and ordered structure of atoms or molecules.
  - Ceramic materials, such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT), which are synthetic and have a polycrystalline structure of grains with random orientations.
  - Polymeric materials, such as polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), which are synthetic and have a flexible and chain-like structure of molecules.
  - Piezoelectric semiconductors, such as zinc oxide (ZnO), which are synthetic and have a narrow band gap and high electrical conductivity.
  - Piezoelectric composites, which are combinations of two or more piezoelectric materials with different properties and shapes.
  - Glass ceramics, such as lithium disilicate (Li2Si2O5) and barium titanate silicate (Ba2TiSiO6), which are synthetic and have a glassy and crystalline structure of atoms or molecules.
- Piezoelectric materials have useful applications in many industries, such as :
  - Sensors and actuators, which can measure or generate force, pressure, acceleration, vibration, sound, etc.
  - Energy harvesting, which can convert ambient mechanical energy into electrical energy for powering low-power devices.
  - Medical imaging and treatment, which can produce or detect ultrasound waves for diagnosis or therapy.
  - Sonochemistry and industrial processing, which can enhance chemical reactions or material properties by using ultrasound waves.
  - Tilt sensing, which can detect the orientation or inclination of an object or a surface.
  - Power monitoring, which can measure the voltage or current of high power applications.
  - Microbalances, which can weigh very small amounts of chemical or biological substances.
  - Strain gauges, which can measure the deformation or strain of a material or a structure.
