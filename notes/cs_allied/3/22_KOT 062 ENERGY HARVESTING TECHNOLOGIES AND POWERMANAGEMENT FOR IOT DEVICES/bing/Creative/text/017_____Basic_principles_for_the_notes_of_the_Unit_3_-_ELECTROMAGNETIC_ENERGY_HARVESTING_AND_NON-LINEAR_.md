### Basic principles for the notes of the Unit 3 - ELECTROMAGNETIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND NON-LINEAR TECHNIQUES in the subject of KOT 062 ENERGY HARVESTING TECHNOLOGIES AND POWERMANAGEMENT FOR IOT DEVICES KCS

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is the process of converting ambient electromagnetic radiation into electrical energy for powering wireless devices or charging batteries .
- Electromagnetic energy harvesting can be classified into two types: near-field and far-field. Near-field harvesting involves capturing the electromagnetic fields generated by nearby sources such as power lines, transformers, or wireless chargers. Far-field harvesting involves capturing the electromagnetic waves emitted by distant sources such as radio, TV, or cellular towers .
- Electromagnetic energy harvesting devices typically consist of three main components: an antenna, a rectifier, and a power management circuit. The antenna captures the electromagnetic waves and converts them into alternating current (AC). The rectifier converts the AC into direct current (DC). The power management circuit regulates the DC voltage and current and delivers it to the load or the battery .
- Non-linear techniques are methods that exploit the non-linear behavior of certain components or systems to enhance the performance of electromagnetic energy harvesting devices. Non-linear techniques can be applied to the antenna, the rectifier, or the power management circuit  .
- Non-linear techniques can improve the bandwidth, efficiency, power density, and adaptability of electromagnetic energy harvesting devices. For example, non-linear antennas can harvest energy from multiple frequency bands or tune to the optimal frequency. Non-linear rectifiers can increase the rectification efficiency or output voltage. Non-linear power management circuits can optimize the impedance matching or voltage conversion  .
- Some examples of non-linear techniques are:
  - Magnetic levitation: using magnets to suspend a coil or a magnet in mid-air and induce relative motion between them by external vibrations or rotations. This creates a variable magnetic flux that generates AC voltage in the coil .
  - Frequency up-conversion: using a switch or a diode to modulate the input AC signal at a higher frequency and then rectify it. This increases the output voltage and reduces the losses due to parasitic capacitance.
  - Synchronized switching: using a switch or a comparator to control the charging and discharging of a capacitor in parallel with the load. This maximizes the power transfer from the source to the load by synchronizing the switching with the input signal.
