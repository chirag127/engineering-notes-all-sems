### Powering Wireless SHM Sensor Nodes through Energy Harvesting

- Wireless sensor nodes and sensor networks are widely used for structural health monitoring (SHM) applications, such as detecting damage, cracks, corrosion, or deformation in buildings, bridges, pipelines, aircraft, etc.
- However, one of the main challenges in wireless SHM systems is how to power the sensor nodes, which typically consist of sensors, microcontrollers, memory, and wireless transceivers, and have limited battery life.
- Energy harvesting is a promising technique to provide continuous and sustainable power to wireless SHM sensor nodes by converting ambient energy sources, such as solar, thermal, vibration, wind, or electromagnetic radiation, into electrical energy.
- Energy harvesting can extend the lifetime of wireless SHM sensor nodes, reduce the maintenance cost and environmental impact of battery replacement, and enable the deployment of wireless SHM systems in remote or inaccessible locations.
- Some of the energy harvesting methods for wireless SHM sensor nodes are:

  - Solar energy harvesting: using photovoltaic cells to convert sunlight into electricity. Solar energy harvesting is suitable for outdoor SHM applications where there is sufficient solar irradiance. However, solar energy harvesting is dependent on the weather and diurnal cycles, and may require energy storage devices to provide power during the night or cloudy days.
  - Thermal energy harvesting: using thermoelectric generators to convert temperature gradients or heat fluxes into electricity. Thermal energy harvesting can exploit the waste heat from industrial processes, machinery, or human activities, or the natural temperature differences between the environment and the structure. Thermal energy harvesting is independent of the time of day, but may require thermal insulation or heat sinks to maintain the temperature gradient.
  - Vibration energy harvesting: using piezoelectric, electromagnetic, or electrostatic transducers to convert mechanical vibrations or strain into electricity. Vibration energy harvesting can utilize the ambient vibrations from traffic, wind, machinery, or human activities, or the structural vibrations induced by the SHM sensors themselves. Vibration energy harvesting is dependent on the frequency and amplitude of the vibrations, and may require tuning or frequency conversion mechanisms to match the resonance frequency of the transducer.
  - Wind energy harvesting: using micro wind turbines to convert wind flow into electricity. Wind energy harvesting can be applied to SHM applications where there is sufficient wind speed and direction, such as bridges, towers, or offshore structures. However, wind energy harvesting is dependent on the weather and seasonal variations, and may require energy storage devices to provide power during calm periods.
  - Electromagnetic energy harvesting: using antennas or rectennas to convert electromagnetic radiation, such as radio waves, microwaves, or light, into electricity. Electromagnetic energy harvesting can exploit the ambient electromagnetic fields from wireless communication, broadcasting, or power transmission systems, or the dedicated electromagnetic fields generated by wireless energy transmission systems. Electromagnetic energy harvesting is independent of the time of day, but may require impedance matching or rectification circuits to optimize the power conversion efficiency.
