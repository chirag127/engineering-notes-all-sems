## Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

- Energy harvesting systems are devices or systems that are used to convert ambient energy, which is derived from external sources existing in the atmosphere, into electrical energy.
- Energy harvesting systems are attractive alternatives to battery-operated systems, especially for long-term, low-power, and self-sustaining electronic systems.
- Energy harvesting systems can utilize various sources of ambient energy, such as mechanical load, vibrations, temperature gradients, light, radio frequency, etc. to generate power in the nW-mW range .
- Energy harvesting systems consist of four main components: energy transducer, energy storage, power management, and load.
- Energy transducer is the component that converts the ambient energy into electrical energy. The type of transducer depends on the type of energy source. For example, piezoelectric, electromagnetic, and electrostatic transducers can be used for vibration energy harvesting; thermoelectric and pyroelectric transducers can be used for thermal energy harvesting; photovoltaic and luminescent transducers can be used for light energy harvesting; and antenna and rectifier can be used for radio frequency energy harvesting.
- Energy storage is the component that stores the electrical energy generated by the transducer for later use. The type of storage depends on the power and energy requirements of the load. For example, capacitors, batteries, and supercapacitors can be used as energy storage devices.
- Power management is the component that controls and regulates the flow of energy between the transducer, the storage, and the load. The power management circuit can perform functions such as voltage conversion, impedance matching, maximum power point tracking, power conditioning, and power monitoring.
- Load is the component that consumes the electrical energy provided by the energy harvesting system. The load can be any electronic device that requires low and intermittent power, such as sensors, microcontrollers, wireless transmitters, etc.
- Energy harvesting systems can be classified into different categories based on the type of energy source, the type of transducer, the type of application, the type of architecture, etc.
- Energy harvesting systems have various applications in different domains, such as biomedical, environmental, industrial, military, consumer, etc . Some examples of energy harvesting systems are:

  - Body sensor networks that harvest energy from body heat, motion, or blood pressure to monitor health parameters.
  - Oceanographic monitoring sensors that harvest energy from ocean waves, tides, or currents to measure water quality, temperature, salinity, etc.
  - Wireless sensor networks that harvest energy from solar, wind, or vibration to monitor physical or environmental conditions, such as temperature, humidity, pressure, etc.
  - Smart buildings that harvest energy from light, heat, or motion to control lighting, heating, ventilation, air conditioning, etc.
  - Radio frequency identification (RFID) tags that harvest energy from radio waves to transmit information about the identity, location, or status of an object.
  - Levitation based energy harvesters that harvest energy from vibrations using a levitating magnet in a closed container.
