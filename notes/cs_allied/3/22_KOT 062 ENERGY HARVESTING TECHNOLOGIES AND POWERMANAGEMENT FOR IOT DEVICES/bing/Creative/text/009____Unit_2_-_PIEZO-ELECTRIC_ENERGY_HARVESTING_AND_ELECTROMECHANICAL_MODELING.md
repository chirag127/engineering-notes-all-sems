## Unit 2 - Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting and Electromechanical Modeling

- Piezoelectric energy harvesting is the process of converting mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using piezoelectric materials, which are materials that generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or strain.
- Piezoelectric energy harvesters are devices that utilize piezoelectric materials to scavenge energy from ambient vibrations, such as those caused by wind, traffic, human motion, etc. They can be used to power low-power electronic devices, such as wireless sensors, microcontrollers, or LEDs, without the need for batteries or wires.
- Electromechanical modeling of piezoelectric energy harvesters is the mathematical description of the coupled dynamics of the mechanical and electrical domains of the system, taking into account the geometry, material properties, boundary conditions, and excitation sources of the harvester.
- Electromechanical modeling can help to understand the behavior, performance, and optimization of piezoelectric energy harvesters, as well as to design and test new configurations and applications of the technology.
- There are different approaches to electromechanical modeling of piezoelectric energy harvesters, depending on the level of detail, complexity, and accuracy required. Some of the common methods are:
  - Lumped-parameter modeling: This method simplifies the harvester as a mass-spring-damper system with a piezoelectric element, and uses ordinary differential equations to describe the motion and voltage of the system. This method is easy to implement and analyze, but it may not capture the effects of higher modes, distributed parameters, or complex geometries of the harvester.
  - Distributed-parameter modeling: This method considers the harvester as a continuous system with spatially varying parameters, and uses partial differential equations to describe the deformation and electric potential of the system. This method can account for the effects of higher modes, distributed parameters, and complex geometries of the harvester, but it may be more difficult to solve and require more computational resources.
  - Finite element modeling: This method discretizes the harvester into a finite number of elements, and uses numerical methods to solve the governing equations of each element. This method can handle arbitrary geometries, material properties, and boundary conditions of the harvester, but it may introduce numerical errors and require more computational resources.
- Electromechanical modeling of piezoelectric energy harvesters can also incorporate different forms of excitation, such as base excitation, tip excitation, airflow excitation, or moving load excitation, depending on the source and nature of the ambient vibrations. Each form of excitation may have different effects on the response and output of the harvester, and may require different modeling techniques and assumptions.
