### Generation of electric power in semiconductor PV cells

- PV cells or photovoltaic cells are devices that convert light energy into electrical energy using the photovoltaic effect .
- The photovoltaic effect is the phenomenon of generating electric voltage or current in a material when it is exposed to light .
- The PV cell is composed of semiconductor material, such as silicon, which can conduct electricity better than an insulator but not as well as a metal .
- The semiconductor material is doped with impurities to create two layers: the n-type layer, which has excess electrons, and the p-type layer, which has excess holes .
- The junction between the n-type and p-type layers is called the p-n junction, which forms an electric field that prevents the electrons and holes from recombining .
- When light photons with enough energy strike the p-n junction, they can excite electrons from the valence band to the conduction band, creating electron-hole pairs .
- The electric field at the p-n junction separates the electrons and holes, pushing the electrons to the n-type layer and the holes to the p-type layer .
- The electrons and holes can then flow through external circuits as electric current, producing power .
- The power generated by a single PV cell is typically only about two watts, so multiple cells are connected in series or parallel to form PV modules or panels that can produce more power .
- The PV modules or panels can be further connected to form PV arrays or systems that can supply power to various applications, such as homes, buildings, or grids .
