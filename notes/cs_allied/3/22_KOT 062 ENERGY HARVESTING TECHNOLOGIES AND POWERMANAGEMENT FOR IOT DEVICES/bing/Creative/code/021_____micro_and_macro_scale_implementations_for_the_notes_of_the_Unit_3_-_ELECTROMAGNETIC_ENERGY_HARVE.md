### Micro and Macro Scale Implementations for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Electromagnetic Energy Harvesting and Non-Linear Techniques

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting is the process of converting ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using electromagnetic transducers.
- Electromagnetic energy harvesters can be classified into two categories based on their size: micro and macro scale.
- Micro scale electromagnetic energy harvesters are typically fabricated using microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) technology and have dimensions in the order of micrometers to millimeters. They can scavenge energy from micro-scale vibrations such as airflow, acoustic noise, or human motion.
- Macro scale electromagnetic energy harvesters are usually composed of discrete coils and magnets and have dimensions in the order of centimeters to meters. They can scavenge energy from macro-scale vibrations such as machinery, vehicles, or bridges.
- The basic principle of electromagnetic energy harvesting is the same for both micro and macro scale devices: a moving magnet induces a voltage in a coil according to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.
- The main difference between micro and macro scale electromagnetic energy harvesters is the design and optimization of the transducer, which depends on the vibration characteristics, the output power requirements, and the fabrication constraints.
- Some of the design parameters that affect the performance of electromagnetic energy harvesters are: the number, shape, and size of the magnets and coils, the relative position and orientation of the magnets and coils, the magnetic flux density, the coil resistance and inductance, the mechanical damping and stiffness, and the electrical load .
- The main challenges of micro scale electromagnetic energy harvesting are: the low electromagnetic coupling due to the small size of the magnets and coils, the high parasitic damping due to the air drag and friction, the low output voltage and power due to the low magnetic flux density and coil turns, and the fabrication limitations due to the MEMS technology .
- The main challenges of macro scale electromagnetic energy harvesting are: the large size and weight of the magnets and coils, the high cost and complexity of the fabrication, the nonlinear behavior and instability of the system due to the large displacement and magnetic forces, and the environmental and safety issues due to the strong magnetic fields .
- Some of the techniques that can improve the performance of electromagnetic energy harvesters are: using non-linear springs or magnets to increase the bandwidth and output power, using resonant or non-resonant structures to match the vibration frequency and mode, using hybrid or multi-modal transducers to scavenge energy from multiple sources or directions, and using power management circuits to optimize the electrical load and increase the efficiency  .
