### Types of Energy Harvesting Systems

Energy harvesting systems are devices or systems that are used to convert ambient energy, which is derived from external resources existing in the atmosphere, into electrical energy. Energy harvesting systems can be classified into different types based on the source of ambient energy, such as:

- **Solar energy harvesting systems**: These systems use photovoltaic cells to capture the solar radiation and convert it into electrical energy. Solar energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as wireless sensor networks, wearable devices, and remote monitoring systems.  
- **Thermal energy harvesting systems**: These systems use thermoelectric generators to convert the temperature difference between two materials or surfaces into electrical energy. Thermal energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as waste heat recovery, body heat harvesting, and environmental monitoring.  
- **Vibration energy harvesting systems**: These systems use piezoelectric, electrostatic, or electromagnetic transducers to convert the mechanical vibrations or motions into electrical energy. Vibration energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as structural health monitoring, industrial machinery, and human motion harvesting.  
- **Radio frequency energy harvesting systems**: These systems use antennas to capture the electromagnetic waves from the radio frequency spectrum and convert them into electrical energy. Radio frequency energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as wireless power transfer, RFID tags, and wireless communication.  
- **Wind energy harvesting systems**: These systems use micro-turbines or micro-generators to convert the kinetic energy of the wind into electrical energy. Wind energy harvesting systems can be used for applications such as remote sensing, weather stations, and outdoor lighting.  
- **Other types of energy harvesting systems**: These systems use other sources of ambient energy, such as acoustic noise, ocean waves, human breath, or blood pressure, to generate electrical energy. These systems can be used for applications such as biomedical implants, acoustic sensors, or underwater devices.
