### Powering Wireless SHM Sensor Nodes through Energy Harvesting

- Wireless sensor nodes (WSNs) are devices that can sense, process, and communicate data wirelessly.
- Structural health monitoring (SHM) is an application of WSNs that aims to monitor the condition and performance of structures such as bridges, buildings, pipelines, etc.
- One of the main challenges of WSNs for SHM is the power supply, as batteries have limited lifetime and may be difficult to replace or recharge in harsh or inaccessible environments.
- Energy harvesting is a technique that can provide a sustainable and autonomous power source for WSNs by converting ambient energy sources such as solar, thermal, vibration, wind, etc. into electrical energy.
- Energy harvesting can extend the lifetime of WSNs, reduce maintenance costs, and enable new applications that require continuous or long-term monitoring.
- Some of the factors that affect the design and performance of energy harvesting systems for WSNs are:
  - The type and availability of the ambient energy source
  - The power consumption and duty cycle of the sensor node
  - The energy conversion efficiency and output power of the energy harvester
  - The energy storage and management components and strategies
- Some of the examples of energy harvesting systems for WSNs for SHM are:
  - Solar cells that can harvest solar radiation and provide power for outdoor or indoor applications   
  - Piezoelectric devices that can harvest mechanical vibrations from the structure or the environment  
  - Thermoelectric devices that can harvest thermal gradients or waste heat from the structure or the environment  
  - Microwave wireless energy transmission that can deliver energy to SHM sensor nodes remotely by using electromagnetic waves in the X-band
