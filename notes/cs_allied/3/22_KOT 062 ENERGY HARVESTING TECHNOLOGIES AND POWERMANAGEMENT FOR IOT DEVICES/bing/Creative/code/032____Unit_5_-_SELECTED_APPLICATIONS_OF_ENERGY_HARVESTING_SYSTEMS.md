## Unit 5 - SELECTED APPLICATIONS OF ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

Energy harvesting systems are devices that collect ambient energy from the environment and convert it into electrical energy for powering low-power electronic components. Energy harvesting systems can extend the battery life of remote sensors, reduce the maintenance cost of replacing batteries, and enable autonomous operation of wireless devices in inaccessible locations. Some of the common sources of ambient energy are solar, thermal, kinetic, and electromagnetic.

Some of the selected applications of energy harvesting systems are:

- **Industrial applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for remote monitoring, predictive maintenance, and energy-efficient solutions in various industrial sectors, such as manufacturing, oil and gas, mining, and agriculture. For example, energy harvesting systems can power wireless sensors that measure temperature, pressure, vibration, humidity, and gas levels in industrial equipment and pipelines, and transmit the data to a central control unit for analysis and action.
- **Consumer electronics applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for powering wearable devices, smart watches, fitness trackers, and other portable electronic devices that require low power consumption and long battery life. For example, energy harvesting systems can use body heat, motion, or light to generate electricity for charging the batteries of these devices.
- **Building and home automation applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for controlling lighting, heating, ventilation, air conditioning, security, and entertainment systems in buildings and homes. For example, energy harvesting systems can use light, temperature, or motion to power wireless switches, sensors, and actuators that communicate with a central hub or a smart phone for adjusting the settings of these systems.
- **Wireless sensor network applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for powering wireless sensor nodes that collect and transmit data for various purposes, such as environmental monitoring, health care, smart cities, and military. For example, energy harvesting systems can use solar, wind, or water energy to power wireless sensors that monitor air quality, water quality, soil moisture, wildlife, traffic, or battlefield conditions .
- **Security system applications**: Energy harvesting systems can be used for powering security devices, such as cameras, alarms, locks, and biometric scanners, that require reliable and continuous operation. For example, energy harvesting systems can use light, vibration, or sound to power security devices that detect intruders, fire, smoke, or gas leaks, and alert the authorities or the owners.
