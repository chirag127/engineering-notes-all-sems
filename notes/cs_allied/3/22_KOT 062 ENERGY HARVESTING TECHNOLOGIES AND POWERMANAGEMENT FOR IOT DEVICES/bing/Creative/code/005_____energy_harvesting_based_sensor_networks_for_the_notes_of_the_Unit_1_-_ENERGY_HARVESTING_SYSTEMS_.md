### Energy Harvesting Based Sensor Networks

- Energy harvesting based sensor networks are wireless sensor networks that use ambient energy sources such as solar, wind, vibration, thermal, etc. to power the sensor nodes and extend their lifetime .
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks have several advantages over battery-powered sensor networks, such as:
  - They can reduce the maintenance cost and environmental impact of replacing batteries.
  - They can enable the deployment of sensor networks in remote, inaccessible, or hazardous locations.
  - They can improve the reliability and availability of sensor networks by avoiding battery failures or depletion.
  - They can support higher data rates and more complex functionalities by providing more energy to the sensor nodes.
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks have several challenges, such as:
  - The energy sources are often intermittent, unpredictable, and variable, which requires efficient energy management and storage techniques.
  - The energy harvesting devices may have trade-offs between size, weight, cost, and efficiency, which may limit the amount of energy that can be harvested.
  - The energy harvesting devices may introduce additional noise, interference, or distortion to the sensor signals, which may affect the data quality and accuracy.
  - The energy harvesting devices may require additional circuitry, such as power converters, regulators, or controllers, which may increase the complexity and power consumption of the sensor nodes.
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks require novel design and optimization methods that consider the characteristics and constraints of the energy sources, the energy harvesting devices, the energy storage devices, the sensor nodes, and the network protocols  .
- Energy harvesting based sensor networks have many potential applications in various domains, such as environmental monitoring, smart buildings, industrial automation, healthcare, agriculture, etc.
