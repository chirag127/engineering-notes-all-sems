### Power generation for the notes of the Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS in the subject of KOT 062 ENERGY HARVESTING TECHNOLOGIES AND POWERMANAGEMENT FOR IOT DEVICES KCS

- Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can capture ambient energy from their surroundings and convert it into usable electrical power for wireless communication and sensing applications .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can offer advantages such as reduced maintenance costs, increased reliability, extended lifetime, and environmental sustainability compared to battery-powered sensors .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can utilize various forms of ambient energy sources, such as solar, thermal, kinetic, radio frequency, and piezoelectric  .
- Solar energy harvesting is the most widely used technique for wireless sensors, as it can provide high power density and availability in outdoor environments. Solar energy harvesting devices typically consist of photovoltaic cells, power management circuits, and energy storage elements.
- Thermal energy harvesting exploits the temperature difference between two points or surfaces to generate electrical power. Thermal energy harvesting devices can use thermoelectric, thermionic, or thermophotovoltaic effects to convert heat into electricity .
- Kinetic energy harvesting converts the mechanical motion or vibration of an object into electrical power. Kinetic energy harvesting devices can use electromagnetic, electrostatic, or piezoelectric transducers to capture the kinetic energy .
- Radio frequency energy harvesting collects the electromagnetic waves emitted by wireless communication systems or other sources and converts them into electrical power. Radio frequency energy harvesting devices can use antennas, rectifiers, and matching networks to capture and convert the radio frequency signals .
- Piezoelectric energy harvesting utilizes the property of certain materials that generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or strain. Piezoelectric energy harvesting devices can use piezoelectric materials, such as ceramics, polymers, or composites, to harvest the energy from vibrations, impacts, or acoustic waves  .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors require efficient power management techniques to optimize the energy conversion, storage, and utilization processes. Power management techniques can include maximum power point tracking, voltage regulation, power switching, and adaptive duty cycling .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can enable various applications in the fields of industrial, environmental, biomedical, and smart city monitoring, such as condition monitoring, asset tracking, wearable health monitoring, and smart lighting   .
