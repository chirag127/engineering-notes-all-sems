### Harvesters

- Harvesters are devices that convert ambient energy sources, such as mechanical vibrations, into electrical energy.
- Piezoelectric harvesters are a type of harvester that use piezoelectric materials, which generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or strain.
- Piezoelectric harvesters can be used to power low-power electronic devices, such as sensors, wireless transmitters, or microcontrollers, for applications in various fields, such as aerospace, civil, biomedical, or environmental engineering.
- Piezoelectric harvesters can have different configurations, such as cantilever, beam, plate, or shell structures, with different types and locations of piezoelectric layers, such as unimorph, bimorph, or multimorph.
- Piezoelectric harvesters can be excited by different forms of mechanical energy, such as base vibrations, fluid flow, acoustic waves, or moving loads.

### Electromechanical Modeling

- Electromechanical modeling is the process of developing mathematical models that describe the coupled mechanical and electrical behavior of piezoelectric harvesters.
- Electromechanical modeling can be used to analyze the performance of piezoelectric harvesters, such as output voltage, power, or efficiency, under different operating conditions, such as frequency, amplitude, or load resistance.
- Electromechanical modeling can also be used to design and optimize piezoelectric harvesters, such as selecting the optimal geometry, material, or configuration, to maximize the energy conversion or adapt to the environment.
- Electromechanical modeling can be classified into two main approaches: lumped-parameter modeling and distributed-parameter modeling.
- Lumped-parameter modeling assumes that the piezoelectric harvester can be represented by a finite number of discrete elements, such as masses, springs, dampers, or capacitors, connected by ideal nodes. Lumped-parameter modeling is simple and intuitive, but may not capture the complex dynamics of the piezoelectric harvester, especially at higher frequencies or modes.
- Distributed-parameter modeling considers that the piezoelectric harvester is a continuous system, with spatially varying properties, such as displacement, stress, strain, or electric potential. Distributed-parameter modeling is more accurate and general, but may require more mathematical complexity and computational effort. Distributed-parameter modeling can be based on different methods, such as the Euler-Bernoulli beam theory, the Timoshenko beam theory, the Kirchhoff plate theory, or the Mindlin plate theory, depending on the assumptions and simplifications made on the piezoelectric harvester.
