### Unit 4 - ENERGY HARVESTING WIRELESS SENSORS

- Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can collect and convert ambient energy from their environment into electrical energy for powering themselves and transmitting data .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors can use various sources of energy, such as solar, thermal, kinetic, electromagnetic, or piezoelectric .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors have several advantages over battery-powered sensors, such as:
  - They are maintenance-free, flexible, and inexpensive to install .
  - They can operate in harsh or inaccessible environments where battery replacement is difficult or impossible .
  - They can reduce the environmental impact of battery disposal and recycling .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors have several challenges, such as:
  - They have to deal with intermittent and variable energy availability .
  - They have to balance the trade-off between energy consumption and data quality .
  - They have to cope with the complexity and diversity of energy harvesting technologies and applications .
- Energy harvesting wireless sensors have many potential applications in various domains, such as:
  - Smart buildings: They can monitor and control temperature, humidity, lighting, occupancy, fire, security, etc .
  - Industrial IoT: They can monitor and optimize machinery, production, logistics, safety, etc .
  - Healthcare: They can monitor and assist patients, elderly, disabled, etc .
  - Environmental: They can monitor and protect wildlife, agriculture, water, air, etc .
