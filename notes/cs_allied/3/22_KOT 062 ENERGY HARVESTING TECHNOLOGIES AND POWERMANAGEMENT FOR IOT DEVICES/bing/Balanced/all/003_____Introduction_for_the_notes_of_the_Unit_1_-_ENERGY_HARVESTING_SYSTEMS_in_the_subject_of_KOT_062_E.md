# Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS in the subject of KOT 062 ENERGY HARVESTING TECHNOLOGIES AND POWERMANAGEMENT FOR IOT DEVICES KCS

- Energy harvesting (EH) is the process of capturing and converting ambient energy from various sources such as light, heat, vibration, and radio frequency (RF) into electrical energy  .
- Energy harvesting systems (EHS) are devices or circuits that use EH techniques to power low-power electronic systems, such as sensors, microcontrollers, wireless transceivers, and actuators  .
- EHS can provide several benefits, such as:
  - Reducing or eliminating the need for batteries, which can be costly, bulky, toxic, and difficult to replace or recharge  .
  - Enabling long-term, autonomous, and self-sustaining operation of electronic systems, especially in remote, inaccessible, or harsh environments  .
  - Enhancing the reliability, performance, and functionality of electronic systems, by providing continuous or intermittent power supply and avoiding power interruptions or failures  .
- EHS can be classified into different types, based on the source of ambient energy, such as:
  - Solar EHS, which use photovoltaic cells to convert light energy into electrical energy  .
  - Thermal EHS, which use thermoelectric generators to convert heat gradients or fluctuations into electrical energy  .
  - Mechanical EHS, which use piezoelectric, electrostatic, or electromagnetic transducers to convert mechanical vibrations, motions, or forces into electrical energy  .
  - RF EHS, which use antennas or rectennas to convert RF signals or electromagnetic waves into electrical energy  .
- EHS can be applied to various domains and applications, such as:
  - Internet of Things (IoT) devices, which are networked devices that can sense, communicate, and interact with the physical world  .
  - Wireless sensor networks (WSNs), which are collections of distributed and autonomous sensors that can monitor and collect data from the environment  .
  - Body sensor networks (BSNs), which are WSNs that can measure and transmit physiological signals from the human body.
  - Biomedical implants, which are electronic devices that can be implanted into the human body to perform diagnostic or therapeutic functions.
  - Smart buildings, which are buildings that can optimize their energy consumption, comfort, and security by using sensors, actuators, and controllers  .
  - Smart grids, which are electrical grids that can integrate renewable energy sources, distributed generation, and demand response by using advanced metering, communication, and control technologies  .
