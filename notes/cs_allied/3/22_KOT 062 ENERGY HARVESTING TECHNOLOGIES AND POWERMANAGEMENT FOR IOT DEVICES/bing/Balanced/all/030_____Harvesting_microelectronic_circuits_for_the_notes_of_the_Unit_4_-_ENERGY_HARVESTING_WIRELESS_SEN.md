# Harvesting Microelectronic Circuits

- Harvesting microelectronic circuits are circuits that can convert ambient energy sources, such as light, heat, vibration, or radio waves, into electrical energy for powering low-power devices, such as wireless sensors, biomedical implants, or wearable electronics.
- Harvesting microelectronic circuits typically consist of three main components: a transducer, a power converter, and a power management unit .
- A transducer is a device that converts one form of energy into another. For example, a photovoltaic cell converts light into electricity, a thermoelectric generator converts heat into electricity, a piezoelectric element converts mechanical stress into electricity, or an antenna converts electromagnetic waves into electricity .
- A power converter is a circuit that adapts the output voltage and current of the transducer to the input requirements of the load or the storage element, such as a battery or a capacitor. Depending on the type and characteristics of the transducer, the power converter can be a rectifier, a voltage multiplier, a DC-DC converter, or a combination of them .
- A power management unit is a circuit that regulates the output voltage and current of the power converter to the optimal level for the load or the storage element, and also controls the charging and discharging cycles of the storage element. The power management unit can also include functions such as maximum power point tracking, power monitoring, and power switching .
- The design of harvesting microelectronic circuits poses several challenges, such as low input power, variable input voltage and frequency, high conversion efficiency, low quiescent power, and small size and weight  .
- The design of harvesting microelectronic circuits also requires a trade-off between performance, complexity, and cost, depending on the application and the available energy source  .
- Harvesting microelectronic circuits have many advantages, such as extending the battery life or eliminating the need for batteries, reducing the maintenance and replacement costs, enabling new applications and functionalities, and improving the reliability and sustainability of the devices   .
