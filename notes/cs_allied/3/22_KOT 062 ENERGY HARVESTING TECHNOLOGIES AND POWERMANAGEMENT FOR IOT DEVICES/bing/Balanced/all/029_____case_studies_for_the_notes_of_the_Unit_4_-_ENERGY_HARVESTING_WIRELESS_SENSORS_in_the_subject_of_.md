# Case Studies for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Energy Harvesting Wireless Sensors

Energy harvesting wireless sensors are devices that can collect ambient energy from various sources such as light, heat, vibration, radio frequency, etc. and convert it into electrical energy to power wireless sensor networks (WSNs). WSNs are networks of sensor nodes that can communicate wirelessly and perform sensing, processing, and transmitting functions. WSNs have many applications in intelligent environment-monitoring, industrial automation, smart cities, health care, and the Internet of Things (IoT). However, WSNs face the challenge of limited battery life and frequent maintenance. Energy harvesting can provide a solution to extend the lifetime and reduce the cost of WSNs by enabling self-powered and maintenance-free sensor nodes.

Some of the case studies of energy harvesting wireless sensors are:

- **Using energy harvesting, radio technologies to power wireless sensors for industrial applications**: This case study describes how energy harvesting and radio technologies can be used to power wireless sensors for industrial applications such as condition monitoring, predictive maintenance, asset tracking, and process optimization. The case study explains the advantages of energy harvesting, such as eliminating the need for batteries, wires, and maintenance, and reducing the environmental impact. The case study also discusses the challenges of energy harvesting, such as the variability and unpredictability of the energy sources, the trade-off between power consumption and performance, and the integration of energy harvesting and radio technologies. The case study presents some examples of energy harvesting sources, such as vibration, RF, and light, and some examples of radio technologies, such as Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), LoRa, and ZigBee.

- **A study on piezoelectric energy-harvesting wireless sensor networks deployed in a weak vibration environment**: This case study presents a piezoelectric energy-harvesting wireless sensor network (PEH-WSN) that can harvest energy from weak vibrations in the environment, such as those generated by human footsteps, wind, or traffic. The case study describes the design and implementation of the PEH-WSN, which consists of piezoelectric energy harvesters, energy storage units, sensor nodes, and a base station. The case study evaluates the performance of the PEH-WSN in terms of energy harvesting efficiency, power consumption, data transmission, and network lifetime. The case study also compares the PEH-WSN with a battery-powered WSN and shows that the PEH-WSN can achieve a longer lifetime and a higher reliability.

- **Energy prediction and energy management in kinetic energy-harvesting wireless sensor networks for industrial applications**: This case study proposes an energy prediction and energy management scheme for kinetic energy-harvesting wireless sensor networks (KEH-WSNs) that can harvest energy from the motion of machines or tools in industrial applications. The case study explains the importance of energy prediction and energy management for KEH-WSNs, which can improve the energy efficiency and the quality of service of the network. The case study introduces a novel energy prediction model based on machine learning, which can estimate the future energy availability of the KEH-WSNs. The case study also presents an energy management algorithm that can dynamically adjust the sampling rate and the transmission power of the sensor nodes according to the energy prediction and the application requirements. The case study evaluates the performance of the proposed scheme in terms of energy prediction accuracy, energy consumption, data delivery, and network lifetime. The case study demonstrates that the proposed scheme can significantly improve the performance of the KEH-WSNs compared to the existing schemes.

- **A comprehensive study of solar energy harvesting system in wireless sensor networks**: This case study provides a comprehensive overview of solar energy harvesting system in wireless sensor networks (SEH-WSNs), which can harvest energy from the sun and store it in batteries or supercapacitors. The case study reviews the state-of-the-art of SEH-WSNs, including the components, the architectures, the challenges, and the applications. The case study also discusses the design considerations and the optimization techniques for SEH-WSNs, such as the solar panel size, the battery capacity, the energy conversion efficiency, the power management, and the load balancing. The case study presents some examples of SEH-WSNs for various applications, such as environmental monitoring, smart agriculture, smart grid, and smart home. The case study concludes with some future research directions and open issues for SEH-WSNs.
