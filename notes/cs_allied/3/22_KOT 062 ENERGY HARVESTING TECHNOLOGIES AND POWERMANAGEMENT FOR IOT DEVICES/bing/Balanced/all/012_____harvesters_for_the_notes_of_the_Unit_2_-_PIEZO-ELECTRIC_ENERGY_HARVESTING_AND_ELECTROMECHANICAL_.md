# Harvesters

- Harvesters are devices that convert ambient energy sources, such as mechanical vibrations, into electrical energy.
- Piezoelectric harvesters are a type of harvester that use piezoelectric materials, which generate electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or strain.
- Piezoelectric harvesters can be used to power low-power electronic devices, such as sensors, wireless transmitters, or microcontrollers, for applications in various fields, such as aerospace, civil, biomedical, or environmental engineering.
- Piezoelectric harvesters can have different configurations, such as cantilever, beam, plate, or shell structures, with different types of piezoelectric materials, such as ceramics, polymers, or composites.
- Piezoelectric harvesters can be excited by different forms of mechanical energy, such as base vibrations, fluid flow, acoustic waves, or human motion.

# Electromechanical Modeling

- Electromechanical modeling is the process of developing mathematical models that describe the coupled mechanical and electrical behavior of piezoelectric harvesters.
- Electromechanical modeling can help to understand, analyze, design, and optimize piezoelectric harvesters for different applications and operating conditions.
- Electromechanical modeling can be performed at different levels of complexity and accuracy, depending on the assumptions and simplifications made.
- Electromechanical modeling can be classified into two main categories: lumped-parameter modeling and distributed-parameter modeling.

## Lumped-Parameter Modeling

- Lumped-parameter modeling is a type of modeling that treats the piezoelectric harvester as a system of discrete masses, springs, dampers, and electrical components, connected by ideal joints or wires.
- Lumped-parameter modeling is based on the application of Newton's laws of motion, Kirchhoff's laws of electric circuits, and the constitutive equations of piezoelectricity.
- Lumped-parameter modeling is relatively simple and fast, but it can only capture the global dynamics of the piezoelectric harvester, and it may neglect some important effects, such as shear deformation, rotary inertia, or higher-order modes.

## Distributed-Parameter Modeling

- Distributed-parameter modeling is a type of modeling that treats the piezoelectric harvester as a continuous medium, with spatially varying mechanical and electrical properties.
- Distributed-parameter modeling is based on the application of the principles of conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, the Maxwell's equations of electromagnetism, and the constitutive equations of piezoelectricity.
- Distributed-parameter modeling is more complex and computationally intensive, but it can capture the local dynamics of the piezoelectric harvester, and it can account for more realistic effects, such as geometric nonlinearity, material anisotropy, or boundary conditions.
