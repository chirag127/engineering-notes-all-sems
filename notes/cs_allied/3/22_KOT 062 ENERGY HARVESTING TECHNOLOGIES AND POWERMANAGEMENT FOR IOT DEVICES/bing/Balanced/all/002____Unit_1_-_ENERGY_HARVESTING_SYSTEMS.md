# Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS

- Energy harvesting systems are devices or systems that are used to convert ambient energy, which is derived from external sources existing in the atmosphere, into electrical energy.
- Energy harvesting systems are attractive alternatives to battery-operated systems, especially for long-term, low-power, and self-sustaining electronic systems.
- Energy harvesting systems can scavenge and convert various sources of energy, such as mechanical load, vibrations, temperature gradients, light, radio frequency, etc., into electrical energy in the nW-mW range .
- Energy harvesting systems consist of four main components: energy transducer, energy storage, power management, and load.
- Energy transducer is the component that converts the ambient energy into electrical energy. It can be based on different principles, such as piezoelectric, electromagnetic, electrostatic, thermoelectric, photovoltaic, etc.
- Energy storage is the component that stores the electrical energy generated by the energy transducer. It can be based on different technologies, such as capacitors, batteries, supercapacitors, etc.
- Power management is the component that regulates and controls the flow of energy between the energy transducer, the energy storage, and the load. It can be based on different circuits, such as rectifiers, voltage converters, maximum power point trackers, etc.
- Load is the component that consumes the electrical energy provided by the energy harvesting system. It can be any electronic device, such as sensor, microcontroller, wireless transmitter, etc.
- Energy harvesting systems have various applications, such as wireless sensor networks, wearable devices, biomedical implants, environmental monitoring, structural health monitoring, etc  .
- Energy harvesting systems have various advantages, such as reducing the need for battery replacement, extending the lifetime of the system, enhancing the reliability and safety of the system, reducing the environmental impact of the system, etc .
