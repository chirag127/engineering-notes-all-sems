# Energy sources for the notes of the Unit 1 - ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEMS in the subject of KOT 062 ENERGY HARVESTING TECHNOLOGIES AND POWERMANAGEMENT FOR IOT DEVICES KCS

- Energy harvesting is the process of capturing and converting ambient energy from various sources into electrical energy for powering low-power devices.
- Energy harvesting systems consist of three main components: an energy source, an energy transducer, and an energy storage device.
- Energy sources are the environmental phenomena that provide energy for harvesting, such as light, heat, vibration, radio waves, etc.
- Energy transducers are the devices that convert the ambient energy into electrical energy, such as photovoltaic cells, thermoelectric generators, piezoelectric elements, etc.
- Energy storage devices are the components that store the electrical energy generated by the transducers, such as batteries, capacitors, supercapacitors, etc.
- The choice of the energy source and the transducer depends on the availability, intensity, and frequency of the ambient energy, as well as the power requirements and specifications of the device to be powered.
- Some of the most common energy sources for energy harvesting systems are:

  - Thermal energy: Thermal energy is the energy associated with temperature differences and heat flows. Thermal energy can be harvested from various sources, such as body heat, industrial waste heat, geothermal heat, solar radiation, etc. Thermal energy can be converted into electrical energy using thermoelectric generators, which are based on the Seebeck effect. The Seebeck effect is the phenomenon of generating a voltage difference across a circuit of two dissimilar conductors when they are subjected to a temperature gradient  .

  - Solar energy: Solar energy is the energy from the sun's radiation. Solar energy can be harvested using photovoltaic cells, which are devices that convert light into electricity using the photovoltaic effect. The photovoltaic effect is the phenomenon of generating an electric current in a material when it absorbs photons of light. Photovoltaic cells are made of semiconductors, such as silicon, that have a band gap that matches the energy of the photons  .

  - Vibration energy: Vibration energy is the energy from mechanical oscillations and movements. Vibration energy can be harvested from various sources, such as human motion, machinery, vehicles, wind, etc. Vibration energy can be converted into electrical energy using piezoelectric elements, which are materials that generate an electric charge when they are deformed by an applied force. Piezoelectric elements are made of crystals, such as quartz, that have a polar structure that aligns the electric dipoles when they are stressed  .

  - Radio frequency (RF) energy: RF energy is the energy from electromagnetic waves in the radio spectrum. RF energy can be harvested from various sources, such as wireless communication signals, broadcast signals, radar signals, etc. RF energy can be converted into electrical energy using RF energy harvesters, which are devices that capture and rectify the RF signals using antennas and diodes. RF energy harvesters can also modulate and transmit data back to the source using backscatter communication  .

  - Other energy sources: There are other energy sources that can be used for energy harvesting, such as wind energy, fluid flow, blood sugar, atmospheric pressure changes, etc. These energy sources can be converted into electrical energy using different transducers, such as wind turbines, microfluidic devices, biofuel cells, piezoelectric membranes, etc  .
