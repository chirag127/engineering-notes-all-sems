# Generation of electric power in semiconductor PV cells

- PV cells are devices that convert light energy into electrical energy using the photovoltaic effect .
- The photovoltaic effect is the phenomenon of generating an electric voltage or current in a material when it is exposed to light .
- The material used in PV cells is usually a semiconductor, such as silicon, which has the property of changing its electrical conductivity when light is absorbed.
- A semiconductor has two types of charge carriers: electrons and holes. Electrons are negatively charged particles that can move freely in the material, while holes are positively charged regions where electrons are missing.
- When light with sufficient energy (or photons) hits a semiconductor, it can excite some of the electrons from the lower energy state (or valence band) to the higher energy state (or conduction band), creating electron-hole pairs .
- The electron-hole pairs are separated by an electric field created by a junction between two layers of semiconductor with different doping levels. Doping is the process of adding impurities to a semiconductor to change its electrical properties.
- The junction can be either a p-n junction, where one layer is doped with a material that creates excess holes (p-type) and the other layer is doped with a material that creates excess electrons (n-type), or a heterojunction, where two different semiconductors are used.
- The electric field at the junction pushes the electrons to the n-type layer and the holes to the p-type layer, creating a potential difference or voltage across the cell.
- When the cell is connected to an external circuit, the electrons and holes can flow through it, generating an electric current and power.
- The power output of a PV cell depends on several factors, such as the intensity and wavelength of the light, the area and efficiency of the cell, the temperature and the load resistance .
- The power output of a single PV cell is typically only about two watts, so multiple cells are connected in series or parallel to form modules, panels or arrays to increase the voltage or current .
- PV cells can be used to generate electricity for various applications, such as residential, commercial, industrial, or remote areas. They can also be integrated into buildings, vehicles, or devices as a form of distributed generation .
