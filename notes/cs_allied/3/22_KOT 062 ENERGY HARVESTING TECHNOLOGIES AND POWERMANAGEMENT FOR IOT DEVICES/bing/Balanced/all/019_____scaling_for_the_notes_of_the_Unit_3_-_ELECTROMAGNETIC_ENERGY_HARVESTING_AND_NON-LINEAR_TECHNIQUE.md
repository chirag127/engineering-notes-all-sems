# Scaling for the notes of the Unit 3 - ELECTROMAGNETIC ENERGY HARVESTING AND NON-LINEAR TECHNIQUES

- Electromagnetic energy harvesting (EMEH) is a technique to convert ambient mechanical vibrations into electrical energy using electromagnetic transducers.
- EMEH devices typically consist of a coil and a magnet, which induce a voltage across the coil when they move relative to each other due to external vibrations.
- EMEH devices can be classified into linear and nonlinear types, depending on the restoring force of the system.
- Linear EMEH devices have a constant natural frequency and a narrow bandwidth of operation, which limits their performance in variable and broadband vibration environments.
- Nonlinear EMEH devices have a variable natural frequency and a wider bandwidth of operation, which enables them to adapt to different vibration frequencies and amplitudes.
- Nonlinear EMEH devices can be realized by introducing nonlinear elements, such as magnets, springs, or circuits, into the system.
- Some examples of nonlinear EMEH devices are:
  - Magnetic levitation-based EMEH, which uses the repulsive force between two magnets to levitate the coil and create a nonlinear restoring force .
  - Bistable EMEH, which uses a snap-through mechanism between two stable positions to create a nonlinear restoring force and large displacements.
  - Nonlinear interface circuit EMEH, which uses a diode or a switch to create a nonlinear electrical load and enhance the power output.
- Nonlinear EMEH devices have advantages such as higher power density, wider bandwidth, and self-tuning capability, compared to linear EMEH devices.
- Nonlinear EMEH devices also have challenges such as complex modeling, optimal design, and efficient power management.
