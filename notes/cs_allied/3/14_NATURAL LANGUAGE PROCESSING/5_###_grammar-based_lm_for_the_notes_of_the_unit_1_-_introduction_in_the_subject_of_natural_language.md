### Grammar-based LM for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Grammar-based language models (LMs) are a type of natural language processing (NLP) model that use grammar rules to generate and predict language. These models are based on the idea that language is structured and follows a set of rules, and they use this structure to generate and predict language.

Components of Grammar-based LMs:

1. Grammar Rules: Grammar-based LMs are based on a set of grammar rules that define the structure of language. These rules specify the relationships between words and phrases, and they are used to generate and predict language.

2. Parsing: Parsing is the process of analyzing the structure of a sentence and determining its constituent parts and relationships. Grammar-based LMs use parsing to determine the structure of a sentence and generate or predict the next word or phrase.

3. Generation: Generation is the process of using grammar rules and parsing information to generate new sentences. Grammar-based LMs use generation to generate new sentences that follow the grammar rules.

Applications of Grammar-based LMs:

1. Text Generation: Grammar-based LMs can be used to generate new text, such as summaries, headlines, and captions.

2. Text Prediction: Grammar-based LMs can be used to predict the next word or phrase in a sentence, which can be useful for applications such as autocomplete and text entry.

3. Text Classification: Grammar-based LMs can be used to classify text into different categories, such as sentiment analysis and topic classification.

In conclusion, Grammar-based language models (LMs) are a type of natural language processing (NLP) model that use grammar rules to generate and predict language. These models are based on grammar rules, parsing, and generation, and they have numerous applications in fields such as text generation, text prediction, and text classification.
