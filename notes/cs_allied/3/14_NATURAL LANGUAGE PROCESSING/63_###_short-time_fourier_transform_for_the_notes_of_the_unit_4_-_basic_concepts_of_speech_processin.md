The Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) is a powerful tool used in speech processing and analysis. It is a time-frequency representation of a speech signal that provides information about both the spectral and temporal characteristics of speech signals.

The STFT is computed by dividing a speech signal into overlapping frames and then applying a Fourier transform to each frame. The result is a time-frequency representation of the speech signal that provides information about the spectral content of the speech signal at each time frame.

One of the key advantages of the STFT is that it can be used to analyze non-stationary signals, such as speech signals, which have time-varying spectral characteristics. The STFT provides a way to visualize the spectral content of a speech signal over time, which can be useful for speech analysis and processing tasks such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, and speech coding.

The STFT is commonly used in combination with other signal processing techniques, such as filtering, windowing, and feature extraction, to extract meaningful information from speech signals. For example, the STFT can be used to extract spectral features, such as Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), which are widely used in speech recognition systems.
