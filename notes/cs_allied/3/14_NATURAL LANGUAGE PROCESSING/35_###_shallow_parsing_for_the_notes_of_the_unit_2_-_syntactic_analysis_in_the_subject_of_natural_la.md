### Shallow parsing for the notes of the Unit 2 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Shallow parsing, also known as chunking, is a process in natural language processing (NLP) that involves the identification of phrases and clauses in a sentence, without analyzing the relationships between the words in the sentence. Shallow parsing is used to identify the structure of a sentence at a high level, and it provides information about the main constituents of a sentence, such as noun phrases, verb phrases, and prepositional phrases.

Shallow parsing is a simpler form of syntactic parsing, and it is used in many NLP applications, such as named entity recognition, text classification, and sentiment analysis. Unlike deep parsing, which analyzes the relationships between words in a sentence, shallow parsing only analyzes the structure of a sentence at a high level.

There are several methods for performing shallow parsing, including:

1. Rule-based parsing: Rule-based parsing involves the use of hand-written rules to identify the structure of a sentence.

2. Statistical parsing: Statistical parsing involves the use of statistical models, such as Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) or Conditional Random Fields (CRFs), to identify the structure of a sentence.

3. Deep learning-based parsing: Deep learning-based parsing involves the use of deep neural networks, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or transformer networks, to identify the structure of a sentence.

Overall, shallow parsing is an important process in NLP, and it plays a critical role in the development of NLP systems and applications. By understanding shallow parsing and its role in NLP, students can gain a deeper appreciation for the technology and its impact on NLP and related fields.
