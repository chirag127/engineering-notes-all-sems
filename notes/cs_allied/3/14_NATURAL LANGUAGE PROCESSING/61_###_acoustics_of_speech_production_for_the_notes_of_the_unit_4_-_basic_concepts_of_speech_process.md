The acoustics of speech production is the study of how speech signals are produced by the human vocal tract. It encompasses the physical and physiological processes involved in speech production, as well as the acoustic properties of speech signals.

The human vocal tract is a complex system that consists of several components, including the lungs, larynx, vocal cords, pharynx, mouth, and nose. These components work together to produce speech signals by modulating air pressure and flow.

The larynx, or voice box, is responsible for producing the basic sound source in speech. The vocal cords, which are located in the larynx, vibrate rapidly to produce sound, which is then modified by the other components of the vocal tract.

The pharynx, mouth, and nose are responsible for shaping the sound produced by the larynx into speech signals. This shaping is achieved by changing the shape and size of the vocal tract, which in turn changes the acoustic properties of the speech signals.
