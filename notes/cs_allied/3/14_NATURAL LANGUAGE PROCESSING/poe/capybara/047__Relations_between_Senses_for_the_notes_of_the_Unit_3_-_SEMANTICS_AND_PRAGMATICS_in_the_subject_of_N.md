### Relations between Senses

In the field of Natural Language Processing, understanding the relations between different senses of words is crucial for building accurate and effective models. Here are some important relations between senses:

- **Synonymy**: This relation refers to words that have the same or similar meanings. For example, "happy" and "joyful" are synonyms.

- **Antonymy**: This relation refers to words that have opposite meanings. For example, "hot" and "cold" are antonyms.

- **Hyponymy/Hypernymy**: Hyponymy refers to words that are more specific than a given word, while hypernymy refers to words that are more general. For example, "apple" is a hyponym of "fruit," while "fruit" is a hypernym of "apple."

- **Meronymy/Holonymy**: Meronymy refers to words that are parts of a whole, while holonymy refers to words that are the whole made up of those parts. For example, "wheel" is a meronym of "car," while "car" is a holonym of "wheel."

- **Polysemy**: This relation refers to words that have multiple meanings. For example, "bank" can mean a financial institution or the side of a river.

- **Homonymy**: This relation refers to words that sound the same but have different meanings. For example, "rose" can refer to a flower or the past tense of "rise."

Understanding these relations between senses is crucial for tasks like word sense disambiguation and semantic role labeling. By identifying the correct sense of a word in context, we can build more accurate and effective language models.
