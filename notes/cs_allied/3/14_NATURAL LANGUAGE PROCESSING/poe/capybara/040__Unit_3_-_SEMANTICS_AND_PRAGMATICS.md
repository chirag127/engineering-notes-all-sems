## Unit 3 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics and pragmatics are two important branches of linguistics. Semantics deals with the meaning of words, phrases, and sentences, while pragmatics deals with how people use language in context.

### Semantics

1. Semantics deals with the study of meaning in language.
2. It is concerned with the relationship between words and their meanings.
3. Semantics is divided into two main areas: lexical semantics and compositional semantics.
4. Lexical semantics deals with the meaning of individual words, while compositional semantics deals with the meaning of phrases and sentences.
5. Semantics is important in natural language processing, machine translation, and information retrieval.

### Pragmatics

1. Pragmatics deals with how people use language in context.
2. It is concerned with the study of language use in social and cultural contexts.
3. Pragmatics is divided into several areas, including speech acts, implicature, and presupposition.
4. Speech acts refer to the actions performed by speakers when they use language, such as making promises, giving orders, and making requests.
5. Implicature refers to the meaning that is conveyed by an utterance even though it is not explicitly stated.
6. Presupposition refers to the assumptions that speakers make about the world when they use language.

In conclusion, semantics and pragmatics are important areas of linguistics that help us understand the meaning of language and how it is used in context. Understanding these concepts is essential for anyone interested in language processing, translation, or communication.
