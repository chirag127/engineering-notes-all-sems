### Thematic Roles

Thematic roles are used to describe the semantic relationships between different entities in a sentence. In natural language processing, understanding these roles is essential for accurate parsing and interpretation of sentences. Here are some of the most common thematic roles:

1. Agent - The entity that performs the action in a sentence. It is typically expressed as the subject of a transitive verb.

2. Patient - The entity that is acted upon in a sentence. It is usually expressed as the object of a transitive verb.

3. Theme - The entity that is being talked about in a sentence. It can be the subject or the object of a sentence.

4. Experiencer - The entity that experiences a particular state or feeling. It is often expressed as the subject of a psychological verb.

5. Instrument - The entity that is used to perform an action. It is usually expressed as a prepositional phrase.

6. Location - The place where an action occurs. It is typically expressed as a prepositional phrase.

7. Time - The specific time at which an action occurs. It is usually expressed as a prepositional phrase.

8. Source - The entity from which something originates. It is typically expressed as a prepositional phrase.

9. Goal - The entity toward which an action is directed. It is usually expressed as a prepositional phrase.

Understanding thematic roles is an important part of natural language processing, as it helps in identifying the relationships between different entities in a sentence. By identifying these roles, it becomes easier to extract meaning from the text and carry out various language-related tasks.
