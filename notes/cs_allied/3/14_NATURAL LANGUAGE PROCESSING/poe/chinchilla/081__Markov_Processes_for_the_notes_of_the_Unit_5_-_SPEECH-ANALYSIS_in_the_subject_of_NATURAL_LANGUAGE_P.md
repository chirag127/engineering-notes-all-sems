### Markov Processes for the notes of the Unit 5 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Markov processes are a type of stochastic process that describes the evolution of a system over time, where the future state of the system depends only on its current state and not on any previous states. In the context of natural language processing, Markov processes are often used to model the probabilistic relationships between words in a text corpus.

Here are some key points to keep in mind about Markov processes:

1. **Markov property:** As mentioned earlier, Markov processes have the property that the future state of the system depends only on its current state and not on any previous states. This property is also known as the memoryless property, since the system has no memory of its past states.

2. **State space:** The state space of a Markov process is the set of all possible states that the system can be in. In the context of natural language processing, the state space can be the set of all possible words in a text corpus.

3. **Transition probabilities:** Markov processes are characterized by their transition probabilities, which describe the probability of moving from one state to another. In the context of natural language processing, the transition probabilities can be estimated from the frequencies of word co-occurrences in a text corpus.

4. **Markov chain:** A Markov chain is a specific type of Markov process where the state space is discrete and the transition probabilities are time-independent. Markov chains are often used to model the probabilistic relationships between words in a text corpus.

5. **Stationary distribution:** A stationary distribution is a probability distribution over the state space that remains unchanged over time. In the context of Markov processes, the stationary distribution describes the long-term behavior of the system.

6. **Applications:** Markov processes have numerous applications in natural language processing, including language modeling, part-of-speech tagging, and machine translation.

In summary, Markov processes are a powerful tool for modeling the probabilistic relationships between words in a text corpus. By estimating the transition probabilities between words, we can build Markov chains that capture the underlying structure of natural language and use them for a variety of NLP tasks.
