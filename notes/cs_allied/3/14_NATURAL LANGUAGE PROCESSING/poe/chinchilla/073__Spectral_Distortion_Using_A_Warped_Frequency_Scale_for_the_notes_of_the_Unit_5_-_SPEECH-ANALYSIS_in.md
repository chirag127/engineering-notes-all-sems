### Spectral Distortion Using A Warped Frequency Scale

In speech analysis, spectral distortion refers to the changes or modifications in the spectral shape of a speech signal. Spectral distortion can be caused by various factors such as noise, channel effects, and coding artifacts. One approach to mitigate spectral distortion is to use a warped frequency scale.

A warped frequency scale is a non-linear frequency scale that can be used to transform the spectral envelope of a speech signal. This transformation can be done in a way that preserves the perceptual quality of the speech signal while reducing the spectral distortion.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when using a warped frequency scale for spectral distortion:

- A warped frequency scale is a non-linear frequency scale that can be used to transform the spectral envelope of a speech signal.
- The transformation is done in a way that preserves the perceptual quality of the speech signal while reducing the spectral distortion.
- The most commonly used warped frequency scale is the Mel scale, which is based on the human auditory system's perception of frequency.
- Other warped frequency scales include the Bark scale and the ERB scale.
- The Mel scale is defined by a set of non-linear frequency transformations that map frequencies to Mel units.
- The mapping function used to transform frequencies to Mel units is based on the critical bands of the human auditory system.
- The Mel scale can be used to extract Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), which are a popular feature in speech recognition.
- MFCCs are obtained by taking the Fourier transform of the speech signal, mapping the frequencies to Mel units, and taking the logarithm of the powers at each Mel frequency.
- The resulting Mel power spectrum is then transformed using the discrete cosine transform (DCT) to obtain the MFCCs.
- Using a warped frequency scale such as the Mel scale can improve the performance of speech recognition systems by reducing the impact of spectral distortion.
