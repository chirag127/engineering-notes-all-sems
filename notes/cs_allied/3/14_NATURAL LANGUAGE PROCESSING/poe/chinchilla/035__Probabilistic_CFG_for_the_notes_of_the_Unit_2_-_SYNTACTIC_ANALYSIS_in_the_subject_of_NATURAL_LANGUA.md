### Probabilistic CFG for the notes of the Unit 2 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING.

Probabilistic Context-Free Grammar (PCFG) is an extension of Context-Free Grammar (CFG), which assigns probabilities to the rules in a CFG. It is widely used in natural language processing for modeling the syntactic structure of sentences in a probabilistic way. In this unit, we will cover the following topics related to PCFG:

1. Introduction to PCFG:
    - Definition of PCFG and its importance in NLP.
    - Comparison of PCFG with CFG.

2. PCFG Formalism:
    - Definition of PCFG rules and their probabilities.
    - Derivation of a sentence using PCFG.
    
3. Training and Parsing with PCFG:
    - Estimation of PCFG probabilities from a corpus using Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE).
    - Parsing a sentence using the Viterbi Algorithm.
    - Challenges in PCFG parsing and possible solutions.

4. PCFG Applications:
    - Parsing and generation of natural language sentences.
    - Syntactic disambiguation in machine translation.
    - Information retrieval from text using syntactic patterns.

5. Evaluation Metrics for PCFG:
    - Precision, Recall, and F1-score.
    - Perplexity and Cross-Entropy.

In summary, PCFG is a powerful tool for modeling the syntactic structure of natural language sentences in a probabilistic way. It enables us to estimate the likelihood of a sentence given a grammar, which is useful for tasks such as parsing, generation, and disambiguation. By the end of this unit, you will have a solid understanding of PCFG and its applications in NLP.
