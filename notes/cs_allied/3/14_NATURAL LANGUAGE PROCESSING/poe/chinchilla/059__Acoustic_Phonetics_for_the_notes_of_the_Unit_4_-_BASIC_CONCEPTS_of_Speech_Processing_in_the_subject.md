### Acoustic Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 4 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Acoustic Phonetics is a subfield of phonetics that deals with the study of the physical properties of speech sounds. It is concerned with the analysis and measurement of the acoustic properties of speech sounds, such as their frequency, intensity, and duration. Here are some of the basic concepts of Acoustic Phonetics:

1. **Sound Waves:** Sound waves are the basic building blocks of speech. These waves are created when the vocal cords vibrate, producing a disturbance in the air that travels through space. The frequency of these waves determines the pitch of the sound.

2. **Frequency:** Frequency is the number of vibrations per second that a sound wave produces. It is measured in Hertz (Hz) and is related to the pitch of the sound. Higher frequencies produce higher-pitched sounds, while lower frequencies produce lower-pitched sounds.

3. **Intensity:** Intensity is the amount of energy that a sound wave carries per unit area. It is measured in decibels (dB) and is related to the loudness of the sound. Higher intensities produce louder sounds, while lower intensities produce quieter sounds.

4. **Spectrogram:** A spectrogram is a visual representation of the frequency and intensity of a sound wave over time. It is a useful tool for analyzing speech sounds and identifying different phonetic features.

5. **Formants:** Formants are the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract. They are important for distinguishing between different vowel sounds and are represented as dark bands in a spectrogram.

6. **Cepstrum:** A cepstrum is a mathematical representation of the spectrum of a signal. It is useful for analyzing the pitch and other periodic features of speech sounds.

7. **Pitch:** Pitch is the perception of the frequency of a sound. It is closely related to the fundamental frequency of a sound wave and is important for distinguishing between different vowel and consonant sounds.

8. **Duration:** Duration is the length of time that a sound persists. It is an important feature for distinguishing between different consonant sounds, as some consonants are produced for longer or shorter periods of time than others.

9. **Spectral Envelope:** The spectral envelope is a plot of the energy of a sound wave at different frequencies. It is useful for analyzing the overall shape of a sound and for identifying different phonetic features.

Acoustic Phonetics is an important field for understanding the physical properties of speech sounds. By analyzing the acoustic properties of speech, linguists and speech scientists can gain insights into the way that sounds are produced and perceived, and how they are used in language.
