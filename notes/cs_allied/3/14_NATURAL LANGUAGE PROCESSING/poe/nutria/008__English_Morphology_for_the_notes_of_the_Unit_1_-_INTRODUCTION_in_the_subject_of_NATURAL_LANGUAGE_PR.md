
### English Morphology

* Morphology is the study of the structure of words and how they are formed. 
* In English, words are composed of morphemes, which are the smallest units of meaning. 
* Morphemes can be either root words or affixes, which are added to root words to modify their meaning. 
* English has two main types of affixes: prefixes and suffixes. 
* Prefixes are added to the beginning of words to modify their meaning, while suffixes are added to the end of words. 
* For example, the word “unhappy” is composed of the prefix “un-” and the root word “happy”. 
* English also has a number of inflectional morphemes, which are used to indicate grammatical functions such as tense, number, and gender. 
* For example, the verb “run” can be inflected to create the forms “runs”, “ran”, and “running”. 
* Finally, English also has a number of derivational morphemes, which are used to create new words from existing ones. 
* For example, the suffix “-ness” can be added to the root word “happy” to create the new word “happiness”.
