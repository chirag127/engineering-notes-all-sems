
### Spectral Distortion Using A Warped Frequency Scale

1. Spectral distortion is a concept used in speech analysis to describe the change in the frequency spectrum of a signal.

2. A warped frequency scale is a technique used to modify the frequency axis of a signal in order to better represent its spectral characteristics.

3. This technique is commonly used in speech-analysis applications, such as speech recognition and speech synthesis.

4. Warped frequency scales can be used to reduce the amount of distortion in a signal by correcting for the nonlinearities in the frequency response of the system.

5. The most common type of warped frequency scale is the Mel-frequency cepstrum (MFC). This technique uses a logarithmic frequency axis to better represent the frequency content of a signal.

6. Other types of warped frequency scales include the Bark scale, the ERB (equivalent rectangular bandwidth) scale, and the linear frequency scale.

7. Warped frequency scales are used to improve the accuracy of speech-analysis algorithms by providing a better representation of the signal's frequency content.
