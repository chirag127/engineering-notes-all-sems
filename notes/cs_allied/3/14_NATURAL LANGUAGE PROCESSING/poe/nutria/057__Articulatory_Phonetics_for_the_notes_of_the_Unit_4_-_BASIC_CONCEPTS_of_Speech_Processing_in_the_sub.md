
### Articulatory Phonetics for the Notes of Unit 4 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the Subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

* Articulatory phonetics is the study of how speech sounds are produced by the human vocal tract. It is concerned with the physical movements of the vocal organs and the acoustic properties of the sounds that they produce.

* The vocal organs used in articulatory phonetics include the lips, tongue, teeth, jaw, larynx and vocal folds. Each of these organs is capable of producing a range of sounds, depending on the position of the organs and the amount of air pressure and airflow that is used.

* The acoustic properties of speech sounds are determined by the way in which the vocal organs interact with each other and the air pressure and airflow that is used. These properties include the frequency, intensity, duration and timbre of the sound.

* Articulatory phonetics is used to analyse the speech of different languages and to describe the sounds of those languages. It is also used to study the differences between dialects and to compare the sounds of different languages.

* Articulatory phonetics can also be used to help identify and diagnose speech and language disorders. By studying the articulation of a person's speech, it is possible to identify any abnormalities or problems with the way that the person is producing speech sounds.
