### Thematic Roles for the notes of the Unit 3 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Thematic roles are a representation used in Natural Language Processing (NLP) to describe the relationships between the participants in an event described by a sentence. The goal of thematic roles is to capture the underlying meaning of a sentence, making it possible to analyze and understand the sentence's meaning.

Thematic roles are typically represented as a set of predefined roles, such as agent, patient, experiencer, and theme. These roles describe the participants in an event and their relationships to each other. For example, in the sentence "John broke the vase", the agent is "John", the patient is "the vase", and the event is "breaking".

Thematic roles are used in NLP to perform semantic analysis, which involves determining the meaning of a sentence. This information is used to generate a semantic representation of the sentence, which can be used for tasks such as machine translation, text generation, and question answering.

One advantage of thematic roles is that they provide a way to represent the meaning of a sentence in a structured and formal way, making it possible to analyze and understand the sentence's meaning. This is in contrast to other approaches to semantic analysis, which can be more subjective and less precise.

Another advantage of thematic roles is that they can handle complex sentences and multi-word expressions, which can be difficult to analyze using other approaches. This makes them a useful tool for NLP tasks that involve complex language, such as machine translation and question answering.

In conclusion, thematic roles are a powerful representation for NLP, as they provide a way to represent the relationships between the participants in an event described by a sentence. By using thematic roles, NLP systems can analyze and understand the meaning of sentences, making it possible to perform a wide range of NLP tasks.
