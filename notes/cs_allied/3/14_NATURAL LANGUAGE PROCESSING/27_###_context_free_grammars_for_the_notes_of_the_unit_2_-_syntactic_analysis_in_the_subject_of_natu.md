### Context Free Grammars for the notes of the Unit 2 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Unit 2 of Syntactic Analysis in the subject of Natural Language Processing may cover the topic of Context Free Grammars (CFG). CFG is a mathematical model used to describe the structure of sentences in natural language.

The following topics may be covered in this unit:

1. Introduction to Context Free Grammars: An overview of CFGs, including their definition, principles, and applications in Natural Language Processing.

2. CFG Terminology: An introduction to the terminology used in CFGs, including symbols, rules, and productions, among others.

3. CFG Notation: An overview of the notation used in CFGs, including Backus-Naur Form (BNF) notation and Extended Backus-Naur Form (EBNF) notation, among others.

4. CFG Parsing: An introduction to CFG parsing, including the principles and methods used to parse sentences using CFGs, such as top-down parsing, bottom-up parsing, and chart parsing, among others.

5. Ambiguity in CFGs: An overview of ambiguity in CFGs, including its definition, causes, and methods for resolving ambiguity, such as disambiguation rules and statistical methods, among others.

6. Applications of CFGs: An overview of the different applications of CFGs in Natural Language Processing, including syntax analysis, semantic analysis, and machine translation, among others.

7. Limitations of CFGs: An overview of the limitations of CFGs, including their inability to handle certain types of linguistic phenomena, such as idiomatic expressions and sarcasm, among others.

In conclusion, Unit 2 of Syntactic Analysis in the subject of Natural Language Processing may provide a comprehensive overview of Context Free Grammars, including their principles, methods, and applications in Natural Language Processing. This knowledge is essential for understanding the use of CFGs in describing the structure of sentences in natural language and for developing effective Natural Language Processing models.
