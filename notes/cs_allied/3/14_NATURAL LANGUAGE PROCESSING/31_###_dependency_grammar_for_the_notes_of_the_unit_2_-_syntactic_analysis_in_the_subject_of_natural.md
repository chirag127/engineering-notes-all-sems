### Dependency Grammar for the notes of the Unit 2 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Dependency grammar is a type of linguistic theory and computational model that describes the relationships between words in a sentence based on their dependencies, or the relationships between the words and their arguments. In natural language processing (NLP), dependency grammar is used to analyze and understand the structure of sentences, and to perform tasks such as syntactic parsing, named entity recognition, and text classification.

Dependency grammar is different from traditional grammars, such as constituency grammars, which describe the structure of sentences in terms of constituents, or phrases and clauses. In dependency grammar, each word in a sentence is treated as a node in a graph, and the relationships between the words are represented as edges between the nodes.

Some of the key features of dependency grammar include:

1. Directed edges: In dependency grammar, the relationships between words are represented as directed edges, which indicate the head word and the dependent words in a relationship.

2. Projectivity: Dependency grammar is projective, which means that the relationships between words are determined by the order of the words in the sentence, and not by the structure of the sentence.

3. Valency: Dependency grammar takes into account the valency of words, which is the number of arguments that a word can take.

4. Non-binary relationships: Dependency grammar allows for non-binary relationships between words, which means that a word can have more than one head or more than one dependent.

Overall, dependency grammar is an important component of NLP, and it plays a critical role in the development of NLP systems and applications. By understanding dependency grammar, students can gain a deeper appreciation for the technology and its impact on NLP and related fields.
