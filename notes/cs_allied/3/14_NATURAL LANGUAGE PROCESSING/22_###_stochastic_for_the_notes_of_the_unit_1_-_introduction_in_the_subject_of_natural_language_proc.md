### Stochastic for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Stochastic is a term used in the field of natural language processing (NLP) to describe a type of process or model that is based on randomness or probability. In NLP, stochastic models are used to model the probability of words or sequences of words in a language, and they play a critical role in many NLP applications, such as language generation, machine translation, and speech recognition.

Stochastic models are typically based on Markov models, which are mathematical models that describe the probability of a sequence of events based on the probability of the previous events. In NLP, Markov models are used to model the probability of words or sequences of words in a language, and they are a fundamental building block of many NLP applications.

There are several types of stochastic models used in NLP, including:

1. N-gram models: N-gram models are a type of Markov model that model the probability of a word based on the previous n-1 words in the sequence.

2. Hidden Markov models (HMMs): HMMs are a type of Markov model that are used to model the probability of a sequence of events, where the events are hidden and can only be inferred from the observed data.

3. Maximum entropy models: Maximum entropy models are a type of stochastic model that are used to model the probability of a sequence of events based on the maximum entropy principle, which states that the model should be as uncertain as possible while still being consistent with the observed data.

Overall, stochastic models are an important component of NLP, and they play a critical role in many NLP applications. By understanding stochastic models and their role in NLP, students can gain a deeper appreciation for the technology and its impact on NLP and related fields.
