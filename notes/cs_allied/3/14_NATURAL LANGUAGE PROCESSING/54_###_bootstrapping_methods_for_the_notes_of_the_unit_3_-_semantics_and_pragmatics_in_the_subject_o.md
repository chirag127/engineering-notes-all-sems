Bootstrapping methods are a type of unsupervised machine learning technique used in natural language processing (NLP) to extract information from text data. The goal of bootstrapping methods is to automatically learn patterns and relationships in the data without being explicitly told what the patterns are.

Bootstrapping methods are based on the idea of starting with a small amount of seed information and iteratively refining it until a complete and accurate representation of the data is obtained. This is done by using the seed information to make initial predictions, and then using the results of these predictions to refine the seed information and make new predictions.

There are several types of bootstrapping methods used in NLP, including word sense disambiguation, named entity recognition, and part-of-speech tagging. In word sense disambiguation, the goal is to identify the correct sense of a word in a given context. In named entity recognition, the goal is to identify and classify named entities, such as people, organizations, and locations. In part-of-speech tagging, the goal is to identify the grammatical role of each word in a sentence.

Bootstrapping methods are particularly useful in NLP because they can be used to extract information from large amounts of text data without the need for extensive manual annotation. This makes them well-suited for use in applications where large amounts of text data are generated, such as social media and news articles.
