### Normal Forms for grammar for the notes of the Unit 2 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Normal Forms in grammar refer to a set of rules and conventions used to simplify and standardize the representation of grammar rules in natural language processing (NLP). Normal forms are used to make it easier to analyze and process sentences, by breaking down complex grammar rules into simpler, more manageable components.

Components of Normal Forms:

1. Chomsky Normal Form (CNF): CNF is a normal form that represents grammar rules as a set of production rules, where each rule defines the relationship between a non-terminal symbol and a sequence of terminal symbols.

2. Greibach Normal Form (GNF): GNF is a normal form that represents grammar rules as a set of production rules, where each rule defines the relationship between a non-terminal symbol and a sequence of terminal symbols, with the first symbol in the sequence being a non-terminal symbol.

Applications of Normal Forms in NLP:

1. Parsing: Normal forms are used in parsing to simplify the process of analyzing the structure of a sentence and identifying its constituent parts and relationships.

2. Grammar Rule Simplification: Normal forms are used to simplify complex grammar rules, making it easier to analyze and process sentences.

3. Automatic Generation of Parsers: Normal forms are used in the automatic generation of parsers, which are software programs that analyze the structure of sentences and identify their constituent parts and relationships.

In conclusion, Normal Forms in grammar are a set of rules and conventions used to simplify and standardize the representation of grammar rules in natural language processing (NLP). Normal forms are used to make it easier to analyze and process sentences, by breaking down complex grammar rules into simpler, more manageable components. The two most commonly used normal forms in NLP are Chomsky Normal Form (CNF) and Greibach Normal Form (GNF), and they are used in parsing, grammar rule simplification, and the automatic generation of parsers.
