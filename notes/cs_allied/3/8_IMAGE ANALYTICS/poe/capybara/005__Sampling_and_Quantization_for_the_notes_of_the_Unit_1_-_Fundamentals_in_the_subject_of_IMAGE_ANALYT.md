### Sampling and Quantization

In the field of Image Analytics, Sampling and Quantization are two important concepts that are used to capture and represent images digitally. Here are some important points to understand these concepts better:

#### Sampling

- Sampling is the process of converting a continuous signal (analog) into a discrete signal (digital) by taking samples at regular intervals.
- In the context of Image Analytics, sampling refers to the process of converting a continuous image into a digital image by taking samples of the image at regular intervals.
- The samples taken are called pixels, which represent the value of the image at that particular point.
- The frequency at which an image is sampled is called the sampling rate, and it determines the quality and resolution of the digital image.
- The Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem states that in order to accurately represent a signal, the sampling rate must be at least twice the highest frequency present in the signal.

#### Quantization

- Quantization is the process of representing continuous values with a finite number of discrete values.
- In the context of Image Analytics, quantization refers to the process of representing the pixel values of an image with a finite number of discrete levels.
- The number of discrete levels used to represent the pixel values is called the bit depth, and it determines the dynamic range of the image.
- The higher the bit depth, the more accurate and detailed the representation of the original image.
- The most commonly used bit depths in Image Analytics are 8-bit (256 levels), 16-bit (65,536 levels), and 32-bit (4.3 billion levels).

In conclusion, Sampling and Quantization are two important concepts that are used in Image Analytics to capture and represent images digitally. Understanding these concepts is essential to properly analyze and manipulate digital images.
