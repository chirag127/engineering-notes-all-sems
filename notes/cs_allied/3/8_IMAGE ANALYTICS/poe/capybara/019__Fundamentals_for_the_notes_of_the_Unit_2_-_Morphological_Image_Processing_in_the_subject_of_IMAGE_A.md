### Fundamentals for the notes of the Unit 2 - Morphological Image Processing in the subject of IMAGE ANALYTICS

Morphological image processing is a branch of digital image processing that deals with the analysis and processing of images by considering their shape and structure. Below are some of the fundamentals to keep in mind when studying morphological image processing.

- **Morphology:** Morphology is the study of the form and structure of objects. In morphological image processing, we use mathematical operations to analyze and manipulate the shapes and structures of images. The two fundamental operations in morphological image processing are dilation and erosion.

- **Dilation:** Dilation is a morphological operation that involves adding pixels to the boundaries of objects in an image. This operation expands the size of objects and fills in gaps between them.

- **Erosion:** Erosion is a morphological operation that involves removing pixels from the boundaries of objects in an image. This operation shrinks the size of objects and separates them from each other.

- **Structuring Element:** A structuring element is a small binary image that is used in morphological operations to define the shape and size of the neighborhood around each pixel in an image. Structuring elements can be of different shapes, such as square, rectangle, or circular.

- **Opening and Closing:** Opening and closing are morphological operations that involve applying erosion and dilation operations in a specific order. Opening is performed by applying an erosion operation followed by a dilation operation, while closing is performed by applying a dilation operation followed by an erosion operation.

- **Boundary Extraction:** Boundary extraction is a morphological operation that involves detecting the boundaries between objects in an image. This operation is useful in image segmentation and object recognition.

- **Thinning and Thickening:** Thinning and thickening are morphological operations that involve reducing or increasing the width of objects in an image. Thinning is performed by repeatedly applying erosion operations until the object is reduced to a single-pixel width, while thickening is performed by repeatedly applying dilation operations until the object reaches the desired width.

Morphological image processing is a powerful tool for analyzing and manipulating the shapes and structures of images. By understanding the fundamentals of morphological operations, you can apply this technique to a wide range of applications, such as image enhancement, object recognition, and pattern analysis.
