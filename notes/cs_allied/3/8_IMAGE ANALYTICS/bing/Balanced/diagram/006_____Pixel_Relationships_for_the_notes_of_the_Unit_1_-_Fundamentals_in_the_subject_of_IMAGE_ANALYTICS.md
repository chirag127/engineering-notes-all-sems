### Pixel Relationships

- A pixel is a discrete unit of information that represents the intensity or color of an image at a specific location.
- Pixels are arranged in a two-dimensional grid, where each pixel has a row and column index.
- The relationship between pixels can be described by their spatial, intensity, and color properties.

#### Spatial Properties

- The spatial properties of pixels include their position, distance, and neighborhood.
- The position of a pixel is given by its row and column index, or by its coordinates in a Cartesian system.
- The distance between two pixels is measured by a metric, such as the Euclidean distance, the Manhattan distance, or the chessboard distance.
- The neighborhood of a pixel is the set of pixels that are adjacent or close to it, according to a predefined criterion, such as the 4-neighborhood, the 8-neighborhood, or the circular neighborhood.

#### Intensity Properties

- The intensity properties of pixels include their value, contrast, and dynamic range.
- The value of a pixel is the numerical representation of its intensity or brightness, which can range from 0 (black) to 255 (white) for an 8-bit grayscale image, or from 0 to 2^n - 1 for an n-bit image.
- The contrast of a pixel is the difference between its value and the average value of its neighborhood, which indicates how well it stands out from its surroundings.
- The dynamic range of an image is the ratio between the maximum and minimum pixel values, which indicates how much variation there is in the image.

#### Color Properties

- The color properties of pixels include their hue, saturation, and value, or their red, green, and blue components, depending on the color model used to represent the image.
- The hue of a pixel is the dominant wavelength of its color, which can range from 0° (red) to 360° (red again) on a color wheel.
- The saturation of a pixel is the purity or vividness of its color, which can range from 0 (gray) to 100% (full color).
- The value of a pixel is the brightness or luminance of its color, which can range from 0 (black) to 100% (white).
- The red, green, and blue components of a pixel are the amounts of each primary color that make up its color, which can range from 0 to 255 for an 8-bit RGB image, or from 0 to 2^n - 1 for an n-bit image.
