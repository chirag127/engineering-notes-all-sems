# Snakes and Level Sets for Image Segmentation

- Image segmentation is the process of partitioning an image into meaningful regions or objects based on some criteria, such as color, texture, shape, etc.
- Snakes and level sets are two popular methods for image segmentation that use deformable curves or surfaces to fit the boundaries of the desired regions or objects.
- Snakes, also known as active contours, are parametric curves that minimize an energy functional composed of internal and external forces. Internal forces are derived from the curve's shape and smoothness, while external forces are derived from the image data and user input. Snakes can be initialized by the user or by some automatic method, and then iteratively deformed to approach the object boundaries by minimizing the energy functional.
- Level sets are implicit surfaces that represent the regions or objects by the zero level set of a higher dimensional function. Level sets can be initialized by the user or by some automatic method, and then evolved by solving a partial differential equation (PDE) that incorporates image information and geometric constraints. Level sets can handle topological changes and multiple components, and can be extended to higher dimensions.
- Snakes and level sets have some similarities and differences. Both methods are based on the idea of evolving curves or surfaces towards the object boundaries, and both methods can incorporate image features and user guidance. However, snakes are explicit and parametric, while level sets are implicit and non-parametric. Snakes can segment one component, while level sets can segment multiple components. Snakes are faster and simpler to implement, while level sets are more robust and flexible. Snakes and level sets can be combined to achieve better performance and accuracy.
