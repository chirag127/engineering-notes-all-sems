### Sampling and Quantization

Sampling and quantization are two fundamental concepts in image analytics. They are essential for converting continuous signals into digital signals that can be processed and analyzed by computers.

1. **Sampling** refers to the process of measuring the value of a continuous signal at regular intervals. This is done by taking samples of the signal at equally spaced points in time. The rate at which the samples are taken is called the sampling rate or sampling frequency. The sampling rate must be high enough to capture all the important information in the signal, but not so high that it results in unnecessary data.

2. **Quantization** refers to the process of approximating the continuous values of the sampled signal with a finite set of discrete values. This is done by dividing the range of possible values into a number of levels, and assigning each sample to the nearest level. The number of levels is determined by the number of bits used to represent each sample, with more bits allowing for more levels and greater accuracy.

Together, sampling and quantization allow for the conversion of continuous signals into digital signals that can be processed and analyzed by computers. This is essential for many applications in image analytics, including image compression, enhancement, and recognition. It is important to carefully choose the sampling rate and quantization levels to balance the trade-off between accuracy and data size.
