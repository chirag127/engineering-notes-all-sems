### Color Fundamentals

1. Color is a visual perception that is created by the brain in response to the wavelengths of light that are reflected or emitted by an object.
2. The visible spectrum of light includes wavelengths from approximately 380 to 740 nanometers, which are perceived as the colors violet, blue, green, yellow, orange, and red.
3. The primary colors of light are red, green, and blue (RGB). These colors can be combined in various proportions to produce all other colors.
4. The primary colors of pigments (such as paint or ink) are cyan, magenta, and yellow (CMY). These colors can be combined in various proportions to produce all other colors.
5. Color can be described using various color models, such as RGB, CMYK, HSL, and HSV. These models represent colors using different combinations of numerical values.
6. Color can also be described using color spaces, which define the range of colors that can be represented within a particular color model. Common color spaces include sRGB, Adobe RGB, and ProPhoto RGB.
7. The perception of color is influenced by various factors, including the lighting conditions, the surrounding colors, and the individual observer. As a result, the same color can appear differently in different contexts.
8. Color theory is the study of how colors interact and how they can be combined to create harmonious color schemes. This includes concepts such as color harmony, color contrast, and color temperature.
9. In image analytics, color can be used to extract information from images, such as identifying objects or detecting changes over time. This can be achieved using techniques such as color segmentation, color tracking, and color-based feature extraction.
