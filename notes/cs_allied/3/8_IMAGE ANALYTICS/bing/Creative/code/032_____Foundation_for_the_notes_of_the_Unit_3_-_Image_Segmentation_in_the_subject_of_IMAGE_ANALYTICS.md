### Foundation for the notes of the Unit 3 - Image Segmentation in the subject of IMAGE ANALYTICS

- Image segmentation is the process of dividing an image into meaningful regions or segments based on some criteria, such as color, texture, intensity, shape, etc.
- Image segmentation has many applications in computer vision, such as object detection, face recognition, medical imaging, scene understanding, etc.
- Image segmentation can be classified into two main types: supervised and unsupervised.
  - Supervised segmentation uses a labeled dataset of images and segments to train a model that can segment new images based on the learned features and rules.
  - Unsupervised segmentation does not use any labels, but relies on the inherent properties and patterns of the image data to group pixels into segments.
- Image segmentation can also be categorized based on the level of granularity or detail of the segments: global and local.
  - Global segmentation divides the image into a few large segments that capture the overall structure and content of the image.
  - Local segmentation divides the image into many small segments that capture the fine details and variations of the image.
- Image segmentation can be performed using various methods and techniques, such as thresholding, clustering, region growing, edge detection, graph-based methods, deep learning, etc.
  - Thresholding is a simple and fast method that segments the image based on a predefined or adaptive intensity value that separates the foreground and background pixels.
  - Clustering is a method that groups pixels based on their similarity in terms of color, intensity, texture, or other features, using algorithms such as k-means, fuzzy c-means, mean shift, etc.
  - Region growing is a method that starts from a seed pixel or region and expands it by adding neighboring pixels that satisfy some homogeneity criteria, such as intensity, color, texture, etc.
  - Edge detection is a method that identifies the boundaries or contours of the objects or regions in the image by finding the pixels where there is a significant change in intensity or gradient, using operators such as Sobel, Canny, Laplacian, etc.
  - Graph-based methods are methods that model the image as a graph, where the nodes are the pixels and the edges are the connections between them, and use graph algorithms such as minimum spanning tree, normalized cuts, graph cuts, etc. to segment the image based on the weights or costs of the edges.
  - Deep learning is a method that uses neural networks, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), generative adversarial networks (GANs), etc. to learn the features and rules for image segmentation from large amounts of data, and can achieve state-of-the-art results for complex and challenging segmentation tasks.
