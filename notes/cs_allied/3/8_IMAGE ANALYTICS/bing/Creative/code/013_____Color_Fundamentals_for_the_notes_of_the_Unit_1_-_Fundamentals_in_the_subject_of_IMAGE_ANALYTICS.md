### Color Fundamentals

- Color is a property of light that depends on the wavelength and intensity of the electromagnetic (EM) radiation.
- Color can be perceived by humans or measured by devices, such as cameras or sensors.
- Color perception is influenced by physical, physiological, and psychological factors, such as the illumination, the viewing conditions, and the observer's mood or expectations.
- Color measurement is based on standard color spaces, models, and systems that define how colors are represented, quantified, and communicated.
- Color image processing is the manipulation and analysis of color images using digital techniques, such as filtering, segmentation, enhancement, compression, and recognition.
- Color image processing has many applications in various domains, such as computer vision, multimedia, art, medicine, and remote sensing.

Some of the main concepts and terms related to color fundamentals are:

- **Spectral power distribution (SPD)**: The function that describes the amount of energy emitted by a light source at each wavelength in the visible spectrum.
- **Tristimulus values**: The three numbers that specify the amount of red, green, and blue light needed to match a given color sensation. They depend on the choice of the primary colors and the color matching functions.
- **Color matching functions**: The functions that describe how the human eye responds to different wavelengths of light. They are derived from experimental data and vary for different observers and conditions.
- **Color space**: A mathematical model that defines a set of colors and their coordinates in a multidimensional space. Examples of color spaces are RGB, CMYK, HSV, and CIELAB.
- **Color model**: A way of representing colors using a set of parameters that have some physical or perceptual meaning. Examples of color models are additive, subtractive, hue-saturation-value, and opponent colors.
- **Color system**: A set of rules and standards that specify how colors are measured, reproduced, and communicated. Examples of color systems are CIE, NTSC, and sRGB.
- **Color gamut**: The range of colors that can be displayed or reproduced by a device or a medium. It is usually represented by a polygon in a color space.
- **Color conversion**: The process of transforming colors from one color space or model to another, using mathematical formulas or lookup tables.
- **Color difference**: The numerical measure of how dissimilar two colors are, based on a color difference formula or a metric. Examples of color difference formulas are CIEDE2000 and CMC.
- **Color constancy**: The ability of the human visual system to perceive the colors of objects as invariant, despite changes in the illumination or the viewing conditions.
- **Color adaptation**: The adjustment of the human visual system to the prevailing illumination or the viewing conditions, resulting in changes in color perception and sensitivity.
- **Color appearance**: The subjective impression of color that depends on the physical, physiological, and psychological factors that affect color perception. Examples of color appearance attributes are brightness, lightness, hue, saturation, and chroma.
