### Segmentation by Region Growing and by Region Splitting and Merging for the notes of the Unit 3 - Image Segmentation in the subject of IMAGE ANALYTICS

Segmentation by Region Growing and by Region Splitting and Merging are two common approaches for image segmentation, which is the process of dividing an image into multiple regions or segments.

Segmentation by Region Growing:
1. Initialization: A seed point is selected in the image, and the region growing process starts from this seed point.
2. Region growing: The region growing process involves expanding the region by adding pixels that are similar to the pixels in the current region. Similarity is typically defined based on color, texture, or intensity.
3. Stopping criteria: The region growing process stops when the region reaches a certain size, or when all similar pixels have been added to the region.

Segmentation by Region Splitting and Merging:
1. Initialization: The entire image is treated as a single region.
2. Splitting: The region is split into multiple sub-regions based on a criterion such as color, texture, or intensity.
3. Merging: The sub-regions are merged based on a criterion such as size, shape, or similarity.
4. Stopping criteria: The region splitting and merging process stops when the regions meet a certain size or similarity criteria, or when the image has been divided into a sufficient number of regions.

In summary, Segmentation by Region Growing and by Region Splitting and Merging are two common approaches for image segmentation. Segmentation by Region Growing involves starting from a seed point and expanding the region until a stopping criteria is met, while Segmentation by Region Splitting and Merging involves dividing the image into sub-regions and merging them based on a criterion.
