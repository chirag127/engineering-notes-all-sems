### Power-Law Transformations for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamentals in the subject of IMAGE ANALYTICS

Power-Law Transformations are mathematical functions used in image processing to adjust the brightness and contrast of an image. They are used to stretch or compress the intensity values of an image, and are defined by a power-law equation.

The key features of Power-Law Transformations include:
1. Brightness adjustment: Power-Law Transformations can be used to adjust the brightness of an image, making it darker or brighter.
2. Contrast adjustment: Power-Law Transformations can be used to adjust the contrast of an image, making it more or less contrasted.
3. Non-linear: Power-Law Transformations are non-linear functions, meaning that they do not preserve the linear relationship between input and output values.
4. Customizable: Power-Law Transformations can be customized by adjusting the parameters of the power-law equation, allowing for fine-tuned control over the brightness and contrast of an image.

In summary, Power-Law Transformations are mathematical functions used in image processing to adjust the brightness and contrast of an image. They are non-linear functions that can be customized for fine-tuned control over the brightness and contrast of an image.
