### Variable Thresholding for the notes of the Unit 3 - Image Segmentation in the subject of IMAGE ANALYTICS

Variable Thresholding is a technique used in image segmentation to separate an image into multiple segments, or regions, based on the intensity values of pixels. The goal of variable thresholding is to find a threshold value that separates the image into meaningful regions.

There are several approaches to variable thresholding, including:
1. Global Thresholding: A single threshold value is applied to the entire image to separate it into two regions.
2. Adaptive Thresholding: The threshold value is computed locally for each region, based on the mean or median intensity value of the surrounding pixels.
3. Otsu's Thresholding: A method that automatically calculates the threshold value based on the histogram of the image intensity values.

The choice of variable thresholding method depends on the type of image and the desired segmentation results. For example, global thresholding may be sufficient for images with clear, distinct regions, while adaptive or Otsu's thresholding may be necessary for images with more complex intensity distributions.

In general, variable thresholding is an important step in image analysis, as it provides a way to separate the image into meaningful regions for further processing and analysis.
