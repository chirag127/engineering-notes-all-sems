## Unit 2 - Morphological Image Processing

Unit 2 - Morphological Image Processing covers the concepts and techniques used to process images using morphological operations. Morphological operations are mathematical operations that are used to process images based on their shape and structure.

The following are key aspects of morphological image processing:

1. Dilation: Dilation is a morphological operation that expands the objects in an image. Dilation is used to connect objects in an image, or to fill in gaps between objects.

2. Erosion: Erosion is a morphological operation that shrinks the objects in an image. Erosion is used to separate objects in an image, or to remove noise.

3. Opening: Opening is a morphological operation that combines erosion and dilation. Opening is used to remove noise from an image and to preserve the overall structure of the objects in the image.

4. Closing: Closing is a morphological operation that combines dilation and erosion. Closing is used to fill in gaps between objects in an image and to preserve the overall structure of the objects in the image.

5. Skeletonization: Skeletonization is a morphological operation that reduces an object in an image to its skeleton, or its thinned representation. Skeletonization is used to preserve the structure of objects in an image, while reducing their size.

By understanding these concepts, organizations can effectively use morphological operations to process images based on their shape and structure.
