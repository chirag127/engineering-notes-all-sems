### Applying spatial relationships, functions, and models for the notes of the Unit 2 - RFID False Authentications in the subject of KOT 061 BIG DATA ANALYTICS FOR IOT KCS

- RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is a technology that uses radio waves to identify objects or people by attaching tags to them. RFID tags contain a unique identifier and some data that can be read by RFID readers.
- RFID authentication is the process of verifying the identity and validity of RFID tags and readers. RFID authentication can prevent unauthorized access, counterfeiting, cloning, and tampering of RFID tags and data.
- RFID false authentication is the situation where a legitimate RFID tag is wrongly rejected by a RFID reader, or a fake RFID tag is wrongly accepted by a RFID reader. RFID false authentication can cause security breaches, data loss, and operational errors.
- RFID false authentication can arise from various factors, such as:
  - The design and implementation of RFID authentication protocols. Some protocols may have flaws or vulnerabilities that allow attackers to spoof, replay, or modify RFID messages. For example, YA-TRAP is a protocol that uses a hash chain to authenticate RFID tags, but it can cause false rejections when the tags are out of sync with the readers .
  - The physical characteristics and limitations of RFID tags and readers. Some tags may have low memory, low power, or low computation capabilities that restrict the use of cryptographic algorithms or random numbers. Some readers may have low range, low accuracy, or low reliability that affect the communication and verification of RFID messages. For example, C1G2 is a standard for passive RFID tags that has limited security features and can cause false authentications when used with incompatible protocols .
  - The environmental conditions and interference of RFID systems. Some factors such as noise, temperature, humidity, metal, or water can affect the performance and quality of RFID signals. Some sources such as other RFID systems, wireless devices, or malicious jamming can cause collisions, corruption, or blocking of RFID messages. For example, a false authentication can occur when a RFID tag is damaged, obscured, or out of range of a RFID reader .
- RFID false authentication can be prevented or mitigated by using various methods, such as:
  - The design and evaluation of RFID authentication protocols. Some protocols may use cryptographic techniques such as encryption, digital signatures, or hash functions to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity of RFID messages. Some protocols may use challenge-response, mutual authentication, or session keys to prevent replay, spoofing, or modification attacks. For example, HB+ is a protocol that uses a lightweight symmetric-key algorithm and a random challenge to authenticate RFID tags.
  - The selection and configuration of RFID tags and readers. Some tags may have higher memory, power, or computation capabilities that enable the use of more secure algorithms or random numbers. Some readers may have higher range, accuracy, or reliability that improve the communication and verification of RFID messages. For example, a RFID tag can be selected based on its security level, data capacity, and compatibility with the RFID reader.
  - The deployment and management of RFID systems. Some factors such as location, orientation, distance, or shielding can affect the optimal placement and alignment of RFID tags and readers. Some sources such as encryption, frequency hopping, or error correction can improve the robustness and resilience of RFID signals. For example, a RFID system can be deployed and managed based on its operational requirements, environmental conditions, and interference sources .
