### Big Data challenges and requirements coming from different SmartCity applications

- Smart city applications are those that use data and technology to improve the quality of life, efficiency of services, and sustainability of urban environments .
- Some examples of smart city applications are traffic management, waste management, energy management, public safety, health care, education, and tourism.
- Big data is the term used to describe the large, complex, and diverse datasets that are generated by smart city applications, as well as other sources such as social media, sensors, mobile devices, and satellites  .
- Big data has the potential to provide valuable insights and solutions for smart city applications, by enabling data-driven decision making, real-time monitoring, predictive analytics, and optimization   .
- However, big data also poses many challenges and requirements for smart city applications, such as    :
  - Data quality: ensuring the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and timeliness of the data collected and processed.
  - Data integration: combining and harmonizing data from different sources, formats, and domains.
  - Data security: protecting the data from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure, and ensuring the privacy and confidentiality of the data subjects.
  - Data governance: establishing the roles, responsibilities, policies, and standards for the management and use of the data, and ensuring the compliance with legal and ethical regulations.
  - Data storage: providing sufficient and scalable storage capacity and infrastructure for the data, and ensuring the availability and reliability of the data.
  - Data processing: applying appropriate methods and techniques for the analysis, mining, and visualization of the data, and ensuring the performance and scalability of the data processing systems.
  - Data sharing: facilitating the access and exchange of data among different stakeholders, such as government agencies, private sector, academia, and citizens, and ensuring the interoperability and compatibility of the data.
  - Data value: extracting and delivering useful and actionable information and knowledge from the data, and ensuring the relevance and impact of the data for smart city applications.
