### Improving Data and Service Interoperability with Structure, Compliance, Conformance and Context Awareness

- Interoperability is the ability of different systems, devices, or applications to exchange and use data or services without requiring coordination or agreement.
- Interoperability is essential for the Internet of Things (IoT), which involves a large number of heterogeneous and distributed entities that need to communicate and cooperate to achieve common goals.
- Interoperability can be achieved at different levels, such as syntactic, semantic, or pragmatic.
- Syntactic interoperability refers to the common format and structure of the data or messages exchanged.
- Semantic interoperability refers to the common meaning and interpretation of the data or messages exchanged.
- Pragmatic interoperability refers to the common understanding and expectations of the behavior and functionality of the services exchanged.
- Interoperability can be improved by using the following concepts:
  - Structure: the organization and representation of the data or services, such as using XML, JSON, RDF, or REST.
  - Compliance: the adherence to a set of rules or standards that define the syntax and semantics of the data or services, such as using XML Schema, JSON Schema, RDF Schema, or WSDL.
  - Conformance: the degree of similarity or compatibility between the data or services and a given compliance specification, such as using validation tools or testing methods.
  - Context awareness: the ability to adapt the data or services to the specific situation and environment of the interaction, such as using metadata, annotations, or ontologies.
- The chapter proposes an architectural style, Structural Services, which combines the service modelling capabilities of SOA with the flexibility of structured resources in REST.
- Structural Services aim to enhance interoperability in the IoT by addressing the following challenges:
  - Scalability: by using REST principles and stateless interactions.
  - Heterogeneity: by using compliance and conformance (less restrictive than schema sharing) and context awareness (which increases the range of applicability).
  - Dynamism: by using self-descriptive and discoverable resources and services.
  - Security: by using standard protocols and mechanisms such as HTTPS, OAuth, or JWT.
- The chapter also presents some examples and applications of Structural Services in the IoT domain, such as smart cities, smart homes, or smart health.
