# GenomicsDriven Wellness Tracking and Management System (GO-WELL)

- GO-WELL is a concept of a health information system that uses genomic variation data, clinical, environmental, and behavioural observations to provide predictive and personalized care for patients .
- GO-WELL aims to engage patients in their own wellness management by providing them with feedback, recommendations, and alerts based on their genomic profile and lifestyle factors .
- GO-WELL consists of four main components: a genomic data repository, a wellness tracking and management engine, a web-based user interface, and a mobile application .
- The genomic data repository stores the genomic variation data of the patients, along with their clinical and demographic information, in a secure and standardized format .
- The wellness tracking and management engine analyzes the genomic data and integrates it with environmental and behavioural data collected from sensors and self-reports, to generate wellness scores, risk assessments, and personalized interventions for each patient .
- The web-based user interface allows the patients and their healthcare providers to access and visualize their genomic and wellness data, as well as to receive and provide feedback and guidance .
- The mobile application enables the patients to monitor their wellness status, receive notifications and reminders, and interact with the system and their healthcare providers anytime and anywhere .
- GO-WELL is designed to be scalable, interoperable, and adaptable to different diseases and populations, as well as to incorporate new genomic and environmental data sources and analytical methods .
- GO-WELL is demonstrated in the context of prostate cancer, a common and complex disease that is influenced by both genetic and environmental factors .
- GO-WELL can help prostate cancer patients to understand their genomic risk, to track their disease progression and response to treatment, and to adopt preventive and therapeutic strategies that are tailored to their individual needs .
