### Potential stakeholders and their complex relationships to data and analytics applications

- Stakeholders are individuals or groups that have an interest or influence in the data and analytics (D&A) activities of an organization or a project.
- Stakeholders can have different roles, responsibilities, expectations, and needs regarding the D&A applications, processes, and outcomes.
- Stakeholders can also have different levels of power, interest, and influence over the D&A activities, which can affect their engagement and support.
- Stakeholder management is the process of identifying, analyzing, communicating, and managing the stakeholder relationships in a D&A project or initiative.
- Stakeholder management can help to align the D&A objectives with the stakeholder expectations, increase the stakeholder buy-in and trust, and resolve any conflicts or issues that may arise.
- Some of the common types of stakeholders in D&A projects or initiatives are:

  - Data owners: They are the ones who own or have the authority over the data sources that are used or generated by the D&A applications. They are responsible for ensuring the quality, security, and availability of the data. They may also have the rights to grant or deny access to the data to other stakeholders.
  - Data providers: They are the ones who collect, produce, or supply the data that are used or generated by the D&A applications. They may be internal or external to the organization, such as employees, customers, suppliers, partners, or third-party vendors. They may have contractual or legal obligations to provide the data in a timely, accurate, and consistent manner.
  - Data consumers: They are the ones who use or benefit from the data or the insights derived from the D&A applications. They may be internal or external to the organization, such as managers, analysts, decision-makers, regulators, or the public. They may have different needs, preferences, and expectations regarding the data quality, format, delivery, and interpretation.
  - Data analysts: They are the ones who perform the D&A activities, such as data cleaning, processing, modeling, visualization, and interpretation. They may use various tools, techniques, and methods to analyze the data and generate insights. They may also communicate the results and recommendations to other stakeholders, such as data consumers or data owners.
  - Data stewards: They are the ones who oversee and coordinate the D&A activities, such as data governance, data quality, data security, data ethics, and data compliance. They may establish and enforce the policies, standards, and best practices for the D&A applications. They may also monitor and evaluate the performance and impact of the D&A applications.
  - Data sponsors: They are the ones who initiate, fund, or support the D&A projects or initiatives. They may be senior executives, business leaders, or external stakeholders, such as investors, donors, or regulators. They may have strategic goals, visions, or interests that are aligned with or influenced by the D&A outcomes. They may also provide the resources, guidance, and approval for the D&A activities.

- The relationships among the stakeholders can be complex, dynamic, and interdependent, as they may have different or conflicting interests, perspectives, and expectations regarding the D&A applications, processes, and outcomes.
- The relationships can also be influenced by various factors, such as the organizational culture, the D&A maturity, the D&A value proposition, the D&A ethics, and the D&A risks.
- The relationships can also change over time, as the D&A activities evolve, the D&A outcomes emerge, and the stakeholder feedback is collected and incorporated.
- Therefore, it is important to identify, understand, and manage the stakeholder relationships in a systematic and proactive way, to ensure the success and sustainability of the D&A projects or initiatives.
