### Building a useful understanding of a social network for the notes of the Unit 5 - SUSTAINABILITY DATA AND ANALYTICS in the subject of KOT 061 BIG DATA ANALYTICS FOR IOT KCS

- A social network is a set of actors (such as individuals, organizations, or devices) and the relationships among them (such as friendship, collaboration, or communication).
- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying the structure, function, and dynamics of social networks using mathematical and graphical tools.
- SNA can help to understand how social networks influence the behavior, decisions, and outcomes of actors, and how they can be leveraged to achieve desired goals.
- SNA can also help to identify the key actors, groups, and positions in a network, and measure their centrality, influence, and diversity.
- SNA can be applied to various domains of sustainability data and analytics, such as:
  - Environmental sustainability: SNA can reveal how environmental resources and information flow through a network, and how they affect the environmental attitudes, behaviors, and outcomes of actors.
  - Economic sustainability: SNA can analyze the consumption habits and economic tendencies of actors based on their social network data, and help to design more efficient and effective marketing strategies .
  - Social sustainability: SNA can examine the social cohesion, trust, and well-being of actors in a network, and how they are influenced by their social ties and interactions.
- SNA can also benefit from the availability of big data, especially from social media platforms, which provide rich and diverse sources of information about the actors and their relationships in a network.
- Big data can enable SNA to capture the complexity, dynamics, and diversity of social networks, and to generate more accurate and timely insights and predictions.
- However, big data also poses some challenges and limitations for SNA, such as:
  - Data quality: Big data may contain noise, errors, biases, or missing values that can affect the validity and reliability of SNA results.
  - Data privacy: Big data may involve sensitive or personal information that can raise ethical and legal issues regarding the consent, protection, and ownership of data.
  - Data analysis: Big data may require advanced and scalable techniques and tools to process, store, and visualize large and complex datasets.
