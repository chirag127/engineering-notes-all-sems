# KCS

KCS stands for Knowledge-Centered Service, which is a methodology for creating and maintaining documentation as part of the support process. KCS aims to leverage the existing knowledge of IT teams and make it accessible to employees, customers, and other engineers. Some of the benefits of KCS are:

- Improved customer satisfaction and loyalty by providing faster and more accurate solutions.
- Reduced costs and workload for the service desk by avoiding duplication and rework.
- Enhanced employee engagement and retention by empowering them to share and reuse knowledge.
- Increased organizational learning and innovation by capturing and analyzing feedback and trends.

KCS is based on four principles:

- Integrate: Knowledge creation and maintenance should be integrated into the support workflow, not a separate activity.
- Evolve: Knowledge should be continuously improved and updated based on demand and usage.
- Collaborate: Knowledge should be shared and reused across teams and channels, not siloed or hoarded.
- Reward: Knowledge workers should be recognized and rewarded for their contributions and outcomes, not activities.

KCS follows a double-loop process that consists of two phases:

- Solve: This phase involves solving the customer's issue and capturing the knowledge in a structured format. The knowledge article should include the issue, environment, cause, and resolution. The article should also be flagged for review if it is new or incomplete.
- Evolve: This phase involves reviewing, improving, and publishing the knowledge articles based on quality and relevance. The articles should be validated, edited, and linked to other related articles. The articles should also be monitored for feedback and usage patterns.

KCS is supported by a set of practices and techniques that help implement the methodology effectively. Some of these are:

- KCS Roles: KCS defines four roles for knowledge workers: Contributor, Publisher, Coach, and Leader. Each role has different responsibilities and permissions for creating and managing knowledge.
- KCS Licensing: KCS requires knowledge workers to obtain a license that certifies their skills and competencies in KCS. There are two levels of license: KCS Candidate and KCS Certified.
- KCS Loop: KCS uses a loop diagram to illustrate the flow of knowledge from capture to reuse. The loop has four stages: Capture, Structure, Reuse, and Improve.
- KCS Article Quality Index (AQI): KCS uses a set of criteria to measure the quality of knowledge articles. The criteria are: Content, Structure, Findability, and Usefulness.
- KCS Article Lifecycle: KCS uses a set of statuses to indicate the maturity and visibility of knowledge articles. The statuses are: Draft, Internal, External, and Archived.
