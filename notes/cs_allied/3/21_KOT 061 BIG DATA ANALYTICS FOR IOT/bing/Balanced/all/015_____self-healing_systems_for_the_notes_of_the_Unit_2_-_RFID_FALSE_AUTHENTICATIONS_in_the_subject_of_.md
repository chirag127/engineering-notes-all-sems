### Self-healing systems for the notes of the Unit 2 - RFID False Authentications in the subject of KOT 061 Big Data Analytics for IoT KCS

- Self-healing systems are systems that can detect and recover from faults, errors, or failures automatically, without human intervention.
- Self-healing systems are desirable for IoT applications, especially for RFID systems, which are prone to false authentications, data corruption, and malicious attacks.
- RFID false authentication is a type of attack where an unauthorized RFID tag or reader pretends to be a legitimate one, and gains access to sensitive data or resources.
- RFID false authentication can compromise the security, privacy, and integrity of the RFID system and the data it collects and processes.
- Self-healing systems can prevent or mitigate RFID false authentication by using various techniques, such as:

  - Anomaly detection: identifying abnormal or suspicious behavior or patterns in the RFID system, such as unexpected tag or reader responses, high error rates, or unusual data values.
  - Fault diagnosis: locating and isolating the source and cause of the fault or error in the RFID system, such as a faulty tag or reader, a corrupted data packet, or a malicious attack.
  - Fault recovery: restoring the normal operation and functionality of the RFID system, such as repairing or replacing the faulty tag or reader, retransmitting or correcting the corrupted data packet, or blocking or removing the malicious attack.
  - Fault prevention: avoiding or reducing the occurrence or impact of faults or errors in the RFID system, such as using encryption, authentication, or verification mechanisms, updating or patching the system software or firmware, or improving the system design or configuration.

- Self-healing systems can use different methods or algorithms to implement these techniques, such as:

  - Rule-based methods: using predefined rules or policies to detect, diagnose, recover, or prevent faults or errors in the RFID system, such as checking the validity or consistency of the tag or reader ID, data, or protocol.
  - Machine learning methods: using data-driven or model-based approaches to learn from the RFID system data or behavior, and to adapt to the changing environment or conditions, such as using clustering, classification, or regression techniques to identify anomalies, faults, or attacks, or using reinforcement learning, genetic algorithms, or neural networks to optimize the system performance or resilience.
  - Hybrid methods: combining rule-based and machine learning methods to leverage the advantages of both, such as using rules to filter out noise or outliers, and using machine learning to handle uncertainty or complexity.

- Self-healing systems can improve the reliability, availability, and robustness of the RFID system, and enhance the security, privacy, and integrity of the RFID data and applications.
