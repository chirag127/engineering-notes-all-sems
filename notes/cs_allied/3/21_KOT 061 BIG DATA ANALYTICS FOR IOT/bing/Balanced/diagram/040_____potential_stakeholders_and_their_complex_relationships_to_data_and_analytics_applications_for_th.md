### Potential stakeholders and their complex relationships to data and analytics applications

- Stakeholders are individuals or groups that have an interest or influence in the data and analytics (D&A) activities of an organization or a project.
- Stakeholders can have different roles, responsibilities, expectations, and needs regarding the D&A applications, processes, and outcomes.
- Stakeholders can also have different levels of power, interest, and influence over the D&A activities, which can affect their engagement and support.
- Stakeholder management is the process of identifying, analyzing, communicating, and engaging with stakeholders to ensure the success and sustainability of the D&A activities.
- Stakeholder management involves understanding the stakeholder's perspective, aligning the D&A objectives with their needs and expectations, and delivering value and insights to them.
- Stakeholder management also involves managing the potential conflicts, risks, and challenges that may arise from the complex relationships among the stakeholders and the D&A activities.
- Some of the common types of stakeholders in D&A activities are:

  - Data providers: They are the sources of data that are used for the D&A applications. They can be internal or external to the organization, such as sensors, devices, databases, systems, or third-party data providers. They have an interest in the quality, security, and privacy of the data they provide, and the benefits they receive from sharing the data.
  - Data analysts: They are the ones who perform the D&A applications, such as data collection, processing, analysis, visualization, and reporting. They can be internal or external to the organization, such as employees, consultants, or contractors. They have an interest in the availability, accessibility, and usability of the data, and the tools and methods they use for the D&A applications.
  - Data consumers: They are the ones who use the results and insights from the D&A applications for decision-making and action. They can be internal or external to the organization, such as managers, executives, customers, partners, or regulators. They have an interest in the accuracy, reliability, and relevance of the D&A outputs, and the value and impact they create for them.
  - Data owners: They are the ones who have the authority and responsibility for the data and the D&A activities. They can be internal or external to the organization, such as business units, departments, or data governance bodies. They have an interest in the strategic alignment, compliance, and performance of the D&A activities, and the risks and benefits they entail for the organization.

- The relationships among the stakeholders can be complex and dynamic, depending on the context, scope, and objectives of the D&A activities. Some of the factors that can affect the relationships are:

  - The level of dependency and interdependency among the stakeholders, such as the degree to which they rely on each other for the D&A activities, and the degree to which they affect each other's outcomes and interests.
  - The level of alignment and misalignment among the stakeholders, such as the degree to which they share the same vision, goals, and expectations for the D&A activities, and the degree to which they have conflicting or competing interests and needs.
  - The level of trust and distrust among the stakeholders, such as the degree to which they have confidence, respect, and credibility for each other, and the degree to which they have doubts, concerns, or suspicions about each other.
  - The level of engagement and disengagement among the stakeholders, such as the degree to which they are involved, informed, and consulted for the D&A activities, and the degree to which they are ignored, excluded, or resisted by the D&A activities.

- The complexity of the stakeholder relationships can pose challenges and opportunities for the D&A activities, such as:

  - Challenges: The stakeholder relationships can create barriers, conflicts, or risks for the D&A activities, such as data quality issues, data access limitations, data privacy concerns, data ownership disputes, data analysis errors, data interpretation biases, data communication gaps, data value disagreements, or data impact uncertainties.
  - Opportunities: The stakeholder relationships can create enablers, synergies, or benefits for the D&A activities, such as data sharing incentives, data integration solutions, data security measures, data governance frameworks, data analysis methods, data visualization techniques, data storytelling skills, data value propositions, or data impact assessments.
