### Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA measures and maps the flow of relationships and relationship changes between knowledge-possessing entities, such as individuals, groups, organizations, or nations.
- SNA is based on theoretical constructs of sociology and mathematical foundations of graph theory.
- SNA is useful for examining the social structure and interdependencies (or work patterns) of individuals or organizations.
- SNA can be applied to various domains, such as health, education, business, politics, or the internet.
- SNA can help identify influential actors, key connectors, subgroups, communities, or network properties, such as density, centrality, or cohesion.
- SNA can also help understand how social networks affect behaviors, outcomes, or diffusion of information, innovations, or diseases.
- SNA requires data collection, network representation, network analysis, and network visualization.
- SNA can use different types of data, such as surveys, interviews, observations, digital traces, or archival records.
- SNA can represent networks using nodes (entities) and ties (relationships) in graphs, matrices, or tables.
- SNA can analyze networks using various methods, such as descriptive statistics, network models, or network algorithms.
- SNA can visualize networks using different layouts, colors, shapes, or labels to highlight network features or patterns.
