### Building a useful understanding of a social network

- A social network is a set of actors (individuals, groups, organizations, etc.) and the relationships or interactions among them.
- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA can help us understand how social networks influence health, behavior, communication, innovation, collaboration, and other outcomes.
- SNA can also be applied to networks outside of the societal realm, such as the Internet, biological systems, transportation networks, etc .
- To conduct SNA, we need to collect data on the nodes (actors) and the ties (relationships) that form the network, and represent them as a graph or a matrix.
- We can then use various methods and metrics to analyze the properties and patterns of the network, such as centrality, density, clustering, distance, etc.
- We can also use visualization tools to display the network graphically and identify its features and characteristics.
- SNA can help us answer questions such as: Who are the most influential or central actors in the network? How cohesive or fragmented is the network? How does information or resources flow in the network? How does the network evolve over time? How does the network affect or respond to external events or interventions?
- SNA can provide useful insights for decision making, policy making, intervention design, evaluation, and optimization in various domains and contexts.
