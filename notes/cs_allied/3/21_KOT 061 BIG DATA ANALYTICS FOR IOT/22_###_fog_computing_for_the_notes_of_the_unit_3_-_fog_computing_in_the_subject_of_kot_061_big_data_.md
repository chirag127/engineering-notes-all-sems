### Fog Computing for the notes of the Unit 3 - FOG COMPUTING in the subject of KOT 061 BIG DATA ANALYTICS FOR IOT KCS

Fog computing, also known as fog networking or edge computing, is a distributed computing architecture that extends cloud computing to the edge of a network. Fog computing is designed to address the challenges of IoT by providing a local, low-latency, and high-bandwidth computing infrastructure that is closer to the devices and sensors that generate and collect data.

In a traditional cloud computing architecture, data from IoT devices is transmitted to a central cloud server for processing and storage. However, this approach can result in significant latency and bandwidth constraints, as well as privacy and security concerns, particularly for IoT devices that are located in remote or low-bandwidth environments.

Fog computing addresses these challenges by providing a distributed computing infrastructure that is located closer to the edge of the network, where IoT devices are located. In a fog computing architecture, data is processed and stored locally, at the edge of the network, rather than being transmitted to a central cloud server. This can result in lower latency and higher bandwidth, as well as improved privacy and security, as data is processed and stored closer to the source.

In the context of KOT 061 Big Data Analytics for IoT, it is important to understand the role and benefits of fog computing in the context of IoT, and to be familiar with the key concepts and technologies associated with fog computing. This may include understanding the architecture and components of a fog computing system, as well as the data processing and analysis techniques that are used in fog computing environments.

Overall, fog computing is an important aspect of IoT, and is critical to the success of big data analytics for IoT. Understanding the role and benefits of fog computing is an important aspect of the study of KOT 061 Big Data Analytics for IoT.
