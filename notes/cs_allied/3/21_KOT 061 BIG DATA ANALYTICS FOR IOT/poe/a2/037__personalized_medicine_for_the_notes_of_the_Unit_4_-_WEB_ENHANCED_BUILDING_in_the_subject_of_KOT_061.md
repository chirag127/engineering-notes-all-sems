### Personalized Medicine

- Personalized medicine is a medical model that proposes the customization of healthcare, with all decisions and practices being tailored to individual patients based on their predicted response or risk of disease.
- It aims to better target intervention to the right patient at the right time.
- It is enabled by recent advances in genomics, proteomics, and bioinformatics that provide improved understanding of the molecular basis of health and disease.
- The key to personalized medicine is the ability to classify individuals into subpopulations that differ in their susceptibility to a particular disease or their response to a specific treatment.
- This enables the prescription of specific therapies targeted to the needs of individual patients based on their predicted response.
- Personalized healthcare has the potential to improve treatment outcomes, but it also raises many ethical issues surrounding privacy, access, and cost.
- Some of the applications of personalized medicine include:
-- Cancer treatment using targeted drug therapies based on tumor genotyping
-- Psychiatric drug prescription based on genetic tests
-- Disease risk assessment using genetic tests and family history
-- Nutritional advice based on genetic variants influencing metabolism
