## Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems

Distributed systems are computer systems that consist of multiple autonomous computers that communicate through a network to achieve a common goal. The goal of Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems is to provide an overview of the fundamental concepts and techniques used to design, implement, and evaluate distributed systems. 

This unit covers the following topics:
1. Characteristics of distributed systems 
2. Types of distributed systems 
3. Communication in distributed systems 
4. Distributed algorithms 
5. Fault tolerance and reliability in distributed systems
6. Security in distributed systems 

Distributed systems have several unique characteristics that set them apart from traditional centralized systems. These characteristics include distribution transparency, scalability, heterogeneity, and concurrent processing. 

There are several types of distributed systems, including client-server, peer-to-peer, and grid computing. Each type has its own unique architecture, communication patterns, and trade-offs. 

Communication in distributed systems is a critical component and can be achieved through various communication protocols and message-passing techniques. 

Distributed algorithms are used to coordinate and synchronize the actions of multiple nodes in a distributed system. These algorithms can be used for tasks such as resource allocation, data consistency, and fault tolerance.

Fault tolerance and reliability are important concerns in distributed systems, as failures can occur at any node. Strategies for achieving fault tolerance and reliability include replication, checkpointing, and voting algorithms. 

Finally, security is a crucial aspect of distributed systems, as they are vulnerable to various types of attacks such as denial-of-service and eavesdropping. Secure communication and authentication mechanisms are used to protect against these attacks.
