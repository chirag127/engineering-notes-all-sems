## Unit 9 - Distributed Transactions

Distributed transactions are a type of transaction that involves multiple nodes in a distributed system. Distributed transactions allow for the coordination of multiple nodes to ensure that all transactions are either fully committed or fully rolled back in the event of a failure.

There are several key concepts related to distributed transactions, including:

1. Atomicity: Atomicity ensures that all transactions are either fully committed or fully rolled back, even in the event of a failure.

2. Consistency: Consistency ensures that the system remains in a consistent state, even in the presence of failures.

3. Isolation: Isolation ensures that transactions are isolated from each other, even in the presence of failures.

4. Durability: Durability ensures that committed transactions are permanent, even in the event of a failure.

There are several types of protocols used for distributed transactions, including:

1. Two-phase commit: Two-phase commit is a protocol used to ensure that all nodes in a distributed system agree on the outcome of a transaction, and that all transactions are either fully committed or fully rolled back in the event of a failure.

2. Three-phase commit: Three-phase commit is a protocol used to ensure that all nodes in a distributed system agree on the outcome of a transaction, and that all transactions are either fully committed or fully rolled back in the event of a failure.

The choice of protocol will depend on the specific requirements of the system, including the number of nodes, the level of fault tolerance required, and the type of failures that the system must be able to tolerate.

In conclusion, distributed transactions are a type of transaction that involves multiple nodes in a distributed system and allow for the coordination of multiple nodes to ensure that all transactions are either fully committed or fully rolled back in the event of a failure. The key concepts related to distributed transactions include atomicity, consistency, isolation, and durability. There are several types of protocols used for distributed transactions, including two-phase commit and three-phase commit, and the choice of protocol will depend on the specific requirements of the system.
