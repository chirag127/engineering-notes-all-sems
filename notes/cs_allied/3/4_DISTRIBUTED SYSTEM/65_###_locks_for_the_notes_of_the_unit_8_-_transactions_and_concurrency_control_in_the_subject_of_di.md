### Locks for the notes of the Unit 8 - Transactions and Concurrency Control in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Locks are a mechanism used in distributed systems to control access to shared resources. They are used to enforce mutual exclusion, ensuring that only one process can access a shared resource at a time.

There are two main types of locks:
1. Exclusive locks: also known as write locks, exclusive locks allow only one process to access a shared resource at a time.
2. Shared locks: also known as read locks, shared locks allow multiple processes to access a shared resource simultaneously, but prevent any of them from modifying the resource.

Locks are used in distributed systems to ensure that concurrent access to shared resources does not result in data inconsistencies or corruption. They are critical for ensuring the consistency and integrity of data in a distributed system.

In summary, locks are a mechanism used in distributed systems to control access to shared resources. They enforce mutual exclusion and ensure that only one process can access a shared resource at a time. There are two main types of locks: exclusive locks and shared locks. Locks are critical for ensuring the consistency and integrity of data in a distributed system.
