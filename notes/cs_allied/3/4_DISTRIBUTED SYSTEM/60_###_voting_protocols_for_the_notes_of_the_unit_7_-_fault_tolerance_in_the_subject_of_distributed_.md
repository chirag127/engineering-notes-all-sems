### Voting protocols for the notes of the Unit 7 - Fault Tolerance in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Voting protocols are a type of fault tolerance mechanism used in distributed systems to ensure that the system continues to operate correctly in the event of failures. Voting protocols are used to ensure that the system reaches a consensus on the outcome of a decision, even if one or more nodes in the system fail.

There are several types of voting protocols used in distributed systems, including:

1. Majority voting: Majority voting is a simple voting protocol in which a decision is made based on the majority vote of the nodes in the system.

2. Byzantine fault tolerance: Byzantine fault tolerance is a type of voting protocol that can tolerate arbitrary failures, including malicious failures. Byzantine fault tolerance is used to ensure that the system reaches a consensus on the outcome of a decision, even if one or more nodes in the system fail in arbitrary ways.

3. Weighted voting: Weighted voting is a type of voting protocol in which each node in the system is assigned a weight, and the outcome of a decision is based on the weighted majority vote of the nodes.

4. Quorum voting: Quorum voting is a type of voting protocol in which a decision is made based on the agreement of a subset of the nodes in the system, known as a quorum.

The choice of voting protocol will depend on the specific requirements of the system, including the number of nodes, the level of fault tolerance required, and the type of failures that the system must be able to tolerate.
