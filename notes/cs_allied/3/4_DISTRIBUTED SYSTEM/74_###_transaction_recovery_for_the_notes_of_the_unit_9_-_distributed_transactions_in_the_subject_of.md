### Transaction recovery for the notes of the Unit 9 - Distributed Transactions in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Unit 9 - Distributed Transactions covers the concept of transaction recovery, which is the process of restoring the system to a consistent state after a failure. Transaction recovery is critical in a distributed system, as it ensures that the system remains consistent and available, even in the face of failures.

The following are key aspects of transaction recovery:

1. Logging: Logging is the process of recording the actions of a transaction in a log. The log is used to recover the system after a failure.

2. Checkpointing: Checkpointing is the process of periodically saving the state of the system to disk. Checkpointing is used to speed up recovery after a failure.

3. Rollback: Rollback is the process of undoing the actions of a transaction that has failed. Rollback is used to restore the system to a consistent state after a failure.

4. Redo: Redo is the process of re-executing the actions of a transaction that has failed. Redo is used to restore the system to a consistent state after a failure.

5. Consistency: Transaction recovery ensures that the system remains in a consistent state, even in the face of failures.

By understanding transaction recovery, organizations can effectively implement distributed transactions that are reliable and maintain consistency, even in the face of failures. Unit 9 - Distributed Transactions covers the use of transaction recovery, including logging, checkpointing, rollback, redo, and consistency, in distributed systems.
