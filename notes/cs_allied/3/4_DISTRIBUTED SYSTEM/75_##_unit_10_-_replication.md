## Unit 10 - Replication

Replication is a technique used in distributed systems to ensure that data is available and accessible even in the event of failures. Replication involves creating multiple copies of data and distributing them across multiple nodes in a distributed system.

There are several types of replication, including:

1. Active-Passive Replication: Active-Passive replication involves having a primary node that is responsible for processing requests, and one or more secondary nodes that are used for failover in the event of a failure.

2. Active-Active Replication: Active-Active replication involves having multiple nodes that are all processing requests, and that can take over in the event of a failure.

3. Master-Slave Replication: Master-Slave replication involves having a primary node that is responsible for processing requests, and one or more secondary nodes that are used for backup and disaster recovery.

The choice of replication technique will depend on the specific requirements of the system, including the volume and velocity of the data, the level of fault tolerance required, and the type of failures that the system must be able to tolerate.

In conclusion, Replication is a technique used in distributed systems to ensure that data is available and accessible even in the event of failures. There are several types of replication, including Active-Passive Replication, Active-Active Replication, and Master-Slave Replication, and the choice of replication technique will depend on the specific requirements of the system.
