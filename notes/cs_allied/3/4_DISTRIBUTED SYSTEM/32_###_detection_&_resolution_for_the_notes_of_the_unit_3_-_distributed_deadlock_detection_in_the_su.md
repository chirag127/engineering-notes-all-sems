### detection & resolution for the notes of the Unit 3 - Distributed Deadlock Detection in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Distributed deadlocks are a common problem in distributed systems, where multiple processes or threads are executing concurrently on different nodes. Deadlocks occur when two or more processes are waiting for each other to release a resource, resulting in a standstill.

Unit 3 - Distributed Deadlock Detection covers the following topics related to deadlock detection and resolution in distributed systems:

1. Deadlock detection algorithms: An overview of algorithms for detecting deadlocks in a distributed system, including wait-for graphs, resource allocation graphs, and time-stamping algorithms.
2. Deadlock prevention: Techniques for preventing deadlocks in a distributed system, including resource ordering, timeout-based algorithms, and deadlock-free algorithms.
3. Deadlock resolution: Techniques for resolving deadlocks in a distributed system, including aborting one or more processes, rolling back transactions, and releasing resources.
4. Performance evaluation: An overview of how to evaluate the performance of deadlock detection and resolution algorithms in a distributed system.

It is important to note that deadlocks are a complex and challenging problem in distributed systems, and the unit only covers the fundamentals. Further study and hands-on experience are necessary to gain a deeper understanding of the subject.
