### Theoretical Foundation for Distributed System for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Theoretical Foundation for Distributed Systems:
1. Characterization of Distributed Systems:
   a. Definition: A collection of independent and autonomous computing devices that work together to achieve a common goal.
   b. Key features: Concurrent processing, resource sharing, communication, and distribution of tasks.
   c. Types: Client-server, peer-to-peer, and hybrid.
2. Models of Distributed Systems:
   a. Architectural Models: Layered, Pipes and Filters, and Event-based.
   b. Computational Models: Message passing, shared memory, and remote procedure call (RPC).
   c. Consistency Models: Strong consistency, eventual consistency, causal consistency.
3. Fundamental Issues in Distributed Systems:
   a. Transparency: Location, migration, replication, and concurrency.
   b. Scalability: Horizontal and vertical scalability.
   c. Fault tolerance: Types of faults, fault detection, and fault tolerance techniques.
4. Design principles for Distributed Systems:
   a. Modularity: Decomposing the system into smaller, independent components.
   b. Decentralization: Allowing for autonomous decision-making by individual components.
   c. Coordination: Managing interactions between components to ensure consistency.
5. Algorithms for Distributed Systems:
   a. Distributed algorithms for consensus, leader election, and clock synchronization.
   b. Distributed algorithms for fault tolerance, such as replication and Byzantine fault tolerance.
