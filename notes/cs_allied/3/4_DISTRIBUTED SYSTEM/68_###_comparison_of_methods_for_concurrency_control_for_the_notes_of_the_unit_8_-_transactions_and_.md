### Comparison of methods for concurrency control for the notes of the Unit 8 - Transactions and Concurrency Control in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Comparison of methods for concurrency control in distributed systems involves evaluating different techniques for controlling concurrent access to shared resources. Some common methods for concurrency control include:

1. Locking: a mechanism for controlling access to shared resources by granting exclusive or shared locks to processes.

2. Timestamp ordering: a mechanism for controlling access to shared resources by assigning a timestamp to each transaction and ordering the execution of transactions based on their timestamps.

3. Optimistic concurrency control: a mechanism for controlling access to shared resources by allowing multiple transactions to access a shared resource simultaneously, and detecting and resolving conflicts at commit time.

4. Serializability: a mechanism for controlling access to shared resources by ensuring that the execution of transactions appears to be serial, even though they may be executed concurrently.

Each method for concurrency control has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of method depends on the specific requirements of the system.

In summary, Comparison of methods for concurrency control in distributed systems involves evaluating different techniques for controlling concurrent access to shared resources. Some common methods include locking, timestamp ordering, optimistic concurrency control, and serializability. The choice of method depends on the specific requirements of the system.
