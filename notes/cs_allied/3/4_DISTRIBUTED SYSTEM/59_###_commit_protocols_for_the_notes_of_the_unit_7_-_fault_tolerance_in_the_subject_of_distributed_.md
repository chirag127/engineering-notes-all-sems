### Commit Protocols for the notes of the Unit 7 - Fault Tolerance in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Commit protocols are a key component of fault tolerance in distributed systems. Commit protocols ensure that transactions are executed correctly and consistently, even in the event of failures.

There are several types of commit protocols used in distributed systems, including:

1. Two-Phase Commit (2PC): 2PC is a protocol for ensuring that a transaction is executed correctly and consistently, even in the event of failures. 2PC involves two phases: a preparation phase and a commit phase.

2. Three-Phase Commit (3PC): 3PC is a protocol for ensuring that a transaction is executed correctly and consistently, even in the event of failures. 3PC involves three phases: a preparation phase, an agreement phase, and a commit phase.

3. Distributed Commit Protocol (DCP): DCP is a protocol for ensuring that a transaction is executed correctly and consistently, even in the event of failures. DCP involves multiple phases, and is designed to be scalable and efficient.

The choice of commit protocol will depend on the specific requirements of the system, including the level of fault tolerance required, the performance requirements, and the complexity of the system.
