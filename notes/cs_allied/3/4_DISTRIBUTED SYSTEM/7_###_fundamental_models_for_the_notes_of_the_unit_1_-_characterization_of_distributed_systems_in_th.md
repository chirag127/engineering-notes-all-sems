### Fundamental Models for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Fundamental Models for Characterization of Distributed Systems:

1. Architectural Models: Describe the structure and organization of the system, including the components, interactions, and relationships between them.

2. Process Models: Describe the behavior of individual components, including communication and coordination mechanisms.

3. Performance Models: Analyze and predict the performance of the system, including response time, throughput, and resource utilization.

4. Failure Models: Describe the behavior of the system in the presence of failures, including fault tolerance, recovery, and consistency.

5. Security Models: Describe the security properties of the system, including confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

6. Economic Models: Analyze the cost-benefit trade-offs of different design choices, including resource allocation and pricing mechanisms.

These models are used to understand and design distributed systems, and to evaluate their properties in terms of performance, reliability, security, and cost. They are essential for making informed decisions about the design and deployment of these systems.
