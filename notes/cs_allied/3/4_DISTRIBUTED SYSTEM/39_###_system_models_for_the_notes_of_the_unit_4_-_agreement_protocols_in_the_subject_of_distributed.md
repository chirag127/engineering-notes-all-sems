### System models for the notes of the Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

System models are abstract representations of a distributed system that are used to study its behavior and design agreement protocols. The following are the key types of system models used in the study of distributed systems:

1. Synchronous Model: A synchronous model assumes that all nodes in the system have synchronized clocks and that messages are delivered in a fixed amount of time.

2. Asynchronous Model: An asynchronous model assumes that messages may be delivered in an unpredictable amount of time, and that nodes may fail or crash.

3. Partial Synchrony Model: A partial synchrony model assumes that some nodes in the system have synchronized clocks, while others do not.

4. Crash Model: A crash model assumes that nodes in the system may fail or crash, and that the system must continue to operate despite these failures.

5. Byzantine Model: A Byzantine model assumes that some nodes in the system may behave maliciously, and that the system must be able to tolerate these malicious nodes.

System models are crucial for understanding the behavior of distributed systems and for designing agreement protocols that ensure that all nodes in the system agree on the state of the system.

Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols covers the design and implementation of agreement protocols in different system models, including synchronous, asynchronous, partial synchrony, crash, and Byzantine models.
