### Interactive consistency Problem for the notes of the Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Interactive consistency is a type of consistency model used in distributed systems to ensure that all nodes have a consistent view of the system, even in the presence of network delays and node failures. The Interactive consistency problem refers to the challenge of ensuring that updates to the system are propagated in a timely and consistent manner.

There are several approaches to solving the Interactive consistency problem, including:
1. Strong consistency: ensures that all updates are immediately visible to all nodes in the system. Examples include linearizability and sequential consistency.
2. Weak consistency: allows for some degree of inconsistency, but ensures that updates eventually become consistent. Examples include eventual consistency and causal consistency.
3. Session consistency: ensures that updates are consistent within a session, but may not be immediately visible to other sessions.

In summary, the Interactive consistency problem refers to the challenge of ensuring that updates to a distributed system are propagated in a timely and consistent manner, and can be solved using strong consistency, weak consistency, or session consistency models.
