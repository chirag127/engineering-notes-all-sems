### deadlock prevention for the notes of the Unit 3 - Distributed Deadlock Detection in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Deadlock prevention is a technique to avoid deadlocks in a distributed system by ensuring that the necessary conditions for deadlocks are never met. The following are common techniques for deadlock prevention:

1. Resource ordering: resources are acquired in a specific order, ensuring that cycles cannot occur.

2. Resource allocation: resources are allocated in a way that avoids cycles, such as by allocating all resources for a process at once.

3. Time-out: processes are prevented from waiting indefinitely for a resource by setting a time-out value.

4. Preemption: processes can be interrupted and their resources can be taken away, allowing other processes to continue.

5. Detection and recovery: deadlocks can be detected and resolved using techniques such as rolling back the state of a process and releasing its resources.

6. Avoidance: resources can be acquired in such a way that deadlocks are avoided, such as by only acquiring resources that are needed for a process to complete.

In summary, deadlock prevention is a technique to avoid deadlocks in a distributed system by ensuring that the necessary conditions for deadlocks are never met. Common techniques include resource ordering, resource allocation, time-out, preemption, detection and recovery, and avoidance.
