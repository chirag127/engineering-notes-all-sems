Sure, here's a detailed explanation of Outcome (CO) and Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL):

Outcome (CO) refers to the intended result or goal of a learning experience. Outcomes are often expressed in terms of what the learner will be able to do or know after completing the learning experience. Outcomes are used to define the purpose of a learning experience and to measure the effectiveness of the learning experience.

Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL) is a categorization of learning objectives based on the type of knowledge or understanding that is being developed. The Bloom's Knowledge Level categorizes learning objectives into six levels, ranging from the lowest level of knowledge (Remembering) to the highest level of knowledge (Creating). The six levels of Bloom's Knowledge Level are:

1. Remembering: recalling previously learned information.

2. Understanding: comprehending the meaning of information.

3. Applying: using information in a new situation.

4. Analyzing: breaking down information into parts and understanding the relationships among the parts.

5. Evaluating: making judgments about the value of information.

6. Creating: putting information together to form a new whole.

In conclusion, Outcome (CO) refers to the intended result or goal of a learning experience, while Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL) is a categorization of learning objectives based on the type of knowledge or understanding that is being developed. Outcomes and Bloom's Knowledge Level are used to define the purpose of a learning experience and to measure the effectiveness of the learning experience, and to help ensure that the learning experience is aligned with the intended outcomes and is of high quality.
