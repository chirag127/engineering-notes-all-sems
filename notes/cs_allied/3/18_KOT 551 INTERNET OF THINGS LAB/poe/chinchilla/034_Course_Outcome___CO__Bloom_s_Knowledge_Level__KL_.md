# Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)

## Introduction
In academic settings, course outcomes (CO) are used to indicate the expected learning outcomes for a particular course. These outcomes are often linked to Bloom's Taxonomy, which is a framework used to classify educational goals into different levels of complexity and specificity. Bloom's Taxonomy consists of six levels, which are Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, and Evaluation. Each level builds on the previous one, leading to a more in-depth understanding of the subject matter.

## Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)
Bloom's Taxonomy's first level is Knowledge, which involves recalling information or previously learned material. The following are the key points to consider regarding the Knowledge level of Bloom's Taxonomy:

- Recognize/identify information or concepts.
- Recall information from memory.
- Understand the meaning of terms and concepts.
- List or describe facts, concepts, or procedures.
- Recognize similarities and differences between concepts.

### Example COs at Knowledge Level
- Identify and recall key terms and concepts related to the course.
- List the major events leading up to a particular historical event.
- Recognize the key characteristics of different literary genres.
- Define key concepts and principles related to the course.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, the Knowledge level of Bloom's Taxonomy is crucial for building a solid foundation of understanding in any given subject. By focusing on key concepts, terms, and facts, students can develop a strong grasp of the material, preparing them for more complex learning tasks in the future. As such, course outcomes that target the Knowledge level can be an essential tool for educators to ensure that students have a solid foundation of understanding in their course of study.
