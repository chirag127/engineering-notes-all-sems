# The Water Cycle

1. Evaporation: The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, evaporating it into water vapor which forms clouds.
2. Condensation: As the water vapor rises higher into the cooler atmosphere, it condenses into water droplets in clouds.
3. Precipitation: Water droplets become too heavy to stay suspended in air and fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail depending on temperatures.
4. Collection: Precipitation either travels over land as surface runoff into lakes, rivers, and oceans, or soaks into the ground to replenish groundwater stores. The water cycle then continues repeating itself.

The water cycle has no starting or ending point, and ensures that water is a renewable resource within the ecosystem. However, the amount of water in the cycle remains relatively constant over time.
