# CO 3 Demonstrate the ability to transmit data wirelessly between different devices. K4

- Wireless transmission involves the transmission of data over a distance without the use of wires or cables. Some common methods of wireless transmission include:
- Bluetooth: Bluetooth is a wireless technology standard for exchanging data over short distances using radio waves in the frequency range of 2.4 gigahertz from fixed and mobile devices, and building personal area networks.
- Wi-Fi: Wi-Fi is a popular wireless networking technology that allows devices to connect to the internet and network with each other using radio waves. Wi-Fi networks have a range of around 30 meters indoors and 100 meters outdoors.
- Infrared: Infrared wireless transmission uses infrared light to transmit data and is often used in remote controls. However, it requires a line of sight and has a shorter range than radio-wave-based technologies like Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.
- Radio Frequency: Radio frequency or RF wireless transmission uses radio waves to transmit data and does not require a line of sight. However, it typically has a shorter range than technologies like Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. RF is used in devices like cordless phones and wireless speakers.

The key advantages of wireless transmission are convenience and mobility. However, wireless transmission may be less secure than wired transmission and can be affected by interference from other devices and objects. The choice of wireless technology depends on factors like speed, range, cost, and security requirements.
