# Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)

- A course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what students should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course.
- A course outcome should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART).
- A course outcome should also align with the program outcomes (POs) and the graduate attributes (GAs) of the institution or accreditation body.
- Bloom's knowledge level (KL) is a classification of the cognitive domain of learning, which describes the types and levels of knowledge that students can acquire and demonstrate.
- Bloom's knowledge level consists of six categories: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create. Each category represents a different level of cognitive complexity and difficulty.
- Bloom's knowledge level can be used to design and assess course outcomes, by matching the verbs and nouns in the CO statements with the appropriate KL category.
- For example, a course outcome that states "Students will be able to explain the basic concepts and principles of thermodynamics" can be classified as an understand level outcome, because the verb explain indicates comprehension and the noun concepts indicates factual knowledge.
- Similarly, a course outcome that states "Students will be able to design and conduct experiments to test the validity of thermodynamic laws" can be classified as a create level outcome, because the verb design indicates synthesis and the noun experiments indicates procedural knowledge.
- By using Bloom's knowledge level, instructors can ensure that their course outcomes cover a range of cognitive skills and abilities, and that they are aligned with the expected learning outcomes of the course and the program.
