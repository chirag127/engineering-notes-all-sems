#### CO 3 Demonstrate the ability to transmit data wirelessly between different devices. K4

- Wireless data transmission is the process of sending and receiving information without using physical wires or cables.
- Wireless data transmission can be achieved by using electromagnetic waves, such as radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, or lasers.
- Wireless data transmission has many advantages, such as mobility, convenience, scalability, and cost-effectiveness.
- Wireless data transmission also has some challenges, such as interference, security, reliability, and power consumption.
- To transmit data wirelessly between different devices, the following steps are required:

  - The sender device encodes the data into a suitable format, such as binary, analog, or digital signals.
  - The sender device modulates the signals onto a carrier wave, which is a high-frequency electromagnetic wave that can travel through the air or space.
  - The sender device transmits the modulated signals using an antenna, which converts electrical signals into electromagnetic waves and vice versa.
  - The receiver device receives the modulated signals using another antenna, which is tuned to the same frequency as the carrier wave.
  - The receiver device demodulates the signals, which is the process of extracting the original data from the carrier wave.
  - The receiver device decodes the data into a readable format, such as text, audio, or video.

- Some examples of wireless data transmission technologies are:

  - Bluetooth: A short-range wireless technology that uses radio waves to connect devices, such as smartphones, headphones, speakers, keyboards, and mice.
  - Wi-Fi: A medium-range wireless technology that uses radio waves to create a local area network (LAN) that connects devices, such as laptops, tablets, printers, and routers.
  - Cellular: A long-range wireless technology that uses radio waves to create a wide area network (WAN) that connects devices, such as mobile phones, modems, and satellites.
  - NFC: A very short-range wireless technology that uses radio waves to enable contactless communication between devices, such as smart cards, payment terminals, and tags.
  - RFID: A short-range wireless technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects, such as products, animals, and vehicles.
  - IR: A short-range wireless technology that uses infrared light to transmit data, such as remote controls, cameras, and printers.
  - Li-Fi: A short-range wireless technology that uses visible light to transmit data, such as LED lamps, smart glasses, and sensors.
