# Area of a given shape (rectangle, triangle and circle) reading shape and appropriate values from standard

- The area of a shape is the amount of space that it covers in a two-dimensional plane.
- To calculate the area of a given shape, we need to know the shape and the appropriate values from the standard, such as length, width, base, height, or radius.
- The standard values are usually given in units of length, such as centimeters, meters, or inches.
- The area is usually expressed in units of square length, such as square centimeters, square meters, or square inches.
- The formula for the area of a shape depends on the shape. Here are some common formulas for the area of a rectangle, a triangle, and a circle:

  - Rectangle: A = lw, where A is the area, l is the length, and w is the width.
  - Triangle: A = (1/2)bh, where A is the area, b is the base, and h is the height.
  - Circle: A = pi*r^2, where A is the area, pi is a constant approximately equal to 3.14, and r is the radius.

- To find the area of a given shape, we need to read the shape and the appropriate values from the standard, and then apply the corresponding formula. For example:

  - If the shape is a rectangle with length 10 cm and width 5 cm, then the area is A = lw = 10 * 5 = 50 cm^2.
  - If the shape is a triangle with base 8 cm and height 6 cm, then the area is A = (1/2)bh = (1/2) * 8 * 6 = 24 cm^2.
  - If the shape is a circle with radius 4 cm, then the area is A = pi*r^2 = 3.14 * 4^2 = 50.24 cm^2.
