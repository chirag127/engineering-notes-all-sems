## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)

- Course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what students are expected to know, understand, or be able to do at the end of a course.
- Bloom's knowledge level (KL) is a classification of cognitive skills that students can demonstrate in relation to a given topic or domain.
- Bloom's taxonomy consists of six levels of knowledge: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Each level of knowledge requires a different type of cognitive process and evidence of learning.
- The higher the level of knowledge, the more complex and abstract the learning outcome.
- Course outcomes should be aligned with the appropriate level of knowledge according to the course objectives and assessment methods.
- Course outcomes should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART).
- Course outcomes should be written using action verbs that indicate the level of knowledge and the cognitive process involved.
- Examples of course outcomes and their corresponding levels of knowledge are:

  - CO1: Recall the basic concepts and principles of accounting. (Remember)
  - CO2: Explain the accounting cycle and the preparation of financial statements. (Understand)
  - CO3: Apply accounting rules and methods to record business transactions and events. (Apply)
  - CO4: Analyze financial statements using ratio analysis and common-size analysis. (Analyze)
  - CO5: Evaluate the financial performance and position of a company using accounting information. (Evaluate)
  - CO6: Create a budget and a cash flow statement for a business scenario. (Create)
