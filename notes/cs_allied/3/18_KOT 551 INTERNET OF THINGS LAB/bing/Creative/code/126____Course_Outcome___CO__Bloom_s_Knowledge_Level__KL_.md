## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Knowledge Level (KL)

- A course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what students should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course. It is usually expressed in terms of specific skills, knowledge, attitudes, or behaviors that are relevant to the course content and objectives.
- Bloom's knowledge level (KL) is a classification of cognitive skills that are involved in learning and performing different tasks. It is based on the taxonomy of educational objectives proposed by Benjamin Bloom and his colleagues in 1956. The taxonomy consists of six levels of cognitive skills, from the lowest to the highest: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- The relationship between CO and KL is that each CO should be aligned with one or more KLs that reflect the level of cognitive skills required to achieve the CO. For example, a CO that states "Students will be able to explain the main concepts of thermodynamics" is aligned with the KL of understand, while a CO that states "Students will be able to design a heat engine based on the principles of thermodynamics" is aligned with the KL of create.
- The benefits of aligning CO and KL are that it helps to:
  - Clarify the expectations and standards for students and instructors.
  - Design appropriate learning activities and assessments that match the CO and KL.
  - Evaluate the effectiveness and quality of the course and its outcomes.
  - Promote higher-order thinking and deeper learning among students.
