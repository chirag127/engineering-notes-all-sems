# Course Outcome and Bloom's Knowledge Level

- A course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what students should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course. It is usually expressed in terms of knowledge, skills, attitudes, or behaviors that are relevant to the course objectives and learning outcomes.
- Bloom's knowledge level (KL) is a classification of cognitive domains that categorizes the types and levels of learning that can occur in a course. It is based on the work of Benjamin Bloom and his colleagues, who proposed six levels of cognitive processes: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create. Each level represents a different degree of complexity and depth of learning.
- The relationship between CO and KL is that the CO should be aligned with the appropriate KL for the course. For example, if the CO is to "explain the principles of object-oriented programming", the KL should be "understand", as this level requires students to interpret, summarize, classify, or compare information. If the CO is to "design and implement a software project using object-oriented programming", the KL should be "create", as this level requires students to generate, plan, produce, or synthesize new or original products or ideas.
- The benefits of using CO and KL are that they can help instructors to:
  - Define clear and measurable learning outcomes for the course
  - Design appropriate learning activities and assessments that match the level of learning expected
  - Evaluate the effectiveness of the course and the achievement of the students
  - Communicate the expectations and standards of the course to the students and other stakeholders
  - Enhance the quality and consistency of the course and the curriculum
