## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

Course outcomes (COs) are statements that describe the knowledge, skills, and abilities that students should possess upon completion of a course. COs are typically aligned with the course objectives and are used to assess student learning.

Bloom’s taxonomy is a framework for categorizing educational goals and objectives into different levels of complexity and specificity. The taxonomy consists of six levels of cognitive learning, ranging from lower-order thinking skills to higher-order thinking skills. These levels are: Remembering, Understanding, Applying, Analyzing, Evaluating, and Creating.

Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL) refers to the level of cognitive learning that a course outcome is designed to achieve. For example, a course outcome that requires students to recall factual information would be at the Remembering level of Bloom’s taxonomy, while a course outcome that requires students to apply knowledge to solve problems would be at the Applying level.

In summary, Course Outcomes (COs) describe the knowledge, skills, and abilities that students should possess upon completion of a course, while Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL) refers to the level of cognitive learning that a course outcome is designed to achieve, as defined by Bloom’s taxonomy. These two concepts are closely related and are used together to design and assess effective educational programs.
