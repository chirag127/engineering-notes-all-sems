### Basic concepts for the notes of the Unit 5 - Genetic Algorithm(GA) in the subject of APPLICATION OF SOFT COMPUTING TECHNIQUES

- Genetic algorithms (GAs) are search algorithms that are based on concepts of natural selection and natural genetics.
- GAs simulate some of the processes observed in natural evolution, such as reproduction, crossover, mutation, and selection.
- GAs can provide solutions to optimization and search problems that are difficult or impossible to solve by other methods.
- GAs have better intelligence than random search algorithms because they use historical data to guide the search to the best performing region within the solution space.
- GAs operate on a population of potential solutions, called individuals or chromosomes, that encode the problem variables.
- Each individual is assigned a fitness value that measures its quality or suitability for the problem.
- GAs use three main operators to create new individuals from the existing ones: selection, crossover, and mutation.
- Selection operator chooses the individuals with high fitness values to pass their genes to the next generation.
- Crossover operator combines the genes of two parent individuals to produce one or more offspring individuals.
- Mutation operator introduces random changes in the genes of some individuals to maintain diversity and avoid premature convergence.
- GAs repeat these operators until a termination criterion is met, such as reaching a maximum number of generations, finding an optimal or near-optimal solution, or reaching a time limit.
- GAs can be applied to various domains, such as engineering, science, art, economics, and biology.
- GAs can handle complex, nonlinear, and multimodal problems that involve discrete, continuous, or mixed variables.
- GAs can also deal with noisy, incomplete, or dynamic data, and can incorporate domain knowledge or constraints into the search process.
- GAs are easy to implement and parallelize, and can exploit the power of modern computing systems.
