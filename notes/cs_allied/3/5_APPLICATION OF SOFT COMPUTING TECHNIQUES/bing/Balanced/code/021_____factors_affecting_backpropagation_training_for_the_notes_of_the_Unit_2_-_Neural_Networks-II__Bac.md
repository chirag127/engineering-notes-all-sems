### Factors affecting backpropagation training

- Backpropagation is a learning algorithm that adjusts the weights of a neural network based on the error between the desired output and the actual output for a given input pattern .
- The training of a backpropagation network is based on the choice of the various parameters and the convergence of the network is influenced by some important learning factors . These factors are as follows:

  - **Initial weights**: The initial random weights chosen are of very small value as the larger inputs in binary sigmoidal functions may lead to saturation at the very beginning, thereby leading the function been stuck at local minima. The initial weights also affect the speed of convergence and the quality of the final solution .
  - **Learning rate**: The learning rate is a parameter that controls the magnitude of the weight updates in each iteration. A high learning rate may cause the network to overshoot the optimal solution and oscillate around it, while a low learning rate may slow down the convergence and increase the training time  . A suitable learning rate should be chosen based on the complexity of the problem and the characteristics of the data.
  - **Updation rule**: The updation rule determines how the weights are modified based on the error gradient. There are different variants of the backpropagation algorithm that use different updation rules, such as gradient descent, momentum, adaptive learning rate, etc. The choice of the updation rule may affect the convergence speed, stability, and robustness of the network .
  - **Size and nature of the training set**: The size and nature of the training set influence the generalization ability of the network. A large and representative training set can help the network learn the underlying patterns and avoid overfitting, while a small and noisy training set may lead to underfitting or memorization of the data  . The training set should also be shuffled and normalized before feeding it to the network.
  - **Architecture**: The architecture of the network refers to the number of layers, the number of neurons in each layer, and the activation functions used. The architecture affects the complexity and expressiveness of the network, as well as the computational cost and memory requirements. A network with too many layers or neurons may overfit the data and be prone to vanishing or exploding gradients, while a network with too few layers or neurons may underfit the data and be unable to capture the nonlinear relationships  . The activation functions should be chosen based on the type of the output and the range of the input.
