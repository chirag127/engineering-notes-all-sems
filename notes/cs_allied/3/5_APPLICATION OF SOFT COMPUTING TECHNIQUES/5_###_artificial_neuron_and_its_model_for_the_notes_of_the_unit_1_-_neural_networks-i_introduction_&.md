### Artificial Neuron and its model for the notes of the Unit 1 - Neural Networks-I (Introduction & Architecture) in the subject of APPLICATION OF SOFT COMPUTING TECHNIQUES

An Artificial Neuron is a mathematical model that is inspired by the structure and function of biological neurons. It receives input signals, processes them using its internal parameters, and produces an output signal.

The model of an artificial neuron consists of inputs, weights, a bias, an activation function, and an output. The inputs are multiplied by the weights and added to the bias to produce a weighted sum. This weighted sum is then passed through the activation function to produce the final output.

The activation function is used to introduce non-linearity into the model and is usually a sigmoid, ReLU, or tanh function. The weights and bias are learnable parameters that are updated during the training process to minimize the error in the model's predictions.

The architecture of a neural network is composed of multiple artificial neurons, with each neuron processing the outputs of the previous neurons to produce its own output. The first layer of neurons is called the input layer, and the last layer is called the output layer. Layers in between are called hidden layers.

In summary, an artificial neuron is a mathematical model that consists of inputs, weights, a bias, an activation function, and an output. The architecture of a neural network is composed of multiple artificial neurons, with each neuron processing the outputs of the previous neurons to produce its own output. Understanding the model of an artificial neuron is crucial for building and training effective neural networks.
