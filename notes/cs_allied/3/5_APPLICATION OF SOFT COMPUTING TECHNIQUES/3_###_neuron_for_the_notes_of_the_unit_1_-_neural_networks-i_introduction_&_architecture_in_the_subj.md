### Neuron for the notes of the Unit 1 - Neural Networks-I (Introduction & Architecture) in the subject of APPLICATION OF SOFT COMPUTING TECHNIQUES

A Neuron is a basic unit of computation in an artificial neural network. It receives input signals, processes them using its internal parameters, and produces an output signal. The architecture of a neuron consists of inputs, weights, a bias, an activation function, and an output. The inputs are multiplied by the weights and added to the bias to produce a weighted sum. This weighted sum is then passed through the activation function to produce the final output. The activation function is used to introduce non-linearity into the model and is usually a sigmoid, ReLU, or tanh function. The weights and bias are learnable parameters that are updated during the training process to minimize the error in the model's predictions.

The architecture of a neural network is composed of multiple layers of neurons, with each layer processing the outputs of the previous layer to produce its own outputs. The first layer is called the input layer, and the last layer is called the output layer. Layers in between are called hidden layers. The number of neurons in each layer, the number of hidden layers, and the type of activation function used can all be adjusted to optimize the performance of the model.

In summary, a neuron is a basic building block of an artificial neural network that receives inputs, processes them using its internal parameters, and produces an output. The architecture of a neural network is composed of multiple layers of neurons, each processing the outputs of the previous layer to produce its own outputs.
