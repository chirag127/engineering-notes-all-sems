### multilayer perception model for the notes of the Unit 2 - Neural Networks-II (Back propagation networks) in the subject of APPLICATION OF SOFT COMPUTING TECHNIQUES

A Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) is a type of feedforward neural network that is widely used in the field of artificial neural networks. It is called a "multilayer" perceptron because it consists of multiple layers of interconnected nodes, or "neurons". The input layer receives the input data, and each subsequent layer processes the data and passes it on to the next layer until it reaches the output layer, which produces the final prediction.

The MLP model is trained using the backpropagation algorithm, which adjusts the weights between the nodes in the network in order to minimize the error between the predicted outputs and the actual outputs. The backpropagation algorithm works by propagating the error backwards through the network, starting from the output layer and working backwards towards the input layer. The weights are adjusted in such a way as to minimize the error, and this process is repeated until the error reaches a minimum.

MLPs have been successfully applied in a wide range of applications, including pattern recognition, image classification, speech recognition, and control systems. They are widely used due to their ability to learn and generalize from examples, and their simplicity and ease of implementation.

In conclusion, the Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) is a type of feedforward neural network that is widely used in the field of artificial neural networks. It is trained using the backpropagation algorithm, and has been successfully applied in a wide range of applications due to its ability to learn and generalize from examples.
