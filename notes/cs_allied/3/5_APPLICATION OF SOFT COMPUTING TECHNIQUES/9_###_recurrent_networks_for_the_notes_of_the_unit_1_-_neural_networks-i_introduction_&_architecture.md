### recurrent networks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Neural Networks-I (Introduction & Architecture) in the subject of APPLICATION OF SOFT COMPUTING TECHNIQUES

Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are a type of neural network that are designed to process sequential data, such as time series data and speech data. Unlike traditional feedforward neural networks, RNNs have an internal state that allows them to remember information from previous time steps.

The architecture of a RNN consists of a hidden state, inputs, and outputs. At each time step, the hidden state is updated based on the current input and the previous hidden state. The hidden state is then used to produce the output for that time step.

There are different types of RNNs, including simple RNNs, LSTMs (Long Short-Term Memory), and GRUs (Gated Recurrent Units). Each type of RNN has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of which type of RNN to use depends on the specific problem being solved.

RNNs are used in a variety of applications, including natural language processing, speech recognition, and time series forecasting. They are particularly useful for processing sequential data because they can capture the dependencies between time steps.

In conclusion, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are a type of neural network that are designed to process sequential data. They have an internal state that allows them to remember information from previous time steps, making them well-suited for processing sequential data. Different types of RNNs are used for different applications, and the choice of which type of RNN to use depends on the specific problem being solved.
