
### Multilayer Perception Model for Unit 2 - Neural Networks-II (Back Propagation Networks)

1. A multilayer perception (MLP) is a type of artificial neural network (ANN) that consists of multiple layers of neurons arranged in a hierarchical structure.

2. MLP networks are commonly used for supervised learning tasks such as classification and regression.

3. MLP networks are composed of three types of layers: an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer.

4. Each layer consists of a set of neurons, which are connected to the neurons in the previous layer.

5. The neurons in the input layer receive the input data, and the neurons in the output layer generate the output.

6. The neurons in the hidden layers are responsible for processing the data and passing it to the next layer.

7. The weights of the connections between neurons are adjusted during the learning process in order to optimize the network's performance.

8. Back propagation is a method of training MLP networks which involves propagating the errors from the output layer to the hidden layers, and then adjusting the weights of the connections accordingly.

9. Back propagation is an iterative process, and the network's performance is improved after each iteration.

10. MLP networks are widely used in a variety of applications, such as pattern recognition, speech recognition, and image processing.
