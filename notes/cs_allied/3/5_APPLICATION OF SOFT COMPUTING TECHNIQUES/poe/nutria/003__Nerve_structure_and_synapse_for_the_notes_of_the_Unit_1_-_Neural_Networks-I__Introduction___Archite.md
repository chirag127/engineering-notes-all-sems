
### Nerve Structure and Synapse

1. Nerves are composed of axons, which are long, thin fibers that carry electrical signals from one nerve cell to another. 
2. A synapse is a junction between two nerve cells, or between a nerve cell and an effector cell. 
3. At the synapse, a nerve cell releases neurotransmitters, which bind to receptors on the other cell and cause a change in the electrical potential of the postsynaptic cell. 
4. This change in potential can either excite or inhibit the postsynaptic cell, depending on the type of neurotransmitter released. 
5. Neurons in the brain and other parts of the nervous system are connected by synapses, which allow them to communicate with each other. 
6. Synapses are also important for learning and memory, as they are the sites where memories are stored and retrieved.
