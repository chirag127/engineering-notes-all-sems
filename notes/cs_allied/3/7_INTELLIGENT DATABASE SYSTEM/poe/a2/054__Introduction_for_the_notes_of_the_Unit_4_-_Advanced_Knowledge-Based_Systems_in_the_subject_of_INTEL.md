### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 4 - Advanced Knowledge-Based Systems in the subject of INTELLIGENT DATABASE SYSTEM

1. Expert Systems:
- Expert systems are knowledge-based systems that contain the knowledge and inferencing capabilities of human experts in a specific domain.
- They are designed to provide an expert's advice or to replicate an expert's decision-making skills in a particular domain.
- The knowledge of an expert is represented in the form of rules and structures in the knowledge base. The inferencing engine applies this knowledge to reach conclusions or provide explanations for the users.

2. Issues in Expert Systems:
- Knowledge acquisition bottleneck: It is difficult and time-consuming to extract knowledge from human experts and convert it into a knowledge base.
- Non-monotonic reasoning: The reasoning process may have to be revised in the light of new evidence or information. Expert systems typically do not handle non-monotonic reasoning well.
- Performance issues: Real-world problems may involve searching a very large knowledge base and the response time may not be acceptable. Heuristic search methods are commonly used to address performance issues.

3. Knowledge Engineering:
- The process of building expert systems involves knowledge engineering. It includes knowledge elicitation, knowledge documentation, knowledge modeling, and verification and validation.
- Knowledge elicitation is the process of extracting knowledge from human experts. Knowledge documentation involves representing the knowledge in terms of rules or frames.
- Knowledge modeling involves finding efficient ways to store and organize knowledge. Verification and validation are necessary to check the accuracy and usefulness of the expert system.
