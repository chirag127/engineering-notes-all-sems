### Characteristics and classification of the knowledge-based systems

1. Representation of knowledge: Knowledge is represented in an explicit and declarative form in a knowledge base. The knowledge base contains both factual knowledge and heuristics.
2. Inference mechanism: A mechanism is provided to infer new conclusions from the knowledge. Automated reasoning is used to deduce new conclusions,make inferences, and solve problems.
3. Knowledge acquisition: There is a way to acquire knowledge and add it to the knowledge base. Knowledge may be acquired from human experts, books, databases, sensors, or inferred from observations.
4. Explainability: The system should be able to explain its conclusions and reasoning process. This is important for the acceptance of the system.

Classification of knowledge-based systems:

1. Expert systems: Designed to exhibit the decision-making skills of human experts. They contain expert knowledge and inferencing mechanisms to provide expert advice and recommendations in a specific domain.
2. Decision support systems: Help in decision making by providing an analysis of a complex problem and aiding in the evaluation of possible solutions or decisions.
3. Diagnostic systems: Help identify the cause of problems by diagnosing the deviation from normal behavior. They are used in medical diagnosis, troubleshooting technical problems, etc.
4. Instructional systems: Present educational material and provide individualized guidance and feedback to students.
5. Robotic systems: Control the physical activities of a robot. They contain knowledge about the robot environment, objects, and tasks to be performed.
