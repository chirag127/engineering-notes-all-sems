1. Expert Systems
- Expert systems are computer systems that emulate the knowledge and decision-making skills of human experts in specialized domains.
- They are designed to solve complex problems in a specific domain that normally would require human expertise.
- The key components of expert systems are:
-- Knowledge base: Contains domain knowledge encoded as rules or facts.
-- Inference engine: Applies rules of inference to the knowledge base to derive solutions.
-- User interface: Accepts input and provides output to the user.
- Examples: Medical diagnosis, technical troubleshooting, financial analysis, etc.

2. Limitations of Expert Systems
- Knowledge acquisition bottleneck: It is difficult and time-consuming to gather and encode domain knowledge into an expert system.
- Narrow applicability: Expert systems are limited to a specific domain and the knowledge is encoded by experts in that domain.
- Lack of common sense: Expert systems lack the common sense reasoning that humans naturally possess, making them prone to making mistakes or producing counterintuitive results in some cases.
- Inability to learn: Traditional expert systems were static and unable to learn and improve from experience. They had to be updated by human experts.
