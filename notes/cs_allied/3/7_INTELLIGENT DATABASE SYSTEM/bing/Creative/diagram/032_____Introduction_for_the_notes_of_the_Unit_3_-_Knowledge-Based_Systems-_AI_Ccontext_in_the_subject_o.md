### Introduction

- Knowledge-based systems (KBS) are computer systems that use artificial intelligence (AI) techniques to represent and reason with knowledge, such as facts, rules, heuristics, and constraints, to solve complex problems that normally require human expertise.
- KBS can be classified into two main types: rule-based systems and logic-based systems. Rule-based systems use a set of if-then rules to encode the knowledge and an inference engine to apply the rules to the given facts. Logic-based systems use a formal logic language, such as propositional logic, predicate logic, or description logic, to represent the knowledge and a theorem prover or a model finder to infer new knowledge from the given facts and rules.
- KBS can be applied to various domains, such as diagnosis, planning, scheduling, configuration, design, natural language processing, computer vision, machine learning, and data mining. KBS can also be integrated with other technologies, such as databases, web services, agents, and ontologies, to enhance their capabilities and functionalities.
- KBS have some advantages and disadvantages compared to conventional systems. Some of the advantages are: 
  - They can capture and reuse the knowledge of human experts, which can be scarce, expensive, or unavailable.
  - They can provide explanations and justifications for their solutions, which can increase the trust and confidence of the users.
  - They can deal with uncertainty, inconsistency, and incompleteness of the knowledge, which are common in real-world problems.
  - They can learn from new data and feedback, which can improve their performance and adaptability.
- Some of the disadvantages are:
  - They can be difficult and costly to develop, maintain, and update, as the knowledge acquisition, representation, and validation processes can be complex and time-consuming.
  - They can be prone to errors and biases, as the knowledge may be incomplete, incorrect, or inconsistent, or the reasoning may be faulty or incomplete.
  - They can be limited by the expressiveness and efficiency of the knowledge representation and reasoning techniques, which may not be able to capture all the aspects and nuances of the problem domain or provide optimal or timely solutions.
