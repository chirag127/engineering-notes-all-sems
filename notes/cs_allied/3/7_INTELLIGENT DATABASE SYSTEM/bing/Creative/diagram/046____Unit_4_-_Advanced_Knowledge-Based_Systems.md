## Unit 4 - Advanced Knowledge-Based Systems

- A knowledge-based system (KBS) is a computer program that reasons and uses a knowledge base to solve complex problems.
- The knowledge base consists of facts and rules that represent the knowledge of a specific domain or problem.
- The reasoning system, also called the inference engine, applies the rules to the facts and derives new facts or conclusions.
- The most common type of knowledge-based system is a rule-based system, which uses if-then rules to encode the knowledge.
- Some examples of knowledge-based systems are expert systems, which provide advice or solutions in a specific domain, such as medical diagnosis, legal reasoning, or financial planning.
- Another example is ontology-based systems, which use a formal representation of concepts and relations in a domain to support semantic web applications, such as information retrieval, data integration, or natural language processing.
- The most recent advancement of knowledge-based systems has been to adopt the technologies, especially a kind of logic called description logic, for the development of systems that use the internet.
- The internet often has to deal with complex, unstructured data that cannot be relied on to fit a specific data model.
- Description logic allows the representation and reasoning of knowledge in a structured and modular way, and supports the development of web ontologies, such as the Resource Description Framework (RDF) and the Web Ontology Language (OWL).
- These web ontologies enable the sharing and reuse of knowledge across different applications and domains, and facilitate the interoperability and integration of heterogeneous data sources.
- Some challenges and limitations of knowledge-based systems are:
  - The acquisition and maintenance of knowledge, which can be time-consuming, costly, and prone to errors.
  - The validation and verification of knowledge, which can be difficult to ensure the correctness, completeness, and consistency of the knowledge base.
  - The explanation and justification of reasoning, which can be hard to provide to the users or other systems, especially when the knowledge or the inference process is complex or uncertain.
  - The scalability and performance of reasoning, which can be affected by the size and complexity of the knowledge base, the number and type of rules, and the nature of the problem.
  - The adaptability and flexibility of knowledge, which can be limited by the rigidity and brittleness of the knowledge representation and the reasoning system.
