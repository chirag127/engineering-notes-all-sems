## Unit 3 - Knowledge-Based Systems - AI Context

- A knowledge-based system (KBS) is a form of artificial intelligence (AI) that aims to capture the knowledge of human experts to support decision-making  .
- A KBS stores knowledge in the form of rules, facts, concepts, or other representations, which are then used to generate solutions to specific problems .
- A KBS typically consists of two main components: a knowledge base and an inference engine  .
  - A knowledge base is a collection of knowledge that is relevant to a specific problem domain, such as medicine, law, engineering, etc.
  - An inference engine is a software component that applies logical reasoning to the knowledge base to derive new knowledge or conclusions.
- A KBS can have different types and uses, depending on the nature and complexity of the problem domain and the knowledge source  .
  - Some common types of KBS are:
    - Expert systems: KBS that emulate the reasoning and advice of human experts in a specific domain, such as diagnosis, planning, design, etc.
    - Case-based reasoning systems: KBS that use past cases or examples to solve new problems by finding similarities and differences.
    - Ontology-based systems: KBS that use formal and explicit definitions of concepts and relationships in a domain to facilitate knowledge sharing and reuse.
    - Neural network systems: KBS that use artificial neural networks to learn from data and perform tasks such as classification, prediction, recognition, etc.
  - Some common uses of KBS are:
    - Decision support: KBS can help humans make better and faster decisions by providing relevant and reliable knowledge and recommendations.
    - Education and training: KBS can enhance human learning and skills by providing interactive and adaptive feedback and guidance.
    - Knowledge management: KBS can help organizations capture, store, and disseminate knowledge among their members and stakeholders.
    - Research and innovation: KBS can help researchers and innovators discover new knowledge and generate novel solutions by combining and analyzing existing knowledge.
