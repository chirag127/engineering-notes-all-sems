### Botnets for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

Botnets are networks of compromised computers or Internet of Things (IoT) devices that are controlled by a single entity, often referred to as a "botmaster". Botnets are used for a variety of malicious purposes, including spamming, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, and data theft.

Botnets are created by infecting computers or IoT devices with malware, which allows the botmaster to remotely control the infected devices. The infected devices are then used to carry out malicious activities, such as sending spam, launching DDoS attacks, or stealing sensitive information.

Botnets are difficult to detect and defend against, as the infected devices are often located in different parts of the world and are controlled remotely by the botmaster. This makes it difficult to track down the source of the malicious activity, and to take action to stop it.

In the context of cyber forensics, botnets present a significant challenge, as they can be used to cover up the tracks of the attacker, making it difficult to determine the source of the attack. Forensic analysts must use a variety of techniques, such as network analysis, malware analysis, and data recovery, to identify the presence of a botnet, and to gather evidence that can be used to track down the botmaster.

In addition, forensic analysts must also be familiar with the different types of botnets, and the methods that are used to control them, in order to effectively detect and defend against botnets.

Overall, botnets are a major threat to the security of the Internet, and pose a significant challenge to cyber forensic analysts. Understanding the nature of botnets, and the methods used to detect and defend against them, is an important aspect of cyber forensic analytics.
