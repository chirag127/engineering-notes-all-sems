Evidence refers to any information that is relevant to a criminal investigation or legal proceedings. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, evidence can include digital data, such as files, emails, and network logs, as well as physical evidence, such as computer hardware and storage devices.

There are several types of evidence that are commonly used in cyber forensic investigations, including:

1. Live Evidence: Live evidence refers to data that is currently stored on a computer or other device, such as files, emails, and network logs.

2. Physical Evidence: Physical evidence refers to physical objects that are related to a crime, such as computer hardware, storage devices, and other electronic devices.

3. Logical Evidence: Logical evidence refers to data that is stored on a computer or other device, but is not currently accessible, such as deleted files or hidden data.

4. Network Evidence: Network evidence refers to data that is transmitted over a network, such as emails, network logs, and other network traffic.

5. Analog Evidence: Analog evidence refers to data that is stored in an analog format, such as audio recordings or video tapes.

In conclusion, evidence refers to any information that is relevant to a criminal investigation or legal proceedings. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, there are several types of evidence that are commonly used, including live evidence, physical evidence, logical evidence, network evidence, and analog evidence. These types of evidence are used to gather information and to support the investigation and prosecution of cyber crimes.
