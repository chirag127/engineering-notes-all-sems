Evidence management is the process of organizing, storing, and preserving evidence in a manner that ensures its authenticity, integrity, and availability for use in legal or administrative proceedings. In the context of Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics, evidence management is a critical aspect of cyber investigations, as it is essential for the proper handling, analysis, and presentation of digital and physical evidence.

Evidence management in cyber investigations involves the following key steps:

1. Evidence collection: This involves the identification, preservation, and analysis of digital and physical evidence, including data stored on computers, servers, and other digital devices, as well as data transmitted over networks and the Internet.

2. Evidence storage: This involves the storage of evidence in a secure and controlled environment, such as a forensic laboratory, to ensure its authenticity and integrity. Evidence may be stored in a variety of forms, including digital images of hard drives and other storage media, and data transmitted over networks.

3. Evidence analysis: This involves the analysis of evidence to identify relevant information and data, and to determine the significance of the evidence in the investigation. Evidence analysis may involve the use of forensic tools and techniques, such as digital forensics, network analysis, and data analysis.

4. Evidence presentation: This involves the presentation of evidence in court or other legal proceedings, including the preparation of reports and the testimony of experts.

In order to ensure the authenticity, integrity, and availability of evidence in cyber investigations, it is important to follow strict evidence management procedures and to use appropriate tools and techniques for the collection, storage, analysis, and presentation of evidence.
