### Prepaid Cards

- Prepaid cards are cards that are loaded with a certain amount of money and can be used for various purposes, such as gift cards, travel cards, payroll cards, etc.
- Prepaid cards are different from regular credit and debit cards issued by banks, as they do not require a bank account, a credit check, or a personal identification number (PIN) to use them.
- Prepaid cards are often anonymous, meaning they are not linked to the identity of the cardholder or the source of the funds.
- Prepaid cards are convenient, flexible, and widely accepted, but they also pose significant risks for cyber crime, fraud, and money laundering.

#### Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime is any illegal activity that involves a computer, a network, or a device connected to the internet.
- Cyber criminals use various methods to steal, manipulate, or destroy data, or to disrupt or harm the functioning of systems or services.
- Cyber crime can affect individuals, businesses, governments, and society as a whole, causing financial losses, reputational damage, privacy breaches, identity theft, and physical harm.
- Cyber crime can be classified into two broad categories: cyber-dependent crimes and cyber-enabled crimes.
- Cyber-dependent crimes are crimes that can only be committed using computers, networks, or devices, such as hacking, malware, denial-of-service attacks, etc.
- Cyber-enabled crimes are traditional crimes that are facilitated or amplified by the use of computers, networks, or devices, such as fraud, theft, extortion, etc.

#### Prepaid Cards and Cyber Crime

- Prepaid cards are often used by cyber criminals to commit or facilitate cyber-enabled crimes, such as fraud and money laundering.
- Prepaid cards are attractive to cyber criminals because they are easy to obtain, anonymous, and difficult to trace.
- Prepaid cards can be used to purchase goods or services online or offline, to withdraw cash from ATMs, or to transfer funds to other cards or accounts.
- Prepaid cards can also be used to store or launder the proceeds of cyber crime, such as ransomware, phishing, or carding.
- Prepaid cards can be purchased with stolen payment information, such as credit or debit card numbers, or with cash obtained from illegal activities.
- Prepaid cards can be loaded with funds from compromised bank accounts, online payment platforms, or cryptocurrency wallets.
- Prepaid cards can be used to pay for illicit goods or services, such as drugs, weapons, or hacking tools, on the dark web or other online platforms.
- Prepaid cards can be used to fund terrorist activities or support extremist groups, as they can bypass the traditional financial system and its regulations.

#### Examples of Prepaid Cards and Cyber Crime

- In 2013, a global cyber crime ring stole $45 million from two Middle Eastern banks by hacking into their prepaid card systems and increasing the withdrawal limits on the cards. The hackers then distributed the cards to operatives in 27 countries, who used them to withdraw cash from ATMs in a coordinated operation.
- In 2016, a group of cyber criminals used prepaid cards to withdraw $2.4 million from 140 ATMs in Taiwan, after infecting the machines with malware that allowed them to remotely control them. The criminals then fled the country with the cash.
- In 2019, a Nigerian man was sentenced to 10 years in prison for his role in a large-scale fraud scheme that involved using prepaid cards to steal millions of dollars from elderly Americans. The man and his co-conspirators used online dating sites to lure the victims into sending them money, gift cards, or prepaid cards, claiming they needed them for various emergencies or expenses.
- In 2020, the US Department of Justice charged 14 members of an international cyber crime group with conspiracy to commit wire fraud, bank fraud, and money laundering, among other charges. The group used phishing emails to trick victims into revealing their online banking credentials, and then used them to access their accounts and transfer funds to prepaid cards or other accounts controlled by the group.
