### On Scene Activities for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

- On scene activities are the actions and procedures that a computer forensic investigator performs at the location where digital evidence is found, such as a crime scene, a suspect's home, or a workplace.
- The main objectives of on scene activities are to identify, secure, document, collect, and preserve digital evidence in a way that maintains its integrity, reliability, and admissibility in court.
- Some of the steps involved in on scene activities are:

  - **Preparation**: The investigator should prepare for the on scene activities by gathering the necessary tools, equipment, authorization, and information about the case and the scene. The investigator should also follow the relevant policies, standards, and guidelines for conducting computer forensics investigations.
  - **Arrival**: The investigator should arrive at the scene safely and securely, and coordinate with other law enforcement personnel or first responders. The investigator should also assess the scene for potential risks, hazards, or threats, and take appropriate measures to ensure safety.
  - **Securing the scene**: The investigator should establish a perimeter around the scene and restrict access to authorized personnel only. The investigator should also protect the scene from environmental factors, such as heat, humidity, dust, or electromagnetic interference, that may damage or alter the digital evidence.
  - **Documentation**: The investigator should document the scene thoroughly and accurately, using notes, sketches, photographs, videos, or audio recordings. The documentation should capture the location, condition, and appearance of the digital evidence, as well as the chain of custody, the actions taken, and the observations made by the investigator.
  - **Identification**: The investigator should identify the potential sources and types of digital evidence at the scene, such as computers, mobile phones, storage devices, network devices, or cloud services. The investigator should also determine the status and state of the digital evidence, such as whether it is powered on or off, encrypted or decrypted, or connected or disconnected to a network.
  - **Collection**: The investigator should collect the digital evidence using the appropriate methods and tools, depending on the nature and state of the evidence. The investigator should follow the principle of least intrusion, which means minimizing the changes or modifications to the original evidence. The investigator should also verify the integrity of the collected evidence using techniques such as hashing or checksums.
  - **Preservation**: The investigator should preserve the digital evidence in a way that prevents any loss, damage, alteration, or contamination. The investigator should store the evidence in a secure and controlled environment, using proper packaging, labeling, and sealing. The investigator should also maintain the chain of custody, which is the record of who handled the evidence, when, where, how, and why.
  - **Transportation**: The investigator should transport the digital evidence from the scene to the laboratory or another designated location, following the same principles of preservation and chain of custody. The investigator should also document the transportation process, including the route, mode, time, and destination of the transportation.

- On scene activities are crucial for the success of a computer forensics investigation, as they ensure that the digital evidence is collected and preserved in a forensically sound manner, and that it can be used to support the investigation and prosecution of cyber crimes .
