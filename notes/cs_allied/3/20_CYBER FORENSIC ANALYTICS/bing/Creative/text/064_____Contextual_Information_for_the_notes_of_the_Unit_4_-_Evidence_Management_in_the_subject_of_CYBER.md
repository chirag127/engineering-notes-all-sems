### Contextual Information for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Contextual information is the information that provides the background, setting, or circumstances of a digital evidence, such as the time, location, source, owner, or purpose of the evidence.
- Contextual information is important for evidence management because it helps to identify, collect, acquire, preserve, and analyze the evidence in a systematic and reliable way.
- Evidence management is the process of handling digital evidence from the scene of a cyber incident or crime to the presentation of the evidence in a court of law.
- Evidence management involves the following steps :
  - Identification: Information needs to be gathered before the digital device is even touched. It won’t often be clear what contextual information is relevant to an investigation until much later. The identification step includes documenting the scene, identifying potential sources of evidence, and obtaining legal authority to access the evidence.
  - Collection: The collection step involves physically securing and transporting the digital devices or media from the scene to a secure location. The collection step should follow the principle of least disturbance, which means minimizing any changes to the original state of the evidence.
  - Acquisition: The acquisition step involves creating a bit-by-bit copy of the digital evidence, also known as a forensic image, using specialized tools and techniques. The acquisition step should ensure the integrity and authenticity of the evidence by using hashing algorithms and write blockers.
  - Preservation: The preservation step involves storing and protecting the forensic images and the original evidence from unauthorized access, modification, or damage. The preservation step should follow the chain of custody, which means documenting every person who handled the evidence and every action that was performed on the evidence.
  - Analysis: The analysis step involves examining the forensic images and extracting relevant data and information that can answer the investigative questions or support the hypotheses. The analysis step should use various tools and methods, such as file carving, keyword searching, timeline analysis, or network analysis, depending on the type and nature of the evidence.
- Evidence management requires a high level of technical skills, analytical skills, and ethical standards, as well as a solid understanding of computer networking concepts, network security methodologies, risk management, computer and privacy laws, and data analytics.
