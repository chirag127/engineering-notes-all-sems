### Soft Lifting

- Soft lifting is a type of software piracy that involves installing or copying a legally licensed software program onto multiple computers, in violation of its licensing agreement  .
- Soft lifting is also known as end user piracy or casual copying.
- Soft lifting is the most common type of software piracy.
- Soft lifting often occurs in business or school environments and is usually done to save money.
- Soft lifting can have negative impacts on the software industry, such as loss of revenue, reduced innovation, increased prices, and legal risks.
- Soft lifting can be prevented by using software asset management tools, educating users about software licensing terms, enforcing software policies, and reporting software piracy incidents .

### Cyber Forensics

- Cyber forensics is a process of extracting data as proof for a crime (that involves electronic devices) while following proper investigation rules to nab the culprit by presenting the evidence to the court.
- Cyber forensics is also known as computer forensics.
- Cyber forensics is a field that follows certain procedures to find the evidence to reach conclusions after proper investigation of matters.
- The procedures that cyber forensic experts follow are:
  - Identification: The first step of cyber forensics experts are to identify what evidence is present, where it is stored, and in which format it is stored.
  - Preservation: The second step of cyber forensics experts are to preserve the evidence by making a copy of it and securing it from any tampering or alteration.
  - Analysis: The third step of cyber forensics experts are to analyze the evidence by using various tools and techniques to extract relevant information and data.
  - Presentation: The fourth step of cyber forensics experts are to present the evidence to the court or the client by preparing a report that summarizes the findings and conclusions.
  - Review: The fifth step of cyber forensics experts are to review the case and the evidence by evaluating the effectiveness and accuracy of the investigation and the report.
- Cyber forensics can be applied to various domains, such as cybercrime, digital forensics, network forensics, mobile forensics, cloud forensics, malware forensics, etc.
- Cyber forensics can face various challenges, such as encryption, anti-forensics, data volume, data volatility, data diversity, legal issues, ethical issues, etc.
