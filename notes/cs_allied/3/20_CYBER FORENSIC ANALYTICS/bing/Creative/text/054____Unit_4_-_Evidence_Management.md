## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

- Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a forensically sound manner.
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and recognizing potential sources of digital evidence, such as devices, files, logs, etc.
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, chain of custody, etc.
  - Preservation: protecting digital evidence from alteration, loss, or damage, such as using write blockers, encryption, backups, etc.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting digital evidence using various methods and tools, such as carving, decoding, recovery, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the results and findings of digital evidence analysis, such as using charts, graphs, timelines, etc.
- Evidence management requires following the principles of digital forensics, such as:
  - Admissibility: ensuring that digital evidence is legally acceptable and valid in a court of law, such as by following the rules of evidence, standards, and best practices.
  - Authenticity: ensuring that digital evidence is original and genuine, and not tampered with or fabricated, such as by verifying the integrity, source, and origin of digital evidence.
  - Completeness: ensuring that digital evidence is comprehensive and sufficient to support the investigation, such as by collecting all relevant and available digital evidence.
  - Reliability: ensuring that digital evidence is consistent and accurate, and not affected by errors or biases, such as by using reliable and tested tools and techniques.
  - Repeatability: ensuring that digital evidence can be reproduced and verified by other examiners, such as by documenting and sharing the procedures, tools, and data used in digital evidence analysis.
