### Real evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Real evidence is any physical object that can be directly observed or examined by the court .
- Real evidence can be used to prove or disprove a fact or a connection between a suspect and a crime .
- Real evidence can also be used to demonstrate how a crime was committed or what the motive was .
- Examples of real evidence in cyber forensics are:
  - The actual computer or device that was used to commit or facilitate a cybercrime .
  - The hard drive, memory card, USB drive, CD-ROM, or any other storage media that contains digital evidence .
  - The network devices, such as routers, switches, firewalls, or servers, that were involved in the transmission or storage of digital evidence .
  - The physical documents, such as printouts, receipts, invoices, or contracts, that are related to the digital evidence .
- Real evidence must be properly collected, preserved, analyzed, and presented in order to be admissible in court .
- The steps involved in the evidence management process are:
  - Identification: The first step is to identify what evidence is present, where it is stored, and in what format it is stored.
  - Collection: The next step is to collect the evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, or copying .
  - Preservation: The third step is to preserve the evidence in a secure and tamper-proof manner, such as using write blockers, encryption, or sealing .
  - Analysis: The fourth step is to analyze the evidence using specialized software and methods, such as data recovery, decryption, or carving .
  - Presentation: The final step is to present the evidence in a clear and convincing manner, such as using reports, charts, or graphs .
