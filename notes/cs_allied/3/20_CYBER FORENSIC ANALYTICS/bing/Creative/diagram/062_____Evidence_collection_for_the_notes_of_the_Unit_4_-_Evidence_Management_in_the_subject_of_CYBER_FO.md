### Evidence collection for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence collection is the process of identifying, accessing, preserving and analyzing data from various sources in the digital environment for forensic investigation .
- Evidence collection plays a vital role in tracing the criminal activities, reconstructing the events, proving or disproving the hypotheses, and supporting or refuting the claims in the court of law .
- Evidence collection involves the following steps  :
  - Planning and preparation: This step involves defining the scope, objectives, and legal authority of the investigation, identifying the potential sources and types of evidence, and selecting the appropriate tools and techniques for data collection.
  - Identification and acquisition: This step involves locating and accessing the relevant data from the physical or logical devices, such as computers, hard drives, mobile phones, cloud servers, etc. The data can be acquired using live or dead methods, depending on whether the device is powered on or off. The data can also be acquired using static or dynamic methods, depending on whether the data is copied as it is or analyzed in real time. The data acquisition should follow the principles of integrity, authenticity, and chain of custody to ensure the admissibility of the evidence in the court of law.
  - Preservation and storage: This step involves creating a duplicate or an image of the original data using a write blocker or a hashing algorithm to prevent any alteration or modification of the data. The duplicate or the image should be stored in a secure and tamper-proof location, such as a locked cabinet or a safe, and should be labeled with the relevant information, such as the date, time, location, and name of the investigator.
  - Analysis and interpretation: This step involves examining and extracting the relevant information from the data using various tools and techniques, such as file carving, keyword searching, timeline analysis, network analysis, etc. The analysis and interpretation should aim to answer the who, what, when, where, why, and how questions related to the investigation.
  - Reporting and presentation: This step involves documenting and communicating the findings and conclusions of the investigation in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, using various formats, such as reports, charts, graphs, tables, etc. The reporting and presentation should follow the standards and guidelines of the relevant jurisdiction and audience, and should include the evidence, the methods, the results, and the limitations of the investigation.

- Evidence collection faces some challenges and issues in the digital environment, such as  :
  - Volume and variety: The digital environment generates and stores massive amounts of data in different formats, such as text, audio, video, image, etc. This poses a challenge for the investigators to collect and analyze the data in a timely and efficient manner.
  - Volatility and fragility: The digital environment is dynamic and constantly changing, which means that the data can be easily altered, deleted, overwritten, or encrypted by the users or the system. This poses a challenge for the investigators to preserve and access the data without compromising its integrity and authenticity.
  - Distribution and diversity: The digital environment is distributed and diverse, which means that the data can be stored and accessed across different devices, networks, locations, and jurisdictions. This poses a challenge for the investigators to locate and acquire the data without violating the privacy and security of the users or the laws and regulations of the countries.
  - Complexity and sophistication: The digital environment is complex and sophisticated, which means that the data can be hidden, obfuscated, or encrypted by the users or the malware. This poses a challenge for the investigators to extract and interpret the data without losing or misinterpreting the information.

- Evidence collection requires the investigators to have the necessary skills, knowledge, and experience in the field of cyber forensics, as well as the awareness of the ethical, legal, and professional issues involved in the investigation. Evidence collection also requires the investigators to use the appropriate tools and techniques that are reliable, valid, and compatible with the data sources and types. Evidence collection is an essential and challenging part of the cyber forensic analytics process that aims to provide the digital evidence that can support or refute the claims in the court of law.
