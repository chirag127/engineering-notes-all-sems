### Digital device for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

- Digital forensics is a branch of forensic science that focuses on the recovery and investigation of material found in digital devices related to cybercrime  .
- Digital forensics can be used to identify, preserve, analyze and document digital evidence, such as files, emails, logs, metadata, etc., that can aid in the conviction of criminals .
- Digital forensics can also be used to identify the cause, scope, impact and source of a cyber attack, as well as to recover lost or damaged data.
- Digital forensics can cover the investigation of any devices that can store digital data, such as computers, hard drives, mobile phones, tablets, cameras, flash drives, etc .
- Digital forensics can be divided into different subfields, such as computer forensics, network forensics, mobile forensics, cloud forensics, etc., depending on the type of device or network involved .
- Digital forensics requires specialized tools and software to extract, analyze and present digital evidence in a forensically sound manner .
- Some examples of digital forensics tools and software are Paraben, EnCase, FTK, Autopsy, X-Ways, Cellebrite, Oxygen, etc.
- Digital forensics follows a standard process that consists of four main phases: collection, examination, analysis and reporting .
- Collection involves identifying, acquiring and preserving digital evidence from the device or network in question, using proper methods and techniques to avoid tampering or alteration .
- Examination involves extracting and filtering relevant data from the digital evidence, using various tools and techniques to recover hidden, deleted or encrypted data .
- Analysis involves interpreting and evaluating the data extracted from the digital evidence, using various methods and techniques to identify patterns, anomalies, connections, etc .
- Reporting involves presenting and documenting the findings and conclusions of the digital forensics investigation, using various formats and tools to ensure clarity, accuracy and validity .
- Digital forensics faces various challenges and limitations, such as large and complex data sets, encryption, anti-forensics techniques, legal and ethical issues, etc .
- Digital forensics requires constant updating and learning, as new technologies, devices, methods and techniques emerge and evolve in the digital world .
