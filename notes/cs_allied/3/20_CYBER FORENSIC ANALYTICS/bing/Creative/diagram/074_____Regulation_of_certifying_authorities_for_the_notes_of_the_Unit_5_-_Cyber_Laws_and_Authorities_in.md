### Regulation of certifying authorities

- A certifying authority (CA) is an entity that issues digital certificates to verify the identity and authenticity of the users of electronic transactions.
- The Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act) has established a regulatory framework for the CAs in India.
- The IT Act empowers the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) to license and supervise the CAs and to lay down the standards and conditions for their functioning.
- The CCA is appointed by the Central Government and has the following powers and functions:
  - To exercise supervision over the activities of the CAs.
  - To certify the public keys of the CAs.
  - To lay down the standards and procedures for the CAs.
  - To specify the qualifications and experience of the employees of the CAs.
  - To specify the conditions subject to which the CAs shall conduct their business.
  - To resolve any conflict of interest between the CAs and the subscribers of the digital certificates.
  - To maintain a database of the public keys of the CAs and the digital signature certificates issued by them.
  - To revoke the license of the CAs for breach of the provisions of the IT Act or the rules and regulations made thereunder.
- The CAs are required to obtain a license from the CCA before commencing their operations. The license is valid for a period of five years and can be renewed for a further period of five years.
- The CAs are required to follow the standards and procedures prescribed by the CCA and to ensure the security and confidentiality of the digital certificates and the keys associated with them.
- The CAs are also required to disclose the terms and conditions of their services to the subscribers and to maintain a record of the digital certificates issued by them.
- The CAs are liable for any damage caused to the subscribers or any third party due to their negligence or misconduct in issuing or revoking the digital certificates.
- The IT Act also provides for the recognition of foreign CAs by the CCA, subject to such conditions and restrictions as may be specified by the Central Government.
