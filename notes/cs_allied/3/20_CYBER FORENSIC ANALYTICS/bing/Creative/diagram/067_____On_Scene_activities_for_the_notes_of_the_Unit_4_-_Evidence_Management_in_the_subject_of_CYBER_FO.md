### On Scene Activities for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

On scene activities are the actions and procedures that a computer forensic investigator performs at the location where digital evidence is found or suspected to be found. On scene activities include the following steps:

- **Recognize and identify digital evidence**: The investigator should be able to recognize and identify potential sources of digital evidence, such as computers, mobile phones, storage devices, network devices, etc. The investigator should also be aware of the types and formats of digital data that can be relevant for the investigation, such as files, logs, metadata, etc. The investigator should document the location, appearance, and condition of each digital device and evidence item.

- **Seize and secure digital evidence**: The investigator should follow the legal and ethical guidelines for seizing and securing digital evidence, such as obtaining proper authorization, respecting privacy rights, minimizing disruption, and avoiding contamination or alteration of the evidence. The investigator should use appropriate tools and techniques to safely disconnect, power off, or isolate the digital devices from the network or power source. The investigator should also label, package, and transport the digital evidence in a secure and tamper-evident manner. 

- **Document the electronic crime scene**: The investigator should create a detailed and accurate record of the electronic crime scene, including the physical layout, environmental conditions, personnel involved, actions taken, and observations made. The investigator should use various methods of documentation, such as notes, sketches, photographs, videos, audio recordings, etc. The investigator should also maintain a chain of custody log that tracks the handling and movement of the digital evidence from the scene to the laboratory. 

- **Analyze digital evidence**: The investigator should perform a preliminary analysis of the digital evidence at the scene, if possible and necessary, to determine its relevance, value, and urgency for the investigation. The investigator should use appropriate tools and techniques to access, examine, and extract the digital data from the devices or media, without altering or damaging the original evidence. The investigator should also verify the integrity and authenticity of the digital evidence using methods such as hashing or digital signatures. 

- **Report and present digital evidence**: The investigator should prepare a clear and concise report that summarizes the findings and conclusions of the on scene activities, including the sources, types, and contents of the digital evidence, the methods and tools used to collect and analyze the evidence, and the limitations and challenges encountered. The investigator should also present the digital evidence in a suitable format and manner for the intended audience, such as law enforcement, prosecutors, judges, or juries, using methods such as charts, graphs, tables, screenshots, etc. The investigator should also be prepared to testify as an expert witness in court, if required.
