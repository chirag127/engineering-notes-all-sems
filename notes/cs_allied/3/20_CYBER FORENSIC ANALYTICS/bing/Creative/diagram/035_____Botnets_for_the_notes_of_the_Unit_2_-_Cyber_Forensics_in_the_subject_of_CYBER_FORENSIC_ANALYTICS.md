### Botnets

- A botnet is a network of compromised computers (bots) that are controlled by attackers (botmasters) for malicious purposes, such as spamming, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, stealing sensitive information, or spreading malware  .
- Botnet forensics is the science that determines the scope of the breach and applies the methodology to find out the type of the infection. It is an investigation of the botnet attacks that includes a collection of activities like collection, identification, detection, acquisition, and attribution of botnet evidence from digital sources .
- Botnet forensics can be divided into three phases: malware, botnet forensic investigator, and botnet forensic analyzer.
  - Malware phase: It involves propagation, infection, communication, and attack that are performed by the botnet malware. This phase can be further divided into four stages: infection, command and control (C&C), update, and action.
  - Botnet forensic investigator phase: This is the second phase of the botnet forensics framework. This phase focuses on the identification and collection of botnet evidence from the infected machines, the network traffic, and the C&C servers. The investigator uses various tools and techniques to gather and preserve the botnet evidence, such as network sniffers, malware analyzers, memory dumpers, disk imagers, etc.
  - Botnet forensic analyzer phase: This is the third and final phase of the botnet forensics framework. This phase involves the examination, analysis, and attribution of the botnet evidence collected in the previous phase. The analyzer uses various methods and tools to extract, correlate, and interpret the botnet evidence, such as static and dynamic analysis, reverse engineering, data mining, machine learning, etc. The analyzer also tries to identify the botmaster, the botnet architecture, the botnet protocol, and the botnet activity.
- Botnet forensics is a challenging and complex task, as botnets are constantly evolving and using various techniques to evade detection and analysis, such as encryption, obfuscation, polymorphism, fast-flux, peer-to-peer, etc  .
- Botnet forensics is also an important and relevant task, as botnets are one of the major threats to the security and privacy of the Internet of Things (IoT) systems, which are composed of interconnected devices that collect and exchange data. Botnets can compromise the IoT systems and exploit them for various malicious purposes, such as DDoS attacks, data theft, ransomware, etc.
- Botnet forensics can help to mitigate the impact of botnet attacks, by providing valuable information and insights about the botnet characteristics, behavior, and origin. Botnet forensics can also help to prevent future botnet attacks, by identifying and removing the botnet malware, blocking the botnet C&C, and prosecuting the botnet operators  .
- Some examples of the biggest botnet attacks to date are:
  - Mirai: A botnet that infected millions of IoT devices, such as routers, cameras, and DVRs, and launched massive DDoS attacks against various targets, such as Dyn, a DNS service provider, Krebs on Security, a cybersecurity blog, and OVH, a web hosting company, in 2016.
  - Emotet: A botnet that started as a banking trojan, but evolved into a malware distribution platform, that delivered various types of malware, such as ransomware, spyware, and keyloggers, to millions of computers worldwide, from 2014 to 2021.
  - Necurs: A botnet that was one of the largest sources of spam and malware, such as Dridex, a banking trojan, and Locky, a ransomware, that infected millions of computers worldwide, from 2012 to 2020.
