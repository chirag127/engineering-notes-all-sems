### Internet Payment Services

- Internet payment services are methods of transferring money online, such as through credit cards, debit cards, e-wallets, mobile payments, etc.
- Internet payment services offer convenience, speed, and efficiency for online transactions, but they also pose various risks and challenges for cyber security and cyber forensics.
- Some of the common cyber crimes that target internet payment services are:
  - **JS-skimming**: This is the method of stealing payment card data from online stores by injecting malicious JavaScript code into the web pages. The code captures the card details entered by the customers and sends them to the attackers' servers .
  - **Phishing**: This is the technique of tricking users into revealing their personal or financial information, such as passwords, account numbers, PINs, etc. by sending them fake emails, messages, or websites that look like legitimate ones. The attackers then use this information to access the users' accounts or make fraudulent transactions.
  - **Malware**: This is the term for any software that is designed to harm or compromise a computer system or network. Malware can infect the devices of the users or the payment service providers and steal, modify, or delete data, or disrupt the normal functioning of the system. Malware can also be used to create botnets, which are networks of compromised devices that can be controlled by the attackers to launch distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, spam campaigns, or other malicious activities.
- Some of the cyber forensic techniques that can be used to investigate and prevent cyber crimes involving internet payment services are:
  - **Digital evidence collection and preservation**: This involves identifying, acquiring, and securing the digital data that can be used as evidence in a cyber crime case, such as logs, files, emails, network traffic, etc. The data should be collected and preserved in a way that maintains its integrity, authenticity, and admissibility in a court of law.
  - **Digital evidence analysis and interpretation**: This involves examining, processing, and interpreting the digital data to extract relevant information, such as the source, destination, content, and timing of the transactions, the identity and location of the attackers and the victims, the methods and tools used by the attackers, etc. The analysis and interpretation should be done using appropriate tools and methods, and following the standard procedures and guidelines.
  - **Digital evidence presentation and reporting**: This involves presenting and communicating the findings and conclusions of the digital evidence analysis and interpretation to the relevant stakeholders, such as law enforcement, prosecutors, judges, or clients. The presentation and reporting should be done in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, using suitable formats and media, and respecting the ethical and legal principles.
