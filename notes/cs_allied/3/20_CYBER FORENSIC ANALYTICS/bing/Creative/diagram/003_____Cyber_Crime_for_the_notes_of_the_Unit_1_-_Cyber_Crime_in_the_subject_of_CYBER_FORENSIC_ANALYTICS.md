### Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime is the term used to describe criminal activities carried out by means of computers or the internet.
- Cyber crime can target individuals, corporations, or governments, and can involve fraud, theft, identity theft, child pornography, hacking, cyberstalking, cyberterrorism, and other illegal or harmful acts .
- Cyber crime can have serious consequences for the victims, such as financial losses, emotional distress, reputational damage, or legal liability.
- Cyber crime can also pose a threat to the security, stability, and integrity of the cyberspace, which is the global network of interconnected devices and systems that enable communication, information exchange, and online services.
- Cyber crime can be classified into two main categories: cyber-dependent crimes and cyber-enabled crimes.
  - Cyber-dependent crimes are crimes that can only be committed using computers or the internet, such as hacking, denial-of-service attacks, or malware infections.
  - Cyber-enabled crimes are crimes that can be committed offline or online, but are facilitated or amplified by the use of computers or the internet, such as fraud, identity theft, or cyberstalking.
- Cyber crime can be prevented or mitigated by adopting various measures, such as using strong passwords, encryption, antivirus software, firewalls, backups, and updating systems and applications regularly.
- Cyber crime can also be detected, investigated, and prosecuted by applying various techniques, such as digital forensics, cyber intelligence, cyber law, and cyber ethics.
