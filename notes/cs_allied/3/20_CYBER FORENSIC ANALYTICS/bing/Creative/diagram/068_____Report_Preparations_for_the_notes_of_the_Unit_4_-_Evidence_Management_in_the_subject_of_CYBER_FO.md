### Report Preparations for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence management is the process of protecting, preserving, and documenting the digital evidence that is collected, examined, and presented in cyber forensic investigations.
- Evidence management is essential for ensuring the integrity, reliability, and admissibility of digital evidence in court, as well as for preventing the loss, contamination, or degradation of the evidence .
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and recognizing the potential sources and types of digital evidence in a crime scene or a device.
  - Collection: acquiring and securing the digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, and logging.
  - Preservation: maintaining the original state and condition of the digital evidence by preventing any unauthorized access, modification, or deletion, and by using proper storage and handling methods, such as encryption, locking, and labeling.
  - Analysis: extracting, interpreting, and correlating the relevant information from the digital evidence using specialized software and methods, such as carving, decoding, and timeline analysis.
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the findings and conclusions from the digital evidence analysis in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, using suitable formats and media, such as charts, graphs, and tables.
  - Disposition: disposing or returning the digital evidence according to the legal and ethical requirements and policies, such as retention, destruction, or release.
- Evidence management requires the use of standard operating procedures, best practices, and guidelines to ensure the quality and consistency of the digital evidence process .
- Evidence management also requires the use of a chain of custody, which is a record of the history and movement of the digital evidence, including the details of who, what, when, where, why, and how the evidence was handled, transferred, or altered .
- Evidence management faces various challenges and issues, such as the increasing volume, variety, and complexity of digital evidence, the rapid evolution and obsolescence of digital technologies, the legal and ethical implications of digital evidence, and the human and organizational factors that affect the digital evidence process  .
