### Investigating audit logs for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Audit logs are records of events that occur on a system or network, such as user actions, system changes, network connections, errors, and security breaches .
- Audit logs are essential for cyber investigation because they can provide evidence of who did what, when, where, how, and why on a system or network .
- Audit logs can help investigators to:
  - Detect and respond to cyber incidents by identifying the source, scope, impact, and timeline of an attack .
  - Analyze and reconstruct the sequence of events and actions that led to an incident .
  - Identify and mitigate vulnerabilities and risks in the system or network .
  - Ensure compliance with regulatory and legal requirements by demonstrating accountability and transparency .
- Audit logs should be collected, stored, and analyzed in a secure and consistent manner, following the best practices and guidelines for audit logging  .
- Audit logs should include the following types of information for each event :
  - Event name as identified in the system
  - Event date and time
  - Event source and destination (IP address, hostname, port, etc.)
  - Event actor (user name, ID, role, etc.)
  - Event action (create, read, update, delete, etc.)
  - Event outcome (success, failure, error code, etc.)
  - Event details (file name, path, size, content, etc.)
- Audit logs should be reviewed and monitored regularly to detect any anomalies, errors, or suspicious activities that may indicate a cyber incident .
- Audit logs should be retained long enough to support incident investigations and meet regulatory requirements. The exact period of audit log data retention may vary depending on the service, system, or network.
