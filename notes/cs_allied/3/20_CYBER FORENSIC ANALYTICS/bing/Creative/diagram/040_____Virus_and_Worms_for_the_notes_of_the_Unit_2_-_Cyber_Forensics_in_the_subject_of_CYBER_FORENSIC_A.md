### Virus and Worms

- A virus is a malicious program that attaches itself to another program or file and executes when the host is run. A virus can infect other files, modify system settings, corrupt data, display messages, or perform other harmful actions. A virus requires human intervention to spread from one system to another, such as opening an infected email attachment or running an infected executable file  .
- A worm is a malicious program that replicates itself and spreads through a network without human intervention. A worm can consume network bandwidth, create backdoors, install other malware, delete or encrypt files, or perform other harmful actions. A worm exploits vulnerabilities in software or protocols to propagate itself to other systems   .
- Some examples of virus and worm attacks are:
  - The ILOVEYOU virus was a Visual Basic script that infected millions of Windows computers in 2000. It spread through email attachments with the subject line "ILOVEYOU" and the attachment "LOVE-LETTER-FOR-YOU.txt.vbs". It overwrote files, stole passwords, and emailed itself to contacts in the address book.
  - The Conficker worm was a Windows worm that infected millions of computers in 2008. It exploited a vulnerability in the Windows Server service and used dictionary attacks to spread through network shares. It created a botnet that could download and execute other malware, disable security services, and block access to security websites.
  - The Stuxnet worm was a sophisticated cyberweapon that targeted industrial control systems in Iran's nuclear facilities in 2010. It spread through removable drives and network shares, and used four zero-day exploits to infect Windows systems. It altered the speed of centrifuges and caused them to malfunction, while hiding its presence from operators.
