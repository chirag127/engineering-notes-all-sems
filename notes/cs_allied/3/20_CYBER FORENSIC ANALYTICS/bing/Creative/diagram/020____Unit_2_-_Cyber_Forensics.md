## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics, also known as computer or digital forensics, is the branch of digital forensic science that deals with the investigation and analysis of evidence from electronic devices, such as computers, smartphones, tablets, etc.   

The main objectives of cyber forensics are:

- To identify the source, nature, and extent of a cyberattack or a cybercrime.
- To collect, preserve, and analyze the digital evidence in a forensically sound manner.
- To present the findings and conclusions in a clear and convincing way in a court of law or other legal proceedings.

Some of the common types of cyber forensics are:

- Disk forensics: The examination of data stored on physical or logical disks, such as hard drives, flash drives, CDs, DVDs, etc.
- Memory forensics: The analysis of data stored in the volatile memory of a device, such as RAM, cache, registers, etc.
- Network forensics: The monitoring and capture of network traffic and packets, such as TCP/IP, UDP, HTTP, FTP, etc.
- Mobile forensics: The extraction and analysis of data from mobile devices, such as phones, tablets, GPS, etc.
- Cloud forensics: The investigation of data stored or processed in cloud computing environments, such as SaaS, PaaS, IaaS, etc.

Some of the common techniques and tools used in cyber forensics are:

- Imaging: The creation of a bit-by-bit copy of a disk or a memory, which preserves the original state and integrity of the evidence.
- Hashing: The generation of a unique numerical value for a file or a disk, which can be used to verify its authenticity and detect any changes or tampering.
- File carving: The recovery of deleted or fragmented files from a disk or a memory, based on their file signatures or headers.
- Timeline analysis: The reconstruction of the chronological sequence of events and activities on a device, based on the timestamps and metadata of files and artifacts.
- Keyword searching: The identification of relevant files or data based on specific words or phrases, using regular expressions or other methods.
- Data hiding and encryption: The detection and decryption of hidden or encrypted data, using steganography, cryptography, or other techniques.

Some of the common careers and roles in cyber forensics are:

- Computer forensics specialist: A more entry-level position that focuses on scanning and researching a breach or a crime scene, using various tools and methods. 
- Computer forensics analyst: A more advanced position that involves analyzing and interpreting the digital evidence, using various techniques and skills.
- Computer forensics examiner: A more senior position that involves conducting and overseeing the entire forensic process, from collection to presentation, using various standards and procedures.
- Computer forensics consultant: A more independent position that involves providing expert advice and guidance to clients or organizations on cyber forensics matters, using various knowledge and experience.
