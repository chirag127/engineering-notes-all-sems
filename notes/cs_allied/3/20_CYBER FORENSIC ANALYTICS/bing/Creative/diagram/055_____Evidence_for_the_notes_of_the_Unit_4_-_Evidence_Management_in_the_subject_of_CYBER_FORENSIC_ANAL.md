### Evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence management is the process of protecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a cyber forensic investigation .
- Evidence management involves the following steps :
  - Identification: finding and selecting potential sources of digital evidence related to the cybercrime, such as computers, mobile phones, networks, etc.
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting the digital evidence using proper tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, logging, etc.
  - Preservation: storing and securing the digital evidence in a way that prevents any alteration, damage, loss or unauthorized access, such as using tamper-evident bags, labels, locks, etc.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting the digital evidence using various methods and tools, such as data recovery, file carving, malware analysis, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and presenting the digital evidence and the findings of the analysis in a clear, concise and accurate manner, such as using charts, graphs, tables, etc.
- Evidence management requires following certain principles and standards to ensure the admissibility, reliability and validity of the digital evidence in a court of law   . Some of these principles and standards are:
  - Chain of custody: maintaining a record of every person who handled or accessed the digital evidence, along with the date, time, location and purpose of each action, such as using forms, logs, signatures, etc.
  - Integrity: ensuring that the digital evidence is not modified, deleted, overwritten or corrupted during the collection, preservation, analysis and presentation stages, such as using hashing, encryption, write-protection, etc.
  - Relevance: ensuring that the digital evidence is related to the cybercrime and the investigation objectives, such as using keywords, filters, queries, etc.
  - Authenticity: ensuring that the digital evidence is original and not fabricated, forged or manipulated, such as using metadata, timestamps, signatures, etc.
  - Completeness: ensuring that the digital evidence is sufficient and comprehensive to support the investigation and the prosecution, such as using multiple sources, cross-validation, corroboration, etc.
