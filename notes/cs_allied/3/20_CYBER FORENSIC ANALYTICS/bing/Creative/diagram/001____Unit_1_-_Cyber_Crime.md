## Unit 1 - Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime is the term used to describe criminal activities carried out by means of computers or the internet.
- Cyber crime can target individuals, corporations, or governments, and can affect their information, privacy, finances, or reputation.
- Cyber crime can take various forms, such as:
  - Fraud: using deception or manipulation to obtain money, goods, or services from others online.
  - Identity theft: stealing or misusing personal or financial information of others online, such as passwords, credit card numbers, or social security numbers.
  - Hacking: gaining unauthorized access to a computer system or network, often to damage, modify, or steal data or disrupt services.
  - Cyberstalking: harassing, threatening, or intimidating someone online, often using personal information or electronic communication tools.
  - Cyberbullying: using electronic communication tools to send or post harmful or abusive messages or images about someone online, often to cause emotional distress or damage their reputation.
  - Phishing: sending fraudulent emails or messages that appear to be from legitimate sources, such as banks, companies, or government agencies, to trick recipients into revealing personal or financial information or clicking on malicious links or attachments.
  - Malware: malicious software that can infect, damage, or control a computer system or network, such as viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, spyware, or adware.
  - Child pornography: producing, distributing, or accessing sexually explicit images or videos of minors online, often using peer-to-peer networks, chat rooms, or social media platforms.
  - Intellectual property theft: violating the rights of the creators or owners of original works, such as music, movies, books, or software, by copying, downloading, or sharing them online without permission or payment.
  - Cyberterrorism: using computers or the internet to cause fear, violence, or disruption for political, religious, or ideological purposes, such as hacking into critical infrastructure, spreading propaganda, or launching cyberattacks.
