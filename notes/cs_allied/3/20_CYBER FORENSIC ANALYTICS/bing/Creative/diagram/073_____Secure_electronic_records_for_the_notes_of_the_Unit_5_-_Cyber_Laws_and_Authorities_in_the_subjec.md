### Secure electronic records for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Secure electronic records are digital documents or data that are protected from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction by using encryption, authentication, or other security measures.
- Cyber laws are the legal rules and regulations that govern the use of information and communication technologies (ICTs) in cyberspace, such as the internet, computer networks, mobile devices, and cloud computing.
- Cyber laws cover various aspects of ICTs, such as intellectual property, contract, jurisdiction, data protection, privacy, and freedom of expression.
- Cyber laws also provide legal recognition to e-documents, e-signatures, e-contracts, and e-commerce transactions, as long as they meet certain security standards.
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, depending on the national legislation, policies, and practices. Some examples of cyber laws in different countries are:
  - The Information Technology Act 2000 (IT Act) in India, which is the primary law that deals with cybercrime, cybersecurity, and e-governance in the country. The IT Act also defines the roles and responsibilities of various authorities, such as the Central Government, the State Governments, the Certifying Authorities, the Controller of Certifying Authorities, the Adjudicating Officers, the Cyber Appellate Tribunal, and the Cyber Regulations Advisory Committee.
  - The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act 1996 (HIPAA) in the United States, which is the main law that regulates the privacy and security of electronic protected health information (e-PHI) in the health care sector. The HIPAA Security Rule establishes a national set of security standards for protecting e-PHI that is held or transferred in electronic form, such as encryption, access control, audit trails, and risk assessment.
  - The General Data Protection Regulation 2016 (GDPR) in the European Union, which is the comprehensive law that harmonizes the data protection and privacy rules across the EU member states. The GDPR applies to any organization that processes personal data of individuals in the EU, regardless of where the organization is located. The GDPR requires organizations to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security, confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data, such as pseudonymization, data minimization, data breach notification, and data protection impact assessment.
- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of scientific methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and present digital evidence in cybercrime investigations, litigation, or audits. Cyber forensic analytics involves the use of various tools and methods, such as data extraction, data recovery, data analysis, data visualization, data mining, machine learning, and artificial intelligence, to identify, preserve, examine, and interpret digital data from various sources, such as computers, mobile devices, networks, cloud services, and social media platforms.
- Cyber forensic analytics can help in solving various types of cybercrimes, such as hacking, phishing, identity theft, fraud, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, cyberterrorism, and cyberwarfare. Cyber forensic analytics can also help in ensuring compliance with cyber laws and regulations, such as the IT Act, the HIPAA, and the GDPR, by detecting and preventing data breaches, data theft, data tampering, and data misuse.
