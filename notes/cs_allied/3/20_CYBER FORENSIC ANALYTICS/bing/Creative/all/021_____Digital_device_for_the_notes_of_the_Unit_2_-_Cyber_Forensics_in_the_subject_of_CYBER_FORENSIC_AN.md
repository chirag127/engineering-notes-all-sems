# Digital device for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

- Cyber forensics is the discipline of finding, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence from various devices, such as computers, mobile phones, or networks, to solve computer-related crimes or incidents  .
- Digital evidence is information stored or transmitted in binary form that may be relied on in court. It can be found on a computer hard drive, a mobile phone, a flash drive, a cloud service, among other places .
- Digital forensics involves a structured process of collecting, identifying, and validating the digital information to reconstruct past events. The process may also include seizing or preserving devices to prevent the evidence from being tampered with or destroyed.
- Digital forensics can be applied to various domains, such as cybercrime, consumer protection, law enforcement, intelligence, or national security  . It can help to track down the culprits, protect the victims, or prevent future attacks .
- Digital forensics requires various tools and techniques to extract, analyze, and present the digital evidence. Some of the tools are:
  - Memory forensics tools, such as FTK Imager or Magnet RAM Capture, that can capture the volatile data from the memory of a device.
  - Disk forensics tools, such as Autopsy or EnCase, that can analyze the data from the hard drive or other storage media of a device.
  - Network forensics tools, such as Wireshark or NetworkMiner, that can monitor and capture the network traffic or packets of a device.
  - Mobile forensics tools, such as Cellebrite or Oxygen Forensic, that can access and extract the data from the mobile phone or tablet of a device.
  - Cloud forensics tools, such as Cloud Forensics Tools or Cloud Extractor, that can access and extract the data from the cloud service or account of a device.
- Digital forensics faces various challenges and limitations, such as:
  - The large and increasing volume and variety of digital data and devices  .
  - The dynamic and evolving nature of digital data and devices  .
  - The legal and ethical issues of accessing, handling, and preserving digital data and devices  .
  - The lack of standardization and interoperability of digital forensics tools and methods  .
  - The shortage of skilled and qualified digital forensics professionals and resources  .
