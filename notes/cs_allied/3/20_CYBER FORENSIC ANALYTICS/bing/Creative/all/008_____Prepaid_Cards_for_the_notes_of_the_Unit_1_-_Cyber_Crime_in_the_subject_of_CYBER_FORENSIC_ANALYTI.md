# Prepaid Cards

- Prepaid cards are cards that are loaded with a certain amount of money and can be used for various purposes, such as gift cards, travel cards, payroll cards, etc.
- Prepaid cards are different from regular credit and debit cards issued by banks, as they do not require a bank account, a credit check, or a personal identification number (PIN) to use them.
- Prepaid cards are convenient and flexible, but they also pose a number of risks for cyber crime, such as fraud, money laundering, and terrorism financing.

## Fraud

- Prepaid card fraud happens when stolen prepaid card information is used to make a purchase or when a fraudster buys a prepaid card with stolen payment information.
- Prepaid card fraud is difficult to detect and prevent, as prepaid cards are often not tied to specific identities, and they can be used anonymously and across borders.
- Prepaid card fraud can cause financial losses for cardholders, merchants, and card issuers, as well as damage the reputation and trust of the prepaid card industry.
- Some examples of prepaid card fraud are:

  - Skimming: A device is attached to a card reader to capture the card information and transfer it to another card or device.
  - Phishing: A fraudulent email or website is used to trick cardholders into revealing their card information or personal details.
  - Scams: Victims of scams are pressured into handing over hundreds or thousands of dollars through prepaid cards, such as gift cards from iTunes and Google Play.

## Money Laundering

- Money laundering is the process of concealing the origin, ownership, or destination of illegally obtained money.
- Prepaid cards can be used for money laundering, as they can be purchased with cash or other illicit funds, and then used to make purchases or transfers that are hard to trace.
- Prepaid cards can also be used to evade currency reporting requirements, as they can be loaded with large amounts of money and carried across borders without detection.
- Some examples of money laundering using prepaid cards are:

  - Structuring: Breaking down large amounts of cash into smaller amounts and loading them onto multiple prepaid cards to avoid suspicion or reporting.
  - Layering: Moving money through various prepaid cards, accounts, or transactions to obscure the source or destination of the funds.
  - Integration: Using prepaid cards to purchase goods or services that can be sold or exchanged for clean money.

## Terrorism Financing

- Terrorism financing is the provision or collection of funds, by any means, directly or indirectly, with the intention or knowledge that they will be used to carry out terrorist acts or support terrorist organizations.
- Prepaid cards can be used for terrorism financing, as they can provide a convenient and anonymous way of transferring funds to terrorists or their affiliates.
- Prepaid cards can also be used to purchase weapons, explosives, or other materials that can be used for terrorist attacks.
- Some examples of terrorism financing using prepaid cards are:

  - Hawala: A system of informal money transfer that relies on a network of brokers who use prepaid cards or other means to settle accounts without leaving a paper trail.
  - Crowdfunding: A method of raising funds from a large number of people through online platforms or social media, where prepaid cards can be used to collect or donate money anonymously.
  - Smuggling: A practice of moving goods or people across borders illegally, where prepaid cards can be used to pay for the transportation or facilitation of terrorists or their materials.
