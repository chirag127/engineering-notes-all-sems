# Concepts of Investigation for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

Cyber investigation is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence from electronic devices to support criminal or civil cases involving cyber crimes. Cyber investigation involves the following concepts:

- **Identification**: This is the first step of cyber investigation, where the investigator identifies what evidence is present, where it is stored, and how it is stored (in which format). Electronic devices can be personal computers, mobile phones, PDAs, etc. Identification also involves determining the scope, objectives, and legal authority of the investigation .
- **Preservation**: This is the second step of cyber investigation, where the investigator isolates, secures, and preserves the data from the electronic devices. Preservation ensures that the data is not altered, destroyed, or corrupted during the investigation. Preservation also involves creating a chain of custody and documenting the actions taken on the evidence .
- **Analysis**: This is the third step of cyber investigation, where the investigator reconstructs fragments of data and draws conclusions based on the evidence. Analysis involves using various tools and techniques to extract, recover, decrypt, and interpret the data. Analysis also involves identifying the sources, destinations, and activities of the data, as well as the suspects, victims, and witnesses involved in the case  .
- **Documentation**: This is the fourth step of cyber investigation, where the investigator creates a record of all the visible data and the findings of the analysis. Documentation involves writing reports, summaries, and logs that describe the evidence, the methods used, the results obtained, and the conclusions drawn. Documentation also involves presenting the evidence and the findings in a clear, concise, and accurate manner that can be understood by the court or the client  .
- **Digital Evidence**: This is the main concept of cyber investigation, where the data from the electronic devices is used as proof for a crime or a dispute. Digital evidence can be any type of data, such as text, images, audio, video, emails, logs, etc. Digital evidence must be relevant, reliable, authentic, and admissible in the court or the client. Digital evidence must also be collected, preserved, analyzed, and documented following proper procedures and standards .
