# Network components for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Network components are the devices, systems and software that enable the transmission and exchange of data over a network.
- Network components can be classified into three categories: end devices, intermediate devices and network media.
- End devices are the sources and destinations of data on a network, such as computers, servers, printers, phones, cameras, etc.
- Intermediate devices are the devices that connect end devices and facilitate data flow, such as routers, switches, firewalls, access points, etc.
- Network media are the physical or wireless means of carrying data signals, such as copper cables, fiber optics, radio waves, etc.
- Network components are essential for network forensics, which is the science of discovering and retrieving information surrounding a cybercrime within a networked environment .
- Network forensics involves the capture, recording and analysis of network packets, which are the units of data that travel across a network, in order to determine the source and nature of network security attacks.
- Network forensics can help identify the attackers, their methods, their motives, their targets, their tools, their location, their impact and their evidence.
- Network forensics can also help reconstruct the timeline and sequence of events that occurred on a network, as well as recover deleted or encrypted data.
- Network forensics can be performed in two modes: live or post-mortem. Live forensics involves monitoring and analyzing network traffic in real time, while post-mortem forensics involves examining stored network data after an incident.
- Network forensics requires specialized tools and techniques, such as packet capture devices, packet analyzers, network scanners, network mappers, network sniffers, network intrusion detection systems, network intrusion prevention systems, etc.
- Network forensics is one of the key components in a defense team’s arsenal, as it can help prevent, detect, respond and recover from network security incidents, as well as provide digital evidence that can be relied on in court  .
