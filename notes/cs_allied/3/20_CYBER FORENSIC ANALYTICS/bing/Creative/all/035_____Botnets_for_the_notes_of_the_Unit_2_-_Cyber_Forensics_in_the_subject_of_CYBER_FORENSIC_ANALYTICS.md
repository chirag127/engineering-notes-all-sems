# Botnets

- A botnet is a network of compromised computers controlled by attackers, usually for malicious purposes such as spamming, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, stealing sensitive information, or spreading malware .
- Botnet forensics is the science that determines the scope of the breach and applies the methodology to find out the type of the infection. It is an investigation of the botnet attacks that includes a collection of activities like collection, identification, detection, acquisition, and attribution.
- Botnet forensics uses postmortem techniques to collect, identify, detect, examine, analyze, and postmortem document for bot shreds of evidence from digital sources. It uses network security tools to uncover facts related to the cybercrimes specifically on the botnet.
- Botnet forensics can be challenging due to the dynamic and distributed nature of botnets, the large volume of data involved, the encryption and obfuscation techniques used by botmasters, and the legal and ethical issues related to the investigation .
- Botnet forensics can be performed using a framework that consists of three phases: malware, botnet forensic investigator, and botnet forensic analyzer.
  - Malware: The first phase is the Malware phase. It involves propagation, infection, communication, and attack that are performed by the botnet. The malware phase can be further divided into four subphases: infection vector, command and control (C&C) channel, botnet topology, and botnet payload.
  - Botnet Forensic Investigator: This is the second phase of the Botnet Forensics Framework. This phase focuses on the identification and acquisition of the botnet evidence from the infected machines and the network traffic. The botnet forensic investigator can use various tools and techniques such as malware analysis, network traffic analysis, network forensics, and memory forensics to collect and preserve the botnet evidence.
  - Botnet Forensic Analyzer: This is the third and final phase of the Botnet Forensics Framework. This phase involves the examination and analysis of the botnet evidence to determine the characteristics, behavior, and impact of the botnet. The botnet forensic analyzer can use various methods such as static analysis, dynamic analysis, reverse engineering, data mining, machine learning, and visualization to analyze the botnet evidence and generate a forensic report.
- Botnet forensics can help in identifying the botnet architecture, protocol, life cycle, C&C server, botmaster, and botnet activities. It can also help in mitigating the botnet threat, preventing future attacks, and prosecuting the attackers .
- Botnet forensics is an important and emerging field of cyber forensics that requires a multidisciplinary approach and a high level of expertise. It can provide valuable insights into the botnet phenomenon and contribute to the cyber security of the Internet of Things (IoT) and other domains.
- Some examples of the biggest botnet attacks to date are:
  - Mirai: A botnet that infected millions of IoT devices such as routers, cameras, and DVRs and launched massive DDoS attacks against various targets such as Dyn, a DNS provider, and Krebs on Security, a security blog, in 2016.
  - Necurs: A botnet that is known for sending spam emails, distributing ransomware and banking trojans, and launching DDoS attacks. It is estimated to have infected over 9 million computers worldwide and is still active as of 2020.
  - Emotet: A botnet that started as a banking trojan but evolved into a modular malware that can deliver various payloads such as ransomware, spyware, and credential stealers. It is also known for spreading via email attachments and exploiting network vulnerabilities. It was disrupted by a global law enforcement operation in 2021.
