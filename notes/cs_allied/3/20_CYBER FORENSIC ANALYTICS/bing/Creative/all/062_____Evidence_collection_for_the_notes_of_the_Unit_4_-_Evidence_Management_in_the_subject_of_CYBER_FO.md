# Evidence collection for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence collection is the process of identifying, preserving, and acquiring digital data that can be used in cyber forensics investigations  .
- Evidence collection involves following a set of procedures and guidelines to ensure the integrity, authenticity, and admissibility of the digital evidence .
- Evidence collection can be performed on various types of digital devices and media, such as computers, hard drives, mobile phones, memory cards, cloud services, etc  .
- Evidence collection can be done in different scenarios, such as live (when the device is powered on and active), dead (when the device is powered off or inactive), remote (when the device is accessed over a network), or onsite (when the device is physically present at the crime scene) .
- Evidence collection requires the use of appropriate tools and techniques, such as write blockers, imaging software, hashing algorithms, encryption, etc., to prevent any alteration, damage, or loss of the digital data  .
- Evidence collection also requires the documentation of the process, such as recording the date, time, location, personnel, actions, observations, and chain of custody of the digital evidence .
- Evidence collection is a crucial step in cyber forensics, as it provides the basis for the analysis, interpretation, and presentation of the digital evidence in court  .
