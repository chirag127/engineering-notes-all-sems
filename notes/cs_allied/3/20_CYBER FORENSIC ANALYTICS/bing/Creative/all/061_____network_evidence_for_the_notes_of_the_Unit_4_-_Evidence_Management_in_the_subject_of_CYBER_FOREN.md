# Network Evidence

Network evidence is the information that can be obtained from the analysis of network traffic data, which can be used to establish the source, nature, and impact of cyberattacks or cybercrimes  . Network evidence can be collected from different sources, such as:

- **Network devices**: These include routers, switches, firewalls, intrusion detection systems (IDS), intrusion prevention systems (IPS), and other network equipment that can capture, record, or log network traffic data .
- **Network hosts**: These include computers, servers, mobile devices, and other network endpoints that can generate, receive, or store network traffic data .
- **Network applications**: These include web browsers, email clients, chat programs, and other network applications that can communicate or exchange data over the network .

Network evidence can be classified into two types, depending on the method of collection:

- **Live network evidence**: This is the network evidence that is collected in real-time or near real-time, while the network is operational and the data is still in transit or in memory . Live network evidence can provide more dynamic and volatile information, such as the current state of network connections, active processes, or open ports . However, live network evidence can also be more challenging to collect, as it requires specialized tools, skills, and permissions, and it can also affect the network performance or integrity .
- **Static network evidence**: This is the network evidence that is collected after the network data has been stored or archived, such as in log files, packet captures, or disk images . Static network evidence can provide more reliable and persistent information, such as the historical records of network events, transactions, or communications . However, static network evidence can also be more limited and incomplete, as it depends on the availability, quality, and integrity of the stored or archived data .

Network evidence can be used for various purposes, such as:

- **Information gathering**: This is the process of collecting and analyzing network evidence to gain more insight into the network environment, such as the network topology, configuration, protocols, services, or devices . Information gathering can help to identify the network assets, vulnerabilities, or threats, and to plan or prepare for further network investigation or security measures .
- **Legal evidence**: This is the process of collecting and presenting network evidence to support or refute a legal claim or allegation, such as a cybercrime or a cyberattack  . Legal evidence can help to establish the facts, circumstances, or responsibilities of a network incident, and to prove or disprove the guilt, innocence, or liability of the involved parties  .
- **Intrusion detection**: This is the process of collecting and analyzing network evidence to detect and respond to unauthorized or malicious activities on the network, such as a cyberattack or a cyber intrusion  . Intrusion detection can help to identify the source, nature, and impact of a network attack, and to mitigate or prevent further damage or compromise  .

Network evidence management is the process of handling network evidence in a systematic and consistent manner, to ensure its validity, reliability, and admissibility . Network evidence management involves the following steps:

- **Identification**: This is the step of locating and recognizing the sources and types of network evidence that are relevant and useful for the network investigation or security purpose .
- **Collection**: This is the step of acquiring and extracting the network evidence from the network devices, hosts, or applications, using appropriate tools, techniques, and procedures .
- **Preservation**: This is the step of protecting and maintaining the network evidence from any alteration, loss, or damage, using proper storage, backup, or encryption methods .
- **Analysis**: This is the step of examining and interpreting the network evidence to extract meaningful and actionable information, using suitable software, algorithms, or methods .
