# Evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence management is the process of protecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a cyber forensic investigation.
- Digital evidence is information stored or transmitted in binary form that may be relied on in court, such as data from computers, mobile phones, networks, cloud services, etc.
- Evidence management involves the following steps :
  - Identification: finding and selecting potential sources of digital evidence related to the cybercrime or incident.
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting the digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, logging, etc.
  - Preservation: ensuring the integrity, authenticity and continuity of the digital evidence by preventing any alteration, loss, damage or contamination.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting the digital evidence using various methods, such as file carving, data recovery, malware analysis, network analysis, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the findings and conclusions of the digital evidence analysis in a clear, concise and accurate manner, using charts, graphs, tables, etc.
  - Disposition: storing, transferring or disposing of the digital evidence according to the legal and ethical requirements and standards.
- Evidence management requires the following skills and knowledge :
  - Technical: understanding the structure, format and function of various types of digital data and devices, and the tools and techniques to access, extract and analyze them.
  - Legal: understanding the laws, regulations and standards that govern the admissibility, relevance and reliability of digital evidence in court, and the procedures and protocols to follow them.
  - Ethical: understanding the ethical principles and values that guide the conduct and behavior of digital forensic practitioners, and the implications and consequences of their actions.
  - Critical: applying logical and analytical thinking to evaluate the digital evidence and its sources, and to draw valid and sound conclusions from them.
  - Communication: using effective verbal and written skills to convey the digital evidence and its analysis in a clear, concise and accurate manner, and to answer questions and challenges from the stakeholders.
