# Future Trends and Emerging Concerns in Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a legally admissible manner. Cyber forensics plays a vital role in combating cybercrime, terrorism, espionage, and other malicious activities in the cyber domain. However, cyber forensics also faces many challenges and issues that need to be addressed in order to keep up with the evolving technology and threat landscape. Some of the future trends and emerging concerns in cyber forensics are:

- **High speed and volumes**: The proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) and cyber-physical systems (CPS) devices, such as smart cars, smart homes, smart cities, and smart grids, generates a huge amount of data that needs to be collected and analyzed in a timely manner. Moreover, these devices often have limited storage, processing, and communication capabilities, which pose challenges for forensic acquisition and examination . Additionally, the increasing use of cloud computing, big data, and artificial intelligence (AI) also creates new challenges for data integrity, privacy, and provenance in cyber forensics .

- **Explosion of complexity**: The complexity of digital systems and networks is increasing due to the diversity of devices, platforms, protocols, applications, and encryption techniques. This makes it harder for forensic analysts to identify, isolate, and interpret relevant digital evidence. Furthermore, the complexity of cyberattacks is also increasing, as attackers use sophisticated methods, such as malware, ransomware, botnets, denial-of-service (DoS), distributed denial-of-service (DDoS), and advanced persistent threats (APTs), to evade detection and attribution  .

- **Development of standards**: The lack of universally accepted standards and best practices for cyber forensics hampers the interoperability, compatibility, and quality of forensic tools and methods. Moreover, the lack of standards also affects the admissibility and reliability of digital evidence in courts of law, as different jurisdictions may have different legal requirements and procedures for cyber forensics. Therefore, there is a need for developing and adopting common standards and frameworks for cyber forensics, such as the ISO/IEC 27037 and 27041 standards, the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, and the Scientific Working Group on Digital Evidence (SWGDE) guidelines  .

- **Privacy-preserving investigations**: The protection of privacy and personal data is a fundamental human right and a legal obligation for many organizations and individuals. However, cyber forensics may involve the collection and analysis of sensitive and confidential data, such as personal information, financial records, health records, and communication logs, which may infringe on the privacy rights of the data owners and subjects. Therefore, there is a need for developing and applying privacy-preserving techniques and methods for cyber forensics, such as encryption, anonymization, pseudonymization, differential privacy, and homomorphic encryption, that can balance the trade-off between forensic utility and privacy protection  .

- **Legitimacy**: The legitimacy of cyber forensics refers to the ethical, legal, and social aspects of conducting forensic investigations and using digital evidence. Cyber forensics may raise various ethical dilemmas and legal challenges, such as the authorization, justification, and proportionality of forensic actions, the respect for human dignity and rights, the accountability and transparency of forensic processes and outcomes, and the trustworthiness and credibility of forensic experts and evidence. Therefore, there is a need for developing and following ethical codes and legal frameworks for cyber forensics, such as the Association of Digital Forensics, Security and Law (ADFSL) Code of Ethics, the European Union (EU) General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and the United Nations (UN) Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)  .
