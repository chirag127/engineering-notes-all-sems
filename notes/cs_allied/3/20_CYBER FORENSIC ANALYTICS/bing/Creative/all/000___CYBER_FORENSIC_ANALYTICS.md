# Cyber Forensic Analytics

- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of data analysis techniques and tools to investigate cyber incidents and collect digital evidence for legal purposes.
- Cyber forensic analytics involves the following steps:
  - Identification: The process of identifying the sources and scope of the cyber incident, such as the devices, networks, and systems involved, the type and extent of the damage, and the potential suspects and motives.
  - Preservation: The process of securing and isolating the digital evidence from further alteration, deletion, or corruption, using methods such as hashing, encryption, and write-blocking.
  - Extraction: The process of retrieving the relevant data from the digital evidence, using tools such as forensic software, hardware, and imaging devices.
  - Analysis: The process of examining and interpreting the extracted data, using techniques such as timeline analysis, pattern recognition, anomaly detection, and data visualization, to reconstruct the events and activities of the cyber incident and identify the actors and methods involved.
  - Reporting: The process of documenting and presenting the findings and conclusions of the analysis, using formats and standards that are acceptable and admissible in the court of law, such as reports, charts, graphs, and tables.
- Cyber forensic analytics faces the following challenges:
  - Volume: The amount of data involved in cyber incidents can be overwhelming and require significant storage and processing resources.
  - Variety: The types and formats of data involved in cyber incidents can be diverse and complex, such as text, images, audio, video, logs, metadata, and encrypted data.
  - Velocity: The speed and frequency of data generation and transmission in cyber incidents can be high and require real-time or near-real-time analysis and response.
  - Volatility: The availability and accessibility of data involved in cyber incidents can be short-lived and require timely and proactive preservation and extraction.
  - Veracity: The accuracy and reliability of data involved in cyber incidents can be questionable and require validation and verification.
- Cyber forensic analytics requires the following skills and knowledge:
  - Technical: The ability to use and understand various forensic tools and technologies, such as operating systems, networks, databases, encryption, malware, and cloud computing.
  - Analytical: The ability to apply and evaluate various data analysis techniques and methods, such as statistics, machine learning, artificial intelligence, and data mining.
  - Legal: The ability to comply and adhere to various laws and regulations, such as privacy, intellectual property, and evidence, that govern the collection and use of digital evidence.
  - Ethical: The ability to act and behave in a professional and responsible manner, such as maintaining confidentiality, integrity, and impartiality, when conducting cyber forensic investigations.
