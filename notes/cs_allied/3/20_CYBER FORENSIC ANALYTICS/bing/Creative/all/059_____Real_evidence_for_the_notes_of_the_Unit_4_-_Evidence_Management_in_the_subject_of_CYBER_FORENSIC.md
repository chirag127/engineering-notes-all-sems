# Real evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Real evidence is any physical object that can be directly observed or examined by the court .
- Real evidence can be used to prove or disprove a fact or a connection between a suspect and a crime .
- Real evidence can also be used to demonstrate how a crime was committed or what the motive was .
- Examples of real evidence in cyber forensics are:
  - The actual computer, hard drive, mobile phone, or other device that contains digital evidence  .
  - The fingerprints, DNA, or other biometric data that can be extracted from the device or its peripherals .
  - The physical documents, notes, or other materials that can be related to the digital evidence or the crime .
- Real evidence must be properly collected, preserved, and analyzed by cyber forensic experts following certain procedures  .
- The procedures that cyber forensic experts follow are:
  - Identification: The first step is to identify what evidence is present, where it is stored, and in which format it is stored.
  - Collection: The next step is to collect the evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, or seizing  .
  - Preservation: The third step is to preserve the evidence in a secure and tamper-proof manner, such as using write blockers, encryption, or chain of custody  .
  - Analysis: The fourth step is to analyze the evidence using specialized software and methods, such as data recovery, data carving, or malware analysis  .
  - Presentation: The final step is to present the evidence in a clear and convincing manner, such as using reports, graphs, or testimony  .
- Real evidence can be challenged by the opposing party on the grounds of relevance, authenticity, reliability, or admissibility .
- Real evidence can be enhanced by the use of other types of evidence, such as testimonial, documentary, or demonstrative evidence .
