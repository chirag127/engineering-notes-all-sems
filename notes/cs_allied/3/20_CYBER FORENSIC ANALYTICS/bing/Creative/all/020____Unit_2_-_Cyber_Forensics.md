## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

- Cyber forensics is the application of scientific methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and present digital evidence in a court of law.
- Cyber forensics can be used to investigate various types of cybercrimes, such as hacking, phishing, identity theft, malware, fraud, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, etc.
- Cyber forensics involves four main phases: preservation, acquisition, analysis, and reporting.
  - Preservation: The process of securing and protecting the digital evidence from tampering, alteration, or loss.
  - Acquisition: The process of extracting and copying the digital evidence from the original source, such as a computer, a mobile device, a network, or a cloud service.
  - Analysis: The process of examining and interpreting the digital evidence using various tools and techniques, such as file carving, hashing, encryption, steganography, etc.
  - Reporting: The process of documenting and presenting the findings and conclusions of the analysis in a clear, concise, and accurate manner, following the legal and ethical standards.
- Cyber forensics requires a multidisciplinary approach, involving knowledge and skills from various fields, such as computer science, law, criminology, psychology, etc.
- Cyber forensics faces various challenges and limitations, such as the volatility of digital evidence, the diversity and complexity of digital devices and systems, the encryption and obfuscation of data, the jurisdiction and privacy issues, the lack of standards and regulations, etc.
- Cyber forensics aims to achieve the following objectives: 
  - To identify the source, nature, and extent of the cybercrime.
  - To recover and preserve the digital evidence related to the cybercrime.
  - To analyze and interpret the digital evidence to reconstruct the events and actions of the cybercrime.
  - To report and testify the findings and conclusions of the analysis in a court of law.
  - To assist in the prevention, detection, and deterrence of future cybercrimes.
