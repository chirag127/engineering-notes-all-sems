# Stylometric Techniques for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Stylometry is the statistical analysis of the style of written texts  .
- Stylometry can be used for authorship analysis, which is the task of identifying or verifying the author of a text based on linguistic features .
- Stylometry can also be applied to computer code and intrinsic plagiarism detection, which involves detecting plagiarism based on changes in writing style within the document.
- Stylometry can be useful for cybercrime investigation, as it can help identify or link anonymous or malicious actors who use online platforms for communication or deception .
- Stylometry can also help mitigate the effects of online anonymity abuse, such as identity theft, impersonation, or fraud, by detecting stylometric similarity or dissimilarity among online users.
- Stylometry involves choosing and extracting stylometric features of the text, which are linguistic elements that reflect the author's writing style  .
- Stylometric features can be categorized into different levels of analysis, such as lexical, syntactic, semantic, structural, and subject-specific  .
- Lexical features include character and letter frequencies, word lengths, vocabulary richness, punctuation, and spelling  .
- Syntactic features include sentence lengths, sentence complexity, part-of-speech tags, function words, and n-grams  .
- Semantic features include word meanings, topics, sentiments, and emotions  .
- Structural features include paragraph lengths, headings, subheadings, and formatting  .
- Subject-specific features include domain-specific terms, jargon, acronyms, and references  .
- Stylometry also involves applying stylometric methods, which are statistical or machine learning techniques that use stylometric features to build stylometric classifiers or models  .
- Stylometric methods can be supervised or unsupervised, depending on whether they use labeled or unlabeled data for training .
- Supervised stylometric methods include decision trees, support vector machines, neural networks, and ensemble methods .
- Unsupervised stylometric methods include clustering, principal component analysis, and latent semantic analysis .
- Stylometry has some limitations and challenges, such as the availability and quality of data, the variability and adaptability of writing style, the influence of external factors, and the ethical and legal implications of stylometric analysis   .
