# Profiling for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Profiling is an investigative technique that aims to identify the major personality and behavioral characteristics of the offender based on the analysis of the crime.
- Profiling can be applied to cybercrime investigations to help understand the motives, methods, and targets of cybercriminals, as well as to speculate on their identities and locations  .
- Profiling can be based on different types of data, such as digital footprints, electronic evidence, attack patterns, and geographic information  .
- Profiling can help cyber investigators to narrow down the list of suspects, generate leads, prioritize resources, and formulate strategies  .
- Profiling can also help cyber investigators to classify cybercriminals into different categories, such as hackers, cyberstalkers, cyberterrorists, cyberbullies, etc., based on their goals, skills, and modus operandi .
- Profiling is not a precise science, but rather a heuristic and probabilistic approach that relies on the experience and intuition of the profiler, as well as the availability and quality of the data .
- Profiling can be influenced by various factors, such as the type and complexity of the cybercrime, the diversity and anonymity of the cybercriminals, the dynamic and evolving nature of the cyberspace, and the ethical and legal issues involved  .
- Profiling can benefit from the use of computational methods, such as case-based reasoning, machine learning, data mining, and geographic information systems, to assist the profiler in analyzing large and complex data sets, generating hypotheses, and testing scenarios  .
