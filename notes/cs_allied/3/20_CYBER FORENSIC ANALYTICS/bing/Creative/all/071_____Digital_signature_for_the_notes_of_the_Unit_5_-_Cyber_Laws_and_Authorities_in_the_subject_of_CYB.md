# Digital Signature for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

- A digital signature is a mathematical scheme to verify the authenticity of digital documents or messages.
- A digital signature uses a digital certificate from a trust service provider (TSP), such as a certificate authority (CA), to authenticate a signer's identity.
- A digital signature also provides integrity and non-repudiation of the electronic document or message, meaning that it cannot be altered or denied by the sender.
- A digital signature is created by encrypting the hash result of the message with the private key of the sender.
- A digital signature is verified by decrypting the encrypted hash with the public key of the sender and comparing it with the hash of the received message.
- A digital signature is valid only if the digital certificate of the sender is valid and has not been revoked or expired.
- A digital signature is legal in every state and U.S. territory where federal law applies, according to the U.S. Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce (ESIGN) Act in 2000 .
- A digital signature is also legal in most U.S. states that have adopted the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA), which Congress passed in 1999.
- A digital signature is recognized as a valid mode of authentication under Section 3 (2) of the Information Technology Act, 2000 in India.
- A digital signature is also admissible as evidence under Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.
- A digital signature is an essential tool for ensuring the security and reliability of electronic transactions and communications in the digital era .
