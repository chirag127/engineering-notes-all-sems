# Contextual Information for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence management is the process of protecting, preserving, and documenting the digital evidence that is collected, examined, and presented in cyber forensics investigations.
- Digital evidence is any information stored or transmitted in binary form that may be relevant, reliable, and admissible in court.
- Digital evidence can be found on various electronic devices, such as computers, mobile phones, smart devices, cloud servers, etc .
- Digital evidence can be used to prove or disprove the involvement of a suspect, victim, or witness in a cyber crime, such as hacking, fraud, identity theft, cyber stalking, etc .
- Digital evidence can also be used to recover important, compromised, or deleted data that may have value for the owner, the investigator, or the court .
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and selecting the relevant sources and types of digital evidence for the investigation
  - Collection: acquiring and copying the digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, etc
  - Preservation: securing and storing the digital evidence in a safe and tamper-proof manner, such as using write blockers, encryption, etc
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting the digital evidence using specialized software and methods, such as carving, decryption, etc
  - Presentation: reporting and presenting the digital evidence and the findings in a clear and concise manner, such as using charts, graphs, etc
  - Disposition: disposing or returning the digital evidence according to the legal and ethical requirements, such as deleting, archiving, etc
- Evidence management requires following the principles of integrity, authenticity, and chain of custody of the digital evidence  .
  - Integrity: ensuring that the digital evidence is not altered, modified, or damaged during the evidence management process
  - Authenticity: ensuring that the digital evidence is original, accurate, and trustworthy, and that its source and origin can be verified
  - Chain of custody: ensuring that the digital evidence is tracked and documented throughout the evidence management process, and that its custody and handling are authorized and recorded
- Evidence management also requires following the standards, guidelines, and best practices of the cyber forensics field, such as those issued by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), the Association of Chief Police Officers (ACPO), etc .
- Evidence management is a critical facet of the cyber forensics field, as it can affect the quality, validity, and admissibility of the digital evidence, and thus the outcome of the investigation and the prosecution  .
