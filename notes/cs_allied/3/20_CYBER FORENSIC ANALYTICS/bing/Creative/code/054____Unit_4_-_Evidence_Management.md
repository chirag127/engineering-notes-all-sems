## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

- Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a forensically sound manner.
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and recognizing potential sources of digital evidence, such as devices, files, logs, etc.
  - Collection: acquiring and copying digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, etc.
  - Preservation: protecting digital evidence from alteration, loss, or damage, using methods such as encryption, write-protection, etc.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting digital evidence using specialized software and methods, such as carving, decoding, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the findings and conclusions of digital evidence analysis, using formats such as charts, graphs, tables, etc.
- Evidence management requires following certain principles and standards, such as:
  - Chain of custody: documenting and tracking the history and handling of digital evidence, such as who, what, when, where, why, and how.
  - Admissibility: ensuring that digital evidence is relevant, reliable, and authentic, and that it complies with the rules and laws of the jurisdiction.
  - Integrity: ensuring that digital evidence is not modified, tampered, or corrupted, and that it can be verified using methods such as hashing, digital signatures, etc.
  - Privacy: ensuring that digital evidence is collected and processed in a lawful and ethical manner, and that it respects the rights and interests of the parties involved.
