### Secure electronic records for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Secure electronic records are digital documents or data that are protected from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction by using encryption, authentication, or other security measures.
- Cyber laws are the legal rules and regulations that govern the use of information and communication technologies (ICTs) in cyberspace, such as the internet, computer networks, mobile devices, and cloud computing.
- Cyber laws cover various aspects of ICTs, such as intellectual property, contract, jurisdiction, data protection, privacy, and freedom of expression.
- Cyber laws also provide legal recognition to e-documents, e-signatures, e-contracts, and e-commerce transactions, as long as they are made via secure electronic channels.
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, depending on the national legislation, policies, and practices of each jurisdiction. Some examples of cyber laws in different countries are:
  - The Information Technology Act 2000 (IT Act) in India, which is the primary law that deals with cybercrime, cybersecurity, and e-governance in the country. The IT Act also defines the roles and responsibilities of various authorities, such as the Central Government, the State Governments, the Controller of Certifying Authorities, the Cyber Appellate Tribunal, and the Adjudicating Officers, in regulating and enforcing cyber laws.
  - The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act 1996 (HIPAA) in the United States, which is a federal law that protects the privacy and security of electronic protected health information (e-PHI) held or transferred by covered entities, such as health care providers, health plans, and health care clearinghouses. The HIPAA Security Rule establishes a national set of security standards for protecting e-PHI, such as administrative, technical, and physical safeguards.
  - The General Data Protection Regulation 2016 (GDPR) in the European Union, which is a comprehensive law that regulates the processing of personal data of individuals in the EU and the European Economic Area (EEA). The GDPR sets out the rights and obligations of data subjects, data controllers, and data processors, as well as the principles, conditions, and rules for lawful data processing. The GDPR also imposes strict penalties for data breaches and non-compliance.
- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of scientific methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and present digital evidence in cybercrime investigations, litigation, or dispute resolution. Cyber forensic analytics involves the use of various tools and methods, such as data extraction, data mining, data visualization, data modeling, machine learning, and artificial intelligence, to identify, preserve, recover, and interpret digital data from various sources, such as computers, mobile devices, networks, cloud services, and social media platforms.
- Cyber forensic analytics can help in solving various types of cybercrimes, such as hacking, phishing, identity theft, fraud, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, cyberterrorism, and cyberwarfare. Cyber forensic analytics can also help in enhancing cybersecurity, compliance, and risk management, by detecting and preventing cyberattacks, monitoring and auditing cyber activities, and enforcing cyber laws and policies.
