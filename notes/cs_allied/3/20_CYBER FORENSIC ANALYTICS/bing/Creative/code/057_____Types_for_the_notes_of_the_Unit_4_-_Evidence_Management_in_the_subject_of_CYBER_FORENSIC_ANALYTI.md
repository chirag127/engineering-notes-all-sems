### Types of Evidence in Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence from electronic devices and networks. Cyber forensics can be used to investigate crimes, disputes, security incidents and other matters involving digital data. 

There are different types of evidence that can be obtained from digital sources, depending on the nature and scope of the investigation. Some of the common types of evidence in cyber forensics are:

- **Logs**: Logs are records of events or activities that occur on a system, network, application or device. Logs can provide information such as date, time, source, destination, action, outcome, user, etc. Logs can be found in various formats and locations, such as operating system logs, web server logs, firewall logs, database logs, etc. Logs can help to establish timelines, identify sources and targets, trace actions and outcomes, and verify or refute claims .
- **Video footage and images**: Video footage and images are visual data that can capture scenes, objects, persons, actions, etc. Video footage and images can be obtained from various sources, such as surveillance cameras, webcams, mobile phones, digital cameras, etc. Video footage and images can help to identify suspects, witnesses, victims, locations, vehicles, weapons, etc. Video footage and images can also contain metadata, such as date, time, location, device, etc., that can provide additional information.
- **Archives**: Archives are collections of files or data that are compressed, encrypted, or otherwise packaged together. Archives can be created for various purposes, such as backup, storage, transfer, or concealment. Archives can be found in various formats and locations, such as zip, rar, tar, iso, etc. Archives can help to recover deleted, hidden, or encrypted files or data, and to reveal the contents, structure, and origin of the files or data.
- **Active data**: Active data is the data that is readily accessible and visible on a system, network, application or device. Active data can include files, folders, documents, emails, messages, contacts, calendars, bookmarks, history, etc. Active data can help to provide information about the user, the system, the activity, the communication, etc. Active data can also contain metadata, such as date, time, author, recipient, etc., that can provide additional information .
- **Metadata**: Metadata is the data that describes or provides information about other data. Metadata can include information such as date, time, location, device, author, recipient, format, size, etc. Metadata can be embedded in files, documents, images, videos, etc., or stored in separate files, such as exif, xml, json, etc. Metadata can help to provide context, origin, authenticity, and integrity of the data .
- **Residual data**: Residual data is the data that is not readily accessible or visible on a system, network, application or device, but can be recovered with special tools or techniques. Residual data can include deleted, overwritten, fragmented, or corrupted files or data, temporary files, cache, swap, slack, or unallocated space, etc. Residual data can help to recover lost, hidden, or altered files or data, and to reveal the previous state, activity, or history of the system, network, application or device .
- **Volatile data**: Volatile data is the data that is stored in the memory or cache of a system, network, application or device, and is lost when the power is turned off or the system is rebooted. Volatile data can include information such as processes, services, connections, ports, registry, configuration, etc. Volatile data can help to provide information about the current state, activity, or configuration of the system, network, application or device, and to identify malware, intrusions, or anomalies .
- **Replicant data**: Replicant data is the data that is copied, duplicated, or replicated from one source to another. Replicant data can be created for various purposes, such as backup, synchronization, replication, or distribution. Replicant data can be found in various formats and locations, such as cloud storage, external drives, removable media, etc. Replicant data can help to provide redundancy, availability, and consistency of the data, and to identify the sources, targets, and methods of the replication.
