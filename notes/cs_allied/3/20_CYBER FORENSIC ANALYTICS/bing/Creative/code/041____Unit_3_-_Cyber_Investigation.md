## Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation

- Cyber investigation is the process of collecting, analyzing, and preserving digital evidence from various sources, such as computers, networks, mobile devices, cloud services, and social media platforms.
- Cyber investigation can be conducted for different purposes, such as criminal investigation, civil litigation, intelligence gathering, incident response, or cyber security auditing.
- Cyber investigation requires a combination of technical skills, legal knowledge, and ethical principles, as well as the ability to communicate effectively with various stakeholders, such as law enforcement, lawyers, judges, clients, and the public.
- Cyber investigation follows a general framework that consists of four main phases: preparation, collection, examination, and reporting.

### Preparation
- Preparation is the phase where the cyber investigator defines the scope, objectives, and methodology of the investigation, as well as the legal and ethical issues involved.
- Preparation involves identifying the sources of potential evidence, the tools and techniques to be used, the personnel and resources required, and the risks and challenges to be anticipated.
- Preparation also involves obtaining the necessary authorization, consent, or warrant to access the evidence, as well as ensuring the security and integrity of the evidence and the investigation process.

### Collection
- Collection is the phase where the cyber investigator acquires the digital evidence from the identified sources, using appropriate tools and techniques, and following the best practices and standards.
- Collection involves creating a forensic image or a copy of the original data, without altering or damaging it, and verifying its authenticity and integrity using cryptographic hashes or digital signatures.
- Collection also involves documenting the chain of custody, which is the record of who handled the evidence, when, where, how, and why, as well as the evidence inventory, which is the list of all the evidence items and their characteristics.

### Examination
- Examination is the phase where the cyber investigator analyzes the digital evidence, using various tools and techniques, and following the relevant laws and policies.
- Examination involves extracting, recovering, and interpreting the relevant data, such as files, metadata, logs, network traffic, emails, messages, images, videos, etc., and identifying the patterns, anomalies, and connections among them.
- Examination also involves applying the relevant theories, models, and frameworks, such as the Locard's exchange principle, the digital evidence lifecycle, the cyber kill chain, the diamond model, etc., to understand the context, motive, and impact of the cyber incident.

### Reporting
- Reporting is the phase where the cyber investigator presents the findings and conclusions of the investigation, using various formats and media, and following the expectations and requirements of the audience.
- Reporting involves organizing, summarizing, and visualizing the evidence and the analysis, using tables, charts, graphs, timelines, maps, etc., and highlighting the key facts, issues, and recommendations.
- Reporting also involves writing a clear, concise, and accurate report, using proper language, style, and structure, and providing the necessary references, citations, and appendices.
