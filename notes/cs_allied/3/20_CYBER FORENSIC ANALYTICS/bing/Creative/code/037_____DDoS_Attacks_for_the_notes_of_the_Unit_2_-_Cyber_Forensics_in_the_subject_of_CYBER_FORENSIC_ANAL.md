### DDoS Attacks

- A DDoS attack is a distributed denial-of-service attack that targets websites and servers by disrupting network services .
- A DDoS attack attempts to exhaust an application’s resources by flooding it with HTTP requests and traffic from multiple sources, such as compromised computers or devices .
- A DDoS attack can result in degraded network performance, service outage, or loss of data and revenue for the targeted site or service .
- Some common techniques of DDoS attacks are:
  - SYN flood: The attacker sends a large number of TCP SYN packets to the target, but does not complete the handshake, leaving the target with many half-open connections that consume resources.
  - UDP flood: The attacker sends a large number of UDP packets to random ports on the target, causing the target to respond with ICMP packets that consume bandwidth and processing power.
  - HTTP flood: The attacker sends a large number of HTTP requests to the target, often using a botnet or a web application, that overwhelm the target's web server or application layer.
  - Amplification: The attacker sends a small number of packets with a spoofed source IP address to a third-party server that responds with a larger amount of data to the target, amplifying the attack traffic.
  - Reflection: The attacker sends a small number of packets with a spoofed source IP address to a third-party server that responds with a packet that contains the same data as the request, reflecting the attack traffic.
- Some common types of DDoS attacks are:
  - Volumetric: The attacker tries to consume the bandwidth of the target or its surrounding network by sending a large amount of traffic, such as UDP or ICMP packets .
  - Protocol: The attacker tries to consume the resources of the target or its intermediate network devices, such as routers or firewalls, by exploiting weaknesses in the network protocols, such as TCP or IP .
  - Application layer: The attacker tries to consume the resources of the target's web server or application layer, such as CPU or memory, by sending a large number of requests that require complex processing, such as HTTP or DNS queries .
- Some common motivations of DDoS attacks are:
  - Cybercrime: The attacker tries to extort money from the target by threatening or launching a DDoS attack, or to disrupt the operations of a competitor or rival .
  - Hacktivism: The attacker tries to protest or promote a political or social cause by launching a DDoS attack against a target that represents an opposing view or interest .
  - Cyberwarfare: The attacker tries to damage or disable the critical infrastructure or services of a nation or organization by launching a DDoS attack as part of a larger cyberattack or conflict .
  - Cyber vandalism: The attacker tries to cause mischief or annoyance by launching a DDoS attack against a target for fun or fame .
