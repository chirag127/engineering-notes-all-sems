# Criminal Behaviour

## Unit 1 - Cyber Crime

### Definition of Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime is the use of a computer or the internet to commit illegal or harmful acts .
- Cyber crime can target individuals, corporations, or governments, and can affect their information, privacy, or finances.
- Cyber crime can take various forms, such as fraud, identity theft, hacking, phishing, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, child pornography, cyberterrorism, etc .

### Types of Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime can be classified into three main categories, based on the target and motive of the offender:

  - **Cybercrime against individuals**: This involves crimes that harm or exploit the rights, property, or identity of individuals, such as cyberstalking, cyberbullying, identity theft, online fraud, phishing, etc.
  - **Cybercrime against property**: This involves crimes that damage or interfere with the property or data of individuals, corporations, or governments, such as hacking, malware, ransomware, denial-of-service attacks, etc.
  - **Cybercrime against government**: This involves crimes that threaten or undermine the security, sovereignty, or integrity of a nation or its institutions, such as cyberterrorism, cyberwarfare, espionage, sabotage, etc.

### Causes of Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime can be motivated by various factors, such as greed, revenge, curiosity, boredom, ideology, or thrill .
- Some of the common causes of cyber crime are:

  - **Lack of awareness**: Many users are unaware of the risks and consequences of cyber crime, and do not take adequate measures to protect themselves or their data online.
  - **Lack of regulation**: Many countries do not have clear or consistent laws or policies to prevent or prosecute cyber crime, and there is a lack of international cooperation and coordination to combat cyber crime across borders .
  - **Lack of security**: Many systems and networks are vulnerable to cyber attacks due to weak or outdated security measures, such as passwords, encryption, firewalls, antivirus, etc .
  - **Lack of ethics**: Many offenders do not have a sense of morality or responsibility for their actions, and do not care about the harm or damage they cause to others or to society .

### Effects of Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime can have serious and lasting impacts on the victims, the offenders, and the society, such as:

  - **Economic losses**: Cyber crime can cause financial losses to individuals, corporations, or governments, due to theft, fraud, extortion, or damage to property or data .
  - **Psychological distress**: Cyber crime can cause emotional or mental harm to individuals, such as fear, anxiety, depression, anger, or trauma, due to harassment, bullying, stalking, or identity theft .
  - **Social disruption**: Cyber crime can affect the functioning and stability of social institutions, such as education, health, justice, or democracy, due to misinformation, propaganda, hacking, or terrorism .
  - **Legal consequences**: Cyber crime can result in legal actions or penalties for the offenders, such as fines, imprisonment, or extradition, depending on the nature and severity of the crime and the jurisdiction of the law .

### Prevention of Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime can be prevented or reduced by various measures, such as:

  - **Education and awareness**: Users should be educated and informed about the types, causes, and effects of cyber crime, and the ways to protect themselves and their data online .
  - **Regulation and enforcement**: Governments should enact and implement clear and consistent laws and policies to prevent and prosecute cyber crime, and cooperate and coordinate with other countries and organizations to combat cyber crime across borders .
