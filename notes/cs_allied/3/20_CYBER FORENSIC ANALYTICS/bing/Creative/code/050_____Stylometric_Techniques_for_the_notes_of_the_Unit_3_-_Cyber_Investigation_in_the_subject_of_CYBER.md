### Stylometric Techniques for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Stylometry is the statistical analysis of the style of written texts  .
- Stylometry can be used for authorship analysis, which is the task of identifying the author of a text based on linguistic features .
- Stylometry can also be applied to computer code and intrinsic plagiarism detection, which involves detecting plagiarism based on changes in writing style within the document.
- Stylometry can be useful for cyber investigation, as it can help identify the anonymous or malicious actors behind online texts, such as phishing emails, social media posts, blogs, forums, etc .
- Stylometry involves choosing and extracting stylometric features from the text, such as lexical, syntactic, semantic, structural, and subject-specific features  .
- Lexical features include character and letter frequencies, word lengths, vocabulary richness, etc  .
- Syntactic features include punctuation, sentence lengths, part-of-speech tags, grammatical structures, etc  .
- Semantic features include word meanings, topics, sentiments, etc  .
- Structural features include paragraph lengths, headings, indentation, formatting, etc  .
- Subject-specific features include domain-specific terms, jargon, acronyms, etc  .
- Stylometry also involves applying statistical or machine learning methods to compare and classify texts based on their stylometric features  .
- Some of the methods include distance measures, cluster analysis, principal component analysis, support vector machines, neural networks, etc  .
- Stylometry has some limitations and challenges, such as the availability and quality of training data, the variability and adaptability of writing styles, the influence of external factors such as genre, topic, audience, etc  .
- Stylometry also raises some ethical and legal issues, such as the privacy and anonymity of authors, the validity and reliability of stylometric evidence, the potential for misuse and abuse of stylometric techniques, etc  .
