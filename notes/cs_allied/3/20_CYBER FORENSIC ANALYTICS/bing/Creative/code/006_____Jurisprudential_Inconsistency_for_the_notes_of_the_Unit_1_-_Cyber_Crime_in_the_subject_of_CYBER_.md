### Jurisprudential Inconsistency for the notes of the Unit 1 - Cyber Crime in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Jurisprudential inconsistency refers to the lack of uniformity or coherence in the legal system or the legal theory that deals with cyber crime and digital forensics.
- Cyber crime challenges the traditional notions of crime and jurisdiction because it is non-local in nature, which throws law enforcement into confusion regarding who should investigate and prosecute the criminal.
- Digital forensics investigations jurisprudence is the theory and philosophy of the study of law and the principles upon which a law is based. For digital evidence to appear at court and be legally admissible, the evidence must be authentic, accurate, complete, and convincing to the jury.
- Jurisprudential inconsistency can arise from various sources, such as:
  - Different legal systems or jurisdictions that have different laws, rules, standards, or procedures for cyber crime and digital forensics.
  - Inconsistencies or loopholes in the laws, rules, standards, or procedures themselves, which may create ambiguity, confusion, or contradiction.
  - Inconsistencies or errors in the digital forensics investigations, such as anomalies, omissions, exaggerations, or tampering, which may compromise the value or admissibility of the evidence .
  - Inconsistencies or biases in the interpretation or application of the laws, rules, standards, or procedures by the judges, lawyers, experts, or jury, which may affect the outcome or fairness of the case.
- Jurisprudential inconsistency can have negative impacts on the justice system, such as:
  - Undermining the credibility or reliability of the digital evidence or the digital forensics investigations .
  - Creating legal uncertainty or unpredictability for the parties involved in the case.
  - Violating the rights or interests of the victims, suspects, or defendants.
  - Hindering the prevention, detection, or prosecution of cyber crime.
- Jurisprudential inconsistency can be addressed or reduced by various measures, such as:
  - Harmonizing or updating the laws, rules, standards, or procedures for cyber crime and digital forensics across different legal systems or jurisdictions.
  - Following the best practices or guidelines for digital forensics investigations, such as preserving the chain of custody, documenting the process, using validated tools, and ensuring quality assurance .
  - Enhancing the education or training of the judges, lawyers, experts, or jury on the technical and legal aspects of cyber crime and digital forensics.
  - Promoting the cooperation or collaboration among the law enforcement, the judiciary, the academia, and the industry in the field of cyber crime and digital forensics.
