### Disk imaging

- Disk imaging is the process of creating an electronic copy of a drive (e.g. a hard drive, USB, etc.) that preserves all the data and metadata in a forensics image format .
- A forensics image format is a file format that supports storing the exact, unaltered, and complete contents of a drive, including deleted or hidden data, slack space, and unallocated space .
- Some examples of forensics image formats are E01, AFF, and RAW .
- Disk imaging is an essential step in digital forensics, as it allows investigators to analyze the data without modifying the original evidence or risking data loss or corruption .
- Disk imaging also enables investigators to perform various tasks, such as data recovery, file carving, malware analysis, password cracking, and timeline analysis .
- Disk imaging can be performed using various tools, such as Tableau TD3, Computer Forensic Tool (CFT), and Dc3dd. These tools have different features, capabilities, and limitations, and should be tested and validated before use .
- Disk imaging should follow certain best practices, such as using write blockers, verifying the integrity of the image, documenting the process, and handling the evidence properly  .
