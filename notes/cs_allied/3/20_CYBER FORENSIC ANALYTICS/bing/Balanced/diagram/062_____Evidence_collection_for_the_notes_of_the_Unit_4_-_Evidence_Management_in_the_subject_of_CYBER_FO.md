### Evidence collection for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence collection is the process of identifying, preserving, and acquiring digital data that can be used in cyber forensics investigations  .
- Evidence collection involves following the principles of digital forensics, such as:
  - Adhering to the legal and ethical standards of the jurisdiction and the organization .
  - Maintaining the integrity and authenticity of the evidence by using proper tools, techniques, and documentation  .
  - Minimizing the impact on the original data and the system or device that contains it .
  - Following the chain of custody and ensuring the security and confidentiality of the evidence  .
- Evidence collection can be performed in different scenarios, such as:
  - Live analysis: collecting evidence from a running system or device, such as memory, network connections, or processes  .
  - Dead analysis: collecting evidence from a powered-off or non-functional system or device, such as hard drives, flash drives, or SIM cards  .
  - Remote analysis: collecting evidence from a system or device that is located in a different physical location, such as cloud storage, email servers, or web servers  .
- Evidence collection can be performed using different methods, such as:
  - Imaging: creating a bit-by-bit copy of the original data or device, which can be verified by using hashing algorithms  .
  - Copying: transferring selected files or folders from the original data or device, which can be verified by using metadata or timestamps  .
  - Logging: recording the actions and events that occur during the evidence collection process, such as commands, outputs, or errors  .
- Evidence collection can be performed using different tools, such as:
  - Hardware tools: devices that can connect to the original data or device, such as write blockers, adapters, or duplicators  .
  - Software tools: programs that can access, analyze, or manipulate the original data or device, such as forensic suites, utilities, or scripts  .
  - Standard tools: tools that are commonly available or built-in in the operating system or device, such as file managers, browsers, or command-line interfaces  .
