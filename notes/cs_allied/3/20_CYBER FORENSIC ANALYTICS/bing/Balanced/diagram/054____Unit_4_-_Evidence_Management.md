## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

- Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a forensically sound manner.
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and recognizing potential sources of digital evidence, such as devices, files, logs, etc.
  - Collection: acquiring and documenting digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, chain of custody, etc.
  - Preservation: protecting digital evidence from alteration, loss, or damage, such as using write blockers, encryption, backups, etc.
  - Analysis: examining and interpreting digital evidence using various methods, such as file carving, data recovery, malware analysis, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the findings and conclusions of digital evidence analysis, such as using charts, graphs, timelines, etc.
- Evidence management requires following the principles of digital forensics, such as:
  - Admissibility: ensuring that digital evidence is legally acceptable and can be used in court, such as by following the rules of evidence, standards, and best practices.
  - Authenticity: ensuring that digital evidence is genuine and has not been tampered with, such as by verifying the integrity, origin, and chain of custody of digital evidence.
  - Completeness: ensuring that digital evidence is comprehensive and covers all relevant aspects of the investigation, such as by collecting all available and pertinent digital evidence.
  - Reliability: ensuring that digital evidence is accurate and consistent, such as by using validated and tested tools and techniques, and documenting the procedures and results of digital evidence analysis.
  - Relevance: ensuring that digital evidence is pertinent and supports the objectives and hypotheses of the investigation, such as by filtering and prioritizing digital evidence based on the case context and scope.
