### Internet Gambling

- Internet gambling is the act of placing bets or wagers on sports, games, or contests using a "wire communication facility", which includes the Internet.
- Internet gambling is illegal in many countries, including the United States, where federal law prohibits it under the Wire Act of 1961 .
- Internet gambling is a lucrative industry, with an estimated global revenue of $66.7 billion in 2020, and a projected growth of 11.5% annually until 2027.
- Internet gambling poses various cyber security risks, such as:
  - Identity theft and fraud: Online gamblers may have to provide personal and financial information to access gambling platforms, which can be stolen or misused by cybercriminals .
  - Account compromise and money laundering: Online gambling accounts can be hacked or breached by cybercriminals, who can then cash out the balances or use them for illicit transactions .
  - Malware infection and data breach: Online gambling platforms may be targeted by cyberattacks, such as distributed denial-of-service (DDoS), ransomware, or phishing, which can disrupt their operations, extort money, or expose sensitive data .
- Internet gambling also raises social and ethical issues, such as:
  - Addiction and problem gambling: Online gambling can be addictive and harmful to the mental and physical health of the gamblers, as well as their families and communities.
  - Underage and vulnerable gambling: Online gambling can be accessed by minors or people with gambling disorders, who may not be aware of the risks or consequences of their actions.
  - Regulatory and legal challenges: Online gambling can be difficult to regulate and enforce, due to the anonymity, cross-border nature, and diversity of the platforms and players .
