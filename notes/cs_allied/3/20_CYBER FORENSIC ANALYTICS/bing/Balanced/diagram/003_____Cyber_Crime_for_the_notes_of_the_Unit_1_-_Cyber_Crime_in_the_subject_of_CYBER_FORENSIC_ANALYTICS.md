### Cyber Crime

Cyber crime is the use of a computer or a computer network as an instrument to further illegal ends, such as committing fraud, trafficking in child pornography and intellectual property, stealing identities, or violating privacy.

Cyber crime can be divided into two main categories :

- **Crimes that target computer networks or devices.** These types of crimes involve different threats (like virus, bugs etc.) and denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, which aim to damage or disrupt the functioning of computers or networks.
- **Crimes that use computer networks to commit other criminal activities.** These types of crimes include cyber stalking, financial fraud or identity theft, which use the internet or other digital platforms to harm, harass, or deceive individuals or organizations.

Some examples of cyber crime are:

- Hacking: unauthorized access to a computer system or network, often with malicious intent.
- Phishing: sending fraudulent emails or messages that appear to be from legitimate sources, in order to trick recipients into revealing personal or financial information.
- Ransomware: a type of malware that encrypts the victim's data and demands a ransom for its decryption.
- Cyber bullying: using the internet or other digital means to harass, threaten, or humiliate another person, often anonymously.
- Cyber terrorism: using the internet or other digital platforms to carry out attacks that cause fear, violence, or disruption, for political or ideological purposes.

Cyber crime poses a serious threat to the security, privacy, and well-being of individuals, organizations, and society. Therefore, cyber crime prevention and cyber crime security are essential to protect oneself and others from the risks and harms of cyber crime. Some of the measures that can be taken to prevent or mitigate cyber crime are:

- Using strong passwords and changing them regularly.
- Installing antivirus software and keeping it updated.
- Avoiding opening suspicious emails or attachments, or clicking on unknown links.
- Being careful about what information is shared online, and who it is shared with.
- Reporting any suspicious or illegal activity to the authorities or relevant platforms.
- Educating oneself and others about the dangers and consequences of cyber crime.
