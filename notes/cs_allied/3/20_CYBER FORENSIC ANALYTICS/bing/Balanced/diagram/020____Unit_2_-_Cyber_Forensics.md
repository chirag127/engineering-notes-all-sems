## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a legally acceptable manner. Cyber forensics can be used to investigate cyber crimes, such as hacking, fraud, identity theft, cyber terrorism, etc. Cyber forensics can also be used to recover lost or deleted data, verify the authenticity of digital documents, or perform audits and compliance checks on digital systems.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- **Cyber forensics process**: The steps involved in conducting a cyber forensics investigation, such as identification, preservation, collection, examination, analysis and reporting of digital evidence.
- **Cyber forensics tools**: The software and hardware tools used by cyber forensics experts to perform various tasks, such as imaging, hashing, carving, searching, recovering, analyzing and presenting digital evidence.
- **Cyber forensics challenges**: The difficulties and limitations faced by cyber forensics experts in dealing with digital evidence, such as encryption, anti-forensics, data volume, data volatility, data diversity, legal and ethical issues, etc.
- **Cyber forensics standards and best practices**: The guidelines and recommendations followed by cyber forensics experts to ensure the quality, reliability, admissibility and integrity of digital evidence, such as ISO/IEC 27037, NIST SP 800-86, ACPO Good Practice Guide, etc.
- **Cyber forensics case studies**: The examples of real-world cyber forensics investigations, such as the Enron case, the BTK killer case, the Sony Pictures hack, etc.
