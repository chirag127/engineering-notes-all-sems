## Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities

Cyber laws are the rules and regulations that govern the use and security of information technology, such as computers, networks, and the Internet. Cyber laws cover various aspects of cyberspace, such as data protection, e-commerce, cybercrime, intellectual property, and freedom of expression. Cyber laws are important to ensure the rights and responsibilities of individuals and organizations in the digital world, as well as to prevent and punish cyberattacks, fraud, hacking, and other malicious activities.

Cyber authorities are the entities that enforce, regulate, and oversee cyber laws. Cyber authorities can be governmental, such as federal, state, or local agencies, or non-governmental, such as industry associations, certifying bodies, or international organizations. Cyber authorities have different roles and functions, such as setting standards, issuing licenses, conducting audits, investigating breaches, imposing sanctions, and providing guidance and education. Cyber authorities are essential to ensure the compliance and accountability of cyber laws, as well as to promote the best practices and cooperation among various stakeholders in cyberspace.

Some examples of cyber laws and authorities are:

- The Information Technology Act, 2000, which is the primary legislation in India that deals with cyber law. It provides the legal framework for electronic transactions, digital signatures, data security, cybercrime, and e-governance. The act also establishes the Certifying Authority, which is the entity that issues digital certificates and regulates the electronic authentication of documents and transactions.
- The Executive Order on Improving the Nation’s Cybersecurity, which is the directive issued by the U.S. President in May 2021, that aims to enhance the security and resilience of the federal government and the critical infrastructure against cyber threats. The order also mandates the development and implementation of various standards, policies, and practices, such as zero-trust architecture, multi-factor authentication, encryption, incident response, and software supply chain security. The order also assigns various roles and responsibilities to different federal agencies, such as the Department of Homeland Security, the Office of Management and Budget, the National Institute of Standards and Technology, and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency.
- The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which is the regulation adopted by the European Union in 2016, that sets the rules for the protection of personal data of individuals in the EU and the European Economic Area. The regulation also applies to any organization that offers goods or services to, or monitors the behavior of, individuals in the EU or the EEA, regardless of their location. The regulation grants various rights to data subjects, such as the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict, and port their data, as well as the right to object and withdraw consent. The regulation also imposes various obligations on data controllers and processors, such as the duty to inform, obtain consent, ensure security, report breaches, and conduct impact assessments. The regulation also establishes the European Data Protection Board, which is the body that coordinates the cooperation and consistency of the data protection authorities of the EU member states.
