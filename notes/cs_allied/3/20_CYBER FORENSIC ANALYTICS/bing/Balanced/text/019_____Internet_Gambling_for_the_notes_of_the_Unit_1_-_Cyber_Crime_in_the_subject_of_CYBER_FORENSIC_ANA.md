### Internet Gambling

- Internet gambling is the activity of placing bets or wagers on games of chance or skill using the internet as a medium.
- Internet gambling is illegal in many countries, including the United States, where federal law prohibits individuals from betting on sports or gambling contests using a "wire communication facility," which includes the internet.
- Internet gambling poses various cyber security risks, such as:
  - Online gambling platforms can be compromised by cybercriminals who can steal personal and financial information, manipulate game outcomes, or cash out balances on hacked accounts.
  - Online gamblers can be targeted by phishing, malware, or ransomware attacks that can trick them into revealing their credentials, infect their devices, or extort money from them.
  - Online gambling can facilitate money laundering, fraud, or other criminal activities by allowing anonymous and untraceable transactions.
- Internet gambling can also have social and psychological impacts, such as:
  - Online gambling can be addictive and lead to compulsive behavior, financial problems, or mental health issues.
  - Online gambling can expose minors to inappropriate or harmful content, such as violence, pornography, or drugs.
  - Online gambling can undermine the integrity and fairness of sports and games by enabling cheating, match-fixing, or corruption.
- Internet gambling can be prevented or regulated by various measures, such as:
  - Implementing strict laws and policies that prohibit or restrict online gambling activities and enforce penalties for violations.
  - Educating and raising awareness among online gamblers and the public about the risks and consequences of internet gambling.
  - Developing and applying security standards and best practices for online gambling platforms and users to protect their data and systems.
  - Collaborating and sharing information among law enforcement agencies, regulators, and industry stakeholders to combat cybercrime and illegal online gambling.
