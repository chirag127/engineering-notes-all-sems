### Evidence collection for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Evidence collection is the process of identifying, accessing, preserving and analyzing data from various sources in the digital environment for forensic investigation .
- Evidence collection plays a vital role in tracing the criminal activities, reconstructing the events, verifying the facts and presenting the findings in a court of law .
- Evidence collection involves the following steps  :
  - Planning and preparation: This step involves defining the scope, objectives, methods and tools of the investigation, as well as obtaining the necessary legal authorization and consent to access the data sources.
  - Identification and acquisition: This step involves locating and securing the potential sources of evidence, such as computers, hard drives, mobile phones, cloud services, network devices, etc. The evidence should be acquired using forensically sound techniques to avoid alteration, damage or loss of data.
  - Preservation and transportation: This step involves creating a verifiable copy of the acquired data, such as an image or a clone, and storing it in a safe and tamper-proof manner. The evidence should be transported with proper documentation, packaging and chain of custody records.
  - Analysis and interpretation: This step involves examining and extracting the relevant data from the preserved evidence, using various tools and techniques, such as file carving, hashing, encryption, steganography, etc. The analysis should be conducted in a logical, systematic and unbiased manner, and the results should be interpreted in the context of the investigation.
  - Reporting and presentation: This step involves documenting and communicating the findings and conclusions of the analysis, using clear, concise and accurate language, and supporting them with evidence and references. The report should be prepared in a format that is suitable for the intended audience, such as a technical report, an executive summary, a testimony, etc.
- Evidence collection faces some challenges and limitations, such as   :
  - Legal and ethical issues: The evidence collection should comply with the applicable laws and regulations, such as privacy, consent, search and seizure, etc. The evidence collection should also respect the rights and interests of the involved parties, such as the owners, users, victims, suspects, etc.
  - Technical and operational issues: The evidence collection should cope with the complexity, diversity, volatility and volume of the digital data, as well as the encryption, obfuscation, deletion and manipulation of the data by the criminals. The evidence collection should also use reliable, validated and updated tools and methods, and follow the best practices and standards of the field.
  - Human and organizational issues: The evidence collection should involve qualified, trained and experienced personnel, who can perform the tasks efficiently, effectively and ethically. The evidence collection should also be coordinated and collaborated with other stakeholders, such as law enforcement, judiciary, industry, academia, etc.
