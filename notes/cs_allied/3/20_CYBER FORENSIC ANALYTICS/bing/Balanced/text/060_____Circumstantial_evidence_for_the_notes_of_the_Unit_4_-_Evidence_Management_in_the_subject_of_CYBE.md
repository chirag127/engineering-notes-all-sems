### Circumstantial evidence

- Circumstantial evidence is evidence that **indirectly** proves a fact or a connection between facts by **inference** or **reasoning**.
- Circumstantial evidence is **not** direct evidence that **explicitly** shows the existence or non-existence of a fact or a connection between facts.
- Circumstantial evidence is often used in **cyber forensics** to establish the **identity** or the **intent** of the **suspect** or the **victim** of a cybercrime.
- Circumstantial evidence can be derived from various sources of **digital evidence**, such as **computer hard drives**, **mobile phones**, **smart devices**, **social media accounts**, **network logs**, etc .
- Circumstantial evidence can be **corroborated** or **contradicted** by other types of evidence, such as **eyewitness testimony**, **physical evidence**, **confessions**, etc.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **strong** or **weak** depending on the **quality** and the **quantity** of the evidence, the **reliability** of the source, the **plausibility** of the inference, and the **consistency** with other evidence.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **challenged** or **disputed** by the **defense** or the **prosecution** based on the **admissibility**, the **relevance**, the **sufficiency**, or the **interpretation** of the evidence.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **presented** or **explained** in court by using **charts**, **diagrams**, **timelines**, **scenarios**, **hypotheticals**, or **expert witnesses**.
- Circumstantial evidence can be **evaluated** or **weighed** by the **judge** or the **jury** based on the **standard of proof**, the **burden of proof**, the **presumption of innocence**, and the **common sense** of the fact-finders.
