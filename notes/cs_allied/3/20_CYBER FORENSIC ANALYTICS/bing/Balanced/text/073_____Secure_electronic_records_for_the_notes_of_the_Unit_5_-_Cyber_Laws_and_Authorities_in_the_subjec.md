### Secure electronic records for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Secure electronic records are digital documents or data that are protected from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction by using encryption, authentication, or other security measures.
- Cyber laws are the legal rules and regulations that govern the use, access, and security of information and communication technologies (ICTs), such as the internet, computers, mobile devices, and networks.
- Cyber laws aim to prevent, detect, and punish cybercrimes, such as hacking, phishing, identity theft, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, cyberterrorism, and cyberwarfare.
- Cyber laws also aim to protect the rights and interests of the users, providers, and regulators of ICTs, such as privacy, data protection, intellectual property, freedom of expression, e-commerce, and e-governance.
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, depending on the legal system, culture, and level of development of each nation. However, there are also some international conventions and agreements that aim to harmonize and coordinate cyber laws across different jurisdictions, such as the Council of Europe Convention on Cybercrime, the Budapest Convention, and the United Nations Convention on the Use of Electronic Communications in International Contracts.
- Cyber authorities are the entities or agencies that are responsible for implementing, enforcing, and overseeing cyber laws and policies in a given country or region. They may include government ministries, departments, or agencies, such as the Department of Homeland Security, the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the National Security Agency, and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency in the United States; the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team, and the National Cyber Security Coordinator in India; and the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity, the European Data Protection Board, and the European Cybercrime Centre in the European Union.
- Cyber authorities may also include non-governmental organizations, such as the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers, the Internet Engineering Task Force, the World Wide Web Consortium, and the Internet Society, that play a role in developing, maintaining, and governing the technical standards and protocols of the internet and other ICTs.
- Cyber forensic analytics is the application of scientific methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and present digital evidence in cybercrime investigations and legal proceedings. It involves the use of various tools and methods, such as disk imaging, data recovery, malware analysis, network analysis, cryptography, steganography, and digital watermarking, to identify, preserve, extract, interpret, and document digital traces left by cybercriminals or cyberattackers on electronic devices, networks, or online platforms.
- Cyber forensic analytics can help cyber authorities to identify the sources, methods, motives, and impacts of cybercrimes or cyberattacks, and to provide reliable and admissible evidence in court. It can also help cyber authorities to prevent or mitigate future cyber threats by enhancing the security and resilience of ICTs and by raising awareness and education among the users and providers of ICTs.
