### DoS for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- DoS stands for **Denial-of-Service**, which is a type of cyber-attack that occurs when an attacker seeks to render a computer or other network inaccessible to its authorized users by momentarily or permanently interrupting the normal operations of a host linked to the Internet.
- DoS attacks can have various motivations, such as extortion, sabotage, activism, or cyberwarfare. They can also be used as a distraction or a cover for other malicious activities.
- DoS attacks can be classified into two main categories: **volumetric** and **application layer**. Volumetric attacks aim to overwhelm the network bandwidth or resources of the target by sending a large amount of traffic or requests. Application layer attacks target the specific functionality or logic of the application or service running on the target, such as web servers, databases, or email servers.
- Some common types of DoS attacks are:
  - **SYN flood**: The attacker sends a large number of TCP SYN packets to the target, initiating a connection request, but never completing the handshake. This causes the target to allocate resources for half-open connections, eventually exhausting its memory or CPU.
  - **UDP flood**: The attacker sends a large number of UDP packets to random ports on the target, causing the target to generate ICMP packets in response, which consume network bandwidth and processing power.
  - **Ping of death**: The attacker sends a malformed or oversized ICMP packet to the target, causing the target to crash or reboot due to a buffer overflow.
  - **HTTP flood**: The attacker sends a large number of HTTP requests to the target, either legitimate or malformed, with the intention of exhausting the target's web server resources or application pool.
  - **Slowloris**: The attacker sends partial HTTP requests to the target, keeping the connections open as long as possible, and sending additional headers at regular intervals. This prevents the target from closing the connections or serving other requests, eventually leading to a denial of service.
- DoS attack forensics is the process of identifying, analyzing, and mitigating DoS attacks. It has several steps, such as:
  - **Detection**: The first step is to detect the presence of a DoS attack, by monitoring the network traffic, system performance, and application logs. Some indicators of a DoS attack are abnormal spikes in traffic volume, high CPU or memory usage, slow response times, or error messages.
  - **Identification**: The next step is to identify the source and type of the DoS attack, by examining the characteristics of the attack traffic, such as IP addresses, ports, protocols, payloads, or patterns. This can help to distinguish the attack traffic from the legitimate traffic, and to employ appropriate mitigation techniques.
  - **Mitigation**: The final step is to mitigate the impact of the DoS attack, by blocking, filtering, or diverting the attack traffic, or by increasing the capacity or resilience of the target system. Some mitigation techniques are firewalls, routers, load balancers, proxies, or cloud-based services.
- DoS attack forensics is important for the investigation of crime and law enforcement, as it can help to trace the origin and motive of the attack, and to collect evidence for prosecution or litigation. It can also help to prevent or reduce the damage caused by the attack, and to improve the security and reliability of the system.
