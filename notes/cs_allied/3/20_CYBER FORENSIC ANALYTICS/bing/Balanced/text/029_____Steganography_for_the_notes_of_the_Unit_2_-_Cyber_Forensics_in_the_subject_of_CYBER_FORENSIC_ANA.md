### Steganography

- Steganography is the practice of concealing messages or information within other nonsecret text or data    .
- Steganography can be used along with cryptography as an extra-secure method in which to protect data.
- Steganography techniques can be applied to images, a video file or an audio file. Typically, however, steganography is written in characters including hash marking, but its usage within images is also common.
- Steganography is different from cryptography, which is the art of writing or solving codes. Cryptography makes the message unreadable, while steganography makes the message invisible.
- Steganography has been around for centuries, and has been used for various purposes, such as hiding political or religious messages, espionage, secret communication, and digital watermarking.
- Steganography can also be used for malicious purposes, such as hiding malware, viruses, or other harmful data within innocent-looking files or messages. Steganography can pose a challenge for cyber security, as it can evade detection by conventional methods.
- Steganography can be classified into different types, depending on the cover medium used to hide the message. Some common types are:

  - Text steganography: hiding the message within a text file, such as by using white spaces, line shifts, or special characters.
  - Image steganography: hiding the message within an image file, such as by altering the least significant bits of the pixels, or using a secret key to encode the message.
  - Audio steganography: hiding the message within an audio file, such as by modifying the amplitude, frequency, or phase of the sound waves, or using a secret key to encode the message.
  - Video steganography: hiding the message within a video file, such as by embedding the message in each frame, or using a secret key to encode the message.
  - Network steganography: hiding the message within a network protocol, such as by using unused or reserved fields, packet headers, or timing delays.

- Steganography can be detected and analyzed by using various methods, such as:

  - Statistical analysis: examining the statistical properties of the cover medium, such as the frequency distribution, entropy, or correlation, and looking for any anomalies or deviations that may indicate the presence of hidden data.
  - Structural analysis: examining the structure and format of the cover medium, such as the file header, footer, or metadata, and looking for any inconsistencies or irregularities that may indicate the presence of hidden data.
  - Visual analysis: examining the visual appearance of the cover medium, such as the color, brightness, or contrast, and looking for any distortions or artifacts that may indicate the presence of hidden data.
  - Auditory analysis: examining the auditory characteristics of the cover medium, such as the volume, pitch, or quality, and looking for any noises or glitches that may indicate the presence of hidden data.
  - Steganalysis tools: using specialized software or hardware tools that can automatically detect and extract hidden data from various cover media, such as StegExpose, StegDetect, or StegSecret.
