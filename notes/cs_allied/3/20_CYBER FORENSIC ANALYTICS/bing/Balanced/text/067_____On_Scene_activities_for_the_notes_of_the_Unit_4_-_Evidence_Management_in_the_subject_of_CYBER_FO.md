### On Scene Activities for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

- On scene activities are the steps that a cyber forensic investigator takes to identify, collect, document, and preserve digital evidence at the scene of a cybercrime.
- On scene activities are crucial for ensuring the integrity, reliability, and admissibility of digital evidence in a court of law.
- Some of the common on scene activities are:

  - **Photographing the scene**: This involves taking pictures of the physical and digital devices, their connections, their locations, and any other relevant details that can help reconstruct the scene later. Photographs can also capture the state of the devices, such as power status, screen display, and indicators.
  - **Sketching the scene**: This involves drawing a diagram of the layout of the scene, showing the location and orientation of the devices, their connections, and any other relevant features. Sketches can also include measurements, labels, and annotations.
  - **Note taking**: This involves writing down the observations, actions, and findings of the investigator at the scene, such as the date and time, the weather conditions, the names and roles of the people present, the serial numbers and models of the devices, the passwords and encryption keys, and any other relevant information. Notes should be clear, accurate, and complete.
  - **Conducting preliminary interviews**: This involves asking questions to the people who are involved or have knowledge of the cybercrime, such as the victims, the suspects, the witnesses, and the system administrators. Interviews can help gather information about the background, motive, opportunity, and means of the cybercrime, as well as the nature, location, and format of the digital evidence.
  - **Recognizing and identifying digital evidence**: This involves determining what types of digital evidence are present at the scene, such as computers, mobile phones, storage devices, network devices, and cloud services. It also involves determining what types of data are stored on the devices, such as files, emails, logs, and metadata. Recognizing and identifying digital evidence can help prioritize and plan the collection process.
  - **Seizing and securing digital evidence**: This involves taking physical and logical custody of the digital evidence, following the legal and ethical guidelines and procedures. It also involves protecting the digital evidence from unauthorized access, modification, damage, or loss, by using appropriate tools, techniques, and containers. Seizing and securing digital evidence can help preserve the original state and condition of the evidence.
  - **Collecting and labeling digital evidence**: This involves copying or acquiring the data from the digital devices, using forensically sound methods and tools. It also involves marking and documenting the digital evidence, using unique identifiers, tags, and labels. Collecting and labeling digital evidence can help establish the chain of custody and the authenticity of the evidence.
  - **Packaging and transporting digital evidence**: This involves preparing and moving the digital evidence from the scene to the laboratory or the storage facility, following the legal and ethical guidelines and procedures. It also involves maintaining the environmental and physical conditions of the digital evidence, such as temperature, humidity, and shock. Packaging and transporting digital evidence can help prevent the degradation or corruption of the evidence.

- On scene activities are part of the computer forensics investigation methodology, which also includes other phases such as analysis, examination, reporting, and presentation .
- On scene activities require the cyber forensic investigator to have the necessary skills, knowledge, and tools to perform the tasks effectively and efficiently. They also require the investigator to follow the best practices and standards of the cyber forensic field, such as the National Institute of Justice (NIJ) guidelines, the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standards, and the Association of Chief Police Officers (ACPO) principles .
