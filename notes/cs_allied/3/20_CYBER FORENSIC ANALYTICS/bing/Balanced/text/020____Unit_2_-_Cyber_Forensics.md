## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

- Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a legally acceptable manner.
- Cyber forensics can be used to investigate various types of cyber crimes, such as hacking, phishing, identity theft, malware attacks, cyber fraud, cyber terrorism, etc.
- Cyber forensics can also be used to recover deleted or encrypted data, trace the source and destination of data transfers, identify the perpetrators and accomplices of cyber crimes, and provide expert testimony in court.
- Cyber forensics involves four main phases: acquisition, examination, analysis and reporting.
  - Acquisition: This phase involves the identification, collection and preservation of digital evidence from various sources, such as computers, mobile devices, networks, cloud services, etc. The evidence should be acquired in a forensically sound manner, which means that it should not be altered, damaged or destroyed during the process. The evidence should also be documented and authenticated using techniques such as hashing, checksums, digital signatures, etc.
  - Examination: This phase involves the extraction and identification of relevant data from the acquired evidence using various tools and techniques, such as file carving, data recovery, password cracking, decryption, etc. The examiner should be able to filter out the irrelevant or redundant data and focus on the data that is related to the case.
  - Analysis: This phase involves the interpretation and evaluation of the extracted data using various methods, such as timeline analysis, link analysis, network analysis, malware analysis, etc. The analyst should be able to reconstruct the events and actions that occurred on the digital devices or networks, identify the motives and methods of the cyber criminals, and establish the connections and correlations between the data and the case.
  - Reporting: This phase involves the presentation and communication of the findings and conclusions of the analysis in a clear, concise and accurate manner. The report should be written in a format and language that is understandable by the intended audience, such as law enforcement, lawyers, judges, etc. The report should also include the evidence, the tools and techniques used, the assumptions and limitations, and the recommendations and suggestions for further actions.
