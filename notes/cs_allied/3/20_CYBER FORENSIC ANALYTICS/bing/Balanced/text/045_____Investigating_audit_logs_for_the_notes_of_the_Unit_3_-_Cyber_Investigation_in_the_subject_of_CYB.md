### Investigating audit logs for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Audit logs are records of events that occur on a system or network, such as user actions, system changes, security breaches, etc.  
- Audit logs are essential for cyber investigation because they can provide evidence of who did what, when, where, how and why on a system or network.  
- Audit logs can help investigators to:
  - Identify the source and scope of an incident
  - Trace the actions and movements of an attacker
  - Recover lost or damaged data
  - Determine the impact and severity of an incident
  - Establish the timeline and sequence of events
  - Verify compliance with policies and regulations
  - Prevent or mitigate future incidents   
- Audit logs can be generated by various sources, such as operating systems, applications, services, devices, etc.   
- Audit logs can contain various types of information, such as event name, date and time, user identity, source and destination IP addresses, action performed, outcome, etc.   
- Audit logs can be stored in different formats and locations, such as text files, databases, cloud services, etc.   
- Audit logs can be analyzed using different tools and techniques, such as log management software, data visualization, anomaly detection, correlation, etc.   
- Audit logs should be collected, stored, and analyzed in a secure and reliable manner, following the best practices of audit log management, such as:
  - Define the objectives and scope of audit logging
  - Configure the audit logging settings and policies
  - Enable and monitor the audit logging functions
  - Protect the integrity and confidentiality of audit logs
  - Retain the audit logs for a sufficient period of time
  - Review and analyze the audit logs regularly
  - Report and respond to the audit log findings
