## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

- Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a forensically sound manner.
- Evidence management involves the following steps:
  - Identification: finding and recognizing potential sources of digital evidence, such as devices, storage media, network logs, etc.
  - Collection: acquiring digital evidence using appropriate tools and techniques, such as imaging, hashing, documentation, etc.
  - Preservation: protecting digital evidence from alteration, loss, or damage, such as using write blockers, encryption, backups, etc.
  - Analysis: examining digital evidence using various methods and tools, such as file carving, data recovery, malware analysis, etc.
  - Presentation: reporting and communicating the findings and conclusions of digital evidence analysis, such as using charts, graphs, timelines, etc.
- Evidence management requires following the principles of digital forensics, such as:
  - Admissibility: ensuring that digital evidence is legally acceptable and relevant to the case, such as following the chain of custody, obtaining proper authorization, etc.
  - Authenticity: ensuring that digital evidence is genuine and not tampered with, such as verifying the integrity, origin, and date of the evidence, etc.
  - Completeness: ensuring that digital evidence is comprehensive and sufficient to support the case, such as collecting all relevant data, avoiding data loss, etc.
  - Reliability: ensuring that digital evidence is accurate and consistent, such as using validated tools and techniques, documenting the procedures, etc.
  - Believability: ensuring that digital evidence is credible and convincing, such as using clear and logical reasoning, providing supporting evidence, etc.
